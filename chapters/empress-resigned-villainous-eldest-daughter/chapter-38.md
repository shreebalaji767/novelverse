# **Chapter 38: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Tea. The Empress Dowager Has Prepared Three Questions. The Emperor Has Been Instructed Not to Bring Any Documents.**

**30th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Empress Dowager had prepared three questions for Yuru.



She had also prepared tea, fruit, and several small dishes.



The questions were written on a sheet of paper.



The tea arrangements were written on another.



The fruit arrangements were written on a third.



Her senior attendant, Madam Zhao, examined the documents.



"Your Majesty, should the questions be given to the former Empress before she arrives?"



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"No."



"Should they be placed in the sitting room?"



"No."



"Then where should they be kept?"



The Empress Dowager folded the sheet.



"With me."



Madam Zhao bowed.



The Empress Dowager looked at the three questions.



She had spent several days considering what to ask.



The first concerned Yuru's future residence.



The second concerned whether she intended to remain close to the imperial family.



The third concerned whether she would continue advising the imperial children.



They were reasonable questions.



She had also written a fourth question.



It concerned the recent changes in the Imperial Household Administration.



She looked at it for a moment.



Then she crossed it out.



Madam Zhao noticed.



"Your Majesty?"



"The emperor can ask about the administration."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Empress Dowager put the paper aside.



"Today, I am inviting her to tea."

Yuru arrived shortly before the appointed hour.



She was received in the Empress Dowager's private sitting room.



There were no officials.



There were no reports.



There were no requests for approval.



Madam Zhao poured tea.



The Empress Dowager looked at Yuru.



"You have been well?"



"Yes, Mother."



"Your residence is comfortable?"



"Very comfortable."



"Have you decided where you will live permanently?"



"Not yet."



The Empress Dowager nodded.



She had expected that answer.



"Your family has invited you to visit again?"



"Yes. My father has asked me to visit next week."



"Will you go?"



"I intend to."



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"Do you wish to remain in the palace?"



Yuru considered the question.



"I am comfortable where I am for now. I have not decided what I wish to do afterward."



The Empress Dowager lifted her teacup.



"You have always been careful with decisions."



Yuru smiled faintly.



"I have sometimes been too careful."



"That is not always a fault."



"No."



The Empress Dowager looked toward the window.



"Do you regret leaving the position?"



Yuru answered without hesitation.



"No."



The Empress Dowager nodded slowly.



She had not expected a different answer.



Still, hearing it directly made the matter more real.

The Empress Dowager set down her teacup.



"The Crown Prince has been working on the Eastern Palace accounts."



"I heard."



"The Second Prince has been assigned transport matters."



"Yes."



"The Third Princess has been studying the household records."



"She visited me yesterday."



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"Do you believe they can manage?"



"They can learn."



"That is not the same as saying they can manage."



"They will make mistakes. The important thing is that they understand which office is responsible and how to correct an error."



The Empress Dowager was silent.



For years, she had regarded Yuru's competence as a source of stability.



She had also assumed that such competence would continue to be available.



Now the palace was being required to distribute responsibilities among people who had previously relied on Yuru's judgment.



It was not an easy transition.



But it was happening.



The Empress Dowager looked at Yuru.



"You will still advise the children?"



"If they ask me personally, I will."



"And the emperor?"



Yuru smiled.



"He is capable of asking questions."



The Empress Dowager almost laughed.



"That is true."

At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, the emperor was receiving an entirely different instruction.



Gao Shun stood before him.



"The Empress Dowager has requested that Your Majesty not send documents to her sitting room today."



The emperor looked up.



"I was not planning to."



"She has also asked that Your Majesty not bring any documents when visiting the former Empress."



The emperor paused.



"Why would I bring documents?"



Gao Shun remained silent.



The emperor looked at him.



"Have I been doing that?"



Gao Shun lowered his head.



"On several occasions, Your Majesty has discussed administrative matters during personal visits."



The emperor thought about it.



There had been the winter supply schedule.



The ceremonial inventory.



The allocation of household servants.



The damaged screens.



He looked down at his desk.



"Very well. I will not bring documents."



Gao Shun bowed.



The emperor returned to his work.



A moment later, he asked, "Has the Department of Rites submitted the revised ceremonial schedule?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Good."



"Would Your Majesty like to review it?"



The emperor looked at him.



Gao Shun bowed.



"I shall leave it with the responsible office."



"That would be wise."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the morning began with a dispute over the northern warehouse records.



Shen Mingyuan had asked Steward Zhou to bring the old transport books.



The steward had brought two volumes.



Mingyuan examined them.



One contained entries for grain movement.



The other contained payments to transport contractors.



The dates did not align perfectly.



He called the clerk who had copied the older entries.



The clerk arrived carrying a bundle of loose papers.



"These were found in the rear records cabinet."



Mingyuan looked at them.



"Why were they not included with the transport books?"



"The documents were not bound together."



"Do they belong to the same year?"



"Some do."



"Which ones?"



The clerk hesitated.



"I have not checked every date."



Mingyuan closed his eyes briefly.



"Then we will check them."



Steward Zhou looked at him.



"Would it not be quicker to send them to Miss Mingzhao?"



Mingyuan looked up.



"No."



The steward bowed.



Mingyuan continued.



"We are responsible for preparing the records. Sun Qiming can examine them when he visits. My sister does not need to sort every loose paper for us."



Steward Zhou nodded.



He had not intended to offend anyone.



He had simply become accustomed to the quickest solution.



Unfortunately, the quickest solution had often been to ask Mingzhao.



Mingyuan began sorting the papers by date.



Several entries were incomplete.



One receipt referred to a cart payment without naming the destination.



Another referred to a storage fee without identifying the goods.



A third was too damaged to read clearly.



He placed each in a separate pile.



The work was slow.



It was also necessary.

Mingzhao was having breakfast in her rented residence.



The kitchen had prepared rice porridge, vegetables, and a small dish of preserved greens.



Qiao Yue placed the morning account sheet beside her.



"The lower room was dry this morning."



"Good."



"The landlord has agreed to inspect the rear drain again after the next heavy rain."



"Have him record the inspection."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Then she hesitated.



"Miss, the landlord has also asked whether we might extend the rental agreement."



Mingzhao looked up.



"Already?"



"He says he would prefer to know in advance."



"Our agreement has not yet reached its end."



"He says he has another tenant interested."



Mingzhao put down her spoon.



"Tell him we will decide according to the agreed notice period."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Should I ask whether the other tenant is willing to wait?"



"No. That is his business."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Then we do not need to hurry?"



"We need to know our options. We do not need to make a decision merely because someone else would prefer an earlier answer."



Qiao Yue smiled.



"That sounds familiar."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Eat your breakfast."



Qiao Yue withdrew, still smiling.
Later that morning, Mingzhao received a letter from Sun Qiming.



The accountant reported that he had reviewed the latest family records and identified several additional documents requiring verification.



The duplicated firewood transport charge remained unresolved.



The northern warehouse grain discrepancy remained unchanged.



The original figure was still forty-seven sacks.



Twenty sacks were covered by a movement instruction to southern storage.



Eighteen were confirmed in the southern storage ledger.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



Sun Qiming had also contacted a former business associate who might know whether Zhao Fusheng's roadside storage yard still had surviving records.



No confirmation had been obtained.



Mingzhao read the letter carefully.



She wrote a reply thanking him for the update.



She asked him to continue distinguishing confirmed facts from possible leads.



Then she added a short note about the disputed property.



She wanted the missing survey register investigated before any purchase decision.



She sealed the reply.



Qiao Yue asked, "Has the grain been found?"



"No."



"Has the roadside merchant been found?"



"Not yet."



"Has the property boundary been established?"



"No."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Nothing has been settled."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Several things have been settled."



"Which things?"



"The rental terms. The partnership payment records. The confirmed repair expense. The accepted silk deliveries."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Those are not the things that keep causing trouble."



"That is because resolved matters usually stop asking for attention."



Qiao Yue thought about that.



Then she nodded.



It was irritatingly reasonable.
At the Empress Dowager's residence, tea had reached its second round.



The conversation had moved to less formal matters.



The Empress Dowager asked whether Yuru had been reading.



"Yes."



"What?"



"Several histories and a collection of essays."



"Do you intend to study something in particular?"



"I have been considering it."



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"What would you like to study?"



Yuru thought for a moment.



"Perhaps the history of commercial institutions. I have spent so much time dealing with household supply arrangements that I would like to understand how some of the larger markets developed."



The Empress Dowager smiled.



"You may finally read something without needing to approve it."



Yuru laughed.



"That is the intention."



Madam Zhao poured more tea.



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"Would you prefer to travel?"



"Perhaps later."



"Or establish a residence with your family?"



"I may stay with my parents for a time, but I do not want to decide permanently yet."



The Empress Dowager nodded.



She had been prepared to advise Yuru.



Instead, she found herself listening.



After a while, she said, "I hope you do not think that leaving the position means you have abandoned the palace."



Yuru looked at her.



"I do not."



"You have served the imperial household for many years."



"I know."



"And your work has mattered."



Yuru was silent for a moment.



Then she said, "I am glad it mattered. But I do not believe that means I must continue doing it forever."



The Empress Dowager looked down at her tea.



"No," she said at last. "It does not."



It was not a complete reconciliation.



It was, however, an honest acknowledgment.
At the palace, the Crown Prince had received a report concerning the Eastern Palace's household expenditure.



He read it twice.



The food account was complete.



The clothing account was missing two supporting receipts.



The transport account included a payment that had been entered under both maintenance and ordinary travel.



He called the responsible clerk.



"Why is this payment listed twice?"



The clerk examined the report.



"The first entry is under the transport account. The second is under maintenance."



"Was the carriage repaired?"



"No, Your Highness."



"Then why is it listed under maintenance?"



"The clerk who prepared the summary believed the payment was related to the carriage."



"Was it a travel payment?"



"It appears so."



"Then correct the classification and attach the supporting record."



The clerk bowed.



The Crown Prince looked at the report again.



He was learning that reviewing an account was not simply a matter of finding mistakes.



It required identifying what had happened, what evidence supported it, and who was responsible for correcting it.



He placed the report aside.



Then he sent a note to the Second Prince asking whether the transport office had a matching record.



He did not send the matter to Yuru.



He did not send it to the emperor.



He asked the office responsible.
At the Marquisate, Mingxiu was reviewing the household purchase records.



She had become more careful about comparing dates, quantities, and delivery notes.



The kitchen supervisor had returned with the inspection results for the damaged pots.



Two could be repaired.



One had a cracked base and required replacement.



Mingxiu examined the report.



"How much will the repairs cost?"



The supervisor gave her the estimate.



"And the replacement?"



He gave her the second estimate.



She compared the figures.



"Repair the two usable pots. Replace the cracked one."



The supervisor bowed.



Mingxiu signed the approval.



Then she paused.



"Keep the old pot until the replacement arrives."



"Why?"



"Because if the new pot is delayed, the kitchen still needs equipment."



The supervisor nodded.



After he left, Mingxiu looked at the signed document.



She had made a decision based on the evidence.



It was not particularly difficult.



Yet she understood why the household had once relied so heavily on Mingzhao.



The work was constant.



A person who handled it had to remember dozens of small things that others rarely noticed.



Mingxiu closed the file.



She was not yet good at it.



But she was beginning to understand the task.
In the afternoon, the emperor received a personal invitation from the Empress Dowager to visit her residence.



He accepted.



He arrived without documents.



The Empress Dowager looked at his hands.



"You have brought nothing?"



The emperor looked at her.



"You instructed me not to."



"Good."



He sat down.



Yuru was already there.



The emperor greeted her.



"Your visit to the Xu residence went well?"



"Yes."



"Your family was pleased?"



"Very."



The Empress Dowager looked at him.



"Do not ask her about the palace."



The emperor paused.



"I was asking about her family."



"Then continue."



Yuru hid a smile behind her teacup.



The emperor looked between them.



He had ruled for eighteen years.



He had negotiated with ministers, handled border disputes, and survived several difficult court factions.



He had not expected a cup of tea to become such a carefully supervised activity.



He decided not to complain.
After tea, Yuru returned to her residence.



The emperor accompanied the Empress Dowager back to her inner rooms.



As they walked, he said, "She seems well."



"She is."



"She has not decided where she will live."



"That is her decision."



"I know."



The Empress Dowager glanced at him.



"Do you?"



The emperor sighed.



"I am trying."



The Empress Dowager nodded.



"Then continue trying."



He accepted the rebuke.



After a moment, he asked, "Do you believe the palace will manage?"



The Empress Dowager stopped.



She considered the question.



"It must."



The emperor nodded.



"I agree."



"Then do not make every small difficulty a reason to call her back."



"I have not."



The Empress Dowager looked at him.



He amended his answer.



"I have not done so recently."



She continued walking.



That was a more accurate statement.
At dusk, Mingzhao finished reviewing the day's household expenses.



The landlord had agreed to inspect the drain again after the next heavy rain.



The missing vegetable receipt had been filed.



The kitchen stock was sufficient.



The servants' wages were recorded.



Her personal claims remained separate from the family settlement.



She then opened the letter from her father once more.



There was a short additional note.



He had written that the next settlement meeting would be arranged after Sun Qiming completed the document review.



He had not asked her to review the accounts.



Mingzhao read the note.



Then she placed it in the family correspondence folder.



Qiao Yue noticed.



"Did the Marquis ask you to return?"



"No."



"Did he ask you to review the accounts?"



"No."



"That is an improvement."



Mingzhao nodded.



"It is."



She did not mistake one letter for a change in the entire family.



But it was a small change.



She could acknowledge it without pretending that everything had been resolved.
That evening, Yuru sat beside her window.



The day had passed without an administrative request.



The Empress Dowager had asked about her future.



The emperor had managed to drink tea without bringing documents.



The Crown Prince had handled an accounting question through the responsible office.



The palace was not perfect.



It was becoming more functional.



Yuru opened the book she had been reading.



On the other side of the capital, Mingzhao closed her household ledger.



Her temporary residence still required maintenance.



Her family settlement remained incomplete.



Her business still had stock waiting for inspection.



The northern canal property's boundary was still disputed.



Neither woman had reached the end of her difficulties.



But neither had returned to the work she had deliberately left behind.



That evening, the palace sent Yuru no documents.



The Marquisate sent Mingzhao no account books.



Both women considered this a successful day.



And in two separate households, several people quietly discovered that the world did not end when a capable woman declined to do their work for them.
