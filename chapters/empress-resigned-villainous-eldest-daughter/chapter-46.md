# **Chapter 46: The Former Empress Has Been Asked to Attend a Family Dinner. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Attend a Family Meeting. One Has Been Promised Eight Dishes. The Other Has Been Promised Eight Documents.**

**8th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The rice arrived shortly after sunrise.



It arrived in a cart with a broken side panel, two sacks tied together with rope, and a driver who appeared deeply offended by the condition of the road.



Mingzhao inspected the delivery note.



The quantity matched the order.



The sacks were dry.



The grain was of the agreed quality.



She signed the receipt.



The driver looked relieved.



"Miss Shen, I hope the delay caused no inconvenience."



"It did."



The driver hesitated.



Mingzhao handed the receipt to her correspondence servant.



"But the quantity and quality are correct. The payment will follow the agreed terms."



The driver bowed.



He left looking considerably happier.



Qiao Yue watched the cart disappear.



"That was surprisingly peaceful."



"The supplier delivered what he promised."



"After a delay."



"Which we recorded."



Qiao Yue glanced at the receipt.



"Would you like to write to him about the broken cart panel?"



"No."



"Why not?"



"Because I did not purchase the cart."



Qiao Yue laughed.



Mingzhao returned to breakfast.

At the palace, Zhao Jingheng was reviewing a proposal concerning the distribution of ceremonial furnishings.



The proposal was only three pages long.



Unfortunately, it had been prepared by three different offices.



The Department of Rites had listed the objects according to ceremonial use.



Palace Stores had listed them according to storage location.



The Imperial Household Administration had listed them according to purchase and maintenance responsibility.



All three lists were correct.



They were also difficult to compare.



The Emperor looked at Gao Shun.



"Why are there three names for the same screen?"



Gao Shun examined the documents.



"The Department of Rites calls it the Eastern Audience Screen. Palace Stores records it as the painted folding screen in Storehouse Four. The Imperial Household Administration refers to it as the screen purchased during the seventh year of Your Majesty's reign."



"Are they the same object?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then why are they not identified by one reference number?"



Gao Shun remained silent.



The Emperor looked at the papers again.



"Have the offices agreed on a common inventory reference?"



"They have proposed one."



"Good."



"There is a small disagreement about which office should maintain the master list."



The Emperor closed his eyes.



Gao Shun waited.



"Which office physically holds the objects?"



"Palace Stores."



"Which office determines their ceremonial use?"



"The Department of Rites."



"Which office records purchases and maintenance?"



"The Imperial Household Administration."



"Then each office should maintain its own relevant record and use the same reference number. Nobody needs to own every piece of information."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor returned to the proposal.



He had begun to suspect that the palace had spent many years treating the word *responsibility* as though it meant *exclusive ownership of every related document*.



It was an expensive habit.

At the Duke of Xu's residence, the household was preparing for a family dinner.



Madam Xu had invited Yuru for the twelfth day of the month.



The invitation specified eight dishes.



It also mentioned that the meal would be informal.



The Duke read the invitation again.



"Why have you listed the dishes?"



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Because she should know what will be served."



"She is our daughter."



"That does not mean she can eat whatever we happen to prepare."



The Duke considered this.



"She has eaten at this residence for thirty-eight years."



"She has also spent years eating according to palace schedules."



The Duke had no immediate reply.



Madam Xu instructed the kitchen to prepare ordinary family dishes.



Then she added a note.



No ceremonial seating arrangement.



No formal reception.



No additional household expenses charged to the courtyard.



The Duke read the note.



"Is this necessary?"



"Yes."



He sighed.



"Very well."

At Mingzhao's temporary residence, a letter arrived from the Marquisate.



It was from Mr Liang, the clan representative.



The letter confirmed that the family intended to hold a preliminary property meeting on the tenth day.



Eight documents would be discussed.



The documents concerned agricultural holdings, warehouse buildings, leases, maintenance obligations, and several disputed expenses.



Mingzhao read the list.



She had already reviewed the eleven-document agenda.



Three documents had been removed because their supporting records were not ready.



Two had been combined because they referred to the same property.



The remaining eight were now arranged in a more useful order.



She placed the letter beside the previous correspondence.



Qiao Yue leaned over.



"Only eight?"



"Eight for this meeting."



"That sounds manageable."



"It is a preliminary discussion."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"You always add another sentence."



"Because the first sentence is rarely sufficient."



Mingzhao wrote her reply.



She confirmed attendance, with Sun Qiming present as independent accountant.



She also requested that the meeting record distinguish between agreed classifications, disputed classifications, and matters requiring additional evidence.



She would not accept a general statement that the family had reached an agreement when the documents did not support one.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan was reviewing the agricultural property register.



He had spread the records across a table in the former accounts room.



Mingxiu sat opposite him.



She was comparing the current household supply list with the previous month's purchases.



Their mother had entered the room earlier and asked whether Mingzhao could be invited to review the accounts.



Mingyuan had refused.



Not rudely.



He had simply explained that his elder sister was no longer responsible for the estate's ordinary accounts.



Madam Liang had not been pleased.



She had nevertheless left.



Mingxiu looked up from her papers.



"Mother thinks the meeting will be easier if Elder Sister attends."



"She will attend."



"That is not what Mother means."



Mingyuan continued reading.



"I know."



Mingxiu put down her brush.



"She wants Elder Sister to correct the records."



"Then we should correct them ourselves."



"Do you know how?"



"Not yet."



Mingxiu stared at him.



He looked up.



"What?"



"Nothing."



She returned to her papers.



After a moment, she said, "The western agricultural property is still listed as leased."



"Is the lease current?"



"No. It expired last year."



"Has the tenant renewed it?"



"Not according to the file."



Mingyuan took the register.



He compared the dates.



"Then we should mark it as a lease with expired documentation, not as an active lease."



Mingxiu nodded.



She wrote the correction in the margin.



"And the southern building?"



"Which one?"



"The building listed as usable."



Mingyuan found the entry.



"The roof?"



"Needs repair."



"Is there an inspection report?"



"Only a note from the steward."



"Then we need an inspection."



Mingxiu sighed.



"Another document."



Mingyuan looked at her.



"Would you prefer that we pretend the roof is sound?"



"No."



"Then we need another document."



She picked up her brush.



The family had discovered that correcting a register required more than changing a word.



It required finding out whether the old word had ever been true.

At the imperial palace, Third Princess Zhao Minglan was in the library.



She had been reviewing the borrowing records for the shared imperial collection.



The books were now divided into three categories.



Books belonging to the private study.



Books belonging to the shared library.



Books temporarily borrowed from the Department of Rites.



The division had been straightforward.



The confirmation was not.



Two books listed as borrowed had already been returned.



One book had been moved to another shelf without a corresponding record.



A fourth entry referred to a volume whose title had been copied incorrectly.



Minglan stood before the librarian.



"Can we identify the fourth book?"



The librarian hesitated.



"The title is incomplete, Your Highness."



"Do we have the author's name?"



"Only the first character."



"Do we have the shelf number?"



"The old shelf number."



Minglan looked at the record.



"Then let us compare the old shelf arrangement."



The librarian bowed.



Minglan examined the shelves.



She had learned from Yuru that a record should not be corrected merely because someone found it inconvenient.



The book had to be identified first.



After some searching, they found two volumes with similar titles.



Neither matched the old description completely.



Minglan instructed the librarian to leave the entry unresolved until the Department of Rites could compare its own catalogue.



The librarian looked relieved.



"Your Highness is very careful."



Minglan smiled.



"I am learning that guessing creates more work."

The Emperor received the library report later that afternoon.



He read it.



Then he placed it beside the ceremonial inventory.



Both reports contained unresolved entries.



Both had been clearly marked.



He found this encouraging.



He also found it irritating.



"At least the reports no longer pretend that everything is settled," he said.



Gao Shun bowed.



"That is progress, Your Majesty."



The Emperor looked at him.



"Do you think the palace has become more efficient?"



Gao Shun considered the question carefully.



"Some offices have become more efficient."



"And the others?"



"They have become more aware of their inefficiency."



The Emperor stared at him.



Gao Shun lowered his head.



After a moment, the Emperor laughed.



"That is an unfortunate distinction."

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received a message from Lin Shuyun.



The buyer had responded to the proposed terms for the additional two-hundred-bolt order.



He accepted staged inspection.



He accepted payment for each accepted delivery group.



He was willing to provide a deposit.



However, he wanted the first delivery earlier than the workshop had estimated.



Lin had not agreed.



She had asked the workshop manager to calculate whether the schedule could be shortened without reducing quality.



The current two corrected bolts had also been presented for buyer inspection.



The buyer had not yet confirmed acceptance.



Mingzhao read the letter twice.



She wrote back:



The proposed additional order should remain unaccepted until the revised production schedule and labour costs are confirmed. The current order must be completed and paid according to its existing contract.



She added that the deposit should not be treated as profit.



Qiao Yue watched her finish the letter.



"Miss, the buyer has agreed to most of the terms."



"Most is not all."



"Could the workshop finish earlier?"



"Perhaps."



"Would that be good?"



"It would be good if the work could be completed properly."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao sealed the letter.



She had no intention of turning a profitable possibility into a confirmed obligation before the numbers were ready.

That evening, Yuru received a message from the Emperor.



It was not an official order.



It was a short personal note asking whether she would join him for a walk in the gardens the following afternoon.



She read it once.



Fang Ru stood nearby.



"Will you go?"



"Yes."



"Should I prepare a carriage?"



"No. We will walk."



"Would you like a cloak?"



"Perhaps."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru placed the note on the table.



The Emperor had not included a report.



He had not included a request for advice.



He had not asked her to inspect a ceremonial object.



It was an ordinary invitation.



She found that she appreciated the difference.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received Mingzhao's confirmation of attendance.



He read the letter.



Then he placed it before Madam Liang.



"She will attend on the tenth."



Madam Liang looked relieved.



"Then she can help settle the property questions."



"She will attend with Sun Qiming."



Madam Liang's expression changed.



"Again?"



"Yes."



"Must every matter be examined by an outside accountant?"



"The accounts are under review."



"She knows the estate better than anyone."



"That is why she is not being asked to review it."



Madam Liang looked at him.



Shen Guowei set down the letter.



"Mingzhao is coming to discuss her property claims and the division. She is not returning to manage the estate."



Madam Liang said nothing.



He continued.



"We agreed to her departure. We cannot now behave as though she never left."



Madam Liang's expression remained unhappy.



"She could still help."



"She has helped for six years."



The words were quiet.



They were also final.



Madam Liang turned away.



Across the estate, Mingyuan and Mingxiu continued their work.



The eight documents were being prepared.



The meeting would proceed.



It would not be easy.

Before going to bed, Mingzhao reviewed her personal ledger.



The rice purchase had been entered.



The household provisions were adequate.



The rental expenses remained within the agreed arrangement.



The silk partnership account remained separate.



The confirmed personal advances to the Marquisate had not yet been incorporated into a final settlement.



The ten uncertain entries remained uncertain.



She closed the ledger.



Qiao Yue brought the lamp closer.



"Tomorrow, will you work on the property papers?"



"Yes."



"And the day after?"



"Probably."



"And the day after that?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Do you have a particular reason for asking?"



"No. I was wondering when you would have a day without documents."



Mingzhao considered it.



"After the property meeting."



Qiao Yue smiled.



"Then I shall prepare for a very busy tenth day."



Mingzhao extinguished the lamp.



She had learned that leaving the Marquisate did not mean leaving its unfinished affairs behind.



It meant that those affairs no longer automatically belonged to her.



That distinction was still being tested.



At the palace, the former Empress was preparing to walk in the gardens.



At the Marquisate, her former household's eldest daughter was preparing to attend a meeting.



One had been invited to dinner.



The other had been invited to discuss property.



Neither invitation had included a promise that everything would be settled.



That was probably wise.
