# **Chapter 120: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Choose a Pattern and Has Discovered That the Dowager's Generosity Does Not Require Her to Finish Anything by a Particular Day. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Learned That a Merchant Can Agree to Every Question Except the One That Determines Whether He Can Supply the Goods. The Marquis's Heir Has Received an Archive Index and Discovered That a Missing Register May Have Been Filed Under a Name Nobody Would Have Thought to Search.**

**24th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had three possible patterns before her.

The first was a small branch with narrow leaves. The second showed a pair of birds perched on a flowering stem. The third was a simple arrangement of clouds and water lines.

She had asked Fang Ru to find a few examples in the old embroidery book kept in the guest residence. Fang Ru had returned with the book, three loose pattern sheets, and a small wooden frame.

Yuru examined the frame first.

"It is better than the other one."

"It was in the lower cabinet. The wood is a little worn, but the joints are firm."

"That is sufficient."

She placed the frame beside the cream cloth sent by the Dowager.

The cloth was fine enough for a small decorative piece. The edge had a woven border, which meant she did not need to cover it with additional stitching.

Yuru examined the three patterns again.

"The branch would be easiest."

Fang Ru nodded.

"The birds would take longer."

"And the clouds?"

"They are simple, but the lines must be even."

Yuru looked at the clouds.

She had spent several minutes considering them the previous evening.

"Let us begin with the branch."

Fang Ru brought the thread box closer.

"Which colour?"

Yuru selected a muted green and a lighter shade for the leaves.

She had no intention of making a large piece. A small branch would be enough to test the cloth and the frame.

After preparing the first stitches, she stopped.

"The pattern sheet is too large."

Fang Ru leaned closer.

"It can be reduced."

"Would that alter the shape?"

"Not if we preserve the proportions."

Yuru looked at her.

"Can you show me?"

Fang Ru took a piece of thin paper and began marking the outline.

She worked carefully, measuring the distance between the stem and the leaves. Yuru watched for a moment, then returned to the thread box.

"You have a good eye for this."

"My mother taught me to copy patterns when I was young."

"Did you enjoy it?"

Fang Ru thought about the question.

"Sometimes. I preferred choosing the colours."

Yuru smiled.

"Then you may choose the thread for the flowers if I make them."

Fang Ru looked pleased.

The pattern was soon reduced to a size suitable for the cloth.

Yuru placed it beneath the fabric and began the first line.

The work was slow, but she did not mind.

The central records collection sent its reply to the Marquisate before noon.

Mingyuan was in the records room when Liang Wen brought the sealed paper.

He recognized the seal and immediately opened it.

The supervisor had found an entry in the general collection index, but the title differed from the one in the transfer list.

The book was recorded as a *Northern Road Movement Supplement*.

Mingyuan read the words twice.

"Is this the dispatch register?"

Liang Wen examined the copy.

"It may be."

"Why is the title different?"

"The original title may have been shortened, or the book may have been entered under a later classification."

Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou.

"Can we request the book?"

"The entry gives a collection number."

"Then request it."

Zhou examined the lower part of the page.

"There is a qualification."

Mingyuan read the note.

The collection number referred to a group of transport records, not a single volume. The index listed several bound books and loose papers under the same heading. The northern dispatch register might be among them, but the collection had not yet been examined individually.

Mingyuan put the paper down.

"At least we have a collection number."

"Yes."

"Can they send the whole collection?"

"Not without an inspection order. Some of the papers are old and damaged."

"Then request permission to inspect them at the collection."

Liang Wen prepared a note.

Mingyuan read the index again.

The different title was irritating, but it was also a useful discovery. The missing register had not necessarily vanished. It might simply have been filed under a later name.

He pointed to the entry.

"Ask them to include every item in the group, even loose papers and incomplete covers."

Liang Wen wrote.

"Should we mention the four-cart movement?"

"Only as a date range and an office reference. We are looking for the register, not asking them to confirm our theory."

Zhou watched him finish.

"You are becoming more patient with old records."

Mingyuan looked up.

"I am becoming less confident that they can be persuaded to answer quickly."

"That is also a kind of patience."

"It is an unpleasant kind."

He signed the request.

Shen Mingzhao received the first supplier's response after lunch.

The merchant had answered the questions about colour and width.

He could guarantee the width within a narrow range, provided the loom was adjusted before production. He could not guarantee an exact colour match. He was willing to accept a limited variation, but he wanted Mingzhao to agree that small differences caused by dyeing conditions would not count as defects.

Mingzhao read the statement.

The merchant had answered the questions that were easiest for him.

She called Qiao Yue.

"Prepare a reply. Ask him to define the colour variation in a way that can be inspected."

"How should he define it?"

"By reference to the approved sample under consistent light, with a written description of what difference he considers acceptable. If he cannot provide a measurable standard, he must say so."

Qiao Yue made a note.

"Would you accept a small variation?"

"That depends on the material and the buyer's requirements. We cannot decide it before the buyer has approved the sample."

"Then the merchant must agree to the buyer's standard?"

"He must agree to the standard we use in the contract. If he will not, we cannot promise the buyer that the goods will meet it."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao looked at the third sample.

The cloth was close enough to tempt a person into believing the remaining difference could be overlooked.

She had learned not to confuse temptation with proof.

"Also ask whether the merchant can produce the sample under the same conditions as the intended batch."

Qiao Yue looked up.

"Would that not be the ordinary method?"

"It should be. But I want it written."

Qiao Yue took the letter.

The foreman entered a little later with the comparison record.

He had added measurements from two separate workers. The results were consistent enough to support the earlier conclusion.

Mingzhao examined the figures.

"The width is acceptable if the loom is adjusted."

"Yes."

"The weave remains tighter than the reference."

"Yes."

"The surface?"

"Still slightly uneven near the edge."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Keep the sample. We shall not authorize production until the supplier answers."

The foreman hesitated.

"Would you consider changing the reference?"

"Why?"

"If the new material is easier to obtain, we could make it our standard for the trial."

Mingzhao considered the suggestion.

It was not unreasonable. The workshop could develop a new product rather than insist on reproducing an older one. But that would require a different proposal and a buyer willing to accept the new material.

"That is possible," she said. "But it would be a new product. We would need to assess its cost and demand separately."

The foreman nodded.

"Then I will not change the sample board."

"Correct. Keep the two possibilities separate."

He bowed and left.

Mingzhao wrote a note in the order book:

*Potential alternative material may be considered as a separate product. No change to current reference. No production authorized.*

She closed the book.

The distinction mattered. A workshop could not maintain a reliable standard if every inconvenient sample caused the standard to change.

In the western guest residence, Yuru had finished the first branch on the cream cloth.

The leaves were small and evenly spaced. She had used the darker thread for the stem and the lighter shade for the leaf edges.

Fang Ru examined the work.

"It is very neat."

Yuru looked at the reverse side.

"The stitches are better than yesterday's."

"Then you have improved."

"Or the frame is better."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Perhaps both."

Yuru placed the cloth on the table.

The embroidery was not finished. She still intended to add a few small details near the end of the branch, but the main pattern was complete.

She looked at the remaining thread.

"Would you like to try the next section?"

Fang Ru blinked.

"Me?"

"You said you enjoyed choosing colours. You may add the small leaves at the lower end."

Fang Ru looked at the cloth.

"Would that not make it uneven?"

"Possibly. But we can practise on a separate piece first."

Fang Ru smiled.

"I would like that."

Yuru brought out a spare piece of cloth and handed her the lighter thread.

"Then begin there."

Fang Ru sat opposite her.

For a while, the two women worked quietly.

Yuru did not correct every movement. She showed Fang Ru how to hold the thread, then allowed her to try. The first leaf was too long. The second was better.

When Fang Ru frowned at the result, Yuru said, "You may undo it."

"I thought I should finish it."

"You need not finish a mistake merely because you have begun it."

Fang Ru looked at her.

Then she carefully removed the stitch.

Yuru returned to her own work.

The room remained quiet except for the occasional movement of thread through cloth.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received permission to inspect the transport collection.

The central records supervisor had agreed to allow two estate representatives to examine the bound books and loose papers under supervision. The collection was not to be removed from the records room, and no original page could be detached.

Mingyuan read the conditions.

"Two representatives."

"Yes," Liang Wen said.

"Who should go?"

"Steward Zhou and me."

Mingyuan considered it.

"You should go. You have been keeping the paper inventory."

Liang Wen bowed.

"Yes, Young Master."

Mingyuan looked at Zhou.

"You should accompany him. I will remain here and review the transfer lists."

Zhou nodded.

The supervisor had also asked that the visitors provide the exact period they wished to inspect.

Mingyuan took out the old movement entry.

"Use the date of the four-cart dispatch and include the surrounding month. The register may have been kept by weeks rather than individual days."

Liang Wen wrote it down.

"Should we request the full year?"

"Not yet. Begin with the relevant period. If the book covers a wider range, inspect the surrounding entries."

Zhou looked at the request.

"That is reasonable."

Mingyuan signed it.

He then returned to the four-cart file.

The repair receipt and return entry remained separate from the dispatch investigation. He had asked the old yard clerk to look for additional records, but no reply had yet arrived.

The possible drivers remained unconfirmed.

The cargo remained unknown.

The destination remained incomplete.

He had begun to understand that a missing register could be found without the mystery being solved. The book might contain only a dispatch date, a clerk's initials, and another unfinished notation.

Still, it was the next thing they needed.

That afternoon, Shen Mingzhao received a visitor from the market.

It was not the trader who had been reluctant to send written terms. Instead, it was a small merchant who had previously purchased cloth from another workshop.

He brought a short description of the material he wanted and a rough estimate of quantity.

Mingzhao read it.

The merchant wanted a cloth suitable for everyday garments. He did not require an exact match to an older sample, but he wanted the colour to remain consistent across a single delivery.

The proposed quantity was small.

"Have you purchased from us before?" Mingzhao asked.

"No, Madam."

"Then why approach this workshop?"

"I was told that you keep written samples and do not change the terms after an order is accepted."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Who told you that?"

"A merchant who received goods from your workshop."

"Which merchant?"

The man gave a name.

Mingzhao recognized it as one of the buyers who had inspected the earlier work.

She placed the description on the table.

"Your proposal is incomplete. You have given me the intended use and approximate quantity, but not the width, weight, colour reference, price, or delivery date."

"I hoped we might discuss those."

"We may. But first I need to know what material you expect."

The merchant nodded.

"I can bring a sample tomorrow."

"Then bring it. We shall examine it before discussing price."

He looked relieved.

"Would you be willing to produce a small quantity?"

"If the sample and terms are acceptable."

The merchant smiled.

"That sounds fair."

Mingzhao did not return the smile immediately.

"Do not tell your customer that the order is accepted. We have not agreed to anything."

The merchant nodded quickly.

"Of course."

After he left, Qiao Yue asked, "Is this a better proposal?"

"It is a more useful inquiry."

"Would you prefer it to the six-bolt trial?"

"That is a separate question. We should not compare them until we know what the new material requires."

Qiao Yue recorded the visitor's name and the sample expected the following day.

Mingzhao looked at the workshop's order book.

The first proposed trial remained uncertain. A second inquiry had arrived with a clearer purpose but no sample.

Neither was an order.

She wrote both entries separately.

At the former Empress's residence, Yuru and Fang Ru had completed their first small practice piece.

Fang Ru's leaves were uneven, but the thread had not tangled. She examined the result with satisfaction.

"I thought it would be more difficult."

"It becomes easier when you stop pulling the thread so tightly."

Fang Ru looked at her work.

"I did not realize I was doing that."

"Neither did I, at first."

Yuru placed the practice cloth beside the cream piece.

"Would you like to continue?"

"Yes."

"Then you may choose the next pattern."

Fang Ru looked through the book.

She selected the small cloud design.

Yuru examined it.

"That one requires straight lines."

"I would like to try."

"Then we shall find a larger piece of cloth."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru closed the book.

The sewing had given them something pleasant to do together, but she was careful not to turn it into another duty. Fang Ru could stop whenever she wished. The cloth could remain unfinished. There was no reason to establish a schedule.

Yuru looked at the tea jar.

The Dowager's gift sat beside the sewing frame, its lid fitting neatly.

She wondered whether the Dowager would ask about the embroidery during their afternoon visit.

If she did, Yuru would show her.

If she did not, there was no reason to mention it.

Before the day ended, Mingyuan reviewed the draft request for the transport collection.

He checked the dates, the collection number, and the names of the two representatives.

Then he noticed an additional note at the bottom.

The records supervisor had asked whether the visitors wished to inspect only bound volumes or also loose papers and incomplete covers.

Mingyuan immediately marked the second option.

"Include both," he told Liang Wen.

"Even damaged covers?"

"Especially damaged covers. The title may have been written on the outside and lost when the register was rebound."

Liang Wen added the instruction.

Mingyuan looked over the request.

"Ask them to record every item examined, even if it is unrelated. I do not want the same collection searched twice because someone forgot to write down what was already opened."

Liang Wen nodded.

The request was sealed.

Steward Zhou returned to collect the copy.

"The inspection is arranged for tomorrow morning."

"Good. Take the old movement entry with you. Do not take the original register pages from our file."

"I will carry the certified copy."

Mingyuan handed it over.

Zhou tucked it into his document case.

"Perhaps the dispatch book will be there."

"Perhaps."

"And if it is?"

"Then we read it."

Zhou smiled.

Mingyuan looked at him.

"I am not going to celebrate before I know what it says."

"That is wise."

"I am beginning to think the records office has trained everyone to celebrate finding a document before checking whether it contains anything useful."

Zhou left with the request.

Mingyuan returned to his desk.

The fifth box had produced a repair receipt. The transfer list had produced a collection number. The collection index had produced a different title. The new inspection might produce the missing register.

Or it might produce another bundle of papers that had been filed under a name no one had thought to search.

He placed the file in order.

At least, unlike the old transport records, he knew exactly where he had put it.

And for the evening, that was a considerable improvement.
