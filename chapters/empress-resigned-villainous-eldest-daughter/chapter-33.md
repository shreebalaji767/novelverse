# **Chapter 33: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Rest. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Negotiate. Neither Invitation Has Considered the Amount of Work Involved.**

**25th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first official invitation received by Xu Yuru after her resignation came from the Empress Dowager.



It was written on ordinary palace stationery.



It bore no imperial seal.



It did not contain an administrative reference number.



It was, in every practical sense, a personal invitation.



Yuru read it over breakfast.



The message was brief.



*The Empress Dowager invites the former Empress to visit her residence for tea this afternoon.*



Yuru looked at the paper.



Fang Ru stood nearby.



"Would you like to accept, Madam?"



"Yes."



"Shall I prepare the carriage?"



"The Empress Dowager's residence is within the palace. I can walk."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru returned to her breakfast.



Then she looked at the invitation again.



"It is rather strange."



"What is, Madam?"



"For years, I have received invitations that included schedules, attendants, ceremonial arrangements, and lists of matters requiring discussion."



Fang Ru smiled.



"And now?"



"Now I have been invited to drink tea."



"That sounds pleasant."



"It does."



Yuru folded the invitation.



"I wonder whether we will actually drink tea."



Fang Ru did not answer.



Both women knew the Empress Dowager.



The probability of an uninterrupted afternoon was uncertain.

At the Empress Dowager's residence, the tea was already prepared.



Empress Dowager Wei sat beside the window.



She had selected a quiet room rather than the main reception hall.



There were no officials present.



No ministers.



No palace administrators.



Only two attendants remained near the entrance.



When Yuru arrived, the Empress Dowager looked up.



"You came."



"You invited me."



"Sit."



Yuru took the seat opposite her.



A servant poured tea.



For several moments, neither woman spoke.



The Empress Dowager finally said, "How is your new residence?"



"Comfortable."



"Are the rooms sufficient?"



"Yes."



"Are the servants reliable?"



"Yes."



"Is the food suitable?"



Yuru smiled.



"Mother, I have not moved to a distant province."



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"I am asking because I wish to know."



"I understand. Everything is satisfactory."



The older woman nodded.



She lifted her tea.



"Then you are truly no longer managing the palace."



"Not in an official capacity."



"And you are content?"



Yuru considered.



"I am adjusting."



"That is not an answer."



"It is the most accurate one."



The Empress Dowager set down her cup.



"You have always been difficult to persuade."



Yuru smiled.



"I learned from the best."



The older woman gave her a sharp look.



Then, unexpectedly, she laughed.



It was a brief laugh.



But it changed the atmosphere of the room.

After the tea, the Empress Dowager asked about Yuru's future arrangements.



"You have not chosen a permanent residence."



"No."



"Will you return to your natal family?"



"I have not decided."



"Your father would welcome you."



"I know."



The Empress Dowager looked at her carefully.



"Do you intend to remain within the palace?"



"For the time being, I will use the guest residence."



"And afterward?"



"I will decide."



The older woman nodded.



She did not press further.



Instead, she asked, "What do you intend to do with your time?"



Yuru glanced toward the garden.



"Read. Visit my family. Spend time with Minglan. Perhaps travel."



"Travel?"



"Within the empire, if the arrangements permit."



The Empress Dowager looked surprised.



"You have not travelled for pleasure in many years."



"That is true."



"Where would you go?"



Yuru smiled.



"I have not decided."



The Empress Dowager considered this.



"Perhaps the southern gardens."



"Perhaps."



"Or the western estates."



"Perhaps."



The older woman narrowed her eyes.



"You are using that word too often."



Yuru laughed.



"I have not yet made the decision."



The Empress Dowager shook her head.



For years, she had seen Yuru as the person who could decide everything.



Now she was watching her deliberately refuse to decide before she had enough information.



It was an unfamiliar sight.



It was also, the Empress Dowager reluctantly admitted, reasonable.

The conversation might have continued peacefully.



It did not.



A palace attendant entered with a small document.



"Your Majesty, the senior palace administrator has sent a question concerning the autumn clothing allocation."



The Empress Dowager looked at the document.



"Why is this being sent to me?"



The attendant hesitated.



"The administrator believed the matter might require Your Majesty's attention."



The Empress Dowager opened the paper.



It concerned the quantity of clothing allocated to senior attendants and servants.



She read the first paragraph.



Then the second.



Then she looked at Yuru.



Yuru was drinking tea.



The Empress Dowager put down the document.



"Do not look at me like that."



Yuru lowered her cup.



"I have said nothing."



"You have said nothing very loudly."



Yuru smiled.



The Empress Dowager turned to the attendant.



"Who is responsible for this allocation?"



"The Imperial Household Administration, Your Majesty."



"Then return it to that office."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The attendant withdrew.



The Empress Dowager looked at Yuru.



"You see? I am capable of returning a document."



"Very capable."



"Do not praise me as though I have learned to walk."



Yuru laughed.



The Empress Dowager took another sip of tea.



"Perhaps your resignation has made everyone else nervous."



"Perhaps they are accustomed to having someone else decide."



"Then they should learn."



Yuru nodded.



"That is what I have been trying to explain."



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"I understand more than you think."



Yuru met her gaze.



For a moment, neither spoke.



Then the Empress Dowager said, "Drink your tea before it becomes cold."



Yuru obeyed.



It was a pleasant afternoon after all.

At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received a different kind of invitation.



It came from Master Luo, the property assessor.



He requested a meeting to discuss the northern canal property and the outstanding boundary issue.



Mingzhao read the letter.



Qiao Yue stood beside her.



"Is this about the house?"



"Yes."



"The one with the roof repairs?"



"And the drainage."



"And the kitchen?"



"And the well."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"That house has more problems than our present one."



"It also has different advantages."



"Such as?"



"More space. Better access to the canal road. Greater potential for storage and future expansion."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Provided the boundary is correct."



"Provided the boundary is correct."



"And the roof is repaired."



"Yes."



"And the drainage."



"Yes."



"And the kitchen."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Are you planning to purchase the property or conduct a public inquiry into its defects?"



Qiao Yue smiled.



"I am trying to understand why you are interested in it."



Mingzhao folded the letter.



"Because a property should be evaluated according to its condition, location, legal status, and future use. Not merely because it is inconvenient to inspect."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Then we should go."



"Tomorrow."



"Why not today?"



"Because Master Luo has proposed tomorrow."



Qiao Yue looked at the letter.



"He has requested the meeting at the northern canal property."



"Yes."



"Then we will have to travel there."



"Yes."



Qiao Yue sighed.



She had hoped the day might involve something less complicated.

Before the meeting, Mingzhao reviewed the earlier valuation.



The property consisted of a main residence, a small courtyard, several storage rooms, a kitchen, a well, and a strip of land near the drainage channel.



The building itself had been assessed separately from the land.



The roof required repairs estimated at one hundred and twenty taels.



The drainage required approximately thirty taels.



The kitchen and well required further inspection.



The boundary dispute remained unresolved.



The old registration line did not fully correspond with the present drainage channel.



A maintenance note recorded that work had been done near the channel.



It did not establish that the boundary had legally changed.



Mingzhao had refused to treat the discrepancy as a minor inconvenience.



She had also refused to assume that the property owner was deliberately concealing anything.



The evidence was incomplete.



That was all.



She placed the valuation beside her personal financial statement.



The purchase would consume a considerable portion of her available personal funds.



It would also require repairs.



Her silk partnership funds were not available for the purchase.



The family's unresolved liabilities could not be treated as her personal money.



She made a note.



*No purchase decision until legal boundary status and total repair costs are clarified.*



Qiao Yue read the note.



"Miss, you have already decided not to buy it."



"I have decided not to buy it today."



"Is that different?"



"Very."



Qiao Yue looked unconvinced.

At the Marquisate, Mingxiu had made her first independent decision concerning household expenditure.



She discovered that two separate kitchens had been receiving firewood from the same supplier.



The quantities were recorded separately.



The prices were not.



She called the clerk.



"Are these deliveries made together?"



"Yes, Second Miss."



"Then why are there two transport charges?"



The clerk checked the record.



"One charge is for the main kitchen. The other is for the servants' kitchen."



"Do they use separate carts?"



"No."



"Then why are there separate transport charges?"



The clerk hesitated.



"I do not know."



Mingxiu looked at the ledger.



"Ask the supplier for the original delivery record."



The clerk bowed.



She continued reviewing the purchases.



When Mingyuan entered, he found her surrounded by papers.



"You have been at this since morning."



"Yes."



"Have you finished?"



"No."



Mingyuan looked at the ledgers.



"How many entries?"



"Forty-three."



"How many have you checked?"



"Twenty-seven."



He nodded.



"That is progress."



Mingxiu looked at him.



"Do you remember how Elder Sister used to review these?"



"Yes."



"How did she manage to finish so quickly?"



Mingyuan pulled out a chair.



"She knew which records were likely to contain errors."



"How?"



"Experience."



Mingxiu returned to the ledger.



"That is not a method I can use immediately."



"No."



He looked at the remaining entries.



"Would you like help?"



Mingxiu considered.



"Can you identify the suppliers and compare their delivery dates?"



"Yes."



"Then do that."



Mingyuan sat down.



For the next hour, the two siblings worked side by side.



They did not agree on every classification.



But they corrected several incomplete entries.



The family had not become harmonious.



It had become slightly more functional.



For the moment, that was sufficient.

The former Empress returned to her residence in the late afternoon.



Fang Ru was waiting with a message.



"Madam, the emperor has sent an invitation."



Yuru accepted the paper.



It was a personal invitation to dine with the imperial family that evening.



No administrative documents were attached.



No official schedule had been included beyond the meal.



Yuru read it.



"Who will attend?"



"The emperor, the Crown Prince, the Second Prince, and the Third Princess."



"And the Empress Dowager?"



"She has already accepted."



Yuru smiled.



"Then it seems I have been invited to another meal."



Fang Ru looked pleased.



"Will you attend?"



"Yes."



Yuru placed the invitation beside her book.



Then she looked at the table.



There was a small stack of papers.



She recognized the first one.



It was the palace's final confirmation of the transfer of her personal belongings.



She had not opened it.



Fang Ru followed her gaze.



"Would you like me to review the document?"



"No."



"Shall I send it to the Archives?"



"It is a personal inventory. I will keep it."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru picked up the book again.



She had begun reading the same historical account the previous evening.



The minister had finally persuaded the court to repair the bridge.



The bridge had collapsed first.



Yuru wondered whether the minister had been satisfied.

The imperial family gathered for dinner.



The emperor was in a good mood.



The Crown Prince looked composed.



The Second Prince had brought a report concerning the transport arrangements for the Eastern Palace.



The Third Princess had brought her account ledger.



Yuru noticed both.



"Are those documents?"



Minglan smiled.



"Only one."



The Second Prince placed his report on the table.



"I brought it because Father asked me to explain the transport records."



Zhao Jingheng looked at him.



"I asked you to explain them tomorrow."



The Second Prince blinked.



"Tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"Then why did you bring them today?"



"I thought it would be useful to prepare."



The emperor looked at Yuru.



Yuru smiled.



"I am not responsible for this."



The Second Prince looked between them.



"I was only trying to be prepared."



The Crown Prince intervened.



"Chengyu, put the report away. We are here for dinner."



The Second Prince obeyed.



Minglan opened her ledger.



The Crown Prince looked at her.



"Are you also bringing documents?"



"Mine are for Mother."



Yuru laughed.



"At least someone has understood the purpose of this evening."



The meal began.



For a while, the conversation remained ordinary.



The emperor asked about Minglan's studies.



The Crown Prince discussed an upcoming court examination.



The Second Prince complained about the difficulty of coordinating transport deliveries.



Yuru listened.



She did not offer to solve the problem.



When the Second Prince asked her what she would have done, she replied, "What have you already tried?"



He described his proposed arrangement.



Yuru asked two questions.



He reconsidered the plan.



Then he said, "I think I should revise the delivery schedule."



Yuru nodded.



"That sounds sensible."



The emperor watched her.



She had given advice without taking responsibility.



It was a small distinction.



But it mattered.

After dinner, Minglan walked with Yuru toward the residence.



"Mother."



"Yes?"



"Will you visit the palace often?"



"When I wish to."



"Will you come to my study?"



"If you invite me."



Minglan smiled.



"Then I will invite you."



Yuru looked at her daughter.



"You do not need to invent reasons to see me."



"I know."



"Good."



They continued walking.



After a while, Minglan said, "The palace feels different."



"How?"



"People are doing things without asking whether you have approved them."



Yuru nodded.



"That is what should happen."



"Some of them are nervous."



"They will become accustomed to it."



Minglan looked thoughtful.



"Will you miss it?"



Yuru did not answer immediately.



She looked at the illuminated palace buildings.



"I will miss some people. I will miss some routines. I will not miss every responsibility."



Minglan nodded.



That answer seemed to satisfy her.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao was reviewing the day's accounts.



The landlord's rent reminder remained on the table.



She had not yet decided whether to extend the agreement.



The property assessment meeting was scheduled for the following day.



The silk partnership had received payment for the accepted delivery.



The remaining stock still required inspection.



The family settlement continued.



She entered the day's household expenses.



Then she examined the receipts.



One receipt was missing.



She looked at the ledger.



The amount was small.



It concerned a purchase of vegetables.



She called the senior maid.



"Was this purchase made?"



"Yes, Miss."



"Where is the receipt?"



The maid searched the basket of documents.



It was not there.



Mingzhao looked at the entry.



"Who made the purchase?"



"The second attendant."



"Ask her to provide the details tomorrow."



Qiao Yue looked at the ledger.



"Miss, it is only a small amount."



"Then it should be easy to verify."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"That is annoyingly reasonable."



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



She looked at the room.



The furniture was still sparse.



The servants were still learning their duties.



The household was still adjusting.



But the day's work had been completed.



For the first time since leaving the Marquisate, Mingzhao felt that the ordinary household routine was beginning to take shape.



It was not comfortable yet.



It was not settled.



But it was hers.

At the palace, the emperor returned to his study after dinner.



Gao Shun brought the evening reports.



The emperor examined them.



There was a report about the corrected tea order.



Another about the former Empress's residence.



A third concerned the transfer of personal servants.



Zhao Jingheng put the papers aside.



"These do not require my attention."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The emperor looked at him.



"How many reports have been sent to me today that do not require my attention?"



Gao Shun considered.



"Four, Your Majesty."



The emperor closed his eyes.



"At least the number is decreasing."



Gao Shun did not know whether to agree.



The emperor picked up the final report.



It concerned the next month's inner household budget.



He read it carefully.



The figures were clear.



The responsible office had signed the document.



The supporting records were attached.



There was no request for the former Empress's approval.



Zhao Jingheng nodded.



"Send it to the Revenue Office for the scheduled review."



Gao Shun accepted the document.



The emperor leaned back.



The palace was learning to function without Yuru.



Slowly.



With occasional mistakes.



And with a remarkable number of reports about matters that should have been resolved by the people responsible for them.

Before sleeping, Yuru received one final message from the Empress Dowager.



It was not an invitation.



It was a short note.



*The tea was good. We should do it again.*



Yuru smiled.



She placed the note beside her book.



At the same time, Mingzhao received a reply from the landlord.



The landlord had accepted her decision to observe the full rental term before discussing an extension.



He had also reminded her that the rent would be due on the agreed date.



Mingzhao placed the letter beside her ledger.



She looked at it for a moment.



Then she wrote the due date into the household calendar.



The former Empress had received an invitation to drink tea.



The Marquis's eldest daughter had received a reminder to pay rent.



Both documents had been placed in their proper locations.



Both women had accepted that their lives would now be governed by different kinds of obligations.



And neither had any intention of returning to the old arrangement merely because other people found it convenient.
