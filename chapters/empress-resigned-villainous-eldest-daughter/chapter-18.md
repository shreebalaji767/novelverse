# **Chapter 18: The Emperor Has Discovered That a Successful Transition Creates More Questions. The Marquis Has Discovered That His Son Can Read an Account Book.**

**10th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Emperor had received a report stating that the palace was functioning without the Empress's intervention.



He had read it three times.



He was not entirely pleased.



This was not because the report contained bad news.



It contained good news.



The medicine-distribution procedure had been tested successfully. Ordinary household purchases were being handled by the responsible administrators. Residence repairs had been transferred. The Crown Prince had begun reviewing the Eastern Palace accounts without sending every question to Chengming Palace.



The problem was that the report made the next question unavoidable.



If the palace could function without the Empress personally managing every ordinary matter, what exactly was the Emperor waiting for?



Zhao Jingheng placed the report on his desk.



Gao Shun stood nearby.



"Your Majesty, Minister Han has arrived."



"Let him enter."



Minister Han came in carrying another document.



The Emperor looked at it.



"Is this the Ministry of Personnel's conclusion?"



"It is a preliminary assessment, Your Majesty."



"Why is it preliminary?"



"Because the Empress's formal position involves matters that cannot be transferred through administrative procedure alone."



"I know that."



Minister Han bowed.



"The Empress's position is established by imperial appointment and ceremonial law. Her administrative duties are only one part of the matter. Her resignation would affect precedence, ceremonies, household authority, and the status of several senior palace offices."



"Then identify those consequences."



"We have."



"Have you identified which require an immediate decision?"



Minister Han hesitated.



"Not yet."



The Emperor leaned back.



"Then you have identified the consequences but not the decisions."



Minister Han bowed again.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Zhao Jingheng looked at the report.



"Prepare a separate list. Matters requiring an imperial decision, matters requiring a ceremonial adjustment, and matters that can proceed under existing regulations."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And do not ask the Empress to prepare it."



Minister Han lowered his head.



"Understood."



After the minister left, the Emperor remained silent.



Gao Shun carefully poured tea.



"Your Majesty, the transition is progressing."



"So I have been informed."



"Perhaps the Ministry of Personnel requires more time."



"Everyone requires more time."



The Emperor picked up his brush.



"Unfortunately, time has never been an acceptable substitute for a decision."

At Chengming Palace, Yuru was examining the next group of responsibilities.



The list included ceremonial gift records, ordinary household education expenses, and the maintenance of several inner-palace residences.



The first two were relatively straightforward.



The third was less so.



Some repairs were routine.



Others involved structural damage that could affect the safety of the occupants.



The current procedure required reports to pass through the Empress's office whenever the cost exceeded a certain amount.



Yuru had already identified the problem.



A large repair was not necessarily an exceptional repair.



A damaged roof could require a substantial sum without becoming a matter of ceremonial or political significance.



She discussed the issue with Fang Ru.



"The amount alone should not determine whether the Empress receives a report."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Then the nature of the work should be considered."



"Yes. Routine maintenance should remain with the administration. Structural damage should be examined by the Works Office or an appropriate technical officer. Emergency repairs should proceed immediately, with a record afterward."



"And if the cost exceeds the ordinary allowance?"



"The responsible office should submit the additional expenditure to the authorized financial office."



Fang Ru wrote this down.



Yuru watched her.



"Do not make every repair a special case."



"I will distinguish the categories."



"Good."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"Your Majesty, the residence administrators may object to taking responsibility for expensive repairs."



"Then they should explain why the responsibility belongs elsewhere."



"And if they refuse?"



"Then their superior should decide whether they are suitable for the position."



Fang Ru looked at the paper.



This was the part of administration that Yuru had previously avoided.



She had often accepted a report, corrected it, and returned it with instructions.



The immediate problem was solved.



The office that had submitted it learned very little.



Now she was making the office explain itself.



It was slower.



It was also necessary.

The Crown Prince visited Chengming Palace before noon.



He carried a revised account procedure.



Yuru read it.



The Eastern Palace had established separate records for medicine received, medicine prescribed, and medicine distributed.



The records remained connected through the recipient and date.



The arrangement was simple.



It was also useful.



"Your Highness has made the records clearer."



Cheng'an bowed.



"The physician suggested keeping the prescription record separate from the distribution record."



"That was sensible."



"The steward objected because it required another comparison."



Yuru looked up.



"And what did you decide?"



"I asked whether the comparison was necessary to confirm that the prescribed medicine had reached the intended recipient."



"What did he say?"



"He agreed."



Yuru smiled.



"Then the additional work has a purpose."



Cheng'an nodded.



He placed another paper before her.



"This concerns the monthly expenditure review."



Yuru examined it.



The Eastern Palace had identified three purchases that were recorded under the same general category despite serving different purposes.



One was a repair expense.



One was a household purchase.



One was a personal purchase made by the Crown Prince.



Cheng'an had separated them.



Yuru looked at the final entry.



"You have charged this to your personal account?"



"Yes."



"Why?"



"It was not necessary for the household."



"What was it?"



"A decorative writing stand."



Yuru looked at him.



"Do you need it?"



Cheng'an paused.



"I wanted it."



"That is not the same answer."



"No, Mother."



She returned the paper.



"Then the classification is correct."



The Crown Prince accepted it.



He had begun to discover that the difference between wanting something and needing something could be expensive.



Yuru suspected that the lesson would remain useful long after the transition was complete.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan had discovered something considerably less complicated.



He could read an account book.



He was not particularly pleased by this discovery.



He had spent much of his adult life regarding household accounts as documents that existed to confirm that the household had sufficient money.



He had not thought much about how the money arrived, where it went, or why the same expense could appear under different categories.



Now he was sitting with the central clerk, examining the previous month's expenditure.



The clerk pointed to an entry.



"This payment was made to the southern residence."



Mingyuan examined the amount.



"What was purchased?"



"Household supplies."



"Which supplies?"



The clerk searched the attached papers.



"Oil, cloth, and several kitchen utensils."



"Why are they recorded together?"



"They were purchased from the same merchant."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"That explains the payment. It does not explain the classification."



The clerk hesitated.



Mingyuan turned the page.



"Separate them by purpose."



"Yes, Young Master."



The clerk began making a note.



Mingyuan looked at the next entry.



"Who authorized this?"



"Steward Zhou."



"Was it within the allowance?"



"Yes."



"Then the record is sufficient."



He continued reading.



There were several entries that needed clarification.



He marked them.



He did not immediately assume that they represented wrongdoing.



That was new.



Previously, he might have dismissed the details as matters for Mingzhao.



Now he had to determine whether the record was complete.



The task was not impossible.



It was simply tedious.



After an hour, he leaned back.



"How did my sister do this every day?"



The clerk lowered his head.



"I cannot say, Young Master."



Mingyuan looked at the stack of papers.



"She must have had more patience than I do."



The clerk did not answer.



He had worked in the Marquisate long enough to know that the Young Master's patience was not a subject on which he should offer an opinion.

In another room, Mingxiu was reviewing the agricultural summary.



The earlier grain entry had been corrected.



The agricultural steward had confirmed that the grain had been moved from one store to another. The central clerk had recorded it as a separate delivery without reducing the original store's balance.



The correction was now complete.



Mingxiu checked the two entries.



The figures matched.



She signed the summary and sent it to Steward Zhou.



Then she noticed another problem.



The agricultural steward had included seed grain in the quantity available for sale.



Mingxiu frowned.



She summoned him.



"Why is this included?"



The steward examined the page.



"It is grain held in the store."



"It is also reserved for planting."



"Yes."



"Then it is not available for sale."



The steward considered the matter.



"That is correct."



Mingxiu pointed to the column.



"Separate stored grain from grain available for sale."



The steward nodded.



He made the correction.



Mingxiu watched him.



She had not solved a complicated financial problem.



She had prevented a future one.



That was enough.



When the steward left, she looked at the revised summary.



For the first time, she understood why Mingzhao had been so particular about categories.



The numbers were not difficult.



The consequences of placing them in the wrong place could be.

At midday, Mingzhao received Sun Qiming's latest report.



The examination of the northern warehouse had produced another document.



The maintenance office had located a payment entry for the roof repairs.



It recorded the hiring of workers, timber purchases, tiles, and transport.



There was also a separate payment to a cart contractor.



The payment description mentioned the movement of grain.



Sun Qiming had requested the contractor's original delivery records.



The entry did not identify the number of sacks transported.



Mingzhao read the report.



"Does this establish where the two unconfirmed sacks went?"



"No," Sun Qiming replied. "It establishes that grain was transported during the repair period. The quantity remains unconfirmed."



"Could the contractor have carried all twenty sacks?"



"Possibly. But the surviving payment record does not state that."



"Could the two sacks have been returned to the northern warehouse?"



"Possibly."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"You seem determined to preserve every possibility."



"I am determined not to turn a possibility into a conclusion."



She nodded.



"Continue."



Sun Qiming turned to the next page.



"The original forty-seven-sack discrepancy remains divided as follows: eighteen sacks recorded as received in southern storage, two sacks covered by a movement instruction but without confirmed receipt, and twenty-seven sacks still unexplained."



Mingzhao looked at the figures.



"Good."



The accountant continued.



"The examination of the three hundred taels is also incomplete. Seventy-two taels are supported by the estate payment ledger and Carpenter Chen's statement, but the original work order and receipt remain missing. The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels are not yet accounted for."



"Has the carpenter been asked for his old records?"



"Yes. He is searching."



Mingzhao nodded.



Sun Qiming placed the report aside.



"There is one other matter."



"What?"



"The estate's old maintenance register contains several payments that were made through the same contractor. Some entries do not identify the exact residence."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"Are you suggesting that the contractor was involved in the three hundred taels?"



"No. I am suggesting that the register may help identify the original work order."



"Then examine it."



Sun Qiming bowed.



Mingzhao was pleased with the answer.



The investigation was expanding, but it was not expanding carelessly.



That distinction mattered.

After the accountant left, Qiao Yue brought in a letter from Lin Shuyun.



The buyer, Zhou Wenqi, had sent the final draft of the silk contract.



The proposed delivery date was acceptable.



The inspection arrangements were included.



The payment terms matched the agreement.



However, the buyer had requested that the seller guarantee the entire shipment against all defects for thirty days after delivery.



Mingzhao read the clause.



Then she read it again.



"Thirty days?"



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Is that unreasonable?"



"It depends on the nature of the defect."



Mingzhao placed the contract on the desk.



"An obvious defect should be identified during inspection. A concealed defect may reasonably require a short claim period. But thirty days for every possible complaint is too broad."



She wrote a note.



She instructed Lin Shuyun to distinguish between visible defects, concealed defects, and damage caused during transportation.



She also asked for a clear procedure for submitting a claim.



Qiao Yue watched her write.



"Will the buyer accept?"



"That is for the buyer to decide."



"And if he refuses?"



"Then we do not sign."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao folded the reply.



The silk partnership was profitable because Lin Shuyun and she had learned to calculate risk.



They would not abandon that discipline simply because a large order had appeared.



The contract remained unsigned.



No deposit had been received.



The stock remained available.

That afternoon, Shen Guowei summoned Mingzhao.



The Marquis was seated in his study.



Madam Liang was also present.



Mr. Chen, the former family legal adviser, had been invited to discuss the property division.



Mingzhao entered and greeted them.



Her father indicated a chair.



"Sit."



She sat.



Mr. Chen opened his papers.



"The independent examination is continuing, but we can begin preparing the property schedule."



Mingzhao nodded.



"That is what I requested."



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"Your brother has begun reviewing the household accounts."



"I have received his reports."



"He is making progress."



"Yes."



Her father seemed to expect more.



Mingzhao did not praise him merely to make the conversation comfortable.



Shen Guowei continued.



"The family has also begun transferring ordinary responsibilities."



"I know."



"Then perhaps we can discuss your departure."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"Have you decided on a date?"



"Not yet."



"Then what is there to discuss?"



Madam Liang frowned.



"Must every conversation become a negotiation?"



Mingzhao turned toward her.



"No. But this conversation concerns a date that has not been chosen."



Mr. Chen intervened.



"The transition period can be defined separately from the final property settlement."



"That is what I have requested from the beginning," Mingzhao replied.



Shen Guowei looked at his daughter.



"You are determined to leave before the division is complete."



"I am determined not to make my departure dependent on every unresolved account."



"And what if the family requires your assistance?"



"Then the family can submit a specific request."



Madam Liang's expression tightened.



"Your own family must now submit requests to you?"



Mingzhao remained calm.



"If the request concerns my property, my business, or a matter in which I have an obligation, it should be stated clearly. If it concerns ordinary household management, the responsible people should handle it."



Her father looked away.



Mr. Chen examined his papers.



"Then we should define the remaining transition duties and the period during which questions may be directed to Miss Mingzhao."



"Agreed," Mingzhao said.



Shen Guowei sighed.



"Very well."



It was not a final settlement.



But the discussion had moved forward.

The property schedule was then examined.



Mingzhao's personal property was listed separately.



Her dowry assets were to be verified through the relevant records.



Her inherited property would be examined according to the documents establishing ownership.



Her commercial interests with Lin Shuyun would remain separate from the Marquisate's estate.



Her documented advances to the family would be treated as claims requiring proof.



The family estate would be valued independently.



Liabilities would be listed rather than deducted arbitrarily.



Mr. Chen reviewed the categories.



"Some items may be disputed."



"Then mark them as disputed," Mingzhao said.



"Some records are incomplete."



"Then mark them as incomplete."



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"Will you accept no property until every matter is resolved?"



"No."



"Then what will you accept?"



"What is established by evidence and determined through the proper division."



Her father rubbed his forehead.



"You make this sound very simple."



"It is not simple."



Mingzhao looked at the schedule.



"It is merely necessary."



Madam Liang remained silent.



The family meeting ended without an agreement on the final division.



But the categories had been accepted as the basis for further examination.



That was more useful than another argument about gratitude.

In the palace, the Empress Dowager received a second report.



The medicine procedure had been tested successfully.



The residence maintenance responsibilities were being reorganized.



The Imperial Household Administration had been instructed to distinguish routine repairs from structural damage.



The Crown Prince's household review had progressed.



The Dowager read the report and asked her attendant to bring the earlier one.



She compared them.



The number of duties still under direct Empress oversight had decreased.



Not all responsibilities could be transferred.



Not all ceremonial duties could be reassigned.



But the ordinary administrative work was no longer being treated as an extension of Yuru's personal authority.



The Dowager placed the reports together.



"Send for the Empress."

Yuru arrived in the Dowager's residence before evening.



She bowed.



"Your Majesty."



The Dowager gestured for her to sit.



"I have read the latest reports."



Yuru nodded.



"The transition is proceeding."



"So I see."



The Dowager examined her.



"Do you intend to complete the transition even if the Emperor delays his decision?"



"Yes."



"Why?"



"Because the duties must be transferred whether I remain Empress or not."



The Dowager was silent.



Yuru continued.



"If I remain, the palace should still function through its offices. If I leave, it must already know how to function."



The Dowager looked at the reports.



"You have managed the inner palace for many years."



"Yes."



"Do you believe the administration can manage it without you?"



"Some offices can. Others need further work."



"And if they fail?"



"They will need to correct their procedures."



The Dowager's expression became stern.



"You speak as though failure is acceptable."



"An uncorrected failure is not acceptable. A mistake that is identified and corrected is different."



The Dowager considered this.



She had spent much of her life in a palace where mistakes were often concealed because acknowledging them was considered more dangerous than allowing them to continue.



Yuru's answer was not entirely comfortable.



It was also difficult to dispute.



"Do you regret submitting the petition?"



"No."



"Even now?"



"No."



The Dowager looked at her for a long moment.



Then she nodded.



"I will not ask you to withdraw it."



Yuru lowered her eyes.



It was not approval.



But it was no longer opposition.

That evening, the Emperor received a message from the Dowager.



It was brief.



*The transition should proceed. The final decision concerning the Empress's position remains with Your Majesty.*



Zhao Jingheng read it.



Then he put it aside.



He had expected the Dowager to resist.



Instead, she had accepted the distinction between the position and the work.



The Emperor looked at the reports on his desk.



He now had three questions to resolve.



What duties could be transferred permanently?



Which ceremonial functions required the Empress's presence?



And whether he would accept her resignation.



The first question was being answered by the administration.



The second was being examined by the Department of Rites.



The third could not be delegated.



He looked at Gao Shun.



"Tomorrow, I will speak with the Empress."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor paused.



"Not about the reports."



Gao Shun waited.



"About what she intends to do after leaving."



Gao Shun lowered his head.



He did not ask why.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao received a final report from Steward Zhou before retiring.



The ordinary household purchases had been completed.



The agricultural summary had been corrected.



Mingyuan had reviewed the expenditure ledger.



Mingxiu had separated seed grain from grain available for sale.



The household had also discovered that the southern residence's water jars needed replacement.



Steward Zhou had authorized the purchase under the ordinary allowance.



Mingzhao read the report.



Then she put it down.



Qiao Yue brought her tea.



"Another day without the household collapsing."



"That is an unreasonably low standard."



"It was the standard everyone seemed to expect."



Mingzhao looked at her.



Qiao Yue immediately lowered her head.



"Forgive me, Miss."



Mingzhao took the tea.



"Tomorrow, we will inspect the property schedule again."



"Yes, Miss."



"And request the old maintenance records."



"Yes."



"And remind Lin Shuyun not to sign the silk contract until the defect clause is revised."



"Yes."



Qiao Yue hesitated.



"Miss, you still have many things to do."



Mingzhao looked at the papers.



"Of course."



"Then perhaps you should rest."



Mingzhao considered the suggestion.



She closed the property schedule.



For once, she did not open another document.



"Bring me the tea."



Qiao Yue smiled.



The household had not collapsed.



The palace had not collapsed.



The world had not ended because two women had stopped doing every task that crossed their desks.



The difficulty was that both institutions were now discovering how much work they had quietly placed upon them.



And neither institution had yet learned how to apologize without attaching another document.



**End of Chapter 18.**
