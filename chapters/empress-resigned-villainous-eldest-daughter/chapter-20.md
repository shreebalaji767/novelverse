# **Chapter 20: The Emperor Has Finally Made a Decision. The Empress Has Asked Him to Put It in Writing. The Marquis Has Discovered That a Departure Date Is Not a Property Settlement.**

**12th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Emperor had spent the previous evening preparing what he intended to say.

He had not written it down.

This was a mistake.

By the time he arrived at Chengming Palace, he had discovered that speaking without a document was considerably more difficult than issuing an order with one.

Yuru received him in her private sitting room.

Fang Ru served tea and withdrew.

The Emperor sat opposite her.

For several moments, he examined the cup.

Yuru waited.

Finally, Zhao Jingheng said, "I have reviewed your petition."

"I know."

"The Ministry of Personnel has examined the legal arrangements."

"I have received the report."

"The Department of Rites has identified the ceremonial changes required."

"Yes."

"The administrative transition is progressing."

"Yes."

The Emperor looked at her.

"You seem to know everything I intend to say."

"I have received the documents."

He almost smiled.

Then he became serious.

"I will accept your resignation."

Yuru did not move.

The words were simple.

She had expected them.

She had prepared herself for them.

Yet hearing them spoken aloud made the room feel strangely unfamiliar.

After a moment, she lowered her eyes.

"Thank you, Your Majesty."

The Emperor continued.

"The formal order will be issued after the ceremonial arrangements are completed. Your administrative duties will remain transferred to the offices already assigned to them. The remaining responsibilities will be settled according to the timetable."

Yuru nodded.

"That is appropriate."

"You will retain your established personal property and household possessions. The arrangements concerning your residence and future status will be discussed separately."

"Yes."

The Emperor looked at her.

"You will not be required to continue performing the administrative duties you have relinquished."

Yuru raised her eyes.

"Please put that in writing."

Zhao Jingheng stared at her.

For a moment, he thought he had misunderstood.

Then he realized she was serious.

"Yuru."

"Your Majesty?"

"I have just accepted your resignation."

"Yes."

"And you are asking for a written order?"

"Yes."

The Emperor leaned back.

"Do you believe I will change my mind?"

"No."

"Then why?"

Yuru folded her hands.

"Because verbal arrangements are difficult to enforce when people become accustomed to the old arrangement."

The Emperor looked at her.

Then, slowly, he laughed.

It was not a particularly dignified sound.

He had spent years asking her to ensure that every important matter was documented.

Now she was asking him to do the same.

"Very well," he said. "It will be written."

Yuru inclined her head.

"Thank you."

The Emperor picked up his tea.

"Is there anything else?"

"Yes."

He looked at her cautiously.

"The order should specify that the transfer of administrative duties is permanent unless changed by a separate imperial decision. It should also identify the offices responsible for the duties already transferred."

Zhao Jingheng put down his cup.

"Anything more?"

"That the remaining duties should not automatically return to my office simply because an office encounters difficulty."

The Emperor stared at her.

"That is quite specific."

"It has happened before."

He sighed.

"Very well."

Yuru waited.

The Emperor looked at her.

"Are you finished?"

"For the moment."

He shook his head.

"Your resignation has created more paperwork than the establishment of several offices."

Yuru's expression remained composed.

"Then perhaps the offices should learn to manage their paperwork."

The Emperor laughed again.

This time, she smiled.

The formal order was drafted before midday.

It stated that the Empress's petition had been accepted in principle, subject to completion of the necessary ceremonial arrangements and the formal issuance of the imperial decree.

It also confirmed that the administrative duties already transferred would remain with their assigned offices.

The remaining duties would be reviewed according to the established schedule.

The Empress would not be required to resume ordinary administrative responsibilities merely because a receiving office encountered difficulty.

The document was reviewed by the Ministry of Personnel and the Department of Rites.

It was then submitted to the Emperor.

Zhao Jingheng read it.

He made one correction.

The phrase *accepted in principle* was replaced with a clear statement that the petition was accepted, with the formal date of departure to be determined after the ceremonial arrangements were completed.

He signed it.

Gao Shun received the order.

For several moments, he looked at the document.

The Empress had served for many years.

The palace had changed during her tenure.

The imperial household had become accustomed to her judgment, her discipline, and her ability to solve problems before they reached the Emperor.

Gao Shun had expected the transition to be difficult.

He had not expected the Empress to be so determined to make it possible.

"Have the relevant offices received copies?" the Emperor asked.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Then the order is complete."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The Emperor looked toward Chengming Palace.

For the first time, the Empress's resignation was no longer merely a petition awaiting consideration.

It was an accepted decision.

The palace now had to prepare for the day she would leave.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had also received a decision.

Unfortunately, it was not the one she wanted.

Her father had sent a message requesting that she attend a family meeting.

She arrived to find Shen Guowei, Madam Liang, Old Madam Shen, Shen Mingyuan, Shen Mingxiu, and Mr. Chen already seated.

A property schedule lay on the table.

Mingzhao examined it.

Several items had been verified.

Others remained under review.

Her documented commercial interests had been listed separately.

Her dowry and inherited property were still being examined.

The family estate had not yet been valued.

The northern warehouse discrepancy remained unresolved.

The three hundred taels had not been fully explained.

It was not a settlement.

It was a working schedule.

Shen Guowei began.

"The family has discussed your request."

Mingzhao waited.

"We agree that the ordinary household responsibilities should remain with the assigned stewards and your brother."

"Good."

"We also agree that your departure should not be delayed solely because the property division remains incomplete."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Good."

Her father continued.

"We propose that you remain at the Marquisate for another month while the transition is completed."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Why a month?"

"To ensure that your brother is ready."

"His readiness cannot be determined by a date."

Shen Mingyuan looked uncomfortable.

His father continued.

"Then perhaps twenty days."

Mingzhao looked at the schedule.

"What duties remain under my responsibility?"

Mr. Chen answered.

"The independent property examination, clarification of your commercial records, and a limited period for answering questions about the previous household accounts."

"Those are not ordinary household responsibilities."

"No."

"Then they do not require me to remain here."

Madam Liang frowned.

"Must you leave as quickly as possible?"

"I have not demanded an immediate departure."

"Then why reject a month?"

"Because no one has explained what the month is meant to accomplish."

Old Madam Shen tapped her cane lightly.

"Your father wants the household to be stable."

"Then the household should become stable."

The old madam looked at her.

"You have managed it for six years. Surely you can remain a little longer."

Mingzhao met her gaze.

"I have managed it for six years. That is precisely why the household must learn to continue without me."

There was silence.

Mingyuan looked at the ledger before him.

Then he spoke.

"I do not need another month."

Everyone turned toward him.

He looked uncomfortable.

"I can manage the ordinary expenditure review. Steward Zhou is handling the household operations. Mingxiu is reviewing the secondary residence and agricultural summaries."

He glanced at Mingzhao.

"I will make mistakes. But I can correct them."

Mingzhao did not respond immediately.

Her brother continued.

"Keeping Second Sister here will not make me learn faster."

Shen Guowei looked at his son.

Mingyuan lowered his head.

"I would prefer that she finish the transition questions and then leave."

The statement was not eloquent.

It was not particularly brave.

But it was honest.

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Then I will complete the remaining training and hand over the records."

Her father sighed.

"Very well. We will set a date after reviewing the transition schedule."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That is acceptable."

It was not the final answer.

But it was the first time her brother had openly acknowledged that her continued presence was not a substitute for his own responsibility.

The family meeting continued with the property schedule.

Mr. Chen presented the verified and unverified categories.

The independent accountant had confirmed several commercial entries.

Mingzhao's partnership with Lin Shuyun remained separate from the Marquisate's estate.

Her personal advances to the family required further examination.

The family estate's liabilities had not yet been fully established.

The old warehouse records were still being reviewed.

Mingzhao pointed to one entry.

"This amount should not be included as a personal liability."

Mr. Chen examined it.

"It was charged to your household account."

"That does not establish that I personally received the money."

Shen Guowei looked at the paper.

"Then where should it be placed?"

"Under disputed expenditure pending verification."

Mr. Chen made the correction.

Madam Liang looked displeased.

"At this rate, every entry will be disputed."

"Only the entries without sufficient evidence."

"That is a considerable number."

"Then the records are incomplete."

The old madam sighed.

The family had spent years treating the household accounts as a collection of totals.

Now the totals were being separated into transactions, authorizations, payments, receipts, and liabilities.

It was unpleasant.

It was also necessary.

Mingzhao looked at the schedule.

"Do not deduct any unverified amount from my share."

Shen Guowei nodded reluctantly.

"That will be recorded."

"Thank you."

The meeting ended with the schedule still incomplete.

Mingzhao left without a settlement.

But she had obtained an agreement that her departure would be considered separately from the final division of property.

That was progress.

At the palace, Yuru received the written order.

She read it carefully.

Fang Ru stood beside her.

The order was clear.

The resignation had been accepted.

The formal date would follow after the ceremonial arrangements.

The administrative responsibilities already transferred would remain with their assigned offices.

The remaining duties would continue through the established transition.

Yuru folded the document.

Fang Ru looked at her.

"Your Majesty."

Yuru glanced at her.

"Yes?"

"Should I prepare the next administrative schedule?"

Yuru looked at the order.

Then she shook her head.

"Prepare it for the Imperial Household Administration."

Fang Ru paused.

"Not for Your Majesty?"

"No."

"Should it be sent here for information?"

"If the regulations require it."

Fang Ru nodded.

The distinction was small.

But it was the distinction Yuru had been trying to establish for years.

The palace would no longer prepare its work for her merely because she had always been the person who reviewed it.

The Third Princess, Zhao Minglan, arrived later that afternoon.

She had heard that the Emperor had accepted the Empress's resignation.

She entered Chengming Palace with an expression that was difficult to interpret.

Yuru put down her book.

"Why are you looking at me like that?"

Minglan sat opposite her.

"Mother, are you leaving?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"After the ceremonial arrangements are complete."

Minglan looked down.

"Will you leave the palace entirely?"

"I will leave my position and the responsibilities attached to it."

"That is not what I asked."

Yuru studied her daughter.

"I have not decided where I will live."

Minglan was quiet.

Then she asked, "Will you still be my mother?"

Yuru's expression softened.

"Of course."

"Will I still be able to visit you?"

"Yes."

"Will you answer my questions?"

"Some of them."

Minglan looked offended.

"Some?"

"If you ask me to explain an account book, I will help. If you ask me to approve the palace's annual expenditure, I will refuse."

Minglan considered this.

"That seems fair."

Yuru smiled.

"Does it?"

"Yes. I would not want to manage the entire palace either."

Yuru looked at her daughter.

"Then you have learned something important."

Minglan nodded.

She had spent several weeks watching the adults around her discuss the Empress's responsibilities as though the position itself were a collection of tasks.

Now she understood that her mother was not abandoning her.

She was choosing a different life.

Minglan stood.

"Mother, may I visit you when you have your own residence?"

"Of course."

"Will you have a garden?"

"I hope so."

"Will there be a pond?"

"Perhaps."

"Will you have servants?"

"Probably."

Minglan considered this.

"Then you will still need someone to manage the household."

Yuru looked at her.

"Do not start."

Minglan laughed.

It was the first time she had laughed about the resignation.

The next morning, the Imperial Household Administration received the formal order.

The senior administrators gathered to discuss the remaining transition.

Madam Zhao presented the updated schedule.

The administrative responsibilities had been divided into three groups.

The first group had been transferred and tested.

The second group was being transferred.

The third group required ceremonial or imperial decisions.

The administration would maintain the records.

The Department of Rites would handle ceremonial adjustments.

The Ministry of Personnel would manage the formal arrangements.

No single office would be responsible for everything.

Madam Zhao finished her presentation.

Then she waited.

A junior administrator raised his hand.

"Who will review the transition after the Empress leaves?"

Madam Zhao answered.

"The Imperial Household Administration will prepare a monthly report for the designated authority."

"And who will approve it?"

"The authority identified in the regulations."

The junior administrator nodded.

Then he asked, "Should the report also be sent to the Empress?"

Madam Zhao looked at him.

"No."

The junior administrator lowered his head.

The meeting continued.

For once, the question had been answered without becoming a new administrative problem.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao began preparing her own departure schedule.

It was not yet a date.

She had listed the matters that required her direct participation.

The commercial accounts needed to be separated from the estate records.

The independent accountant needed to complete the preliminary asset examination.

Her personal property needed to be inventoried.

The household records she had managed needed to be handed over.

The family had agreed that ordinary responsibilities would remain with Steward Zhou, Mingyuan, and Mingxiu.

The unresolved property division would continue separately.

Qiao Yue examined the list.

"Miss, there are still many things."

"Yes."

"Will you be able to finish them soon?"

"Some."

"And the others?"

"They will continue after I leave."

Qiao Yue looked at the schedule.

"Your father may not like that."

"That is not a reason to remain indefinitely."

Mingzhao closed the document.

She had not yet found a residence.

The northern canal house still required substantial repairs.

The eastern property still had an unresolved boundary matter.

She would not purchase either simply to have an address.

She would continue searching.

Her silk partnership was also not yet settled.

The buyer's contract had been approved in principle, but the deposit had not arrived.

The estate's warehouse records remained incomplete.

The three hundred taels had not been fully explained.

There was still much to do.

But she had stopped treating every unresolved matter as a reason to stay.

That evening, the Emperor and Empress met for dinner.

The meal was quiet.

There were no reports on the table.

No ministers had been summoned.

No administrator had been asked to explain an expenditure.

After the meal, Zhao Jingheng walked with Yuru through the garden.

"The order has been issued," he said.

"Yes."

"The remaining arrangements will take some time."

"I understand."

"Have you considered what you would like to do first?"

Yuru thought for a moment.

"I would like to sleep late."

The Emperor looked at her.

"That is your first plan?"

"Yes."

"And after that?"

"I have not decided."

He nodded.

"Perhaps you could visit the Xu family."

"Perhaps."

"Or travel."

"Perhaps."

The Emperor looked at her.

"You seem to have many plans involving the word perhaps."

Yuru smiled.

"It is a useful word when one has spent years being expected to know everything in advance."

He accepted that.

They continued walking.

The palace was quiet.

The administrative transition was proceeding.

The Empress's resignation had been accepted.

The Marquis's eldest daughter was preparing to leave.

Neither woman had yet reached the life she wanted.

But for the first time, the decision to move toward it had been formally recognized.

Unfortunately, the palace still had a ceremonial schedule to prepare, and the Marquisate still had a property division to complete.

Freedom, it appeared, was not issued without supporting documents.

**End of Chapter 20.**
