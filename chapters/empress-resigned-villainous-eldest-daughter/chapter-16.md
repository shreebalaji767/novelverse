# **Chapter 16: The Empress Has Been Given Ten Days to Become Unnecessary. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Given Ten Days to Become Unavailable.**

**8th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The palace had received a timetable.

This was considered progress.

The timetable contained ten days.

This was considered alarming.

The Imperial Household Administration had been informed that the first group of responsibilities currently handled through the Empress's office must be transferred within that period.

The administration had responded by preparing a schedule.

The schedule had been reviewed by the Department of Rites.

The Department of Rites had returned it with several corrections.

The corrections had been incorporated.

The revised schedule had then been submitted to the Ministry of Personnel.

The Ministry of Personnel had requested clarification concerning the meaning of the word *transfer*.

By the time the document reached the Emperor, it had acquired four seals, three supplementary notes, and a small tear near the lower corner.

Zhao Jingheng examined it.

"Does the transfer mean that the duties will be performed by another office?"

Minister Han answered, "Yes, Your Majesty."

"Does it mean that the office will have the authority to perform them?"

"Yes."

"Does it mean that the office will be responsible when it performs them incorrectly?"

Minister Han hesitated.

The Emperor put down the document.

"Then the transfer is not complete."

The minister bowed.

"Your Majesty is correct."

Zhao Jingheng looked at the timetable.

"Ten days."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Do not spend eight days explaining what the word means."

Minister Han bowed again.

The Emperor returned to his work.

He had begun to suspect that the palace's greatest obstacle was not a lack of intelligence.

It was the belief that a sufficiently detailed document could replace the act of making a decision.

At Chengming Palace, Yuru received the first report of the transition.

The Imperial Household Administration had transferred responsibility for ordinary residence repairs.

The report was short.

The responsible office had been identified.

The authorization procedure had been clarified.

The records would be maintained by the residence administrators and reviewed monthly.

Yuru read it.

Then she placed it in the outgoing tray.

Fang Ru looked at the document.

"Your Majesty has not made any corrections."

"No."

"Should it be reviewed more carefully?"

"It has been reviewed by the responsible offices."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru turned to the next report.

It concerned the replacement of household utensils.

The report was longer.

It included a list of existing utensils, the proposed replacement quantities, the expected cost, and the authority under which the purchase would be made.

It also contained a request for the Empress's approval.

Yuru looked at the final sentence.

"Who prepared this?"

"The household stores office."

"Who reviewed it?"

"The Imperial Household Administration."

"Who authorized it?"

Fang Ru examined the document.

"The administration has not yet signed the authorization."

"Why?"

"The office believes the purchase should be approved by Your Majesty."

Yuru closed the report.

"Then the transfer of residence repairs has been completed, but the transfer of ordinary purchases has not."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru looked at the timetable.

The first responsibility had been transferred.

The second had not.

The distinction was important.

She had spent years receiving reports that described work as completed when the actual authority remained unclear.

She would not allow the transition to repeat the same mistake.

"Return the report."

Fang Ru waited.

"Tell the administration that the purchase should proceed under the existing household allowance. If the allowance is insufficient, it must identify the additional amount and the authority required."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru looked at the remaining documents.

There were still many.

But the timetable had begun to distinguish between duties that had been transferred and duties that merely appeared to have been transferred.

That was progress.

The Crown Prince arrived shortly before midday.

He had brought a report from the Eastern Palace.

The report concerned the monthly review of household accounts.

The review had identified several ordinary purchases that were being recorded under the same category as exceptional expenditure.

The steward had explained that the classification had been used for convenience.

Cheng'an had not accepted the explanation.

He had asked for the categories to be separated.

Yuru read the report.

"Your Highness has made a useful distinction."

The Crown Prince bowed.

"Thank you, Mother."

She looked at the ledger.

"Who will maintain the revised categories?"

"The Eastern Palace steward."

"Who will review them?"

"The household administrator."

"And who will approve ordinary purchases?"

"The steward, within the established allowance."

Yuru nodded.

"Then the arrangement is clear."

Cheng'an looked at her.

"Mother, I have a question."

"Ask."

"If a household administrator makes an error, should the error be corrected immediately or included in the monthly review?"

Yuru considered the question.

"That depends on the error."

The Crown Prince waited.

"If the error affects payment, inventory, or the safety of the household, it should be corrected immediately. If it concerns a classification that does not affect the amount or the authority, it may be recorded and corrected during the review."

Cheng'an nodded.

"Then the procedure should distinguish urgent corrections from ordinary review."

"Yes."

He looked at the report.

"I will add that."

Yuru smiled faintly.

"Do not add another register merely to record the distinction."

The Crown Prince paused.

Then he smiled as well.

"I will revise the existing procedure."

"Good."

He bowed.

Before leaving, he glanced at the documents on her desk.

"Mother, the palace's transition is progressing."

"Some parts."

"That is more than yesterday."

Yuru looked at him.

"Do not become too pleased with yesterday."

Cheng'an accepted the correction.

He had begun to learn that progress was not a reason to stop examining the work.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had also begun the first day of her own transition timetable.

The household had been informed that ordinary responsibilities would be transferred in stages.

Mingyuan had received the monthly expenditure ledger.

Mingxiu had received the secondary residence summaries.

Steward Zhou had been assigned responsibility for ordinary estate operations.

The central clerk would maintain the records.

The independent accountant would continue examining unresolved matters.

The arrangement was sensible.

It was also immediately inconvenient.

A kitchen steward had submitted a request for replacement cooking pots.

The request had previously gone directly to Mingzhao.

Now it had been sent to the central clerk.

The clerk had forwarded it to Steward Zhou.

Steward Zhou had asked Mingyuan whether the purchase was within the ordinary allowance.

Mingyuan had asked Mingzhao.

Mingzhao had returned the request to the central clerk.

By noon, the kitchen steward had received four answers and no pots.

Mingzhao examined the request.

The old pots were damaged.

The replacement cost was modest.

The household allowance was sufficient.

The request had been properly recorded.

She looked at Qiao Yue.

"Why has this not been approved?"

Qiao Yue examined the papers.

"Everyone is waiting for someone else."

Mingzhao sighed.

"Bring Steward Zhou."

He arrived shortly afterward.

Mingzhao placed the request before him.

"Is this an ordinary purchase?"

"Yes."

"Is the amount within the allowance?"

"Yes."

"Is the need established?"

"Yes."

"Then who should approve it?"

Steward Zhou answered.

"The central clerk should confirm the allowance, and I should authorize the purchase."

"Correct."

Mingzhao turned to Mingyuan.

"Why did you send it to me?"

Mingyuan looked uncomfortable.

"I was uncertain whether the purchase should be made before the monthly review."

"Does the household have sufficient funds?"

"Yes."

"Are the pots necessary?"

"Yes."

"Then the purchase should proceed."

Mingyuan looked at the request.

"I thought the transition required all purchases to be reviewed."

"It requires ordinary purchases to be handled by the responsible office."

Mingzhao pointed to the papers.

"This is precisely why the authority was written down."

Steward Zhou accepted the request.

He signed it.

The kitchen steward would receive the pots before the evening meal.

The matter had taken half a day.

Mingzhao looked at the clock.

"Tomorrow, ordinary purchases should not be sent to me."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Yes."

She looked at him.

"That was not a request."

He lowered his head.

"Understood."

The next difficulty concerned the estate's agricultural accounts.

Mingxiu had prepared the monthly summary.

She had separated grain received, grain sold, grain retained for seed, and grain remaining.

The format was clear.

But the figures did not reconcile.

The agricultural steward had recorded a quantity of grain as retained for seed.

The central ledger had recorded the same quantity as transferred to the household granary.

Mingxiu had noticed the duplication.

She had not yet determined which entry was correct.

Mingzhao examined the summary.

"Have you checked the original records?"

"Yes."

"Have you checked the receiving ledger?"

"Yes."

"Have you asked the agricultural steward?"

"Not yet."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Why not?"

Mingxiu hesitated.

"I wanted to understand the discrepancy before asking."

"That is reasonable."

Mingzhao returned the papers.

"But you do not need to understand the entire discrepancy before asking the person responsible for the record."

Mingxiu nodded.

She had been trying to avoid making a mistake.

Mingzhao recognized the habit.

It was the same habit she had developed herself.

She had often delayed asking a question until she had enough information to make the question difficult to dismiss.

That approach could be useful.

It could also make her the only person who knew how to begin an investigation.

Mingzhao looked at the ledger.

"Ask the steward to explain the entries. Record the explanation. Then compare it with the receiving record."

Mingxiu nodded.

"Yes."

She collected the documents.

The transition was not only about assigning authority.

It was about teaching people that uncertainty did not have to be hidden until someone could resolve it alone.

In the palace, the Department of Rites had completed its review of the ceremonial responsibilities.

The report identified several duties that could not simply be transferred to the Imperial Household Administration.

Some required the Empress's formal participation.

Others could be performed by designated senior women under established ceremonial rules.

The Ministry of Personnel had prepared a separate note concerning the consequences of accepting the Empress's resignation.

The Emperor read both reports.

Then he summoned Yuru.

She arrived in the Hall of Mental Cultivation.

The Emperor placed the documents before her.

"These are the remaining ceremonial questions."

Yuru read them.

The reports were more precise than before.

They identified the duties that depended on the Empress's position.

They also identified those that could be performed by other senior members of the imperial household.

Yuru looked at the Emperor.

"Some of these duties can be transferred."

"Yes."

"Some cannot."

"Yes."

"And the position itself remains your decision."

Zhao Jingheng nodded.

Yuru folded her hands.

"Then the administrative transition should continue."

The Emperor looked at her.

"Even if the final decision is delayed?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because the palace should not remain dependent on an unresolved question."

Zhao Jingheng considered this.

He had not expected her to answer so calmly.

He had thought that the timetable would be a means of forcing a decision.

It was.

But it was also something more.

Yuru wanted the palace to function without her.

That desire did not disappear merely because the Emperor had not yet decided whether she would remain Empress.

He looked at the reports.

"The first group of duties has been transferred."

"Yes."

"The second group will be transferred within the next several days."

"Yes."

"The remaining duties require further review."

"Yes."

The Emperor put down his brush.

"Then the timetable will continue."

Yuru inclined her head.

It was not the final decision.

But it was the first time the Emperor had treated the transfer as something that would proceed independently of his hesitation.

That evening, the palace's household stores office submitted the revised purchase request.

The ordinary allowance had been confirmed.

The purchase authority had been identified.

The report no longer required the Empress's approval.

Yuru read it.

Then she placed it in the outgoing tray.

Fang Ru looked at the document.

"Another responsibility has been transferred."

"Yes."

"Would Your Majesty like to review the remaining reports?"

Yuru glanced at the stack.

There were still several.

She considered them.

Then she looked at the clock.

"Tomorrow."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru rose.

The palace had not collapsed because she had stopped reading documents after dinner.

The charitable fund had issued its revised procedure.

The residence repairs had been transferred.

The household purchases were being handled under the existing allowance.

The Crown Prince had begun reviewing the Eastern Palace accounts.

The administration had begun identifying which duties belonged to it.

The palace was not yet independent.

But it was learning.

And Yuru was learning something else.

She could stop working without stopping caring.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao received the report concerning the cooking pots.

The purchase had been completed.

The kitchen steward had signed for the delivery.

The central clerk had recorded the expenditure.

Steward Zhou had authorized it.

Mingyuan had reviewed the monthly account.

No one had asked Mingzhao to approve it.

She read the report.

Then she put it aside.

It was a small matter.

That was precisely why it mattered.

A household did not become independent through a single dramatic declaration.

It became independent when ordinary matters could be completed without the person who had always completed them.

Mingzhao looked at the transition schedule.

The first day had produced one successful transfer.

It had also produced several unnecessary delays.

The second day would probably produce more.

She did not expect the family to become competent overnight.

She expected them to become responsible for their own mistakes.

That was a more demanding expectation.

Before retiring, Mingyuan reviewed the monthly expenditure ledger.

He found a purchase that had been recorded under the wrong category.

The amount was small.

The payment was correct.

The authorization was correct.

Only the classification was wrong.

He could have left it for the monthly review.

Instead, he corrected the entry and attached a note.

He then sent the ledger to the central clerk.

The clerk checked the correction.

Steward Zhou reviewed it.

The record was complete.

Mingyuan looked at the page.

It was not difficult.

He had simply never been expected to do it before.

At the same time, Mingxiu was preparing the agricultural summary.

She had asked the steward about the duplicated grain entry.

The steward explained that the grain had been moved from the agricultural store to the household granary, but the transfer had been recorded as both a retention and a delivery.

The records could be corrected.

Mingxiu wrote the explanation.

She did not pretend to understand the entire agricultural system.

She had, however, identified the person who could explain the entry.

Mingzhao received both reports the following morning.

She examined them.

The expenditure ledger was correct.

The agricultural summary was corrected.

The transition had produced two more completed tasks.

It had also produced two people who had begun to understand that responsibility was not the same as knowing everything.

On the ninth day of the fourth month, the palace would transfer another group of duties.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the family would continue the transition.

The Empress's position remained undecided.

Mingzhao's property division remained unresolved.

The northern warehouse discrepancy remained under examination.

The house search remained open.

But the ordinary responsibilities were beginning to move.

The palace had learned that a document could not make an office responsible merely by naming it.

The Marquisate had learned that a daughter could not be made responsible forever merely because she had once been capable.

Both institutions had begun to discover the same inconvenient truth.

A person could be useful without being indispensable.

And the proof was not a declaration.

It was a completed task.

**End of Chapter 16.**
