# **Chapter 89: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Household Management Book Can Recommend Inspecting Every Roof Without Explaining Who Should Pay for the Inspection. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Merchant's Sample and Has Discovered That a Good Deposit Does Not Make an Incomplete Specification Complete. The Marquisate Has Begun to Learn That a Register Is Not a Substitute for Knowing What Happened.**

**23rd Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The former Empress had reached the chapter on roof maintenance.

She had not expected it to be particularly interesting.

The book her father had lent her was titled *Notes on the Management of Aristocratic Households*. Its author, a retired estate steward, had spent the first several chapters explaining the responsibilities of a household head. According to him, the head should personally inspect the principal buildings, review important purchases, supervise the stewards, examine household accounts, and ensure that servants understood their duties.

Xu Yuru had found the first three recommendations reasonable.

The fourth had made her pause.

The fifth had caused her to put down her tea.

Now, several pages later, the author had arrived at the subject of roofs.

He recommended that the household head inspect every roof before the rainy season, examine the condition of each supporting beam, question the carpenter responsible for repairs, and personally verify the quality of replacement tiles.

Yuru read the passage twice.

Then she turned to Fang Ru.

"How many roofs are there in this residence?"

Fang Ru looked up from the folded clothing she was arranging.

"I don't know the exact number, Madam."

"Neither do I."

"Would you like me to ask?"

"No."

Fang Ru waited.

Yuru looked down at the book again.

"If I ask, someone will count them. Then someone will prepare a report. Then someone will ask whether I wish to inspect them."

"That is possible."

"And if I inspect them, someone will ask whether I approve of their condition."

"Also possible."

"Then the author will have successfully turned a book about household management into a building inspection."

Fang Ru lowered her eyes.

She was not entirely successful in hiding her smile.

Yuru closed the book.

"The author has confused knowing that a task exists with being the person who must perform it."

Fang Ru considered this.

"Perhaps he wrote for household heads who do not trust their stewards."

"Then he should have written a book about finding trustworthy stewards."

"Would that be a shorter book?"

"It might be a more useful one."

Yuru returned to the page, marked the passage with a narrow paper slip, and opened the second book.

This one concerned provincial granaries.

It was less concerned with the personal habits of a household head and more interested in the movement of grain: collection, storage, inspection, deterioration, transport, and distribution.

The author had a habit of distinguishing between grain recorded as received and grain physically present in a warehouse.

Yuru appreciated this.

A quantity written in a register was not necessarily a quantity in a storehouse. A storehouse might contain grain that had not been entered correctly, or a register might retain a quantity after the grain had already been moved.

The distinction was simple.

Its consequences were not.

She read a paragraph describing the practice of recording damaged grain separately rather than quietly deducting it from the total.

Then she stopped.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Do you remember the old household accounts I used to review?"

"I remember."

"How often did I find that someone had recorded a quantity as distributed when it had only been approved for distribution?"

Fang Ru thought for a moment.

"Often enough that you made a separate column for approval and actual delivery."

Yuru looked at her.

"I had forgotten that."

"You made the change after the spring festival several years ago."

"Did it help?"

"It did. The accounts became clearer."

"And the people submitting them?"

"They complained that the forms had become longer."

Yuru gave a small nod.

"That is usually the first complaint when a vague process becomes a clear one."

She resumed reading.

For a while, the room was quiet.

The two books lay side by side on the table. One advised the household head to personally inspect every roof. The other advised the record keeper to distinguish between grain approved for movement and grain actually moved.

Yuru found the second recommendation considerably more persuasive.

She had spent years being the person who discovered the difference after everyone else had stopped looking.

Now she could read about it without having to issue an instruction.

That was a small pleasure.

It was also a pleasure she intended to keep.

At the Shen residence, the new purchasing register had acquired a place on the accounts office table.

The clerks had not yet grown fond of it.

They had, however, stopped asking whether it was truly necessary.

That was progress.

Liang Wen was reviewing the previous day's entries when Shen Mingyuan entered with Steward Zhou.

"Have the kitchen repairs been closed?" Mingyuan asked.

Liang Wen turned to the relevant page.

"The water channel work was completed yesterday. The original estimate was exceeded because more stone was required at the lower bend. The additional quantity was recorded separately, and the receiving steward signed for it."

"Has the carpenter's statement been attached?"

"Yes."

"Then the file is complete?"

"Except for the final payment notation."

Steward Zhou frowned.

"Payment was made this morning."

"Then the payment notation is not an exception," Mingyuan replied. "It is simply unfinished."

Liang Wen immediately added the entry.

The steward looked at him.

"You have become particular about words."

"I have become particular about what the words are supposed to mean."

"That sounds like the same thing."

"It is not."

Liang Wen continued writing.

Mingyuan checked the completed page and moved on.

The register was not intended to make every purchase complicated. It was intended to prevent a purchase from becoming three separate stories told by three different people.

A request was one thing.

An authorization was another.

An order was another.

Receipt, inspection, and payment were separate events.

The household had previously allowed these stages to overlap whenever the people involved knew one another and trusted one another.

That had saved time.

It had also made later questions unpleasant.

Mingyuan was discovering that the accounts office had a remarkable capacity to remember that a process had been convenient while forgetting why convenience had become a problem.

He set down the register.

"Has there been any further word from Zhang?"

"Not yet," Steward Zhou said.

"And the old purchasing voucher?"

"Liang Wen has checked the next bundle. Nothing."

Mingyuan looked at the accounts clerk.

"Do not mark the entry as settled merely because we have searched for the voucher."

"I haven't."

"Good."

Liang Wen looked faintly offended.

"I know the difference between an absent document and a completed payment."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Then we understand one another."

Steward Zhou sighed.

"At this rate, the next generation will be able to inherit the estate and understand every purchase made during the present generation."

"That is the purpose of records," Mingyuan said.

"It is also a great deal of reading."

"That is the purpose of inheritance."

The steward looked at him.

For the first time that morning, Liang Wen smiled.

The grain inquiry remained more difficult.

The former warehouse steward Zhang had now examined the certified copies of the old sheets in chronological order.

He had explained the difference between the total quantity recorded in the warehouse book and the quantity considered immediately available for distribution.

That explanation accounted for a discrepancy between two categories of stock.

It did not explain the missing twenty-seven sacks.

The figures remained:

- Forty-seven sacks recorded in the original stock entry.
- Twenty sacks listed in the later instruction.
- Eighteen sacks recorded as received.
- Two sacks without confirmed receipt.
- Twenty-seven sacks whose destination remained unexplained.

Zhang had not claimed to know where the twenty-seven sacks went.

He had said that he remembered a period of repeated grain movements, but he could not identify a particular delivery without the corresponding records.

Mingyuan had instructed that his recollection be written separately from the document-confirmed facts.

There was another unresolved entry: a transport payment with no destination recorded.

It had been tempting to connect the payment to the grain movement.

Mingyuan had resisted that temptation.

A payment without a destination was suspicious.

A grain movement without a confirmed destination was also suspicious.

Two suspicious entries did not become one proven transaction merely because they appeared in the same old accounts.

He wrote a short note to Liang Wen.

"Keep the transport payment separate. If a document connects it to the grain, we can revise the classification. Until then, it remains an independent unresolved entry."

Liang Wen read the note.

"Would it not be faster to place it under the grain inquiry?"

"It would be faster to place almost anything under the grain inquiry. That does not make it correct."

Liang Wen nodded and amended the index.

Mingyuan had begun to understand that an honest account could contain unanswered questions.

An answer invented for the sake of a tidy page was worse than an empty space.

Shen Mingzhao received Gao Wen's sample shortly before noon.

It arrived in a wooden case with a written specification and a letter from the merchant's agent.

Qiao Yue placed the case on the table.

"Should I open it?"

"Yes."

The sample was a length of finely woven silk, folded carefully around a sheet of protective paper.

Mingzhao examined the surface first.

The weave was even. The edges were clean. The colour was consistent, although she wanted to inspect it in daylight before making a judgment.

She turned it over.

Then she examined the selvage.

"Who delivered this?"

"The merchant's agent. He is waiting in the front room."

"Did he bring the full specifications?"

Qiao Yue handed her the letter.

Mingzhao read it.

The proposed order was for eighty bolts. Gao Wen offered a substantial deposit before production, with the balance payable upon delivery to the buyer's warehouse. Inspection would be conducted against the sample and written specifications. Corrections would be limited to defects that failed to meet the agreed standard.

The terms were considerably better than those of the earlier trader who had wanted payment deferred until after resale.

But the letter still contained omissions.

Mingzhao read the delivery clause again.

"The buyer's warehouse is not named."

Qiao Yue looked at the letter.

"It says delivery to the buyer's warehouse."

"Which warehouse?"

"Perhaps the agent will explain."

"Then the contract should explain it."

She placed the letter beside the sample.

"The inspection period is also missing."

"Would that matter?"

"It matters very much. If the buyer can inspect the goods whenever he wishes, the balance can remain unpaid whenever he wishes."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao continued.

"The correction period is described as limited, but there is no number of days. The sample is attached, but the written specifications do not state the acceptable variation in colour."

She tapped the page.

"And the delivery period begins from the receipt of the deposit. We need to know whether that means the day the deposit is sent or the day it reaches our account."

Qiao Yue glanced at her.

"The merchant seems more careful than the last one."

"He is."

"Then perhaps the order is worth accepting."

"It may be."

Mingzhao lifted the silk sample again.

"That is why we will not accept it before checking the workshop."

She asked Qiao Yue to invite the agent into the room.

The man entered, bowed, and introduced himself as Gao Wen's commercial representative, He Zhen.

Mingzhao did not waste time on unnecessary pleasantries.

"Your principal's proposed payment arrangement is acceptable in principle."

He Zhen looked pleased.

"In that case, may I report that the order is likely to proceed?"

"You may report that the terms are under review."

His expression changed slightly.

Mingzhao continued without apology.

"The sample is suitable for further consideration. The contract requires a named delivery warehouse, a defined inspection period, a precise correction period, and a written standard for colour variation. We also need clarification on when the delivery period begins."

He Zhen listened carefully.

"I can take those questions to my principal."

"Please do."

"Would your workshop be able to produce eighty bolts?"

"I have not yet confirmed its available capacity."

"Your previous order was completed successfully."

"It was. That does not mean the workshop is empty today."

He Zhen paused.

Mingzhao looked at him.

"A workshop that has completed one order may already have accepted another. A merchant who asks whether it can produce eighty bolts should expect the answer to depend on the work already promised."

He Zhen bowed.

"That is reasonable."

"I am pleased we agree."

He was shown out with the sample still on the table.

Qiao Yue waited until the door closed.

"Do you think he will return with the revised terms?"

"If Gao Wen wants the order, he will."

"And if he does not?"

"Then we have spent an afternoon reading a letter."

Qiao Yue considered this.

"That is not a great loss."

"Compared with accepting an incomplete contract, it is an excellent one."

In the workshop, Lin Shuyun was reviewing the current work schedule.

The successful completion of the two-hundred-bolt order had brought a modest profit, but it had not transformed the workshop into a place where production capacity could be invented at will.

The workers had been paid. The training expenses had been recorded. The additional packing and transport costs had been settled. The buyer's remaining payment had been received.

The order was finished.

That did not mean the next order could begin without preparation.

Mingzhao arrived with the silk sample.

Lin Shuyun examined it carefully.

"This is a good sample."

"Do you see any problem with the weave?"

"Not from this piece. I would want to examine the full specification before promising a consistent result."

"That is what I told the agent."

Lin Shuyun looked at the sample again.

"How many bolts?"

"Eighty."

"That is manageable in principle."

"In principle?"

"Several workers are finishing a smaller commitment. We also have to account for the time needed to prepare the dyeing materials and inspect the first batch."

Mingzhao took out a writing sheet.

"How many days?"

Lin Shuyun did not answer immediately.

She checked the schedule and called the senior workshop supervisor.

They reviewed the remaining work, the number of workers available, the condition of the looms, and the time required for preparation.

One experienced worker was expected to be absent for several days to attend to a family matter. Another had asked to reduce his hours temporarily.

The trainees were now more capable than they had been during the first order, but neither could yet be counted as an independent finisher.

Lin Shuyun made the distinction clear.

"They can contribute to production. They cannot yet replace an experienced worker."

Mingzhao wrote it down.

"What delivery period would you accept?"

"Not the period in the merchant's letter."

"How much longer?"

"That depends on whether the colour can be prepared consistently from the sample. We should test a small quantity before setting the final schedule."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Prepare the capacity report. Include the existing commitments, available workers, training requirements, and a realistic first-batch date."

Lin Shuyun looked at her.

"You are not asking us to accept the order?"

"No."

"Then what are we preparing for?"

"To find out whether accepting it would be sensible."

Lin Shuyun smiled faintly.

"That is a different kind of preparation."

"It is usually cheaper."

They returned to the sample.

Lin Shuyun placed it beside a piece of silk from the workshop's own stock.

The difference was subtle, but visible.

"The merchant's sample is slightly denser."

"Can we reproduce it?"

"Probably. But I want the dyer to examine the colour and the senior weaver to compare the thread tension. If the specification requires the exact appearance of this sample, we must know what variation the buyer will permit."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Do not promise exactness before we know what exactness means."

Lin Shuyun laughed.

"That sentence should be written above the workshop door."

"Then the workers would spend their day reading it instead of weaving."

"They might improve."

"They might ask for wages to read it."

Lin Shuyun shook her head.

The capacity report would be ready the following day.

Until then, the sample would remain on the workshop table, and the proposed order would remain unaccepted.

That afternoon, Mingyuan received another note from the accounts office.

The clerks had examined the next bundle of general purchasing records. It contained several entries concerning routine household purchases and two payments made during the period under investigation.

Neither was the payment attributed to Mingzhao.

There was no original voucher.

There was no named recipient.

There was no supporting delivery record.

The estate purchasing seal still appeared in the surviving ledger entry, and the seal register still identified Chen Rui as its custodian on the relevant date.

Mingyuan read the note twice.

Then he instructed the clerk to record the bundle examined and the documents not found.

He did not send Mingzhao another request.

She had already answered the question she had been asked. She had not recognized the entry, could not identify the recipient, and had not claimed knowledge of the payment.

There was nothing useful to gain by asking her to repeat herself.

Steward Zhou entered later with the latest household account summary.

"Your father asked whether the revised property schedule is ready for the next family discussion."

"Has he supplied an agenda?"

"Not yet."

"Then the schedule is not the only thing missing."

Steward Zhou placed the summary on the table.

"Your sister has asked whether the southern kitchen repairs will affect her household allowance."

"That is a separate question."

"She was told that the repair expenditure had already been authorized."

"Then the accounts office can tell her which account will bear it."

Steward Zhou watched him.

"Your sister is not accustomed to asking the accounts office directly."

"Then she can become accustomed."

The steward sighed.

Mingyuan did not look up.

"She is learning the household's maintenance records. It would be useful for her to learn where the expenditure is recorded."

"She may not appreciate being told to learn."

"She does not have to appreciate it."

Steward Zhou considered arguing, then decided against it.

Mingyuan was not refusing to help his sister.

He was refusing to become the person who answered every question on her behalf.

The distinction was beginning to spread through the household.

Not everyone liked it.

That was probably how he knew it was real.

In her temporary residence, Mingzhao reviewed the property schedule once more before dinner.

The figures had not changed.

Nine advances remained confirmed.

Four payments had been confirmed as having been made, but the arrangements for repayment remained unclear.

Ten entries were still uncertain.

The seventy-two taels spent on study repairs remained classified as estate maintenance paid from her personal funds, with reimbursement undecided.

The additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed and unverified.

The furnishing expenditure remained in the estate accounts because it had been paid from the estate furnishing account. Her name as the account manager did not turn it into her personal property.

The northern canal property remained outside the family division and under her independent consideration.

She placed the schedule beside Gao Wen's sample letter.

The two documents were very different.

One concerned an old family estate and obligations that had been left unclear for years.

The other concerned a possible commercial order whose terms could still be negotiated before anyone began work.

Yet both required the same discipline.

A promise was not payment.

An instruction was not delivery.

A name in a ledger was not proof of personal receipt.

A favourable offer was not yet a good contract.

Mingzhao closed the property schedule.

She had once believed that if she controlled every detail, she could prevent others from making mistakes.

The estate had taught her otherwise.

Some mistakes had been made because no one wanted to take responsibility. Others had been made because people trusted her to notice and correct everything.

She had participated in that arrangement for years.

Leaving it had not made the consequences disappear.

But she no longer intended to return merely because the household had discovered that records required someone to read them.

She folded Gao Wen's letter and placed it in a separate file.

The merchant would have to provide the missing terms.

The workshop would have to provide its capacity report.

Only then would she decide.

For once, the decision did not need to be made before the question was fully understood.

That evening, she instructed Qiao Yue to keep the sample safely stored and to send a message to Lin Shuyun requesting the report by the following afternoon.

Then she turned to her own accounts.

There were still questions to answer.

But none of them required her to pretend that an incomplete answer was a final one.

In the palace guest residence, Xu Yuru finished the day's reading shortly before sunset.

She had made three marks in the estate management book and two in the provincial granary book.

Fang Ru placed a small dish of sliced fruit beside her.

"Have you finished both?"

"No."

"Would you like me to put them away?"

Yuru looked at the two books.

"Not yet."

She placed the estate management book on the left and the granary book on the right.

"Tomorrow, I will continue with the granary book."

"And the other one?"

"I will leave it alone for a day."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru picked up a slice of fruit.

"The author of the estate book believes a household head should inspect everything personally."

"Yes, Madam."

"The author of the granary book believes records should distinguish between what was ordered and what was received."

"Yes."

"I prefer the second author."

"Because his advice is more practical?"

"Because he does not require me to climb onto a roof."

Fang Ru lowered her head.

This time she did not attempt to hide her laughter.

Yuru smiled and returned to her fruit.

Outside, the evening bells marked the end of another ordinary day.

No palace official had arrived with a document.

No steward had requested her judgment.

No one had asked her to inspect a roof.

The former Empress found that she was quite content with this arrangement.

For the moment, her greatest responsibility was to return two books within three months.

She considered the matter.

Then she looked at the books again.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Make sure the borrowing dates are recorded correctly."

"They are."

"Good."

Yuru picked up her tea.

"Then there is no need to inspect them again tonight."
