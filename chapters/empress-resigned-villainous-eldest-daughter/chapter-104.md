# **Chapter 104: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Garden Can Be Improved by Removing a Diseased Branch Rather Than Ordering Every Tree to Grow Straight. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That a Finished Bolt Still Requires a Buyer Who Is Willing to Receive It. The Marquis's Heir Has Discovered That Two Men Can Remember a Missing Notebook Differently Without Either Remembering Where It Went.**

**8th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru spent the morning reading the seasonal planting notes.

The author had reached the subject of garden paths.

A path, he explained, should follow the natural arrangement of the garden. It should provide convenient access without cutting through the roots of established trees or forcing visitors to walk around unnecessary obstacles.

Yuru approved.

The author then added that the owner should inspect the path personally every morning to ensure that fallen leaves had been removed.

Yuru closed her eyes.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Why does every author eventually decide that the owner has nothing better to do?"

Fang Ru glanced at the page.

"Perhaps they believe that personal attention produces the best result."

"Personal attention produces a great many things. It also produces a person who cannot finish breakfast."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru reopened the book.

She did not dislike the advice about paths. It was useful. She simply had no desire to become responsible for every fallen leaf in a garden she had not even chosen.

She read the next passage.

The author recommended that gardeners inspect paths after heavy rain, when loose stones and damaged drainage could create hazards. Routine sweeping, however, could be assigned to the gardeners responsible for the grounds.

Yuru looked at the paragraph.

"That is better."

She took her notebook and wrote:

*Inspect according to risk. Ordinary work belongs to the assigned worker. Unusual damage requires a report and an appropriate response.*

She considered the sentence.

It was applicable to gardens, residences, and rather more complicated institutions.

She did not add another paragraph.

Instead, she turned the page.

The seasonal notes discussed the planting of flowering shrubs in shaded corners.

Yuru continued reading.

For the first time in several days, she was not trying to find a better way to divide responsibility.

She was simply interested in what happened to plants when they received the right amount of sunlight.

At the workshop, Shen Mingzhao received a report that the remaining twenty bolts had completed weaving.

They had entered the finishing stage.

The first sixty bolts had passed internal inspection. The final twenty had not yet been examined.

The supervisor had included the measurements from the weaving table and a note that one bolt required a second check because its width approached the lower limit of the approved specification.

Mingzhao read the note.

"Has it been rejected?"

"No," Qiao Yue replied. "The measuring clerk marked it for re-examination. The finishing supervisor has not yet signed the result."

"Then it remains under examination."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao examined the other measurements.

They were consistent.

The production report showed that the workshop had completed the scheduled weaving work without requiring additional material.

She looked at the cost sheet.

The order remained within the expected range, although the additional inspection expenses had increased the total slightly.

"Ask Sun Qiming to update the estimate when the final twenty pass," she said. "I want the labour, finishing, and inspection costs separated."

"Will you visit the workshop?"

"Not today."

Mingzhao returned to the contract folder.

She had already visited the workshop the previous afternoon. The supervisors understood the requirements, the approved sample was available, and the inspection procedure was being followed.

There was no reason to repeat the visit merely because she could.

She had once believed that a manager who did not appear was a manager who had neglected the work.

Now she was beginning to see that a manager who appeared unnecessarily could also interrupt it.

The Marquisate's transport office sent another document before noon.

It was a copy of the surviving personnel roster for the northern warehouse.

The roster identified the transport clerk who had served during the period of the four-cart dispatch.

His name was Li Shun.

Mingyuan examined the dates.

Li Shun had been assigned to movement records during the relevant months. His employment record showed that he had later transferred to another estate office.

There was no dismissal record.

There was no disciplinary entry.

There was no statement that he had taken the missing notebook.

Mingyuan called Liang Wen.

"Please prepare an inquiry to the office where Li Shun was transferred."

"Should we ask him about the four carts?"

"Yes, but separate the questions."

Liang Wen took out a sheet.

Mingyuan dictated.

Had Li Shun maintained the northern warehouse movement notebook?

Did he remember the four-cart dispatch?

Did he record the cargo or destination?

Had he transferred the notebook to another clerk or supervisor?

Did he retain any copies of the relevant movement records?

Liang Wen wrote each question.

Mingyuan read the draft.

"Do not say that he was responsible for the missing notebook."

"I have not."

"Good. We are asking what he remembers and what records he may have retained."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan signed the inquiry.

The new information was useful.

It identified the clerk who had handled movement records.

It did not establish that he had handled the particular dispatch, nor did it explain what the carts carried.

The grain account remained separate.

Twenty-seven sacks were still unexplained.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received a copy of the personnel roster.

He read the name.

"Li Shun."

"Yes, Father."

"He worked at the northern warehouse?"

"According to the personnel record."

"Then he should know what happened."

"He may remember."

The Marquis looked dissatisfied.

"Why do you always say 'may'?"

"Because we have not spoken to him yet."

The Marquis placed the roster on the table.

"Then speak to him."

"The inquiry has been sent."

"Good. I want this matter concluded."

Mingyuan bowed.

The Marquis looked toward the revised property schedule.

"And the division?"

"The council is still checking the supporting documents."

"How long will that take?"

"I cannot give a reliable date."

The Marquis frowned.

He wanted the estate's affairs settled. He also wanted the family to stop discussing the past.

But the two wishes were not easily reconciled.

The property schedule contained claims that had been made over several years, payments recorded in different accounts, and expenses that had been classified according to who supervised them rather than who owned the money.

The family could not resolve all of that by deciding that it had become inconvenient.

"Continue," the Marquis said.

Mingyuan bowed again.

At her residence, Mingzhao received a short letter from the workshop supervisor.

The final twenty bolts had entered finishing.

The bolt marked for re-examination had been measured again using the standard instrument.

Its width was within the approved range.

The supervisor had recorded the second measurement and signed the result.

The finishing work continued.

Mingzhao read the note and put it aside.

"That was the only measurement requiring another check?"

"Yes."

"Was the original measurement retained?"

"Yes. The corrected measurement is written beneath it, with the time and the measuring clerk's signature."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Good."

Qiao Yue looked at the report.

"Madam seems satisfied."

"The record is satisfactory."

"Is that different?"

"Yes. I do not need to be satisfied with every worker. I need to know whether the work meets the contract."

Qiao Yue considered this.

Mingzhao returned to her desk.

She had once taken pride in knowing every person involved in an order and remembering every stage of production.

That knowledge had helped her catch errors.

It had also made the workshop dependent on her memory.

A record could not replace judgment, but it could prevent judgment from becoming the only source of information.

She placed the report in the order file.

The remaining twenty bolts would be examined when finishing was complete.

The buyer would inspect them after the full order was ready.

Until then, the order remained unfinished.

In the afternoon, the family council sent Mingzhao another letter.

It confirmed that the original receipt for one of the nine advances had not yet been found.

The council would continue searching the archive.

It also stated that the payment entry previously attached to the same claim might refer to a supplier payment rather than money delivered directly to Mingzhao.

The council did not yet have the voucher needed to establish the recipient.

Mingzhao read the paragraph.

She wrote a brief reply.

The possibility of a supplier payment should be investigated against the corresponding purchasing records. Until the recipient and purpose were established, the entry should not be treated as repayment to her.

She then reviewed the remaining classifications.

The nine confirmed advances remained confirmed.

The four payment-confirmed entries remained unclear as to repayment arrangements.

The ten uncertain entries remained uncertain.

The seventy-two taels for study repairs remained estate maintenance paid from her personal funds, with reimbursement unresolved.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.

The furnishing payment remained estate expenditure.

She put down the brush.

There was a temptation to become impatient.

The family had enough records to make progress, but not enough to finish. Every corrected entry seemed to produce another question.

Yet she had insisted on this process.

She could not demand careful accounting and then complain that careful accounting took time.

She folded the reply.

"Send it tomorrow morning."

Qiao Yue accepted the letter.

Mingzhao returned to the workshop reports.

That evening, Xu Yuru finished the chapter on garden paths.

The author had written that paths should be repaired before they became dangerous, but that ordinary maintenance should remain with the gardeners.

Yuru smiled.

"Fang Ru, this author has finally discovered delegation."

Fang Ru looked at the book.

"Perhaps he has improved."

"Or perhaps he has reached the chapter written by someone who actually worked in a garden."

Yuru set the book aside.

She looked toward the courtyard.

The plants outside the guest residence had been watered. A gardener was clearing a narrow path near the wall.

He did not appear to be waiting for instructions.

Yuru watched him remove a fallen branch and carry it away.

The work was simple.

It was also being done without requiring the former Empress to supervise it.

She turned back to her tea.

There was something pleasant about seeing a task completed without becoming responsible for its completion.

The next morning's papers would bring more questions.

Li Shun might remember the notebook.

He might remember the four carts.

He might remember neither.

The workshop would finish the final twenty bolts, then wait for inspection and delivery.

The family council would continue examining the property schedule.

And Yuru, who had once believed that a well-managed household depended on her noticing everything, had begun to enjoy a garden in which the gardener knew what to do.

For the moment, that was enough.
