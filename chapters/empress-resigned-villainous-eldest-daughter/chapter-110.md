# **Chapter 110: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Tree Can Recover From Pruning Without Receiving a Daily Report, While Her Attendant Has Discovered That a Quiet Courtyard Is Not an Invitation to Add More Duties. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Found Two Products Worth Studying but Has Refused to Produce Goods Before Knowing Whether Anyone Intends to Buy Them. The Marquis's Heir Has Learned That a Driver's Name on a Wage List Does Not Mean He Was Sitting on the Cart.**

**14th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had been in the courtyard for less than a quarter of an hour when Fang Ru brought her a small note.

"Madam, the palace library has sent a list of books available for borrowing."

Yuru looked at it.

"Did I request one?"

"No, Madam. The library sent it with the morning correspondence. They said several new volumes had been catalogued."

"Is there a request for me to choose one?"

"No. They have merely provided the list."

Yuru unfolded the paper.

There were books on household management, provincial granaries, ceremonial precedents, agricultural taxation, gardens, and the cultivation of medicinal plants.

She read the titles.

Then she folded the list again.

"Put it with the other correspondence."

"Shall I send a reply?"

"No. They have not asked a question."

Fang Ru accepted the paper.

Yuru looked toward the courtyard.

The gardener had finished trimming the damaged branch. He was examining the remaining shrub, while the younger worker collected the cuttings.

"Does the library send this list to every residence?" she asked.

"I do not know, Madam."

"Then there is no reason to assume it was sent because someone expects me to borrow a book."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru took her tea.

She had once received lists of books because the palace required her to know the contents. She had received lists of ceremonies because she had to approve their arrangements. She had received lists of attendants because she was expected to understand which household had sufficient staff and which had requested additional hands.

The list before her was simply a list.

She could choose a book.

She could also choose none.

The freedom to leave a list unread was surprisingly pleasant.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received the foreman's updated material estimates.

The dye merchant had confirmed his price for the two specifications she had marked the previous evening. The silk supplier had also given a quotation, though the price would remain valid for only five days.

Mingzhao placed the estimates beside the market summary.

Qiao Yue read the figures.

"The first specification can be produced with a smaller purchase."

"Yes."

"The second requires more material."

"And the expected selling price is lower."

"Then the first is better?"

"Not necessarily."

Mingzhao tapped the market summary.

The first specification had sold to several merchants, but the quantities had varied. Some had purchased only a few bolts. The second specification had fewer buyers, but those who purchased it tended to order a larger quantity.

Neither was an obvious choice.

A product with several small buyers might sell steadily but require more effort in collection and delivery. A product with fewer buyers might be more efficient, but it would leave the workshop exposed if one merchant changed his plans.

Mingzhao drew two columns on a clean sheet.

"Ask the merchants who purchased the first specification whether they expect to buy again before the end of the season. Ask the larger buyers of the second whether they would consider a smaller initial quantity."

Qiao Yue wrote the instructions.

"Should we ask for written commitments?"

"Ask what they are willing to confirm. We are not offering them a contract yet."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao looked at the foreman's estimate.

"If we make a trial batch, how many bolts can be produced without disturbing the ordinary workshop schedule?"

"The foreman says twelve, perhaps fifteen."

"Then ask him to give a separate estimate for twelve. I do not want him to assume that fifteen is already approved."

"Understood."

Mingzhao returned the estimate.

A small trial would be useful only if it remained small.

She had no desire to let an experiment become a full production commitment merely because the workshop had already begun preparing the looms.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan was waiting for a reply from the former transport supervisors.

Instead, he received a note from the accounts office.

The note concerned the old purchasing voucher.

Liang Wen had found a reference to a payment in a general purchasing summary. The amount matched the amount listed in the disputed entry. The summary described it as a payment made to a supplier, but the supplier's name had been written in abbreviated form.

There was no voucher attached.

Mingyuan examined the paper.

"Is this the same payment?"

Liang Wen answered carefully.

"The amount and date match. The description may refer to the disputed entry, but the surviving summary does not identify the recipient in full."

"Can we identify the supplier from the abbreviation?"

"We have found two merchants whose names could fit."

"Then it is not identified."

"No, Young Master."

Mingyuan put the paper down.

"Have the purchasing registers been checked for either merchant?"

"One appears in the earlier quarter. The other appears in a separate account, but not on the same date."

"Ask whether either has a surviving receipt or delivery record."

Liang Wen bowed.

"Should we add the payment summary to the old voucher file?"

"Yes. Mark it as a possible reference, not as the voucher itself."

Liang Wen made a note.

Mingyuan looked again at the amount.

It was tempting to treat the matching date and sum as proof. The abbreviated supplier name offered another possibility. But the missing voucher still left the recipient and purpose uncertain.

The payment might have been made to a supplier.

It might have been recorded under a general purchasing description.

It might have been something else.

Mingyuan refused to decide before the evidence did.

"Keep this separate from the four-cart movement," he said.

Liang Wen nodded.

"I have done so."

"Good."

Mingyuan returned the paper.

The estate had managed to produce another document that was useful without being conclusive.

He was beginning to suspect that the archives contained a remarkable number of such documents.

At the western guest residence, Yuru received a visit from Empress Dowager Wei.

The Dowager had sent word in advance, making it clear that the visit was personal and that no formal reception was required.

Yuru greeted her in the small sitting room.

"Mother, you need not have come so far."

The Dowager settled into her chair.

"I wished to see you."

"Then I am glad you came."

Fang Ru served tea and withdrew.

The Dowager glanced toward the open window.

"Your courtyard looks pleasant."

"It is quiet."

"Do you spend much time there?"

"More than I expected."

The Dowager smiled.

"Have you chosen a permanent residence?"

"Not yet."

"Do you intend to remain here?"

"For the time being."

The Dowager nodded.

She did not ask why Yuru had not selected the Duke Xu residence, nor did she ask whether the guest residence was sufficient. The matter had been discussed before, and she understood that the decision belonged to Yuru.

Instead, she noticed the book on the table.

"Another garden book?"

"Yes."

"Is it useful?"

"Very."

"What have you learned?"

"That a tree should not be pruned without first understanding why its branches are damaged."

The Dowager considered this.

"That sounds like good advice for more than trees."

"It may be."

Yuru poured the tea.

The Dowager glanced at her.

"Are you enjoying the book?"

"Yes."

"Then continue reading it."

Yuru smiled.

There was no request hidden inside the suggestion.

The Dowager took a sip of tea.

"I heard the library sent you a list of new books."

"It did."

"Did you choose one?"

"No."

The Dowager raised an eyebrow.

"Not one?"

"I have not finished the books I already borrowed."

"Then you were sensible."

Yuru looked amused.

"I did not think the library would be offended."

"It may be. Libraries have their own pride."

The former Empress laughed softly.

The conversation moved to the Dowager's garden, where one of the old pomegranate trees had produced fewer fruits than expected. The Dowager described how the gardener had blamed the unusual rain, while the head attendant suspected that the tree had been watered too frequently.

Yuru listened.

She did not offer a solution.

The Dowager did not ask for one.

For a while, they spoke about trees, weather, and the inconvenience of fruit that ripened unevenly.

It was an ordinary conversation.

Yuru found that she had missed such conversations more than she had realized.

Mingzhao received replies from two merchants before the afternoon.

The first expected to purchase the familiar specification again, but could not yet state the quantity. The second believed that a small batch of the other specification might sell, provided the price remained within the previous season's range.

Neither offered a firm order.

Mingzhao read the replies.

Qiao Yue waited.

"Should we proceed with the twelve-bolt trial?"

"Not yet."

"The foreman has prepared the estimate."

"Then keep it ready."

Qiao Yue looked at the merchant's note.

"They both seem interested."

"Interest is not an order."

"Could we not produce a few bolts and sell them afterward?"

"We could. But then we would be speculating on the market."

"Would that be unwise?"

"Not always. But we should decide how much money we are willing to keep tied up in unsold goods before beginning."

Mingzhao took the account book.

She wrote down the expected cost of twelve bolts, including silk, dye, labour, finishing, and storage.

Then she added the cost of transporting the goods to the market.

The total was not alarming.

It was also not negligible.

"If we produce twelve bolts," she said, "we must be prepared to hold them if the merchants delay their purchases."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"How long?"

"That depends on the market. We should not decide the holding period before we know the expected selling price."

She closed the book.

"Ask the foreman to prepare the looms for the trial, but do not authorize the purchase of material. I want the merchants' price expectations in writing first."

Qiao Yue repeated the instruction.

Mingzhao glanced at the completed Gao Wen account.

The previous order had taught her that a clear contract made a large quantity manageable.

This possible trial was different. It would involve making goods before a buyer had committed to purchasing them.

The risk was smaller than accepting a badly structured contract, but it was still a risk.

She wanted to understand it before taking it.

At the Marquisate, the former transport supervisor He Shun sent a brief reply.

He remembered that the transport office had received instructions for a northern-road dispatch during the relevant month. He could not recall whether the instruction had been written in a separate order book or delivered verbally.

He also remembered that the movement notebook had been used by more than one clerk.

He did not remember who had carried it out of the office.

Mingyuan read the letter.

Zhou De stood nearby.

"That is consistent with what he said before," Zhou observed.

"Partly."

"He remembers the office handling the dispatch but not the notebook's final custody."

"Yes."

"Should we ask Zhao Liang again?"

"Yes. Ask whether he remembers any separate order book for northern-road dispatches."

Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan placed He Shun's reply beside the earlier correspondence.

The notebook had been issued to the transport supervisor's office. Its register page was damaged. He Shun remembered the office using it. Zhao Liang remembered receiving several records when he took over, but not that particular notebook.

Li Shun remembered preparing the four-cart entry, but not transferring the notebook or receiving a signed return.

The evidence suggested that the notebook had been used in the office.

It did not establish who had held it last.

Mingyuan looked at the damaged register copy.

"Could the torn page have been removed deliberately?"

Zhou considered the question.

"It is possible."

"Do we have evidence that it was?"

"No."

"Then we should not write that."

Zhou inclined his head.

Mingyuan turned the page over.

"Have the remaining register pages been checked for signs of other missing sections?"

"Yes. Liang Wen found no other torn signature columns in the surviving pages."

"Record that too."

Zhou made a note.

The inquiry was not moving quickly.

But it was moving.

The Dowager left the guest residence shortly before the afternoon light began to soften.

Yuru accompanied her to the courtyard.

"Will you visit me again?" the Dowager asked.

"If you wish."

"I do."

"Then I will come."

The Dowager took her hand briefly.

"Do not feel obliged to fill your days merely because they are empty."

Yuru looked at her.

"I have discovered that a day can be quite full without being managed."

The Dowager smiled.

"That is a useful discovery."

After the Dowager's carriage departed, Fang Ru returned to the sitting room.

"Would you like to rest, Madam?"

Yuru glanced at the garden book.

"I think I will walk for a while."

They went outside.

The gardener was speaking to the younger worker again. This time, he was explaining how to distinguish a branch that had been damaged by weather from one that had been weakened by disease.

Yuru listened for a moment as she passed.

She did not interrupt.

Fang Ru followed her along the path.

"Did the Dowager enjoy the visit?"

"Yes."

"She seemed pleased."

"I was pleased too."

They walked beneath the tree.

The afternoon was warm, but the shade was comfortable.

Yuru looked up at the branches.

She had once thought that leaving the palace administration would make her feel as though she had stepped away from the center of everything.

Instead, she was discovering how much of the world continued to exist outside the center.

Trees grew.

People worked.

Books waited to be read.

A visit could be pleasant without becoming an obligation.

She had not lost the ability to understand a household.

She had simply stopped being required to understand every household at once.

Mingzhao's final task of the day was to review the workshop's current cash position.

The Gao Wen order had been fully settled. The remaining funds were available, but she had already separated the amount needed for ordinary expenses from the money that could be used for new materials.

The foreman's maintenance estimate was modest.

The trial production estimate was larger.

The replenishment estimate was larger still.

Mingzhao wrote three headings in her notebook:

**Ordinary operating reserve.**

**Possible trial production.**

**Future expansion.**

She placed the figures beneath them.

The first amount would remain untouched except for ordinary expenses.

The second could be used if the merchant replies supported a trial.

The third would not be committed until a larger order or reliable demand justified it.

Qiao Yue looked over the page.

"That leaves less money available for immediate use."

"It leaves less money available for careless use."

Qiao Yue accepted the correction.

Mingzhao closed the notebook.

"Tell the foreman that the maintenance work may proceed. The looms should be kept in good condition. No additional materials are to be purchased yet."

"Yes, Miss."

"And ask him to tell the workers plainly that the next order has not been decided."

Qiao Yue bowed.

The workshop had completed its work and earned a profit.

It did not need to pretend that the next transaction had already begun.

That evening, Mingyuan received Zhao Liang's reply.

The former supervisor remembered a separate dispatch order book used during periods of heavy transport activity. He could not recall whether it had been retained after the movement notebook was transferred to his office. He believed the two books had sometimes been kept together, but he was not certain.

Mingyuan read the letter and put it beside He Shun's.

"Another possible book," Zhou said.

"Yes."

"Do we know where it was kept?"

"No."

"Should we search the old transport office?"

"Yes. But first ask whether the office has a list of books and registers transferred when it was reorganized."

Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan added the question to the inquiry.

He then looked at the four-cart file.

The entry was still there.

Four carts.

Cargo blank.

Destination incomplete.

No arrival receipt.

No confirmed driver.

No confirmed final custodian of the movement notebook.

He closed the folder.

It was not a satisfying conclusion.

It was not supposed to be one.

Before retiring, Yuru returned to the garden book and read another page.

Mingzhao had left the workshop's trial estimate unsigned.

Mingyuan had left the transport inquiry open.

The Dowager had gone back to her own residence without asking Yuru to inspect a single palace document.

The former Empress read until the lamp began to burn low.

Then she marked the page and closed the book.

She had not finished it.

She was not required to finish it tonight.

That, she decided, was another useful thing to learn.
