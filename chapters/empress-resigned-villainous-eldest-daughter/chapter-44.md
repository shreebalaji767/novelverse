# **Chapter 44: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to the Imperial Gardens Again. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Review Eleven Documents. One Woman Has Brought a Hat. The Other Has Brought Three Accountants.**

**6th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The morning began with an argument about hats.



Xu Yuru had received a message from the Empress Dowager requesting her company in the imperial gardens. The weather was expected to be warm, and the Dowager intended to walk beneath the trees before the afternoon heat.



Fang Ru had prepared three hats.



One was made of pale silk, with a narrow brim.



Another was a little larger and had a decorative ribbon.



The third was a practical travelling hat, suitable for a longer walk.



Yuru examined them.



"Why are there three?"



Fang Ru replied, "The weather is expected to be warm."



"I can see that."



"The Empress Dowager may wish to walk for some time."



"Then I will wear a comfortable hat."



"Which one?"



Yuru pointed to the third.



Fang Ru looked disappointed.



"The first is more elegant."



"I am going to walk beneath trees, not receive foreign envoys."



"The second is also suitable."



"Then perhaps you should wear it."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



Yuru picked up the practical hat.



"Have you prepared one for yourself?"



"Yes, Madam."



"Good. Then we are both properly equipped."



Fang Ru smiled.



The matter was settled.



It had taken longer than necessary.

The Empress Dowager was waiting near the eastern garden pavilion.



She wore a simple outer robe and had brought only two attendants.



When Yuru arrived, the Dowager looked at her hat.



"That is practical."



"It is comfortable."



"Your attendants wanted something more elegant?"



"One of them prepared three choices."



The Dowager laughed.



"Mine prepared four."



Yuru looked towards the pavilion.



"Did you choose the practical one?"



"I chose the one that was easiest to put on."



The Dowager began walking.



The garden was quiet. The trees had filled out after the spring rains, and the paths were dry beneath the morning sun. A gardener was trimming a row of shrubs near the western pond.



The Dowager watched him for a moment.



"That tree was planted before I entered the palace."



Yuru looked towards the old tree.



"It is still healthy."



"The gardener says it may need pruning."



"Then it should be pruned."



The Dowager glanced at her.



"You did not ask for a report."



"I am learning."



They continued along the path.



After a while, the Dowager said, "The Emperor told me dinner went well."



"It did."



"He said you spoke with the children."



"Yes."



"He also said he did not bring any documents."



Yuru smiled.



"That was considerate."



The Dowager gave a small laugh.



"He was rather proud of himself."



"I noticed."



"He has been asking whether you intend to visit the palace again."



"I am here now."



"He means after you leave."



Yuru looked at the pond.



"I will visit when I wish to see the family."



"Not when the palace requires you?"



"Not for ordinary administration."



The Dowager nodded.



"That is fair."



She paused beside a stone bench.



"I have been thinking about the position."



Yuru turned towards her.



"The position of Empress?"



"Yes."



The Dowager looked towards the garden.



"When I first heard that you intended to resign, I thought the palace would become disorderly. I thought the inner household would lose its centre."



"And now?"



"Now I think it has relied too heavily on its centre."



Yuru did not answer immediately.



The Dowager continued.



"That does not mean the position is unimportant. It does not mean the next woman who holds it should have no responsibilities. But the palace should not require one person to become the answer to every question."



Yuru smiled faintly.



"That is what I hoped the transition would demonstrate."



"I understand that now."



The Dowager sat on the bench.



Yuru sat beside her.



For a moment, neither spoke.



Then the Dowager looked at Yuru's hat.



"That is still a very practical hat."



Yuru glanced at her.



"Would you like to borrow it?"



The Dowager laughed.



"No. I have four."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan was preparing for a meeting with the independent accountant.



He had arranged the northern warehouse records into separate bundles.



The first contained the original stock count.



The second contained the movement instruction for twenty sacks.



The third contained the southern storage ledger.



The fourth contained the transport payments.



The fifth contained uncertain papers, including the possible reference to Zhao Fusheng.



He had also prepared a separate list of questions.



Mingxiu entered carrying a small tray.



"You have arranged the records."



"Yes."



"How many bundles?"



"Five."



"How many questions?"



"Eleven."



She looked at the list.



"Why eleven?"



"Because there are eleven things I cannot establish from the records."



Mingxiu placed the tray down.



"That is a reasonable number."



Mingyuan looked at her.



"Would you like to review the household purchase accounts?"



"I already did."



"Any problems?"



"Two. One supplier has not provided the delivery confirmation for the kitchen charcoal. The other has submitted a payment receipt without the original purchase note."



"Are they large amounts?"



"No."



"Then perhaps they can wait."



Mingxiu shook her head.



"They can wait until the supplier replies. They should not be marked complete."



Mingyuan nodded.



He returned to his list.



Their mother entered shortly afterwards.



Madam Liang looked at the bundles.



"Why are there so many papers?"



"The accountant is coming to review the warehouse records."



"Could he not simply tell us where the grain went?"



Mingyuan looked at her.



"Not without examining the records."



Madam Liang sighed.



"Your sister would have settled this already."



Mingyuan's hand stopped.



"Mother, she did not settle it."



Madam Liang looked at him.



"She managed the warehouse accounts for years."



"She managed the records. That is why we have been able to identify the gaps."



Madam Liang frowned.



Mingyuan continued, more carefully.



"She did not know where the missing sacks went. She had not established it before she left. Neither have I."



The room became quiet.



Mingxiu looked at the tray.



Madam Liang eventually said, "Then continue your review."



She left.



Mingyuan returned to the papers.



He did not feel pleased.



But he had finally said something he should have said earlier.

At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received the revised agenda for the family property discussion.



The envelope contained eleven documents.



She counted them.



Then she counted them again.



Qiao Yue watched.



"Miss, are there eleven?"



"Yes."



"Why count twice?"



"Because the letter says eleven."



"Would you be disappointed if there were twelve?"



"I would ask why."



Qiao Yue sighed.



Mingzhao opened the first document.



It was a revised classification of the agricultural holdings.



The second contained the leased properties.



The third listed fallow land.



The fourth described buildings requiring maintenance.



The fifth concerned incomplete registration records.



The sixth valued the northern warehouse structure separately from the grain stock.



The remaining papers covered maintenance expenses, personal payments, disputed property records, and outstanding valuations.



Mingzhao read the documents carefully.



The revised classification was better than the previous version.



It still contained two errors.



One agricultural parcel had been listed as leased even though the lease had expired.



Another building had been included among the estate's usable properties despite an earlier inspection noting structural repairs.



She marked both entries.



Qiao Yue leaned over.



"Are those serious?"



"They affect the valuation."



"Will you correct them?"



"I will ask for the supporting records."



Mingzhao wrote a note beside each entry.



She then turned to the northern warehouse valuation.



The building itself had been assessed separately from the grain stock.



That was correct.



The structure was sound, though some drainage work remained necessary.



The grain stock was listed as a separate matter with unresolved quantities.



Mingzhao nodded.



At least someone had understood the distinction.



She reached the final page.



It contained a proposed list of people expected to attend the next meeting.



Mr Liang.



Shen Guowei.



Madam Liang.



Old Madam Shen.



Shen Mingyuan.



Shen Mingxiu.



Mr Chen.



Sun Qiming.



Master Luo.



And two representatives from the family land office.



Mingzhao read the list.



"Three accountants?"



Qiao Yue counted.



"Sun Qiming, the family accountant, and the land office clerk?"



"Yes."



"Perhaps they can agree on something."



"Perhaps."



Mingzhao placed the documents in order.



She had not yet confirmed her attendance.



The meeting date was still pending.



But she would attend when the supporting records were ready.



There was no reason to spend an afternoon arguing over figures that had not been checked.

At the imperial palace, the Emperor was reviewing the Department of Rites' inventory.



The thirty-two ceremonial objects had been divided into three groups.



Twenty-seven had been identified.



Five remained uncertain.



The first group consisted of objects still used during annual ceremonies.



The second contained objects held as replacements.



The third contained items whose purpose was unclear.



Zhao Jingheng read the list.



"Why are these five uncertain?"



Gao Shun answered.



"The descriptions in the old records are incomplete. The stores office has found several objects that may correspond to them, but the identification has not been confirmed."



"Have they compared the dimensions?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And?"



"Two objects appear similar. Three have no matching description."



The Emperor set down the list.



"Then do not transfer them."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor turned to the next page.



"How long will the comparison take?"



"The Department of Rites has requested another three days."



"Grant it."



Gao Shun accepted the order.



The Emperor looked at him.



"Has anyone suggested sending the matter to the former Empress?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



He returned to the report.



After a moment, he asked, "Have the palace stores finished the inventory of the western residence?"



"Not yet."



"Why?"



"The inventory is being reconciled with the former Empress's personal property list."



The Emperor nodded.



"Make sure the palace property and personal property are separated. Nothing should be transferred merely because it was kept in her rooms."



Gao Shun bowed.



The instruction was sensible.



It was also the kind of instruction Yuru would once have issued herself.



The Emperor did not need to send it to her.

At the northern canal property, Master Luo was waiting for a response from the land office.



The neighbouring parcel's registration description had not yet arrived.



He had, however, obtained an older map showing the drainage channel as it had existed before the recent repairs.



The map was incomplete.



It showed the channel's general route but not its exact width.



Master Luo sent a copy to Mingzhao.



She received it shortly after midday.



The map was old and slightly damaged.



She examined it with care.



The drainage channel appeared to have been narrower in the earlier period.



The current channel had been widened at some point.



The map did not identify who had ordered the work.



Nor did it establish whether the land boundary had been altered.



Mingzhao read Master Luo's accompanying note.



He recommended waiting for the neighbouring registration record before drawing any conclusion.



She agreed.



She wrote a reply asking him to preserve the original map and obtain a certified copy if possible.



She also asked whether the land office could identify the date of the channel's widening.



The matter remained unresolved.



Qiao Yue looked at the map.



"Does it help?"



"Yes."



"Does it settle anything?"



"No."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"That is becoming familiar."



"It is still useful."



Mingzhao folded the map carefully.



She was beginning to understand why property disputes could continue for years.



People remembered different things.



Records described different periods.



Repairs altered the physical appearance of a place.



And everyone involved preferred the boundary that benefited them.



The map had not solved the problem.



But it had established that the current channel was not necessarily the original one.



That was enough to justify further examination.

At the silk workshop, Lin Shuyun was discussing the proposed two-hundred-bolt order with the workshop manager.



The merchant had offered a price.



He had also requested delivery within a fixed period.



The workshop manager believed the deadline was possible.



Lin did not.



"How many workers can you assign without delaying the existing orders?"



"Six."



"How many additional workers would be required?"



"At least four."



"Have you found them?"



"Not yet."



"Then the schedule is not confirmed."



The manager frowned.



"If we wait until every worker is found, the buyer may go elsewhere."



"That is possible."



"The order is profitable."



"Have you calculated the full cost?"



"The materials and labour are known."



"What about dyeing failures, transport, inspection delays, and the deposit?"



The manager hesitated.



Lin opened her ledger.



"The buyer has not agreed to a deposit large enough to cover the initial material cost. If we purchase everything ourselves and he delays payment, the partnership carries the risk."



The manager looked dissatisfied.



"You merchants are always cautious."



Lin smiled.



"And you workshop owners are always confident until the invoice arrives."



The manager laughed despite himself.



They agreed to prepare a revised cost estimate.



No order was accepted.



No deposit was received.



The two corrected bolts from the previous order remained scheduled for inspection with the next group.



Lin returned to her office and sent Mingzhao a short update.



The partnership was moving forward.



It was not moving quickly.



That was preferable to moving in the wrong direction.

That afternoon, Yuru returned to her temporary residence after the garden visit.



Fang Ru helped her remove the hat.



"Did the Empress Dowager enjoy the walk?"



"Yes."



"Did she ask you to review anything?"



"No."



Fang Ru looked surprised.



"Nothing?"



"She asked me about the trees."



"That is unusual."



"She also asked whether I preferred the eastern courtyard."



"And what did you say?"



"That I had not decided."



Fang Ru placed the hat on its stand.



"Perhaps you should decide soon."



Yuru looked at her.



"Why?"



"Because the palace may begin preparing for your departure from the temporary residence."



"They can wait until I decide where I want to live."



Fang Ru nodded.



She had expected that answer.



Yuru sat beside the window.



There was a letter from her father on the table.



The Duke had written that the eastern courtyard was available whenever she wished to use it.



He had also written that the household would not prepare another furniture arrangement.



Yuru smiled.



At least one matter had been settled.

In the early evening, Shen Mingzhao received a second letter from Mr Liang.



The family property meeting had been tentatively scheduled for the tenth day of the fifth month.



The independent accountant expected to complete the first stage of his review by then.



Mingzhao examined the date.



It was acceptable.



She wrote her confirmation.



She also requested that the eleven supporting documents be circulated in advance, with any new records clearly identified rather than inserted into the meeting bundle without explanation.



Qiao Yue watched her seal the letter.



"Will the meeting finally settle the property division?"



"Not necessarily."



"Then why attend?"



"Because the meeting may establish which parts are ready for settlement and which are not."



"That sounds like another unfinished result."



"It is better than pretending everything is finished."



Qiao Yue sighed.



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Would you prefer that we divide the estate without knowing what it contains?"



"No."



"Then you understand."



"I understand. I still dislike it."



Mingzhao almost smiled.



"That is permitted."



She put the letter aside.



The tenth day would bring another meeting.



Until then, she had other matters to attend to.



The temporary household needed its ordinary supplies.



The silk partnership needed its production costs reconciled.



The property dispute needed more records.



The grain investigation needed evidence.



And her personal claims needed to remain separate from the estate's obligations.



There was no shortage of work.



But none of it required her to return to the Marquisate and manage the entire household.



That distinction mattered.

At the palace, the Empress Dowager received a note from the Emperor.



It contained a single sentence:



*The former Empress appeared comfortable during the garden visit.*



The Dowager read it.



Then she looked at the paper.



"Why has he written this?"



Her attendant replied, "Perhaps His Majesty wished to inform Your Majesty."



"I saw her myself."



The attendant lowered her eyes.



The Dowager put the note aside.



"Men sometimes require considerable effort to discover that a person they have known for years is perfectly capable of telling them how she feels."



The attendant did not respond.



The Dowager picked up her tea.



"At least he did not send a report."

That night, Yuru read quietly in her residence.



Mingzhao reviewed the eleven documents once more.



Neither woman had made a permanent decision about her future home.



Neither had been asked to resume the responsibilities she had relinquished.



The palace had identified twenty-seven ceremonial objects and was still looking for five.



The Marquisate had prepared eleven documents and was still waiting for the independent accountant.



The northern canal property had acquired another historical map and another unanswered question.



The silk partnership had received an offer for two hundred bolts and had not accepted it.



Nothing was finished.



But several matters were clearer than they had been the day before.



In Yongjing, that was beginning to count as progress.



And somewhere in the imperial stores, five ceremonial objects remained unidentified.



They had not been lost.



They had simply not yet been found in the correct inventory.



The distinction was important.



The palace had learned to respect distinctions.



Mostly.
