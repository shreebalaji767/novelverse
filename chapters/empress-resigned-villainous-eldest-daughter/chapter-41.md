# **Chapter 41: The Former Empress Has Been Asked to Visit Her Family Again. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Measure a Boundary Again. Both Women Have Discovered That Repetition Is Not the Same as Progress.**

**3rd Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The owner of the northern canal property arrived twenty minutes late.



Mr Qian arrived fifteen minutes early.



Master Luo arrived precisely on time.



The three men therefore spent fifteen minutes standing in the same courtyard, each privately considering the punctuality of the others.



Shen Mingzhao arrived last.



This was intentional.



She had no desire to reward Mr Qian's early arrival by making him believe that his impatience could determine the schedule.



The property owner, a narrow-faced man named Zhou, greeted her with a bow.



"Miss Shen."



"Mr Zhou."



Mr Qian gave her a brief nod.



Master Luo had already opened his document case. His survey assistant stood beside him with a measuring rope, wooden stakes, and a small tool for checking the old boundary marks.



Mingzhao looked at the materials.



"Have the original registration documents arrived?"



Master Luo raised a hand towards his assistant.



The young man produced a wrapped bundle.



"These are the copies provided by the land office. The original registration record has not yet been released."



Mingzhao examined the first sheet.



The property had been registered several decades earlier. Its eastern and northern boundaries were described by reference to a drainage channel, an old stone marker, and the neighbouring parcel.



The wording was clear enough to identify the general location.



It was not clear enough to establish the exact line.



"Where is the stone marker?" she asked.



Mr Zhou pointed towards the rear of the property.



"It was there when my father purchased the house."



"Was it moved?"



"I do not know."



Mr Qian folded his arms.



"It was not moved by my family."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"I did not say it was."



"Your earlier inspection suggested the channel crossed into my land."



"My earlier inspection established that the channel extended beyond the line shown in the old registration description. It did not establish who owned the disputed strip."



Mr Qian looked dissatisfied.



"That is a convenient distinction."



"It is an important one."



Master Luo cleared his throat.



"If everyone is ready, we should begin with the registered points."



Mingzhao stepped aside.



"Please proceed."



The survey assistant began measuring from the southern corner. He checked the remaining visible markers and compared their positions with the old description.



The first measurement was straightforward.



The second required him to clear a section of weeds.



The third produced an argument.



Mr Qian insisted that an old tree had always marked the boundary. Mr Zhou said the tree had been planted by his father and had never been used as a legal marker.



The survey assistant measured the tree's position.



It did not correspond precisely with either man's account.



Master Luo made a note.



"An old tree is not a registered boundary marker."



Mr Qian frowned.



"It has stood there for years."



"So have several walls," Master Luo replied. "That does not make every wall a boundary."



Mingzhao watched the assistant record the measurement.



She was not interested in winning an argument about the tree.



She wanted to know what the land records established.



After another half hour, the survey assistant reached the drainage channel.



He measured the visible edge.



Then he measured the older line described in the registration record.



The two did not coincide.



He repeated the measurement.



The result remained the same.



Master Luo examined the notes.



"The drainage channel appears to extend beyond the older described line."



Mr Qian immediately spoke.



"Then it is on my land."



Mr Zhou objected.



"The channel was maintained by the previous owner."



"Maintenance does not establish ownership," Master Luo said.



Mingzhao looked at the survey assistant.



"Can you establish the area of the disputed strip?"



"Not conclusively from these documents. The old marker is missing, and the registered description refers to a feature that no longer exists in its original form."



"Can you establish the area that is not disputed?"



"Yes. I can prepare a preliminary measurement."



"Do so."



Mr Qian looked annoyed.



"Why continue measuring if the boundary is uncertain?"



"Because the undisputed portion is still part of the property."



"That does not settle the disagreement."



"No. It tells us what is not in disagreement."



Mr Qian stared at her.



Mingzhao remained composed.



The survey assistant continued.



By midday, they had a preliminary measurement of the undisputed area and a separate description of the disputed strip.



The result was useful.



It was not a final boundary determination.



Master Luo closed his document case.



"I recommend obtaining the older registration map from the land office and comparing it with the neighbouring parcel's record. If the records conflict, a formal boundary determination may be necessary."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Please prepare the preliminary measurements and list the documents still required."



Mr Zhou looked towards her.



"Does this mean you are still interested in purchasing?"



"It means I am still examining the property."



"Would you be willing to pay a deposit while the matter is settled?"



"No."



His expression tightened.



"The house will not remain available indefinitely."



"Then you may sell it to someone else."



The words were delivered without anger.



Mr Zhou looked at her for a moment, then lowered his eyes.



Mingzhao did not ask him to wait.



She had no intention of paying money for land whose boundaries remained uncertain.



On the way out, Qiao Yue leaned towards her.



"Miss, I think Mr Zhou expected you to agree."



"He expected me to be afraid of losing the property."



"Are you not?"



"No."



"Even after all the time spent inspecting it?"



Mingzhao looked back at the courtyard.



"It is a house. It is not the last house in Yongjing."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Then she added, "Although it may be the last house with so many people arguing about a drainage channel."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"That is possible."

At the Xu family residence, the steward had reduced the furniture arrangements from three to one.



Unfortunately, he had chosen the largest.



Xu Yuru found it occupying most of the study.



The writing desk stood near the window. A long couch had been placed against the western wall. Two bookcases stood opposite one another, and a decorative screen had been installed between the study and the adjoining room.



Yuru examined the arrangement.



Madam Xu sat beside the tea table.



"The steward said this was the most practical arrangement."



"For whom?"



Madam Xu smiled.



"That is what I asked him."



Yuru walked around the desk.



The desk was well made, but too large for the room. The couch blocked part of the passage. The screen reduced the available light.



She looked at the steward.



"Did the household accountant provide the breakdown for the twelve taels?"



"Yes, Madam."



He presented the revised sheet.



The amount covered replacement curtain cords, two new cushions, additional cleaning materials, and the repair of a wooden cabinet.



Yuru read the list.



"Why are the cushions included?"



"The steward believed the existing ones might not be comfortable enough."



"Have they been inspected?"



"Not yet."



"Then remove them from the estimate."



The steward bowed.



"And the curtain cords?"



"The existing cords are worn."



"That is a reasonable repair. Keep it."



"The cleaning materials?"



"Are they included in the ordinary household supply account?"



The steward hesitated.



"They may be."



"Then check before charging them separately."



The steward bowed again.



Madam Xu watched Yuru with an expression of restrained amusement.



When the steward had left, she said, "You have not been here for a full morning, and the household has already received three instructions."



"Only two."



"You asked the steward to remove the cushions."



"That was one instruction."



"You asked him to check the cleaning materials."



"That was another."



"And you asked him to keep the curtain cords."



"That was a classification."



Madam Xu laughed.



Yuru sat down.



She had not intended to inspect the accounts in detail. Yet when someone presented an expense estimate, she naturally wanted to understand what it contained.



The difference was that she could now stop when she wished.



She did not need to supervise the repair.



She did not need to check whether the cushions were eventually purchased.



She did not need to receive a report when the curtain cords were replaced.



She could simply ask the question and leave the work to the people responsible.



It was a small difference.



It was also a difficult one.



Madam Xu poured tea.



"Your father has asked whether you will visit again next week."



"I would like to."



"Would you like him to prepare the eastern courtyard?"



Yuru considered the question.



"Not yet."



"Then what would you like?"



"Nothing special. I would rather visit without the household preparing an event."



Madam Xu nodded.



"I will tell him."



Yuru lifted her cup.



After a moment, Madam Xu asked, "Have you considered where you will live permanently?"



"Not seriously."



"Would you prefer to remain near the palace?"



"I do not know."



"Would you prefer to live here?"



"I do not know that either."



Madam Xu looked at her daughter.



"Are you unhappy with the choices?"



"No. I am simply not ready to choose."



Madam Xu accepted this.



She had expected Yuru to have a plan for everything.



Perhaps that expectation had been unfair.



"Then you can take your time," she said.



Yuru looked at her.



"Thank you."



The two women drank tea.



For a while, no one brought them a document.



It was a pleasant afternoon.

The Emperor received the preliminary report from the Imperial Household Administration shortly after noon.



The report was shorter than the previous one.



It separated routine matters from matters requiring a decision.



He read the first page.



The replacement of ordinary kitchen equipment had been assigned to the relevant stores office.



The eastern corridor lamps had been classified by their primary use, with ceremonial exceptions recorded separately.



The Crown Prince's Eastern Palace had submitted revised reporting forms.



The Second Prince's household expenditure had been separated from the expenses of his personal attendants.



The third princess's educational expenditure remained under review because the existing records did not distinguish between books purchased for her private study and books held in the shared palace library.



The Emperor reached the final page.



There was no reference to the former Empress.



He looked at Gao Shun.



"How many matters remain unresolved?"



"Seven items require additional records. Four require a decision from the relevant office. None currently requires Your Majesty's intervention."



Zhao Jingheng looked at the report again.



"Are you certain?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then why did you bring it to me?"



Gao Shun bowed.



"Because Your Majesty requested the monthly summary."



The Emperor paused.



He had.



He had also requested that the summary distinguish between matters requiring his attention and matters that did not.



He looked at the first page again.



"Leave it."



Gao Shun placed the report on the desk.



The Emperor considered the difference between being informed and being required to act.



It was not a distinction he had previously needed to make often.



Now he was learning that it mattered.



"Has the Crown Prince submitted his revised forms?"



"Yes."



"Did he ask the former Empress to review them?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



Gao Shun waited.



The Emperor turned to another document.



"Has the third princess's library account been clarified?"



"Not yet."



"Then the relevant office should continue reviewing it."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



There was a brief silence.



Then Zhao Jingheng asked, "Is the former Empress visiting her family today?"



"She visited yesterday, Your Majesty."



"And today?"



"I have received no information."



The Emperor nodded.



He had not meant to ask for a report.



He had merely asked a question.



He decided not to send another message.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received a reply from the supplier concerning the cracked lamp oil jar.



The supplier confirmed that one jar had been replaced before delivery.



The replacement had been recorded in the supplier's own book, but the household copy had not been updated.



Mingxiu read the note.



"So the quantity is correct."



"The original delivery record was incomplete."



"Then we should attach the supplier's confirmation."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Yes."



She took the note.



"Should I place it in the kitchen account?"



"Place it with the purchase record and add a cross-reference to the kitchen account."



Mingxiu looked at him.



"You have learned to use cross-references."



"I have learned that people keep asking the same question when records are difficult to find."



"That is progress."



Mingyuan did not smile.



He was looking at another paper.



The duplicate firewood transport charge remained unresolved.



Two payments had been recorded for what might have been one delivery. The supplier had not yet produced a second delivery receipt.



Mingxiu followed his gaze.



"Have you asked the supplier again?"



"Yes. He says the second payment was for another journey."



"Can he identify the date?"



"He has given us a date, but it does not match the household receiving record."



"Then the matter remains incomplete."



Mingyuan nodded.



He had once been inclined to accept explanations that sounded reasonable.



Now he found himself wanting to know whether the dates matched.



It was an inconvenient habit.



He was beginning to understand why his elder sister had insisted on records.



Madam Liang entered carrying a small household account book.



"Have you finished the lamp oil matter?"



"Yes, Mother."



"And the firewood?"



"Not yet."



Madam Liang looked towards the papers.



"Must every small purchase be examined so carefully?"



Mingxiu answered before her brother could.



"Not every purchase. Only the ones where the records disagree."



Madam Liang looked at her daughter.



"That is a great deal of work."



"It is less work than arguing about the same payment next month."



Madam Liang did not respond.



She placed the household account book on the table.



"The kitchen has requested a replacement pot."



Mingxiu opened the book.



"Which pot?"



"The large one."



"Was it repaired?"



"One of the servants said it could be repaired."



"Then why is it being replaced?"



Madam Liang frowned.



"Because the kitchen says it is inconvenient."



Mingxiu looked at the entry.



There was no repair estimate and no note from the kitchen storekeeper.



She closed the book.



"I will ask for the inspection note."



Madam Liang looked dissatisfied.



"Your sister would have known what to do."



Mingyuan's expression changed.



Mingxiu did not look up.



"Then we should not use that as a reason to avoid learning."



The room became quiet.



Madam Liang looked at her daughter for a long moment.



Then she turned towards the door.



"Do what you think necessary."



After she left, Mingyuan returned to the firewood records.



Mingxiu remained standing.



Neither sibling mentioned their sister.



They did not need to.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao returned from the canal property shortly before the evening meal.



She placed the preliminary survey notes on her desk.



Qiao Yue removed the outer document wrapper.



"Will Master Luo send the measurements tomorrow?"



"He said he would prepare them after comparing the available registration descriptions."



"And the owner?"



"He wants an answer."



"Will you give him one?"



"When I have enough information."



Qiao Yue looked at the notes.



"The undisputed portion was measured."



"Yes."



"The disputed portion was not."



"Correct."



"Then the property is still uncertain."



"That is why I did not purchase it."



Mingzhao took out her personal ledger.



She recorded the day's expenses: carriage hire, the survey assistant's agreed fee, and the cost of obtaining copies of the registration descriptions.



The expenses were entered separately from the family settlement accounts and the silk partnership account.



She checked the total.



Then she checked it again.



Qiao Yue watched.



"Miss, is the amount wrong?"



"No."



"Then why are you checking it again?"



"Because I want to know whether it is correct."



Qiao Yue sat down.



"Could you not trust the first calculation?"



"I could."



"Then why do it twice?"



Mingzhao put down her brush.



"Because I have learned that trusting a calculation does not make it correct."



Qiao Yue nodded.



That answer was difficult to argue with.



A messenger arrived before the evening meal.



This time the message came from Lin Shuyun.



The remaining two silk bolts had completed another finishing inspection.



One had passed.



The other still showed uneven dye near the edge and required correction.



Lin Shuyun had not approved the second bolt for delivery.



The buyer, Zhou Wenqi, had been informed that the inspection was incomplete.



The accepted forty bolts remained recorded as accepted: thirty-two at the full agreed price and eight at the adjusted rate.



The two remaining bolts had not been added to the accepted quantity.



Production costs remained outstanding.



Mingzhao read the letter.



"Lin Shuyun is being careful."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Will the buyer be angry?"



"Possibly."



"Then why not send the second bolt?"



"Because the buyer agreed to inspect the goods. Sending a defective bolt would create a different problem."



"Even if it delays payment?"



"Especially if it delays payment."



Mingzhao wrote a reply approving the additional correction.



She also asked Lin Shuyun to record the extra finishing cost separately, so that it could be distinguished from the original production expenditure.



When she finished, she sealed the letter.



The household was quiet.



The rear drain had been repaired.



The lower room remained dry after the previous cleaning, though it would need to be observed after another heavy rain.



The landlord had not yet asked about extending the lease.



Mingzhao had not decided whether she wanted to remain beyond the agreed three months.



There were still many matters to settle.



But she was no longer waiting for the Marquisate to decide what she should do next.



That was a considerable improvement.

Late that evening, Yuru received a short letter from the Empress Dowager.



It contained no official seal.



The Dowager had written that she intended to visit the imperial gardens the following morning and asked whether Yuru wished to accompany her.



The letter also mentioned that the third princess had asked for another lesson in reviewing household accounts.



Yuru read it twice.



She placed the letter on the table.



Then she wrote a reply accepting the garden visit and agreeing to speak with Minglan.



She added one sentence:



*I will offer advice if asked, but the princess should continue working with the offices responsible for her accounts.*



She sealed the reply.



Outside, the night air was cool.



The palace was quiet, though not entirely still. Somewhere beyond the residence, servants were completing the final preparations for the following morning.



Yuru set aside her writing brush.



She had spent years believing that if she stopped paying attention, something would go wrong.



Now she was learning that other people could pay attention too.



Not perfectly.



Not without mistakes.



But they could learn.



And perhaps that was the purpose of leaving.



At the northern canal property, a boundary remained uncertain.



At the Marquisate, two siblings had corrected one account and left another unfinished.



At the palace, seven matters still required additional records.



At the Xu residence, one courtyard awaited a decision.



The fifth month had barely begun.



Nobody had solved everything.



For once, nobody had been asked to.
