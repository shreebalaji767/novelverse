# **Chapter 116: The Former Empress Has Discovered That Accepting a Family Invitation Does Not Require Choosing a Permanent Residence. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That a Second Sample May Be Better Than the First Without Becoming the Same as the Original. The Marquis's Heir Has Discovered That a Cart's Return to the Main Yard Can Be Recorded Without Anyone Remembering Who Brought It Back.**

**20th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The former Empress's reply to her aunt was brief.

She accepted the invitation to visit at the end of the month and thanked her for remembering her.

Fang Ru read the address before sealing the letter.

"Shall I ask the Duke's residence to send a carriage?"

Yuru looked up from the garden book.

"Not yet. The date is still several days away."

"Would you prefer to use the palace carriage?"

"I have not decided."

Fang Ru placed the letter in the dispatch tray.

Yuru returned to the page before her. The author was describing the use of trellises to support climbing plants. The explanation was detailed, though the diagrams were less clear than she would have liked.

She read the passage twice.

Then she closed the book.

"Fang Ru, have you chosen another book?"

"Not yet, Madam."

"Why not?"

"I have not finished the first one."

Yuru glanced at the book on the table.

"How much have you read?"

"Three chapters."

"And how many remain?"

"Five."

"Then you have made a beginning."

Fang Ru hesitated.

"I thought I should finish it before choosing another."

"You may do so if you wish. But you need not treat the library as an examination."

Fang Ru smiled faintly.

"Yes, Madam."

Yuru rose and walked toward the window.

The courtyard was quiet. The gardener was speaking to a junior attendant near the water jars. A small delivery had arrived, and the two men were checking the number of clay pots before carrying them to the storage corner.

Yuru watched for a moment.

She had once been responsible for ensuring that such deliveries were properly received, recorded, and distributed.

Now the attendant was checking the pots with the gardener.

Neither had asked her to confirm the count.

She turned away from the window.

"Would you like to walk with me later?" she asked Fang Ru.

"Yes, Madam."

"Then finish your reading first."

Fang Ru looked down at her book.

Yuru returned to her seat.

There was no urgency.

At the workshop, the supplier's second dyed sample arrived before noon.

Mingzhao examined it beside the original reference piece.

The colour was closer.

The difference was no longer obvious at first glance, but it remained visible when the cloth was placed directly beside the reference.

Luo Shun examined both.

"This is much better."

"Yes."

"Would it be acceptable?"

"Perhaps."

The foreman waited.

Mingzhao took the samples to the window. The daylight was brighter there, and the difference became clearer.

She then brought them back to the table and examined the weave.

The second sample had been dyed using the same material batch as the first. The supplier had altered the mixture, but the written note did not give the exact proportions.

Mingzhao read the note.

"He says the colour is now within the usual range."

"Is that sufficient?"

"No. We did not agree to the usual range. We agreed to a reference."

Luo Shun nodded.

"Should we reject it?"

"Not yet."

She placed both samples on the table.

"Ask the supplier whether he can produce a third sample using this mixture and whether he can guarantee that the bulk dyeing will follow the same process."

"He may not guarantee an exact match."

"Then ask him what variation he will accept responsibility for."

Luo Shun considered this.

"That will require a written agreement."

"Yes."

"Would the supplier agree?"

"We shall find out."

The foreman gathered the sample note.

Mingzhao stopped him.

"Do not return the second sample."

"Should we keep it?"

"Yes. It is part of the record. Mark it as the second sample, not the approved sample."

Luo Shun wrote the label.

Mingzhao checked the production book.

The trial remained unapproved.

No material had been purchased.

No workers had begun production.

The workshop's ordinary work was proceeding separately, and the existing reserve remained untouched.

She had no wish to make a small experiment more expensive simply because she had already spent time considering it.

"Miss Shen," the foreman said, "if the supplier cannot guarantee the colour, should we approach the other merchant?"

"Which other merchant?"

"The one who supplied the earlier reference cloth."

Mingzhao considered the possibility.

"Ask whether he can provide the same material and dye. Do not tell him that we have decided to purchase."

"Yes."

She signed the note and returned it.

The supplier's second sample was better.

That was all it had established.

The fourth box at the old accounts storehouse contained a register that Mingyuan had not expected to find.

It was a record of repairs and returns for carts belonging to several transport offices.

The cover was worn, but the pages were mostly intact.

Mingyuan read the heading.

"Is this the main yard register?"

The storehouse keeper nodded.

"Yes, Young Master. It records repairs, inspections, and returns. It was kept separately from the dispatch records."

Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou.

"Then we should compare it with the assignment register."

The two registers were placed side by side.

The clerk began checking the four cart numbers.

Three appeared in the return register.

The entries recorded the date, the condition of each cart, and the name of the receiving yard clerk.

The fourth cart appeared several days later.

Its return entry was incomplete.

The date was present, but the receiving clerk's name had been written over an earlier entry. The original characters could no longer be read.

Mingyuan examined the page.

"Is there a correction mark?"

The clerk looked closely.

"Yes. The ink is darker in the name column."

"Does the register record who made the correction?"

"No."

Mingyuan leaned closer.

"Then record that the name was altered and that the person responsible is not identified."

Steward Zhou examined the entry.

"At least the fourth cart returned."

"According to this register, yes."

"Does that help establish where it went?"

"It establishes that the cart was recorded as returned. It does not establish where it travelled before returning."

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan turned the page.

There was a separate entry for repairs to the fourth cart. The repair concerned a damaged axle and a replacement wheel fitting.

The date was close to the return entry.

"Does this repair correspond to the one in the assignment register?"

The clerk compared the amounts.

"The costs are similar, but the descriptions are not identical."

"Could they refer to the same repair?"

"They could."

"Could they be separate repairs?"

"Yes."

Mingyuan looked at the two registers.

"Then we need the repair receipts."

Steward Zhou glanced at the bundle list.

"The repair receipts may be in the fifth box."

"Then record the question and continue."

Mingyuan turned back to the return entry.

There was another detail.

The three other carts had been received by the same yard clerk. The fourth had a different date and an altered name.

"Was this clerk still employed at the time?"

The storehouse keeper checked the roster.

"His name appears in the northern office list."

"Can we identify him?"

The clerk searched the attached roster.

"His name is recorded as Gao Shun."

Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou.

"Is this the same Gao Shun who served in the household's transport office?"

Steward Zhou shook his head.

"No. The name is common. This man was a yard clerk, not the palace attendant."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Record his full office designation. We must not confuse them."

The clerk wrote it down.

The fourth box had provided a return entry, a repair reference, and the name of a clerk whose handwriting might have been altered.

It had not provided the cargo or destination.

Mingyuan was beginning to understand why the previous investigation had stalled.

Each document described a narrow part of the work. None had been designed to tell the entire story.

Mingzhao received the latest household inquiry in the afternoon.

This time, it did not concern the four-cart dispatch.

It concerned the seventy-two taels spent on repairs to her former study.

The Marquisate's accounts office had located a maintenance entry showing that the estate had paid for some work in the same room during an earlier year.

The letter asked whether Mingzhao could identify the repairs she had paid for personally and explain how they differed from the estate's earlier expenditure.

Mingzhao read the question carefully.

She remembered the repairs.

The study had required work on the floorboards, the window frame, and part of the ceiling. The earlier estate expenditure had concerned a different repair, completed before she had taken charge of the room's maintenance.

She did not intend to rely on memory alone.

She opened her old personal account book.

The entry listed the carpenter, the date, the amount, and the materials.

She also found a separate note identifying the work performed.

Mingzhao compared the dates.

The two expenditures were separated by more than a year.

She wrote a reply explaining the distinction and attached a copy of the personal account entry.

She did not state that the Marquisate must reimburse her immediately.

The amount remained a personal expenditure for which reimbursement had not yet been decided.

Qiao Yue watched her finish the letter.

"Will they accept the explanation?"

"They may."

"Would you prefer to meet them and explain it?"

"Not unless they have a question that cannot be answered in writing."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao placed the reply in the dispatch tray.

The property division was still incomplete, but at least the family had begun asking questions that could be answered with records.

She returned to the workshop account.

The second dyed sample remained on the table.

She compared it once more with the reference.

The difference was small.

The difference was still there.

She put both pieces away.

At the palace, Yuru received a note from her father.

It was not a request to visit.

It was a short account of a book he had recently read, followed by a question about whether she had finished the two volumes she had borrowed from him.

Yuru smiled.

She had not.

She wrote a reply explaining that she had finished the first volume and was still reading the second. She added that she would return both when she next visited the family residence.

Fang Ru placed the reply in the dispatch tray.

"Does your father often ask about books?"

"Often."

"Does he expect you to finish them quickly?"

Yuru thought about it.

"He expects me to read them."

"That sounds similar."

"It is not."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru looked at the two borrowed volumes on the shelf.

Her father had given her three months to return them. There was still time.

She had not forgotten the books.

She had simply stopped treating them as urgent.

The garden book lay open on the table.

Yuru returned to it and read a section about placing stepping stones along a damp path.

The author recommended leaving enough space between them for a natural stride.

Yuru glanced toward the courtyard.

The path there had been laid with uneven stones many years earlier. She had never particularly noticed the spacing.

Now she did.

She did not call the gardener.

She merely closed the book and went to prepare for the evening meal.

Mingyuan received the copies of the fourth box's records before dusk.

He read the return entries and the repair note.

Three carts had been received by the same yard clerk. The fourth had a later return entry with an altered receiving name. The repair register contained a similar but not identical cost.

The old transport records had not yet established whether the four carts had returned together or separately.

Nor had they established whether the damaged axle had occurred during the recorded dispatch.

Mingyuan looked at the summary.

"Have we found the repair receipts?"

"Not yet," Liang Wen replied. "The fifth box has not been opened."

"Then add the repair receipts to tomorrow's examination list."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan considered the altered name.

"Can we compare the handwriting with the yard clerk's other entries?"

"Yes. There are several pages written by Gao Shun."

"Make copies of the relevant pages. Do not ask him to identify his own handwriting until we have compared the records."

"Understood."

Steward Zhou stood beside the desk.

"Young Master, should we ask Gao Shun to attend?"

"Not yet. First establish whether the entry was altered by him or someone else."

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan placed the papers beside the earlier assignment register.

He now had three connected records:

- the temporary allocation list;
- the main yard return register;
- the repair register.

Together, they suggested that the four carts had been assigned to the northern transport office and later recorded as returned.

They still did not reveal what the carts had carried.

He looked at the grain discrepancy schedule.

Twenty-seven sacks remained unexplained.

He did not add the carts to the grain inquiry.

The evidence did not justify it.

Mingyuan signed the day's inventory.

"Tomorrow we open the fifth box."

Liang Wen bowed.

When the room had emptied, Mingyuan remained at his desk.

The family had once expected him to resolve the accounts quickly because he was the heir. Now he understood that the title did not make missing documents appear, nor did it give him the right to turn uncertain entries into convenient answers.

He had become more patient.

He had also become less popular with people who preferred quick conclusions.

This seemed an acceptable exchange.

He closed the file.

There were two boxes left after tomorrow.

The northern dispatch book was still missing.

The cart records were becoming clearer.

And somewhere in the Marquisate's old accounts, a repair receipt might explain why one cart had returned later than the other three.

Mingyuan hoped it would.

He also hoped it would not turn out to be another receipt for an entirely different cart.

The records, unfortunately, had shown no concern for his hopes.
