# **Chapter 22: The Empress Has Ten Days Left. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Prove That Her Own Money Is Actually Hers.**



**14th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first problem with leaving the Marquisate was that Shen Mingzhao had spent six years making it difficult to determine which things belonged to her.



This was not because she had deliberately mixed her property with the family estate.



It was because the family had found it convenient to treat her personal resources as available household resources whenever necessary.



A merchant needed an advance.



The estate needed repairs.



A relative required a temporary loan.



A shipment arrived before the household's monthly accounts were settled.



Mingzhao had paid for some of these things from her own funds, expecting reimbursement. Other expenses had been recorded as advances. A few had been treated as gifts because she had not insisted on repayment at the time.



The distinction had seemed unimportant when she lived in the Marquisate.



It had become extremely important now.



At breakfast, Sun Qiming placed three ledgers before her.



"Miss Shen, I have separated the entries into three groups."



Mingzhao looked at them.



"Explain."



"The first contains payments made directly from your personal account, with supporting receipts or merchant acknowledgments."



He opened the first ledger.



"The second contains payments made from your personal account for which the family estate later provided partial reimbursement."



He opened the second.



"The third contains payments for which the purpose is recorded, but the source of funds or repayment arrangement is uncertain."



Mingzhao examined the entries.



"How many uncertain entries?"



"Twenty-three."



She frowned.



"That many?"



"Some are small."



"Small amounts are still amounts."



Sun Qiming nodded.



"I have not included them in the confirmed claims."



Mingzhao turned a page.



One entry concerned the repair of a section of the eastern estate wall.



Another concerned the purchase of grain seed.



A third was a payment to a travelling physician who had treated several household servants during an illness.



Mingzhao remembered the physician.



She also remembered paying him.



She did not remember whether the estate had later reimbursed her.



"Keep these separate. I will examine my own correspondence."



Sun Qiming wrote it down.



"Should I include them in the preliminary valuation?"



"Include them as disputed or unverified advances. Do not include them as confirmed assets."



"Understood."



Mingzhao leaned back.



The process was becoming tedious.



She had expected that.



What she had not expected was how much of her own financial history had been kept in other people's records.



She had managed the estate.



She had not managed the documentation of every personal transaction.



That was an error she would not repeat.



At Chengming Palace, the Empress was examining a list of possessions.



Fang Ru had prepared it carefully.



The list included furniture, books, clothing, writing materials, ornaments, and household objects.



Some belonged to the imperial household.



Others had been presented to Yuru personally.



A few had been purchased from her private funds before her marriage.



Yuru read the list.



"Why is the second writing table marked as an imperial household asset?"



Fang Ru checked.



"It was purchased for the palace study."



"Then it should remain here."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And the books?"



"Most were acquired for Chengming Palace. The books marked in red were Your Majesty's personal purchases."



Yuru looked at the red markings.



There were fewer than she expected.



She had accumulated books over the years, but many had been purchased through the palace's educational and administrative budgets.



She would not remove them merely because she had read them.



"These should remain with the palace library."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru continued.



"What about the small collection in my private room?"



"Those are recorded as personal purchases."



"Good."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"Your Majesty, there is also the matter of the servants."



Yuru looked up.



"Have they decided?"



"Some wish to remain. Others have asked whether they may accompany Your Majesty."



"Have their circumstances been examined?"



"Not yet."



"Then do not promise anyone a position."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru put down the list.



"I do not intend to take the entire Chengming Palace with me."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



"Of course, Your Majesty."



Yuru looked at her.



"That was not a criticism."



"I understand."



"Do you?"



Fang Ru gave a small smile.



"I believe I do."



Yuru returned to the document.



The palace was not a personal household in the ordinary sense.



Its servants had appointments, responsibilities, and established arrangements. Some had served the Empress for decades, but that did not mean they belonged to her.



She would leave with her personal possessions.



She would not dismantle an institution to make her departure easier.



The Emperor's morning meeting was less peaceful.



The Imperial Household Administration had submitted a report on the remaining responsibilities.



The first page showed the completed transfers.



The second showed duties still under observation.



The third contained questions about ceremonial authority.



Zhao Jingheng read the report.



"Why is the monthly review of the imperial children's educational expenditure still listed under Chengming Palace?"



Madam Zhao answered.



"The office has begun receiving the reports directly, Your Majesty. The entry remains because the first monthly reconciliation has not yet been completed."



"Then mark it as transferred and note that the first reconciliation is pending."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor continued.



"Why is the review of senior attendants' household allowances still assigned to the Empress's office?"



Fang Ru was not present. Madam Zhao answered on behalf of the administration.



"The relevant regulation gives the Empress authority to supervise certain senior household arrangements."



"Does it require her to personally examine every allowance?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Then why is it written that way?"



Madam Zhao paused.



"The previous practice was to send the consolidated list to Chengming Palace."



"That is not an answer."



"No, Your Majesty."



He placed the report down.



"Who is responsible for the allowances?"



"The Imperial Household Administration."



"Who verifies the figures?"



"The financial office."



"Who decides disputes?"



"That depends on the nature of the dispute, Your Majesty."



"Then write that."



Madam Zhao bowed.



The Emperor looked at the officials around the table.



"Do not preserve a habit by disguising it as a regulation."



Nobody answered.



He had begun to understand that the palace's greatest administrative problem was not the absence of rules.



It was the number of people who preferred not to use them.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao was summoned to the family hall.



Her father had arranged for a property assessor to attend.



The assessor, Master Luo, was a middle-aged man with experience valuing land, buildings, warehouses, and commercial properties.



He bowed to Shen Guowei before taking his seat.



Mr. Liang, the clan representative, was already present.



Sun Qiming sat beside Mingzhao.



Mr. Chen had prepared the preliminary property schedule.



Shen Guowei began.



"We must establish the value of the family estate before discussing the division."



Mingzhao nodded.



"That is necessary."



Master Luo opened his notes.



"The Marquisate's property includes agricultural land, residential buildings, storage facilities, commercial holdings, and several investments."



He placed a summary on the table.



"The first difficulty is that the family estate does not have one simple market value."



Shen Guowei frowned.



"Why not?"



"Because land, buildings, and commercial assets must be valued according to their nature. A warehouse may have a property value, but the goods stored inside it are separate assets. A commercial partnership may have a value based on its capital and obligations, not simply the amount of money currently held."



Mingzhao looked at her father.



"This is why I requested a complete valuation."



Shen Guowei did not respond.



Master Luo continued.



"Some agricultural land is leased. Some is cultivated directly. Several buildings require repairs. The northern warehouse has unresolved inventory questions."



"Will that reduce the value?" Shen Guowei asked.



"It may affect the valuation, depending on the condition and the evidence."



"How much?"



"I cannot give a reliable figure before inspecting the properties."



Shen Guowei looked dissatisfied.



Mingzhao almost smiled.



The family had spent years expecting her to produce numbers whenever someone asked.



Now that an independent assessor was involved, the numbers required actual work.



"Which properties will you inspect first?" she asked.



"The principal estate, the northern warehouse, and the agricultural holdings."



"Will you inspect the commercial properties separately?"



"Yes."



"Will the inventory be valued independently of the buildings?"



"Yes."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then the schedule is acceptable."



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"Do you have any objection to the assessor's method?"



"No. I have objections to incomplete records, not to proper valuation."



Master Luo lowered his eyes.



He was beginning to understand why the family meetings had become so long.



The inspection of the northern warehouse took place that afternoon.



Steward Zhou accompanied Master Luo, Sun Qiming, and Mingzhao.



The warehouse stood near the northern edge of the estate, close to a road used by grain carts.



Its roof had been repaired recently.



The old timber beams were visible beneath the newer sections.



Master Luo examined the structure.



"Who authorized the repairs?"



Steward Zhou answered.



"The Marquis ordered maintenance after water entered the eastern storage area."



"Was the work completed?"



"Yes."



"Do you have the work order?"



Steward Zhou looked at Sun Qiming.



"We have the payment record and the maintenance ledger. The original work order has not yet been located."



Sun Qiming made a note.



Master Luo continued inspecting the building.



He examined the walls, doors, roof supports, and drainage.



Then he asked to see the storage records.



Steward Zhou brought out the available ledgers.



Sun Qiming opened them.



"The original discrepancy was forty-seven sacks."



Master Luo looked up.



"I was told eighteen were later confirmed in the southern storage ledger."



"Correct."



"And twenty were covered by a movement instruction?"



"Correct."



"Two remain without confirmed receipt, and twenty-seven remain unexplained."



Master Luo examined the movement instruction.



"Does it identify the person who ordered the transfer?"



"Yes. The Marquis."



Mingzhao looked at the document.



Her father had already admitted that he instructed the movement.



The instruction itself did not explain the destination of every sack.



Master Luo continued.



"Does it identify the carrier?"



"The transport payment record identifies a contractor."



"Then he should be asked to confirm the delivery."



Sun Qiming nodded.



"We have requested his records."



Master Luo returned to the building inspection.



"Until the inventory is reconciled, I will value the warehouse structure separately from the grain stock."



Mingzhao approved.



"That is correct."



Steward Zhou looked relieved.



He had feared that the unresolved grain would prevent the property valuation entirely.



At least the building itself could be examined.



The transport contractor arrived near the end of the afternoon.



His name was Liu Shun.



He had operated carts for the Marquisate for several years and had transported grain, timber, and household supplies.



He bowed to Shen Mingzhao and Steward Zhou.



Sun Qiming placed the transport payment record before him.



"Do you recognize this entry?"



Liu Shun examined it.



"Yes. I received payment for transporting grain during the warehouse repairs."



"Do you remember how much grain?"



Liu Shun frowned.



"It was several loads."



"Do you have a delivery record?"



"I kept a small notebook, but I do not have it with me."



"Can you bring it?"



"Yes."



Sun Qiming waited.



Liu Shun looked uncomfortable.



"Sir, I cannot promise that the quantity will be recorded. At the time, I was paid for the carts and journeys, not for counting individual sacks."



"Then do not claim that you counted them."



"I did not."



"Did you deliver the grain to the southern storage facility?"



"I remember going there."



"Do you remember how many sacks you delivered?"



Liu Shun hesitated.



"I cannot say."



Mingzhao watched him.



"Do you remember whether any sacks were unloaded elsewhere?"



Liu Shun thought for a moment.



"There may have been a stop near the outer road. I remember waiting while the carts were rearranged."



"Who gave the instruction?"



"I do not remember."



"Was the grain unloaded?"



"I cannot say."



Sun Qiming wrote down the answers.



Mingzhao asked no further questions.



Liu Shun had not confirmed the missing sacks.



He had not denied transporting them.



His testimony established only that he had participated in grain transport during the repair period.



That was useful.



It was not enough.



"Bring the notebook tomorrow," she said.



Liu Shun bowed.



"Yes, Miss."



After he left, Steward Zhou looked at the record.



"At least we know he transported grain."



"We already knew that from the payment ledger," Mingzhao replied.



"Then we have not learned much."



"We have learned that he remembers a possible stop near the outer road. That is something to examine."



Sun Qiming closed his notebook.



"It is not proof of delivery."



"I know."



Mingzhao looked toward the warehouse.



The missing sacks were still missing.



The records were still incomplete.



But the investigation had moved one step beyond the ledger.



That evening, Shen Mingyuan came to see her.



He carried a small bundle of papers.



"I found these in the old study cabinet."



Mingzhao looked at the bundle.



"What are they?"



"Receipts and notes. Some concern the study repairs. Others are ordinary purchases."



She accepted them.



"Did you examine them?"



"Yes."



"Did you identify which ones concern the three hundred taels?"



"Some."



He placed the papers on the table.



"Here are the records for the seventy-two taels. I found a note from the steward confirming that the carpenter was paid."



Mingzhao examined it.



The note was not the original work order.



It was an acknowledgment.



She passed it to Sun Qiming, who had remained in the adjoining room.



He examined the document.



"This supports the payment, but it does not establish every detail of the work."



Mingyuan nodded.



"I understand."



He looked at Mingzhao.



"There are other expenses. I may have used some money for my own rooms. I cannot remember exactly how much."



Mingzhao did not look surprised.



"Then list what you remember."



"What if I remember incorrectly?"



"Then mark it as uncertain."



"What if the amount is wrong?"



"Then do not invent an amount."



Mingyuan looked at the papers.



For a moment, he seemed almost embarrassed.



"I used to think that because the estate paid for everything, I did not need to know where the money came from."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"That was convenient."



He gave a short, uncomfortable laugh.



"Yes."



She returned to the papers.



"These records will be examined. If they establish that you owe money personally, it will be recorded. If they establish that the expense belonged to the estate, it will remain an estate expense."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Thank you."



Mingzhao looked up.



"Do not thank me for requiring an accurate account."



He left with a quieter expression than when he had arrived.



Mingzhao continued examining the papers.



The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels were still unverified.



The new documents might explain some of them.



They might also explain nothing.



She would not decide before the examination.



At Chengming Palace, the Third Princess was having a different kind of difficulty.



Minglan had received a list of household expenditures from the educational office.



She had been asked to review the format before the next monthly report.



She brought it to Yuru.



"Mother, I think this list is wrong."



Yuru examined it.



"Which part?"



"The total."



She looked at the figures.



The princess had noticed that two entries had been counted twice.



Yuru checked the original records.



"You are correct."



Minglan looked pleased.



"Then I should send it back?"



"Yes. But do not simply write that the total is wrong. Identify the duplicated entries and ask the office to correct the original record."



Minglan nodded.



"Should I also tell them to change the format?"



"Not unless the format caused the mistake."



The princess looked at the list again.



"I think I understand."



Yuru smiled.



"Good."



Minglan hesitated.



"Mother, after the ceremony, will I still be allowed to bring you these?"



"If you wish to ask me for advice, yes."



"Even if the matter concerns the palace?"



"If it is something I can answer without interfering with the authority of the responsible office."



Minglan considered this.



"That sounds complicated."



"It is."



"Will you explain the difference every time?"



"At first, perhaps."



Minglan sighed.



Yuru looked at her.



"That is what learning is."



The princess reluctantly accepted this.



Later, Yuru received a report from the Department of Rites.



The formal ceremony had been scheduled.



The required officials had been notified.



The imperial family had received the announcement.



The seals would be counted before being returned.



Yuru read that final line twice.



She looked at Fang Ru.



"Have they decided who will count them?"



"The Imperial Archives will send an official."



"Good."



"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites also asks whether the seals should be counted in the presence of the Emperor."



Yuru considered it.



"That is reasonable."



Fang Ru nodded.



"The Department of Rites has also asked whether the official counting the seals should sign the inventory before or after the Emperor acknowledges their return."



Yuru put down the report.



"Tell them the Emperor can acknowledge the return after the inventory is signed."



Fang Ru bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Yuru leaned back.



The palace was preparing to return three seals.



It had managed to turn the task into a matter requiring three departments, two inventories, and an imperial acknowledgment.



She wondered whether the palace would ever learn to do something without asking for a separate procedure.



Then she remembered that she had spent years teaching it to ask her.



The realization was not pleasant.



It was, however, useful.



She would have to leave room for the palace to make its own mistakes.



Before the evening meal, Mingzhao received the preliminary report from Master Luo.



The assessor had completed the first inspection of the northern warehouse.



The building was structurally sound after repairs, though some drainage work remained necessary.



The grain stock was excluded from the valuation until the inventory examination was complete.



The report also identified several agricultural parcels requiring separate inspection.



Mingzhao read it carefully.



"Has he provided a value?"



"Only a preliminary range for the building," Sun Qiming replied. "The full valuation will follow after the remaining inspections."



"Good."



She placed the report with the other documents.



Qiao Yue brought her tea.



"Miss, the northern house seller has sent another message."



Mingzhao looked up.



"Which message?"



"The owner is asking whether you intend to make an offer."



"Tell him I have not decided."



"He says another buyer has expressed interest."



"Then he should speak to that buyer."



Qiao Yue blinked.



"Should we not try to secure the property?"



"Not before the inspection is complete."



"But if someone else buys it?"



"Then someone else will own it."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



Mingzhao took a sip of tea.



"I am not going to purchase a damaged house simply because its owner has become impatient."



Qiao Yue nodded.



The eastern property still had an unresolved boundary question.



The northern property still required repairs.



The third option had not yet been inspected.



Mingzhao had no intention of choosing a residence merely to prove that she could leave.



Her departure would be difficult enough without buying herself a roof that leaked.



That night, Yuru and Mingzhao each reviewed a different list.



Yuru reviewed the remaining ceremonial arrangements.



Mingzhao reviewed the remaining ownership questions.



The Empress had nine days until her formal departure.



The Marquis's eldest daughter still had no confirmed departure date.



The palace had finished preparing the seal inventory.



The Marquisate had not finished determining where twenty-seven sacks of grain had gone.



And the family had finally discovered that a person who had managed its accounts for six years could not simply be handed a document saying, *Everything in your room belongs to you.*



The document had to be examined.



The receipts had to be found.



The property had to be valued.



The debts had to be identified.



The family had to agree.



Mingzhao looked at the growing stack of papers.



She had once believed that leaving would require only a decision.



Now she understood that the decision was the easiest part.



She closed the ledger.



"Tomorrow," she said.



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Tomorrow, Miss?"



"Tomorrow we will examine the next list."



Qiao Yue looked at the stack.



"How many lists are there?"



Mingzhao glanced at the papers.



"Enough."



It was not a comforting answer.



It was, unfortunately, an accurate one.
