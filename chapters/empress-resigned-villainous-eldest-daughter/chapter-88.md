# **Chapter 88: The Former Empress Has Begun Reading a Book About Estate Management and Has Discovered That Even a Person Who Has Resigned Can Still Find a Paragraph Irritating. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Completed a Profitable Order and Has Been Offered Another One with Better Terms but No Reason to Hurry. The Marquisate's New Procedure Has Discovered That a Missing Voucher Cannot Be Found by Staring at Its Empty Space.**

**22nd Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first book borrowed from the Duke of Xu was about household estates.



Yuru had read the introduction the previous evening and continued with the chapter on land management after breakfast.



The author divided estate administration into five principal responsibilities: collecting rents, maintaining buildings, supervising labour, managing stores, and keeping accounts.



The arrangement was sensible.



The author's description of the relationship between a household head and its stewards was less so.



According to the book, a capable household head should personally examine every important decision, while the stewards should carry out instructions without making independent judgments beyond their assigned duties.



Yuru read the passage twice.



Then she put the book down.



Fang Ru, who was arranging the tea tray, glanced at her.



"Is there a problem?"



"The author believes that a household head should personally examine every important decision."



"Is that wrong?"



"Not entirely."



Fang Ru waited.



"He has not explained how the household head is expected to examine every important decision without becoming the person responsible for every decision."



Fang Ru looked at the page.



"Perhaps he assumes the household head has sufficient time."



"Perhaps he has never managed a large household."



"That seems possible."



Yuru turned several pages.



The author gave an example involving a manor with three villages, two storehouses, and several hundred tenants. He advised the estate head to review the monthly reports, inspect the accounts, and personally confirm major repairs.



There was nothing inherently unreasonable about these duties.



The problem was that the author had treated personal supervision as a substitute for a functioning system.



Yuru had once made that mistake herself.



Not because she believed that no one else was capable, but because she had become accustomed to noticing problems before anyone else did. Each time she corrected one, the next person waited for her to correct another.



Eventually, the household had learned to bring everything to her.



She closed the book.



Fang Ru poured tea.



"Will you mark the passage?"



"No."



"Why not?"



"Because I do not intend to revise the book."



Fang Ru smiled.



"That is sensible."



Yuru picked up her cup.



She was reading for herself. She did not need to turn every disagreement with an author into a new regulation.



After a short pause, she opened the book again.



This time she continued to the chapter on maintenance expenses.



It was less irritating.

At the silk workshop, Mingzhao received a new inquiry from a merchant house shortly before noon.



The letter came from a different trading company than the one that had previously proposed payment after resale.



This merchant, represented by a man named Gao Wen, had asked about the workshop's capacity and whether it would consider producing a future order of eighty bolts.



The proposed terms were clearer.



A substantial deposit would be paid before production. The remaining balance would be paid upon delivery to the buyer's warehouse. The buyer would inspect the goods according to an agreed sample, and any corrections would be limited to defects against the written specifications.



Mingzhao read the letter carefully.



Lin Shuyun sat across from her.



"Better terms."



"Yes."



"An eighty-bolt order is also smaller than the last one."



"Yes."



"Then we should consider it."



"We should."



Lin waited.



Mingzhao folded the letter.



"We should not accept it today."



Lin sighed.



"I knew there would be another sentence."



"The workshop has just completed a two-hundred-bolt order. We need to review the workers' capacity, the current stock of material, and the time required for production."



"The buyer has not demanded an immediate answer."



"Then we have time."



Lin leaned back.



"That is a rather comfortable position."



"It is better than agreeing to a delivery date before knowing whether we can meet it."



Mingzhao asked the clerk to prepare a capacity report.



The report would include available workers, the current workload, raw silk stock, expected material prices, and the time required for inspection and packing.



She also wanted the buyer's proposed sample and written specifications before agreeing to a final price.



Gao Wen's letter did not contain the sample.



It merely asked whether the workshop would be willing to discuss an order.



That was not enough to calculate a price.



Lin read the draft reply.



*We are willing to examine the proposed order. Please provide the sample, written specifications, expected delivery period, and proposed payment schedule. A production commitment will be considered after review of the materials and workshop capacity.*



"Very cautious," Lin said.



"Very ordinary."



"Most merchants would prefer a more enthusiastic answer."



"Then they may be disappointed."



Lin smiled.



"Send it."



The clerk carried the reply away.



Mingzhao turned to the workshop report.



The two trainees were now working with less supervision. One had become sufficiently accurate to handle the tighter edge sections, though the supervisor still checked the first pieces of each batch. The other was faster and had improved consistency, but still needed occasional correction.



The revised folding method had been retained for work that matched the specifications of the previous order. It had not been adopted as a universal standard.



Mingzhao approved the report.



"Keep the workers' training records separate from the production records."



Lin nodded.



"And do not assume that because the last order was profitable, the next one will be."



"That is understood."



Mingzhao glanced at her.



"Is it?"



Lin laughed.



"I was merely hoping to enjoy the success for another day."



"You may enjoy it. You may not use it to calculate a price for a different order."



"An unfortunate restriction."

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan had spent most of the morning reviewing the old payment entry.



The accounts office had searched the general purchasing files, the household expenditure records, and the surviving register of seal use.



The original voucher remained missing.



The payment entry itself was genuine as an accounting record. It had appeared in a ledger and had been carried into a summary. The estate purchasing seal had been used on the relevant date, and the seal register identified Chen Rui as its custodian.



But none of those facts established the recipient or the purpose.



Mingyuan had asked the accounts clerk to prepare a list of all surviving documents that referred to the entry.



The list contained:

- The original ledger entry.
- A summary of the payment.
- The seal register showing Chen Rui's custody.
- A general purchasing file index.
- A reference to a missing supporting voucher.

There was no receipt.



There was no supplier's name.



There was no description of the purchased goods.



Mingyuan read the list.



"Have we examined the old monthly reconciliation books?"



"Not all of them."



"Then add them to the search."



The clerk bowed.



"Could the entry have been an advance to the eldest young lady?"



"It could have been."



"Then should we ask her whether she remembers receiving it?"



"She has already said she does not recognize the payment. We should not ask the same question again without new information."



The clerk hesitated.



"Perhaps she may remember if we give her the date."



"Then send the date and the surviving entry, not an accusation."



The clerk nodded.



Mingyuan took a fresh sheet of paper.



He wrote a short inquiry to Mingzhao, attaching a copy of the ledger entry and the seal-register reference. The wording was careful.



*The accounts office has not located the original voucher. The surviving ledger records a payment on the stated date under the estate purchasing seal. The seal register identifies Chen Rui as custodian. No recipient or purpose is recorded in the surviving copy. If you recall any relevant purchase or instruction from that date, please inform us. No conclusion has been reached.*



He read the note again.



Then he sealed it.



He did not expect an immediate answer.



He had finally learned that a question could be asked without assuming the answer.

The grain inquiry remained no better.



Zhang's latest statement had explained the two different warehouse totals but had not identified the missing sacks.



The original stock record still showed forty-seven.



The instruction still referred to twenty.



The receipt record still showed eighteen.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



The separate transport payment with no destination remained separate.



Mingyuan looked over the papers.



"Has anyone identified the carrier?"



"Not yet."



"The old transport register?"



"Some pages are missing."



"Then list the missing pages by date."



The clerk did so.



Mingyuan looked at the list.



"Do not assume that the blank destination in the payment record refers to the grain."



"Yes, young master."



"And do not combine the payment with the twenty-seven sacks simply because both remain unexplained."



"Understood."



Mingyuan signed the instruction.



The clerk took it away.



Mingyuan remained at his desk.



He had expected the estate accounts to become clearer once he began examining them. Instead, he was finding more questions.



But at least the questions were now properly separated.



That was a form of progress, even if it was not the satisfying kind.

At the temporary residence, Mingzhao received her brother's letter shortly after the afternoon meal.



Qiao Yue placed the envelope on the table.



"From the Marquisate."



Mingzhao opened it and read the attached copy of the payment entry.



She recognized the format.



The entry was dated during the period when she had still managed several estate purchases. The amount was not one she immediately remembered, and the seal impression was not unusual. The estate purchasing seal had been used for many transactions.



She read Mingyuan's note.



There was no accusation.



There was no demand that she accept responsibility.



There was no suggestion that she should return to the estate to search the accounts.



She examined the date again.



"I do not remember this payment."



Qiao Yue looked at the copy.



"Does the amount mean anything?"



"No."



"Could it have been a purchase?"



"It could have been. The seal was used for estate purchasing."



"Then perhaps it was."



"Perhaps."



Mingzhao placed the paper down.



"But the entry does not say what was purchased, who received it, or who authorized it."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Then what will you tell your brother?"



"That I do not recognize it."



"Nothing more?"



"Nothing more."



Mingzhao wrote a short reply.



*I have examined the copy and do not recognize the payment or recall giving an instruction concerning it. The use of the estate purchasing seal does not establish that I personally held or used it. I cannot identify the recipient or purpose. I agree that the original voucher must be located before any conclusion is drawn.*



She read it once and signed.



Qiao Yue looked at the letter.



"You have not been asked to come back."



"No."



"That is good."



"Yes."



"Your brother appears to be learning."



Mingzhao folded the letter.



"He is learning to examine records."



"That is what I meant."



Mingzhao gave the reply to the messenger.



She did not feel any need to add an explanation about her past management. If she had remembered the payment, she would have said so. If a new document appeared, she would examine it.



Until then, she would not invent a memory to fill an empty space.

In the western guest residence, Yuru reached the chapter on estate repairs.



The author described the duties of a steward responsible for building maintenance. The steward was expected to inspect the structure, obtain estimates, arrange labour, record expenditure, and report completion.



Yuru read the section with more interest.



It was a reasonable arrangement.



The author then added that the household head should personally inspect all completed repairs.



Yuru stopped.



Fang Ru, who was sewing a loose seam on a sleeve, glanced up.



"Another disagreement?"



"Yes."



"Is the author wrong again?"



"Not entirely."



Fang Ru smiled.



Yuru turned the page.



The book had a table of repair categories and suggested reserve funds. The figures were old, but the method of separating ordinary maintenance from major reconstruction was useful.



She read the explanation carefully.



For a moment, she remembered the palace's many years of repairs: roofs, drains, storehouses, kitchens, servants' quarters, and ceremonial halls. She remembered the reports that had come to her because no one else wanted to decide whether a small crack was a maintenance issue or a structural concern.



She also remembered that the palace had employed skilled officials and experienced stewards.



They had been capable.



She had simply become the person who checked everything.



Yuru closed the book and looked at the window.



"Fang Ru."



"Yes, Madam?"



"Do you think a person can become so accustomed to doing something that she forgets it was never meant to be hers alone?"



Fang Ru considered the question.



"Yes."



"Even when others are capable?"



"Especially then, perhaps."



Yuru nodded.



She did not need to explain further.



The book was interesting.



It was also a reminder of an old habit.



She opened it again.



This time, she did not mark the passage.

At the Marquisate, the new purchasing register recorded another ordinary purchase.



This time, the difficulty was not a missing quantity or an unclear unit.



It was a disagreement between the purchasing clerk and the receiving steward over the condition of the goods.



A delivery of lamp oil had arrived in sealed jars. The purchasing clerk recorded the full quantity as received because the number of jars matched the order. The receiving steward accepted the jars but noted that one seal appeared damaged.



The clerk argued that the jar itself was intact.



The receiving steward refused to mark the delivery as fully verified until the contents had been checked.



Liang Wen reviewed the entry.



"How many jars are there?"



"Twelve," said the purchasing clerk.



"How many seals are intact?"



"Eleven."



"And the remaining jar?"



"The jar is intact. The seal is damaged."



Liang Wen looked at the receiving steward.



"Has the contents been checked?"



"Not yet."



"Then record twelve jars physically received, eleven seals intact, and one jar pending inspection."



The purchasing clerk frowned.



"That will make the delivery appear incomplete."



"It is incomplete in one respect."



"The jar is there."



"The seal is damaged."



"That does not mean the oil is missing."



"No. It means the contents have not been verified."



The receiving steward nodded.



Liang Wen entered the distinction.



The jar was opened in the presence of both men. The contents were examined and found to be sufficient. The damaged seal was recorded, and the receiving steward signed the confirmation.



The purchasing clerk looked at the register.



"So the delivery is complete."



"Now it is."



"After all that."



Liang Wen closed the book.



"Yes."



The clerk shook his head.



"One jar of oil has required three signatures."



"Four," Liang Wen said. "You forgot mine."



The clerk stared at him.



Liang Wen returned to his desk.



The register was becoming longer.



It was also becoming harder to misunderstand.

That evening, Mingzhao received a brief reply from Lin Shuyun.



The new merchant had acknowledged their request for a sample and written specifications. He would send them within several days.



Lin had added a note of her own.



*At least this one has answered the questions we asked.*



Mingzhao smiled faintly.



She placed the letter beside the workshop's final account.



The previous order was complete. The new inquiry remained under consideration. The workshop was not idle, but neither was it committed to work that had not been agreed.



The estate accounts remained unresolved.



Her personal property division remained unfinished.



The northern canal property remained only a possibility.



There were still matters she wanted to settle.



But she no longer intended to treat every unfinished matter as an emergency.



Qiao Yue entered with the evening tea.



"Have you finished the account?"



"Yes."



"Have you accepted the new order?"



"No."



"Have you answered the Marquisate?"



"Yes."



"Then perhaps we have finished everything for today."



Mingzhao looked at the papers on the table.



"Everything that needed to be finished today."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"I shall learn to use that phrase."



"You should."



Qiao Yue poured the tea.



Mingzhao picked up her cup.



Outside, the market was quieter than it had been in the afternoon. The workshop had closed, the workers had gone home, and the final account was safely filed.



Tomorrow would bring its own decisions.



They did not need to be made tonight.
