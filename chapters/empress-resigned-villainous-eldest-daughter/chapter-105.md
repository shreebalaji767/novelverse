# **Chapter 105: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Garden Path Can Be Kept Safe Without Requiring Its Owner to Count Every Stone. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received Confirmation That the Last Twenty Bolts Have Passed Internal Inspection, Although the Buyer Has Not Yet Seen Them. The Marquis's Heir Has Sent an Inquiry to a Former Clerk and Has Discovered That Finding the Correct Name Is Not the Same as Finding the Correct Answer.**

**9th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had begun to enjoy the garden book.

It was not because every recommendation was sensible. The author still occasionally wandered into the belief that an owner ought to inspect things personally, but the book contained enough practical information to compensate.

That morning, Yuru read about drainage.

The author explained that water should be allowed to flow away from the roots of plants that disliked excessive moisture. He warned against blocking natural channels merely to make a path appear orderly.

Yuru looked at the small courtyard beyond her window.

The rainwater had left a shallow damp patch near the edge of the stone path.

"Fang Ru, does the gardener know about that?"

"I believe so. He was here yesterday."

"Then there is no need to summon him."

Fang Ru glanced at the book.

"Would Madam like to ask him whether the water has drained properly?"

"No. If the patch remains tomorrow, he can report it."

Yuru returned to the page.

The author had recommended checking drainage after heavy rain, particularly near walls and the foundations of buildings.

That was reasonable.

She wrote a short note in her notebook:

*Observe the result after the relevant conditions occur. Do not mistake repeated inspection under unchanged conditions for greater care.*

She paused.

It was a useful distinction.

She had once reviewed certain palace accounts several times in a single day because she feared that an error might remain undiscovered. The second review had occasionally found something the first had missed.

The fifth review had usually found nothing except a more irritated clerk.

Yuru smiled and closed her notebook.

She had no desire to revisit that habit.

"Fang Ru, I shall continue reading."

Fang Ru poured the tea.

The morning passed quietly.

At the workshop, Shen Mingzhao received the final internal inspection report for the Gao Wen order.

The last twenty bolts had passed.

The measuring clerk had recorded the width of each bolt. The finishing supervisor had checked the edges against the approved sample. The colour comparison had been completed under the same conditions used for the earlier groups.

All eighty bolts had now passed internal inspection.

Mingzhao read the report carefully.

She checked the total.

The first thirty had passed.

The next twenty had passed.

The final thirty had passed.

The count was correct.

She then examined the separate record of corrections and re-examinations. The earlier edge adjustments had been completed, and the bolt whose width had required a second measurement had been confirmed within specification.

No material had been written off.

The supervisor had signed the final inspection record.

Mingzhao placed the report beside the contract.

"Send the buyer the completion notice," she said.

Qiao Yue nodded.

"Shall we request inspection tomorrow?"

"Ask when his representative can attend. Do not assume a date before he confirms it."

"Should the goods be packed now?"

"Prepare the packing materials and separate the order by the agreed bundles. Do not seal the containers until the buyer's inspection arrangements are confirmed."

Qiao Yue wrote down the instructions.

Mingzhao looked at the finished goods.

The workshop had completed its production.

That was an achievement.

But the contract still required the buyer to inspect the order at the designated eastern receiving warehouse in River Quarter. Delivery had to follow the agreed terms, and the remaining balance would be payable according to the inspection clause.

She had received the deposit.

She had not received the balance.

She would not count it before it arrived.

"Have Sun Qiming update the cost summary," she added. "Include the additional inspection expenditure and the packing estimate separately."

Qiao Yue accepted the report.

Mingzhao returned to her desk.

The order had reached another stage.

It was not yet closed.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received confirmation that the inquiry to Li Shun had been delivered to the office where the former clerk was now employed.

The office had acknowledged receipt and promised to forward the questions.

Mingyuan read the acknowledgment.

It contained no substantive reply.

He placed it in the transport investigation file.

Liang Wen entered with another document.

"The northern warehouse archive has located a list of movement-record clerks."

Mingyuan looked up.

"From the relevant year?"

"Yes. It is a duty roster, though the surviving copy is incomplete."

He examined it.

Li Shun's name appeared beside the movement-record office for part of the relevant period. Another clerk, Wang De, was listed for the preceding months.

The dates were important.

The four-cart dispatch had occurred during Li Shun's assignment.

That made him a relevant person to question.

It did not prove that he had personally written the entry.

Mingyuan compared the dates with the loose dispatch pages.

The handwriting resembled Li Shun's, according to Zhang's earlier observation, but the identification had not been certain.

He wrote a note.

The personnel roster supported Li Shun's responsibility for movement records during the relevant period. The handwriting attribution remained unconfirmed.

Liang Wen watched him.

"Does that make Li Shun our principal witness?"

"It makes him a relevant witness."

"Is there a difference?"

"There is a difference between asking someone about a record they may have handled and deciding that they are responsible for everything the record does not explain."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan placed the roster beside the damaged notebook-issue register.

The documents were beginning to form a more coherent picture of the office.

But the four-cart dispatch still had no confirmed cargo or destination.

The twenty-seven sacks remained unexplained.

The missing notebook remained missing.

The Marquis of Qingyuan came to the accounts office after lunch.

He had received a report that Li Shun's name had been found.

"Then we have the clerk."

"We have identified the clerk assigned to movement records during the relevant period."

"Is that not enough?"

"Not yet."

The Marquis looked at the roster.

"He wrote the dispatch entry?"

"The handwriting has not been confirmed."

"Zhang said it resembled his."

"Zhang said it resembled his handwriting. He also said he could not be certain."

The Marquis frowned.

"Must we question every person who ever worked in that warehouse?"

"Only those whose duties or records make them relevant."

"Then question Li Shun."

"The inquiry has been delivered."

The Marquis picked up the damaged register.

"And the notebook?"

"The issue register confirms that a notebook was issued to the northern warehouse transport supervisor's office. The surviving page does not show who signed for it."

"One of the supervisors must have had it."

"Possibly. The first remembers it being used by the clerk. The second remembers receiving several records during the transfer of duties but cannot identify the notebook."

The Marquis put the register down.

"Then someone must know."

Mingyuan did not disagree.

Someone might know.

But the family had not yet found that person.

At Mingzhao's residence, the buyer Gao Wen replied to the workshop's completion notice.

He confirmed that his representative could inspect the eighty bolts on the following day at the named receiving warehouse in River Quarter.

The message also confirmed that the inspection would be conducted against the approved sample and written specifications attached to the contract.

Mingzhao read the letter.

"Prepare the delivery arrangements," she told Qiao Yue. "The goods must arrive with the original inspection records and the packing list."

"Should the carrier collect them tomorrow morning?"

"After the inspection arrangements are confirmed with the warehouse."

Qiao Yue hesitated.

"Will the buyer's representative inspect the goods before the carrier takes them?"

"That is the agreed arrangement. The inspection is at the receiving warehouse."

"Then the goods must be transported there first."

"Yes. The carrier should sign for the quantity collected from the workshop. The warehouse should separately record the quantity received."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao wrote an instruction for the supervisor.

The eighty bolts were to be packed in the agreed bundles. Each bundle would carry the order reference and quantity. The carrier would receive a dispatch list, and the receiving warehouse would confirm the actual count.

She checked the contract again.

The buyer had three days to inspect the goods and submit any written defect notice. If no inspection or defect notice was submitted within that period, the remaining balance became payable on the fourth day.

The contract was clear.

The delivery records still had to be correct.

Mingzhao folded the letter.

"Send the confirmation."

In the afternoon, Sun Qiming delivered the updated cost summary.

He had separated the costs into materials, wages, training, finishing, inspection, packing, and transport estimates.

The additional inspection expenditure was higher than the original estimate.

However, the revised folding method had reduced the time required for several production stages. The training costs had been higher than expected, but the two trainees had improved sufficiently that the workshop had not needed to replace them or discard material.

The total cost remained within the expected range.

Mingzhao examined the figures.

"Is the transport cost final?"

"No. We have an estimate from the carrier, but the final amount depends on the number of containers and the actual delivery arrangement."

"Then mark it as estimated."

Sun Qiming nodded.

"Would you like a provisional profit figure?"

"Yes, but keep it separate from the final account."

He wrote the provisional figure on a separate sheet.

Mingzhao examined it.

The order appeared likely to produce a modest positive margin.

She did not celebrate.

The buyer had not yet inspected the goods, and the remaining payment had not been received.

"Keep the estimate with the cost file," she said. "We will close the account after delivery and payment."

Sun Qiming collected the papers.

Mingzhao looked at the packing list.

The production work was finished.

The commercial work was not.

At the palace guest residence, Yuru received a personal letter from her mother.

Madam Xu wrote that the weather had become warmer and that the household garden needed some attention after the recent rain.

She also mentioned that Duke Xu had asked whether Yuru wished to borrow another book.

Yuru smiled.

Her father had apparently not abandoned his ambition to supply her with books.

She wrote a reply.

She thanked her mother for the letter and said the garden book had been more agreeable than the household manual. She had learned something about drainage and had not yet been instructed to inspect every leaf.

She added that she was enjoying the two books already borrowed and did not require another immediately.

Fang Ru watched her finish.

"Will Madam send the reply today?"

"Yes."

Yuru sealed the letter.

She returned to the garden notes.

The book had reached a section about plants that could be grown near walls.

She read for a while, then set it aside.

Outside, the gardener was speaking to another servant about a damp section of the path.

He pointed toward the drainage channel, and the other man went to fetch a small tool.

Yuru watched them for a moment.

The matter was being handled.

No one had asked her to inspect it.

She returned to her tea.

That evening, the workshop completed its packing preparation.

The eighty bolts were arranged in the agreed bundles, with labels and quantities checked against the production record.

The goods remained at the workshop.

They had not yet been delivered to River Quarter.

The buyer's inspection was scheduled for the following day.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan's inquiry to Li Shun remained unanswered.

The personnel roster had established that he was assigned to movement records during the relevant period, but the four-cart dispatch still had no confirmed cargo or destination.

The grain discrepancy remained separate and unresolved.

And at the palace guest residence, Xu Yuru had reached the chapter about plants growing beside walls.

She read one final paragraph before closing the book.

The author advised that plants should not be forced to compete for space when their roots required different conditions.

Yuru smiled.

It seemed that even a garden book had discovered the importance of allowing different things to grow in different ways.

She put the book away.

Tomorrow, the buyer would inspect the bolts.

Li Shun might answer the inquiry.

And the former Empress would continue reading about gardens, where at least the plants did not expect her to settle their accounts.
