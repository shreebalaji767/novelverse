# **Chapter 92: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Provincial Granary Can Have Three Different Measures of Success and That None of Them Requires the Former Empress to Inspect Its Roof. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Signed a Contract for Eighty Bolts and Has Discovered That Receiving a Deposit Is the Beginning of an Obligation, Not the End of Negotiation. The Marquis's Heir Has Asked an Old Warehouse Steward to Identify a Handwriting Sample and Has Been Told That Memory Is Not a Seal of Office.**

**26th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The former Empress had reached the chapter on warehouse inspections.

The author of the provincial granary book was less concerned with the appearance of a storehouse than with the condition of the grain inside it.

He recommended examining the walls, ventilation, floor, drainage, and roof, but he also insisted that an inspection should record what had actually been observed.

A statement that a warehouse was in good condition was insufficient.

The inspector should identify which parts had been examined, what defects had been found, what repairs had been ordered, and whether those repairs had been completed.

Yuru read the passage and nodded.

"That is better."

Fang Ru was pouring tea.

"Better than the other book?"

"Better than the instruction to inspect every roof personally."

"Because this author explains what should happen afterward?"

"Yes. An inspection that produces no record is merely a walk around a building."

Fang Ru placed the teapot down.

"Some people would say that a walk around a building is still useful."

"Certainly. If the purpose is to walk."

Yuru turned the page.

The author gave an example of a warehouse where an inspector had found water entering through a damaged roof. The repair had been ordered, but no one had recorded whether the work was completed. During the next rainy season, the same section leaked.

The inspector had blamed the carpenter.

The carpenter had blamed the steward.

The steward had blamed the official who had approved the repair.

The author concluded that a repair order should be followed by a completion record.

Yuru closed the book.

"That sounds familiar."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Would you like to mark it?"

"I already have."

Yuru looked at the paper slips protruding from the pages.

She had marked several passages in both books, but she had not written any instructions or plans based on them.

That was deliberate.

She could find an argument useful without deciding that she must personally introduce it somewhere.

She opened the book again.

A few moments later, she read a paragraph concerning the responsibility of inspectors when they discovered defects outside their assigned authority.

The author advised them to record the defect and refer it to the appropriate office rather than attempting to resolve every matter themselves.

Yuru paused.

Then she laughed softly.

Fang Ru looked up.

"What is it?"

"At last, an author who understands that noticing a problem does not make it one's personal property."

Fang Ru glanced at the book.

"Perhaps your father chose well."

"Perhaps he did."

Yuru resumed reading.

The afternoon remained quiet.

No one arrived with a roof report.

The final contract arrived at Shen Mingzhao's residence before noon.

He Zhen brought it himself.

He also brought the written approval from Gao Wen confirming the amendments to the deposit clause and the production schedule.

Mingzhao received him in the front room.

"Have you brought the original sample?"

"Yes."

He Zhen placed it on the table.

"And the signed contract?"

"Two copies. My principal has signed both."

Mingzhao examined the documents before responding.

She checked the named warehouse, the delivery period, the inspection period, the correction period, the payment schedule, and the attached specifications.

The contract now stated that production would begin only after the deposit had been received in full.

The trial sample would be examined against the original.

The first ten bolts would be inspected separately before the remaining production continued.

If the buyer failed to inspect the completed delivery within three days and submitted no written defect notice, the remaining payment would become due on the fourth day.

The correction obligation was limited to defects against the written specifications.

Mingzhao read the final page.

Then she looked at He Zhen.

"Has your principal accepted the delivery schedule in full?"

"Yes."

"And the named warehouse?"

"Yes."

"The sample attached to both copies is the same sample?"

"Yes. We have marked it with the contract date."

Mingzhao compared the two samples.

They were identical.

She signed both copies.

He Zhen signed beneath her name as the merchant's representative.

Mingzhao retained one copy and handed him the other.

"The order is agreed. Production will begin after the deposit is received and the trial sample is approved."

He Zhen bowed.

"I will report the execution to my principal immediately."

"Please do."

He Zhen hesitated.

"Would you be able to begin the trial today?"

"The workshop has already prepared the materials. It will begin once the deposit arrives."

"That may be tomorrow."

"Then it will begin tomorrow."

He Zhen smiled.

"You are very strict about the order of things."

"I have found that the order of things matters."

He bowed again and departed.

Qiao Yue collected the signed contract.

"Shall I tell Lin Shuyun?"

"Yes. Tell her the contract has been executed, but the production authorization remains conditional on receipt of the deposit."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao placed the contract in its folder.

For the first time, the eighty-bolt order had become a real obligation.

She did not feel triumphant.

She felt responsible for making sure that the agreement could be fulfilled.

That was different.

The deposit arrived in the early afternoon.

The merchant's agent brought the payment confirmation and the corresponding remittance record.

Mingzhao had Qiao Yue verify the amount against the contract before accepting it.

The figures matched.

The payment was entered in the order account as a deposit received, not as completed income.

Mingzhao checked the entry.

"Record the date and the source."

"Yes."

"Attach the remittance confirmation."

Qiao Yue did so.

Only then did Mingzhao sign the production authorization.

The authorization stated that the workshop could begin the trial preparation and dyeing process. It did not authorize the full production run until the trial sample had been approved.

Mingzhao sent the signed order to Lin Shuyun.

The workshop now had a clear contract, a received deposit, and a limited initial instruction.

There was no need to guess what the merchant expected.

There was no need to guess what the workshop was permitted to do.

The order had begun.

At the workshop, Lin Shuyun received the authorization with visible satisfaction.

She gathered the dyer and senior weaver.

"The trial is approved to begin," she said. "We are not producing the full order yet. We are preparing the sample section for comparison."

The dyer examined the original silk and the written specifications.

"The colour can be reproduced, but I will need to test the dye mixture before preparing the full quantity."

"Do that."

The senior weaver checked the sample's thread tension.

"The weave is tighter than our ordinary stock."

"Can we reproduce it?"

"Yes. But the loom must be adjusted."

Lin Shuyun looked at the production schedule.

"Then the first trial will take two days."

The dyer nodded.

The trainees were assigned to prepare materials and assist with the simpler stages.

Neither was permitted to make an independent adjustment to the dye mixture or loom settings.

One of them asked whether this meant they would not be allowed to work on the new order.

Lin Shuyun answered calmly.

"You will work on it. You will not decide the specifications."

The trainee looked disappointed.

The senior weaver explained, "If you change the tension because you think it looks better, the result may no longer match the sample."

The trainee nodded.

The second trainee asked whether the first ten bolts would be inspected separately.

"Yes," Lin Shuyun replied.

"Does that mean the remaining seventy will be inspected less carefully?"

"No."

"Then why divide them?"

"Because if the first ten reveal a problem, we can correct the method before it affects the remaining seventy."

The trainee considered this.

"That makes sense."

Lin Shuyun smiled.

"It is meant to."

She checked the materials once more and authorized the trial preparation.

The work began.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received a reply from Zhang, the former northern warehouse steward.

Zhang had examined the certified copy of the four-cart entry.

He had not identified the driver's name with certainty.

However, he recognized the clerk's handwriting.

He believed the entry had been written by a clerk who had worked in the northern warehouse during the relevant period.

He also remembered that four carts had been used for a transport movement from the warehouse, but he could not remember the cargo.

The original entry did not identify the destination.

Mingyuan read the report carefully.

"Did Zhang provide the clerk's name?"

"Yes. He believes it was a man named Li Shun."

"Believes?"

"He says he recognizes the handwriting, but he cannot be certain without seeing other examples."

Mingyuan placed the report beside the copy.

"Do we have other examples?"

"The archives contain several warehouse sheets signed by Li Shun."

"Then send certified copies to Zhang. Do not show him the name before he examines the handwriting."

The clerk bowed.

Mingyuan turned to Steward Zhou.

"Zhang remembers the four carts, but not their cargo."

"That is something."

"It is something. It is not the answer."

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan continued reading.

The report also stated that the warehouse had used a separate transport notebook during periods of heavy grain movement. The notebook was not among the records already examined.

Mingyuan looked up.

"Where was it kept?"

"Zhang believes the transport notebook was stored with the warehouse dispatch records."

"Has that section been searched?"

"Not yet. The clerk thought it was included in the general warehouse bundle."

"Then we should check the actual archive location."

The clerk bowed.

Mingyuan made a note.

The new information did not prove where the twenty-seven sacks had gone.

It did, however, identify another record category that might contain the missing details.

He placed the report in the inquiry folder.

For the first time, the search had a specific next step.

In the afternoon, Shen Guowei asked Mingyuan to attend a short meeting.

Madam Liang was present, along with Steward Zhou.

The Marquis had the revised property schedule before him.

"Your sister's division remains unfinished," he said.

"Yes."

"The family has made several corrections. The accounts are clearer than before."

"Yes."

"But the remaining questions cannot be left indefinitely."

Mingyuan looked at the schedule.

"Which question does Father wish to resolve first?"

Shen Guowei frowned.

"All of them."

"Then we should set out the supporting records required for each."

Madam Liang looked at him.

"Must every family matter become an accounting exercise?"

"Not every matter. The property division does require accounts."

"Your sister has already left. Must we continue treating the family as though it were a commercial partnership?"

Mingyuan remained composed.

"The division concerns property, advances, expenditure, and liabilities. If we do not distinguish them, we cannot settle them."

Shen Guowei tapped the schedule.

"The family also needs a clear arrangement for future estate administration."

Mingyuan looked at him.

"Liang Wen is maintaining the consolidated purchasing register under Steward Zhou's supervision. The revised procedure has been introduced."

"That is not the same as having your sister's experience."

"No. But she has stated that she will not resume estate management."

Madam Liang's expression tightened.

"She could at least advise us."

"She has agreed to answer specific written questions about her own past authorizations and claims. She has not agreed to supervise current administration."

Shen Guowei leaned back.

"She was the person who understood the accounts."

"Then we should preserve the records that explain them."

The room became quiet.

Mingyuan continued.

"The family cannot treat her knowledge as an obligation that continues after she has left. If we need her to answer a specific question, we should provide the relevant document and ask that question. We should not ask her to return to manage the household."

Madam Liang looked at her son.

"You speak as though you have become her advocate."

"I am speaking about what she has agreed to do."

Shen Guowei studied him for a moment.

Then he looked down at the property schedule.

"Very well. Prepare a list of the remaining evidence required for the division. We will arrange another meeting when the documents are ready."

Mingyuan bowed.

He did not consider the matter resolved.

But the Marquis had at least agreed to separate the property division from the question of future estate administration.

That was a useful distinction.

At the western guest residence, Yuru finished the chapter on warehouse inspections.

She had marked two passages.

One concerned the need to record defects discovered during an inspection.

The other concerned the obligation to verify that repairs had actually been completed.

Fang Ru brought her a small dish of fruit.

"Have you finished the book?"

"Not yet."

"How much remains?"

"Several chapters."

"Will you finish it tomorrow?"

"Perhaps."

Yuru closed the book.

"Why?"

"Your father may have another one ready."

Yuru smiled.

"He has been instructed to wait."

"By Madam Xu?"

"Yes."

Fang Ru placed the fruit beside her.

"Then perhaps you have enough reading for the next few days."

"I certainly do."

Yuru picked up a slice of fruit.

She looked at the two books on the table.

One had taught her to question the habit of personally supervising every household task.

The other had reminded her that an inspection, an authorization, a delivery, and a completed repair were different things.

She was grateful for both.

She was also grateful that neither book had come with an expectation that she must implement its recommendations.

Outside, the palace residence remained quiet.

The former Empress had no administrative duties to complete that evening.

She had no meeting to chair, no household dispute to settle, and no accounts waiting for her signature.

She had two books to return within three months.

That was a sufficiently clear obligation.

By nightfall, the workshop's trial dye preparation had begun.

The merchant's deposit had been recorded.

The contract had been signed.

The production authorization had been issued.

The first sample was not yet ready.

At the Marquisate, the transport notebook had not yet been found.

The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.

The old purchasing voucher remained missing.

The property division remained incomplete.

Yet several matters had moved forward without anyone pretending that they were finished.

Mingzhao reviewed the workshop's first-day report before retiring.

Yuru placed a paper marker in the granary book.

Mingyuan sent instructions to the archive clerk.

Each had a different task.

None had been asked to perform the other's.

For one day, at least, the distinction held.
