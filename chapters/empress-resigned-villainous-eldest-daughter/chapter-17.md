# **Chapter 17: The Palace Has Transferred Another Responsibility. The Marquis Has Transferred Another Ledger. Neither Has Managed to Transfer the Consequences.**

**9th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The second day of the palace's transition had begun with a disagreement about medicine.

Not an unusual disagreement.

The palace had enough medicines to treat a considerable number of illnesses, provided that the correct medicine reached the correct person before the correct person became impatient.

The difficulty was that three offices had been maintaining three different records.

The Imperial Pharmacy recorded medicines received and prepared.

The residence administrators recorded medicines distributed.

The physicians recorded prescriptions and treatment.

The Empress's office had historically received consolidated reports whenever the three records did not agree.

This arrangement had functioned because Yuru knew which office to summon, which physician to question, and which administrator was likely to have copied a figure from the previous month's ledger without checking it.

It was not, however, a proper administrative procedure.

That morning, Madam Zhao arrived at Chengming Palace carrying a document.

"Your Majesty, the Imperial Household Administration has prepared the medicine-distribution procedure."

Yuru accepted it.

The document was four pages long.

She read the first page.

Then the second.

Then she returned to the first.

"Why does the procedure require every ordinary medicine request to be reported to the Imperial Household Administration?"

Madam Zhao answered carefully.

"To ensure that the administration remains informed."

"Does it need to approve every request?"

"No, Your Majesty."

"Then why must every request pass through it?"

Madam Zhao paused.

"To maintain oversight."

Yuru placed the document on the table.

"Who maintains the medicine inventory?"

"The Imperial Pharmacy."

"Who confirms that the medicine is appropriate?"

"The physician."

"Who records its distribution?"

"The residence administrator."

"Then what is the administration overseeing?"

Madam Zhao considered the question.

"The consolidated records, Your Majesty."

"Then it should review the consolidated records. It need not become another stop between the physician and the pharmacy."

Madam Zhao bowed.

"Your Majesty is correct."

Yuru turned to the last page.

There was a clause requiring the Empress's approval for unusual medicine requests.

She tapped it.

"What does unusual mean?"

Madam Zhao looked at the clause.

"It has not yet been defined."

"Then the clause cannot be used."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru returned the document.

"Define the circumstances. An ordinary prescription should proceed through the physician and pharmacy. A medicine unavailable in the ordinary stock should be recorded with its source and cost. A disputed prescription should be reviewed by the senior physician. An emergency should be treated immediately, with the record completed afterward."

Madam Zhao listened carefully.

"And the Empress's office?"

"It should receive information where the regulations require it. It should not become the pharmacy."

Madam Zhao accepted the document.

For a moment, she looked as though she wished to say something.

Yuru noticed.

"Speak."

"Your Majesty, some administrators may be uncomfortable making decisions without your approval."

"Then they must learn which decisions belong to them."

"Some may make mistakes."

"They already make mistakes."

Madam Zhao lowered her eyes.

Yuru continued.

"The purpose of this arrangement is not to create a palace in which nobody makes mistakes. It is to create one in which mistakes can be identified and corrected by the responsible office."

Madam Zhao bowed.

This time, she did not look uncertain.

She left with the document.

Yuru watched her go.

The medicine procedure was not yet complete.

But at least it had stopped pretending that an additional seal could cure a missing rule.

In the Eastern Palace, Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an was examining his household's medicine records.

He had asked the physician to explain why two medicines had been charged to the same household account despite having different recipients.

The physician had explained that one was prescribed for the Crown Prince and the other for an attendant.

The expenditure had been recorded together because both medicines were purchased from the same pharmacy.

Cheng'an examined the ledger.

"The payment is correct?"

"Yes, Your Highness."

"The recipients are recorded?"

"Yes."

"Then why were they placed under the same category?"

The steward answered, "It was convenient."

Cheng'an looked at him.

"Convenient for whom?"

The steward did not immediately reply.

The Crown Prince put down his brush.

"Keep the purchase record together if necessary. The distribution record must identify the recipients."

"Yes, Your Highness."

Cheng'an had begun to understand that a record did not have to be divided into dozens of books to become useful.

It simply had to answer the questions that mattered.

He sent the corrected arrangement to the household administrator.

Then he returned to his studies.

He had discovered that administrative work could consume an entire morning without producing anything that looked particularly impressive.

He was beginning to respect his mother's exhaustion.

He was also beginning to understand why she had been unwilling to continue.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the medicine question had been replaced by a grain question.

The northern warehouse records had not changed.

Twenty sacks had been ordered moved during the roof repairs.

Eighteen were recorded as received in the southern storage facility.

Two remained unconfirmed.

Twenty-seven sacks from the original discrepancy remained unexplained.

Sun Qiming had not yet determined whether the missing records represented an incomplete transfer, a counting error, or a separate movement that had never been entered into the surviving books.

He had therefore refused to write a conclusion.

Mingzhao appreciated this.

She did not particularly enjoy it.

There was a difference.

She sat in the accounting room with Sun Qiming, Steward Zhou, and the former warehouse steward Zhang.

The old steward had brought another bundle of papers.

The papers were folded and tied with faded string.

Sun Qiming examined them before opening the bundle.

"These are your personal notes?"

"Some are. Some were copied from the warehouse records before I retired."

"Are the dates original?"

"The dates were written at the time."

"By whom?"

"Mostly by me."

"Mostly?"

Zhang hesitated.

"Sometimes by the assistant steward."

Sun Qiming nodded.

He did not treat the uncertainty as proof of dishonesty.

"Then we should identify which entries are yours and which belong to the assistant."

Zhang looked relieved.

The old steward had expected an accusation.

He had not expected a question about handwriting.

Sun Qiming unfolded the first sheet.

It contained a list of repairs.

The northern warehouse roof had been damaged by heavy rain.

The repairs had required grain to be moved temporarily.

The movement note recorded twenty sacks.

The southern storage ledger recorded eighteen.

The two remaining sacks were not mentioned again.

The notes contained no additional quantity.

Sun Qiming placed the sheets side by side.

"This confirms the movement instruction, but it does not confirm the final destination of the two sacks."

Zhang nodded.

"That is correct."

Mingzhao looked at the papers.

"Could they have remained in the northern warehouse?"

"They could have," Zhang replied. "But the roof repairs were extensive. I cannot say whether all the grain was returned."

"Who supervised the work?"

"The repair foreman."

"Name?"

"Chen."

Mingzhao looked at Steward Zhou.

"Do we still have the repair foreman's records?"

Steward Zhou considered.

"Possibly. The work was arranged through the estate maintenance office."

"Then request the work records."

Sun Qiming added, "And any payment records for transporting the grain."

Mingzhao nodded.

The investigation had acquired another direction.

It had not acquired an answer.

That was acceptable.

She would rather have an incomplete truth than a convenient conclusion.

After the meeting, Steward Zhou remained behind.

"Young Miss, the household has received another question concerning the grain."

Mingzhao looked up.

"From whom?"

"Marquis Shen."

Her father had asked whether the discrepancy could be settled before the next clan meeting.

Mingzhao read the message.

It was politely worded.

It was also a request for a result by a date that had no connection to the availability of evidence.

She put it down.

"Reply that the examination is continuing."

"Should we mention the two sacks?"

"Yes. State that the movement note records twenty, the southern ledger records eighteen, and the destination of two remains unconfirmed. Also state that twenty-seven of the original forty-seven remain unexplained."

Steward Zhou nodded.

"Should we mention the possibility that they were returned to the northern warehouse?"

"Only as a possibility."

"Yes."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Do not make the report sound more certain than the evidence."

Steward Zhou accepted the instruction.

After he left, Qiao Yue placed a cup of tea beside Mingzhao.

"Your father wants the matter settled quickly."

"Then he should help locate the records."

Qiao Yue smiled.

"That would require him to do something."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Do not become too pleased with your own observation."

Qiao Yue lowered her head.

"Yes, Miss."

Mingzhao returned to the papers.

The next matter concerned the three hundred taels charged to Shen Mingyuan's residence.

The accountant had separated the amount.

Seventy-two taels related to repairs to Mingyuan's study and rooms.

The payment had been supported by the estate ledger and Carpenter Chen's testimony, but the original work order and receipt were still needed to complete verification.

The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels had not been explained.

Mingyuan had begun preparing his own statement.

He had written down the furniture he remembered purchasing, the repairs he had requested, and the gatherings held in his residence.

He had also admitted that he did not remember the details of several payments.

Mingzhao read the statement.

It was more useful than his earlier reply.

She passed it to Sun Qiming.

The accountant examined it.

"This identifies several possible expenses. We should compare them with the payment records."

"Can the remaining amount be assigned to him?" Mingzhao asked.

"Not yet. A statement of recollection is not a complete payment record."

"Then continue the examination."

Sun Qiming nodded.

Mingzhao looked at the amount.

Two hundred and twenty-eight taels.

It was large enough to matter.

It was also too easy to turn into a weapon.

She would not claim that her brother had spent the money merely because it had been charged to his residence.

Nor would she accept that the estate had spent it correctly merely because he said he did not remember.

The records would decide what could be established.

Everything else would remain unresolved.

At midday, Mingzhao received a letter from Lin Shuyun.

The merchant Zhou Wenqi had accepted the revised silk-sale terms in principle.

The inspection would take place before dispatch.

The buyer would examine the agreed samples and specifications.

Payment would be made upon delivery through an independent carrier.

A short period would be allowed for claims concerning concealed defects.

The final written contract and deposit had not yet arrived.

Lin Shuyun had added a note.

*The buyer is willing to proceed, but he wants the delivery schedule confirmed before signing. I have not promised a date.*

Mingzhao read the letter twice.

Then she wrote a reply.

She instructed Lin Shuyun not to reserve the entire stock until the contract was signed and the deposit received.

She also asked for a delivery schedule that would not interfere with other orders.

The partnership could not afford to treat an interested buyer as a completed sale.

Qiao Yue watched her fold the letter.

"Will the silk be sold?"

"If the contract is signed and the conditions are met."

"And if they are not?"

"Then it will not."

Qiao Yue considered this.

"That seems rather obvious."

"Many expensive mistakes begin with people deciding that an obvious condition can be ignored."

Qiao Yue accepted this explanation.

She had no intention of purchasing two hundred bolts of silk herself.

In the palace, Empress Dowager Wei had received the latest transition report.

She read the summary of duties transferred so far.

Residence repairs had been assigned to the appropriate administrators.

Ordinary purchases were being handled under existing allowances.

The medicine procedure remained under review.

Ceremonial responsibilities were being examined separately.

The Dowager placed the report on the table.

"Has the Empress stopped receiving these matters?"

Her attendant answered, "Some still reach Chengming Palace, Your Majesty."

"Why?"

"The offices are accustomed to sending them there."

The Dowager looked at the document.

"Then the habit has not changed."

"No, Your Majesty."

The Dowager was silent for a moment.

She had not opposed the Empress's petition because she believed Yuru incapable of fulfilling her duties.

Quite the opposite.

Yuru had fulfilled them so consistently that the palace had begun treating her competence as an ordinary resource.

The Dowager had benefited from that arrangement.

She had also come to rely upon it.

But the report showed that some of the Empress's work had been transferred without any immediate disorder.

That was difficult to dismiss.

"Has the Emperor decided anything?"

"Not yet, Your Majesty."

The Dowager nodded.

"Then send him the report."

Her attendant hesitated.

"Should Your Majesty include a recommendation?"

"No."

The Dowager looked again at the list.

"Let him decide what he intends to do with his Empress."

She paused.

"After all, he is the Emperor."

That afternoon, Yuru met with the senior palace administrators.

The medicine procedure had been revised.

It now distinguished ordinary prescriptions, unavailable medicines, disputed prescriptions, and emergencies.

The Imperial Pharmacy would maintain the stock record.

Physicians would remain responsible for prescriptions.

Residence administrators would record distribution.

The Imperial Household Administration would review consolidated reports and investigate discrepancies.

Madam Zhao presented the arrangement.

Yuru listened without interrupting.

When the presentation ended, she asked, "Who is responsible if the pharmacy records a medicine as available when the stock has already been exhausted?"

"The pharmacy administrator," Madam Zhao replied.

"Who is responsible if the physician prescribes a medicine without checking whether an appropriate substitute is available?"

"The physician must consult the pharmacy."

"And if the residence administrator records a medicine as distributed but the recipient does not receive it?"

"The residence administrator must account for the distribution, and the pharmacy record will be compared against it."

Yuru nodded.

"Good."

Madam Zhao looked at her.

"Your Majesty, should the procedure be submitted for final approval?"

"Yes. Submit it to the Imperial Household Administration and the appropriate authority."

"Should it also be sent to Your Majesty?"

"For information, if required."

Madam Zhao bowed.

The procedure was ready to be tested.

Yuru had not needed to write it herself.

She had not needed to summon every physician.

She had not needed to determine the location of every medicine container.

She had asked questions.

The administrators had answered them.

It was a small difference in appearance.

It was a considerable difference in responsibility.

The test began before the evening meal.

A physician prescribed a medicine that was not included in the ordinary pharmacy stock.

The pharmacy administrator reported the shortage.

The residence administrator asked whether a substitute was available.

The physician reviewed the alternatives and approved one.

The distribution was recorded.

The purchase request was sent to the appropriate office.

No report reached Chengming Palace.

At least, not immediately.

Instead, a consolidated note arrived later that evening.

It described the shortage, the approved substitute, the distribution, and the expected replenishment.

Yuru read it.

Fang Ru waited.

"Is there a problem?" she asked.

"No."

Yuru put down the report.

"Then it has been handled."

Fang Ru smiled slightly.

The smile was brief, but Yuru noticed.

"Are you pleased?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Why?"

"Because the report arrived after the matter was completed."

Yuru considered this.

"That is a reasonable reason."

Fang Ru bowed.

The Empress looked at the remaining documents.

"Tomorrow, we will review the next group."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru closed the folder.

She had not become less interested in the palace.

She had simply begun to allow other people to be responsible for it.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the day ended with another small success.

Mingyuan had reviewed the expenditure ledger.

Mingxiu had completed the agricultural summary after obtaining the steward's explanation.

Steward Zhou had authorized two ordinary purchases without referring them to Mingzhao.

The central clerk had recorded the transactions.

None of the tasks had required her intervention.

There had also been a disagreement.

The household cook had complained that the replacement pots were smaller than the previous ones.

Steward Zhou had examined the order.

The order specified the number of pots but not their capacity.

The kitchen steward had selected the smaller size because it was cheaper.

The cook wanted the larger pots.

Steward Zhou had asked whether the household allowance could cover the difference.

It could.

He had authorized the replacement.

The smaller pots had not yet been used.

Mingzhao heard the account from Qiao Yue.

"So the purchase was authorized correctly, but the specification was incomplete."

"Yes, Miss."

"And the matter was corrected?"

"Yes."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Then record the specification properly next time."

Qiao Yue smiled.

"Steward Zhou has already done so."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Good."

Qiao Yue waited.

"Miss, you did not ask to see the purchase request."

"I heard the explanation."

"You usually ask to see the original."

"I am not reviewing every pot in the household."

Qiao Yue looked at her with undisguised surprise.

Mingzhao raised an eyebrow.

"Do not look so astonished."

"I was only thinking that the household might survive without your examination of its cooking utensils."

"It had better."

Qiao Yue laughed.

Mingzhao returned to her desk.

The property division remained unresolved.

The warehouse discrepancy remained unresolved.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unexplained.

The house search had not produced a suitable residence.

The silk contract had not been signed.

There was still a great deal to do.

But the kitchen had received its pots.

The agricultural accounts had been corrected.

The monthly expenditure ledger had been reviewed.

The household was beginning to operate without sending every question to her.

Mingzhao found that she was not entirely comfortable with the result.

She had spent six years being the person everyone asked.

It was difficult to stop expecting the next question.

That evening, the Emperor received the report on the medicine procedure.

He read it once.

Then he read the summary of the latest transition.

Two groups of responsibilities had been transferred.

One group remained under testing.

Ceremonial duties were still under separate review.

The Emperor put down the report.

Gao Shun stood beside the desk.

"Your Majesty, the Empress has not sent another petition."

"She does not need to."

"Should I ask Chengming Palace whether Her Majesty requires anything?"

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

"Why?"

Gao Shun hesitated.

"I thought—"

"Do not ask whether she requires something simply because she has stopped asking us to do everything."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The Emperor returned to the report.

After a moment, he said, "The palace has begun to function."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"That should have been possible years ago."

Gao Shun did not answer.

There were observations that did not require an attendant's agreement.

The Emperor looked at the final page.

The report contained a list of responsibilities transferred, the names of the responsible offices, and the procedures for review.

At the bottom was a short note:

*No exceptional intervention by the Empress was required during the test period.*

Zhao Jingheng read it twice.

Then he placed the document beside the others.

The palace had finally learned to complete a task without asking the Empress to finish it.

Unfortunately, the Emperor had not yet learned what to do with an Empress who was no longer required to finish everything.

**End of Chapter 17.**
