# **Chapter 36: The Former Empress Has Received a Second Invitation from Her Family. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Attended a Property Meeting. The Family Has Discovered That Dividing an Estate Is Easier When Nobody Pretends the Numbers Are Finished.**

**28th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The first disagreement at the Qingyuan Marquisate's property meeting concerned a chair.

It was not an important chair.

It was an ordinary wooden chair with a slightly loose backrest, placed near the end of the long table in the eastern study.

Unfortunately, it had been assigned to the wrong person.

Mr. Chen, the family's legal adviser, had arrived early. Sun Qiming, the independent accountant, arrived shortly afterward. Master Luo brought the property valuation documents, while Mr. Liang represented the clan.

Steward Zhou had arranged the seats according to the previous meeting's attendance list.

That list had placed Sun Qiming beside Shen Guowei.

The new arrangement placed Mr. Chen there instead.

Sun Qiming looked at the seating plan.

Mr. Chen looked at the seating plan.

Mr. Liang looked at both men.

Steward Zhou stood nearby, waiting.

"Is there a problem?" he asked.

Sun Qiming replied calmly, "I have no objection to sitting anywhere. I only wish to know whether I am attending as the independent accountant or as a guest."

Steward Zhou hesitated.

Mr. Chen cleared his throat.

"The arrangement is merely for convenience."

"Then I shall sit wherever there is space," Sun Qiming replied.

He moved the chair to the opposite side of the table.

The loose backrest made a faint cracking sound.

Everyone looked at it.

Sun Qiming sat down carefully.

"Perhaps this chair should be repaired."

Steward Zhou immediately instructed a servant to bring another.

The meeting had not yet begun.

Already, the household had discovered a maintenance issue.

Shen Mingzhao arrived shortly afterward.

She carried a document case and was accompanied by Qiao Yue.

Her arrival was greeted by Shen Guowei, Madam Liang, Shen Mingyuan, Shen Mingxiu, and Old Madam Shen.

Mingzhao greeted each person.

She then placed her documents on the table.

Shen Guowei looked at the case.

"You have brought the records?"

"Yes."

"All of them?"

"Those relevant to the proposed division."

He nodded.

Mr. Liang opened the meeting.

"The purpose today is to establish which categories of property can be considered confirmed, which require further assessment, and which claims must remain unresolved."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That is acceptable."

Mr. Chen opened the legal summary.

"The family proposes that the estate be divided into four broad categories: fixed property, movable property, commercial interests, and outstanding obligations."

Mingzhao looked at the document.

"Those categories are too broad."

Mr. Chen paused.

"Which part do you object to?"

"Fixed property must distinguish registered land from buildings, leased land, and disputed boundaries. Movable property must distinguish personal belongings from estate equipment and household goods. Commercial interests must distinguish partnership capital from individual investment. Outstanding obligations must distinguish confirmed debts from claims still requiring evidence."

Mr. Chen examined the summary again.

"Those distinctions can be added."

"Then they should be added before any division is proposed."

Shen Guowei looked at her.

"Mingzhao, we are only discussing the framework."

"Then the framework should be accurate."

There was a short silence.

Old Madam Shen adjusted her sleeves.

"She is right about the categories."

Madam Liang glanced at her.

"Mother—"

"The purpose of a settlement is to avoid disputes later. Why should we object to clarity?"

Madam Liang closed her mouth.

Mr. Liang nodded.

"Let us proceed with the more detailed categories."

Sun Qiming began with the property records.

He placed several documents on the table.

"The confirmed agricultural properties have been separated from the disputed parcels. The leased fields are recorded according to their existing contracts. Fallow land is listed separately, as are buildings requiring maintenance."

Shen Guowei examined the papers.

"Can we establish the total value?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"Because the values of the buildings and land have not been combined with the outstanding lease payments. The maintenance costs also require separate treatment."

Mingzhao looked at the valuation summary.

"Have the northern warehouse repairs been included?"

"Only the estimated structural repairs. The grain stock discrepancy is not part of the building valuation."

"Good."

Sun Qiming turned a page.

"The warehouse structure is sound. Some drainage work remains necessary. The building's value can be assessed independently of the stock held inside it."

Shen Mingyuan leaned forward.

"Then the warehouse itself is not the problem?"

"The building is not the source of the current discrepancy."

Sun Qiming looked at him.

"The grain records are a separate matter."

Mingyuan nodded.

He had learned that a building and its contents were not interchangeable merely because both appeared in the same warehouse report.

It was a small improvement.

It was also the sort of improvement that had taken several weeks to achieve.

The next subject was the disputed northern canal property.

Master Luo opened his survey documents.

"The roof repairs are estimated at approximately one hundred and twenty taels. Drainage repairs are estimated at thirty taels. The kitchen requires less work than originally expected. The well stones require attention."

Mingzhao listened.

"And the boundary?"

Master Luo looked at the survey assistant.

The assistant produced the relevant drawing.

"The drainage channel extends slightly beyond the older registered line. The original boundary markers are missing. The neighbouring owner disputes the extent of the property."

"Can the exact area be established?"

"Not from the existing documents."

"Can the older records be obtained?"

"Possibly. We have not yet found the complete survey register."

Mr. Chen examined the drawing.

"If the registered boundary is confirmed, the dispute may be resolved without further litigation."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"That is possible. It is not yet established."

Mr. Chen nodded.

"Correct."

Shen Guowei looked at the valuation.

"Can we assign a provisional value?"

"Only with a clear notation that the disputed area is excluded or separately assessed," Sun Qiming replied.

Mingzhao agreed.

"Then record it that way."

Madam Liang looked at her daughter.

"You are considering purchasing this property?"

"I am considering it."

"Even with the repairs?"

"Repair costs can be estimated. Uncertain ownership cannot."

Master Luo nodded.

That was a reasonable distinction.

The property might still be worth purchasing.

It might also become an expensive dispute.

Neither possibility could be settled by calling it convenient.

The meeting moved to the personal claims.

Sun Qiming opened a separate ledger.

"The documented seventy-two taels paid toward the repairs of the Second Young Master's study have been verified. The original receipt, authorization, and carpenter's testimony support the payment."

Shen Mingyuan looked uncomfortable.

"I have already explained that I did not personally arrange the repair."

"That is not the question," Mingzhao said.

He looked at her.

"The payment was an estate maintenance expense. It should not be counted as a personal advance to you."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Agreed."

Sun Qiming continued.

"The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels have not been verified."

Shen Guowei frowned.

"Can we not simply record them as a household expense?"

"No," Sun Qiming replied. "Not until the supporting documents are found or the nature of the payments is established."

Mingzhao folded her hands.

"That amount should remain unclassified."

Madam Liang looked at her.

"Are you suggesting your brother owes the family two hundred and twenty-eight taels?"

"No."

"Then what are you suggesting?"

"That we do not assign the amount to anyone without evidence."

The answer was so direct that Madam Liang had nothing immediate to say.

Mingyuan looked at the ledger.

He had initially believed the matter could be resolved by explaining that the study had been repaired.

Now he understood that one documented repair did not explain every payment recorded under the same general heading.

He turned to Sun Qiming.

"I will continue checking the old household records."

"Please do," the accountant replied.

The next section concerned Mingzhao's personal advances to the family.

Sun Qiming placed the latest summary before the participants.

"Twenty-three entries were initially uncertain. Nine are now confirmed advances. Four have confirmed payments but incomplete repayment arrangements. Ten remain uncertain."

Shen Guowei examined the figures.

"Can the nine be included in the settlement?"

"Yes, subject to the supporting documents already collected."

"And the four?"

"They should be listed as partially confirmed claims. The payment itself is established, but the terms of repayment are not sufficiently clear."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That is acceptable."

Madam Liang looked at the list.

"Some of these payments were made years ago. Why were they not discussed at the time?"

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Because I was managing the household."

"That does not answer the question."

"It does. The payments were made to settle household obligations. I did not separate every transaction into a personal claim at the time."

"Then why claim them now?"

"Because the household is being divided, and the records show that some of my personal funds were used."

Madam Liang's expression tightened.

Mingzhao continued.

"If the evidence shows a payment was a gift, it should be classified as a gift. If it was a family expense, it should be classified as a family expense. If it was an advance, it should be recorded as an advance."

Old Madam Shen nodded.

"That is fair."

Madam Liang looked down.

She had wanted the matter to be simpler.

Unfortunately, the documents had no interest in making it simple.

The commercial partnership was discussed next.

Sun Qiming turned to the silk records.

"The partnership with Lin Shuyun is separate from the Marquisate's estate. Its stock, payments, production costs, and outstanding obligations are maintained in its own accounts."

Mr. Chen looked at the documents.

"Does the partnership contain any capital belonging to the family?"

"No such ownership has been established," Sun Qiming replied.

Mingzhao added, "The partnership was formed under my independent commercial arrangement with Lin Shuyun. The stock and payments are recorded separately."

Shen Guowei looked at her.

"How much has the partnership earned?"

"Some deliveries have been accepted and paid. Production expenses remain outstanding. There is no final profit calculation."

"Can it be valued?"

"Not accurately until the remaining stock is inspected and the costs are reconciled."

Sun Qiming nodded.

"That is correct."

Mingzhao closed the folder.

"The partnership should not be included in the divisible family estate."

Mr. Chen made a note.

The distinction was important.

A business did not become family property simply because one of its partners belonged to an aristocratic household.

Nor did a family claim ownership merely because the business had once operated in a building connected to the family.

The records had to establish the actual relationship.

By midday, the meeting had produced a new list of unresolved matters.

It was longer than the original list.

Shen Guowei stared at it.

"Have we made progress?"

Sun Qiming replied, "Yes."

The Marquis looked at him.

"The list is longer."

"Because we have separated matters that were previously combined."

"That sounds like more work."

"It is more work."

Shen Guowei leaned back.

Mingzhao almost smiled.

She did not.

The purpose of the meeting was not to make the family feel that the settlement was nearly complete.

It was to establish what could actually be settled.

Mr. Liang reviewed the minutes.

"The confirmed categories can proceed to valuation and allocation. The disputed property, unverified expenses, and incomplete claims will remain separate."

"Agreed," Mingzhao said.

Shen Guowei nodded reluctantly.

"Agreed."

Madam Liang looked at the documents.

"Then when will the division be finished?"

Sun Qiming answered before anyone else.

"When the necessary records are complete."

Madam Liang closed her eyes briefly.

It was not the answer she had hoped to receive.

It was, however, the only answer the accountant could responsibly give.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Qiao Yue was preparing lunch when a messenger arrived.

He carried a note from Lin Shuyun.

Mingzhao read it after returning from the meeting.

Lin Shuyun reported that the next group of silk had entered the final finishing stage. The two bolts returned for correction were being worked on again. The remaining production schedule had been adjusted to allow a more careful inspection.

Mingzhao read the note twice.

She then wrote a reply.

She asked Lin Shuyun to retain the rejected inspection records, record any additional dyeing costs, and avoid promising a delivery date until the finishing work was complete.

Qiao Yue looked at the reply.

"Does Miss Lin think you are too strict?"

"She knows what the contract requires."

"That is not quite an answer."

Mingzhao sealed the letter.

"Lin Shuyun has her own business to protect. She would not thank me for accepting defective goods merely to make the delivery schedule look better."

Qiao Yue considered this.

"Then the two of you are equally troublesome."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"That is one way to describe a partnership."

At the Xu family residence, Yuru was preparing to return to her temporary residence.

Madam Xu had invited her to stay for another night.

Yuru declined politely.

"I would like to return today."

"Then we will not keep you."

The Duke looked disappointed.

"You could stay until tomorrow."

"I have already imposed on your household for the afternoon."

Madam Xu gave him a look.

"She is our daughter. She has not imposed on us."

The Duke nodded.

"That is true."

Yuru smiled.

"I will visit again."

Madam Xu took her hand.

"Come whenever you wish. There is no need to send a formal letter every time."

Yuru looked at her.

"Then I shall remember that."

The Duke accompanied her to the carriage.

Before she entered, he said, "Your rooms will remain ready."

"Thank you, Father."

He hesitated.

"Do not let anyone persuade you to take on another household's affairs simply because you are capable of doing so."

Yuru looked at him.

For a moment, she wondered whether he was speaking about the palace or his own residence.

Then she smiled.

"I will try."

The Duke seemed satisfied.

He had not noticed that his own steward had already asked whether the eastern courtyard's monthly expenditure should be submitted to Yuru for review.

The Duke had rejected the request before she arrived.

The steward had not asked again.

In the palace, the Emperor received the day's administrative summary.

The Imperial Household Administration had classified the firewood purchase.

It was a kitchen provision expense.

The supporting delivery note had been attached.

The payment would be processed through the ordinary household account.

The emperor read the report.

"Good."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Your Majesty, the office has also requested clarification concerning the replacement of damaged serving trays."

The emperor looked up.

"Why?"

"The trays were used during ceremonial meals, but they are stored with ordinary kitchen equipment."

"Who maintains them?"

"The kitchen administration."

"Who determines whether they are required for ceremonies?"

"The Department of Rites provides the ceremonial requirements."

"Then the two offices should exchange the relevant records."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The emperor paused.

"Do they need me to approve the exchange of records?"

"No, Your Majesty."

"Then why is the request here?"

Gao Shun lowered his head.

"I shall return it."

The emperor resumed writing.

He had once believed the palace needed a more efficient manager.

Now he was discovering that it needed fewer people asking the emperor to approve ordinary communication.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao returned to her household ledger.

The day's expenses were entered.

The vegetable receipt had been matched.

The cooking oil stock was checked.

The servants' provisions were recorded.

Her rental expenses remained separate from the family settlement.

She placed the latest property meeting minutes in a new folder.

The meeting had not concluded the division.

It had, however, clarified several important points.

The seventy-two-tael repair expense was no longer disputed.

The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unresolved.

Nine advances were confirmed.

Four were partially confirmed.

Ten remained uncertain.

The northern canal property's boundary remained disputed.

The grain discrepancy remained open.

The silk partnership remained independent.

Mingzhao read the summary.

Then she added one final line:

*No final allocation agreed.*

Qiao Yue looked over her shoulder.

"That seems disappointing."

"It is accurate."

"Will there be another meeting?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"When the necessary documents are ready."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Then perhaps we should prepare more tea."

Mingzhao closed the ledger.

"Prepare enough for everyone."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"Should I also order another chair?"

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Why?"

"The last meeting had a chair problem."

Mingzhao considered this.

Then, despite herself, she laughed.

That evening, Yuru sat in her study at the temporary residence.

She had received a short message from the emperor.

The palace was functioning.

The revised reporting arrangements had been followed.

No urgent matter required her attention.

She read the note and placed it aside.

A little later, Fang Ru entered with tea.

"Madam, the Duke of Xu has sent another invitation."

Yuru looked up.

"Another invitation?"

"He asks whether you would like to visit again next week."

Yuru smiled.

"Tell him I will consider it."

Fang Ru nodded.

Then she hesitated.

"There is one more matter."

"What is it?"

"The household at the Xu residence asked whether you might be willing to review their annual accounts during your next visit."

Yuru slowly put down her teacup.

Fang Ru looked at her.

"Shall I decline?"

Yuru considered the question.

Then she smiled.

"Tell them I am visiting as their daughter."

Fang Ru bowed.

"And if they ask again?"

"Tell them their daughter is not an accounting office."

Fang Ru lowered her head.

Her shoulders moved slightly.

Yuru looked at her.

"Are you laughing?"

"No, Madam."

"You are."

"I am trying not to."

Yuru laughed too.

For the first time in many years, she could refuse a household request without explaining why the accounts should be reviewed by someone else.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had reached a similar conclusion.

The family could not finish its settlement by asking her to correct the records.

The palace could not complete its transition by asking Yuru to manage every remaining inconvenience.

Both households were learning that the departure of a capable woman did not mean that her responsibilities had disappeared.

It meant that everyone else would finally have to identify which responsibilities belonged to them.
