# **Chapter 100: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Household Manual Can Assign Five Duties to Five Servants and Still Leave the Household Head Responsible for Finding the Sixth. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That an Authorized Order Is Not Yet a Delivered Order. The Marquis's Heir Has Received a Request to Explain Twenty-Seven Missing Sacks and Has Found That the Records Contain Twenty-Seven Missing Answers Instead.**

**4th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had reached the chapter concerning household expenditure.

The author began with a sensible recommendation: every household should establish a regular allowance for ordinary expenses and a separate reserve for unexpected repairs.

Yuru approved.

The author then advised that the household head should personally examine all expenditure requests before money was released.

Yuru turned the page.

The next paragraph recommended that the household head also inspect the receipts, verify the quantities, compare the prices, and question the servants responsible for purchasing.

Yuru stopped reading.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Does this author believe that a household head has no other occupation?"

Fang Ru glanced at the page.

"He appears to believe that household management is the occupation."

"Then he should have written a book explaining how the household head is supposed to eat."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru returned to the passage.

There was a distinction the author had not considered. A household head could establish limits, require records, and appoint people responsible for particular duties. The head could review unusual expenses and examine the accounts periodically.

But if every purchase required personal approval, the household would eventually become incapable of buying a broom without waiting for the head to finish reading a report about the previous broom.

Yuru took her notebook.

She wrote:

*Ordinary expenditure should follow an established rule. Unusual expenditure should be reported. A person who approves a purchase should not be the only person who confirms its receipt.*

She paused.

The final sentence was familiar.

It had appeared in several of the palace's old supply arrangements. She had once insisted on separating approval from receipt after discovering that one office had been expected to authorize, purchase, receive, and record the same goods.

The rule had been correct.

The difficulty had been ensuring that the offices followed it after she stopped personally reminding them.

Yuru looked at the notebook.

She had no intention of sending the passage to the palace.

The Crown Prince was already learning to distinguish quantities purchased from quantities received. The household offices were establishing their own procedures. If she continued sending every useful observation to them, she would simply become the author of another manual that everyone praised and no one knew how to implement without her.

She closed the notebook.

"Fang Ru, I think I shall read the next chapter tomorrow."

"Because the author has exhausted your patience?"

"Because I have exhausted the tea."

Fang Ru looked at the cup.

It was empty.

Yuru stood and walked toward the window.

The morning was clear, and the western path was dry.

She decided to walk before the heat increased.

At the eastern-market workshop, Shen Mingzhao received the latest production record.

The first thirty bolts had passed internal inspection.

The next twenty had also passed after two minor corrections.

The final thirty were now authorized and beginning production.

The supervisor had submitted a revised schedule showing the expected completion of the remaining work.

Mingzhao read it carefully.

The production schedule was reasonable, but the report contained one sentence she did not like:

*The order is now substantially complete.*

She drew a line beneath it.

Qiao Yue, standing beside the desk, looked at the correction.

"Is that not true?"

"No."

"Thirty bolts remain."

"Thirty bolts remain in production. None of the goods have been inspected by the buyer or delivered."

"But the workshop has completed most of the work."

"That is not the same as completing the order."

Mingzhao wrote a brief note.

The report should state that fifty bolts had passed internal inspection, while thirty remained in production. The order would not be described as complete until the buyer had inspected the goods, delivery had been confirmed, and the contractual payment arrangements had been fulfilled.

Qiao Yue took the corrected paper.

Mingzhao looked at the schedule again.

The workshop had made steady progress.

The afternoon inspection arrangement had worked without changing the approved sample. The additional inspection costs were recorded separately. The workers had corrected small defects before they became larger problems.

That was good.

But a good production record was not a delivery receipt.

She placed the schedule beside the contract.

"Ask the supervisor to send the next report after the remaining thirty bolts have passed internal inspection."

"Will you visit the workshop then?"

"Not unless there is a problem that cannot be resolved by the supervisor and inspector."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao returned to her accounts.

She had learned that a business could be harmed by two opposite habits.

One was to ignore details until a problem became expensive.

The other was to inspect every detail so personally that no one else learned how to manage it.

She intended to avoid both.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received a formal inquiry from the family council.

The inquiry asked him to explain the discrepancy in the northern warehouse grain records.

It was not a hostile document.

It was, however, written as though the explanation already existed and Mingyuan merely had to provide it.

The request listed the figures:

Forty-seven sacks in the original stock record.

Twenty sacks covered by the instruction.

Eighteen sacks confirmed as received.

Two sacks unconfirmed.

Twenty-seven sacks unexplained.

It then asked whether the missing twenty-seven sacks had been included in the four-cart dispatch recorded in the loose northern warehouse pages.

Mingyuan read the question twice.

He took a sheet of paper and began his reply.

The four-cart entry had no confirmed cargo description.

The destination was incomplete.

The movement notebook had been issued to the northern warehouse transport supervisor, but the surviving issue record did not identify the individual who received it.

The personnel register showed that two men had held the supervisor's position during the relevant period. Neither had yet been confirmed as the person responsible for the dispatch.

No arrival record had been found.

No evidence connected the four carts to the twenty-seven unexplained sacks.

Mingyuan stopped writing.

The family council had asked whether the sacks were included in the four-cart dispatch.

The correct answer was not yes.

It was not no.

It was that the records did not establish whether they were connected.

He wrote that sentence.

Then he added that the transport inquiry and grain reconciliation should remain separate until evidence established a relationship between them.

He signed the reply.

Liang Wen read it before carrying it to the family council.

"Will they be satisfied?"

Mingyuan looked at the paper.

"They asked for an explanation."

"Yes."

"Then they have received the explanation the records support."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan returned to the archive table.

He had once believed that an accounts clerk's duty was to produce a balanced total.

Now he was discovering that sometimes the most important work was preventing an unsupported total from being produced.

The grain account remained unresolved.

The four-cart dispatch remained unexplained.

The missing movement notebook remained missing.

The old payment voucher attributed to Mingzhao remained unlocated.

The property division remained incomplete.

Mingyuan had no desire to make these matters sound more settled than they were.

The family council received his reply before the afternoon session.

One of the senior members read it aloud.

"Twenty-seven sacks unexplained. Four-cart dispatch not connected. Movement notebook not located."

He frowned.

"That is not much of an answer."

Mingyuan, who had been called to clarify the report, stood beside the table.

"It is the answer supported by the records."

"Surely the carts carried something."

"Certainly."

"Then what did they carry?"

"The dispatch record does not say."

The senior member looked irritated.

"How can a warehouse send four carts and fail to record the cargo?"

Mingyuan did not pretend to know.

"The record may have been incomplete when written. The missing notebook may have contained the information. We have not found it."

Another council member asked, "Can the former supervisor be questioned?"

"Once the personnel office confirms which supervisor held the duty on the date of dispatch."

"Why not question both?"

"We may do so. But the inquiry should distinguish between a person who held the position and a person who actually handled the dispatch."

The council members exchanged looks.

They had expected a name.

Mingyuan had brought them two possible names and a reason not to choose one prematurely.

The senior member finally turned to the grain schedule.

"Then the twenty-seven sacks remain unexplained."

"Yes."

The answer was plain.

It was also the first time the council had heard the discrepancy stated without an attempt to attach it to a convenient transaction.

Mingyuan bowed.

The meeting ended without a resolution.

He returned to the accounts office.

There was still work to do.

At her residence, Mingzhao received the corrected property schedule from the Marquisate.

This time, the document distinguished the categories more carefully.

The nine confirmed advances were listed with their supporting records.

The four payment-confirmed entries were described as payments whose repayment arrangements remained unclear.

The ten uncertain entries were not included in the confirmed total.

The seventy-two taels for the study repairs were classified as estate maintenance paid from Mingzhao's personal funds, with reimbursement undecided.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified and disputed.

The furnishing payment remained estate expenditure.

The northern canal property was excluded from the family division.

Mingzhao read the schedule slowly.

She found that one of the supporting references for a confirmed advance pointed to a copy rather than the original receipt.

That was not necessarily an error.

The original might be in the estate archive.

She marked the reference and wrote a short question asking whether the original receipt had been located or whether the copy was the only surviving record.

She did not reject the entry.

She did not accept it without clarification.

Qiao Yue looked at the schedule.

"Is it better than the previous one?"

"Yes."

"Will you attend the meeting now?"

"Not until the supporting documents are ready and the family proposes a date."

Mingzhao returned the schedule to its envelope.

The family had made progress.

It had not completed the division.

She was willing to acknowledge the first without pretending the second had happened.

That afternoon, Xu Yuru returned from her walk.

The path had been pleasant, and the pavilion was quiet.

Fang Ru brought tea and the household manual.

Yuru looked at the book.

"Have we reached another chapter about inspecting something?"

"Today it is the household accounts."

Yuru laughed.

"Then I shall leave it until tomorrow."

She sat by the window and read a letter from her father.

Duke Xu had replied to her earlier observation about the author's insistence on personal supervision.

He agreed that the author had confused responsibility with constant attendance. He also admitted that several of the household's own procedures had been established only because Madam Xu had insisted on dividing duties more clearly.

Yuru smiled at that.

Her father had not written a long explanation.

He had simply acknowledged the point.

She folded the letter and placed it in her notebook.

There was no need to answer immediately.

She could write tomorrow.

The afternoon was still hers.

At the workshop, the final thirty bolts continued in production.

The supervisor's new schedule showed that the remaining work could be completed within the agreed delivery period.

Mingzhao reviewed the estimate.

The materials were accounted for.

The labour allocation was reasonable.

The inspection costs had been included.

She approved the schedule.

No additional order had been accepted.

No buyer inspection had taken place.

No delivery had been made.

No balance had been received.

The current order remained a work in progress.

Mingzhao placed the papers in the contract folder.

The workshop had learned to record what was complete and what was not.

The Marquisate was learning the same lesson.

At the palace guest residence, Yuru had begun writing questions rather than instructions.

Three different lives were moving forward through unfinished work.

None had reached a final settlement.

But the records were becoming clearer, the responsibilities were being divided more carefully, and no one had been forced to pretend that a missing answer was a completed one.

For the first time in a long while, that seemed like a satisfactory day's work.
