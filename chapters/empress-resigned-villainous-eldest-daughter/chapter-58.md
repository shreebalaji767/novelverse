# **Chapter 58: The Former Empress Has Returned from a Garden Visit and Has Been Asked Whether She Enjoyed Herself. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Approved a Faster Folding Method and Has Been Asked Whether It Will Increase Profits. Both Women Have Discovered That People Can Turn Any Answer into Another Question.**

**22nd Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



Xu Yuru had been back at the guest residence for less than a day when the Emperor sent a message.



It was not a summons.



It was not a request for advice.



It was a question.



*Did you enjoy yesterday's visit?*



The former Empress read it while sitting beside the window.



Fang Ru waited.



Xu Yuru considered the wording.



The Emperor had not asked whether the garden was properly maintained. He had not asked whether the pavilion was comfortable. He had not asked whether the Dowager's attendants had followed the correct arrangement.



He had asked whether she had enjoyed herself.



Xu Yuru found this a surprisingly difficult question to answer in writing.



She took a sheet of paper.



*The visit was pleasant. The path was comfortable, the tea was good, and the conversation was ordinary.*



She paused.



Then she added:



*I particularly appreciated the absence of documents.*



Fang Ru glanced at the final sentence.



"Madam, shall I send it?"



"Yes."



Fang Ru folded the reply.



The former Empress returned to her book.



A short while later, another message arrived.



The Emperor had read her answer.



He had also appreciated the absence of documents.



Xu Yuru placed the second message beside the first.



She did not reply.



The conversation had reached a natural conclusion.



She was determined not to improve it.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingzhao had been asked whether the revised folding method would increase profits.



The question came from a family representative who had been reviewing the commercial schedule.



Mingzhao read it.



Then she read it again.



The revised method had reduced handling time.



The buyer had approved it conditionally.



The workshop had not yet completed the full production comparison.



The new order had a deposit.



The existing forty-two-bolt group had been accepted and paid for.



The finishing costs remained under calculation.



No net profit had been established.



Mingzhao placed the question beside the ledger.



Qiao Yue stood nearby.



"Will it increase profits?"



"It may reduce labour time."



"That sounds profitable."



"It may also require additional inspection."



"That sounds less profitable."



Mingzhao looked at her.



Qiao Yue bowed.



"I am only observing."



"Observe quietly."



Qiao Yue became quiet.



Mingzhao took up her brush.



She wrote a reply.



*The revised method is expected to reduce handling time. Its effect on total cost cannot yet be determined. The workshop will record the results after the first completed batch. No profit figure should be entered before production costs, wages, inspection, transport, and other agreed expenses have been reconciled.*



She read the answer.



It was precise.



It was also longer than the question.



Mingzhao sent it.



The family representative would have to wait.

At the Imperial Palace, the Crown Prince had received a report about the Eastern Palace's oil records.



The clerk had completed the first reconciliation.



Several entries had been matched.



Some remained uncertain.



One container had been recorded in a unit that did not appear elsewhere in the same register.



The Crown Prince read the report.



"Has the original entry been preserved?"



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Has the uncertain unit been converted?"



"No."



"Good."



The clerk bowed.



The Crown Prince examined the page.



He had become less comfortable with totals that appeared neat.



A total could be correct.



A total could also conceal an error.



He had learned that the difference was not visible from the final number alone.



"Continue the review," he said. "Do not alter the original register."



The clerk withdrew.



The Crown Prince looked at the report.



It was not complete.



But it was honest.



He considered that a useful improvement.

In the western palace residence, Consort Liu received an invitation to attend a small gathering.



The invitation had been issued by a senior palace lady.



The gathering was intended to discuss the coming season's household arrangements.



Consort Liu read the invitation.



Then she asked her attendant, "Is this an administrative meeting?"



The attendant hesitated.



"It appears to be a personal gathering, Your Highness."



"Then why does it mention household arrangements?"



"The hostess may wish to discuss them."



Consort Liu folded the invitation.



She had spent enough time in the palace to understand that a personal gathering could become an administrative meeting if the right person brought the right question.



She did not dislike administration.



She disliked being invited to a gathering under one description and finding herself expected to perform another duty.



"Reply that I will attend," she said.



The attendant bowed.



Consort Liu added, "And ask whether the discussion is intended to be formal."



The attendant paused.



"Should I ask directly?"



"Yes."



Consort Liu returned to her embroidery.



She had no intention of arriving at a tea gathering and discovering that she had been appointed to chair a committee.

At the eastern market, the workshop began recording the revised folding method.



The written instruction included the original sample, the approved revised sample, and the buyer's conditions.



The senior finisher demonstrated the method.



The experienced workers followed.



The trainees watched.



One trainee completed the first bolt quickly.



The second completed it more slowly.



Both were inspected.



The first had folded the edge correctly but needed to improve the consistency of the corner.



The second had maintained the corner but required more time.



The senior finisher marked their results.



Mingzhao examined the record.



"Do not write that the first trainee is better."



The finisher looked up.



"His work was faster."



"His work was faster in this sample."



The man considered the distinction.



"Yes, Miss Shen."



"And do not write that the second trainee is worse."



"Her work was slower."



"Her work was slower in this sample."



The finisher bowed.



Mingzhao looked at the two records.



The trainees had different strengths.



The workshop needed both speed and accuracy.



It did not need a ranking based on one morning's work.



Lin Shuyun arrived with the production schedule.



"The first batch can begin tomorrow," she said.



"After the written instructions are copied for the workers."



"They will be."



"And the inspection arrangement?"



"One experienced worker will check each trainee's work."



Mingzhao nodded.



The method was now approved.



The production could proceed.



The result remained uncertain.



That was not a contradiction.



It was simply the stage of the work.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received a letter from the old warehouse steward Zhang.



The letter did not contain a confession.



It did not identify the person responsible for the missing grain.



It did not explain the blank-destination transport payment.



It contained several practical details about the old warehouse's recordkeeping.



Zhang recalled that the warehouse had maintained separate sheets for total stock and stock available for immediate distribution.



He also recalled that some entries had been copied into a later register without the original headings.



Mingyuan read the letter carefully.



He took it to Steward Zhou.



"Does this explain the difference between the two books?"



"It may explain why their totals differ."



"Does it explain the missing twenty-seven sacks?"



"No, Young Master."



Mingyuan nodded.



The original count had recorded forty-seven sacks.



The movement instruction had recorded twenty.



Eighteen had been confirmed at the southern storage.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



The difference between total stock and available stock could explain why two registers appeared inconsistent.



It could not explain where the twenty-seven sacks had gone.



Mingyuan placed Zhang's letter beside the earlier records.



"Enter this as a statement about the warehouse's recordkeeping."



"Yes, Young Master."



"Do not enter it as proof of delivery."



The steward bowed.



Mingyuan looked at the papers.



He was beginning to understand why his sister had refused to accept an explanation merely because it sounded reasonable.



A reasonable explanation could still be incomplete.



And an incomplete explanation could become a false conclusion if someone wrote it into the final account too early.

In the guest residence, Xu Yuru received a visit from Madam Zhao, the senior palace administrator.



Madam Zhao bowed.



"Madam, the transition records have been updated."



Xu Yuru looked at her.



"Have they been submitted to the appropriate offices?"



"Yes."



"Have the unresolved items been marked?"



"Yes."



"Then the work is complete for now."



Madam Zhao hesitated.



"There are two matters that require clarification."



Xu Yuru set down her book.



"Which matters?"



"The inventory of the writing table and chairs."



Xu Yuru's expression remained calm.



"Have they been confirmed as my property?"



"No, Madam."



"Then they remain in the palace inventory."



"Yes."



"The robe purchased through palace stores?"



"The original order has not been found."



"Then it remains there as well."



Madam Zhao bowed.



She had expected the former Empress to request further investigation.



Instead, Xu Yuru had accepted the uncertainty.



The table could remain in the palace.



The chairs could remain in the palace.



The robe could remain in the palace.



The world would not end because an object had not yet been assigned to the correct owner.



Madam Zhao looked at the former Empress.



"Should I continue searching for the original order?"



"If the records require it."



"Yes, Madam."



Xu Yuru picked up her book again.



Madam Zhao withdrew.



The visit had taken only a few minutes.



No new duty had been created.



Xu Yuru considered this another successful morning.

That evening, the Emperor received a short report from Gao Shun.



The garden visit had been completed.



The former Empress had returned safely.



The Empress Dowager had been pleased.



The pavilion had been left in good order.



The fruit had been consumed.



The Emperor read the report.



Then he looked at Gao Shun.



"Why is there a report about the fruit?"



Gao Shun bowed.



"The report records the visit's arrangements, Your Majesty."



"Does it record how much fruit was consumed?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



The Emperor placed the report aside.



He had intended to ask whether the former Empress had enjoyed the visit.



He had received an answer.



He had intended to make certain that the visit remained informal.



It had remained informal.



He had intended to avoid giving her another administrative responsibility.



He had succeeded.



For once, the Emperor did not ask Gao Shun to prepare a follow-up.



He dismissed him.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao reviewed the day's production record.



The first samples had been completed.



The revised method had saved handling time.



The corner consistency required further attention.



The trainees' results had been recorded separately.



The buyer's condition had been followed.



The workshop had not yet completed the full batch.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



Lin Shuyun sat opposite her.



"Will you tell the family representative that the method is successful?"



"No."



"Will you tell him that it has failed?"



"No."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"Then what will you tell him?"



Mingzhao looked at the record.



"That the first samples are promising, and the full result remains pending."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



"That is a very small answer."



"It is the correct answer."



The workshop continued working.



The palace continued functioning.



The Marquisate continued examining its accounts.



No one had discovered a miraculous solution.



No one had completed every task.



But the former Empress had spent a garden visit without being made responsible for the garden.



The eldest daughter had approved a method without pretending that approval was profit.



The Crown Prince had preserved an uncertain entry.



Mingyuan had recorded a lead without turning it into proof.



And the Great Yong Dynasty had once again discovered that a matter could remain unfinished without becoming an emergency.



This was progress.



It was also, in the opinion of several senior officials, an extremely inconvenient form of progress.
