# **Chapter 34: The Former Empress Has Been Offered a Permanent Residence. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Offered a Property with a Disputed Boundary. Neither Woman Is Impressed by the Word "Convenient."**

**26th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress received her second invitation of the week before breakfast.



Unlike the invitation from the Empress Dowager, this one came with a formal document.



It was not addressed to the Empress.



It was addressed to Xu Yuru.



That distinction was deliberate.



The document came from her natal family.



Her father, the Duke of Xu, had sent a letter proposing that she spend some time at the Xu family residence. The letter was warm, respectful, and unusually cautious.



It mentioned that the family had prepared several rooms in the eastern courtyard.



It also mentioned that the garden had recently been repaired.



The final paragraph contained the actual question.



*If you wish to establish a more permanent residence outside the palace, the Xu family would be pleased to receive you.*



Yuru read it twice.



Fang Ru waited beside the table.



"Will Madam accept?"



"I have not decided."



"Would you prefer to visit first?"



"That seems sensible."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru folded the letter.



"Have the Duke's household been informed that I am no longer responsible for palace administration?"



"Yes, Madam."



"Good."



Fang Ru looked at her.



"Do you expect them to ask you to manage their household?"



Yuru smiled.



"I sincerely hope they have learned something from watching me leave the palace."



Fang Ru lowered her head.



She was smiling too.

At the Xu family residence, Duke Xu received Yuru's reply.



She had accepted the invitation to visit.



She had not agreed to move permanently.



The Duke read the letter and handed it to his wife.



"She is coming."



Madam Xu smiled.



"That is good."



"She has not agreed to stay."



"She has only just left the palace. Why should she decide immediately?"



The Duke looked at his wife.



"Do you think she will wish to return to the palace?"



"I think she will wish to decide for herself."



He nodded.



The Duke had been concerned that his daughter might feel isolated after leaving her position.



He had also been concerned that the imperial family would continue to rely on her.



He had not considered that the Xu household might accidentally do the same.



He turned toward the steward.



"Prepare the eastern courtyard. Do not disturb the other rooms."



"Yes, Duke."



"And do not move the household accounts into her study."



The steward looked confused.



"Your Excellency?"



"She is coming to visit, not to take charge of the estate."



Madam Xu glanced at her husband.



"That is a sensible instruction."



The Duke nodded.



He had no intention of repeating the palace's mistakes.



Unfortunately, the Xu family had not yet discovered that a household could produce administrative problems without anyone deliberately assigning them.

At the former Empress's residence, Yuru reviewed the arrangements for her visit.



She intended to travel with a small group of attendants.



Fang Ru would accompany her if she chose to remain with Yuru.



The other servants had not yet decided whether to continue in palace service or transfer to her personal household.



Yuru did not intend to pressure them.



She had learned that an orderly transition required people to make decisions about their own responsibilities.



She also intended to take no palace documents.



There was no reason to bring administrative records to her natal home.



She had enough books of her own.



As she reviewed the arrangements, Gao Shun arrived with a message from the emperor.



Zhao Jingheng wished to know whether she required an escort for the visit.



Yuru read the note.



She wrote a reply.



*The ordinary arrangements are sufficient. Thank you.*



Gao Shun accepted it.



He hesitated.



"Madam, His Majesty also asked whether you would prefer to use an imperial carriage."



Yuru looked at him.



"Why?"



"The journey is short, but His Majesty believes the carriage would be more comfortable."



"I have no objection to comfort. But I do not require a ceremonial procession."



Gao Shun bowed.



"I will convey the message."



Yuru returned to her book.



Then she added, "Tell His Majesty that I am visiting my family, not attending a state ceremony."



Gao Shun nodded.



He had a feeling the emperor would understand.



He also had a feeling that the emperor would still send an escort.

At the northern canal property, Shen Mingzhao discovered that the boundary dispute had become more complicated.



Master Luo had arrived with the latest assessment papers.



A local survey assistant accompanied him.



The property owner was also present.



Mingzhao stood beside the main courtyard while Master Luo examined the old registration map.



"The building itself remains sound," he said. "The roof requires the repairs previously estimated. The drainage needs attention. The kitchen requires some work, though less than originally expected."



"Has the well been examined?"



"Yes. It is usable, but the surrounding stones need repair."



"And the boundary?"



Master Luo unfolded the map.



"That remains the difficulty."



Mingzhao studied the markings.



The old registration line ran along the western side of the property.



The drainage channel extended slightly beyond it.



The maintenance note recorded work on the channel several years earlier.



But it did not identify a legal transfer of land.



"Can the present boundary be established by physical inspection?" she asked.



"Not conclusively."



"Why?"



"The channel has been altered. The older markers are missing. The neighbouring landowner disputes the current line."



"Has the neighbouring landowner provided his own registration documents?"



"Not yet."



Mingzhao looked at the property owner.



"Then the matter cannot be resolved today."



The owner frowned.



"The property is perfectly usable."



"I have not said otherwise."



"The drainage channel has always been maintained this way."



"That may be true. It does not establish ownership."



Master Luo looked down at the map.



Mingzhao continued.



"I am willing to consider the property. I am not willing to purchase a disputed boundary merely because the buildings are convenient."



The owner appeared offended.



Master Luo intervened.



"Miss Shen's position is reasonable. The boundary should be clarified before a final agreement."



The owner reluctantly nodded.



Mingzhao returned the map.



"Please obtain the neighbour's registration documents and the earlier survey records. I will review them when they are available."



Master Luo bowed.



"Understood."



The inspection ended without an agreement.



Mingzhao was not disappointed.



She had not come to buy the house.



She had come to determine whether it was worth considering.



That question remained unanswered.

On the journey back, Qiao Yue looked at the property notes.



"Miss, if the boundary is not resolved, will you reject the house?"



"Not necessarily."



"Why not?"



"Because the disputed strip may not affect the usable portion of the property. But I need to know what I am purchasing."



Qiao Yue looked at the repair estimates.



"Even without the boundary, the roof, drainage, kitchen, and well will cost money."



"Yes."



"And the purchase price?"



"Has not been renegotiated."



"And the landlord of our present residence?"



"Has accepted the existing rental term."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"We have one house with a rent agreement and another house with a boundary dispute."



Mingzhao looked out of the carriage.



"That is an accurate description."



"Would it not be easier to remain where we are?"



"For three months, perhaps."



"Then why are we looking at another house?"



"Because I need to know what alternatives exist before deciding."



Qiao Yue stared at her.



"Miss, you make even choosing a house sound like a government investigation."



Mingzhao did not answer.



She was examining the figures.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received a letter from the former northern warehouse steward.



The old man had identified the operator of the roadside storage yard.



It had been managed by a merchant named Zhao Fusheng, who had worked with several transport contractors.



The name appeared in the old transport records.



Mingyuan immediately sent the information to Sun Qiming.



He did not make a conclusion.



He simply provided the new lead.



Sun Qiming replied that he would attempt to locate the merchant or his surviving business records.



Mingyuan entered the information into the investigation register.



The original grain discrepancy remained unchanged.



Forty-seven sacks.



Twenty covered by the movement instruction.



Eighteen confirmed in the southern warehouse.



Two unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven unexplained.



The roadside yard might explain part of the movement.



It might also explain nothing.



The evidence was not yet sufficient.



Mingyuan closed the register.



For several seconds, he considered sending the update to Mingzhao.



Then he remembered that she had asked to receive material information about the settlement, not every minor development.



He sent a short note through the accountant.



It contained the merchant's name and the fact that further inquiry was being made.



He did not ask her to return.



He did not ask her to review the records.



He did not tell her how to conduct the investigation.



He had begun to understand that the difference mattered.

At the Xu family residence, preparations continued.



The eastern courtyard was cleaned.



The guest rooms were aired.



The garden paths were swept.



The Duke personally inspected the rooms.



He found the study satisfactory.



He found the bedroom satisfactory.



He found the sitting room satisfactory.



Then he noticed a large desk in the corner.



"Who placed this here?"



The steward bowed.



"The household believed Her Highness might require a desk."



The Duke looked at it.



"It is too large."



"Should it be removed?"



The Duke considered.



"Yes. Replace it with a smaller one."



The steward bowed.



Then the Duke noticed a stack of account books on a nearby shelf.



"What are these?"



The steward hesitated.



"Household records, Your Excellency."



"Why are they here?"



"We thought Her Highness might wish to review them."



The Duke stared at him.



"Why would she review them?"



The steward looked uncertain.



"She has considerable experience in household administration."



The Duke closed his eyes.



He had been careful.



He had specifically told the steward not to place household accounts in the study.



The steward had interpreted this as a request to place them on a shelf in the sitting room.



"Remove them."



"Yes, Your Excellency."



"And do not bring them back."



The steward bowed.



The Duke looked at the smaller desk.



He wondered whether he should also issue an instruction prohibiting the household from asking Yuru to inspect the garden accounts.



Then he decided that such an instruction would be absurd.



The Xu household was not the imperial palace.



It did not need to make every ordinary decision into a formal rule.

That afternoon, the former Empress received another visitor.



It was the Crown Prince.



He had come without a document.



Yuru looked pleased.



"Cheng'an."



"Mother."



He bowed.



"Have you come to discuss something?"



"Not exactly."



"Then sit."



The Crown Prince sat opposite her.



For a while, they spoke about ordinary matters.



Then he said, "The inner household administration has completed the first review of the transferred responsibilities."



Yuru listened.



"How many?"



"Twenty-nine of the original responsibilities have been fully reviewed and assigned. The remaining matters are proceeding according to the timetable."



"Good."



"The offices have asked for clarification on two procedures."



"Have they identified the offices responsible?"



"Yes."



"Then they should submit their questions through the proper channels."



The Crown Prince nodded.



He had expected that answer.



After a pause, he said, "The emperor is pleased."



"I am glad."



"He believes the arrangements are becoming stable."



"That is encouraging."



The Crown Prince looked at her.



"Do you not want to know more?"



Yuru smiled.



"I am interested. I am not responsible."



He considered this.



Then he laughed.



"I think Father is still learning the difference."



"Your father has managed the empire for many years. He will learn."



The Crown Prince nodded.



He looked toward the window.



"Mother, I have a question."



"Ask."



"If I need your advice, how should I approach you?"



Yuru looked at him.



"Like a son visiting his mother."



The Crown Prince blinked.



"And if I have an administrative problem?"



"Then you should first identify the responsible office, the relevant facts, and the available options."



"And then?"



"Then you may ask me for personal advice if you still wish."



He smiled.



"I understand."



"Good."



She poured him tea.



This time, no one brought an administrative report.

At the Marquisate, Mingxiu completed her first review of the ordinary household purchases.



She had found three issues.



The first was a duplicated transport charge.



The second was an incomplete record for lamp oil.



The third concerned a small kitchen purchase that had been entered under general maintenance.



None of the issues was large.



Together, they showed that the household's records were not being maintained consistently.



Mingxiu brought the summary to her father.



Shen Guowei read it.



"Have you confirmed the duplicated transport charge?"



"Not yet. I have requested the supplier's original delivery record."



"Good."



"The lamp oil entry can be corrected from the issue register."



"Then correct it."



"The kitchen purchase requires confirmation from the receiving servant."



Shen Guowei nodded.



He looked at his daughter.



"You have done well."



Mingxiu lowered her eyes.



"Thank you, Father."



Madam Liang, who had been listening, looked uncomfortable.



"These are small matters."



"Yes," Shen Guowei said. "They are small matters. That is why they should be easy to record correctly."



Madam Liang did not respond.



Mingxiu gathered the papers.



As she left, she passed the study where Mingyuan was reviewing the old transport records.



The two exchanged a brief greeting.



Neither mentioned Mingzhao.



But both were beginning to understand how much of the household's daily operation had depended on work that had rarely been acknowledged.

That evening, Yuru received a second note from her father.



The Duke had confirmed the date of her visit.



He had also written that the eastern courtyard would be prepared according to her preferences.



Yuru smiled.



Fang Ru asked, "Is everything arranged?"



"Yes."



"Will you stay overnight?"



"Possibly."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Shall I prepare the luggage?"



"Only what is necessary."



Yuru looked at the room.



She had not yet decided whether to remain in the palace guest residence permanently.



She had not decided whether to live with her natal family.



She had not decided whether to establish a separate household.



For years, she had made decisions according to what the palace required.



Now she had the unfamiliar luxury of considering what she wanted.



It would take time.



She was willing to allow it.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received Sun Qiming's latest letter.



The accountant had identified Zhao Fusheng as the former operator of the roadside storage yard.



He had not yet located the merchant himself.



However, one surviving business register suggested that the yard had closed several years earlier.



The register did not list the grain consignment.



It did contain a reference to Liu Shun's transport business.



Mingzhao read the note carefully.



"Does this establish that Liu Shun delivered the grain there?" she asked.



Qiao Yue shook her head.



"Not yet. It establishes that his business had dealings with the yard."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then the next step is to identify the nature of those dealings."



She wrote an instruction.



"Ask Sun Qiming to obtain the full register if it can be lawfully accessed. Also ask whether Zhao Fusheng left any surviving accounts or business partners."



Qiao Yue accepted the letter.



"Yes, Miss."



Mingzhao looked at the papers.



The investigation was moving.



Slowly.



It was not yet clear whether the new evidence would establish delivery, temporary storage, or an unrelated transaction.



She refused to make the conclusion before the records were examined.



She returned to her household ledger.



The rent was due soon.



The kitchen expenses needed review.



The next silk inspection had to be scheduled.



And the property question remained open.



She had wanted independence.



She had obtained it.



It had arrived with more decisions than she had expected.

At the palace, the emperor received a brief report concerning the former Empress's visit to the Xu family residence.



He read it.



Then he called Gao Shun.



"Why is this here?"



Gao Shun bowed.



"The residence office believed the emperor should be informed of her travel arrangements."



"Has she requested an escort?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Has she requested an imperial carriage?"



"No."



"Then what is the purpose of the report?"



Gao Shun hesitated.



"To keep the palace informed."



The emperor looked at him.



"She is visiting her family. She is not conducting an inspection of the northern frontier."



Gao Shun lowered his head.



The emperor returned the document.



"Keep the necessary security arrangements. Do not turn a family visit into a state event."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He paused.



Then he added, "And do not send her a question about the arrangements."



Gao Shun bowed.



The emperor returned to his work.



He was slowly learning that allowing Yuru to live independently meant allowing her to make ordinary decisions without imperial supervision.



It was a simple principle.



It was also surprisingly difficult for a palace accustomed to recording everything.

That night, Yuru read another chapter of her book.



The bridge in the historical account had finally been repaired.



The minister had retired.



The court had immediately discovered that the repaired bridge required maintenance.



Yuru laughed quietly.



The author had understood something important about administration.



Completing a project did not eliminate the need for future work.



It merely changed the nature of the work.



At the eastern market, Mingzhao completed her own accounts.



She entered the rent due date.



She separated the partnership payment from personal funds.



She recorded the remaining household expenses.



Then she placed the northern canal property assessment in a separate folder marked *Pending Boundary Confirmation*.



Neither woman had found a permanent solution to her future.



But both had made one decision clear.



They would not accept a convenient arrangement merely because someone else had prepared it.



The former Empress would visit her family as a daughter, not as an unpaid household administrator.



The Marquis's eldest daughter would examine a property as a buyer, not as someone desperate to leave her rented rooms.



And in both households, several people were discovering that the most difficult part of losing a capable woman was not finding someone to replace her.



It was learning to stop expecting her to do everything.
