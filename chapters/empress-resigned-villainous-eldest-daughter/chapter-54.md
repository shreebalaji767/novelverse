# **Chapter 54: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Visit the Imperial Gardens. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Inspect the Workshop. One Invitation Promises Flowers. The Other Promises Four Workers Who Have All Discovered That Training Is Not the Same as Working.**

**17th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Emperor had sent three possible dates for the garden visit.



Yuru looked at the message.



Then she looked at Fang Ru.



"Why are there three dates?"



Fang Ru examined the note.



"Perhaps His Majesty wishes you to choose whichever is convenient."



"Then one date would have been sufficient."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Perhaps he is trying to be considerate."



Yuru set the note down.



The proposed dates were the nineteenth, twenty-first, and twenty-third days of the month.



She had no objection to visiting the gardens. She also had no intention of arranging her days around the palace's convenience simply because the Emperor had finally learned to send personal invitations.



"Reply that the twenty-first is suitable."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Should I ask whether the visit will include the Empress Dowager?"



"Yes. If Her Majesty wishes to join us, I would be pleased."



Fang Ru prepared the reply.



Yuru returned to her book.



After a moment, she added, "And ask whether the garden paths have been repaired."



Fang Ru looked up.



"Would you like to inspect them?"



"No. I would simply prefer not to fall into a hole."



Fang Ru laughed.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao was already reviewing the previous day's production record.



The first difficulty of the new order had been contained.



The material handled incorrectly by the newly hired worker had passed additional inspection. It remained usable, although the workshop had spent extra time checking it.



The senior finisher had revised the training instructions.



The two inexperienced workers would now practise separately before being assigned to the main production group.



Mingzhao examined the revised schedule.



"Why have you placed both trainees under the same supervisor?"



Lin Shuyun looked at the page.



"The senior finisher suggested it."



"Can she supervise both while completing her own work?"



Lin paused.



"Probably not."



"Then the training schedule is still incomplete."



Lin took the paper back.



The workshop had four additional workers, but only one experienced person had been assigned to train them.



The other two new workers had experience with silk finishing. They could begin ordinary work, though they would still need to learn the buyer's specific requirements.



Lin crossed out the original arrangement.



"We can assign one experienced worker to each trainee during the first three days."



"Will that reduce the existing production output?"



"Yes."



"Then record the reduced output in the internal schedule."



Lin looked at Mingzhao.



"You have found another expense."



"It was already an expense. We had simply not written it down."



Lin laughed despite herself.

The buyer's representative, Zhou Wenqi, arrived before midday.



He brought a payment confirmation for the two corrected bolts from the previous order.



Mingzhao checked the amount against the agreed adjusted terms.



The two bolts had been accepted after correction and inspection.



The payment was now received.



She entered it in the existing partnership ledger.



Lin watched her finish.



"That closes the payment for the forty-two bolts."



"It closes the payment for the accepted goods."



"Is there a difference?"



"The production costs still need to be settled."



Lin nodded.



The distinction mattered.



The forty-two bolts had been delivered and accepted. The buyer had paid for them. But the workshop still had to calculate wages, dye, materials, transport, and other costs.



There was no reason to declare a profit before the expenses were known.



Zhou Wenqi glanced at the new production schedule.



"How is the two-hundred-bolt order progressing?"



"Production has begun," Lin replied.



"Will the first group be ready on the agreed date?"



Mingzhao answered.



"The schedule remains achievable, but the workshop is still adjusting the training arrangements."



Zhou nodded.



"Will that affect the delivery?"



"We will notify you if the agreed schedule becomes uncertain. We will not promise a date that the workshop cannot meet."



The merchant appeared satisfied.



He signed the payment acknowledgment and left.



Mingzhao placed the document in the correct file.



The previous order was now fully paid by the buyer.



The new order had its own deposit and production account.



Neither had yet produced a confirmed net profit.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan was preparing to send the western field agreement to the tenant.



The legal adviser had reviewed the final wording.



The agreement allowed temporary cultivation for a limited period.



It stated the payment rate, the notice period, and the responsibilities of both sides.



It also made clear that the arrangement did not renew the expired lease.



Mingyuan read the document once more.



Then he handed it to Steward Zhou.



"Take this to the tenant. Let him read it before signing."



"Yes, Young Master."



"Do not ask him to sign immediately."



The steward nodded.



Mingyuan hesitated.



"And make certain he receives a copy."



"Of course."



Steward Zhou paused at the door.



"You are handling this carefully."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"Should I not?"



"Your father would usually have asked the steward to settle it."



"Then the steward would have had to decide what the family intended."



Steward Zhou did not answer.



Mingyuan returned to his papers.



The family had spent years treating estate management as a task that could be passed to whichever person happened to be available.



Mingzhao had been available.



Now she was not.



The estate would have to learn to make decisions without expecting her to correct them afterward.

Mingxiu received the contractor's corrected roof estimate.



She checked the number of tiles, the labour charge, and the transport cost.



The document was finally consistent.



She took it to her father.



Shen Guowei examined it.



"How much?"



Mingxiu gave him the figure.



He compared it with the earlier estimate.



"It is lower."



"Because the earlier document included too many replacement tiles."



"Was the contractor careless?"



"He copied a quantity from another repair schedule."



Shen Guowei looked at the corrected estimate.



"Should we approve it?"



"Not yet. The repair cost is now clearer, but we should confirm whether the building needs the work immediately or whether it can wait until the next maintenance period."



Her father looked at her.



"That is reasonable."



Mingxiu seemed slightly surprised.



She had expected him to approve the repair simply because the amount was lower.



Instead, he handed the paper back.



"Ask Steward Zhou to provide the building's current condition report."



Mingxiu nodded.



She returned to the office.



For a moment, she wondered whether her father would have asked the same question if Mingzhao were still managing the estate.



She suspected he would have asked Mingzhao to decide.



That thought was uncomfortable.



She returned to the report.

At the palace, the Empress Dowager received the former Empress's reply.



She read the proposed date for the garden visit.



"The twenty-first?"



The attendant bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Dowager considered it.



"Tell the Emperor I will join them."



The attendant hesitated.



"Should the garden pavilion be prepared?"



"Only if it needs cleaning."



"Should refreshments be arranged?"



"Ordinary refreshments."



The attendant bowed.



The Dowager looked at her.



"Do not prepare a banquet."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The attendant withdrew.



The Dowager placed the letter on the table.



She had once believed that the former Empress's departure from the palace would create a permanent absence that the imperial household could not easily overcome.



Instead, Yuru was still visiting.



She still spoke with the imperial children.



She still met the Dowager.



She simply no longer managed every matter that crossed the palace doors.



The Dowager was beginning to accept that these things were not contradictory.

In the Hall of Mental Cultivation, the Emperor was reading a report about the repair of an eastern corridor.



The report included a request for additional funds.



He examined the original estimate.



The repair involved several damaged floorboards and a section of roof drainage.



The office responsible had submitted a revised cost.



However, the request did not explain whether the drainage work was included in the original maintenance allocation.



The Emperor called Gao Shun.



"Has the Imperial Household Administration checked the existing allocation?"



"They have requested the maintenance ledger."



"Then the ledger should be examined before additional funds are approved."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor returned the report.



Gao Shun accepted it.



The Emperor looked at the next document.



It concerned a request from the Department of Rites for a ceremonial rehearsal.



He read the first page.



Then he stopped.



"Why is this addressed to me?"



Gao Shun checked the heading.



"The department requests confirmation of the rehearsal time."



"Who determines the rehearsal time?"



"The Department of Rites."



"Then why are they asking me?"



Gao Shun remained silent.



The Emperor sighed.



"Return it."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor had begun to suspect that the palace's greatest administrative reform would be teaching officials to make decisions they already had the authority to make.

At the workshop, the two trainees were practising the finishing process under supervision.



One worked too slowly.



The other worked too quickly.



The senior finisher corrected both.



Mingzhao watched from the side.



The slower worker was careful but hesitant. The faster worker had confidence but overlooked small irregularities.



Lin approached.



"They will improve."



"They may."



"You do not sound convinced."



"They have different problems."



Lin followed her gaze.



"One needs confidence. The other needs patience."



"Then train them accordingly."



Lin smiled.



"You make it sound simple."



"It is not simple. But treating them as though they are identical will not make it easier."



The senior finisher called one trainee over.



She demonstrated how to check the material's edge before proceeding.



The worker tried again.



This time, the result was acceptable.



Mingzhao nodded.



"Record the completed practice work separately."



Lin looked at her.



"Another record?"



"Yes."



Lin shook her head.



"I will soon need an entire room for your records."



"Only if you continue hiring people."



Lin laughed.

That afternoon, Sun Qiming sent Mingzhao an update on the northern warehouse investigation.



The original counting sheets had been located for one part of the relevant period.



They showed that the warehouse had counted grain under several categories.



The total stock included grain reserved for seed and damaged grain awaiting disposal.



The distribution record listed only grain available for immediate issue.



This explained why the two books had different totals.



It did not explain the forty-seven sacks.



Sun had not yet completed the reconciliation between the original total stock and the movement records.



He also had not established the destination of the twenty-seven unexplained sacks.



The roadside storage yard associated with Zhao Fusheng remained a lead only.



No delivery record had been found.



Mingzhao read the update and placed it beside the previous report.



She wrote a brief instruction:



*Continue the reconciliation. Preserve the original figures. Do not classify the forty-seven sacks as accounted for until the quantities and movements are independently supported.*



She signed it.



Qiao Yue brought fresh tea.



"Another report?"



"Yes."



"Has the grain been found?"



"Not yet."



Qiao Yue looked at the papers.



"Perhaps the grain has been eaten."



Mingzhao looked up.



"By whom?"



Qiao Yue shrugged.



"Forty-seven sacks are a great deal of grain. Perhaps a very large family."



Mingzhao returned to her writing.



"If a family of that size exists, I would prefer to know where it lives."



Qiao Yue laughed.

The following morning, the Emperor received Yuru's confirmation that she would attend the garden visit on the twenty-first.



He was pleased.



He immediately instructed Gao Shun to ensure the garden pavilion was ready.



Gao Shun bowed.



"Should refreshments be prepared?"



"Ordinary refreshments."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor considered the matter.



"Perhaps the tea she prefers."



Gao Shun nodded.



"Certainly."



"And make certain the paths are clear."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor returned to his papers.



After a moment, he looked up.



"Do not make the visit ceremonial."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Understood."



The Emperor continued reading.



Then he added, "And do not ask her to inspect the gardens."



Gao Shun paused.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He left the hall.



The Emperor looked at the empty doorway.



He had not realized how much effort it required to invite someone somewhere without asking them to do something.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received the workshop's first production summary.



The four workers had begun their assigned duties.



The two experienced workers were completing preparation work.



The two trainees were still under supervision.



The earlier handling mistake had caused additional inspection but no material loss.



The production schedule remained within the agreed range.



Mingzhao examined the figures.



"How much material has been prepared?"



Lin gave her the quantity.



"How much has passed the first inspection?"



Lin gave her the second figure.



"And how much remains under examination?"



Lin supplied the final figure.



Mingzhao checked the totals.



They matched.



She signed the internal report.



Lin leaned back.



"Is that sufficient?"



"For today's production record."



"Only today's?"



"Tomorrow's work has not happened yet."



Lin laughed.



"You are impossible."



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



"I am trying to prevent tomorrow's work from becoming today's mistake."

The seventeenth day of the fifth month ended with several small developments.



The former Empress had accepted the garden invitation for the twenty-first.



The Empress Dowager had agreed to join her.



The workshop had completed its first training adjustments.



The two corrected silk bolts from the previous order had finally been paid for.



Mingyuan had prepared a temporary cultivation agreement.



Mingxiu had obtained a corrected roof estimate and refused to approve it without a condition report.



Sun Qiming had found more warehouse records, though the missing grain remained unexplained.



The palace continued learning to operate without Yuru.



The Marquisate continued learning to operate without Mingzhao.



And in the workshop, four new workers had learned an important lesson:



A merchant could agree to pay for two hundred bolts.



A contract could specify the delivery date.



A deposit could be received.



But none of those things could make silk finish itself.
