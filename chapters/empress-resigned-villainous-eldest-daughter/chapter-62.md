# **Chapter 62: The Former Empress Has Arrived at Her Parents' Home and Has Been Offered More Food Than She Requested. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Arrived at the Family Meeting and Has Been Offered More Responsibilities Than She Accepted. Both Women Have Discovered That Their Families Are Very Generous with Things They Do Not Have to Carry.**

**26th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



Xu Yuru arrived at the Xu residence shortly before midday.



Her carriage stopped outside the eastern gate.



Fang Ru stepped down first and helped her alight.



The household servants bowed.



The former Empress returned their greetings and entered without a formal procession.



Duke Xu was waiting in the main courtyard.



He had changed his robe twice that morning.



The first had been too formal.



The second had been too plain.



The third, which he was now wearing, had been selected by Madam Xu, who had informed him that the purpose of the visit was to eat, not to receive an imperial delegation.



When he saw his daughter, he smiled.



"Yuru."



"Father."



He looked at her from head to toe.



"You look well."



"I am well."



"Have you been eating properly?"



"Yes."



"Sleeping?"



"Yes."



"Is the guest residence comfortable?"



"Comfortable enough."



The Duke nodded.



He had prepared several more questions.



Madam Xu appeared behind him.



"She has just arrived. Let her sit down."



Duke Xu stepped aside.



Xu Yuru smiled.



"Mother."



Madam Xu took her hand.



"You have become thinner."



Xu Yuru looked at her.



"Mother, I was told that I looked well."



"Your father does not notice these things."



Duke Xu protested.



"I noticed that she looks well."



"You also noticed that the garden path was clear."



"That was important."



Madam Xu led her daughter inside.



"Come. The meal is ready."



Duke Xu followed.



He had intended to ask whether she wanted anything else prepared.



Madam Xu had already made it clear that this question would not be welcome.

The dining table contained six dishes.



There was also soup.



Duke Xu looked at the table with satisfaction.



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Six dishes."



The Duke cleared his throat.



"The soup was already prepared."



"Yes."



"It was not an additional dish."



"I did not say it was."



Xu Yuru sat down.



She looked at the table.



"Everything looks delicious."



The Duke brightened.



"Then eat more."



Madam Xu served her daughter rice.



"Eat what you like."



Fang Ru was given a place at the side table with the household attendants.



The former Empress looked at the dishes.



There was fish, seasonal vegetables, braised meat, tofu, a cold dish, and a small plate of pickled greens.



The soup was clear and fragrant.



It was a generous meal.



It was also considerably less elaborate than the Duke had originally wanted.



Xu Yuru began eating.



For a while, the conversation remained ordinary.



Madam Xu asked about her sleeping arrangements.



Duke Xu asked whether she had been able to walk comfortably during the garden visit.



Xu Yuru told them about the pavilion and the pond.



She mentioned that the Empress Dowager had enjoyed the tea.



She did not mention the absence of documents until her father asked whether the Emperor had brought any official business.



"No," she said.



The Duke looked surprised.



"None?"



"None."



"Not even a report?"



"Not even a report."



Duke Xu considered this.



"Then the visit must have been very short."



Xu Yuru smiled.



"It was long enough."



Madam Xu gave her husband a warning look.



The Duke returned to his rice.

After the meal, they moved to the eastern sitting room.



Madam Xu brought tea.



Duke Xu sat opposite his daughter.



He had been waiting for an opportunity to ask about her future residence.



He had also been told not to turn the meal into a discussion about her future.



He attempted to wait.



The attempt lasted several minutes.



"Have you considered staying here for a while?" he asked.



Xu Yuru looked at him.



"At the Xu residence?"



"Yes."



"My guest residence is comfortable."



"That was not what I asked."



Madam Xu set down the tea.



"She has not decided."



The Duke looked at his wife.



"I am only asking."



"And she has answered."



Xu Yuru placed her cup on the table.



"Father, I appreciate the offer. I may stay here for a few days at some point, but I have not decided where I will live permanently."



The Duke nodded slowly.



"Your eastern courtyard remains available."



"I know."



"It has been kept in order."



"Thank you."



"There is no need for you to make a decision immediately."



Xu Yuru smiled.



"That is what I intend to do."



The Duke accepted this.



He had wanted to offer a solution.



Instead, he had been given a boundary.



It was not an unpleasant one.



Madam Xu looked at her daughter.



"Would you like to come again next week?"



Xu Yuru smiled.



"Yes."



The Duke looked pleased.



"Then we should prepare another meal."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Next week."



"Yes."



"Not tomorrow."



The Duke sighed.



The kitchen steward, standing outside the room, heard the exchange and silently thanked the household ancestors.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingzhao arrived for the family meeting with her document case.



The meeting room had been prepared.



The Marquis, Shen Guowei, sat at the head of the table.



Madam Liang sat to his right.



Shen Mingyuan and Shen Mingxiu were present.



Old Madam Shen had also attended.



The family representative, Mr Liang, sat near the end of the table.



Steward Zhou De stood beside the documents.



Mingzhao greeted the elders.



She did not bow more deeply than propriety required.



She took the seat prepared for her.



Her document case was placed on the table.



Shen Guowei looked at it.



"You have brought the corrected schedule?"



"Yes, Father."



"Good. We have also reviewed the revised version."



Mingzhao opened the case.



"Then we can begin."



Mr Liang cleared his throat.



"The purpose of today's meeting is to bring the property division closer to completion."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"That is also my purpose."



The representative nodded.



He had prepared a list of questions.



Mingzhao had prepared documents.



Both considered themselves ready.



The first matter concerned the personal advances.



Mr Liang read the revised entry.



"The family acknowledges that several sums were paid by the eldest young lady."



"How many?"



He looked down.



"Thirteen."



Mingzhao placed a paper on the table.



"That is incorrect."



Mr Liang looked up.



"The family has confirmed thirteen payments."



"The family has confirmed that money changed hands in thirteen entries. It has not confirmed thirteen debts."



She opened the supporting section.



"Nine advances are confirmed. Four additional entries have payment evidence, but the repayment arrangements are unclear. Ten further entries remain uncertain."



Mr Liang looked at the document.



"Why are the four separate?"



"Because payment is not the same as a confirmed loan agreement."



Shen Guowei frowned.



"Did you not provide the money?"



"I did."



"Then why are we discussing the classification?"



"Because the question is not whether money moved. The question is what obligation followed."



Mingzhao's voice remained even.



She did not raise it.



She did not need to.



"If the family wishes to classify all thirteen as confirmed debts, it must provide the relevant agreements or evidence of repayment obligations. If it cannot, the entries must remain separate."



Mr Liang looked at the Marquis.



Shen Guowei studied the papers.



After a moment, he nodded.



"Keep the classifications separate."



Mr Liang made a note.



Mingzhao moved to the next page.



The meeting had lasted less than half an hour.



The first dispute had not been resolved.



It had, however, been described correctly.

The next matter concerned the seventy-two taels for study repairs.



Mr Liang pointed to the schedule.



"The family agrees that the repairs were completed."



"Yes."



"And that you paid the amount."



"Yes."



"Then it should be listed as an advance."



"No."



Mingzhao turned to the supporting receipt.



"The expenditure was estate maintenance. The study was part of the family property. The payment was recorded as a repair expense, not a personal loan."



Mr Liang examined the receipt.



"Could it not be treated as an advance made on behalf of the estate?"



"It could be listed as an amount paid from my personal funds for estate maintenance, if the family wishes to record the source of payment separately. It should not be classified as a personal debt without an agreement."



Mr Liang looked at the receipt again.



Shen Mingyuan leaned forward.



"The repair was necessary," he said.



Mingzhao looked at him.



"That is not disputed."



Mingyuan nodded.



He had reviewed the study records.



He understood why the expense had been incurred.



He also understood that the correct classification mattered.



Shen Guowei considered the matter.



"Record it as estate maintenance paid from Mingzhao's personal funds. The settlement can determine whether reimbursement is due."



Mingzhao nodded.



That was acceptable.



She had not asked for an immediate decision.



She had asked for an accurate record.

The next issue was the additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels.



The family representative described the amount as an unverified expenditure.



Mingzhao agreed.



She did not claim it as a confirmed advance.



She did not withdraw the question.



The records had not yet established what the money had been used for.



Mr Liang suggested that it could be removed from the schedule until further evidence was found.



Mingzhao shook her head.



"It should remain listed as disputed."



"Why?"



"Because removing it would make the schedule appear complete when it is not."



Old Madam Shen looked at her.



"Must every uncertain amount remain in the family records?"



"If it affects the settlement, yes."



The old lady frowned.



"That will make the process very long."



Mingzhao met her gaze.



"It has already been long."



No one replied.



The Marquis looked down at the schedule.



He had hoped that the meeting would produce a final figure.



Instead, it was producing a more accurate list of what remained unknown.



He found this frustrating.



He also recognized that the previous figures had been unreliable.



"Keep it listed," he said.



Mr Liang made another note.

The meeting continued.



The grain discrepancy was discussed.



The original count was forty-seven sacks.



The movement instruction was twenty.



Eighteen had been confirmed at southern storage.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



The old warehouse steward Zhang had provided information about the distinction between total stock and immediately available stock.



That explained why two registers could show different totals.



It did not establish the destination of the missing grain.



Mingzhao placed Sun Qiming's report on the table.



"The two matters should remain separate," she said.



Mr Liang looked at the transport payment.



"Why?"



"Because there is no evidence connecting the blank-destination payment to the grain movement."



"Could they be connected?"



"They could."



"Then why not examine them together?"



"Because possibility is not evidence."



Shen Mingyuan spoke.



"We have requested Zhang's assistance. He will examine copies of the original sheets."



Mr Liang looked at him.



"Will he identify the recipient?"



"We do not know."



The Marquis looked toward Mingyuan.



"You have not yet spoken with him?"



"No, Father. We have requested his examination of the records first."



Shen Guowei nodded.



Mingzhao watched her younger brother.



He had not made an unsupported claim.



That was an improvement.



It did not settle the grain question.



It did make the meeting less chaotic.

By the time the meeting reached the property schedule, everyone was tired.



The northern canal property was raised as a footnote.



Mingzhao immediately objected.



"It is not part of the family estate."



Mr Liang looked at the note.



"It was included for completeness."



"It has not been purchased."



"Your intention to purchase it has been mentioned."



"My intention is not ownership."



Shen Mingxiu looked at her sister.



"Are you still considering it?"



"Yes."



"Even with the repair costs?"



"Especially with the repair costs. I need to know the total before deciding."



Mingxiu nodded.



She had reviewed enough repair estimates to understand that a low purchase price could become expensive after the roof, drainage, kitchen, and well were examined.



Mingzhao continued.



"The boundary is still disputed. The old stone marker is missing. The neighbouring registration has not arrived. The survey is incomplete. No deposit has been paid."



Mr Liang crossed out the footnote.



The matter was removed from the family schedule.



The meeting had now lasted several hours.



The family representative looked at the remaining pages.



"Then we have made progress."



Mingzhao closed her document case.



"We have corrected several classifications."



"That is progress."



"It is not a final settlement."



Mr Liang nodded.



"Not yet."

At the Xu residence, Xu Yuru had spent the afternoon listening to her younger brother complain about a minor dispute with a neighbour.



The dispute concerned a tree branch.



The neighbour believed that the branch extended over his courtyard.



Xu Yuru's brother believed that the tree belonged to the Xu family.



Duke Xu believed that the neighbour should not have complained.



Madam Xu believed that someone should simply arrange for the branch to be trimmed.



Xu Yuru listened.



Then she asked, "Does the branch damage anything?"



Her brother thought about it.



"Not yet."



"Does it block the neighbour's path?"



"No."



"Then why are you arguing?"



Her brother opened his mouth.



Then he closed it.



Duke Xu looked at his daughter with admiration.



Madam Xu looked at her husband.



"Do not turn this into another discussion."



The Duke lowered his eyes.



Xu Yuru smiled.



She had once managed disputes involving servants, household property, and people who believed that a slight inconvenience was a personal insult.



A tree branch was a modest improvement.



She did not offer to settle it.



She simply enjoyed the tea.

At the Marquisate, the family meeting ended without a final settlement.



The corrected schedule would be revised again.



The disputed entries would remain open.



The nine confirmed advances, four payment-confirmed but unclear entries, and ten uncertain entries would retain separate classifications.



The study repairs would be recorded as estate maintenance paid from Mingzhao's funds, with reimbursement still to be determined.



The two hundred and twenty-eight taels would remain disputed.



The grain discrepancy would remain unresolved.



The transport payment would remain separate.



The northern canal property would remain outside the estate division.



Mingzhao gathered her documents.



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"Will you attend the next meeting?"



"When it is scheduled."



"Will you continue assisting with the records until then?"



Mingzhao paused.



"I will provide documents concerning my claims and the property division. I will not resume managing the estate."



The room became quiet.



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"We did not ask you to resume management."



"You asked whether I would continue assisting with the records."



"That is different."



"Then we agree."



Mr Liang looked down at his notes.



Shen Mingyuan remained silent.



Madam Liang's expression tightened.



Old Madam Shen appeared dissatisfied.



Mingzhao stood.



"I will wait for the revised schedule."



She bowed to the elders and left.



Qiao Yue followed with the document case.



Outside the meeting room, Mingzhao stopped.



"How many pages did we correct?"



Qiao Yue looked at the notes.



"Several."



"How many matters were settled?"



"Not many."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then we will not call it a final settlement."



Qiao Yue looked toward the carriage.



"Shall we return home?"



"Yes."



The temporary residence was waiting.



The workshop was waiting.



The business records were waiting.



Her life was waiting.



The Marquisate was not the only place that required her attention.



And she had no intention of returning simply because the family had not finished learning how to manage without her.

That evening, the Emperor received a short message from the Xu residence.



The former Empress had enjoyed her family meal.



She would return to the guest residence that evening.



The Emperor read it and placed it aside.



Gao Shun waited.



"Your Majesty?"



"Nothing."



The Emperor looked toward the window.



He had once assumed that Yuru would remain available whenever the palace needed her.



Now she was visiting her parents, reading books, and deciding where she wished to live.



The palace was still functioning.



The transition was still incomplete.



But the work was being handled by the people responsible for it.



The Emperor returned to his memorials.



At the Xu residence, Duke Xu had finally accepted that six dishes were enough.



At the Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had left a meeting without accepting another household responsibility.



And in the palace, the Emperor had received a message about the former Empress without finding a reason to summon her.



Three families had spent the day learning something.



One had learned that a meal did not need an additional dish.



One had learned that a property schedule did not become complete merely because everyone was tired of reading it.



And one had learned that a former Empress could visit her parents without submitting a report afterward.



None of these lessons had been inexpensive.



All of them would need repeating.
