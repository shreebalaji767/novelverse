# **Chapter 99: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Household Can Survive Without Its Head Inspecting Every Shelf. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That a Production Schedule Is More Useful When It Includes the Work That Has Not Yet Been Done. The Marquis's Heir Has Received a Request for an Explanation and Has Learned That the Most Honest Answer May Be a List of Documents Still Missing.**

**3rd Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru woke to a clear morning.

The rain had washed the western path, and the garden stones shone faintly beneath the early light. Fang Ru had already opened the windows and placed the household manual on the table.

Yuru looked at it.

"Has the author found another reason for the household head to lose sleep?"

Fang Ru glanced at the marked page.

"Today he explains how to inspect the servants' sleeping quarters."

"Does he recommend inspecting them personally?"

"Yes, Madam."

Yuru sighed.

"At least he is consistent."

She sat down and read the section.

The author recommended that a household head inspect the sleeping quarters at regular intervals, check whether the servants had sufficient bedding, and ensure that no one had been assigned a room unsuitable for the season.

The practical advice was reasonable.

The conclusion was not.

The author believed that the household head should perform the inspection personally, because a servant assigned to supervise the sleeping quarters might overlook a deficiency.

Yuru placed the book aside.

"Fang Ru, if I ask you to inspect the rooms, will you do it?"

"Of course."

"And if you find that one room lacks sufficient bedding?"

"I would arrange for it to be corrected."

"Would you wait for me to inspect the room before doing so?"

Fang Ru looked at her.

"No, Madam."

"Then the author has failed to consider that a responsible servant can correct a problem without waiting for the household head."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Perhaps he believes that the household head should know the problem has been corrected."

"That is different."

Yuru took up her notebook.

She wrote:

*The person responsible for a task should have enough authority to complete it. The household head should know whether the task has been completed, but need not perform the task again merely to prove that it has been completed.*

She read the sentence.

It was not a perfect rule.

There would be matters requiring approval, expenses exceeding a limit, or decisions that affected the household's wider arrangements.

But the distinction was useful.

Yuru marked the page and closed the notebook.

She had no intention of inspecting every shelf in the guest residence.

The shelves were standing.

The servants knew their duties.

The accounts were being kept.

That was sufficient for the morning.

At the eastern-market workshop, Shen Mingzhao received the next production report.

The first thirty bolts had passed internal inspection.

The next twenty were in production.

The final thirty had not yet been authorized.

The report included the afternoon inspection trial and the additional inspection costs.

Mingzhao read it, then turned to the workshop supervisor's note.

The supervisor had proposed beginning the final thirty bolts once the current twenty had passed inspection.

The proposal was reasonable, but Mingzhao did not approve it immediately.

She checked the contract schedule.

The workshop had sufficient time to complete the order without starting all remaining work at once. The deposit had covered the initial production requirements, but the accounts still needed to confirm the cost of the first groups before the final authorization.

She wrote a short instruction.

The next twenty bolts were to complete inspection first. The final thirty could be authorized once the supervisor submitted the updated material and labour estimate.

She added that the estimate should include the additional inspection time, rather than treating it as an unexpected expense after production.

Qiao Yue read the instruction before taking it to the workshop.

"Will the supervisor be disappointed?"

"Perhaps."

"He wants to begin the final group."

"He may begin it when the cost is known."

"Would it not be faster to authorize everything?"

"Faster is not always better."

Mingzhao looked at the production sheet.

The workshop had already learned that changing the folding method without comparing it to the approved sample could create problems. It had also learned that a small correction recorded early was less expensive than a large correction discovered at the end.

She did not intend to abandon those lessons merely because the workers wanted to move quickly.

"Tell him that the schedule is not being delayed," she said. "The final group has not been promised an earlier start."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao returned to the account papers.

The order was progressing.

The costs were becoming clearer.

That was enough.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received a letter from the northern administrative office.

The office had located a further page from the transport personnel register.

It confirmed that the two men previously identified had held the position of northern warehouse transport supervisor during different portions of the relevant season.

The page did not establish which man had been on duty when the four carts were dispatched.

It also did not identify the person who had received the movement notebook.

Mingyuan read the letter.

"Have they found the issue register?"

Liang Wen shook his head.

"Not yet. The central archive says the relevant volume is among the damaged records awaiting examination."

"Can they tell us when it will be examined?"

"They have not given a date."

Mingyuan placed the letter on the table.

The investigation had established that the notebook had been issued to the transport supervisor's office.

It had not established who held it.

It had not established what the notebook contained.

It had not established whether the four carts carried grain.

The grain discrepancy remained separate.

Forty-seven sacks in the original record.

Twenty covered by the instruction.

Eighteen confirmed as received.

Two unconfirmed.

Twenty-seven unexplained.

Mingyuan looked at the copied dispatch page.

The cargo column was still blank.

The destination was still incomplete.

He could not make the page more informative by staring at it.

"Send another request for the issue register," he said. "Ask the archive to notify us when the volume is available for examination."

Liang Wen wrote it down.

"And ask the northern office whether any local copy of the movement notebook was kept."

"Yes."

Mingyuan turned to Steward Zhou.

"Have we received any reply from the former supervisors?"

"Not yet. The southern district is verifying the address of the transferred man. The other man's recorded address is being checked."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Then we wait for the records."

Steward Zhou hesitated.

"Some people may say we are waiting too long."

"Some people may say that."

Mingyuan returned the documents to their folder.

He was beginning to understand that a record could be incomplete without being useless.

It could show where the next question belonged.

That was not a conclusion.

It was still progress.

The Marquis of Qingyuan had a different view.

When Mingyuan brought him the report, Shen Guowei frowned.

"Another request?"

"The archive has not examined the issue register."

"Then ask them to examine it immediately."

"They have not given a date."

"Why should an estate wait for a government archive?"

Mingyuan answered carefully.

"We can request priority, Father. We cannot claim the register contains an answer before it has been examined."

The Marquis looked at him.

"You have become very cautious."

Mingyuan did not deny it.

"The records have taught me to be cautious."

His father turned to the property-division papers.

"Are those ready?"

"Not yet. The corrected schedule is being checked against the supporting documents."

"Your sister will be pleased to hear that."

"She asked for the documents to be accurate."

The Marquis's expression grew colder.

"She has always been exacting."

Mingyuan looked at the schedule.

"That is not a reason to send her an inaccurate one."

For a moment, the Marquis said nothing.

He had expected the family to settle the matter more quickly. He had also expected Mingzhao to continue explaining the accounts, as she had done for years.

Instead, the estate was now required to prepare records without relying on her.

It was a difficult adjustment.

Mingyuan understood that.

He also understood that the difficulty did not justify asking his sister to resume the work.

"The schedule will be sent when it is ready," he said.

The Marquis dismissed him.

Mingyuan left the study with the documents under his arm.

He had not resolved the grain discrepancy.

He had not found the notebook.

He had not completed the property division.

But he had refused to turn missing evidence into a convenient accusation.

That was a result he could defend.

At the temporary residence, Mingzhao received a letter from the workshop supervisor.

The next twenty bolts had completed weaving.

The afternoon inspection would begin after the fabric had rested.

The supervisor had also submitted the updated estimate for the final thirty bolts.

The estimate included materials, labour, finishing, and the additional inspection time.

Mingzhao compared it with the earlier figures.

The costs were within the expected range.

She approved the final production authorization, subject to the same sample and specifications.

The supervisor could begin the last thirty bolts.

She signed the paper.

Qiao Yue took it.

"Now the whole order is underway?"

"Not the whole order. The first thirty are complete. The next twenty are being finished. The final thirty have been authorized."

Qiao Yue smiled.

"You always correct the wording."

"Because the wording tells us what has actually happened."

Mingzhao looked at the production schedule.

Thirty completed.

Twenty in progress.

Thirty authorized.

The numbers were clear.

The order was not yet complete.

The buyer had not inspected the goods.

The balance had not been paid.

There was no profit to calculate until the costs and final payment were known.

She placed the authorization with the contract.

The workshop could now proceed.

She did not need to go there.

That afternoon, Xu Yuru received a short note from Empress Dowager Wei.

The Dowager had asked whether Yuru's reading had progressed and whether she had found the household manual useful.

Yuru read the note with amusement.

She replied that the book contained useful advice about assigning duties, but its author appeared to believe that a household head should personally inspect every room, shelf, account, and servant.

She added that she had begun writing down the questions the author had forgotten to answer.

The reply was brief.

She did not mention the palace.

She did not offer to review any household arrangements.

She simply answered the Dowager's personal question.

Fang Ru sealed the letter.

"Will the Dowager ask for your notes?"

"Perhaps."

"Would you send them?"

Yuru considered it.

"If she asks, I may share the thoughts. But they are not palace regulations."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru returned to her book.

The distinction mattered.

A useful thought did not automatically become an instruction.

A personal conversation did not automatically become an administrative duty.

And a former Empress could read a household manual without turning it into a reform proposal.

At the Marquisate, the corrected property schedule remained under review.

The nine confirmed advances were still confirmed.

The four payment-confirmed entries still lacked clear repayment arrangements.

The ten uncertain entries remained uncertain.

The seventy-two taels in study repairs were still estate maintenance paid from Mingzhao's personal funds, with reimbursement undecided.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.

The furnishing payment remained estate expenditure.

The northern canal property remained outside the family division.

Mingyuan checked the classifications once more.

He found no new evidence.

He did not alter the figures.

He sent the schedule back to the accounts office with instructions to complete the supporting-document references.

The family would have to wait.

Near evening, Mingzhao received the workshop's inspection result.

The next twenty bolts had been examined after the fabric rested.

Eighteen passed the first inspection.

Two required minor corrections.

Both corrections had been completed, and the bolts passed the second inspection.

The report recorded the workers responsible and the time of correction.

Mingzhao read it.

She checked the quantities.

Thirty bolts completed and passed.

Twenty more completed and passed.

The final thirty were authorized and beginning production.

No buyer inspection had taken place.

No delivery had been made.

No remaining payment had been received.

She put the report away.

The workshop had made progress.

The family had not completed its division.

The transport office had not found the notebook.

The palace had not asked her to inspect its shelves.

It was an ordinary day, full of unfinished matters.

Mingzhao found that she could accept such a day.

Xu Yuru found that she could accept one too.

Neither woman had solved everything.

Neither had been required to.

And for both, that was becoming a more useful measure of independence than any declaration of it.
