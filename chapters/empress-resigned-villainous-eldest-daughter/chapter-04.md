# Chapter 4: The Palace Has Discovered That Nobody Knows Who Orders the Brooms. The Marquisate Has Discovered That Nobody Knows Who Owns the Grain.

**26th Day of the Third Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The first problem of the morning was a broom.

The second was a warehouse.

By noon, both had acquired official documentation, three separate opinions, and a degree of importance that would have surprised anyone who had not spent a lifetime working inside either the imperial palace or an aristocratic household.

At Chengming Palace, Empress Xu Yuru was informed that the Department of Palace Stores had submitted a written clarification concerning the annual replacement of brooms.

She read it once.

Then she read it again.

Finally, she placed the paper on the desk.

“Who wrote this?”

Madam Fang, standing beside the desk, answered, “The deputy director of the Department of Palace Stores, Your Majesty.”

“And did he understand the question?”

Madam Fang paused.

“He believes he did.”

“That is not the same thing.”

The report explained that brooms were purchased according to an established annual allowance. The Department of Palace Stores received the funds, recorded the incoming goods, and distributed them to the various palace divisions. The inner palace administrators recorded the quantities issued. The Empress's office received consolidated reports and approved exceptional replacements.

It was a perfectly respectable explanation.

It also failed to answer the question.

Xu Yuru picked up the report again.

“Does the Department of Palace Stores decide how many brooms are required?”

“Yes.”

“Does it inspect the existing stock?”

“It receives inventory reports.”

“Does it verify those reports?”

Madam Fang hesitated. “The report does not say.”

“Does the inner palace administration inspect the brooms before requesting replacements?”

“The report does not say.”

“Does anyone compare the number of brooms purchased with the number actually distributed?”

Madam Fang looked at the document.

“The report does not say.”

Xu Yuru set it down.

“Then we have received a report explaining that the brooms exist.”

Madam Fang lowered her eyes, but the slight movement at the corner of her mouth suggested she was struggling not to smile.

Xu Yuru did not smile.

She had spent eighteen years learning that a problem could become much more expensive once everyone agreed to stop looking at it.

“Send this back,” she said. “Ask for the original purchase record, the inventory at the beginning of the previous year, the quantities distributed, the remaining stock, and the number discarded as unusable.”

Madam Fang took the report.

“Should the Empress's office continue approving the annual allowance?”

“No.”

“Then who should approve it?”

“The department responsible for purchasing should submit its requirements to the authority responsible for expenditure. The department responsible for storage should verify the goods received. The divisions using the goods should report what they actually need.”

“And if their numbers differ?”

“Then they should explain the difference before asking me to decide which number is correct.”

Madam Fang nodded.

Xu Yuru added, “Do not write that I have abolished the annual broom allowance.”

“I would not dare, Your Majesty.”

“Someone will write it if we are not careful.”

Madam Fang bowed and withdrew.

Xu Yuru looked at the remaining documents.

The resignation petition had not yet been accepted. The emperor had not issued any decree, and the review of her responsibilities remained incomplete.

Yet the palace had already begun behaving as though the announcement had removed a supporting pillar from the building.

The problem was not that the pillar had disappeared.

The problem was that everyone had grown accustomed to leaning against it.

In the Hall of Mental Cultivation, Emperor Zhao Jingheng was experiencing the same difficulty from a different direction.

Grand Secretary Wei Zhong had arrived with a stack of documents.

The emperor looked at the stack.

“How many?”

“Twenty-three, Your Majesty.”

“Twenty-three responsibilities?”

“Twenty-three questions concerning the responsibilities already under review.”

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

Wei Zhong maintained a perfectly composed expression.

The emperor picked up the top document.

“Why does the Department of Palace Stores need permission from the Ministry of Revenue to inspect its own inventory?”

“It does not, Your Majesty.”

“Then why is this question here?”

“The department is uncertain whether the cost of damaged goods should be charged to its own operating allocation or submitted as an exceptional expenditure.”

“Who determines that?”

“The existing regulations distinguish between ordinary replacement and exceptional loss.”

“Then why are they asking?”

Wei Zhong replied carefully, “Because the regulations do not specify who must verify the cause of the loss.”

The emperor leaned back.

That, at least, was a genuine question.

He opened the next document.

“And this?”

“The Ministry of Rites requests clarification regarding ceremonial household expenditure.”

“Why?”

“The Ministry of Rites believes it should approve the ceremonial requirements. The imperial household believes it should approve the internal expenditure. The Department of Palace Stores believes it should purchase the materials. The Ministry of Revenue believes it should examine the accounts.”

“That sounds reasonable.”

“It would be, Your Majesty, if they agreed on the order in which those responsibilities should be exercised.”

The emperor put the paper down.

Wei Zhong continued.

“The departments have not yet identified whether they are disputing authority or merely the sequence of procedures.”

Zhao Jingheng rubbed his forehead.

“Then tell them to identify the difference.”

“I have.”

“And?”

“They have requested a meeting.”

The emperor stared at him.

Wei Zhong bowed.

“An official meeting.”

“Of course.”

“Several departments wish to attend.”

“Of course.”

“They have also requested that the Empress be present.”

The emperor's expression changed.

“No.”

Wei Zhong waited.

“The purpose of this review is to determine how the palace operates without requiring the Empress to personally settle every disagreement. If we summon her whenever the departments become confused, what exactly are we reviewing?”

Wei Zhong bowed more deeply.

“Your Majesty is correct.”

The emperor glanced at the documents again.

“Arrange the meeting. The Empress will not attend. You will preside. The Minister of Revenue, the Minister of Rites, the senior officials of Palace Stores, and the relevant inner palace administrators will be present.”

“Understood.”

“And no one is to leave the meeting by submitting a memorial requesting that another department take responsibility.”

Wei Zhong paused.

“Should I include that instruction in the meeting notice?”

“Yes.”

“Would Your Majesty prefer a formal prohibition?”

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

“Do you believe it requires a formal prohibition?”

Wei Zhong answered without hesitation.

“I believe it requires a formal prohibition, Your Majesty.”

The emperor closed his eyes.

“Write it.”

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao was looking at a warehouse inventory.

It was not the same inventory she had examined the previous evening.

This one had been prepared by Steward Zhou De after he retrieved the original records from the northern granary.

The discrepancy remained forty-seven sacks.

However, the new report revealed something more irritating than the missing grain.

The records had been prepared by three different clerks over the course of the previous year. Each clerk had used a different method of recording transfers.

One recorded grain when it left the granary.

Another recorded it when it arrived at the receiving estate.

The third recorded it according to the monthly allocation schedule, whether or not the delivery had actually occurred.

Mingzhao placed the papers side by side.

“Who approved this system?”

Steward Zhou stood across the table.

“The previous estate manager introduced the monthly allocation ledger. The receiving records were maintained by the granary clerks.”

“And who compared them?”

“Your Ladyship.”

“Before I took over the accounts?”

“No.”

“Then nobody compared them.”

Steward Zhou lowered his head.

“That appears to be the case.”

Mingzhao picked up one of the ledgers.

A line from the ninth month showed a transfer of twenty sacks to a tenant settlement. The receiving record listed eighteen.

In the tenth month, the monthly allocation ledger showed twelve sacks transferred to the same settlement, while the granary record listed fourteen.

There were several similar entries.

“Have you spoken to the receiving estate?”

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“I wished to bring the original records to Your Ladyship first.”

Mingzhao nodded.

“That was sensible.”

She turned another page.

The forty-seven sacks might represent actual missing grain. They might also represent incomplete recording, delayed deliveries, or entries that had been counted twice.

She was not prepared to accuse anyone merely because a column failed to balance.

A careless accusation could be as costly as a careless payment.

“Send a written request to the receiving estate,” she said. “Ask for their original delivery records for the ninth and tenth months, including the signatures of whoever accepted the grain.”

“Yes, Eldest Miss.”

“Do not ask whether they received forty-seven sacks. Ask them to provide the records. We will calculate the difference after both sides have been examined.”

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingzhao placed the ledger down.

“And the three hundred taels charged to the Second Young Master's residence?”

“Still unsupported.”

“Has the Second Young Master submitted the receipts?”

“No.”

“Has anyone asked him again?”

“I sent a reminder yesterday.”

“Send another today.”

Steward Zhou hesitated.

“Eldest Miss, the Second Young Master may find repeated requests insulting.”

“Then he should submit the receipts and end the insult.”

Steward Zhou lowered his head to conceal his expression.

Mingzhao continued, “If the expenditure was legitimate, the records will establish it. If it was not, his irritation will not make it legitimate.”

“Yes.”

She turned to the next page.

The account books were beginning to resemble a battlefield in which every participant had insisted that the other side had started the fighting.

Mingzhao had not expected the settlement to be easy.

She had expected it to be tedious.

Unfortunately, the two conditions had proved compatible.

The family meeting began shortly before the hour of Si.

Mr. Chen, the marquisate's former legal adviser, had arrived with a clerk and a document case. He was a narrow-faced man in his late forties, with the careful manner of someone who had spent years explaining to wealthy families that a signature was not the same thing as a legal conclusion.

Shen Guowei sat at the head of the room.

Madam Liang sat to his right, while Old Madam Shen occupied the central seat beside him. Shen Mingyuan and Shen Mingxiu had also been summoned.

Mingzhao arrived with Qiao Yue and Steward Zhou.

She carried three bundles of documents.

Mr. Chen looked at them.

“Eldest Miss has prepared the records?”

“I have prepared the records currently available to me.”

“That distinction is important.”

“It will become more important once we begin examining them.”

Shen Guowei gestured for her to sit.

“Let us settle the matter calmly.”

Mingzhao sat.

“I have no objection.”

Old Madam Shen's expression remained displeased.

“You speak as though this were an ordinary business arrangement. You are asking to divide the family property.”

“I am asking to identify which property belongs to whom, which property is shared, which sums are owed, and which obligations remain outstanding.”

“Must every family matter be reduced to an account?”

“No,” Mingzhao said. “Only the financial matters.”

Shen Mingxiu lowered her eyes.

Shen Mingyuan frowned.

Madam Liang intervened before the disagreement could grow.

“Mr. Chen has come to advise us. We should first hear his assessment.”

Mr. Chen opened his document case.

“I have reviewed the preliminary inventory supplied by Steward Zhou. However, there is a difficulty.”

Mingzhao looked at him.

“Which difficulty?”

“Your personal property, your dowry property, the assets managed by you on behalf of the marquisate, and the commercial interests held jointly with the family are not consistently separated in the existing records.”

“That is why I requested an independent valuation.”

Mr. Chen nodded.

“An independent valuation may be necessary. But the family must first establish the categories of property to be examined.”

“Agreed.”

He took out a sheet.

“I propose four initial categories. First, property clearly belonging to Eldest Miss personally, including her recorded dowry assets and inherited property. Second, commercial interests held independently in her name. Third, funds advanced by her to the marquisate or its household, where supporting records exist. Fourth, property belonging to the marquisate, which may be subject to lawful division or other settlement according to the relevant family and legal rules.”

Mingzhao listened without interrupting.

Mr. Chen continued.

“Any debts, guarantees, or obligations connected to those assets must also be recorded. A property cannot be valued without considering its liabilities.”

“Correct.”

Shen Guowei looked at his daughter.

“Your father is not refusing to recognize what belongs to you.”

Mingzhao turned toward him.

“Then we should have no difficulty recording it.”

The marquis's jaw tightened.

Mr. Chen glanced down at his papers.

“There is another matter. Because I have previously advised the marquisate, I cannot claim to be entirely independent of the family.”

Mingzhao looked at him with renewed interest.

“Are you willing to act as the family's legal adviser while an independent accountant examines the accounts?”

“Yes.”

“And are you willing to accept that the independent accountant may reach conclusions unfavorable to the marquisate?”

Mr. Chen did not answer immediately.

Shen Guowei looked displeased.

Madam Liang's fingers tightened around her handkerchief.

Finally, Mr. Chen said, “An accountant's findings should be based on records. They should not be adjusted according to whom they inconvenience.”

Mingzhao nodded.

“Then we can proceed.”

Shen Mingyuan leaned back.

“Must we hire another person? The estate already has accountants.”

“The estate accountants prepared the accounts being reviewed.”

“That does not mean they are dishonest.”

“I did not say they were.”

“Then why are you treating them as though they cannot be trusted?”

Mingzhao looked at him.

“Because they are not independent.”

Shen Mingyuan's face reddened.

“You have managed the estate accounts for six years. Are you saying you could not manage them properly?”

“I am saying that I have participated in the administration. I cannot be the only person who verifies my own claims.”

For a moment, nobody spoke.

That answer was difficult to argue against without appearing unreasonable.

Mr. Chen cleared his throat.

“I recommend appointing an accountant who has no prior business relationship with the marquisate. The accountant should examine the records under the supervision of both parties.”

Shen Guowei looked at Mingzhao.

“Would that satisfy you?”

“It would allow the examination to begin.”

“Then we shall arrange it.”

Mingzhao opened the first bundle of documents.

“There is one more issue.”

Shen Guowei's expression became wary.

“What now?”

“The grain inventory.”

She placed the northern warehouse ledger on the table.

“The preliminary records show a discrepancy of forty-seven sacks. The receiving documents are incomplete. I have requested the original delivery records from the receiving estate.”

Shen Mingyuan frowned.

“Why is that part of this discussion?”

“Because the grain belongs to the family estate. Its value affects the estate's accounts.”

“Then the estate can investigate it.”

“That is what I am doing.”

“You are no longer willing to manage the household, yet you are still examining its warehouses?”

Mingzhao regarded him calmly.

“I am completing the examination I began before the family division was proposed. I will not leave an unresolved discrepancy in the accounts and allow it to be attributed to me later.”

Shen Mingyuan opened his mouth.

Then he closed it.

The explanation was unpleasant, but entirely reasonable.

Old Madam Shen looked at her granddaughter.

“You could have avoided all this by simply agreeing to remain in charge until your brother was more experienced.”

Mingzhao turned toward her.

“How much longer?”

Old Madam Shen's face hardened.

“Do not speak to your grandmother in that manner.”

“I am asking how much longer I would be expected to remain responsible.”

“You are the eldest daughter.”

“I have been the eldest daughter for twenty-six years.”

“And your brother is the heir.”

“Then he should begin learning how to manage the responsibilities that come with being the heir.”

Shen Mingyuan's expression darkened.

Madam Liang spoke sharply.

“Mingzhao.”

Mingzhao fell silent.

Not because she had been persuaded, but because the point had already been made.

Mr. Chen carefully rearranged the papers.

“Perhaps we should proceed with the appointment of the independent accountant first. Other questions can be addressed after the preliminary valuation.”

For once, everyone accepted the suggestion.

At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru was receiving the first draft of the proposed responsibility register.

It was not a particularly elegant document.

The original eighty-seven responsibilities had been listed in one column. The five informal duties discovered by the Empress's office had been added below them, followed by two further matters identified during the review.

The total now stood at ninety-four.

However, the numbers did not mean that ninety-four separate departments were required.

Several responsibilities belonged to the same administrative process.

The register attempted to distinguish between routine duties, exceptional decisions, ceremonial approvals, financial authorization, and matters that required the emperor's direct attention.

Xu Yuru read it slowly.

Madam Fang stood nearby.

“Grand Secretary Wei sent the draft for Your Majesty's review.”

“Does he expect me to approve it?”

“He requests comments on the accuracy of the responsibilities currently handled through Chengming Palace.”

“That is reasonable.”

Xu Yuru turned to the first page.

She recognized the handwriting of several clerks. Some entries had been copied from old regulations. Others were based on the records maintained by her office.

She found the section concerning disputes among palace servants.

The proposed arrangement placed routine disciplinary matters under the senior administrators, with serious cases referred to the appropriate authority.

She nodded.

Then she found the section concerning ceremonial expenditure.

The Ministry of Rites was listed as responsible for determining ceremonial requirements, while the imperial household was responsible for internal arrangements and procurement.

She made a small correction.

“Add that expenditure must be checked against the approved allocation.”

Madam Fang noted it.

Xu Yuru continued.

The next section concerned the distribution of medicine to senior servants.

She paused.

“Why is this listed as an Empress's responsibility?”

Madam Fang looked at the register.

“It has been handled through Your Majesty's office for many years.”

“That does not answer the question.”

“The physician's office determines the medicine. The palace administrators arrange distribution.”

“Then the Empress's office does not need to be involved.”

Madam Fang made another note.

Xu Yuru moved to the section concerning the imperial children's household arrangements.

She read the entry twice.

“Who is responsible for approving the personal household expenditure of the princes and princesses?”

“The current arrangement places it under Your Majesty's supervision.”

“Does that mean every prince's household must submit every ordinary expense to me?”

Madam Fang looked uncomfortable.

“It has become the practice.”

“Then the practice must be examined.”

She placed the register aside.

“Some responsibilities are genuine. Others are simply habits that survived because nobody asked whether they were necessary.”

Madam Fang nodded.

“Your Majesty has always been able to resolve these matters quickly.”

“That is precisely the difficulty.”

Xu Yuru looked toward the window.

For years, she had taken pride in knowing how every part of the inner palace functioned. She could recognize a false expense, detect a strained relationship between servants, identify a ceremony that would create unnecessary work, and predict which department would fail to deliver a requested item on time.

She had become efficient.

Perhaps too efficient.

When a department made a mistake, she corrected it. When an official failed to communicate, she sent the necessary instruction. When two divisions disagreed, she settled the matter.

The palace had learned to wait.

It had learned that the Empress would eventually read the documents, understand the problem, and decide.

She had believed she was keeping the system functioning.

Now she could see how much of the system had quietly ceased to function without her.

Xu Yuru picked up her brush.

“Add a column for the person or office currently performing each task. Then add another for the authority that should perform it under the regulations.”

Madam Fang looked at her.

“That will make the register longer.”

“Yes.”

“Considerably longer.”

“Then perhaps it will finally become useful.”

The meeting arranged by Grand Secretary Wei Zhong began shortly after midday.

It took place in an administrative hall outside the inner palace, where the senior officials could review records without disturbing the Empress's private residence.

Minister Lu of Revenue sat beside Minister Han of Personnel. The senior official from the Department of Palace Stores sat opposite them, accompanied by two clerks. Three inner palace administrators had also been summoned.

Wei Zhong opened the meeting.

“The emperor has ordered us to determine the proper distribution of responsibilities currently handled through the Empress's office. We are not here to decide which department would prefer to avoid a task.”

The senior official from Palace Stores lowered his eyes.

Minister Lu folded his hands.

“Then we should begin with expenditure authorization.”

Minister Han looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because financial matters require clear authority.”

“Personnel matters require clear authority as well.”

Wei Zhong raised one hand.

“We will proceed according to the records.”

The first subject was the annual purchase of palace supplies.

The Department of Palace Stores explained that it received requests from the inner palace administrators and purchased the required goods under approved expenditure limits.

Minister Lu asked who verified the quantity.

The official answered that the receiving clerks did.

Minister Lu asked who verified the quality.

The official answered that the receiving clerks did.

Minister Lu asked who verified the price.

The official explained that prices were compared with approved market rates.

Minister Lu asked who compared the purchased quantities with the amount actually distributed.

The official paused.

The inner palace administrator answered that her division maintained distribution records.

Minister Lu asked whether the two sets of records were compared.

The administrator looked at the senior official.

The senior official looked at the clerk.

The clerk looked at the table.

Wei Zhong waited.

Finally, the administrator said, “The consolidated report was submitted to the Empress's office.”

Minister Lu asked, “That was not my question.”

“No, Minister.”

“Were the records compared?”

“Not routinely.”

Wei Zhong made a note.

The next subject was the approval of replacement goods.

The discussion lasted nearly half an hour.

By the end of it, the officials had established that three departments possessed pieces of the same process, but none was responsible for checking whether the entire process had been completed correctly.

Wei Zhong set down his brush.

“Then the procedure should be written as follows. The requesting division records its requirements. Palace Stores verifies existing stock and purchases the approved quantity. The receiving clerks record goods received. The distributing division records goods issued. A designated reviewing office compares the records.”

Minister Lu nodded.

“And expenditure remains subject to the approved budget.”

“Correct.”

The senior official from Palace Stores looked uneasy.

“Who will designate the reviewing office?”

Wei Zhong looked at him.

“The department responsible for the accounts should propose an arrangement.”

“And who will approve the arrangement?”

Wei Zhong stared at him.

Minister Lu coughed into his sleeve.

Minister Han looked down at his papers.

After a moment, Wei Zhong answered.

“The emperor will approve the revised procedure.”

The official bowed.

The meeting continued.

It was not a triumph.

No one had discovered a hidden conspiracy, no official had been dragged away in disgrace, and no miraculous solution had appeared.

They had simply discovered that the palace's administration contained gaps large enough for ordinary goods to pass through without anyone being able to say exactly where they had gone.

For a government, this was a rather embarrassing discovery.

For a palace, it was also expensive.

By late afternoon, Mingzhao had received the first response concerning the independent accountant.

The proposed candidate was a man named Sun Qiming, who had previously examined the accounts of two merchant associations and one provincial grain transport office. He had no recorded business dealings with the Shen family.

Mingzhao examined his references.

“Where did he study?”

Steward Zhou answered, “He trained under an accountant in the capital and later worked with several commercial houses.”

“Does he have experience with estate land and agricultural accounts?”

“Yes. One of his previous examinations involved a large grain estate.”

“Has he examined family dowry records?”

“His references do not specify.”

Mingzhao put the papers down.

“Ask whether he is willing to examine both commercial and household accounts. Also ask for his fee and the conditions under which he would withdraw from the work.”

Steward Zhou nodded.

“Would Eldest Miss like to meet him?”

“Yes, after he confirms that he has no conflict of interest.”

Qiao Yue, who had been arranging the documents, glanced at the growing stack.

“Eldest Miss, at this rate, the settlement will require more paper than the estate has grain.”

Mingzhao did not look up.

“Paper is cheaper.”

“That is true.”

“And it does not spoil in storage.”

Qiao Yue considered this.

“Unless it gets wet.”

“Then we shall keep it dry.”

Qiao Yue sighed.

She had served Mingzhao long enough to know that the conversation was finished.

A servant entered with another document.

It was the property inquiry from the northern district.

Mingzhao opened it.

The smaller residence near the canal had been inspected by the agent. The roof required repairs, the rear drainage channel was partially blocked, and the courtyard wall showed signs of water damage.

The title records were complete.

The owner was willing to negotiate.

The eastern district property remained more attractive, but its title documents were incomplete because a previous owner had transferred part of the land through a private arrangement that had not been fully registered.

Mingzhao placed the two reports side by side.

“Do not make an offer on either property yet.”

Qiao Yue looked at the canal residence.

“Even though its title is complete?”

“Especially because it requires repairs. We do not know the full cost.”

“And the eastern residence?”

“Do not pay for a house whose ownership is still being explained.”

Qiao Yue nodded.

“Then where will we live?”

“In the eastern courtyard until the settlement is complete.”

Qiao Yue looked toward the window.

“That sounds sensible.”

“It is.”

“It also sounds like we will be here for a long time.”

Mingzhao turned another page.

“That is why we are doing the work properly.”

At the palace, the emperor received the first summary from Wei Zhong before the evening meal.

The summary was brief.

It stated that the review had identified several administrative responsibilities being handled through the Empress's office despite existing departments already possessing the authority to perform them.

It also noted that certain procedures lacked clear verification arrangements.

Zhao Jingheng read the report.

“Has the Empress seen the register?”

“She has reviewed the responsibilities currently handled through Chengming Palace and submitted several corrections.”

“Did she approve the proposed distribution?”

“She has not been asked to approve it.”

“Good.”

Wei Zhong waited.

The emperor turned to the final page.

“Has the broom question been resolved?”

Wei Zhong remained silent for a moment.

“The purchase and distribution procedure has been clarified.”

“And the number of brooms?”

“The Department of Palace Stores has been ordered to submit its stock records.”

“So the question has not been resolved.”

“Not entirely, Your Majesty.”

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

Wei Zhong bowed.

The emperor placed the report on his desk.

“Very well. Continue the review.”

He considered the matter for a moment.

“Has the Crown Prince been informed?”

“Not yet.”

“Inform him that he is to attend the next review session concerning the administration of the imperial children's households.”

Wei Zhong looked up.

“His Highness the Crown Prince?”

“Yes.”

“Should the Second Prince also attend?”

Zhao Jingheng thought about it.

“Yes. If the responsibility register includes the household arrangements of the princes, they should understand the procedures that concern their own households.”

Wei Zhong bowed.

“And the Third Princess?”

The emperor glanced at him.

“She has expressed an interest in the household accounts.”

“She has, Your Majesty.”

“Then she may attend as an observer if the Empress considers it appropriate.”

Wei Zhong accepted the instruction.

The emperor looked toward the open doors.

For years, the imperial household had been managed by the Empress with such consistency that he had rarely considered how many people depended on her personal attention.

He had believed that her competence was a source of stability.

He was beginning to understand that it had also become a convenient excuse for everyone else.

The emperor did not intend to let the palace collapse merely because the Empress wished to resign.

But neither could he ask her to remain indefinitely simply because the departments had grown comfortable.

That would not be a solution.

It would merely be another way of postponing one.

That evening, at the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao received the second reminder from the estate clerks.

Shen Mingyuan had still not submitted the receipts for the three hundred taels charged to his residence.

She read the note and placed it beside the account ledger.

Steward Zhou stood nearby.

“Would you like me to visit the Second Young Master's residence personally?”

“No. Send the formal request through the household office.”

“Understood.”

“If he claims the expenditure was approved verbally, ask him to identify who approved it and when.”

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingzhao closed the ledger.

She had not expected her family to welcome the division of property. She had not expected her father to surrender authority without argument, her mother to abandon concern for reputation, or her brother to suddenly become diligent.

But she had expected the records to be examined.

That was the first condition of any settlement.

Outside, the courtyard lamps were being lit.

Qiao Yue entered carrying a small tray.

“Eldest Miss, the kitchen has sent your evening meal.”

Mingzhao glanced at the tray.

“Why is there only one dish?”

“The kitchen said the other dishes were being prepared.”

“Were they ordered?”

“Yes.”

“By whom?”

Qiao Yue hesitated.

“The household office.”

Mingzhao looked at the tray again.

“Then we should wait for the rest.”

Qiao Yue smiled faintly.

“Perhaps the kitchen is waiting for someone to approve the remaining dishes.”

Mingzhao looked at her.

“Do not start.”

Qiao Yue immediately lowered her head.

“Yes, Eldest Miss.”

But Mingzhao's expression softened slightly.

For the first time in six years, she had begun to wonder what the household would do when she no longer answered every question.

The thought was not entirely satisfying.

It was, however, necessary.

She returned to the papers.

There were still accounts to examine, property to value, debts to identify, and a residence to find.

Her departure had not yet begun.

But the family could no longer pretend that she had not announced it.

And in the imperial palace, the emperor had finally begun to understand that an institution could not be maintained forever by relying on one woman's ability to correct everyone else's mistakes.

Neither settlement was close to completion.

Both had finally acquired something they had lacked for years.

A beginning.

**End of Chapter 4.**
