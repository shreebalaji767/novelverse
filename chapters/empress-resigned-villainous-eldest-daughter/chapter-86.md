# **Chapter 86: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Borrow Two Books and Has Decided That an Invitation Need Not Become an Immediate Obligation. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received Confirmation of a Full Delivery and Has Discovered That Receiving Goods and Paying for Them Are Two Separate Talents. The Marquisate's New Procedure Has Encountered Its First Urgent Purchase and Is Being Tested by People Who Would Prefer Not to Complete Forms.**

**20th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The former Empress had not yet visited her father's library.

The two books remained ready in the eastern courtyard, and the wrapped volume she intended to return remained beside her writing table.

There was no difficulty.

At least, there was no difficulty until Fang Ru asked whether the books should be moved closer to the door.

Yuru looked up from her breakfast.

"Why?"

"So that they can be taken when you visit."

"They are in the eastern courtyard."

"Yes."

"And we are in the western guest residence."

"Yes."

"Then moving them closer to the door will not make them any closer to us."

Fang Ru considered this.

"That is true."

Yuru returned to her tea.

Fang Ru was silent for a moment.

"Would you like to visit your parents today?"

Yuru looked at the weather beyond the window. The morning was clear. There was no rain forecast, and the garden paths would be pleasant.

"I may visit in a few days."

"Shall I send word?"

"Not yet. I have not chosen a day."

Fang Ru nodded.

The answer was reasonable.

It was also a reminder that Yuru no longer needed to arrange every free afternoon in advance. She could decide to visit her parents when she wished, rather than when a schedule made it convenient.

After breakfast, she took her usual walk.

The western path was quiet. A gardener was trimming a low hedge, and two attendants carried a basket of fresh leaves toward the kitchen. Yuru greeted them and continued toward the pavilion.

She stopped there for a while.

From the pavilion, the western garden could be seen through the trees. The gardeners had recently replaced several broken paving stones near the water channel. The work had been completed without requiring her to inspect it.

She found that mildly satisfying.

Fang Ru stood a short distance away.

"Madam, would you like to sit?"

Yuru sat.

For several moments, neither spoke.

Then Fang Ru said, "You used to come here with a list of things to inspect."

"I did."

"Sometimes you would leave with a longer list than the one you brought."

"That also happened."

"Do you miss it?"

Yuru considered the question.

She missed the certainty of a completed task. She missed knowing that a matter had been resolved because she had examined it, corrected it, and seen the result.

She did not miss being the person to whom every matter was brought.

"Sometimes," she said. "But I do not miss being unable to leave."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru looked toward the water channel.

"Perhaps I shall visit my parents in a few days. I would like to return Father's book and borrow the others."

"Then we shall prepare when you decide."

"Yes."

She remained at the pavilion until the sun climbed higher.

There was no document waiting.

No official required her answer.

No one had asked her to supervise the gardeners.

The morning passed pleasantly.

At the eastern market, the buyer's representative arrived at the transfer point with a collection team.

The remaining two hundred bolts were counted against the dispatch records.

The first consignment contained twenty-eight bolts. The second contained one hundred and seventy-two. The total matched the contract.

The receiving clerk examined the outer containers, checked the seals, and confirmed that no visible damage had occurred during transit.

The buyer's representative signed the collection record.

By the middle of the afternoon, the goods had reached Zhou Wenqi's designated warehouse.

Mingzhao received the confirmation before the evening meal.

Qiao Yue entered with the letter held carefully between both hands.

"The buyer's warehouse has received all two hundred bolts."

Mingzhao set down her brush.

"All two hundred?"

"Yes. The warehouse count matches the transfer-point records. The outer containers were intact, and the buyer's representative has signed the receipt."

Mingzhao took the letter.

She read the confirmation, then examined the attached count.

Twenty-eight.

One hundred and seventy-two.

Two hundred.

The figures matched.

"Send the buyer an acknowledgment," she said. "Ask for the remaining payment according to the contract."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"Will the order finally be complete?"

"The goods have been delivered."

"That sounds like completion."

"The payment remains."

Qiao Yue looked at her.

"I knew it."

Mingzhao ignored the remark and checked the delivery record again.

The contract had required staged inspection, staged dispatch, and final settlement after delivery. The inspection had been completed. All two hundred bolts had been accepted. Both consignments had reached the buyer's warehouse.

The delivery obligation was now fulfilled.

The remaining payment was due.

That was progress, and she allowed herself to be pleased by it.

Lin Shuyun arrived before the acknowledgment was sent.

She read the report and smiled.

"All two hundred."

"All two hundred."

"No damage."

"None reported."

"No rejected goods."

"None."

Lin put the paper down.

"Then I shall say it plainly. We have completed the order."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"We have completed production, inspection, dispatch, and delivery."

Lin closed her eyes.

"That is what I meant."

"Then say that."

"I refuse to conduct business with a woman who has so many definitions of the word 'complete.'"

"You have conducted business with me for several months."

"And I have suffered."

Mingzhao smiled faintly.

Sun Qiming arrived shortly afterward with the revised cost statement.

He had included the final transport charges, the additional packing materials, and the wages of the four additional workers. The training wages remained separate from ordinary wages. The extra inspection time was recorded as an actual cost, not hidden inside general production expenses.

The statement now contained the full expenditure.

Sun placed it before them.

"The delivery is confirmed. The carrier's charges are final. The buyer's remaining payment is still outstanding, but the expenditure side can now be closed."

Mingzhao read through the entries.

"Have you checked the packing charges against the four additional containers?"

"Yes."

"And the training wages?"

"Separated."

"The revised folding method?"

"Recorded as a reduction in working time for the stages where it was used. I have not treated the full theoretical saving as an actual saving."

"Good."

Lin leaned forward.

"Then what is the result?"

Sun looked at the figures.

"The order has produced a positive margin on the recorded production and transport costs. However, I recommend waiting until the remaining payment is received before closing the account."

Mingzhao looked up.

"How much is the margin?"

Sun gave her the amount and explained the calculation.

The figure was modest.

It was not the enormous profit that Lin had once imagined when the order was first proposed. The training expenses had been higher than expected, and the revised folding method had not saved as much as the earliest estimate suggested. Additional packing and inspection had also reduced the margin.

But the order had not lost money.

Mingzhao examined the calculation.

"Your figures are consistent."

"Yes."

"Then record the preliminary profit as provisional until payment."

Sun nodded.

Lin leaned back.

"Positive."

"Provisional," Mingzhao corrected.

"Positive and provisional."

"That is acceptable."

Lin laughed.

"At least we have reached a point where I can say something pleasant without being corrected."

Sun gathered the supporting documents.

"The account will be final when the remaining payment arrives."

Mingzhao nodded.

She was pleased.

She had negotiated the contract, supervised the production arrangements without taking every task into her own hands, managed the training costs, and insisted on a proper inspection schedule. The goods had been delivered in full.

There had been mistakes, adjustments, and delays.

There had also been a positive margin.

It was not a fortune.

It was the result of a business conducted under agreed terms.

That mattered more.

The buyer's payment arrived the following afternoon.

It came with a remittance note and a reference to the contract number.

Mingzhao did not open the account book until Sun Qiming had checked the payment against the agreed balance.

He compared the amount, date, and contract reference.

Then he nodded.

"The remaining payment has been received in full."

Lin Shuyun looked at him.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"Could there be another amount hidden somewhere?"

Sun looked at her.

"Do you want me to search for one?"

"No. I would prefer to have received everything."

"Then you have."

Mingzhao reviewed the receipt.

The payment matched the balance.

She instructed the clerk to enter the amount and attach the remittance note to the contract record.

The account was now ready for final closure.

Sun completed the final statement.

It showed the original material cost, ordinary wages, training wages, inspection expenses, packing expenses, transport charges, and the full contract revenue. The revised folding method had produced a limited saving in certain stages, but that saving had been partly offset by training and inspection costs. The final margin remained positive.

Mingzhao read the statement carefully.

"Keep the production records with the cost statement. We will need them if we accept another order."

Sun nodded.

"That is sensible."

Lin looked at the final figure.

"Now may I say that we made a profit?"

"Yes."

"Without an additional qualification?"

"Yes."

Lin smiled broadly.

"Then I shall say it twice."

"Once is enough."

"We made a profit."

Mingzhao allowed herself a moment of satisfaction.

The profit was not large enough to justify extravagance. It was large enough to demonstrate that the workshop could complete an order, satisfy a buyer, pay its workers, and retain a margin after all costs were counted.

That was a better result than a grand promise.

Sun closed the ledger.

"The account is complete."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Now it is."

Lin raised her cup.

"To the two hundred bolts."

"To the workers who made them," Mingzhao said.

"And to the buyer who paid for them."

"That too."

They drank tea.

For once, no one mentioned the remaining documents.

At the Marquisate, the new purchasing procedure encountered its first urgent purchase.

A section of the southern kitchen's water channel had cracked during the morning. Water had begun leaking into the adjoining storage passage. The kitchen steward reported that delaying repairs until the next ordinary purchasing cycle could damage stored fuel and several wooden shelves.

The matter was urgent.

It was also a relatively small repair.

The kitchen steward sent the request to Steward Zhou.

Zhou read it, inspected the damage, and authorized the purchase of replacement stone and labour.

The purchasing clerk immediately began gathering the necessary information.

Then he stopped.

"The authorization form requires the estimated quantity."

"Then estimate it," Zhou said.

"The mason has not yet measured the full length."

"Then ask him to measure."

"The repair should begin today."

"Then begin the necessary work after recording the urgent authorization."

The clerk looked uncertain.

"Should we wait for the complete estimate?"

"No. The channel is leaking now."

The clerk hesitated.

"But the procedure—"

"Has an urgent-purchase provision."

Zhou pointed to the relevant section.

"Record why the purchase cannot wait. Record what has been authorized. When the mason finishes measuring, attach the actual quantity. When the work is completed, the receiving steward must confirm what was used."

The clerk nodded.

The repair began.

By late afternoon, the mason had replaced the damaged section. The final quantity of stone was slightly greater than the first estimate, because the crack extended farther beneath the storage passage than had been visible from the surface.

The additional quantity was recorded.

The original estimate remained visible.

The difference was explained and signed by the mason, the receiving steward, and the purchasing clerk.

Liang Wen examined the entry.

"Everything is in order."

The purchasing clerk looked relieved.

"Then we can close it?"

"Once the payment record is attached."

The clerk sighed.

Liang Wen did not sympathize.

The repair had been completed without delay. The urgent-purchase provision had worked as intended. The extra stone had been recorded, and no one had needed to pretend that the original estimate had been correct.

The register was longer than it would have been under the old system.

It was also more useful.

Steward Zhou examined the completed record.

"Good."

Liang Wen bowed.

Zhou pointed to the urgent-purchase provision.

"Make sure the other stewards know they can use this procedure when necessary. I do not want anyone delaying a repair because they are afraid of the register."

"Yes, Steward."

"And I do not want anyone calling every purchase urgent because they dislike waiting."

"Yes."

Zhou walked away.

Liang Wen looked at the page.

The procedure had survived its first urgent purchase.

It had also survived the complaint of a clerk who thought a neat register was more important than an accurate one.

The clerk in question was now asking whether the new form could be printed on a smaller sheet.

Liang Wen decided not to answer immediately.

Mingyuan received the repair report before dinner.

He read it and approved the additional quantity.

"Was the original estimate preserved?"

"Yes."

"Was the difference explained?"

"Yes."

"Was the urgent authorization recorded before the work began?"

"Yes."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Then file it."

The clerk bowed.

Mingyuan glanced at the other reports on his desk.

The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.

The old payment voucher had still not been found.

The property division schedule had not been finalized.

The northern canal property remained outside the family estate division.

The new procedure had not solved these matters.

But it had allowed an urgent repair to proceed without sacrificing the record.

That was enough for one day.

He set the report aside.

Across the estate, Shen Mingxiu was still reviewing the southern roof repair schedule. She had learned that a repair estimate was not the same as an authorization, and that approving work in principle did not mean that a mason could begin without a confirmed quantity and agreed price.

She had complained about the paperwork.

She had also discovered that the paperwork prevented a contractor from presenting a second estimate that quietly included work already covered by the first.

She was beginning to understand why Mingzhao had insisted on detailed records.

She did not yet appreciate the full extent of her elder sister's methods.

But she was learning.

That evening, in her temporary residence, Mingzhao received a brief note from the workshop.

The order's final account had been closed.

The two hundred bolts had been delivered and paid for. The buyer had acknowledged receipt. The workers' wages had been paid according to the agreed schedule. The workshop's records were complete.

Mingzhao read the note and placed it beside the final cost statement.

Qiao Yue entered carrying tea.

"Is everything finished?"

"The order is."

"Then perhaps tomorrow will be quiet."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Why would tomorrow be quiet?"

"Because today we have completed an entire order."

"That does not prevent another problem from appearing."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Must you always be correct?"

"No."

"That is reassuring."

Mingzhao picked up the final account.

The order had been profitable.

The profit was modest, but it was real. The workers had been paid, the buyer had received what had been promised, and the records could be understood by someone who had not been present during production.

She had not needed to borrow from the Marquisate.

She had not needed to conceal an expense.

She had not needed to promise delivery before the goods were ready.

It was a small independent success.

She would not pretend otherwise.

"Prepare the final copies," she said. "One for Lin Shuyun, one for Sun Qiming, and one for the workshop archive."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"And the next order?"

"We have not accepted one."

"Then we shall not prepare for one."

Qiao Yue considered this.

"That is a very unusual answer."

"It is the correct answer."

Mingzhao put the account away.

She had once managed an entire estate because everyone expected her to do so. Now she was building something smaller, with boundaries she could understand.

The work was not easy.

It was, however, hers.

In the palace, Yuru received a short reply from her father.

He thanked her for the message and said that the books would remain available whenever she wished to visit.

He had also mentioned that he had found another volume concerning the management of household estates.

Yuru read the note.

Fang Ru looked over her shoulder.

"Another book?"

"Yes."

"How many does your father intend to lend you?"

"I do not know."

"Perhaps he is building a library in the guest residence."

"That would require moving the furniture."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Then perhaps he will stop at three."

Yuru folded the letter.

She had not decided when to visit the eastern courtyard. She had not decided where she would eventually live. The Duke's offer of a residence remained open, but she did not need to accept it merely because it was available.

The guest residence was comfortable for now.

Her attendant was with her.

Her parents were nearby.

The Dowager had invited her to visit whenever she wished.

The Emperor had not sent administrative documents.

The palace had continued to function.

Yuru looked toward the window.

"Perhaps I shall visit Father in two days."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Shall I prepare the wrapped book?"

"Yes. We can return it then."

"And borrow the other two?"

"If I still wish to."

Fang Ru looked at her.

"Then I shall prepare for a possible visit."

"That is all."

Yuru returned to her tea.

There was no need to make the decision larger than it was.

She could visit her father, return a book, borrow another, and come back.

It was an ordinary thing.

She found herself looking forward to it.
