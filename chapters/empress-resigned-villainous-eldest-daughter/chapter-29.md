# **Chapter 29: The Empress Has Three Days Left. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has One Full Day of Independence. The Palace Has Rehearsed Its Ceremony. The New Household Has Misplaced a Cooking Pot.**

**21st Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first full morning of Shen Mingzhao's independent household began with a missing cooking pot.



It was not an expensive cooking pot.



It was not an heirloom.



It had not been included in the personal property inventory because it belonged to the lodging house.



Nevertheless, it was missing.



The senior maid had discovered the problem while preparing breakfast. She had checked the kitchen shelves, the rear courtyard, and the storage corner.



The pot was nowhere to be found.



Qiao Yue examined the kitchen.



"Did we bring it from the Marquisate?"



"No," the maid replied. "Madam Wu said it belonged to the house."



"Then perhaps she moved it."



"She says she did not."



Qiao Yue looked at the other utensils.



"How many pots are there?"



"Two."



"How many should there be?"



"Three."



Qiao Yue sighed.



She had expected the first day of independence to involve a little confusion.



She had not expected it to involve a missing cooking pot.



Mingzhao entered the kitchen carrying her household ledger.



"What happened?"



"The third pot is missing."



"Was it listed in the rental inventory?"



The maid nodded.



"Yes, Miss."



"Then it is the owner's responsibility to identify its location."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Should I ask Madam Wu?"



"Yes. Politely."



Qiao Yue went to the receiving room.



Madam Wu arrived a few moments later, looking mildly offended.



"I have not taken anything from the kitchen."



"Nobody has accused you," Qiao Yue said.



"Then why are you asking?"



"Because the pot was listed in the inventory and is not here."



Madam Wu thought for a moment.



Then she turned toward the rear courtyard.



"Ah. The carpenter moved it."



The carpenter, who had repaired the kitchen shelves the previous day, had placed the pot in the storage corner to make room for his tools.



It had been covered with a piece of cloth and forgotten.



The pot was recovered.



The kitchen resumed its work.



Mingzhao entered the result in the household notes.



Qiao Yue stared at her.



"Are you recording the cooking pot?"



"It was an item included in the rental inventory."



"Do we need a separate record for every bowl?"



"Not every bowl."



"That is a relief."



"Only the bowls belonging to the landlord."



Qiao Yue looked toward the ceiling.



Independence, she was discovering, had not made her mistress less particular.



It had merely changed the number of people required to endure it.

After breakfast, Mingzhao reviewed the first household expenditure report.



The total was higher than she had expected.



The rental deposit had already been recorded separately. The rent was due at the beginning of the month. The transport expenses were complete. The carpenter's fee had been paid.



The remaining expenses concerned food, water, fuel, servant wages, and several small household purchases.



She compared the figures with her initial estimate.



The difference was not large.



It was, however, enough to demonstrate that the cost of maintaining a small household did not consist only of rent and servants' wages.



There were small purchases that had previously been absorbed into the Marquisate's general expenditure.



A bundle of kindling.



A replacement water jar.



Oil for the lamps.



Cleaning materials.



The cost of having a damaged door hinge repaired.



Each item was minor.



Together, they formed an actual household budget.



Mingzhao put down the report.



"How much fuel remains?"



The senior maid answered.



"Enough for several days."



"How much oil?"



"Two jars."



"How much is required for the week?"



The maid considered.



"One more jar should be sufficient."



Mingzhao wrote it down.



Qiao Yue leaned over her shoulder.



"At this rate, you will calculate the cost of every meal."



"That is how a household knows what it spends."



"At the Marquisate, the kitchen prepared meals without asking you to calculate the price of each bowl of rice."



"At the Marquisate, the kitchen had an established supply account."



"And here?"



"Here we have to establish one."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Perhaps we should have brought the kitchen account book."



"We brought the copies of the relevant supply arrangements."



"That is not the same thing."



"No."



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



She had known that independence would require money.



She had not fully appreciated how much ordinary household life depended on arrangements that were invisible when someone else maintained them.



The realization was irritating.



It was also useful.



She added a weekly supply review to the household schedule.

At the imperial palace, the final rehearsal began shortly after the morning bell.



The Department of Rites had arranged the ceremonial hall according to the approved schedule.



The attendants stood in their assigned positions.



The ceremonial officials checked the order of entry, the placement of the invited relatives, and the sequence of formal announcements.



The Imperial Household Administration supervised the preparation of the inner palace.



The Department of Personnel confirmed the attendants' assignments.



The Palace Stores had delivered the necessary ceremonial objects.



Everything had a responsible office.



This was precisely what Xu Yuru wanted.



It did not mean everything worked perfectly.



The first rehearsal stopped because one of the ceremonial attendants had been assigned to the wrong side of the hall.



The second stopped because a document carried by the announcing official had the previous day's date.



The third stopped because a junior clerk had placed the reserve ceremonial screens in the position intended for the issued screens.



Yuru observed from the designated place.



She did not intervene.



Fang Ru stood behind her.



When the rehearsal stopped for the third time, the senior ceremonial official approached.



"Your Majesty, the arrangement has been corrected."



Yuru nodded.



"Then continue."



The official bowed.



He seemed to expect another instruction.



None came.



He returned to his position.



The rehearsal resumed.



A few moments later, the Crown Prince arrived.



Zhao Cheng'an bowed to Yuru before taking his place.



"Mother."



"Your Highness."



He glanced at the ceremonial officials.



"Has the rehearsal proceeded smoothly?"



"Not entirely."



"Will it be completed?"



"That is the responsibility of the Department of Rites."



The Crown Prince nodded.



He watched the next sequence carefully.



The ceremonial official announced the order of proceedings. The attendants moved according to the schedule. The officials responsible for the transfer stood in their assigned places.



There was a pause when one attendant moved too early.



The senior official stopped the rehearsal.



He corrected the instruction.



Then he began again.



Yuru watched without speaking.



The Crown Prince looked at her.



"You are not going to correct them?"



"No."



"But you know what went wrong."



"Yes."



"Then why not tell them?"



"Because they have already identified the error."



The Crown Prince looked toward the ceremonial official, who was explaining the correction to the attendants.



Yuru continued.



"If I correct every mistake before the responsible person has an opportunity to identify it, they will learn only to wait for me."



The Crown Prince considered this.



"And if they fail to correct it?"



"Then the responsible office must answer for that failure."



"Even during the ceremony?"



"Especially during the ceremony."



He looked toward the hall again.



The next sequence proceeded correctly.



For the first time, the Crown Prince understood that his mother's refusal to intervene was not indifference.



It was part of the transition itself.

The Second Prince arrived later than his brother.



Zhao Chengyu had been assigned to review the transport arrangements for the ceremony. He had completed the task but had found the work considerably less interesting than he had expected.



He approached the Crown Prince.



"Did the rehearsal begin?"



"Yes."



"How many times has it stopped?"



"Three."



"Only three?"



The Crown Prince looked at him.



"Would you prefer more?"



"No. I was merely surprised."



Chengyu glanced toward the ceremonial officials.



"I checked the transport list. The number of carts is correct."



"Did you compare it with the actual arrivals?"



"Yes."



"Did you compare the actual arrivals with the receiving records?"



Chengyu paused.



"I checked the transport list."



"That was not my question."



The Second Prince frowned.



"Then I will check the receiving records."



The Crown Prince returned his attention to the rehearsal.



Chengyu looked at his brother.



"You have become rather particular."



"I have been particular for some time."



"Mother has made you worse."



The Crown Prince did not answer.



The Second Prince looked back at the transport report.



He had expected to complete a task by checking the list.



Now he had to examine whether the list corresponded to what had actually happened.



He suspected that his mother's departure would make many ordinary activities considerably less convenient.



He was beginning to understand why she had wanted to leave.

In the eastern market district, Mingzhao received a letter from Sun Qiming.



The independent accountant had reviewed the additional statements from the two former drivers and compared them with the transport notebook.



His conclusion was cautious.



The statements supported the existence of the three-cart journey along the southern road.



They did not establish the quantity carried or the final destination.



The separate outer-road journey remained unverified.



The original grain discrepancy therefore remained unchanged.



Mingzhao read the letter twice.



She wrote a short reply.



She asked Sun Qiming to preserve the drivers' statements with the original transport records and to continue examining the old warehouse accounts.



She did not ask him to reach a conclusion before the evidence supported one.



Then she opened the property file.



Master Luo had sent the preliminary boundary measurements.



The drainage channel extended beyond the line shown in the older registration, but the precise area remained disputed.



The old maintenance record did not establish an agreed transfer.



Mingzhao placed the report beside the family settlement papers.



There was no reason to alter her property claims yet.



She would wait for the full assessment.



A servant entered.



"Miss, a messenger from the Marquisate has arrived."



"Who sent him?"



"Young Master Mingyuan."



Mingzhao accepted the letter.



It contained a brief update.



Mingyuan had begun reviewing the regular household expenditure. Steward Zhou had taken over the storehouse records. Mingxiu had corrected the secondary residence report. The Marquis had received the preliminary boundary findings.



At the end, Mingyuan had added a personal sentence.



*I have not yet found the authorization for the twelve-tael repair expense. I will continue looking.*



Mingzhao read it.



Then she placed the letter in the correspondence file.



She did not reply immediately.



There was no need to turn every ordinary update into a conversation about their relationship.



Still, she noted that he had not asked her to identify the expense.



That was progress.

At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, Emperor Zhao Jingheng was reviewing a report from the Department of Revenue.



It concerned a proposed adjustment to the annual allocation for the imperial household.



The report had been prepared after the administrative review initiated by the Empress's resignation.



Several expenses had previously been recorded under broad household categories. The review had separated them according to the offices responsible for authorizing and receiving the goods.



The emperor examined the new arrangement.



"Why has the ceremonial supply expenditure decreased?"



Minister Lu answered.



"Several reserve quantities were previously recorded as though they had been issued. The corrected register now distinguishes between stock held in reserve and goods actually distributed."



"Does that mean the previous expenditure was overstated?"



"It means the previous report did not distinguish the categories clearly enough, Your Majesty. The money was not necessarily spent. The record was insufficiently precise."



The emperor nodded.



"And the stores?"



"The Palace Stores have begun maintaining a separate reconciliation record."



"Who checks it?"



"The responsible administrative officials."



"Who checks their work?"



"The appointed supervisory officials."



"Good."



The emperor turned to the next page.



It concerned the division of responsibilities between the Imperial Household Administration and the Department of Rites.



The two offices had agreed that ceremonial requirements would be specified by the Department of Rites, while the procurement and storage of materials would remain under the relevant administrative offices.



There were still disagreements about exceptional purchases.



The emperor marked the paragraph.



"Return this section. They must define what qualifies as an exceptional purchase."



Minister Lu bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



After the minister left, Zhao Jingheng looked at Gao Shun.



"Has the Empress been informed of this report?"



"The report was sent to the designated offices, Your Majesty."



"Good."



Gao Shun hesitated.



"The Department of Revenue has asked whether Her Majesty would be willing to provide a final comment on the revised allocation."



The emperor looked up.



"Why?"



"They believe she has the greatest familiarity with the previous arrangements."



"She has already provided the records."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then the department should use them."



Gao Shun bowed.



Zhao Jingheng returned to his work.



It was becoming a habit to refuse requests that should not have reached Chengming Palace.



He hoped the departments would eventually develop the same habit.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan discovered that his sister's absence had changed the household more quickly than he expected.



A dispute arose between the kitchen and the stores concerning the quantity of oil issued for the coming week.



The kitchen claimed that the quantity was insufficient.



The stores claimed that the kitchen had requested less than the usual amount.



Mingyuan received both reports.



His first instinct was to send them to Mingzhao.



He stopped.



He read the two entries.



The kitchen's request was based on the expected number of meals. The stores' issue record was based on the previous month's quantity.



The two figures did not correspond because the number of household guests had increased.



Mingyuan called for the kitchen steward.



"How many additional meals are expected this week?"



The steward answered.



"Approximately thirty."



"Why was this not included in the request?"



"We did not know the number when the request was submitted."



"Then the request should have been marked provisional."



The steward looked uncomfortable.



Mingyuan turned to the stores clerk.



"Can the additional quantity be issued from the reserve?"



"Yes, Young Master."



"How much?"



"One jar should be sufficient."



Mingyuan examined the record.



"Then issue one jar and record the reason."



The matter was settled.



It had taken longer than it would have taken Mingzhao.



But it had not required her.



When Steward Zhou heard about the decision, he nodded.



"That was correct, Young Master."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"Was it?"



"Yes."



"Then why did it take me so long?"



Steward Zhou considered his answer.



"Because you were learning the procedure."



Mingyuan looked down at the report.



He had spent years believing that managing a household meant making decisions.



He was beginning to understand that making a decision without knowing how the records connected was merely guessing with authority.



He returned to the account room.

In the palace, the final rehearsal concluded shortly before noon.



The Department of Rites reported that the ceremonial sequence had been completed.



There were still minor adjustments to the placement of the attendants, but no major issue remained.



The senior ceremonial official approached Yuru.



"Your Majesty, the rehearsal has been completed."



"Thank you."



"The final corrections will be entered into the official schedule."



"Good."



The official hesitated.



"Would Your Majesty like to review the corrected copy?"



Yuru looked at him.



"Who is responsible for confirming it?"



"The Department of Rites."



"Then you should confirm it."



The official bowed.



He seemed relieved and embarrassed at the same time.



When he left, the Crown Prince approached his mother.



"The ceremony will proceed."



"That is the intention."



"You seem less concerned than I expected."



Yuru looked at the hall.



"I am concerned."



"But you do not intervene."



"Those are not opposites."



The Crown Prince nodded slowly.



He had once thought that leadership meant being present wherever a decision was made.



Now he was beginning to see that leadership also involved allowing other people to carry the responsibility assigned to them.



"Mother," he said, "after the ceremony, may I still ask for your advice?"



Yuru looked at him.



"Of course."



"Even about household matters?"



"Personal advice is different from administrative responsibility."



"How?"



"If you ask me what I think, I may answer. If you ask me to approve an ordinary expenditure, I will direct you to the responsible office."



The Crown Prince considered this.



"That will be inconvenient."



"Then you will learn to ask better questions."



He smiled faintly.



Yuru returned his smile.

In the eastern market district, Mingzhao had begun the first proper household meeting.



It was not a large meeting.



There were six people in the room: Mingzhao, Qiao Yue, the senior maid, the second attendant, the correspondence worker, and the servant responsible for provisions.



Mingzhao had prepared a list of duties.



Qiao Yue would coordinate the household and personal correspondence.



The senior maid would supervise the rooms and domestic servants.



The second attendant would assist with errands and supplies.



The correspondence worker would maintain letters, copies, and delivery records.



The provision servant would maintain kitchen stock and ordinary purchases.



Mingzhao would retain responsibility for the household budget and major decisions.



She read the arrangements aloud.



Then she looked around the room.



"Does anyone have a question?"



The senior maid raised her hand.



"Miss, who should approve purchases for the kitchen?"



Mingzhao answered.



"Routine purchases within the weekly allowance may be approved by the senior maid. Any purchase outside the allowance should be brought to Qiao Yue."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Why me?"



"Because you coordinate the household."



"And if I disagree?"



"Then bring it to me."



The second attendant raised another question.



"Who should approve repairs?"



"Small repairs within the household allowance may be arranged by the senior maid. Larger repairs require my approval."



The correspondence worker asked, "Who may open letters addressed to the household?"



"Letters addressed personally to me should be brought unopened. Routine household notices may be opened by Qiao Yue."



Qiao Yue looked at the list.



"That seems manageable."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then we will review it after one month."



The senior maid hesitated.



"Miss, may we make suggestions if the arrangement does not work?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Yes."



The maid appeared surprised.



Mingzhao continued.



"If a duty is unclear, tell me. If a procedure is unnecessarily difficult, tell Qiao Yue. If you believe an instruction is wrong, explain why before carrying it out."



Qiao Yue glanced at her.



"That last instruction may produce a great many explanations."



"That is preferable to a great many mistakes."



The meeting ended.



It was the first time Mingzhao had formally established a household in which the people working for her were invited to identify problems in the arrangements.



She was not entirely comfortable with it.



She was also aware that a household of six people could not be managed in the same way as a large estate with dozens of servants and several administrative offices.



She had no intention of recreating the Marquisate on a smaller scale.



At least, not deliberately.

After the meeting, Qiao Yue brought her a letter from Lin Shuyun.



The silk partnership's remaining stock was progressing.



Forty-two bolts had been prepared for inspection. Two had been returned for correction. The next group would be presented to Zhou Wenqi after the finishing work was complete.



The partnership account remained separate from Mingzhao's household expenses.



Lin Shuyun had also written a short personal note.



*I heard you have moved. The eastern market is convenient, though the streets become crowded before the evening shops close. Do not mistake a convenient address for a quiet one.*



Mingzhao read the note.



She looked out through the window.



A merchant was arguing with a cart driver in the street below.



A shopkeeper was calling to a customer.



Someone was moving a stack of wooden boxes.



The street was not quiet.



She wrote a reply.



*The address is temporary. I have not mistaken it for a permanent residence.*



Qiao Yue read the line over her shoulder.



"That sounds like something you would say to a property assessor."



"It is accurate."



"Could you write something less formal?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"What would you suggest?"



"Perhaps: Thank you for the warning. I have already discovered that the street is noisy."



Mingzhao considered it.



Then she added the sentence.



It was not a profound change.



It was, however, the first letter she had written from her new household that was not concerned with an account, a contract, or a legal claim.

At Chengming Palace, the Empress Dowager Wei requested a private meeting with Yuru.



The older woman had received the final ceremonial arrangements.



She had also received the list of invited relatives.



"Your Xu clan elders will attend," she said.



"Yes."



"Your father would have wanted that."



Yuru looked at the invitation list.



"Perhaps."



The Empress Dowager set down the paper.



"Have you considered where you will live after the ceremony?"



"I have considered it."



"And?"



"I have not made a final decision."



The older woman looked surprised.



"You have not arranged a residence?"



"I have several possibilities."



"That is not the same as a decision."



"No."



The Empress Dowager examined her.



"You have spent years arranging every detail for other people. Now you are leaving the palace without deciding where you will go."



Yuru smiled faintly.



"I am not leaving tomorrow."



"You have three days."



"Three days are sufficient to decide whether a room has a good window."



The Empress Dowager looked at her.



"Only a room?"



"At first."



The older woman shook her head.



"You are determined to make everything difficult."



"I am determined not to make a decision merely because a date is approaching."



The Empress Dowager considered this.



Then she said, "You may stay in the palace guest residence until you have decided."



Yuru looked at her.



"Thank you."



"It is not an invitation to resume your duties."



"I understand."



"And if the emperor sends you documents?"



"I will return them to the responsible offices."



The Empress Dowager gave a short laugh.



"At least you have learned to say no."



Yuru's expression softened.



"I learned it rather late."



The older woman did not answer.



But when she rose to leave, she placed one hand briefly over Yuru's.



It was a small gesture.



Neither woman mentioned it.

That evening, Emperor Zhao Jingheng reviewed the final ceremonial arrangements.



The report stated that the rehearsal had been completed, the attendants' assignments confirmed, and the required materials accounted for.



He signed the final approval.



Then he looked toward Gao Shun.



"Three days."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Has the Empress requested anything?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Nothing?"



"She requested that the guest residence be prepared for temporary use after the ceremony."



The emperor looked at him.



"Temporary?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"She has not chosen a permanent residence?"



"Not yet."



Zhao Jingheng put down his brush.



"She has spent years arranging the residences of everyone else."



Gao Shun remained silent.



The emperor looked at the signed schedule.



"Then perhaps she should have the time to arrange her own."



He returned to the report.



Three days remained.



The palace was preparing to mark the end of Yuru's tenure.



In the eastern market, Mingzhao was preparing to close her first independent household ledger for the day.



The missing cooking pot had been found.



The household duties had been assigned.



The rent had been recorded.



The servants had received their first full day of instructions.



And the family accounts remained unresolved.



Mingzhao closed her ledger.



"Tomorrow," she said, "we will review the kitchen expenditure."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"Can we have one day without reviewing something?"



Mingzhao considered the request.



Then she said, "We can review it the day after tomorrow."



Qiao Yue smiled.



"An extraordinary concession."



Mingzhao extinguished the lamp.



Across the capital, the palace was also preparing for another day of work.



The difference was that its departments now knew which reports belonged to them.



Whether they would remember that without being reminded remained to be seen.
