# **Chapter 69: The Former Empress Has Gone for a Walk Without an Escort of Officials. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Gone Through Another Account Without Finding a Convenient Answer. The Workshop Has Discovered That Two Experienced People Can Disagree About a Folding Method for an Entire Afternoon.**

**3rd Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The morning was clear.



Xu Yuru had asked to walk along the path outside the western guest residence, and Fang Ru had arranged for two attendants to accompany them at a distance.



There was no formal procession.



There was no carriage.



There was no official waiting with a list of matters requiring her attention.



Yuru wore a simple pale robe and comfortable shoes. Fang Ru carried a small pouch containing a handkerchief, a little money, and a folded note with the guest residence's address.



The latter was unnecessary for someone who had lived in the palace for years.



Fang Ru had included it anyway.



Yuru noticed.



“Do you think I will become lost?”



“No, Madam.”



“Then why bring the address?”



“In case someone else becomes confused.”



Yuru glanced at her.



“That is a reasonable explanation.”



They walked along the outer path.



The western residential section was quieter than the main palace grounds. Servants carried baskets between buildings, a pair of junior officials crossed the far end of the walkway, and a gardener was trimming a hedge that had grown unevenly.



Yuru stopped to look at the hedge.



The gardener noticed her and immediately put down his shears.



“Madam.”



“Continue.”



He bowed, but did not resume cutting.



Yuru looked at the uneven section.



“Why have you stopped?”



The gardener hesitated.



“I did not wish to disturb you.”



“You are not disturbing me.”



He picked up the shears.



Yuru stepped aside.



The gardener resumed his work.



Fang Ru watched the exchange.



“You did not ask him to change the hedge.”



“Was there a reason to?”



“It is uneven.”



“It is being trimmed.”



Fang Ru smiled.



They continued walking.



A few minutes later, they passed a small storage building. Two attendants were carrying a wooden chest inside.



One complained that the chest was heavier than expected.



The other replied that the inventory listed it as empty.



Yuru heard the exchange.



She did not stop.



Fang Ru looked at her.



“Madam?”



“They will discover whether the inventory is correct.”



“Should we ask?”



“No.”



The attendants eventually set the chest down and opened it.



A bundle of folded cloth lay inside.



One of them looked annoyed.



The other looked triumphant.



Yuru continued walking.



The former Empress had spent many years being the person summoned whenever two attendants disagreed about a chest.



She found that she preferred not to know what happened next.

At the Crown Prince's residence, Zhao Cheng'an was learning that a new accounting format could produce an entirely new kind of argument.



The Eastern Palace clerks had submitted the monthly stock summary.



The Crown Prince examined the first page.



“Why are these two entries combined?”



The senior clerk leaned forward.



“They concern the same type of oil, Your Highness.”



“Do they have the same recorded capacity?”



“No.”



“Then why are they combined?”



“The previous register treated them as one category.”



Cheng'an looked at the figures.



“The previous register also left the capacity of several containers blank.”



The clerk lowered his head.



“Yes.”



“Separate them.”



The clerk hesitated.



“Would that not make the total less convenient?”



“It will make the total more accurate.”



The clerk bowed.



Cheng'an turned another page.



The same problem appeared in the issue records.



Some entries recorded jars.



Others recorded weight.



A few recorded only the number of containers without identifying their capacity.



He put down the paper.



“Do not convert a jar into a weight unless the capacity is recorded.”



“Yes, Your Highness.”



“And if the original record is incomplete?”



“Retain the original description and mark the quantity unresolved.”



Cheng'an nodded.



The clerk gathered the papers.



The Crown Prince had begun to understand that his responsibilities would not become easier simply because he had learned the correct principles.



The records still had to be examined.



The clerks still needed supervision.



And the palace still consumed supplies whether or not the accounts were ready.



He looked at the next file.



“Bring the purchase records as well.”



The clerk bowed.



Cheng'an had hoped to finish before lunch.



He was beginning to doubt that possibility.

The Second Prince Zhao Chengyu had a different problem.



He had received an invitation to a small gathering at a relative's residence.



The invitation listed the guests.



The accompanying estimate listed food, tea, attendants, transport, and several additional provisions.



Chengyu read the estimate.



“Why are there thirty-six cups?”



The household clerk answered.



“Some guests may bring attendants, Your Highness.”



“How many?”



“The invitation does not specify.”



“Then why are there thirty-six cups?”



The clerk hesitated.



“To ensure there are enough.”



Chengyu looked at the paper.



“Are we hosting thirty-six people?”



“Not necessarily.”



“Then we are buying cups for people who may not come?”



“They can be used later.”



Chengyu stared at him.



The clerk became very still.



The Second Prince remembered the previous gathering, when an inflated estimate had been revised after the actual number of guests and attendants was checked.



He tapped the paper.



“Separate the confirmed number from the possible number.”



“Yes, Your Highness.”



“And do not purchase the additional provisions until the attendance is confirmed.”



The clerk bowed.



Chengyu leaned back.



He had once considered this kind of scrutiny unnecessary.



Now he found that it prevented him from having to explain why an ordinary gathering required enough cups for half a court audience.



It was an improvement.



He still disliked the smaller estimate.



But he disliked being questioned about wasteful expenditure even more.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Mingyuan had reached the end of the fifth principal cash book.



He had also reached the limit of his patience with faded ink.



Steward Zhou placed a supporting bundle beside him.



“This belongs to the fifth book.”



Mingyuan untied the string.



Inside were several receipts, a delivery acknowledgment, and two sheets with signatures.



He examined the first.



“Thirty-two taels, household transport.”



“Yes.”



“The receipt identifies the cart contractor.”



“Yes.”



“Does it identify the destination?”



“No.”



Mingyuan looked at the receipt again.



“Then we know who was paid, but not what was transported.”



“That is correct.”



He put the receipt aside.



The next sheet concerned a purchase of household furnishings.



Mingyuan recognized the entry.



It was the same expenditure that had been mistakenly included in Mingzhao's personal advances before the schedule was corrected.



He read the supporting record.



The payment had been made from the estate furnishing account.



The goods had been delivered to the Marquisate.



Mingzhao's name appeared as the person supervising the purchase.



Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou.



“This is clearly an estate expenditure.”



“Yes.”



“Then why was it ever placed under her advances?”



“The earlier schedule used the authorization column as a reference for personal claims.”



“That was wrong.”



“It was.”



Mingyuan examined the original entry.



“Have we corrected the master schedule?”



“Yes. The furnishing payment has been removed from the personal advances and restored to estate expenditure.”



“Good.”



He placed the sheet in the completed group.



The next entry was less clear.



A payment had been made to a household servant, but the description was abbreviated.



There was no attached receipt.



Mingyuan frowned.



“Can we identify this person?”



Steward Zhou checked the name.



“He was employed here at the time. He may still live in the capital.”



“Find out.”



“Yes, Young Master.”



Mingyuan looked at the growing pile of unresolved entries.



“How many are left?”



“Two books, together with their supporting papers.”



Mingyuan nodded.



He had expected to finish the review in a few days.



He was now beginning to understand why the old steward had never promised a completion date.



The records were not arranged to answer the questions being asked of them now.



They had been written to serve the immediate needs of a household.



Some of those needs had long since disappeared.



The gaps had remained.

Mingzhao received a message from Sun Qiming in the afternoon.



The accountant had reviewed another group of entries.



One payment had been confirmed as an estate expense.



Another had been recorded as a payment to a former household assistant, but the supporting receipt was missing.



A third entry could not yet be classified.



No repayment to Mingzhao had been established.



She read the report and set it beside the earlier ones.



Qiao Yue was sorting correspondence.



“Another report?”



“Yes.”



“Another entry that is not a repayment?”



“Yes.”



Qiao Yue looked at the growing stack.



“Will the family ever finish?”



“They will finish when they have examined the records.”



“Could you not simply tell them which payments were yours?”



“I have already identified the advances I can establish from my own accounts.”



“And the rest?”



“The rest must be checked.”



Qiao Yue put down the letter she was holding.



“You seem very determined to let the records decide.”



Mingzhao looked at her.



“The records are not deciding. They are evidence.”



Qiao Yue thought about that.



“Then what decides?”



“The agreement, the law, and the evidence.”



“That sounds like three things that will take a long time.”



“It may.”



Mingzhao opened her personal account book.



She had kept it separate from the Marquisate's books for years. The entries were not perfect, but they were consistent enough to establish what she had personally paid and what had been returned to her.



She checked the dates again.



The nine confirmed advances remained supported.



The four payment-confirmed advances still lacked clear repayment arrangements.



The ten uncertain entries remained uncertain.



She did not add them together.



She did not alter the classifications.



She wrote a note for Sun.



*Continue to distinguish the original payment from any later repayment. An estate expenditure authorized by me must not be treated as a personal receipt. If a payment was made to an intermediary, establish whether it reached me.*



She signed it.



Qiao Yue sealed the letter.



“Would you like to rest before reviewing the workshop report?”



“No. Bring it.”



Qiao Yue shook her head.



“You have two sets of accounts, a family settlement, and a silk order.”



“And you have a talent for listing things I already know.”



“I am trying to remind you that you are not required to do everything yourself.”



Mingzhao looked at her.



For a moment, her expression was unreadable.



Then she said, “I am reviewing the reports. I am not personally folding the silk.”



“That is true.”



“And Sun is reviewing the accounts.”



“That is also true.”



“And Lin is managing the workshop.”



Qiao Yue smiled.



“Then perhaps you are learning.”



Mingzhao opened the workshop report.



“Do not become too confident.”

At the workshop, Lin Shuyun had already spoken to the senior finisher.



The conversation had not gone smoothly.



The worker believed that his experience entitled him to greater discretion in deciding how the silk should be folded.



Lin believed that experience was valuable but did not override the buyer's specifications.



The two had disagreed for nearly half an hour.



When Mingzhao arrived, Lin was standing beside the inspection table.



The senior finisher was examining a sample.



“Manager Shen,” he said.



Mingzhao nodded.



Lin explained the disagreement.



“The worker believes the inspection process is too frequent. He thinks the experienced finishers should be allowed to use their judgment.”



Mingzhao looked at the sample.



“What does the contract say?”



Lin brought out the written specification.



The buyer had approved the revised folding method on the condition that the measurements and appearance remained consistent across the order.



The specification included the fold width, edge tension, and final appearance.



Mingzhao placed the paper beside the sample.



“Can you meet these requirements without checking each group?”



The senior finisher answered confidently.



“Yes.”



“Can the other workers?”



He paused.



“Some can.”



“Can the trainees?”



“They are still learning.”



“Then the inspection cannot be removed.”



The worker frowned.



“I am not asking for the trainees to be left alone. I am asking that experienced workers not be treated as beginners.”



Mingzhao considered this.



It was a fair complaint.



The current procedure required the same written checks for every group, even when the workers had already demonstrated consistent results.



But removing the checks entirely would create a different problem.



She turned to Lin.



“Can we separate the production record by worker?”



Lin thought for a moment.



“Yes.”



“Then record the first group from each worker under the full inspection procedure. If an experienced worker produces three consecutive groups that meet the specification without correction, the supervisor may use a reduced check for the next group. The full inspection resumes if a discrepancy appears.”



The senior finisher looked at her.



“And the trainees?”



“They remain under full inspection until they demonstrate consistent results.”



He considered the proposal.



“That is reasonable.”



Mingzhao nodded.



“It is also subject to the buyer's specification. We cannot change the finished product.”



“I understand.”



Lin looked relieved.



The procedure was more flexible without abandoning the standard.



It also gave the experienced workers a reason to maintain consistent quality.



Mingzhao asked the senior finisher to produce a trial group under the revised inspection arrangement.



The group would be recorded separately.



If the result was satisfactory, the workshop could use the reduced check for qualified workers.



If not, the full inspection would remain.



The worker agreed.



After he left, Lin looked at Mingzhao.



“You could have proposed that yesterday.”



“I had not considered the distinction between the workers' records.”



“You were too busy proving that he was wrong.”



Mingzhao gave her a cool look.



“He was partly wrong.”



“And partly right.”



“Yes.”



Lin smiled.



Mingzhao returned to the production table.



She did not enjoy being corrected.



But she disliked making a poor decision more.

The workshop's next group was ready by late afternoon.



The senior finisher's trial group passed the full inspection.



Three consecutive groups from his team had now met the recorded specifications without correction.



Lin entered the result in the production register.



The reduced check could begin with the next group, provided the supervisor continued to record the results and restored full inspection if a discrepancy appeared.



The trainees remained under full inspection.



One of them had improved noticeably.



The other still tended to tighten the fold too much when working quickly.



Mingzhao watched him complete a bolt.



“Slow down.”



The trainee obeyed.



He finished the fold.



The supervisor examined it.



The edge was even.



Mingzhao nodded.



“Better.”



The trainee looked relieved.



“Thank you, Manager Shen.”



“Do not thank me. Repeat it.”



He lowered his head and returned to work.



Lin watched him.



“He is trying hard.”



“He is.”



“He may become a good finisher.”



“If he continues improving.”



Lin smiled.



“You never praise anyone without adding a condition.”



“Conditions are useful.”



“Not always.”



Mingzhao looked toward the workshop floor.



The order was progressing.



The first forty-eight bolts had been accepted by the buyer.



The next forty-eight had passed internal inspection, but still awaited buyer inspection.



One hundred and four remained to be produced.



The revised method was saving time for experienced workers.



The trainees were improving.



The wages were being paid.



The costs were still being calculated.



It was a promising position.



It was not yet a profitable one.

At the Imperial Palace, Empress Dowager Wei received a brief report about the former Empress's morning walk.



The report had been included with the guest residence's ordinary household summary.



It stated that Xu Yuru had walked along the western path and returned without incident.



The Dowager read it.



Then she set it aside.



A senior attendant stood nearby.



“Would Your Majesty like to send a message?”



The Dowager looked at her.



“What would I say?”



“That the former Empress may visit whenever she wishes.”



“She already knows.”



The attendant bowed.



The Dowager picked up her tea.



“She has only just begun to enjoy having no schedule imposed on her. Do not send another invitation so soon.”



“Yes, Your Majesty.”



The Dowager looked toward the window.



She had once believed that the former Empress's ability to manage the inner palace was a reason she should continue doing so.



It was an understandable belief.



It was also a convenient one.



The palace had relied on Yuru because Yuru had been capable.



The Dowager had not always considered whether that reliance was fair.



She did not intend to interfere with the former Empress's new arrangements merely because she missed having someone who could solve a problem quickly.



The tea cup rested on its saucer.



“Has the Crown Prince's monthly report been submitted?”



“Yes, Your Majesty.”



“Has he reviewed it?”



“He is still examining the unresolved entries.”



The Dowager nodded.



“Then let him continue.”



The attendant bowed.



The Dowager had no desire to receive an account that looked complete only because someone had hidden its uncertainties.

That evening, Yuru returned from another short walk along the western path.



Fang Ru had accompanied her.



The gardener was still trimming the hedge.



The storage attendants had moved the wooden chest inside.



No one had asked Yuru to inspect either matter.



She had not asked what happened to the cloth.



Fang Ru poured tea.



“Madam, the weather should remain clear tomorrow.”



“Then we may walk again.”



“Would you like to go farther?”



Yuru considered the question.



“Perhaps next time.”



Fang Ru nodded.



There was no need to decide the route in advance.



Yuru sat beside the window.



She thought about the Third Princess's catalogue, the Dowager's message, and the palace clerks who were learning to distinguish an incomplete record from a completed one.



She thought about her parents' letter.



She thought about the fact that she had spent the morning walking without being summoned.



It was a modest day.



But it had belonged to her.

In the Marquisate, Mingyuan finished the fifth cash book before dinner.



He had not found a repayment to Mingzhao.



He had not found evidence that none had ever been made.



He had identified several estate expenditures, one payment to a former assistant steward, and two entries requiring additional supporting documents.



He closed the book.



Steward Zhou gathered the papers.



“Shall we continue tomorrow?”



“Yes.”



“Would you like the sixth book prepared?”



Mingyuan looked at the remaining bundles.



“Prepare it.”



Steward Zhou bowed.



Mingyuan stood and stretched his shoulders.



He had spent most of the day reading other people's handwriting.



It was not the work he had imagined when he first began learning estate management.



He had expected to inspect fields, approve repairs, meet tenants, and oversee household arrangements.



He was discovering that the estate's past had to be understood before its future could be managed properly.



He looked at the closed cash book.



His elder sister had handled these records for six years.



He had rarely asked how much work was involved.



Now he was beginning to understand.



He did not yet know what he would say to her when the settlement was complete.



For once, he decided not to prepare an explanation before he had finished the work.



There was enough to do tomorrow.



And the sixth month was still young.
