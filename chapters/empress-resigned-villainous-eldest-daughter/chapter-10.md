# **Chapter 10: The Palace Has Created a New Register for Exceptional Expenses. The Marquisate Has Created a New List of People Who Claim They Know Nothing.**

**2nd Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The first difficulty with introducing a new register was that everyone wanted to know who would maintain it.

The second difficulty was that nobody wanted to maintain it.

The third difficulty was that the people who had volunteered to explain why they should not maintain it had already spent half the morning arguing about its columns.

At the imperial palace, the new register had become a matter of considerable importance.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the new list of people who knew nothing had become considerably longer.

Neither development was encouraging.

The Department of Palace Stores had prepared a sample register.

It contained columns for:

- Date of request.
- Name of requesting office.
- Description of goods or service.
- Quantity.
- Estimated cost.
- Reason for purchase.
- Authorizing officer.
- Receiving officer.
- Verification of payment.
- Final record location.

The register was placed before the emperor.

He examined it.

Then he looked at the official who had submitted it.

"Where is the column for whether the goods were actually received?"

The official pointed to the receiving officer.

"That column is intended to cover it, Your Majesty."

"Then why does it say receiving officer rather than quantity received?"

The official lowered his head.

"Your Majesty is correct."

"Where is the column for damaged goods?"

"That would be recorded in the description."

"Would it?"

The official hesitated.

The emperor turned to Wei Zhong.

"How many people have examined this register?"

"Four offices, Your Majesty."

"And nobody noticed?"

Wei Zhong did not answer.

The emperor returned the register.

"Add the quantity received, the condition of the goods, and any difference between the amount ordered and the amount delivered."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Do not make the register so complicated that an ordinary purchase requires a scholar to complete it."

The official bowed.

The emperor looked at Minister Lu.

"Who is responsible for checking the register?"

"The Department of Palace Stores will maintain the original record. The financial office will verify the payment, and the review office will conduct periodic inspections."

"Who checks whether the receiving officer has entered the correct quantity?"

"The stores supervisor."

"And who checks the supervisor?"

Minister Lu paused.

"An assigned reviewing clerk."

"Who appoints the reviewing clerk?"

Wei Zhong answered.

"The head of the review office, under the existing appointment procedure."

The emperor nodded.

"Then write that down."

Wei Zhong made a note.

The emperor looked again at the register.

"One more thing. This procedure applies to exceptional expenditure. Do not use it to delay ordinary supplies."

The stores official bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The emperor added, "And do not create a separate register for every kind of broom."

The official's face remained expressionless.

Wei Zhong lowered his eyes.

Minister Lu looked at the ceiling.

In Chengming Palace, Yuru was reviewing the proposed register with Fang Ru.

She read the columns carefully.

"This is better."

Fang Ru looked relieved.

"The emperor has approved the general structure."

"Has the receiving office tested it?"

"Not yet."

"Then it has not been tested."

Fang Ru made a note.

Yuru turned to the next page.

"Where is the procedure for urgent repairs?"

"It has not yet been included."

"Why?"

"The Department of Palace Stores believes urgent requests should use the same register."

Yuru looked up.

"Suppose a water pipe breaks in a residence and floods a storeroom. Should the administrator wait for the financial office to complete the register before ordering repairs?"

"No, Your Majesty."

"Then the procedure must allow an emergency request to be recorded first and verified afterward."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Should there be a limit?"

"Yes. An emergency procedure must not become an excuse for ordinary expenditure. Define what qualifies as an emergency, who can authorize it, and when the report must be submitted."

Fang Ru wrote it down.

Yuru paused.

"And include the person who reports the emergency. If an administrator exaggerates a problem to obtain funds, that should also be traceable."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

She leaned back.

"Do not make every problem a reason to create another office."

Fang Ru looked at the register.

"At this rate, Your Majesty, the palace may need a separate office to manage the registers."

Yuru looked at her.

"Do not give them ideas."

Fang Ru smiled.

The empress returned to her work.

A moment later, another attendant entered.

"Your Majesty, the Third Princess requests an audience."

"Send her in."

Zhao Minglan entered carrying a small wooden box.

She bowed.

"Mother."

"What have you brought?"

"The account records from the princess's residence."

Yuru looked at the box.

"Why?"

"I want to examine them."

"Why?"

Minglan hesitated.

"Because I want to understand how the household works."

"That is a reasonable reason. What have you already examined?"

"The monthly expenditure records and the clothing inventory."

"And?"

"The clothing inventory lists twelve new winter garments, but the storage record lists fourteen."

Yuru looked at her.

"Have you checked the previous year's inventory?"

"No."

"Have you checked whether two garments were carried forward from the previous year?"

"Not yet."

"Then you do not yet have a discrepancy."

Minglan nodded.

"I know. I wanted to ask how to proceed."

Yuru gestured to the chair.

"Sit."

The princess sat and opened the box.

Yuru examined the records.

"First, establish the opening inventory. Then list purchases, transfers, repairs, and disposals. You cannot compare a purchase record directly with a storage record unless you know what was already in storage."

Minglan listened carefully.

"What if the previous year's record is incomplete?"

"Then record that limitation. Do not invent an opening quantity to make the arithmetic work."

Minglan wrote it down.

After a moment, she asked, "Mother, if a record is incomplete, does that mean someone has done something wrong?"

"No."

"Then why do people become frightened when we ask for records?"

Yuru looked at her daughter.

"Because a missing record may reveal an honest mistake, careless work, or deliberate wrongdoing. People do not always know which one it will be."

Minglan thought about that.

"And sometimes they are afraid because someone will blame them even if they did nothing."

"That happens too."

The princess looked down at the account book.

"Then how do we make them willing to tell us?"

Yuru was quiet for a moment.

"By making it clear that the purpose of an examination is to establish what happened. Not to find someone to punish before we know what happened."

Minglan nodded.

Yuru touched the edge of the account book.

"Remember this. A good record does not guarantee that a person is honest. But a poor record makes it much harder to establish the truth."

Minglan closed the book.

"I understand."

"Do you?"

The princess smiled.

"I am beginning to."

Yuru smiled back.

"That seems to be the answer of the day."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Sun Qiming had reached the northern warehouse.

The former steward, Zhang, was a narrow-faced man in his late fifties. He had retired several years earlier and appeared displeased to have been summoned to discuss records from his former employment.

He sat opposite Sun Qiming in the small receiving room.

Steward Zhou was present.

Mingzhao had chosen not to attend the first part of the conversation.

She wanted the accountant to ask his questions without her presence influencing the answers.

Sun Qiming opened his notebook.

"Mr. Zhang, you managed the northern warehouse during the ninth and tenth months of last year?"

"Yes."

"Do you remember the grain deliveries?"

"Not every delivery."

"Do you remember the delivery of eighteen sacks in the ninth month?"

Zhang frowned.

"Possibly."

"Do you remember fourteen sacks in the tenth month?"

"That was during the repair period."

"Which repair period?"

"The eastern storehouse roof had been damaged. Some grain was moved temporarily."

Sun Qiming looked up.

"Moved from the northern warehouse?"

"Some grain was moved into the northern warehouse."

Steward Zhou leaned forward.

"Do you mean the northern warehouse received grain from another storehouse?"

"Yes."

"Was it recorded as a new delivery?"

Zhang hesitated.

"I cannot say without seeing the register."

Sun Qiming took out the copies.

"These entries are from the receiving estate. They show eighteen sacks in the ninth month and fourteen in the tenth."

Zhang examined them.

"These are receiving records."

"Do you recognize the clerk's seal?"

"I believe so."

"Can you confirm it?"

"Not from this copy."

Sun Qiming placed another paper before him.

"This is the dispatch record showing forty-seven sacks leaving the main granary."

Zhang read it.

Then he frowned.

"The dates are not the same."

"Correct."

"These are not necessarily the same deliveries."

Sun Qiming made a note.

"Can you explain the difference?"

"Not without the old movement register."

"Where is it?"

Zhang looked toward Steward Zhou.

"It should have been kept in the warehouse office."

"Was it transferred when you retired?"

"I handed over the books that were in my custody."

"Do you have a personal copy?"

Zhang hesitated.

"I may have a few old notes."

Sun Qiming looked at him.

"Would you be willing to provide them for examination?"

"Provided they are returned."

"Of course."

Zhang rubbed his forehead.

"I remember that grain was moved during the roof repairs. The main storehouse was not safe. Some sacks were transferred to the northern warehouse temporarily."

"How many?"

"I cannot remember."

"Was the transfer recorded?"

"It should have been."

"Where?"

"In the movement register."

Sun Qiming wrote another note.

The conversation continued for nearly an hour.

By the end of it, they had established no final quantity.

But they had discovered something important.

The forty-seven sacks might not all have been ordinary deliveries.

Some grain could have been transferred temporarily between warehouses, with the same goods appearing in one register as a dispatch and another as a receipt.

The discrepancy might therefore involve double-counting, delayed recording, or incomplete transfer records.

It might also involve an actual shortage.

The evidence did not yet distinguish between those possibilities.

Sun Qiming closed his notebook.

"Mr. Zhang, I would like to examine your old notes."

"I will look for them."

"Please do."

Zhang stood.

"Will I be blamed if the records are incomplete?"

Sun Qiming looked at him.

"I am not here to assign blame. I am here to establish what the records show."

Zhang gave him a doubtful look.

"That is what everyone says."

Sun Qiming did not argue.

He had no reason to promise that no consequences would follow.

He could only promise that the examination would not invent them.

Mingzhao was waiting in the side study when Sun Qiming returned.

"Well?" she asked.

"The discrepancy may involve temporary grain transfers during warehouse repairs."

"May?"

"Yes."

"Do we have the movement register?"

"Not yet. The former steward believes it existed."

"Believes?"

"He has offered to search his personal notes."

Mingzhao looked at the report.

"Then we wait for the notes and obtain the original register."

Sun Qiming nodded.

"There is another matter. If grain was moved temporarily, we must distinguish the movement from a new receipt. Otherwise, the same sacks may appear twice in the combined accounts."

Mingzhao considered this.

"That would explain why the figures do not reconcile."

"It would explain part of the discrepancy."

"Not all forty-seven?"

"Not yet."

She nodded.

"Continue."

Sun Qiming then handed her another document.

"This concerns the three hundred taels."

Mingzhao read it.

"Your brother has found the repair ledger?"

"He has provided a copy of an entry concerning work in his study."

"How much?"

"Seventy-two taels."

"Does it identify the carpenter?"

"Yes. A man named Chen, employed by the estate's repair contractor."

"Does the entry establish that the payment came from the three hundred taels?"

"No. It establishes that the repair work was recorded in the estate account."

Mingzhao looked at the document again.

"Then this portion should not be treated as an unexplained personal expense."

"That is my view."

"Does the payment appear in the original account?"

"Yes."

"Then record it as verified estate maintenance, subject to confirming the work order and payment receipt."

Sun Qiming nodded.

Mingzhao put down the paper.

"How much of the three hundred remains unexplained?"

"Two hundred and twenty-eight taels."

"Then that is the figure requiring further examination. Do not keep referring to the original three hundred as though none of it has been explained."

"Agreed."

Sun Qiming collected his papers.

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Thank you."

He bowed.

"I will continue."

When he had left, Qiao Yue entered.

"Miss, Second Young Master has sent another note."

Mingzhao looked at it.

It was a short message.

Shen Mingyuan had found the repair ledger and was willing to provide the original.

Mingzhao placed it beside Sun Qiming's report.

"At least he has begun looking."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"That is good."

"It is useful."

"Must everything be useful?"

"At present, yes."

Shen Mingyuan was not pleased with himself.

He had spent most of the morning searching through old records, and the discovery of the seventy-two-tael repair entry had been more difficult than it should have been.

The ledger had been kept in a storage cabinet with several old household inventories.

He had found it because his attendant remembered that the former steward had placed repair accounts in the same cabinet as the winter maintenance records.

Mingyuan had initially been irritated.

Then he had become embarrassed.

The work had been carried out in his rooms.

He had remembered the repairs.

He had simply never considered where the money had come from.

Now he was beginning to understand why Mingzhao had insisted on examining the records.

A payment could be real without being personal.

A benefit could be received without being the person who authorized the expense.

And a missing receipt did not automatically mean that someone had stolen money.

He looked at the remaining entries.

Two payments were still unclear.

One was for furniture.

The other was for a gathering.

He could remember the furniture.

He could not remember the exact payment.

He remembered the gathering clearly.

He had invited several friends.

He had assumed the expenses were covered by the household allocation.

That assumption now appeared less comfortable.

His attendant entered.

"Young Master, the carpenter has arrived."

Mingyuan looked up.

"Which carpenter?"

"The man who repaired your study."

"Bring him in."

The carpenter entered, bowed, and stood by the door.

Mingyuan showed him the ledger entry.

"Do you remember this work?"

The carpenter examined the page.

"Yes, Young Master."

"Who paid you?"

"The estate office."

"Did you receive seventy-two taels?"

"Yes."

"Was that the full amount?"

The carpenter thought.

"Yes. The work was completed in two stages. The first payment was made after the timber was delivered. The second after the repair was finished."

"Do you have the receipts?"

"I believe the estate office kept the original."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Did I authorize the work?"

The carpenter hesitated.

"The steward gave the order. I was told the work had been approved."

Mingyuan leaned back.

"By whom?"

"I do not know, Young Master."

The answer was disappointing.

It was also honest.

Mingyuan dismissed him and sat alone.

The repair had happened.

The payment had happened.

The records established that the estate had paid.

But the authorization remained unclear.

He took out a sheet of paper and began writing what he remembered.

For the first time, he did not begin with the sentence that the money had been used for necessary household matters.

He wrote down what he knew.

Then he wrote down what he did not know.

It was an unpleasant exercise.

He found it more difficult than expected.

In the imperial palace, the broom investigation had reached a similar stage.

The repair office had produced a separate record showing that forty-four brooms had been issued to maintenance crews.

The records identified three repair teams.

One had worked in the eastern corridors.

Another had repaired the southern kitchens.

The third had worked on drainage channels near the servants' residences.

Twenty-seven brooms had been recorded as worn out.

Seventeen had been issued to the teams.

The dates matched several maintenance orders.

The records were not complete, but the explanation was increasingly plausible.

Minister Lu submitted the revised report to the emperor.

The emperor read it.

"Have the repair teams confirmed receipt?"

"Two have. The third team has been dissolved, and its members have been reassigned."

"Have the twenty-seven discarded brooms been inspected or recorded individually?"

"No, Your Majesty."

"Then we cannot verify the disposal."

"No."

The emperor looked at the report.

"Are the seventeen issued brooms still in use?"

"Some may be."

"Some?"

"The repair teams have not been able to locate all of them."

The emperor put down the report.

"Then do not write that the matter is resolved."

Minister Lu bowed.

"We have classified the original discrepancy as likely resulting from separate inventory categories."

"Likely is acceptable. Resolved is not."

Wei Zhong opened his notes.

"Your Majesty, the revised register will require the repair office to record materials received, issued, returned, and discarded."

"Good."

"Should the previous records be treated as a procedural failure?"

The emperor considered this.

"Yes, but not necessarily as misconduct. The office failed to reconcile its records. That is established. Whether anyone deliberately concealed goods is not."

Minister Lu nodded.

The emperor looked at him.

"Do not punish people for a conclusion we have not reached."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"And do not allow the matter to disappear simply because we have found a plausible explanation."

"Understood."

The emperor leaned back.

"At last, the brooms have taught us something."

Wei Zhong kept his face still.

Minister Lu remained silent.

Neither wished to encourage the emperor.

That afternoon, Yuru met with the Crown Prince and Third Princess in the gardens.

Minglan had brought her account book again.

The Crown Prince carried a separate folder.

Yuru looked at both.

"Have you come to compare your findings?"

Minglan nodded.

"I have found that two garments were carried forward from the previous winter."

"And?"

"The purchase register counted twelve new garments. The storage register counted fourteen garments in total."

"Then?"

"There is no discrepancy."

Yuru smiled.

"Good."

Minglan looked pleased.

The Crown Prince opened his folder.

"I have identified a similar problem in the lamp oil records. Some oil purchased in the previous month remained in storage, but the monthly expenditure report treated the entire purchase as consumed."

Yuru looked at him.

"Have you established the remaining quantity?"

"Not yet. The storekeeper has agreed to conduct an inventory."

"Good."

The Crown Prince hesitated.

"Mother, the review office asked whether the Eastern Palace should report all household expenditure monthly."

"Why?"

"They want to establish a consistent procedure."

"Would that be useful?"

"Possibly. But some expenditure is already recorded daily. A monthly summary may duplicate the work."

Yuru nodded.

"Then ask them what problem the monthly summary is intended to solve."

The Crown Prince looked thoughtful.

"If the daily records are complete, a monthly summary should be a consolidation rather than a second record."

"Exactly."

Minglan looked between them.

"Then why do so many people want the same information written in different books?"

Yuru considered her daughter's question.

"Because they do not always trust the records kept by another office."

The Crown Prince added, "Or because they want to be able to say that they have checked something, even if they have not checked the original."

Yuru looked at him.

"That is possible."

Minglan sighed.

"Then there are too many books."

Yuru smiled.

"There are certainly too many books."

The three of them walked along the garden path.

For a little while, no one spoke about registers.

Minglan asked whether she could continue examining the princess's household accounts.

Yuru agreed, provided she did not interfere with ordinary duties or make accusations without evidence.

The Crown Prince said he would continue reviewing the Eastern Palace.

Yuru told him to send his questions through the designated office.

He nodded.

Then he asked, "Mother, are you still determined to resign?"

Yuru looked at him.

"Yes."

The Crown Prince was quiet.

"Will the palace be able to function?"

"It must."

"That is not what I asked."

Yuru stopped walking.

She looked at her eldest son.

"The palace may struggle. Some arrangements may fail. Some people may make mistakes. The purpose of the review is to reduce the damage before the transition."

"And if the emperor refuses?"

"Then I will continue to make my case."

Minglan looked down.

"Will you leave us?"

Yuru's expression softened.

"I am your mother. I am not leaving you."

"But you may leave the palace?"

"I may leave the position. Those are not the same thing."

The Crown Prince lowered his eyes.

He had spent his life thinking of the empress as the center of the inner palace.

It was difficult to imagine the palace without her.

It was even more difficult to imagine her as a person who might want something that had nothing to do with the palace.

Yuru resumed walking.

"Do not make the mistake of thinking that a person's duty is the only thing that makes them valuable."

Neither child replied.

But both remembered the words.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao received a message from the northern canal property's broker.

The seller had agreed to permit a second inspection.

The inspection would include the roof beams, the courtyard well, the kitchen drainage, and the boundary wall.

The broker had also asked whether Mingzhao wished to make a preliminary offer.

She read the message.

Then she wrote a reply.

She would not make an offer until the inspection and title verification were complete.

Qiao Yue looked at the reply.

"Will the seller become impatient?"

"That is possible."

"What if another buyer purchases it?"

"Then another buyer purchases it."

Qiao Yue stared at her.

"You are willing to lose the house?"

"I am willing to lose a house with uncertain costs rather than buy it because I am afraid someone else might."

She sealed the letter.

Then she opened her budget again.

The additional repair reserve had made the total uncomfortable.

She looked at the figure.

For years, she had controlled expenses by reducing waste, negotiating prices, and ensuring that purchases were properly recorded.

Now she was applying the same discipline to her own future.

It was not as satisfying as she had expected.

When managing the Marquisate, she could order a repair and assign the expense to the estate.

When managing her own household, every repair reduced the money available for something else.

There would be no larger family treasury to absorb an unexpected cost.

There would be no convenient account into which an inconvenient expense could be placed.

She understood the difference.

She was beginning to feel it.

That evening, the Shen family gathered for dinner.

Mingzhao arrived after the others.

The meal was quieter than usual.

Shen Guowei asked whether the accountant had made progress.

"Some," Mingzhao replied.

"Has he found the missing grain?"

"Not yet. The former warehouse steward believes that some of the grain may have been moved temporarily during repairs. We are waiting for the original movement register."

Her father nodded.

"And the three hundred taels?"

"Seventy-two taels have been identified as estate maintenance. The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight are still under examination."

Mingyuan looked up.

"I have provided the repair ledger."

"I received it."

"I also spoke with the carpenter."

"Good."

Mingyuan hesitated.

"The carpenter said the steward authorized the work."

"Then the authorization record should be located."

"I do not know whether it exists."

"Then the matter remains incomplete."

Mingyuan looked irritated.

"You cannot expect me to know every detail."

"I do not."

"Then why—"

"Because the account is still open."

Mingyuan fell silent.

Madam Liang placed food on her plate.

"Must you discuss the accounts at dinner?"

Mingzhao looked at her mother.

"No. We can discuss them tomorrow."

Old Madam Shen shook her head.

"All this examination has made the household unpleasant."

Shen Mingxiu looked down at her bowl.

"It was not especially pleasant before."

Her mother glanced at her.

Mingxiu immediately began eating.

Shen Guowei sighed.

"Enough. The family should be able to have one meal without discussing the settlement."

Mingzhao nodded.

She did not object.

For the rest of the meal, nobody spoke about property.

They spoke about the weather, a neighbour's visit, and the upcoming seasonal ceremony.

It was almost peaceful.

Then Shen Mingyuan asked whether the family should replace the northern warehouse roof before the summer rains.

Mingzhao looked up.

Shen Guowei closed his eyes.

Madam Liang put down her chopsticks.

And Shen Mingxiu quietly moved her bowl farther away from the discussion.

The family had achieved eight minutes of peace.

It had been a respectable result.

Late that night, Yuru reviewed the latest responsibilities register.

The number remained ninety-four.

The categories were clearer.

The offices were beginning to acknowledge their duties.

The broom investigation had identified a likely explanation for the inventory discrepancy, though the disposal records remained incomplete.

The Crown Prince was learning to review his own household.

The Second Prince was still attempting to create a rule that would prevent him from being asked to approve too many purchases.

The Third Princess had discovered that two garments were not missing at all.

Yuru read the final page.

At the bottom was a new column.

**Responsibilities requiring the personal judgment of the Empress.**

There were twelve entries.

She read them carefully.

Some concerned ceremonial precedence.

Some concerned disputes among senior palace women.

Others involved the welfare of imperial children and elderly household members.

She did not intend to pretend that these responsibilities could simply be assigned to a clerk.

But neither did she accept that every decision must remain hers forever.

She marked three entries for further review.

Then she placed the register aside.

For the first time in many years, she finished the day's work without requesting another bundle.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao did something similar.

She closed the property accounts.

She had not completed the settlement.

She had not purchased a house.

She had not received the full amount she believed might be owed to her.

But she had stopped working on the accounts for the night.

Qiao Yue brought her tea.

"Miss, you have finished?"

"For today."

"Will you continue tomorrow?"

"Of course."

Qiao Yue smiled.

"Then perhaps we should be grateful for today."

Mingzhao looked at the closed account book.

"Perhaps."

The palace and the Marquisate had not become simpler.

But the work was beginning to move away from the two women who had carried too much of it.

For the first time, that movement was visible.

It was not yet freedom.

It was the beginning of an accounting.

**End of Chapter 10.**
