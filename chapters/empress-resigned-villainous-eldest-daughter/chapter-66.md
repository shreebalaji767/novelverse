# **Chapter 66: The Former Empress Has Been Given a Box of Pastries and Has Been Warned Not to Share Them Before Tasting Them. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Given a Revised Property Schedule and Has Been Warned That the Family Still Has Questions. Both Women Have Discovered That Gifts Often Arrive with Advice, While Documents Often Arrive with More Documents.**

**30th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The pastries from the Empress Dowager arrived in the guest residence with a second message.



Xu Yuru was reading beside the window when Fang Ru entered.



"Madam, the Dowager's attendant has sent another note."



Yuru looked at the small box.



"Is there a problem?"



"No. Her Majesty says that the pastries should be eaten while fresh."



Fang Ru placed the note beside the box.



Then she added, "She also says that you should taste them before deciding whether to share them."



Yuru looked at her.



"Did Her Majesty write that?"



"Yes."



"Then she must suspect that I will give them away."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Perhaps she remembers how you used to distribute gifts among the palace attendants."



Yuru opened the box.



Inside were eight small pastries, carefully arranged in two rows.



They were made with a thin outer layer and a filling of sweet bean paste. A little sesame had been sprinkled across the tops.



Yuru picked one up.



She took a bite.



The pastry was crisp.



The filling was not too sweet.



She nodded.



"These are good."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Shall I prepare tea?"



"Yes."



"And the remaining pastries?"



"Two for you. Two for the attendants. Keep the others here."



Fang Ru began arranging the portions.



Yuru looked at the note again.



The Dowager had given her a gift and instructed her not to distribute it immediately.



It was a small request.



It was also rather unlike the way the Dowager had once spoken to her.



Yuru smiled.



She was being treated as someone who could enjoy a pastry without first deciding how many people should receive it.



That was a pleasant change.

At the Empress Dowager's residence, the Dowager asked whether Yuru had received the pastries.



Her attendant confirmed that the box had been delivered.



"Did she receive the message?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Good."



The Dowager took a sip of tea.



She had deliberately chosen a small box.



A larger gift might have prompted Yuru to distribute it among the attendants.



The Dowager had wanted her to enjoy something herself.



She did not say this aloud.



Instead, she asked whether the palace gardens had been watered.



The attendant replied that the gardeners had completed the morning work.



The Dowager nodded.



She had not asked Yuru to review it.



The gardeners were responsible for their work.



If they made a mistake, the proper officials could correct it.



The Dowager was beginning to find this arrangement rather convenient.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received the revised property schedule.



It was delivered by a family messenger.



The messenger waited while Qiao Yue brought the document inside.



Mingzhao opened it.



The family representative had incorporated most of her previous corrections.



The nine confirmed advances remained separate.



The four payment-confirmed entries with unclear repayment arrangements remained in their own section.



The ten uncertain entries were still listed as requiring evidence.



The seventy-two taels for study repairs remained classified as estate maintenance paid from Mingzhao's funds.



The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The furnishing payment had been removed from her personal advances and returned to estate expenditure.



The grain discrepancy remained separate from the blank-destination transport payment.



The northern canal property remained excluded.



The western field was correctly described as temporary cultivation.



Mingzhao turned to the final pages.



There was another note.



It concerned the remaining personal advances.



The family representative wanted Mingzhao to provide a statement confirming that she had not received repayment for the nine confirmed sums.



Mingzhao read the request carefully.



Then she called Qiao Yue.



"Bring the repayment ledger."



Qiao Yue retrieved it from the document case.



Mingzhao compared the ledger with the revised schedule.



The nine advances had been confirmed as payments from her personal funds.



The family had not yet provided evidence of repayment.



But the absence of a repayment entry in her own ledger was not, by itself, proof that no repayment had ever occurred through another account.



She turned to the relevant page.



"Prepare a response."



Qiao Yue brought paper.



Mingzhao dictated slowly.



"The nine advances are confirmed as payments made from my personal funds. No repayment has been recorded in my personal accounts. If the family claims repayment was made through another account, it should identify the date, amount, and recipient, with supporting records."



Qiao Yue wrote it down.



Mingzhao continued.



"The statement should not imply that the absence of a personal ledger entry establishes the absence of every possible repayment."



Qiao Yue looked up.



"Must we leave room for the family to find something?"



"We must leave room for the records to establish the truth."



Qiao Yue nodded.



She understood the distinction.



Mingzhao did not want to claim more than she could prove.



She also did not intend to surrender a confirmed claim merely because the family might produce evidence later.

At the Marquisate, Mr Liang read Mingzhao's reply.



He was not surprised by it.



The representative had wanted a simple confirmation.



Mingzhao had supplied a qualified statement.



He called the clerk.



"Check the estate cash books for the relevant periods."



The clerk looked at the schedule.



"All of them?"



"All accounts that could reasonably have been used for repayment."



"That will take time."



"Then it will take time."



The clerk bowed.



Mr Liang returned to the schedule.



The family had been accustomed to treating an entry as settled once someone had signed it.



Mingzhao was insisting that the underlying transaction be established.



It was a more demanding process.



It was also more defensible.

Shen Mingyuan spent the afternoon reviewing the estate's older cash books.



He had requested that the clerks identify payments made to Mingzhao during the relevant years.



The first records were straightforward.



There were household expenses paid to merchants.



There were sums transferred between estate accounts.



There were payments for servants and repairs.



Some entries carried Mingzhao's authorization.



Others carried the steward's.



None could automatically be treated as repayment of a personal advance.



Mingyuan placed a marker beside each possible transaction.



"Do not classify these as repayments yet," he told the clerk.



The clerk looked surprised.



"But the payments were made under the eldest young lady's authorization."



"That does not establish that they were paid to her."



"Yes, young master."



Mingyuan continued reading.



He was discovering that a large account book contained many transactions that looked similar when viewed quickly.



A payment to a merchant.



A payment to a servant.



A transfer to another account.



A reimbursement to someone who had paid from personal funds.



The differences were not decorative.



They determined who owed what to whom.



He turned another page.



"Find the receipts for these two entries."



The clerk bowed.



Mingyuan looked at the growing pile.



He had once believed that his sister had made the estate accounts complicated.



Now he was discovering that the accounts had been complicated long before he began reading them.

At the workshop, the first twenty-four bolts of the next group had passed internal inspection.



The trainee who had previously struggled with the revised folding method had improved.



Her work was slower than that of the experienced workers, but the edges were consistent.



The second trainee was faster.



He still needed reminders to check the underside of the fold.



Mingzhao watched them work.



Lin Shuyun stood beside her.



"The first trainee is becoming more reliable."



"Yes."



"The second is still careless."



"Less careless than before."



Lin Shuyun glanced at her.



"You always find a way to make praise sound like a warning."



Mingzhao looked toward the worktable.



"Would you prefer that I tell him he is excellent when he has not yet mastered the process?"



"No."



"Then we agree."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



The senior finisher approached with the completed inspection sheet.



"All twenty-four have passed."



Mingzhao examined the entries.



"Any corrections?"



"Two minor adjustments. Both were completed without material loss."



"Who checked them?"



"Zhao checked the first. I checked the second."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Record both corrections and retain the inspection notes."



The finisher bowed.



Lin Shuyun looked at the completed group.



"We are moving steadily."



"Yes."



"Could we begin the next group tomorrow?"



"After the workers finish their current assignments and the material is prepared."



Lin Shuyun sighed.



"Tomorrow, then."



"Tomorrow, if the preparation is complete."



"Must I put a condition on every sentence?"



"Only the ones concerning production."

The buyer's representative had accepted the forty-eight completed bolts.



The next group was now ready for inspection.



The remaining stock was being processed.



The revised folding method continued to save handling time.



However, the cost statement was still incomplete.



Mingzhao reviewed the wage records.



The experienced workers' wages were listed separately from the trainees' wages.



Training time was recorded.



Supervision time was recorded.



The material used for corrections had not resulted in a write-off.



She checked the figures twice.



Then she closed the ledger.



Lin Shuyun watched her.



"Can we at least say the method is working?"



"Yes."



"Can we say the order is profitable?"



"No."



Lin Shuyun looked at the ledger.



"Not even approximately?"



"Not without the remaining production and delivery costs."



Lin Shuyun sighed.



"One day, I shall ask you whether the sun has risen and you will tell me that the light has not yet been entered into the accounts."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"If the sun is being sold by the hour, we should record it."



Lin Shuyun laughed loudly enough for the workers at the next table to look over.

At the Xu residence, Madam Xu received a letter from her daughter.



It was brief.



Yuru thanked her for the preserved vegetables and said that the meal had been enjoyable.



Madam Xu read it twice.



Then she placed it beside the household correspondence.



Duke Xu entered.



"Has Yuru written?"



"Yes."



"What did she say?"



"She enjoyed the meal."



The Duke smiled.



"Good."



He looked toward the kitchen.



"Perhaps we should invite her again."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"We have only just seen her."



"That is no reason not to invite her."



"She has her own arrangements."



"She can decide whether to come."



Madam Xu considered this.



That was reasonable.



She had no objection to inviting her daughter.



She only objected to making every invitation into an obligation.



"Write to her next week," she said. "Ask whether she is free."



Duke Xu nodded.



"Should we prepare another meal?"



"That depends on whether she accepts."



He looked disappointed.



Madam Xu added, "And six dishes."



The Duke sighed.



"Must we always discuss the number?"



"Yes."

At the palace, Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an received the latest oil inventory report.



The clerks had completed another comparison.



Several entries could now be reconciled using the recorded jar capacities.



Others remained uncertain because the original documents did not specify the size of the containers.



The Crown Prince examined the table.



"How many entries remain unresolved?"



The clerk gave him the number.



"Have the original descriptions been preserved?"



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Then keep them."



The clerk bowed.



Cheng'an looked at the remaining stock column.



The figures were more consistent than before.



They were not complete.



He set the report aside.



The Second Prince entered with a different document.



"Brother."



Cheng'an looked up.



"What is it?"



"My gathering expenses."



"Have you submitted the final request?"



"Yes."



"Then why are you bringing it here?"



"I wanted you to see the revised arrangement."



Cheng'an took the paper.



The confirmed guest count was listed separately from possible attendance.



The number of attendants was justified by the requirements of the gathering.



The food expenditure had been adjusted.



The Second Prince watched his brother read.



"Is it acceptable?"



"Yes."



Chengyu looked pleased.



"Then I have learned something."



"What?"



"That if I want a larger budget, I should first make the smaller one accurate."



The Crown Prince looked at him.



"That is not what you should have learned."



Chengyu smiled.



"I know."



He took the document back.



The two brothers returned to their separate tasks.

In the afternoon, Xu Yuru received another small parcel.



This time, it came from the Xu residence.



Fang Ru brought it inside.



"Madam Xu has sent another packet."



Yuru looked at it.



"What is it?"



"Preserved fruit."



Yuru smiled.



"Mother is determined to keep the kitchen occupied."



Fang Ru opened the parcel.



There were two small packets, each wrapped separately.



One was intended for Yuru.



The other was marked for Fang Ru.



Fang Ru looked surprised.



"Madam Xu has remembered me."



"Of course she has."



Yuru examined the labels.



"Mother has also remembered that I will share things."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Perhaps she has decided to prepare for it."



Yuru picked up her packet.



She did not feel the need to distribute it immediately.



She would eat it later.



The palace could wait.

At the temporary residence, Mingzhao finished reviewing the family schedule.



The representative's latest version was better.



It was still incomplete.



The family had begun checking the cash books for possible repayments.



The former warehouse steward Zhang had not yet completed his examination of the grain records.



The southern roof repairs had been approved in principle, but the carpenter's final schedule was still required.



The workshop's new order continued.



The northern canal property remained under consideration.



The lower room was still being monitored for dampness.



There were many unfinished matters.



Mingzhao wrote a short note for the family representative.



She acknowledged receipt of the revised schedule and stated that she would review the next version when the supporting records were available.



She did not promise to attend another meeting before receiving them.



Qiao Yue watched her seal the letter.



"Do you think the family will finally stop asking you to manage the estate?"



"No."



"Then what will happen?"



"They will ask again."



"And you will refuse?"



"If the request concerns work I have already relinquished, yes."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"That seems straightforward."



"It should have been."

That evening, the Dowager received a report that Yuru had enjoyed the pastries.



The attendant also mentioned that the former Empress had shared some with her attendants.



The Dowager smiled.



"Then she ignored my instruction."



The attendant looked uncertain.



"Your Majesty?"



The Dowager laughed.



"She tasted them first, I hope."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then I suppose the instruction was followed."



She returned to her tea.



She had not intended to control what Yuru did with the pastries.



She had merely wanted her to enjoy them.



There was a difference.

Before retiring, Yuru placed the small packet of preserved fruit beside her book.



Mingzhao, in her temporary residence, placed the corrected property schedule beside her account ledger.



One had received gifts from people who wanted her to enjoy them.



The other had received documents from people who still wanted her to help them finish what they had left incomplete.



Neither woman had reached a permanent arrangement.



Yuru had not chosen her residence.



Mingzhao had not completed the family settlement.



The palace still had unresolved inventory entries.



The Marquisate still had disputed accounts.



The workshop still had most of its order to produce.



But the direction of their lives was no longer determined solely by what other people wanted them to do.



For the former Empress, that meant being able to enjoy a pastry before sharing it.



For the Marquis's eldest daughter, it meant being able to read a document without automatically accepting responsibility for every number inside it.



Both were modest freedoms.



Both had taken considerable effort.
