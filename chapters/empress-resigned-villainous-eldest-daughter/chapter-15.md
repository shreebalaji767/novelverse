# **Chapter 15: The Empress Has Been Asked to Attend a Meeting About Her Own Replacement. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Train the People Who Will Replace Her.**

**7th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Ministry of Personnel had finally discovered that the Empress's resignation could not be examined indefinitely without someone eventually having to make a decision.

This discovery had not made the ministry enthusiastic.

It had, however, made its officials punctual.

At the appointed hour, three representatives arrived at the imperial palace carrying documents concerning the transfer of the Empress's administrative duties.

They were accompanied by two representatives from the Ministry of Rites and one senior official from the Imperial Household Administration.

The Emperor had ordered the meeting to take place in the Hall of Mental Cultivation.

He had also ordered that the Empress attend.

Yuru received the summons while reviewing the monthly expenditure of the charitable fund.

She read it once.

Then she handed it to Fang Ru.

"At last."

Fang Ru looked at the summons.

"Your Majesty?"

"They have decided to discuss the matter in my presence rather than in a report."

"That should be useful."

"It may be."

Yuru returned to the charitable fund's register.

She checked the emergency allowance.

The revised procedure had been issued the previous day. The administration had already updated the eligibility list and sent instructions to the pharmacy store.

There was one remaining question concerning the record of home deliveries.

Yuru wrote a short clarification.

Then she closed the ledger.

"Prepare my carriage."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru rose.

She had been asked to attend a meeting about the position she intended to relinquish.

It was a strange arrangement.

But she had learned that strange arrangements were often the only way to make the palace examine something it had previously considered permanent.

The meeting began with the Ministry of Personnel presenting its report.

The official responsible for the presentation was Minister Han.

He spoke carefully.

The Empress's position was established by imperial authority and ceremonial regulation. The existing laws did not permit the Empress to transfer the position to another person by private agreement.

If the Emperor accepted her resignation, the consequences would have to be considered separately from the transfer of her administrative duties.

The Ministry of Rites had prepared a second report.

It identified the ceremonial obligations associated with the position, including the Empress's role in certain state observances, the inner palace's formal hierarchy, and the status of the imperial children.

The Imperial Household Administration had prepared a third report.

It listed the duties currently performed through the Empress's office.

The Emperor listened.

Then he asked, "Which of these duties require the Empress's formal position?"

Minister Han consulted his papers.

"Some ceremonial duties, Your Majesty."

"Which?"

The Ministry of Rites representative answered.

"Certain observances, the formal reception of senior women, and the established duties concerning the imperial household's principal ceremonies."

"And the administrative duties?"

"The majority can be assigned to existing offices."

"Then why have they not been assigned?"

The official from the Imperial Household Administration replied.

"Because the current arrangement has been maintained through the Empress's office."

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

"That is a description of the arrangement."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"It is not an explanation of why the arrangement must continue."

The official lowered his head.

Yuru sat quietly.

She had heard the same argument in different forms for years.

The palace had become accustomed to her involvement.

The habit had been mistaken for necessity.

Minister Han turned toward her.

"Your Majesty, the ministry wishes to understand whether you intend to relinquish all responsibilities associated with the position or only the administrative duties."

Yuru answered calmly.

"I intend to relinquish the position itself."

The room became still.

She continued.

"I do not intend to abandon the imperial household. I intend to cease being its permanent administrator."

The Ministry of Rites representative looked at her.

"Your Majesty, certain ceremonial responsibilities cannot be separated from the position."

"I understand."

"Then the transition may be difficult."

"Yes."

Yuru folded her hands.

"That is why we are examining it."

The Emperor looked toward the ministers.

"Continue."

The discussion lasted nearly two hours.

The ministries identified three categories.

The first consisted of duties that could be transferred to existing offices under current regulations.

The second consisted of duties that required revised procedures but did not require a new office.

The third consisted of responsibilities connected directly to the Empress's formal position.

The third category could not be resolved by administrative reassignment alone.

The Emperor would have to decide whether the position itself would be relinquished and what arrangement would follow.

Minister Han presented a proposed transition.

The administrative duties would be transferred in stages.

The existing offices would receive written authority.

The Empress's office would continue to provide clarification during the transition.

The final date would be determined after the principal duties had been transferred.

Yuru read the proposal.

Then she looked at the Emperor.

"Your Majesty."

"Speak."

"The transition should not depend on the offices becoming entirely free of mistakes."

Minister Han looked surprised.

Yuru continued.

"If every error requires the Empress's personal intervention, the transfer will never be complete."

The Emperor nodded.

"Then what should determine completion?"

"The establishment of clear authority, a workable procedure, and a means of correcting errors."

Minister Han considered this.

"That may require a period of observation."

"Yes."

"During which the Empress's office would remain available."

"Available for clarification," Yuru replied. "Not as the final authority for every ordinary matter."

The Imperial Household Administration representative made a note.

The Emperor looked at him.

"Write that distinction into the proposal."

The official bowed.

Yuru examined the remaining pages.

There was a clause stating that the Empress would continue to receive all reports concerning the inner palace until the transfer was complete.

She marked it.

"All reports?"

Minister Han answered.

"It was intended to preserve continuity."

"Then it should specify which reports."

The Emperor looked at the clause.

"Correct it."

The official from the Imperial Household Administration bowed.

The meeting ended without a final decision.

But it had produced a clearer arrangement.

The Empress's position would be examined separately from the transfer of duties.

The ministries would prepare a revised proposal.

The Emperor would decide the final question.

And the administrative transition would continue whether or not the ministries had reached agreement on every ceremonial consequence.

Yuru returned to Chengming Palace.

She had not resigned.

She had not been refused.

She had, however, forced the palace to stop treating her intention as a vague complaint and begin treating it as a matter requiring a decision.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had been asked to conduct the first training session for the people who would take over her responsibilities.

She had expected this.

She had also expected the family to choose the most convenient method.

The convenient method would have been to sit everyone in the study, explain the accounts, and answer questions until the afternoon.

Mingzhao had rejected it.

Instead, she had prepared three separate sessions.

The first concerned ordinary estate expenditure.

The second concerned the monthly agricultural accounts.

The third concerned the transfer of records and unresolved matters.

She had also prepared a list of responsibilities that should no longer be assigned to a single person.

At the appointed hour, Shen Mingyuan arrived in the eastern study.

He brought the expenditure ledger.

Steward Zhou brought the estate's monthly account.

Shen Mingxiu brought the list of secondary residences.

Mingzhao stood beside the table.

"Today we will examine ordinary expenditure."

Mingyuan looked at the ledger.

"Why not the property division?"

"Because the property division is not complete."

"Then why are we doing this?"

"Because the household must continue operating while the property division is examined."

Mingyuan sighed.

Mingzhao ignored him.

She opened the ledger.

"An ordinary expenditure should have four things."

She wrote them on a sheet.

**Purpose.**

**Authority.**

**Amount.**

**Evidence of payment or delivery.**

Mingyuan looked at the list.

"That is obvious."

"Then it should be easy."

She turned to the first entry.

The household had purchased oil for the eastern residence.

The purpose was clear.

The amount was recorded.

The receipt was present.

But the entry did not identify who had authorized the purchase.

Mingzhao pointed to the blank column.

"Who approved this?"

Steward Zhou answered.

"The residence steward."

"Under what authority?"

"The ordinary household allowance."

"Where is the allowance recorded?"

Zhou opened the monthly account.

The amount was included.

Mingzhao nodded.

"Then the entry should refer to the allowance."

Mingyuan looked at the page.

"Why does the ledger need so many references?"

"Because the ledger is not merely a record of money leaving the household. It is a record that allows someone else to understand why the money was spent."

Mingyuan considered this.

He had previously believed that accounting was the process of counting money.

He was beginning to understand that it was also the process of explaining decisions.

Mingzhao turned to the next entry.

A repair had been charged to the eastern residence.

The receipt was present.

The work had been completed.

But the expenditure exceeded the ordinary allowance.

"Who authorized this?"

Steward Zhou answered.

"The previous household manager."

"Was the authorization written?"

"Yes."

"Then attach it."

Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan looked at the entry.

"What happens if the authorization is missing?"

"Then the expenditure remains under examination."

"Even if the repair was necessary?"

"Necessity and authorization are different questions."

Mingyuan looked irritated.

Mingzhao continued.

"A necessary repair may still require a record of who approved it. The absence of that record does not automatically make the repair improper. It means the record is incomplete."

Mingxiu looked at her.

"That is the same distinction the accountant is making about the northern warehouse."

"Yes."

Mingzhao closed the ledger.

"Good. You are beginning to understand the principle."

The second session concerned the agricultural accounts.

Steward Zhou explained the monthly records.

The estate received grain from several fields.

The amounts varied according to the harvest, the weather, and the condition of the land.

Some grain was sold.

Some was retained for household use.

Some was used as seed.

Some was distributed to workers according to their agreements.

The records were not complicated.

But they had been maintained in different formats by different stewards.

Mingxiu pointed to a column.

"This records the grain received."

"Yes," Zhou replied.

"And this records the grain sold."

"Yes."

"Where is the grain retained for seed?"

Zhou looked at the ledger.

"It is recorded in the agricultural account."

Mingxiu looked at the page.

"Then why is it not shown here?"

"Because this is the household summary."

Mingxiu looked at Mingzhao.

"The summary should show the total received, the total sold, the amount retained, and the remaining balance."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That would make it easier to compare the records."

Zhou considered the suggestion.

"It would require the agricultural steward to submit the figures in a consistent format."

"Then he should."

Mingyuan looked at the page.

"Will this require another register?"

Mingzhao answered.

"Not necessarily. The existing agricultural account can be revised."

She turned to Steward Zhou.

"Do not create a new register unless the existing one cannot perform the task."

Zhou nodded.

The instruction was sensible.

It was also a warning.

Mingzhao had spent years correcting the family's records by creating new summaries, new lists, and new methods of comparison.

She had often done so because the existing documents were inadequate.

But she had also sometimes created a new document when a clearer instruction would have been sufficient.

The household did not need more paper merely because paper was easier to control than people.

The third session concerned the transfer of unresolved matters.

Mingzhao placed the northern warehouse examination on the table.

The twenty sacks recorded in the surviving movement note had not yet been confirmed as received by the southern storage building.

The remaining discrepancy had not been explained.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels charged to Shen Mingyuan's residence remained unverified.

The study repair expenditure of seventy-two taels was supported by partial records, but the original work order and final receipt were still being sought.

The house search remained incomplete.

The property division remained unresolved.

Mingzhao looked at the three members of the family.

"These matters are not to be treated as completed merely because I leave."

Mingyuan frowned.

"Then who will continue the examination?"

"The accountant will continue examining the records. The estate steward will provide the documents. The family will decide the legal and financial consequences after the evidence is complete."

"Who will answer the questions?"

"The person responsible for the relevant records."

Mingyuan looked at her.

"That is not always obvious."

"Then the transition schedule must identify the responsible office."

Mingxiu examined the list.

"The northern warehouse records should be held by the agricultural steward, but the movement notes should be attached to the repair records."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Correct."

"And the study repair expenditure should be reviewed by the central estate clerk, because the payment came from the estate."

"Correct."

Mingyuan looked at his sister.

"You have an answer for everything."

"No."

She closed the file.

"I have a procedure for identifying who should answer."

That was not the same thing.

And she was beginning to understand that the distinction was the entire point.

At the end of the training session, Mingyuan looked exhausted.

He had spent three hours examining records that he had previously regarded as ordinary household matters.

He had also discovered that the estate's finances were not as simple as he had assumed.

The family had money.

It had land.

It had income.

But it also had obligations, incomplete records, and arrangements that had continued for years without anyone asking whether they were still appropriate.

Mingyuan looked at the ledger.

"Will the next session be this long?"

Mingzhao considered the question.

"Only if you continue asking questions that have already been answered."

He stared at her.

Mingxiu laughed.

Steward Zhou lowered his head to conceal a smile.

Mingyuan stood.

"I will review the monthly accounts tomorrow."

"Good."

"And I will ask the accountant to explain the agricultural summary."

"Good."

He hesitated.

"Will you be present?"

Mingzhao looked at him.

"If the question concerns a record I maintained, I will answer it during the agreed transition period."

"And after that?"

"After that, you will ask the person responsible for the record."

Mingyuan looked away.

He did not like the answer.

But he had begun to recognize that the answer would not change merely because he disliked it.

That afternoon, the former warehouse steward Zhang arrived with another bundle of papers.

The southern storage ledger had been found.

Sun Qiming examined it first.

The ledger recorded the arrival of eighteen sacks during the relevant period.

The movement note had recorded twenty sacks leaving the northern warehouse.

Two sacks remained unaccounted for.

The remaining twenty-seven sacks from the original discrepancy had not been connected to the movement.

Sun Qiming placed the documents side by side.

Mingzhao examined them.

"Are the eighteen entries dated?"

"Yes."

"Do they correspond to the movement note?"

"Most likely. The dates are close, but not identical."

"Then we cannot yet establish that they are the same twenty sacks."

"Correct."

Mingzhao looked at the documents.

The examination had made progress.

It had also made the remaining uncertainty more precise.

Twenty sacks had been recorded as moved.

Eighteen had been recorded as received.

Two were still missing from the movement record.

Twenty-seven remained unexplained by the surviving documents.

The original discrepancy of forty-seven had not disappeared.

It had been divided into smaller questions.

Mingzhao found that more useful than a convenient conclusion.

"Ask Zhang whether he remembers the two sacks."

Sun Qiming nodded.

"And ask whether the southern storage building had a separate opening inventory."

"That will be necessary."

Mingzhao looked at the ledger.

"Do not close the matter."

Sun Qiming replied.

"I will not."

In the palace, the Emperor received the revised transition proposal.

It now separated the Empress's formal position from the administrative duties.

The ministries had identified the duties that could be transferred.

They had also identified the ceremonial responsibilities that would require a decision if the Empress's resignation were accepted.

The proposed transition would proceed in stages.

The Imperial Household Administration would assume ordinary supervision.

The Department of Rites would clarify ceremonial matters.

The Ministry of Personnel would prepare the appointment arrangements if a successor or alternative structure became necessary.

The Empress's office would provide advice during the transition but would not remain the final authority for ordinary administrative decisions.

The Emperor read the document.

Then he looked at Yuru.

"Is this acceptable?"

Yuru examined the proposal.

"It is workable."

"That is not the same as saying it is complete."

"No. It is not."

The Emperor nodded.

"Then what remains?"

"Several duties require clarification. The ceremonial responsibilities require a separate decision. The administrative transfer requires a period of observation."

"How long?"

Yuru considered the question.

"Long enough to establish that the offices can perform their duties. Not so long that the transition becomes permanent merely because the palace has grown accustomed to it."

Zhao Jingheng looked at the ministers.

"Prepare a timetable."

Minister Han bowed.

The Emperor turned to Yuru.

"I have not accepted your resignation."

"I know."

"I have not rejected it."

"I know."

"Then why do you look satisfied?"

Yuru's expression remained composed.

"Because the palace has finally begun to distinguish between what I wish to relinquish and what it wishes to preserve."

The Emperor looked at her for a moment.

Then he returned to the report.

"That is progress."

Yuru inclined her head.

It was not the decision she wanted.

But it was a decision beginning to take shape.

That evening, Mingzhao received a letter from her father.

The letter was brief.

He had reviewed the transition schedule.

He accepted the transfer of ordinary responsibilities.

He agreed that the departure date should be established separately from the completion of the property division.

He also requested that Mingzhao remain available for questions concerning the records for a defined period.

Mingzhao read the letter.

Then she looked at Qiao Yue.

"Father has agreed."

Qiao Yue smiled.

"Then the departure date can be settled."

"Not yet."

Qiao Yue's smile faded.

"Why?"

"He has agreed that the date should be established separately. He has not established it."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Young Lady, you are impossible to satisfy."

Mingzhao folded the letter.

"I am satisfied with progress."

She placed it beside the transition schedule.

The household had begun transferring responsibilities.

The accountant had found records that explained part of the warehouse movement.

Mingyuan had begun examining the accounts.

Mingxiu had accepted responsibility for the monthly summaries.

The house search remained unresolved.

The property division remained incomplete.

But the family had finally agreed that Mingzhao's departure was not a reward to be granted after everyone else had become comfortable.

It was a decision that required preparation.

And preparation had begun.

Before bed, Yuru reviewed the transition timetable.

The palace had agreed to transfer the first group of administrative duties within ten days.

The second group would follow after the revised procedures had been tested.

The remaining ceremonial responsibilities would be examined separately.

The Emperor would make the final decision concerning her position.

Yuru looked at the timetable.

Ten days.

It was not a resignation.

It was not freedom.

But it was a date by which the palace would have to demonstrate that it could function without sending every ordinary matter to her office.

She closed the document.

For years, she had been the person who made sure the palace was ready for tomorrow.

Now she was preparing the palace for a tomorrow in which she might not be there.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao was doing the same.

Neither woman had completed her departure.

Neither had received the final answer she wanted.

But both had stopped waiting for the institutions around them to become comfortable with the idea.

The palace had been given ten days.

The Marquisate had been given a transition schedule.

And the people who had spent years relying on two women were beginning to discover that responsibility could not be transferred merely by writing another person's name beside it.

It had to be learned.

**End of Chapter 15.**
