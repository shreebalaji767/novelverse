# **Chapter 37: The Former Empress Has Refused to Review Her Father's Accounts. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Refused to Review Her Father's Accounts. Both Fathers Have Discovered That Their Daughters Are Consistent.**

**29th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Duke Xu received his daughter's reply shortly after breakfast.

He read it once.

Then he read it again.

The message was polite.

It was also unmistakable.

*I shall be pleased to visit next week. However, I am visiting as your daughter, not as an accounting office.*

The Duke put the letter on his desk.

His steward, who had been waiting beside the door, asked cautiously, "Does Madam Xu's daughter intend to visit?"

"Yes."

"Should the eastern courtyard be prepared?"

"Yes."

"Should I arrange the household accounts in the study?"

"No."

The steward hesitated.

"Your Excellency, the annual accounts are due for review."

"Then have the household accountant review them."

"The accountant has raised several questions."

"Then let him ask the people responsible for the accounts."

The steward bowed.

The Duke looked at the letter again.

"Why did you think my daughter would review them?"

The steward lowered his head.

"She has considerable experience."

"That is not an answer."

The steward considered his words.

"Because she has reviewed the household accounts during previous visits."

The Duke was silent.

That was true.

Before her resignation, Yuru had occasionally examined her natal family's records. She had identified errors, questioned unexplained payments, and suggested changes to household procedures.

Her assistance had been useful.

It had also become habitual.

The Duke folded the letter.

"Her circumstances have changed. Do not send the accounts to her."

"Yes, Your Excellency."

"And do not ask her to inspect the garden expenses."

The steward looked surprised.

"Even the garden expenses?"

"Especially the garden expenses."

The steward bowed again.

The Duke returned to his writing.

He was beginning to understand that welcoming his daughter home and employing his daughter as an unpaid administrator were two different activities.

He hoped the household would understand it as well.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao was examining a different letter.

Her father had written to her.

The message was brief.

The previous day's meeting had been useful. The family intended to continue with the confirmed property categories. Shen Mingyuan had been instructed to collect the remaining household records.

Then came the final sentence.

*Since you understand the estate accounts better than anyone, perhaps you could review the old expenditure records before the next meeting so that the family may proceed more quickly.*

Mingzhao read the sentence twice.

Qiao Yue, who was arranging the morning tea, noticed her expression.

"Another request?"

"Yes."

"From the Marquis?"

"Yes."

"Does he wish you to return?"

"No."

Qiao Yue looked relieved.

"Then what does he want?"

Mingzhao handed her the letter.

Qiao Yue read the last sentence.

She looked at Mingzhao.

"That is rather convenient."

"It is."

"Will you do it?"

"No."

Qiao Yue returned the letter.

Mingzhao took a sheet of paper and began writing.

She thanked her father for the update. She confirmed that she would attend the next settlement meeting once the relevant records had been prepared. She also stated that she would not resume reviewing the estate's ordinary expenditure accounts.

The records could be examined by Sun Qiming, Steward Zhou, and the family members responsible for the relevant transactions.

Her own claims would be supported by the documents already submitted.

She would answer questions concerning those claims.

She would not take responsibility for reconstructing the entire household's accounts.

Qiao Yue watched her write.

"That is a very long refusal."

"It needs to be clear."

"Could you not simply write that you are busy?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because if I write that I am busy, they may ask when I will become less busy."

Qiao Yue thought about this.

"That is true."

Mingzhao sealed the letter.

"Send it."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan was learning that the absence of his elder sister did not automatically make him capable of managing every matter.

He had spent the previous morning reviewing the transport expenses.

The duplicated firewood transport charge identified by Mingxiu had been entered twice under different descriptions.

The first entry was recorded as delivery to the main kitchen.

The second was recorded as additional transport for the same delivery.

Mingyuan had compared the dates.

They matched.

He had also checked the supplier's name.

That matched too.

The problem was that the second entry did not have a separate delivery record.

He called Steward Zhou.

"Was there another cart?"

The steward examined the document.

"I cannot confirm that."

"Was there a second delivery?"

"I do not remember one."

"Can the driver be asked?"

"The driver who made the first delivery is still employed by the estate."

"Then ask him."

Steward Zhou hesitated.

"Should I ask your elder sister to examine the entry?"

Mingyuan looked at him.

"No."

The steward bowed.

Mingyuan returned to the account book.

He was uncomfortable.

He was also aware that he had asked the same question several times in previous days.

His sister had usually answered such questions immediately.

Now he had to find the answer himself.

He called the clerk responsible for recording the delivery.

The clerk arrived with the original ledger.

Mingyuan asked him to explain the two entries.

The clerk examined them.

After a moment, he said, "The second charge may have been for the cart returning with empty containers."

"Where is the return record?"

The clerk searched.

"I do not know."

"Was the return journey charged separately?"

"I cannot say."

Mingyuan looked at the two entries.

"Then we cannot assume the second charge was valid."

The clerk lowered his head.

"No, Young Master."

Mingyuan made a note.

*Second transport charge: duplicate possible; original delivery and return record required.*

He did not remove the charge.

He did not approve it.

For once, he left a question unanswered until evidence could be found.

It was not a dramatic achievement.

It was still progress.

Mingxiu was having a different difficulty.

She had discovered that reviewing household purchases was considerably less pleasant than criticizing the people who made them.

The kitchen had submitted a request for replacement cooking utensils.

The request included three large pots, six smaller pots, two wooden trays, and a set of ladles.

Mingxiu examined the list.

"Why are there three large pots?"

The kitchen supervisor answered, "The old ones are damaged."

"All three?"

"Two are damaged. One is worn."

"Can the worn one still be used?"

"For some purposes."

"Then why replace it?"

The supervisor hesitated.

"Because the new ones are more convenient."

Mingxiu looked at the request.

"Convenience is not the same as necessity."

The supervisor lowered his head.

Mingxiu turned to the inventory sheet.

"Where are the two damaged pots?"

"They are in the rear kitchen."

"Have they been inspected?"

"Not yet."

"Then how do you know they need replacement?"

The supervisor did not answer.

Mingxiu put down the request.

"Have them inspected first. If they cannot be repaired, record that. If they can be repaired, record the cost."

The supervisor bowed.

Mingxiu watched him leave.

She leaned back.

She had expected household administration to involve making decisions.

She had not expected so many people to arrive with decisions already made and ask her to sign beneath them.

She understood, perhaps for the first time, why Mingzhao had been so particular about documents.

In the palace, the Emperor was reviewing the monthly report from the Imperial Household Administration.

The report was shorter than the previous month's.

He regarded this as an improvement.

Gao Shun stood nearby.

"Your Majesty, the revised reporting procedure has been followed in most departments."

"Which departments have not followed it?"

"The Palace Stores submitted a request directly to the Hall of Mental Cultivation concerning damaged ceremonial storage boxes."

The Emperor looked up.

"Why?"

"The department was uncertain whether the boxes belonged to ceremonial equipment or general storage."

"Where are they kept?"

"In the Palace Stores."

"Who is responsible for maintaining them?"

"The Palace Stores."

"Then why did they ask me?"

Gao Shun bowed.

"I shall return the request."

The emperor sighed.

"Tell them to consult the Department of Rites only if the boxes are required for a ceremonial purpose. If they are ordinary storage boxes, the Palace Stores may handle them under its existing authority."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"And tell them not to create a new category of storage boxes merely because the old ones are inconvenient."

Gao Shun paused.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The emperor resumed his work.

After a moment, he looked at the report again.

"How many matters were resolved without being sent here?"

Gao Shun gave him the number.

The emperor nodded.

"Good."

He paused.

"How many were sent here unnecessarily?"

Gao Shun gave him another number.

The emperor put down his brush.

"That number is also improving."

Gao Shun bowed.

The emperor looked at him.

"Why do you look relieved?"

"Because Your Majesty has not asked me to establish a new office to investigate the unnecessary reports."

The emperor stared at him.

Then he laughed.

At the temporary palace residence, Yuru was reading.

Fang Ru entered with a letter.

"Madam, the Duke of Xu has replied."

Yuru accepted it.

The Duke thanked her for the clarification. He had instructed the household to leave the accounts with the household accountant.

There was also a short note at the end.

*I hope you will still inspect the garden during your next visit.*

Yuru smiled.

"That is reasonable."

Fang Ru placed the tea beside her.

"Will you inspect the garden?"

"If the weather is pleasant."

"And if the gardener asks you to review the garden expenses?"

"I shall ask him to show the accounts to the household accountant."

Fang Ru smiled.

"That seems fair."

Yuru looked at her.

"You are enjoying this."

"A little."

Yuru returned to her book.

The residence was quiet.

No officials were waiting outside.

No household department was expecting her approval.

The palace had not collapsed.

Her natal family had not collapsed.

Both institutions were learning to operate without treating her as the answer to every question.

She found the development encouraging.

Later that afternoon, the Third Princess visited her.

Zhao Minglan carried a small notebook.

Yuru closed her book.

"What brings you here?"

"I wanted to ask you something."

"Go ahead."

Minglan sat down.

"The Eastern Palace has been asked to provide a summary of its household expenditure for the coming quarter."

"That is reasonable."

"The Crown Prince has asked the offices to prepare separate reports for food, clothing, transport, and maintenance."

"That is also reasonable."

"But the offices have used different formats."

Yuru nodded.

"That is common when departments have developed their own records."

"Should I tell them to use one format?"

Yuru considered the question.

"Before you do that, ask what information each office needs to record."

Minglan opened her notebook.

"Why?"

"Because a format should serve the work. If you make everyone use the same form without understanding their responsibilities, they may spend more time filling it out than maintaining accurate records."

Minglan wrote this down.

Yuru continued.

"Start with the information that must be consistent: date, amount, purpose, responsible office, and supporting document. Then allow additional sections where necessary."

Minglan nodded.

"That makes sense."

"Have you discussed it with the Crown Prince?"

"Not yet."

"Then do so. He is responsible for the Eastern Palace's administration."

Minglan closed her notebook.

"Would you review the proposed format?"

Yuru looked at her.

Minglan quickly added, "Only if you have time. I know you are no longer responsible for the palace offices."

Yuru smiled.

"I can give you personal advice. I will not approve it as an administrative document."

Minglan nodded.

"That is what I wanted."

Yuru studied her for a moment.

"You are becoming more careful."

Minglan smiled.

"I am trying."

"Good. Continue."

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received a visit from Lin Shuyun.

Lin arrived carrying a narrow document case.

She greeted Mingzhao and sat down.

"The next silk inspection will be delayed by one day."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Why?"

"The two bolts returned for correction have not yet passed the finishing test. The dyer believes another treatment will improve the colour, but I would rather inspect them before promising anything."

"That is sensible."

Lin placed the updated production record on the table.

"The other stock is progressing. I have also reviewed the costs."

Mingzhao read the figures.

"Why has the finishing cost increased?"

"The first group required additional work because the dye was uneven."

"Was that included in the original estimate?"

"Partly. The additional treatment was not."

Mingzhao examined the supporting note.

"Was the additional work necessary to meet the agreed quality?"

"Yes."

"Then record it separately."

Lin nodded.

"I have."

Mingzhao continued reading.

"Will the increased cost affect the agreed price?"

"No. The accepted delivery prices remain as contracted. The additional production cost affects the partnership's margin."

Mingzhao looked up.

"Then we should not change the buyer's invoice."

"Agreed."

Lin leaned back.

"The buyer has asked whether the next delivery can be completed before the end of the month."

"What did you tell him?"

"That I would confirm after inspection."

"Good."

Lin smiled.

"You are not going to tell me to hurry?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because a late delivery with acceptable goods is better than an early delivery with defective goods."

Lin nodded.

"That is what I told him."

For a moment, both women sat quietly.

Then Lin looked at Mingzhao.

"How is the property settlement?"

"Slow."

"Is that good or bad?"

"It is accurate."

Lin laughed.

"That sounds like you."

Mingzhao gave her a dry look.

Lin smiled.

"At least your family is not trying to claim the silk stock."

"They have not established any claim to it."

"Good. I would dislike having to explain the partnership accounts to your relatives."

"So would I."

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received Mingzhao's reply.

He read it in silence.

Madam Liang sat across from him.

"What did she say?"

"She will attend the next meeting. She will not review the estate accounts."

Madam Liang looked dissatisfied.

"She knows the records better than anyone."

"Yes."

"Then why should we not ask her?"

Shen Guowei put the letter down.

"Because she is no longer responsible for the estate."

"She still has an interest in the settlement."

"That does not make her responsible for correcting our accounts."

Madam Liang looked at him.

"She could finish the work more quickly."

Shen Guowei sighed.

"That is precisely why we must stop asking her to do it."

Madam Liang did not reply.

He continued.

"We agreed that she would leave the household management. If we keep sending her every unfinished task, then we have not actually allowed her to leave."

Madam Liang looked toward the window.

"She has always been difficult."

"Yes."

"She insists on everything being written down."

"Yes."

"She questions every payment."

"Yes."

Madam Liang looked at him.

"You are agreeing with me?"

Shen Guowei considered this.

"She is difficult."

He paused.

"But the accounts were usually correct."

Madam Liang said nothing.

It was not an apology.

It was not reconciliation.

But it was an admission that the family's convenience had depended on a person whose labour they had rarely considered.

The next morning's administrative report arrived at the palace.

It contained no urgent matter.

The Emperor read it.

Gao Shun waited.

"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites has confirmed the requirements for the next ceremonial inventory."

"Good."

"The Palace Stores will maintain the physical count. The Department of Rites will maintain the ceremonial reference. The Imperial Household Administration will record purchases and repairs."

"Good."

"The three offices have agreed to exchange records every quarter."

The emperor nodded.

"That is sufficient."

Gao Shun hesitated.

"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites has asked whether the quarterly exchange should be supervised by the emperor."

The emperor slowly raised his eyes.

Gao Shun bowed.

"I have not answered."

"Good."

The emperor took a breath.

"Tell them that if three offices cannot exchange three sets of records without imperial supervision, the problem is not the absence of an empress."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

He turned to leave.

"Wait."

Gao Shun stopped.

"Tell them not to quote me in the next report."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The emperor returned to his work.

That afternoon, Yuru received a personal invitation from the Empress Dowager.

It was for tea the following day.

The message contained no official seal and no administrative attachment.

Yuru read it.

Fang Ru asked, "Will you attend?"

"Yes."

"Should we prepare a reply?"

Yuru nodded.

"Thank the Empress Dowager. I will attend."

Fang Ru accepted the message.

Then she asked, "Would you like me to prepare anything?"

Yuru looked at her.

"Only the usual things."

Fang Ru bowed.

For several moments, Yuru remained seated.

The next day would be a visit with the Empress Dowager.

The day after that, perhaps a visit to her natal family.

She had not yet decided where she would live permanently.

She had not yet decided whether she wished to travel.

She had not yet decided what work she might pursue.

For once, the questions did not require immediate answers.

She closed her book and looked toward the garden.

There was no official schedule waiting for her.

This was becoming a habit.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao entered the latest household expenses.

The cooking oil had been purchased.

The vegetable receipt had been verified.

The servants' wages were recorded.

The rent remained due according to the agreement.

She then reviewed the family settlement minutes once more.

The next meeting would not occur until the remaining records were prepared.

She placed the documents in their proper folders.

Qiao Yue entered.

"Miss, the landlord has sent a message."

Mingzhao looked up.

"About the rent?"

"No. He wants to know whether the rear drain has been inspected."

"It was inspected."

"Then he wants to know whether we are satisfied with it."

Mingzhao considered this.

"Tell him the drainage is functioning, but the lower room remains damp after heavy rain."

"Should we ask him to repair it?"

"Yes. The condition was recorded in the rental agreement."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Then she asked, "Will he agree?"

"If he does not, we will discuss the terms."

"And if he refuses?"

"Then we will consider the consequences."

Qiao Yue looked at her.

"You make everything sound like a settlement meeting."

Mingzhao returned to her ledger.

"Most disagreements become easier when the terms are clear."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Perhaps I should start writing down who borrowed the kitchen ladle."

Mingzhao looked up.

"Was it borrowed?"

Qiao Yue stopped.

Then she smiled.

"I will investigate."

Mingzhao returned to her work.

The ladle was eventually found beside the cooking pot.

No formal inquiry was necessary.

At least, not this time.

That evening, the Duke of Xu received the household's revised account summary.

The household accountant had separated the garden expenses from the ordinary maintenance expenses.

The damaged pavilion tiles were listed under repairs.

The new flower pots were listed under garden purchases.

The gardener's wages were recorded separately.

The Duke read the summary.

He found one discrepancy.

A payment for twelve taels had been entered under garden maintenance, but the supporting receipt described the purchase of ornamental stones.

He called the steward.

"Why is this classified as maintenance?"

The steward examined the record.

"It was placed there because the stones were used in the garden."

"That does not make them repairs."

The steward nodded.

"Should I ask Madam Xu's daughter to review it?"

The Duke looked at him.

The steward lowered his head.

"That was a poor question."

"Yes."

The Duke pointed to the record.

"Ask the accountant to correct the classification and attach the receipt."

The steward bowed.

The Duke looked at the document again.

He was beginning to discover that he could understand his own household accounts.

He had simply become accustomed to having someone else explain them.

The day ended without a major event.

The palace had continued its work.

The Xu household had corrected its garden accounts.

The Qingyuan Marquisate had begun reviewing its own transport expenses.

Mingzhao's rented residence had a functioning drain, although the lower room remained damp.

The silk partnership had delayed its next inspection rather than risk accepting defective goods.

And Yuru had received an invitation to tea that contained no request for a report.

For the first time in a long while, the two households were learning the same lesson from different directions.

A person could be capable of doing a task without being permanently responsible for it.

Unfortunately, this lesson had arrived after years of everyone else finding her usefulness extremely convenient.
