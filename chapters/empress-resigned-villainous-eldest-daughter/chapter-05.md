# **Chapter 5: The Crown Prince Has Been Given a Household Account Book. The Marquis's Heir Has Been Asked for Three Hundred Taels' Worth of Receipts.**

**27th Day of the Third Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Crown Prince had received many difficult assignments in his twenty-one years.

He had studied the classics under distinguished scholars, attended military briefings, reviewed provincial memorials, accompanied the emperor to ancestral ceremonies, and endured lengthy discussions about the proper conduct of a future sovereign.

None of those experiences had prepared him for the account book placed before him that morning.

It was thick.

It was bound in dark blue cloth.

It had three separate sections, two attached inventories, and a small paper slip marking a page near the middle.

Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an regarded it with the solemnity usually reserved for an important military dispatch.

Across from him, Emperor Zhao Jingheng drank his tea.

“Read it.”

Cheng'an opened the book.

“Father, this is the account of the Eastern Palace?”

“It is the account of the Eastern Palace's ordinary household expenditure.”

“Why has it been sent to me?”

“Because you are the master of the Eastern Palace.”

The Crown Prince looked down again.

“Has the Empress reviewed it?”

“She has not.”

“Then should I wait for her instructions?”

The emperor put down his cup.

“No.”

Cheng'an raised his eyes.

Zhao Jingheng regarded his eldest son.

“You are twenty-one. You will inherit the throne one day. You should understand the administration of your own household.”

“I understand the general arrangement.”

“Then you will have no difficulty.”

The Crown Prince looked at the account book.

He had the distinct impression that his father had just handed him a problem that had been quietly waiting for him since childhood.

“What am I expected to examine?”

“The ordinary household expenditure, the number of servants, the supplies received, the supplies distributed, and the authority responsible for approving them.”

“Should I examine the previous year's records as well?”

“Yes.”

“How far back?”

“Begin with the previous year. If you find a discrepancy that requires further investigation, you may request earlier records.”

Cheng'an nodded.

The emperor continued, “You are not to interfere with the Empress's review of the palace administration. You are to understand the responsibilities of your own household.”

“I understand.”

“And do not send every question to Chengming Palace.”

The Crown Prince paused.

“I had not intended to.”

“Good.”

Zhao Jingheng picked up another document.

The Crown Prince closed the account book.

He rose, bowed, and left the hall.

The emperor watched him go.

After a moment, he turned toward Chief Eunuch Gao Shun.

“Do you think he will manage?”

Gao Shun answered cautiously.

“His Highness is diligent.”

“That was not my question.”

Gao Shun lowered his head.

“Then perhaps Your Majesty should allow him to discover the answer himself.”

The emperor looked at the closed doors.

It was an irritatingly sensible suggestion.

The Eastern Palace had been operating under established arrangements for years.

Its senior steward, Liang Fu, was a careful man who understood the daily requirements of the Crown Prince's household. He knew how much grain was needed for the kitchens, how many attendants were assigned to the various courtyards, how often clothing was replaced, and which suppliers delivered goods on schedule.

He also knew that most of these matters had traditionally been reported through the Empress's office.

When the Crown Prince summoned him, Liang Fu arrived with the household ledgers.

“Your Highness.”

Cheng'an gestured toward the table.

“Sit and explain the accounts.”

Liang Fu looked surprised.

“Your Highness wishes to examine them personally?”

“Yes.”

“Should I first prepare a summary?”

“No. Bring the original records.”

The steward hesitated.

“Some records are quite extensive.”

“Then we will take the necessary time.”

Liang Fu bowed.

He opened the first ledger.

The Crown Prince began with the monthly grain allocation.

The figures appeared orderly. The kitchen received a fixed quantity, the attendants' quarters received another, and a small reserve was maintained for unexpected requirements.

Cheng'an compared the monthly totals.

“Why is the reserve allocation the same in every month?”

Liang Fu answered, “It is the established amount.”

“Does the household require the same reserve in every season?”

“The kitchen's requirements vary, but the reserve is maintained according to the annual allowance.”

“Then why does the record show that additional grain was purchased in the eighth month?”

Liang Fu looked at the page.

“That was an exceptional purchase.”

“Why?”

“The original allocation was insufficient.”

“Was the reserve exhausted?”

“I believe so.”

“Is there a record?”

Liang Fu searched through the attached papers.

After some time, he found a note.

“The reserve was used to replace damaged grain.”

“Why was the damaged grain not recorded?”

“It may have been included in the kitchen's monthly report.”

“May have been?”

Liang Fu bowed.

“I will verify it.”

Cheng'an nodded.

“Do so. I am not accusing anyone. I want to understand how the accounts were prepared.”

The steward looked relieved.

The Crown Prince continued.

The next section concerned clothing.

The records listed seasonal garments for the Crown Prince, his attendants, and the servants assigned to the Eastern Palace.

Cheng'an examined the quantities.

“Why were six sets of formal robes ordered for me last year?”

“Your Highness required garments for three ceremonial occasions, with replacements for damaged clothing.”

“Were all six used?”

Liang Fu turned to the inventory.

“Four were recorded as issued. Two remained in storage.”

“Then why were they included in the completed expenditure?”

“They were purchased, Your Highness.”

“I asked why they were recorded as completed expenditure rather than stored inventory.”

Liang Fu looked at the page.

He did not have an answer.

Cheng'an leaned back.

He was beginning to understand why his father had insisted on original records.

A summary could make an account appear complete even when the underlying process was not.

“Mark the two remaining garments as stored goods,” he said. “Do not count them as consumed.”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

The Crown Prince looked down at the ledger again.

He had reviewed provincial tax reports before.

Those reports had contained complicated figures, competing claims, and disputes between local officials.

Yet the account of his own household had somehow managed to become equally difficult.

He wondered how many such matters had been quietly resolved by the Empress.

He also wondered how often he had accepted the finished results without asking how they had been obtained.

At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru was reviewing the corrected responsibility register when Third Princess Zhao Minglan arrived.

The princess carried a narrow bundle of papers.

She bowed.

“Mother.”

Xu Yuru looked up.

“You have brought your account book again.”

Minglan placed it on the table.

“I have corrected the mistakes you pointed out yesterday.”

“Have you?”

“Yes.”

“Then show me.”

Minglan opened the book.

The entries were neater than before. The quantities were clearly marked, the prices had been written in a separate column, and the total had been calculated at the bottom of each page.

Xu Yuru examined the first page.

“Better.”

Minglan's face brightened.

Xu Yuru turned to the second page.

“Why have you written the total twice?”

Minglan's expression faltered.

“I wanted to make sure I had not made a mistake.”

“Did you check the figures?”

“Yes.”

“Then one total is sufficient. If you wish to verify it, use a separate calculation sheet.”

Minglan nodded.

Xu Yuru moved to the next page.

“Your quantities are correct. Your prices are not.”

Minglan leaned forward.

“Which one?”

“The price of lamp oil.”

“I used the amount from the previous month.”

“Was the previous month's price still valid?”

“I did not ask.”

“Then you do not know whether it was.”

Minglan looked down.

Xu Yuru did not scold her.

“An account is not merely a record of what someone remembers. It is a record of what can be verified.”

“I understand.”

“Good. Ask the storekeeper for the current purchase record. Do not alter the old entry. Add a note explaining the difference.”

Minglan nodded and began writing.

After a moment, she glanced at the responsibility register.

“Mother, is that the list of duties you are giving up?”

“It is the list of duties currently being reviewed.”

“Will all of them be transferred?”

“Some will be transferred. Some will remain with their existing departments. Some may no longer require anyone's approval.”

“Will you still supervise the palace?”

Xu Yuru considered the question.

“I have not yet decided the exact form of the transition.”

“But you want to leave.”

“Yes.”

Minglan looked at her.

“Are you angry with Father?”

“No.”

“Then why?”

Xu Yuru put down her brush.

“Because I have been doing too much for too long.”

Minglan lowered her eyes.

“Will the palace manage without you?”

“That is what we must find out.”

The princess looked at the register.

“Would it be difficult?”

“Very.”

“Then why not continue until it becomes easier?”

Xu Yuru regarded her daughter.

It was an understandable question.

It was also the question she had asked herself many times.

“Because if I continue every time something becomes difficult, the palace will never learn to manage without me.”

Minglan was silent.

Xu Yuru turned the account book toward her.

“Now, tell me what is wrong with this page.”

The princess examined it.

The conversation returned to lamp oil.

For the moment, that was enough.

The Qingyuan Marquisate had a less comfortable morning.

Shen Mingyuan was informed that the household office had sent another written request for the receipts relating to three hundred taels charged to his residence.

He read it once.

Then he threw it onto the table.

“Again?”

His personal attendant, who had been standing beside the door, lowered his head.

“The household office said the previous request was unanswered.”

“I know that.”

“The steward asked whether Your Young Master could provide the original receipts or identify the person who approved the expenditure.”

Mingyuan frowned.

“Why must I explain every small payment?”

“Because it is recorded under Your Young Master's residence.”

“Then the household office should already know what it was for.”

The attendant did not answer.

Mingyuan paced across the room.

He remembered the expenditure.

Some had been spent on repairs, some on furnishings, and some on a gathering attended by several of his friends. The money had been requested through the household office, and he had assumed that the matter had been settled.

He had not kept the receipts.

He had not thought it necessary.

His sister had always managed the accounts.

If something was missing, she would ask the steward. If the steward could not answer, she would find the relevant clerk. If a payment had been incorrectly recorded, she would correct it.

The system had been convenient.

Now it was asking him to explain himself.

“Send a reply,” he said.

“What should it say?”

“Tell them the expenditure was for household purposes.”

The attendant hesitated.

“Should I specify which purposes?”

Mingyuan glared at him.

“Do you think I remember every small purchase?”

The attendant bowed.

“I will write the reply as instructed.”

Mingyuan sat down.

He was beginning to feel that the entire estate had developed a personal interest in making his life difficult.

The reply arrived at the household office shortly before noon.

Steward Zhou read it.

Then he carried it to Mingzhao.

She examined the document.

It contained one sentence.

*The three hundred taels were expended on necessary household matters and should be recorded accordingly.*

Mingzhao read it twice.

Qiao Yue, standing nearby, looked at the paper.

“That is all?”

“That is all.”

“Does it say what was purchased?”

“No.”

“Does it include receipts?”

“No.”

“Does it identify who approved the expenditure?”

“No.”

Qiao Yue considered the matter.

“Perhaps the Second Young Master believes the word 'necessary' is sufficient.”

Mingzhao placed the reply on the table.

“Then he has misunderstood the purpose of an account.”

Steward Zhou waited.

Mingzhao took a fresh sheet.

“Reply that the household office is not disputing whether the expenditure was necessary. It is requesting the supporting records required to classify and verify the payment.”

She wrote the next sentence herself.

“If the original receipts are unavailable, he must provide a description of each expenditure, the approximate date, the recipient, and the person who authorized it.”

She passed the document to Steward Zhou.

“Also state that the amount will remain unverified until the supporting information is provided.”

Steward Zhou accepted it.

“Should the amount be deducted from his personal allowance?”

“No. Not at this stage.”

Qiao Yue looked at her.

“Why not?”

“Because an unverified expense is not automatically a personal debt. We do not yet know whether the money was spent on legitimate household repairs, personal purchases, or a mixture of both.”

Mingzhao returned to the ledger.

“We are examining accounts, not punishing people.”

Qiao Yue nodded.

Steward Zhou withdrew with the reply.

Mingzhao continued reviewing the records.

After a while, she found an old entry relating to a payment made to a carpenter.

The amount was modest, but the description was incomplete.

She marked it for verification.

Then she found another.

And another.

The three hundred taels were not the only problem.

They were merely the most visible one.

At the imperial palace, the Crown Prince's examination had produced a similar discovery.

By mid-afternoon, Zhao Cheng'an had identified three entries requiring further explanation.

The first concerned grain.

The second concerned clothing.

The third concerned lamp oil.

He had begun to suspect that the palace was governed by a mysterious principle under which every object became more complicated once it was written into an account book.

Liang Fu stood beside the table.

“Your Highness, the storekeeper has brought the purchase records.”

“Good. Ask him to wait.”

The Crown Prince compared the purchase record with the household ledger.

The lamp oil had been purchased at a different price from the previous month.

The difference was small.

The quantity, however, was larger than expected.

“Why was so much purchased?”

Liang Fu answered, “The household required additional oil for the winter ceremonies.”

“But this was purchased in the seventh month.”

“The preparations began early.”

“Were the ceremonies already scheduled?”

“Yes.”

“Was the quantity approved?”

“The purchase was made under the annual allowance.”

Cheng'an looked at the record.

“Then the purchase may have been correct. But where is the inventory showing how much remained from the previous year?”

Liang Fu searched the papers.

He found no such record.

The Crown Prince sighed.

“Ask the storekeeper to provide it.”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

He turned to the steward.

“Has the Eastern Palace previously submitted these records to Chengming Palace?”

“Yes.”

“Did the Empress's office examine them?”

“The consolidated records were submitted there.”

“Did the Empress personally examine them?”

Liang Fu hesitated.

“Your Highness, I cannot say which documents Her Majesty reviewed personally.”

The Crown Prince nodded.

That was a fair answer.

He had no reason to assume that every document passed through his mother's hands. He also had no reason to assume that the reports had been examined in sufficient detail.

He looked down at the account book.

“From now on, the Eastern Palace will maintain its own complete inventory. The storekeeper will record goods received and goods issued. The steward's office will compare the two records every month.”

Liang Fu bowed.

“Should the report be sent to Chengming Palace?”

“No. Send it to the authority designated under the revised procedure once the review is complete. Until then, I will examine the Eastern Palace accounts myself.”

Liang Fu looked slightly startled.

“Your Highness intends to review them personally every month?”

“For now.”

The Crown Prince closed the ledger.

He had not yet decided whether this was an efficient use of his time.

But he was beginning to understand that responsibility could not be delegated simply because one disliked the paperwork.

That evening, Emperor Zhao Jingheng received a report from the Crown Prince.

It contained three identified discrepancies, two requests for missing records, and a proposal to introduce monthly inventory comparisons in the Eastern Palace.

The emperor read it carefully.

“Did he find any evidence of deliberate misuse?”

“No, Your Majesty,” Gao Shun replied. “The Crown Prince has only identified incomplete records and differences in the way the accounts were prepared.”

“Good.”

The emperor put the report down.

“Did he complain?”

Gao Shun lowered his head.

“His Highness did not submit any complaint.”

“That is unfortunate.”

Gao Shun looked up slightly.

The emperor continued, “If he complains, I can tell him that administration is difficult. If he does not complain, I must assume he has accepted the work.”

Gao Shun wisely said nothing.

The emperor picked up the report again.

“Send word that I have read it. His proposal may be used for the Eastern Palace's own accounts, provided it does not conflict with the revised imperial household regulations.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

“And inform the Second Prince that he is to submit his household accounts as well.”

Gao Shun paused.

“His Highness the Second Prince?”

“Yes.”

“Should he be given the same instructions?”

“Why should he be treated differently?”

Gao Shun bowed.

“I will deliver the order.”

The emperor returned to his documents.

The Second Prince would not enjoy it.

That was not a reason to avoid it.

The following morning's official correspondence brought another development to the Qingyuan Marquisate.

The receiving estate had replied to Mingzhao's request for the northern warehouse delivery records.

Its steward confirmed that the estate had received grain from Qingyuan during the ninth and tenth months. However, the quantities listed in its records did not match the amounts recorded in the marquisate's monthly allocation ledger.

The letter included copies of the receiving entries.

Mingzhao read them carefully.

The ninth-month delivery was recorded as eighteen sacks.

The tenth-month delivery was recorded as fourteen.

She placed the documents beside the northern warehouse ledger.

The figures were not yet enough to explain the forty-seven-sack discrepancy.

But they established something important.

The difference was not simply a missing entry in one book.

The records on both sides disagreed.

“Have the original delivery receipts been sent?” she asked.

Steward Zhou shook his head.

“Only copies. The receiving estate says the originals are kept in its warehouse office.”

“Then request that the originals be inspected.”

“Yes.”

“Do not accuse them of withholding grain.”

“Understood.”

Mingzhao compared the signatures.

The receiving entries had been signed by two different clerks. One signature was clear. The other was difficult to read.

She marked both for examination.

Qiao Yue leaned closer.

“Could the grain have been stolen?”

“It could have.”

“Or?”

“Recorded incorrectly, delivered late, damaged, redirected, or counted twice.”

Qiao Yue looked at the papers.

“There seem to be many possibilities.”

“That is why we examine the records before deciding.”

Mingzhao put the documents into a separate folder.

The investigation would take time.

She was not pleased about that.

But she had spent six years watching people turn uncertainty into confident accusations whenever the truth was inconvenient.

She would not repeat the habit merely because she was now the person asking questions.

In the palace, the broom report finally arrived.

The Department of Palace Stores had submitted its opening inventory, purchase record, and distribution summary for the previous year.

The figures were not complete, but they were sufficient to reveal the first clear discrepancy.

The department had purchased more brooms than the number recorded as distributed.

The remaining stock was listed as zero.

No explanation was attached.

Grand Secretary Wei Zhong carried the report to the emperor.

Zhao Jingheng read it.

Then he read it again.

“Where are the remaining brooms?”

Wei Zhong replied, “The Department of Palace Stores has been asked to explain.”

“Does the distribution record account for all the purchases?”

“No, Your Majesty.”

“Does the inventory show the remaining stock?”

“No.”

The emperor put the paper down.

“Then we have purchased goods that cannot be accounted for.”

“That is what the records currently indicate.”

“Could they have been damaged?”

“Possibly.”

“Could they have been distributed without being recorded?”

“Possibly.”

“Could the original purchase quantity be wrong?”

“Possibly.”

The emperor stared at the report.

Wei Zhong stood silently.

After a moment, Zhao Jingheng asked, “Why is the palace unable to tell me what happened to its brooms?”

Wei Zhong answered with perfect seriousness.

“Because, Your Majesty, the current records were designed to show that the brooms had been purchased and distributed. They were not designed to establish whether every broom could be accounted for.”

The emperor closed his eyes.

It was not a satisfactory answer.

It was, unfortunately, a useful one.

“Continue the examination. No accusation is to be made until the records have been checked.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

“And do not ask the Empress to resolve it.”

“I would not presume to.”

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

“You have already sent her a register containing ninety-four responsibilities.”

“We have.”

“Do not let the number become ninety-five because someone cannot find a broom.”

Wei Zhong bowed.

“I will be careful, Your Majesty.”

At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru received the corrected register before the evening meal.

The document was longer than the previous draft.

It now included the office currently performing each task, the office legally authorized to perform it, the relevant regulations, and the proposed method of verification.

She read through the entries.

Several matters could be transferred without difficulty.

Others required revised instructions.

A few required the emperor's decision because they crossed the boundary between the inner palace and the civil administration.

She made several corrections.

Then she reached the final page.

There was a note concerning the imperial children's household accounts.

The Crown Prince had begun reviewing his own records. The Second Prince had been ordered to do the same. The Third Princess had requested permission to observe the revised procedures.

Xu Yuru paused.

She was pleased that the Crown Prince had begun examining his household affairs.

She was less certain about how the Second Prince would respond.

“Madam Fang.”

“Yes, Your Majesty?”

“Has the Second Prince received the emperor's instruction?”

“He has.”

“And?”

“His Highness has requested clarification on the extent of the examination.”

Xu Yuru looked at her.

“What clarification?”

“He wishes to know whether the examination includes his personal expenditure.”

Xu Yuru was silent for a moment.

Then she said, “The answer depends on what is being charged to the household account.”

Madam Fang nodded.

“Should I explain that to him?”

“Yes. If the expenditure is recorded as an official household expense, it must be supported by the relevant records. His private spending is a separate matter unless it has been charged to the household.”

Madam Fang bowed.

Xu Yuru returned to the register.

She had not yet resigned.

She had not yet been released from her duties.

But the work was beginning to move beyond her office.

That was what she had wanted.

It was also more difficult than she had imagined.

She had spent years correcting the palace's mistakes. Now she had to resist the temptation to correct every mistake herself.

She put down her brush.

“Send the revised register to Grand Secretary Wei.”

Madam Fang accepted it.

“And Madam Fang?”

“Yes, Your Majesty?”

“Do not let the departments use the new register as an excuse to delay their existing work.”

“I will remind them.”

Xu Yuru nodded.

Outside, the palace bells marked the end of the day's administrative hours.

In the Eastern Palace, the Crown Prince was still examining grain records.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao was comparing two versions of a warehouse ledger.

And somewhere in the Department of Palace Stores, an official was preparing to explain why a number of brooms had been purchased, distributed, and apparently vanished without leaving a sufficiently convincing paper trail.

The emperor had wanted the palace to function without relying on the Empress.

The Marquisate wanted Mingzhao to continue managing its affairs while the family discussed her departure.

Both institutions were discovering the same difficulty.

It was much easier to say that someone else should take responsibility than to decide exactly what that responsibility involved.

**End of Chapter 5.**
