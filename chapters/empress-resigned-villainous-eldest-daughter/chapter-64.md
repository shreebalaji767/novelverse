# **Chapter 64: The Former Empress Has Been Asked to Confirm Whether a Basket of Fruit Belongs to Her. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Confirm Whether a Family Debt Belongs to Her. The Palace Has Discovered That Fruit Is Easier to Classify Than Money, While the Marquisate Has Discovered That Neither Becomes Someone Else's Property Simply Because It Is Convenient.**

**28th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first problem Xu Yuru encountered that morning was a basket of pears.



It stood on the table beside the window.



The basket had arrived the previous evening from the Xu residence, carried by a household servant and received by Fang Ru.



The fruit had been placed with the other personal gifts.



Madam Zhao had subsequently asked for confirmation that it should be recorded as property belonging to the former Empress.



Yuru had confirmed this.



The matter should have ended there.



Unfortunately, the household clerk had entered the basket under the guest residence's food inventory.



Madam Zhao had brought the register for correction.



"Madam, the clerk has acknowledged the mistake."



Xu Yuru examined the entry.



"Why was it entered as guest residence food?"



"The basket was delivered to the residence, and the receiving servant signed the household receipt."



"Did the receipt identify the sender?"



"Yes. It records the Xu residence."



"Then the clerk had enough information."



Madam Zhao lowered her eyes.



"Yes, Madam."



Yuru handed the register back.



"Correct it. The fruit is mine. The kitchen may distribute it according to my instructions, but it should not become part of the guest residence's general stores."



"Understood."



Madam Zhao hesitated.



"There is another matter."



Yuru looked at her.



"The preserved vegetables?"



"Those have been entered correctly."



"Then what is it?"



"The honey jar."



Yuru paused.



Her father had attempted to send honey.



Her mother had removed it.



Apparently, the matter had not been entirely settled.



Madam Zhao continued carefully.



"The kitchen steward believes the jar was included in the basket when it was delivered."



"Was it?"



"Fang Ru's receiving note lists fruit and preserved vegetables. It does not mention honey."



"Then there is no honey to record."



Madam Zhao bowed.



"Yes, Madam."



When she left, Fang Ru looked at Yuru.



"Perhaps Duke Xu managed to put it in after all."



"Perhaps."



"Would you like me to ask the kitchen?"



"No. If the jar is there, it will eventually be found."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Then the palace will have to investigate the honey."



Yuru opened her book.



"Only if the honey submits a formal complaint."



Fang Ru laughed.



The former Empress returned to reading.



The morning had begun without a single memorial.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingzhao received a letter from the family representative.



It contained the revised schedule from the previous day's meeting.



She opened it at her temporary residence.



Qiao Yue placed tea beside her.



"Has the family finished the corrections?"



"They have corrected some of them."



"That sounds promising."



"It is incomplete."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"That sounds familiar."



Mingzhao began reading.



The nine confirmed advances were listed separately.



The four payment-confirmed entries with unclear repayment arrangements remained in a different section.



The ten uncertain entries were still marked as requiring evidence.



The seventy-two taels for study repairs had been reclassified as estate maintenance paid from Mingzhao's personal funds.



The additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The grain discrepancy was still listed separately from the blank-destination transport payment.



The northern canal property had been removed.



The western field arrangement was described as temporary cultivation.



Mingzhao read the schedule again.



Then she placed it beside her own copy.



"At least they have corrected the classifications."



Qiao Yue looked over her shoulder.



"Is it accurate?"



"Mostly."



"Mostly?"



"There is a new entry."



Mingzhao pointed to the final page.



The family had included an amount paid several years earlier to a supplier of household furnishings.



The payment had been made from an account that Mingzhao had managed.



The schedule described it as an advance made by her.



Mingzhao frowned.



"That payment was not mine."



Qiao Yue leaned closer.



"Did you not authorize it?"



"I authorized the payment from the estate account."



"Then why have they listed it under your advances?"



"Because the account was under my supervision."



Qiao Yue looked confused.



Mingzhao explained.



"An account being under my supervision does not mean the money in it belonged to me."



She opened her old ledger.



The entry was there.



The payment had been made from the estate's household furnishing account. The supplier's receipt identified the Marquisate as the purchaser. The goods had been delivered to the main residence.



There was no personal contribution.



No loan.



No reimbursement agreement.



No reason to include it among her advances.



Mingzhao placed the receipt beside the revised schedule.



"Add this to the correction list."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Will they argue?"



"Probably."



"Why?"



"Because the payment was made while I was managing the estate."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Then perhaps you should have managed the estate less efficiently."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"That would have produced a different dispute."



"At least the records would be shorter."



Mingzhao almost smiled.



"Prepare a copy of the receipt."

At the Marquisate, Mr Liang received Mingzhao's correction before midday.



He examined the furnishing payment.



The entry had been included because the family representative had found Mingzhao's name beside the account in an old management register.



The representative had assumed that the payment might be another personal advance.



Now the receipt showed otherwise.



Mr Liang called the clerk.



"Why was this included?"



The clerk pointed to the management register.



"Her name was recorded beside the account."



"Was the payment made from her personal funds?"



"No."



"Was there a loan agreement?"



"No."



"Then why was it listed as her advance?"



The clerk hesitated.



"Because she managed the account."



Mr Liang closed his eyes briefly.



He had heard this explanation before.



"Management is not ownership."



The clerk bowed.



"Yes, sir."



"Remove the entry from her advances. Retain it in the estate expenditure records."



"Yes."



Mr Liang looked at the schedule.



He was beginning to understand why Mingzhao had insisted on separate classifications.



The family had repeatedly treated her responsibility for an account as proof that she owned every amount moving through it.



That was convenient.



It was also wrong.

Shen Mingyuan was informed of the correction that afternoon.



He examined the receipt and the management register.



"The goods were delivered to the main residence," he said.



Steward Zhou De nodded.



"Yes. The furnishings are still in the eastern hall."



Mingyuan looked at the entry.



"Then it should never have been included in her personal advances."



Zhou De did not answer.



Mingyuan placed the papers aside.



He had inherited the expectation that his elder sister would manage the family estate.



He had also inherited the benefit of that arrangement.



Now he was discovering that the records of her work had become entangled with the records of her money.



He had once thought the distinction was obvious.



It was not.



He asked the steward to retrieve the original furnishing account and keep it with the estate expenditure records.



"Do not send the original to the representative," he instructed. "Send a certified copy."



"Yes, young master."



Mingyuan looked at the papers again.



"Also check whether any other estate expenses were entered as Mingzhao's personal advances simply because her name appeared in the management register."



Zhou De nodded.



That instruction would require time.



It might also produce more corrections.



Mingyuan was beginning to suspect that a family could spend years making the same mistake in different handwriting.

At the workshop, Lin Shuyun was waiting for the buyer's representative.



The next group of silk bolts had passed its internal inspection.



The revised folding method remained consistent with the approved sample.



The one corrected bolt from the previous group had been checked again.



Its edge now met the required standard.



No material had been discarded.



Mingzhao examined the inspection record.



"How many are ready for the buyer?"



"Forty-eight from the new order."



"All completed?"



"All completed and checked."



"Separate them from the unfinished stock."



Lin Shuyun gestured toward the marked storage area.



"They are already separated."



Mingzhao examined the tags.



Each bolt carried the order number, group number, finishing method, and inspection mark.



The buyer's representative arrived shortly afterward.



His name was Zhou Jian, and he had been sent by Zhou Wenqi to inspect the production.



He greeted the two women.



"Miss Shen. Miss Lin."



Mingzhao returned the greeting.



"Please examine the goods."



Zhou Jian inspected the first bolt.



He checked the edge, the fold, the surface, and the measurements.



He then compared it with the approved sample.



The second bolt was examined more quickly.



The third took longer because he wanted to check whether the revised folding method had produced any visible difference across the width.



He asked for the original sample.



Lin Shuyun brought it.



The two samples were placed side by side.



Zhou Jian examined them carefully.



"The appearance is consistent."



"Does it meet the approved standard?" Mingzhao asked.



"Yes."



He inspected several more bolts.



The process took nearly an hour.



When he finished, he placed his inspection notes on the table.



"Forty-eight accepted."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



Mingzhao nodded.



"Thank you. We will record the acceptance by group."



Zhou Jian looked at the remaining stock.



"How soon can the next group be ready?"



"That depends on production and inspection."



"Could it be faster?"



"It could be faster if we reduce inspection."



Zhou Jian looked at her.



Mingzhao continued.



"We will not reduce inspection."



The representative considered this.



"Then maintain the current schedule."



Lin Shuyun looked pleased.



The buyer had accepted the goods.



The revised method had passed another inspection.



The workshop had made progress.



But Mingzhao did not allow anyone to describe the entire order as nearly complete.



There were still one hundred and fifty-two bolts remaining.



There were wages to pay.



There were finishing costs, transport expenses, and the possibility of further corrections.



Acceptance of forty-eight bolts was good news.



It was not profit.

After the representative left, Lin Shuyun looked at the inspection record.



"Forty-eight."



"Yes."



"All accepted."



"Yes."



"You could sound more pleased."



"I am pleased."



"You look as though you are examining a tax notice."



Mingzhao put the document away.



"An accepted inspection is good. A completed order is better. A profitable order is better still."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



"Must everything be compared with profit?"



"No. But this is a business."



Lin Shuyun leaned against the worktable.



"The workers will be happy."



"They should be. Their work passed."



"And you?"



Mingzhao looked toward the production area.



"I will be happier when the cost statement is complete."



Lin Shuyun shook her head.



"You have a remarkable ability to postpone celebration."



"I prefer not to celebrate money I have not earned."

At the palace, Empress Dowager Wei received a message from the former Empress.



It was not a formal report.



It was a short personal note thanking the Dowager for the garden visit and mentioning that Yuru had enjoyed seeing her parents the previous day.



The Dowager read it twice.



Her attendant asked whether she wished to reply.



"Yes."



The Dowager thought for a moment.



"Tell her that I am glad she enjoyed her visit. And tell her that she may come to see me whenever she wishes."



The attendant prepared to write.



The Dowager added, "Do not say that I require her presence."



The attendant paused.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Dowager looked at her.



"That distinction matters."



The attendant bowed.



The Dowager had not entirely abandoned her old expectations.



She still believed that a former Empress's experience was valuable.



She still believed that Yuru understood the palace better than almost anyone.



But she had begun to recognize that appreciation and obligation were not the same thing.



She did not need to turn every invitation into a request.

The Emperor received a brief update from Gao Shun later that day.



"The former Empress has received a personal message from the Empress Dowager."



"Good."



"Madam Zhao has also completed the latest guest residence inventory."



"Any unresolved matters?"



"The ownership of the writing table and chairs remains uncertain. The robe purchased from palace stores is still awaiting the original order."



The Emperor nodded.



"Leave them in the palace inventory until the records are established."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And the household?"



"Operating normally."



The Emperor looked at Gao Shun.



"Then there is no need to trouble her."



Gao Shun bowed.



There had been a time when the Emperor would have considered asking Yuru to review the inventory personally.



Now he understood that the inventory belonged to the household administration.



The household administration had people responsible for it.



They could make mistakes.



They could correct them.



They could also ask for assistance through the proper channels if necessary.



It was not a complicated principle.



It had simply taken the palace a long time to practice it.

In the afternoon, Xu Yuru received the Dowager's reply.



Fang Ru read it aloud.



Yuru smiled.



"That is kind of Her Majesty."



"Would you like to visit her?"



"Not today."



Fang Ru folded the message.



"Shall I reply?"



"Yes. Thank her. Tell her I will visit when an opportunity arises."



Fang Ru prepared the reply.



Yuru returned to her book.



After a moment, she looked up.



"Fang Ru."



"Madam?"



"Do you think Mother will send more fruit?"



Fang Ru considered the question seriously.



"Probably."



"Then we should make room."



"Yes, Madam."



"And if Father sends honey?"



Fang Ru lowered her head.



"I shall make a separate receiving note."



Yuru laughed.

At the Marquisate, Mingzhao's correction list was ready by evening.



The furnishing payment had been removed from her personal advances and returned to the estate expenditure section.



She reviewed the remaining entries.



There were still unresolved matters.



The nine confirmed advances required settlement.



The four payment-confirmed entries required clarification of repayment arrangements.



The ten uncertain entries required evidence.



The seventy-two taels for study repairs remained estate maintenance paid from her personal funds, with reimbursement still undecided.



The additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.



The blank-destination transport payment remained separate.



The northern canal property was still outside the division.



The western field remained under temporary cultivation.



Mingzhao closed the schedule.



Qiao Yue brought her supper.



"Will the family finish the property division soon?"



"No."



"Do you think they will?"



"Eventually."



Qiao Yue placed the bowl on the table.



"That does not sound reassuring."



"It is not meant to be."



Mingzhao began eating.



After a while, Qiao Yue asked, "Are you satisfied with today's corrections?"



Mingzhao thought about it.



"The schedule is more accurate than yesterday."



"That is not quite the same as being satisfied."



"No."



Qiao Yue nodded.



She understood.



The family was still arguing over property.



The workshop was still producing silk.



The temporary residence still needed watching.



The northern canal property remained uncertain.



And Mingzhao had not yet reached the point where she could stop thinking about the Marquisate's records.



But she had not returned to manage them.



That distinction mattered.

Before retiring, Mingzhao received a short note from Lin Shuyun.



The buyer had confirmed acceptance of the forty-eight bolts.



The workshop's production record had been updated.



The next group would continue under the approved method.



Mingzhao placed the note beside the cost ledger.



She did not write a profit figure.



There was not enough information to calculate one.



She did, however, mark the inspection as completed.



It was a small entry.



It was also a correct one.



In the palace, the basket of fruit had finally been recorded under the former Empress's personal property.



The honey had not appeared.



At the Marquisate, the furnishing payment had been removed from Mingzhao's personal advances.



The family had not yet finished correcting the schedule.



At the workshop, forty-eight bolts had passed inspection.



One hundred and fifty-two remained.



Three separate matters had moved forward.



None had reached completion.



And in all three places, someone had discovered that the easiest way to make a record look finished was to stop asking questions.



Fortunately, the people responsible for these records had begun asking more of them.
