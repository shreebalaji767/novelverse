# **Chapter 51: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Stay for Breakfast. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Inspect a Field. One Invitation Comes with Freshly Prepared Porridge. The Other Comes with a Tenant Who Has Not Paid Rent in the Usual Way.**

**13th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress woke to the sound of birds in the courtyard.



It was a pleasant morning.



The weather was clear, the curtains moved gently in the breeze, and the servants had prepared tea before she rose.



Fang Ru entered with a folded letter.



"Madam, a letter arrived from the Duke's residence."



Yuru took it.



The handwriting belonged to Madam Xu.



The letter was short.



She thanked Yuru for coming to dinner the previous evening and invited her to return for breakfast whenever convenient. There was no formal date, no list of arrangements, and no mention of household matters.



Yuru read it twice.



"Mother writes as though I might forget where the residence is."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Perhaps she wants to make certain you know you are welcome."



"I know."



Yuru folded the letter.



"Send a reply. I will visit again when I have settled the arrangements here."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Would you like to return this week?"



"Not yet. I have several personal matters to arrange."



Fang Ru accepted the instruction.



Yuru looked at the small table beside the window.



There were books, a writing brush, and a list of belongings still awaiting confirmation.



Her life had become quieter.



It had not become entirely simple.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan was preparing to visit the western field.



The old lease had expired.



The tenant continued cultivating the land.



The spring rent had been accepted.



No renewed lease had been found.



The matter had been recorded in the revised property schedule as an arrangement requiring confirmation.



Mingyuan had read that line several times.



He now understood why Sun Qiming disliked vague descriptions.



A field could be cultivated.



A payment could be received.



Neither fact, by itself, established the legal terms under which the land was being used.



He called Steward Zhou.



"Has the tenant been informed that we wish to speak with him?"



"Yes, Young Master. He will meet us at the field."



"Did he bring the old lease?"



"He says he has a copy, though he does not know whether it is complete."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Bring the estate records as well."



Steward Zhou hesitated.



"Should I inform the eldest young lady?"



"No."



The answer came quickly.



Mingyuan looked at him.



"This is our responsibility. We should not send every question to my sister."



Steward Zhou lowered his head.



"Understood."



Mingyuan took the relevant papers.



He had no intention of making the meeting difficult.



He also had no intention of agreeing to something simply because it would make the morning shorter.

Mingzhao was already awake.



Her temporary residence had become more familiar over the previous weeks.



The repaired rear drain had worked properly during the last rainfall. The lower room remained slightly damp, but the problem had not worsened.



The kitchen shelves were stable.



The upper latch no longer required repeated lifting.



These were modest improvements.



They were also the sort of improvements that had once been treated as insignificant because someone else was expected to arrange them.



Qiao Yue entered carrying a breakfast tray.



"Miss, the landlord's servant has come."



Mingzhao looked up.



"What does he want?"



"He says the landlord wishes to know whether the lower room requires another inspection."



Mingzhao considered the question.



"Tell him the room should be checked after the next heavy rain. The drain has been repaired, but the dampness has not disappeared."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"And the rent?"



"Not due yet."



"I told him that."



"Good."



Qiao Yue placed the tray on the table.



"There is also a letter from Master Luo."



Mingzhao opened it.



The property assessor had obtained another response from the local land office.



The office had located a reference to the neighbouring parcel's registration, but the certified copy had not yet been issued.



The old drainage maintenance entry remained incomplete.



It recorded joint work on the channel approximately twelve years earlier, but did not establish whether the work had changed the legal boundary.



Master Luo requested additional time.



Mingzhao wrote a short reply.



She would wait for the certified record.



She would not pay a deposit.



She would not treat a maintenance entry as a transfer of ownership.



Qiao Yue watched her write.



"That house has required more paperwork than the house you are currently renting."



"It has a disputed boundary."



"Your current house has a damp room."



"The damp room has a landlord."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"That is an advantage."



"It is."

At the western field, Mingyuan found the tenant waiting beside a low wooden cart.



The man bowed.



"Young Master."



Mingyuan returned the greeting.



"I understand you have continued cultivating the field."



"Yes."



"And that you paid the spring rent."



"Yes."



Mingyuan opened his papers.



"The previous lease expired last year."



The tenant nodded.



"I know."



"Was a new agreement made?"



The man hesitated.



"The steward said I could continue until the family decided what to do with the land."



Mingyuan looked towards Steward Zhou.



The steward's expression changed.



"I remember discussing the matter," he said carefully. "I do not recall issuing a new lease."



The tenant reached into his coat and produced a folded paper.



"I kept this."



Mingyuan examined it.



It was a copy of the previous lease, with a handwritten note on the reverse.



The note acknowledged receipt of the spring payment and stated that cultivation could continue temporarily pending further arrangements.



It did not specify a new rental period.



It did not establish a new annual rate.



It did not provide a long-term renewal.



Mingyuan handed it to Sun Qiming, who had accompanied him to examine the records.



Sun read it.



"This supports the tenant's explanation of temporary permission. It does not establish a renewed lease."



The tenant looked worried.



"Does that mean I must leave?"



Mingyuan considered the question.



He had not come to remove the tenant.



He had come to establish the arrangement.



"No decision has been made to end your cultivation. But the family must clarify the terms."



The tenant nodded slowly.



"Then I would like to continue."



"That is something we can discuss after reviewing the land's future use and the rent."



Sun added, "The payment already received must also be classified correctly."



Mingyuan looked at the paper again.



"Prepare a copy. The original will remain with the tenant."



Steward Zhou nodded.



The tenant appeared relieved.



Mingyuan did not promise him a new lease.



He also did not treat him as an unlawful occupant.



For the first time, the young master understood that a family property could not be managed simply by deciding who was allowed to remain on it.



There were agreements, records, expectations, and consequences.

At the palace, the Crown Prince was reviewing the Eastern Palace's monthly supply report.



The report was not particularly complicated.



It listed rice, oil, charcoal, paper, and other ordinary provisions.



However, one item had been entered under two different headings.



A delivery of lamp oil appeared once in the kitchen supply register and again in the general maintenance summary.



The Crown Prince examined the supporting receipt.



There was only one payment.



He called the responsible clerk.



"Why is this recorded twice?"



The clerk bowed.



"The kitchen register records the delivery. The maintenance summary includes the same purchase because the oil was issued to several rooms."



"Then the summary should refer to the original delivery record."



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Do not record it as a second purchase."



The clerk bowed again.



The Crown Prince marked the report.



He had learned this lesson from Yuru.



A repeated entry did not necessarily mean a repeated payment.



But a record that failed to distinguish between the two could create unnecessary confusion.



He handed the corrected report to his secretary.



"Return it to the office for amendment."



The secretary accepted it.



The Crown Prince looked at the remaining papers.



There were still many things he did not understand.



That was no longer an excuse to leave them untouched.

The Second Prince had a different problem.



He had received a request for additional funds for a private gathering.



The request listed food, decorations, musicians, and additional attendants.



He examined the figures.



The amount was higher than he expected.



He called the clerk responsible for the estimate.



"Why are there so many attendants?"



"The original arrangement included twelve."



"Why does this version include twenty?"



"The organizer believed the gathering might be larger."



"Has the number of guests increased?"



"No, Your Highness."



The Second Prince stared at the paper.



"Then why have the attendants increased?"



The clerk had no answer.



The prince put down his brush.



"Return the estimate. Ask for the actual number of guests and the necessary arrangements."



The clerk bowed.



The Second Prince leaned back.



He had once assumed that approving an expenditure was the same as authorizing an event.



Now he was beginning to understand that the two were not identical.



He still disliked the process.



He disliked it less than the prospect of being questioned about twenty attendants at a gathering for twelve guests.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingxiu was examining the southern building.



The roof required repair.



The structure itself had been assessed as sound, but the existing estimate did not specify how many tiles needed replacement.



She had asked the repair contractor to inspect it again.



The contractor stood in the courtyard, pointing towards the roof.



"These sections require replacement. The rest can be repaired."



Mingxiu looked up.



"How many tiles?"



"Approximately sixty."



"Approximately?"



"Some may break when removed."



"Then write an estimated range."



The contractor frowned.



"That is difficult."



"Then write the number you can confirm and explain what may change."



He considered this.



"That can be done."



Mingxiu nodded.



She was not yet comfortable negotiating repair estimates.



She was learning that the first number offered was not necessarily the final cost.



She asked the steward to record the inspection date and the contractor's name.



Then she returned to the household office.



Madam Liang was waiting.



"Have you finished?"



"Not yet. The contractor will provide a revised estimate."



Madam Liang sighed.



"Your father says the next meeting may be delayed."



"Until the supporting records are ready."



"Your sister would have finished this more quickly."



Mingxiu looked at her mother.



"Perhaps."



Madam Liang appeared surprised by the answer.



Mingxiu continued.



"But she would also have asked why the estimate was incomplete."



Madam Liang did not respond.



Mingxiu returned to her papers.



She had no intention of becoming her sister.



She did, however, want to stop being the person who merely waited for her sister to finish everything.

In the afternoon, Mingzhao received Sun Qiming's report.



The accountant had reviewed the original inventory headings.



The two warehouse books did indeed use different counting categories.



One included total stock.



The other recorded grain available for immediate distribution.



This meant that comparing their totals without reconciliation had been improper.



However, the finding did not resolve the forty-seven-sack discrepancy.



Sun had requested the original supporting counting sheets.



He had also asked for the movement records relating to the twenty sacks.



Mingzhao read the report carefully.



She wrote beneath the final paragraph:



*The distinction between total stock and available stock must be reconciled. It does not establish the location or disposition of the forty-seven sacks. Continue tracing the movement instruction and the remaining quantities separately.*



She signed it.



Qiao Yue glanced at the page.



"Does this mean the grain has been found?"



"No."



"Does it mean the accounts were wrong?"



"It means the accounts used different categories."



"Is that better?"



"It is more accurate."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"You and the accountant have very similar answers."



Mingzhao put the report aside.



"That is why I hired him."

Later that afternoon, Lin Shuyun sent word that the proposed two-hundred-bolt contract was ready for final execution.



The buyer had confirmed the deposit amount.



The four additional workers had been selected.



Two would require training before they could work independently.



The revised production schedule included their training period.



Mingzhao reviewed the final draft.



She checked the delivery stages, inspection rules, payment terms, correction periods, and responsibility for transport.



Then she examined the clause concerning delayed delivery.



The buyer had proposed that any delay caused by the workshop would result in a reduction in the payment for the affected group.



Mingzhao frowned.



She called Lin.



"This clause is too broad."



Lin read it.



"It does not distinguish between a delay caused by the workshop and one caused by the buyer's failure to inspect."



"Exactly."



Lin considered the wording.



"We can amend it."



"Add a separate provision for delays caused by the buyer's inspection schedule. Neither side should be responsible for a delay caused by the other."



Lin nodded.



"That is reasonable."



Mingzhao returned the contract.



"Once both sides sign the amended version and the deposit is received, production may begin."



Lin smiled.



"You have become very cautious."



"I have become familiar with the cost of vague agreements."



Lin laughed.



"That is a useful education."



The two women arranged for the amended contract to be signed the following day.



Until then, the additional order remained proposed.



The existing partnership accounts remained separate.

That evening, Yuru received another personal visit from the Third Princess.



Minglan brought a small notebook.



"I have a question."



Yuru looked up from her book.



"Ask."



Minglan opened the notebook.



"I found two entries in the library catalogue that may refer to the same book."



"Why?"



"The titles differ by one character, but the author and subject appear similar."



"Have you checked the first pages?"



"One has a damaged title page. The other has an old borrowing mark."



Yuru examined the entries.



"Do the publication dates match?"



"Not exactly. One record gives the year of the author's death, not the year of publication."



Minglan looked surprised.



"How did you know?"



"Because the catalogue has copied the wrong date from an earlier note."



Yuru returned the notebook.



"Do not merge the entries yet. Find the oldest catalogue that lists either book. Then compare the binding, opening lines, and any surviving borrowing records."



Minglan wrote this down.



"That will take time."



"Yes."



"Could I simply ask the librarian?"



"You should ask. But you should also examine the evidence."



Minglan nodded.



She closed the notebook.



"Thank you."



Yuru smiled.



"You're welcome."



Minglan hesitated.



"Are you enjoying your new life?"



Yuru looked towards the window.



The question was simple.



She did not answer immediately.



"I am learning how to live it."



Minglan nodded.



"That sounds difficult."



"It is less difficult than I expected."



"Then I hope it becomes easier."



Yuru smiled.



"So do I."

At the end of the day, the palace had corrected a duplicate expenditure entry.



The Crown Prince had returned an inflated estimate.



The Second Prince had learned that a gathering for twelve people did not require twenty attendants.



The Third Princess had discovered that a library catalogue could contain two different kinds of mistakes.



At the Marquisate, Mingyuan had clarified the western field's temporary cultivation arrangement without promising a new lease.



Mingxiu had obtained a more precise roof repair estimate.



Mingzhao had kept her disputed property, uncertain grain records, and proposed commercial order separate from matters already established.



No one had solved everything.



But fewer people had pretended that an incomplete record was a completed decision.



The former Empress had received an invitation to breakfast.



The Marquis's eldest daughter had received a tenant's explanation.



One invitation required a reply.



The other required a proper agreement.



And in both households, someone was finally learning that a polite answer was not the same as a binding commitment.
