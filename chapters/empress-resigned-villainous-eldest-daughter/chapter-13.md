# **Chapter 13: The Empress Has Been Given a Choice Between Two Committees. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Given a Choice Between Two Bad Houses.**

**5th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Emperor had discovered that the palace could produce two committees for almost any problem.

It could also produce a third committee to investigate why the first two had failed to reach an agreement.

He was beginning to suspect that this was not the most efficient way to govern.

The latest example arrived shortly after the morning audience.

The Imperial Household Administration had submitted a proposal concerning the supervision of palace residences.

It contained two recommendations.

The first was to establish a permanent committee under the Empress's office.

The second was to establish a permanent committee under the Imperial Household Administration, with the Empress serving as its senior supervisor.

The Emperor read both recommendations.

He put them down.

Then he called Gao Shun.

"Who wrote this?"

"The Imperial Household Administration, Your Majesty."

"Who approved it?"

"The senior administrator."

"Who reviewed it?"

"The Department of Rites and the Ministry of Revenue."

"Who proposed the first committee?"

"The Department of Rites."

"And the second?"

"The Imperial Household Administration."

Zhao Jingheng looked at the report again.

"Why are there two?"

Gao Shun answered cautiously.

"The departments have different opinions regarding the appropriate authority."

"Then they should determine the appropriate authority."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Instead, they have proposed two committees."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The Emperor picked up his brush.

He wrote a short instruction across the top of the report.

*Return for clarification. Identify the existing legal authority and the office responsible for ordinary supervision. Do not propose a new committee until the need for one has been demonstrated.*

He handed it to Gao Shun.

"Send it back."

Gao Shun accepted the report.

The Emperor continued reading.

After a moment, he said, "Gao Shun."

"Your Majesty?"

"If they return with three committees, I will make them responsible for counting the members."

Gao Shun lowered his head.

"Your Majesty is wise."

The Emperor looked at him.

"That was not an invitation to praise me."

"No, Your Majesty."

At Chengming Palace, Yuru received a different proposal.

It concerned the supervision of the palace's charitable distribution fund.

The fund had existed for many years. It provided assistance to retired attendants, elderly servants without family support, and certain dependants of palace workers who had died in service.

The money came from an allocated portion of the imperial household budget, supplemented by occasional donations.

The fund was not enormous.

However, it required careful records because the recipients' circumstances changed. Some recovered financially. Some died. Some moved away. Others became eligible after years of service.

The existing arrangement was simple in principle.

The Empress's office reviewed the annual distribution list and approved exceptional assistance.

The Imperial Household Administration maintained the accounts.

The charitable fund's clerks recorded payments.

The problem was that the annual list had become a document that everyone expected Yuru to examine personally.

She read the proposal.

It recommended that the Empress continue approving the list, while the Imperial Household Administration handled all other matters.

Yuru looked at Fang Ru.

"Why?"

Fang Ru examined the paper.

"The fund has traditionally been associated with the Empress's charitable responsibilities."

"That explains the tradition. It does not explain why the office cannot verify the list."

"The administrators may be concerned about approving assistance to people whose circumstances are not fully documented."

"Then they should improve the documentation."

"Some recipients are elderly and cannot provide written records."

Yuru paused.

"That is a legitimate difficulty."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Some have no surviving family members. Others have worked in the palace for decades but have no complete service records."

Yuru looked at the list.

This was not a matter that could be resolved by demanding more paperwork from people who had no way to produce it.

"Who confirms their circumstances now?"

"The senior attendants' office, usually through the residential supervisors."

"Do they know the recipients personally?"

"Most of them."

"Then their testimony should be recorded."

Fang Ru considered this.

"Would that be sufficient?"

"Not by itself in every case. But it is evidence. A written statement from the supervisor, a service record if available, and confirmation of the recipient's circumstances should be enough for ordinary cases. Exceptional cases can be reviewed separately."

Yuru turned to the proposal.

"The question is not whether I should continue approving every name. The question is how the fund can distinguish genuine need from incomplete records without excluding the people it was created to help."

She placed the document aside.

"Arrange a meeting with the fund's clerks and the senior attendants' office."

Fang Ru hesitated.

"Would Your Majesty attend?"

"I will attend the first discussion."

"To approve the revised arrangement?"

"To hear what information they actually have."

Yuru looked at the report again.

"After that, they must decide within their authority."

The meeting took place in the afternoon.

The fund's chief clerk, an elderly man named Xu Liang, arrived carrying three ledgers.

He had worked in the palace for twenty-eight years and had developed a deep distrust of new procedures.

Not because he disliked improvement.

He disliked procedures that looked elegant on paper and became impossible when applied to people.

He bowed to Yuru.

"Your Majesty."

"Sit."

Xu Liang placed the ledgers on the table.

The first contained the annual list of recipients.

The second contained payments.

The third contained special assistance.

Yuru opened the first.

"How many recipients?"

"Forty-six, Your Majesty."

"How many have complete service records?"

"Thirty-one."

"And the remaining fifteen?"

"Some records were lost during earlier transfers. Several attendants served in private residences before the current records were established. Two were widowed dependants of palace workers. One is an elderly woman whose original service record was never entered into the central register."

Yuru examined the list.

"How do you confirm their eligibility?"

Xu Liang answered without hesitation.

"The residential supervisors know them. They can confirm their service and present circumstances."

"Is that recorded?"

"Usually."

"Usually?"

"Sometimes the confirmation is written on the back of the distribution sheet."

Yuru looked at him.

"Why?"

Xu Liang looked mildly uncomfortable.

"Because the printed form has no space for it."

There was a pause.

Fang Ru glanced at the form.

The observation was so simple that it was difficult to object to.

Yuru took a blank sheet.

"Then the form is inadequate."

Xu Liang seemed relieved.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Do not create a complicated form. Add a section for the supervisor's confirmation, the basis of the confirmation, and the date."

Xu Liang nodded.

"What about recipients who cannot sign?"

"Record the payment in the presence of two witnesses."

"And if they cannot travel to the distribution office?"

"Then the authorized representative should deliver the payment and obtain confirmation of receipt."

Xu Liang hesitated.

"That would require additional travel."

"How much?"

He gave an estimate.

The cost was modest, but it was not negligible.

Yuru listened.

The fund could accommodate the expense if the deliveries were grouped by district.

"Then group them," she said.

Xu Liang looked down at the ledger.

There was another matter.

"Your Majesty, there are occasional requests for emergency assistance."

"How many?"

"Five last year."

"What happened?"

"Two recipients required urgent medical treatment. One lost her room after the landlord died. One elderly attendant needed food after her nephew stopped sending support. Another required travel to return to her home district."

"Who approved them?"

"Your Majesty's office."

"How long did it take?"

Xu Liang hesitated.

"Usually several days."

Yuru looked at him.

"Several days?"

"The requests had to be verified."

"Could the fund provide a small emergency allowance under a fixed limit?"

Xu Liang considered the idea.

"If the circumstances are confirmed by a residential supervisor and the payment is recorded afterward, yes."

"Then propose a limit."

He looked surprised.

"Your Majesty?"

"A limit. A person authorized to release the funds. A record of the reason and amount. A time limit for submitting the supporting documents afterward."

Xu Liang nodded slowly.

The meeting continued.

They discussed the eligibility list, incomplete service records, payment receipts, emergency assistance, and annual review.

By the end, they had identified a procedure that did not require Yuru to approve every ordinary payment.

She reviewed the draft.

Then she put down her brush.

"One more question."

The clerks looked up.

"What happens when a recipient no longer qualifies?"

Xu Liang answered, "The payment is stopped."

"Who informs the fund?"

"The residential supervisor."

"And who confirms the change?"

"The senior attendants' office."

"How often?"

"At the monthly review."

Yuru nodded.

"Then include it."

She rose.

"The fund exists to help people. Do not turn its records into a barrier that prevents assistance. But do not treat money as though it does not require accounting."

Xu Liang bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru left the room.

Fang Ru followed her.

"Your Majesty, the procedure still requires your approval for the emergency limit."

"Only the limit?"

"Yes."

"Then I will review that once the administration submits the proposed amount."

Fang Ru looked at her.

"Would that not be an additional responsibility?"

Yuru smiled faintly.

"Some responsibilities must be transferred in stages. I am not going to pretend that an old arrangement can be replaced in a single afternoon."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had been presented with two houses.

Neither was satisfactory.

The first was the northern canal residence she had been considering.

The second was a smaller courtyard property in the eastern district.

The northern residence had a clear title but required substantial repairs.

The eastern property was cheaper, closer to the commercial street, and apparently in better condition.

It also had an unresolved boundary issue.

Mingzhao sat with Qiao Yue and examined the documents.

"How serious is the boundary issue?"

"The seller says the neighbouring property has used a narrow strip of the rear courtyard for storage."

"How long?"

"Several years."

"With whose permission?"

"The seller says it was temporary."

"That is not an answer."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"The neighbouring owner says the arrangement was agreed by the previous owner."

"Is there a written agreement?"

"No."

"Is there a survey?"

"No."

"Then we do not know where the boundary is."

Qiao Yue looked at the price.

"It is considerably cheaper."

"Of course."

"Young Lady, it is also closer to the market."

"An advantage."

"And the building is in better condition."

"Possibly."

Qiao Yue looked at her.

"Is there anything about it that you like?"

"The price."

"That is all?"

"The courtyard is practical."

"Then why not buy it?"

Mingzhao placed the documents on the table.

"Because I do not want to purchase a dispute."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"The northern house is too expensive. The eastern house has a boundary dispute. The smaller residence near the western district has no private well."

"Correct."

"At this rate, we will be living in the road."

"Only if the road has a clear title."

Qiao Yue stared at her.

Mingzhao returned to the papers.

She was not trying to be difficult.

A house was not simply a building. It was a collection of obligations.

If the title was unclear, the problem could become a legal dispute.

If the roof was damaged, the repairs could consume her reserve.

If the well was shared, she might have to negotiate with neighbours over daily use.

If the location was inconvenient, the cost of transporting goods and maintaining the household could rise.

She wanted independence.

She did not want a new collection of expensive dependencies.

"Ask the eastern seller to produce the original survey and any written agreement concerning the courtyard."

"And the northern house?"

"Ask for the second carpenter's inspection."

"Will we make an offer?"

"Not yet."

Qiao Yue collected the documents.

"Young Lady, the sellers may become impatient."

"Then they may sell to someone else."

Qiao Yue nodded.

She had begun to recognize that Mingzhao was willing to lose an opportunity rather than accept an obligation she did not understand.

It was not always convenient.

It was, however, less expensive than some of her previous decisions.

In the afternoon, Sun Qiming arrived with a new development.

The former northern warehouse steward, Zhang, had found a small bundle of papers.

They were not the complete movement register.

They were copies of work instructions and several notes written during the warehouse repairs.

Mingzhao opened them.

The first document recorded the temporary clearing of one storage section.

The second listed the workers assigned to move grain.

The third contained a partial count.

She read the number.

"Twenty sacks?"

"That is what the note says," Sun Qiming replied.

"Where were they moved?"

"The document identifies the southern storage building."

"Was it part of the same estate?"

"Yes."

"Was it included in the original warehouse inventory?"

"That is what we need to establish."

Mingzhao looked at the note again.

"Does the southern storage building have a receiving record?"

"There is a separate ledger. I have requested it."

"How many sacks were recorded there?"

"That has not yet been confirmed."

She placed the document beside the northern warehouse ledger.

Twenty sacks.

The discrepancy was forty-seven.

Even if the twenty had been moved temporarily, the remaining twenty-seven still required explanation.

And a movement instruction did not prove that all twenty sacks arrived.

"Do not mark the twenty as accounted for yet."

"I would not."

"Record it as a documented movement instruction, with receipt unconfirmed."

Sun Qiming made the note.

Mingzhao leaned back.

It was progress.

Not a resolution.

She found that the distinction mattered more than she had expected.

For weeks, the family had spoken of the missing grain as though there were only two possibilities: theft or an accounting mistake.

Now there was evidence of a third possibility.

A temporary movement that had not been properly reconciled.

That did not explain everything.

But it changed the questions that needed to be asked.

"Ask Zhang whether he remembers who supervised the southern storage building."

Sun Qiming nodded.

"And ask the current steward for the receiving ledger."

"Already requested."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"You are becoming efficient."

"I am trying to finish the examination."

"Those are not necessarily the same thing."

Sun Qiming gave her a brief, dry smile.

"That is true."

The same evening, Shen Mingyuan received the latest request from the accountant.

The southern storage ledger.

He read the note.

Then he looked at Steward Zhou.

"Why is this being sent to me?"

"The former warehouse steward believes you may remember the arrangement."

"I was not responsible for the warehouse."

"No, Young Master."

"Then why?"

"Because the repairs were discussed in the family. You may remember who was present."

Mingyuan looked at the request again.

He remembered a meeting.

His father had been present.

Mingzhao had been present.

The former steward had spoken about the roof.

He also remembered being irritated because the discussion had interrupted a gathering he had planned.

He did not remember the number of sacks.

He did remember that his father had told the steward to move the grain before the roof work began.

Mingyuan sat down.

He wrote:

*I remember that Father instructed the warehouse steward to move grain before the northern warehouse repairs. I do not remember the quantity. I was not present during the movement and did not inspect the receiving store.*

He paused.

Then he added:

*I cannot confirm whether the twenty sacks listed in the surviving note were the only grain moved.*

He signed the statement.

Steward Zhou accepted it.

Mingyuan looked at the paper.

"Will this be enough?"

"It will be added to the examination."

"That is not what I asked."

Steward Zhou considered his answer.

"It is enough to record what you remember."

Mingyuan leaned back.

He was beginning to understand that an honest answer could still be unsatisfactory.

He disliked that.

But he disliked the possibility of being accused of inventing a number even more.

At Chengming Palace, the Emperor received the revised report on the palace charitable fund.

The proposal was shorter than the previous version.

It included an updated eligibility list, written confirmation from residential supervisors, two witnesses for recipients unable to sign, grouped home delivery, a monthly reconciliation, and a limited emergency allowance.

It also included a proposed spending limit.

Zhao Jingheng read the document.

"Who proposed the limit?"

"The Imperial Household Administration, Your Majesty."

"Who reviewed it?"

"Madam Zhao and the fund's chief clerk."

"And the Empress?"

"Her Majesty attended the initial discussion and identified the need for an emergency provision. The administration prepared the procedure afterward."

The Emperor read the last page.

"Good."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Should it be sent to Her Majesty for final approval?"

Zhao Jingheng looked at him.

"Does the existing regulation require her approval?"

"The current wording requires approval for exceptional assistance."

"Then the new procedure should specify what counts as exceptional assistance and what may be authorized under the emergency allowance."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Send the relevant portion to the Empress for review of the authority. The administration can implement the ordinary procedure."

Gao Shun accepted the report.

The Emperor paused.

"How many responsibilities remain under the Empress's direct authority?"

"The revised register lists twelve."

"How many have been examined?"

"Five in detail. Seven remain under review."

"And the duties requiring recommendation?"

"Eight."

"Information only?"

"Twenty-one."

Zhao Jingheng looked at the figures.

The register was finally becoming useful.

It was also revealing how much of the palace had depended on custom rather than clearly recorded authority.

He put the report aside.

"Have the ministries been informed of the revised arrangement?"

"Not yet."

"Inform them. And make it clear that the Empress's intention to relinquish her position is still under consideration. The review is not permission for every department to abandon its duties."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The Emperor returned to his work.

He had not accepted the Empress's resignation.

He had not rejected it.

He had, however, begun to understand that postponing a decision did not mean postponing the necessary preparations.

Yuru received the charitable fund proposal later that evening.

She reviewed the emergency limit and the revised authority.

Then she wrote her approval for the limited emergency arrangement, subject to the monthly report and the requirement that every payment be recorded.

She did not approve the ordinary distribution list.

That responsibility remained with the administration.

Fang Ru placed the document in the outgoing tray.

"Your Majesty has completed the review."

"For this matter."

Fang Ru smiled slightly.

"That is an improvement."

Yuru glanced at her.

"Do not become too optimistic."

"I would not dare."

Yuru looked at the remaining register.

Seven responsibilities still required review.

Eight recommendation duties.

Twenty-one matters requiring information rather than approval.

And beyond those were the ordinary tasks that had not yet been assigned clearly.

She picked up the next report.

Then she stopped.

"Tomorrow."

Fang Ru looked surprised.

"Your Majesty?"

"It can wait until tomorrow."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Yuru put down her brush.

The palace would continue to exist overnight.

The fund would not collapse because she had stopped reading documents after dinner.

She had known this in theory for years.

She was beginning to practise it.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao received Lin Shuyun's reply.

Zhou Wenqi had accepted the revised inspection and payment conditions.

He had also agreed to use an independent carrier whose delivery records would be signed by both parties.

The buyer wanted the inspection to take place two days before dispatch.

Lin Shuyun had agreed, provided the sample and specifications were signed before inspection.

The sale was not yet complete.

The deposit had not yet been received.

But the terms were now sufficiently clear to prepare a contract.

Mingzhao read the letter.

Qiao Yue stood nearby.

"That is good news."

"Yes."

"Will the sale proceed?"

"If the buyer signs the contract and pays the deposit."

"Then it is nearly finished."

"No."

Qiao Yue looked at her.

"Why not?"

"Because a merchant's agreement in a letter is not payment."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Young Lady, you make every good thing sound unfinished."

"Most good things are unfinished until they happen."

She placed the letter beside the partnership accounts.

The silk stock was valuable.

But the partnership's cash position would remain limited until the sale was completed and payment received.

She would not spend money that had not yet arrived.

She turned to the house estimates.

The northern residence remained under inspection.

The eastern residence remained under investigation.

Her family still had not agreed to the final property division.

The warehouse discrepancy was only partly explained.

Her brother had begun to answer questions.

Her mother still wanted her to remain.

Her father still hoped that the transition could be delayed.

Mingzhao looked at the list she had prepared the previous evening.

The first list was getting longer.

The second list was not.

That was probably the correct direction.

She closed the ledger.

"Qiao Yue."

"Yes, Young Lady?"

"Tomorrow, ask the steward to prepare a list of household responsibilities that can be transferred immediately."

"To the young master?"

"Some to him. Some to the senior steward. Some may need a new administrator."

"And the matters you currently handle?"

"All of them."

Qiao Yue looked at her.

"All?"

"Especially the ones that have no proper owner."

Qiao Yue considered the difficulty.

"That may take some time."

"Then we will begin tomorrow."

Mingzhao extinguished the lamp.

For the first time, she was not merely preparing a list of things she intended to take with her.

She was preparing a list of things she intended to leave behind.

And she was beginning to understand that the two lists required entirely different kinds of courage.

**End of Chapter 13.**
