# **Chapter 121: The Former Empress Has Been Asked Whether She Intends to Finish a Piece of Embroidery and Has Discovered That Other People Can Become Surprisingly Concerned About a Needle. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a New Inquiry and Has Learned That a Clearer Request Is Not Necessarily a Better Business Opportunity. The Marquis's Heir Has Finally Been Allowed to Examine the Missing Register's Collection and Discovered That the Records Office Has Preserved Several Books Whose Titles Have Not Survived Their Covers.**

**25th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The former Empress Xu Yuru had just finished threading a needle when Fang Ru asked whether she intended to complete the embroidery before the afternoon.

Yuru looked at the small branch on the cream cloth.

"No."

Fang Ru paused.

"Then shall I put it away?"

"After I finish this leaf."

Yuru made three stitches, examined them, and adjusted the thread.

The leaf was not difficult, but the cloth was fine enough that pulling too tightly would distort it. She had already learned this once and had no wish to repeat the mistake.

Fang Ru waited.

Yuru finished the leaf and set the needle down.

"Now you may put it away."

Fang Ru lifted the cloth carefully.

"Would you like to take it out again tomorrow?"

"Yes, if I feel like it."

Fang Ru smiled.

She had begun to understand that Yuru's answers were not evasions. They were simply answers that did not promise more than the moment required.

The embroidery box was closed and placed on the side table.

Yuru picked up the tea jar sent by the Dowager and inspected the lid.

"It still fits."

"Would you like tea?"

"Not yet. I have already had two cups."

Fang Ru looked at the empty cup.

"Then perhaps fruit?"

Yuru glanced at the dish of dried plums.

"One."

Fang Ru passed it to her.

Yuru ate it slowly.

The plum was still sour.

She looked at the dish.

"Your aunt's household has sent us a very consistent batch."

Fang Ru laughed.

At the central records collection, Steward Zhou and Liang Wen were received by the assistant supervisor shortly after the morning bell.

The collection occupied a long building behind the Revenue Office's western compound. Its shelves were arranged by the later classification system, though many of the older papers had been moved there before the current arrangement was adopted.

The assistant supervisor examined the request.

"Your estate seeks the northern dispatch register?"

"We seek the records listed under the Northern Road Movement Supplement," Zhou replied. "We have a recorded date range and the original office reference."

The supervisor glanced at the attached copy.

"Some of these books have incomplete covers. You should not assume that the title written in the index will appear on the volume itself."

"We understand."

The supervisor led them inside.

A clerk brought out the collection register.

The first group contained three bound volumes, two bundles of loose papers, and one damaged cover without a surviving title.

Liang Wen began recording each item.

The first volume contained general transport movements from an earlier period. The second contained cart assignments and repair entries. The third was a collection of road toll receipts and freight payments.

None was the northern dispatch register.

Zhou checked the dates.

"These do not cover the period we require."

The supervisor consulted the index.

"Then the loose papers may be more relevant."

The first bundle contained several incomplete transport slips. The second contained a mixture of warehouse transfer notes and road toll records.

Liang Wen recorded each item.

He was careful not to turn a reference to the northern road into proof that the paper concerned the four carts.

After the first hour, they had found several records that were related to transport but none that matched the missing register.

The assistant supervisor looked at the remaining shelf.

"There is one more group."

It contained two books with damaged covers and a stack of loose pages tied with faded cord.

The first book had no title on its outer cover.

The second had a partial inscription, but the final characters were missing.

Zhou opened the first book.

Its entries were arranged by date, with columns for dispatching office, cart number, destination, and receiving acknowledgment.

Liang Wen leaned closer.

"This may be it."

Zhou turned several pages.

The book contained entries from the northern transport office.

He checked the date range.

It covered the month they were investigating.

For a moment, both men were silent.

Then Zhou called the supervisor.

"We may have found the register."

The supervisor examined the first page.

"It appears to be a northern transport record."

"Can we compare the entries with this certified copy?"

Zhou produced the copy of the four-cart movement page.

The supervisor allowed him to place it beside the book, provided no writing instrument touched the original.

Liang Wen compared the date and the cart numbers.

The book contained an entry for four carts dispatched on the relevant day.

The numbers matched.

The destination column, however, was incomplete.

The first cart's entry contained a reference to the northern road. The second and third had abbreviated marks. The fourth had no destination written at all.

The cargo column was blank.

Zhou read the remarks.

The same phrase appeared beside the four entries:

*To be confirmed upon arrival.*

Liang Wen looked at him.

"This is the same wording as the loose movement page."

"Yes."

The book had been found.

The journey had not been explained.

Mingyuan received the first message from the records collection before noon.

It was brief.

The northern transport register had been located under the title *Northern Road Movement Supplement*. It contained an entry corresponding to the four-cart dispatch. The cargo column was blank, and the destination was incomplete.

Mingyuan read the message twice.

Then he stood.

"Bring the full report when it arrives."

Liang Wen's assistant bowed and left.

Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou's earlier note.

The book existed. The entry existed. The four cart numbers matched.

But the most important columns were empty.

He sat down and waited for the detailed comparison.

For the first time in several days, he felt that the investigation had reached a point where the next question could be asked directly.

Who had written the entry?

Why had the cargo been left blank?

And what had happened when the carts reached the northern road?

He took out the list of possible drivers.

Sun Shou remained a possible name because he had been employed during the relevant period. Wei Zhen remained unconfirmed because his employment had ended shortly before the dispatch. Neither had been linked to the carts by a daily assignment record.

The newly found register might contain additional names.

Mingyuan turned to the last page of the old movement notebook.

The register was no longer missing.

Now he needed to know what it could actually prove.

At the workshop, Shen Mingzhao received the new merchant's sample.

The merchant had sent a small length of everyday cloth, accompanied by a written description.

It was plain, moderately thick, and intended for ordinary garments. The colour was a muted brown, and the weave was less delicate than the material used in her earlier orders.

Mingzhao examined it.

The cloth was serviceable.

The merchant had also included an approximate quantity of twenty bolts, with a proposed delivery period of thirty days.

But the price was missing.

She called Qiao Yue.

"Did he forget the price?"

"He says he wishes to discuss it after you approve the sample."

Mingzhao looked at the letter.

"Then he is asking us to approve a product without knowing whether we can afford to make it."

"He may believe the price can be settled later."

"It can. But we cannot decide whether to accept the work without it."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao turned to the sample.

The cloth was not unsuitable. It might have a market among small garment makers, and its simpler weave could be produced without changing the workshop's equipment.

Still, twenty bolts was not a trivial quantity.

She asked the foreman to examine it.

He came shortly afterward and handled the cloth.

"This is easier to make than the other material."

"Would the workers need additional training?"

"Not much. The weave is straightforward."

"Would the dye be difficult to repeat?"

"Not if the merchant's colour reference is stable."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Then prepare an estimate of material cost, labour, dyeing, and delivery. Do not assume the merchant's price."

The foreman took the sample.

"Should we reserve time for the twenty bolts?"

"No."

He paused.

"Even provisionally?"

"No. We have not received a price or agreed to the specifications."

The foreman nodded.

Mingzhao placed the merchant's letter in the inquiry file.

A clearer proposal had arrived, but it still lacked the information needed to become an order.

She would not let the workshop schedule be occupied by an imagined contract.

At the records collection, Zhou and Liang Wen continued examining the newly found register.

The four-cart entry was not the only relevant one.

Several pages before it contained dispatches to the northern road. Some included cargo descriptions, others did not. A few had destination names written in full, while others used abbreviations.

The entries were not consistent.

Zhou examined the handwriting.

"Different clerks may have completed different columns."

The assistant supervisor nodded.

"That was common when the dispatch office was busy. One clerk might enter the cart numbers, another the destination, and a third the receiving acknowledgment."

Liang Wen looked at the four-cart entry.

"Then the blank cargo column may have been left for another clerk."

"It may have been."

"Can we identify the hand that wrote the cart numbers?"

The supervisor examined the page.

"The entries on this page appear to be in the same hand. The remarks may have been added later."

Zhou pointed to the phrase.

"These words?"

"Yes. The ink is darker, and the writing is narrower."

Liang Wen recorded the observation.

The next page contained a dispatch to the northern road with a completed cargo description and a receiving acknowledgment. The handwriting in the remarks column resembled that of the four-cart entry.

Zhou compared them.

"It may be the same clerk."

The supervisor shook his head slightly.

"We should not identify a writer from one comparison alone."

Zhou nodded.

"Can we examine other pages?"

"Yes, but do not turn the pages too quickly. The binding is weak."

They continued.

The register contained no separate page listing the four carts' cargo. There was no matching acknowledgment elsewhere in the volume.

The four-cart entry had been recorded.

Its destination had not been completed.

Its cargo had not been recorded.

The phrase *to be confirmed upon arrival* appeared beside all four carts, but there was no corresponding arrival entry.

Liang Wen looked at Zhou.

"Could the arrival record have been kept in another book?"

"Yes."

"Which one?"

"We do not know yet."

The assistant supervisor consulted the index.

"There may have been a receiving register kept by the northern depot. The collection contains some depot records, but not all."

Zhou looked at him.

"Can you show us the relevant index?"

The supervisor nodded.

The missing register had been found, but it had opened another question.

Mingzhao's foreman returned with the cost estimate.

The figures were carefully separated. The material cost was based on the merchant's sample description, while the dye and labour estimates were based on the workshop's existing rates.

Mingzhao checked the calculations.

"Does this include waste?"

"Yes. I have allowed for a small amount of damaged material."

"Does it include the cost of the sample?"

"No. The merchant provided it."

"Then keep that separate."

She compared the estimated cost with the price the merchant might reasonably accept.

There was room for a modest profit, but only if the merchant's actual price did not fall below the workshop's minimum.

She looked at the foreman.

"Can the workshop produce twenty bolts without affecting other work?"

"Yes, if the material arrives on time."

"Would the workers need to work longer hours?"

"Not if the delivery period is thirty days."

Mingzhao considered the estimate.

The inquiry was worth pursuing.

It was not yet worth accepting.

"Send the merchant the estimated price range and ask him to state his intended price. Also ask whether he accepts the sample as the reference for the full quantity."

The foreman nodded.

"Should we offer a smaller quantity?"

"Not yet. First find out whether he can accept the price."

The foreman left.

Qiao Yue looked at the sample.

"This one may become an order."

"It may."

"Would you prefer it to the six-bolt trial?"

Mingzhao glanced at the two files.

"The twenty-bolt inquiry is a clearer commercial possibility. The six-bolt trial is still a separate product test. Neither has been approved."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao returned to her account book.

She had no intention of allowing the workshop to begin two uncertain projects merely because both looked promising.

At the former Empress's residence, Yuru received a note from the Dowager's household confirming their afternoon visit for the following day.

The note was short.

The Dowager suggested that they meet in the smaller western pavilion rather than the main reception room. The weather was expected to be warm, and the pavilion had better shade.

Yuru read the message.

"That sounds pleasant."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Would you like me to prepare the light outer robe?"

"Yes. The plain one."

"Would you like to bring the embroidery?"

Yuru looked at the sewing box.

"Perhaps. If I finish the small branch tomorrow morning, I may show it to her."

"And if you do not?"

"Then I shall leave it here."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru folded the message and placed it in the correspondence box.

She looked at the cloth on the table.

The Dowager might admire it. She might ask about the pattern. She might prefer to discuss the garden or the tea.

Yuru did not need to decide in advance what the conversation would contain.

She had spent enough years arranging conversations for other people.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan finally received the detailed report from Zhou and Liang Wen.

The northern dispatch register had been located and examined.

The four-cart entry was present.

The cart numbers matched the movement pages already recovered.

The cargo column was blank.

The destination was incomplete.

The remarks column stated that confirmation would be made upon arrival.

No corresponding arrival acknowledgment had been found in the register.

Mingyuan read the report carefully.

He looked at the final section.

The records supervisor had identified a possible receiving register from the northern depot, but its location was uncertain. Some depot records had been transferred to the central collection, while others might have remained with the old transport office.

Mingyuan placed the report on the table.

"So we have the dispatch book."

"Yes," Zhou said.

"And it confirms the four carts were dispatched."

"Yes."

"But not what they carried."

"Correct."

"And not where they arrived."

"Correct."

Mingyuan looked at the entry.

The record was both more useful and less satisfying than he had expected.

At least the dispatch had not been invented. The four carts had genuinely been entered in the northern register. Their numbers matched the main yard records. The register had preserved the same incomplete notation seen on the loose movement page.

But the cargo was still unknown.

The receiving record was still missing.

The possible drivers remained unidentified.

Mingyuan took a clean sheet.

"Prepare a new inquiry for the northern depot receiving register. Ask for the date range around the dispatch and the records covering the four cart numbers."

Liang Wen wrote.

"Should we also ask about the drivers?"

"Only if the receiving register contains driver names or signatures. Do not ask them to identify a driver from memory."

Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan continued.

"Also request a copy of the pages before and after the dispatch entry. We need to see how the office normally completed these records."

Liang Wen finished the note.

Mingyuan signed it.

He then placed the report beside the earlier evidence.

For the first time, the four-cart investigation had a confirmed dispatch entry from the actual register.

It had not solved the mystery.

But it had narrowed the question.

The carts had gone somewhere on the northern road.

Someone had expected their arrival to be confirmed.

That confirmation had not yet been found.

Mingyuan looked at the stack of papers.

"At least we no longer need to search for the dispatch book."

Zhou gathered the documents.

"That is a relief."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Now we need the receiving register."

Zhou paused at the door.

"Shall I tell the clerks to search for it?"

"Yes."

"And if they cannot find it?"

Mingyuan looked at him.

"Then we shall find out where it was last recorded."

Zhou bowed and left.

Mingyuan returned to the file.

The missing book had finally been found, and it had answered only the question of whether the four carts were truly dispatched.

It had also provided another missing record to pursue.

He placed a fresh label on the next inquiry folder.

**Northern Depot — Receiving Records.**

Then he added the date.

The investigation would continue.

Somewhere in the estate's old archives, another book was waiting to be discovered under a title that might have nothing to do with the question being asked.
