# **Chapter 111: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Garden Can Be Improved by Knowing When to Leave It Alone, While the Palace Library Has Discovered That Sending a Catalogue Does Not Guarantee a Borrowing. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Learned That Twelve Unsold Bolts Are Still Twelve Bolts That Must Be Stored, Counted, and Eventually Sold. The Marquis's Heir Has Found That an Old Dispatch Office May Have Kept Two Books, Neither of Which Can Yet Explain Four Carts.**

**15th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had finished the chapter about pruning.

She had also reached the part of the book that discussed the planting of shrubs near paths.

The author recommended leaving sufficient room for growth, warning that a shrub planted too close to a walkway might eventually obstruct it. The author further advised that the gardener should consider the height of the mature plant rather than its size at the time of planting.

Yuru read the passage twice.

Then she glanced toward the narrow path outside.

A small flowering shrub stood beside it. It was still young, but its branches had begun to extend over the stones.

She could see why the author had included the warning.

Fang Ru entered carrying a letter.

"Madam, a reply from the palace library."

Yuru accepted it.

The librarian had written to thank her for reviewing the catalogue and to explain that the list had been sent to several residences. The new volumes had been entered into the borrowing register, and the library wished to know whether she intended to reserve any of them.

Yuru read the note.

"That is a polite question," she said.

"Would you like to reserve something?"

"No."

Fang Ru waited.

Yuru folded the letter.

"Please reply that I am still reading the books I borrowed earlier and do not require a reservation."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Shall I mention that you may borrow something later?"

"No need. If I want another book, I will ask."

The attendant prepared to leave.

Yuru looked at the catalogue again.

"Actually, include my thanks for sending the list."

Fang Ru bowed.

When she had gone, Yuru returned to the garden book.

The library had not demanded that she borrow anything. It had merely asked whether she wished to reserve a title.

The answer was no.

There was no conflict to resolve.

Yuru found the simplicity almost amusing.

She had once spent considerable effort ensuring that every request received an answer, every delay an explanation, and every arrangement a responsible person.

Now a letter could be answered in two sentences.

She turned the page.

The author had begun discussing paths.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received the merchants' written estimates of the quantities they might purchase.

Neither was a firm order.

The first merchant expected to buy between four and six bolts of the familiar specification, depending on the market price at the end of the month.

The second believed he could sell between six and eight bolts of the other specification, but only if the colour remained consistent with the sample from the previous season.

Mingzhao placed both letters beside the foreman's estimate.

Qiao Yue looked at the figures.

"Twelve bolts would be more than either merchant expects to purchase."

"Yes."

"But together they may take fourteen."

"They may."

Qiao Yue glanced at the letters again.

"Would it be reasonable to produce twelve?"

Mingzhao leaned back.

The combined expected demand was not a promise. One merchant might purchase four bolts instead of six. The other might decide that the colour was unsuitable after seeing the sample.

If the workshop produced twelve bolts, it might sell them all.

It might also sell only four.

The question was not whether twelve bolts could be produced.

The question was whether Mingzhao wished to keep the remaining stock if the expected sales did not occur.

"Ask the foreman for the cost of six bolts," she said. "Then ask how much additional cost would be required to produce another six later."

Qiao Yue wrote the instruction.

"Would that delay the work?"

"Possibly. But it would allow us to test the market without committing to the entire quantity."

"Then we should produce six first?"

"Not until the samples and prices are confirmed."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao looked again at the letters.

She had no objection to taking a reasonable risk.

A business that never accepted uncertainty would never grow.

But the uncertainty had to be measured. She wanted to know how much money would be committed, how long the goods might remain unsold, and whether the workshop could bear that cost without affecting its ordinary obligations.

She had not left the Marquisate to exchange one set of obligations for another.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received the result of the search for the old transport office's register list.

The office had located a transfer record from the period when its storage rooms were reorganized. The list named several account books, dispatch registers, and movement notebooks, but the descriptions were general.

One entry referred to "transport movement records, several volumes."

Another referred to "northern dispatch book."

Neither included a date range or a receiving signature that could be matched with the damaged notebook register.

Mingyuan examined the copy.

"Does this establish that the separate dispatch book existed?"

"It establishes that a book with that description was listed for transfer," Liang Wen replied.

"Not that it was the book Zhao Liang remembers."

"No, Young Master."

"Nor that it contained the four-cart order."

"No."

Mingyuan placed the list beside the two former supervisors' letters.

The evidence was beginning to suggest that the transport office had maintained more than one kind of record. That was useful. It might explain why the former supervisors remembered different books.

But it did not identify the missing notebook or the contents of the separate dispatch book.

"Ask the records room whether the northern dispatch book was received into its collection," he said. "Search under both the full description and the shorter title."

Liang Wen bowed.

"And if it was received?"

"Then ask whether it survives and whether the relevant month is included."

"Understood."

Mingyuan looked at the transfer list again.

There was a small note at the bottom stating that several volumes had been received without individual page counts because the transfer had taken place during a room clearance.

He pointed to it.

"What does this mean?"

Liang Wen read it.

"The receiving clerk accepted the bundles by volume, but did not record the number of pages in each book."

"So a volume could have been incomplete at the time of transfer."

"Yes."

Mingyuan set down the list.

"Record that limitation."

He did not want the transfer record to become proof that the missing book had been received intact.

The estate's archives had already taught him that the word "received" could conceal several unanswered questions.

The palace library received Yuru's reply before noon.

The librarian entered it in the correspondence register.

No reservation was made.

No book was sent.

The matter ended.

In the former Empress's guest residence, the garden book remained open on the table.

Yuru had reached the chapter about paths, which described how a path should follow the natural shape of the ground rather than forcing the land into an unnecessarily complicated arrangement.

The author gave examples of gardens where straight paths had been laid across uneven ground. The result, he wrote, was a walkway that required repeated repairs because rainwater collected in the wrong places.

Yuru found the explanation practical.

She remembered several palace courtyards where water had gathered after heavy rain. The repairs had often involved replacing stones, raising sections of the path, or clearing blocked channels.

She had not been responsible for the garden's construction.

Yet she had frequently been asked to approve the repair arrangements.

She wondered whether the original paths had been planned according to the ground or according to someone's desire for symmetry.

Fang Ru entered.

"Madam, the gardener has asked whether the flowering shrub beside the path should be trimmed."

Yuru looked up.

"Why?"

"He says the branches are beginning to extend over the walkway."

"Does he believe they should be removed?"

"He says a few branches should be shortened, but he wished to confirm whether you prefer the shrub to remain as it is."

Yuru closed the book.

"Tell him to use his judgment. If the branches obstruct the path, he may trim them."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Should I tell him not to cut too much?"

"Tell him what you have heard him say. He knows how much needs to be removed better than I do."

The attendant bowed and left.

Yuru reopened the book.

The author had just begun explaining that paths should be wide enough for ordinary use but should not occupy more ground than necessary.

She smiled.

The gardener had asked a sensible question.

She had given a sensible answer.

No written instruction was required.

Mingzhao received the foreman's estimate for six bolts shortly after midday.

The cost was lower than producing twelve, but the price per bolt was slightly higher because certain preparation costs would not be spread across a larger batch.

The foreman had also estimated the additional cost of producing another six later.

Mingzhao examined the figures.

"How much time would the second group require?" she asked.

Qiao Yue checked the note.

"Four or five days, depending on the dye."

"Would the first group have to be stored separately?"

"Yes. The foreman recommends keeping each group under a separate batch number."

"Good."

Mingzhao drew a line beneath the estimate.

Six bolts would be enough to test the quality and obtain a response from the merchants. If the first group sold, the workshop could produce another six under the same specification.

If it did not sell, the loss would be limited.

The arrangement was not risk-free, but it was manageable.

She looked at the market letters again.

The merchants had not promised to buy.

They had, however, provided enough information to make a small trial worth considering.

"Prepare a written production instruction for six bolts," she said. "Use the approved specifications from the previous season, but prepare a fresh sample for confirmation before the full batch is woven."

Qiao Yue wrote.

"Should the foreman begin today?"

"After the sample is confirmed and the material price is fixed."

"That may mean another delay."

"Then there will be another delay."

Mingzhao signed the instruction with a clear limitation:

**Authorization applies to the sample and the first six bolts only. No further quantity is approved.**

She handed it to Qiao Yue.

The workshop could now begin preparing for a small trial.

It still had no large order.

That was entirely acceptable.

At the Marquisate, Liang Wen returned with the records room's answer.

The northern dispatch book had been received into the collection during the later transfer, but the volume could not be located under its original title.

A separate register listed one northern dispatch book among several volumes moved to a secondary storage room. The entry did not record its final location.

Mingyuan read the note.

"Can we search the secondary room?"

"The room was cleared several years later. Some records were moved to the old accounts storehouse."

"Have those bundles been checked?"

"Not yet."

Mingyuan looked at the transfer record.

"Then ask for a search of the old accounts storehouse."

Liang Wen nodded.

"Should we include the movement notebook?"

"Yes, but keep the search terms separate. We are looking for two possible records, not assuming they are the same book."

Liang Wen wrote it down.

Mingyuan paused.

"Has Zhao Liang replied about the separate dispatch book?"

"Not yet."

"Then do not send another letter until we receive his answer."

Liang Wen gathered the documents.

Mingyuan looked at the folders on his desk.

The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.

The purchasing voucher remained missing.

The four-cart movement remained unconfirmed in its cargo and destination.

The property division remained incomplete.

There was no reason to combine these matters simply because they all involved old records.

He had begun to understand that the estate's problems were not one large mystery.

They were several smaller failures of recordkeeping, each with its own history and its own evidence.

Some might eventually be explained.

Others might remain uncertain.

He could not promise the family a complete answer.

He could promise that the accounts would no longer conceal the difference between what was known and what was merely suspected.

In the western guest residence, the gardener trimmed the flowering shrub.

He removed only the branches that extended over the path and collected the cuttings in a basket.

When Fang Ru passed by, he showed her the remaining shape of the plant.

"It should grow evenly now," he said.

Fang Ru looked at it.

"Madam said you could use your judgment."

The gardener bowed.

"Then I have done what was necessary."

Fang Ru returned to the sitting room.

Yuru was reading.

"The shrub has been trimmed, Madam."

"Is the path clear?"

"Yes."

"Then it is well."

Fang Ru stood for a moment.

"Would you like to see it?"

Yuru considered the suggestion.

"After I finish this page."

The attendant smiled.

"Of course."

Yuru continued reading.

The chapter explained that a garden path should be repaired when the stones became loose, but that unnecessary reconstruction could damage the ground beneath it.

She read the paragraph slowly.

There was a lesson in it, though she did not feel the need to write one down.

A person could maintain something without controlling every detail.

A person could also allow someone else to make a decision within the responsibility already entrusted to them.

She turned the page.

That afternoon, Mingzhao received a short confirmation from one of the merchants.

He was willing to inspect the first six bolts when they were finished, but he would not promise to purchase them before seeing the goods.

The second merchant had not yet replied.

Mingzhao placed the confirmation beside the production instruction.

The arrangement was reasonable enough for a trial. The workshop would bear the cost of six bolts, but the amount was within the limit she had set. The merchant would inspect the goods, and she would retain the right to sell them elsewhere if he declined.

She wrote a note to the foreman.

**Proceed with the approved six-bolt trial after the sample and material price are confirmed. Goods may not be reserved for the merchant without a separate written agreement.**

Qiao Yue read it.

"Should we ask for an inspection date now?"

"Ask when the sample is ready. There is no need to arrange an inspection for goods that do not yet exist."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao returned to her account book.

The workshop had a small task to complete.

It did not yet have a buyer.

She was content to let the distinction remain.

Before sunset, Mingyuan received Zhao Liang's reply.

The former supervisor remembered that a separate northern dispatch book had been used during busy periods, but he could not say whether it had been kept in the transport office or moved with the movement notebook. He also remembered that clerks sometimes copied dispatch instructions into the movement notebook after the order had been issued.

The two records might therefore have contained overlapping information.

Mingyuan read the letter carefully.

"Does this tell us which book held the four-cart entry?" Zhou asked.

"No."

"But it explains why the supervisors remember different books."

"It offers an explanation. It does not prove that the four-cart entry appeared in both."

Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan placed Zhao Liang's letter in the folder.

He wrote a new note:

**Separate northern dispatch book reportedly used during busy periods. Possible overlap with movement notebook; no surviving evidence yet identifies the four-cart entry in either record beyond the loose movement page already found.**

He read it again.

Then he signed and dated the note.

The inquiry remained open.

He closed the folder.

At night, Yuru finally walked to the shrub beside the path.

The branches no longer extended over the stones. The plant still looked full, and the cut had been made cleanly.

She examined it for a moment.

Fang Ru stood beside her.

"Does it look better?" the attendant asked.

"It looks as though someone has done exactly what was needed."

"The gardener said it should grow more evenly."

"Then we shall see."

Yuru continued along the path.

She did not ask the gardener to explain the cut, inspect the tools, or submit a note about the plant's condition.

The path was clear.

That was enough.

Across Yongjing, Mingzhao had authorized six bolts of trial production, but no more. Mingyuan had found evidence that two transport records may have existed, but neither had yet explained the four carts.

The palace library had sent a catalogue and received a polite refusal to reserve anything.

The gardener had trimmed a shrub.

Each matter had advanced by a small amount.

None had become larger merely because someone wanted a more impressive result.

For the moment, the city could manage with that.
