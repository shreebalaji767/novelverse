# **Chapter 23: The Empress Has Been Asked to Choose Her Successor. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Choose Which Debts She Is Willing to Inherit.**



**15th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**







The Emperor had not intended to discuss a successor that morning.







He had intended to discuss the completion of the Empress's administrative transition.







Unfortunately, the Ministry of Personnel had submitted a document with the heading:







**Preliminary Considerations Concerning the Future Management of the Inner Palace.**







The first section contained the remaining responsibilities of the Empress's office.







The second section described the qualifications expected of a future Empress.







The third section contained a list of possible arrangements if no immediate appointment was made.







The fourth section recommended that the current Empress provide her opinion regarding a suitable successor.







Zhao Jingheng read the fourth section twice.







Then he looked at Minister Han.







"Why?"







Minister Han bowed.







"Your Majesty, the Ministry believes Your Majesty's decision should be informed by the experience of the current Empress."







"Her experience has already been recorded."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







"Her administrative responsibilities have already been examined."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







"Her resignation has already been accepted."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







The Emperor placed the document down.







"Then why are you asking her to choose the person who will replace her?"







Minister Han hesitated.







"The Ministry believes she would be best placed to identify a suitable candidate."







The Emperor looked at the paper.







"Does the Ministry believe the Empress is also responsible for appointing her successor?"







"No, Your Majesty."







"Then why does the Ministry recommend it?"







Minister Han lowered his head.







The Emperor pushed the document toward him.







"Revise it. The Ministry may submit qualifications, legal considerations, and possible arrangements. The appointment is mine."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







"And do not send the revised document to Chengming Palace for approval."







Minister Han accepted the paper.







The Emperor leaned back.







He had accepted the Empress's resignation.







He had not expected the ministries to interpret that decision as an opportunity to transfer the appointment process to her.



At Chengming Palace, Yuru received the same document shortly afterward.







Not because the Emperor had approved it.







Because someone in the Ministry of Personnel had sent an earlier copy to the Empress's office before the Emperor returned it.







Fang Ru placed it on the table.







"Your Majesty, the Ministry requests your opinion on the possible candidates."







Yuru read the heading.







Then she read the final section.







She was silent for a moment.







"Did the Emperor approve this request?"







Fang Ru shook her head.







"The document was returned for revision."







"Then why was it sent here?"







"The Ministry's messenger had already departed."







Yuru closed the document.







"Return it."







"Should I include a response?"







"Yes. State that the Ministry should follow the Emperor's instructions and submit its recommendations to him."







Fang Ru bowed.







Yuru looked at the paper again.







"Also tell them that I will not recommend a successor."







Fang Ru hesitated.







"Your Majesty, would it not be helpful to provide some guidance?"







"Guidance about what?"







"Experience, temperament, administrative ability."







Yuru considered her answer.







"A future Empress will have her own responsibilities and circumstances. I can explain what I have learned, but I cannot select a person based on the assumption that she must manage the palace exactly as I did."







Fang Ru nodded slowly.







Yuru continued.







"That would defeat the purpose of this transition."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







She looked toward the courtyard.







The palace was already trying to turn her departure into another responsibility.







She had spent years becoming indispensable.







Now she had to resist being indispensable even in the process of leaving.



The Crown Prince arrived before noon.







He had heard that the Ministry of Personnel was considering future arrangements.







"Mother, does the Emperor intend to appoint another Empress immediately?"







"I do not know."







"Would that be preferable?"







"That depends on the Emperor's judgment and the circumstances."







Cheng'an looked thoughtful.







"If there is no Empress, who will oversee the inner palace?"







"The offices responsible for their respective duties."







"Some matters still require the Empress's authority."







"Then those matters will remain subject to the regulations governing them until the Emperor makes a decision."







The Crown Prince looked uncertain.







"Would it not be easier to appoint someone before the ceremony?"







Yuru gave him a measured look.







"Easier for whom?"







He paused.







"For the palace."







"Would it be easier for the person appointed?"







Cheng'an did not answer.







Yuru continued.







"Do not mistake the need for an office to function with the need to fill a position immediately. The palace has had years to develop its arrangements. It should not use my departure as an excuse to make a hurried appointment."







The Crown Prince bowed.







"I understand."







"Do you?"







He looked up.







"I understand that the decision should not be rushed."







"Good."







Yuru returned to her papers.







"And you should continue examining the Eastern Palace accounts."







Cheng'an's expression changed.







"Mother, I have already reviewed them."







"Have you completed the monthly reconciliation?"







"Not yet."







"Then you have not completed them."







The Crown Prince bowed again.







He had hoped that the conversation about the future Empress might occupy more time.







It had not.



At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao had a different problem.







The family had begun discussing liabilities.







It was one thing to determine which assets belonged to the Marquisate.







It was another to determine which obligations should remain with the estate after the division.







Sun Qiming had prepared a separate schedule.







He placed it before Shen Guowei, Mr. Liang, Mr. Chen, and Mingzhao.







"These are the obligations identified so far."







Shen Guowei examined the first page.







"Land taxes."







"Yes. They are estate obligations."







"Maintenance costs?"







"Those incurred for estate property remain with the estate, subject to verification."







"Commercial debts?"







"Those require examination of the relevant contracts and accounts."







Mingzhao turned to the next page.







"What about the advances made to merchants?"







Sun Qiming pointed to the entries.







"Some are documented loans. Others are investments or partnership contributions. They cannot all be treated as debts owed to you."







Mingzhao nodded.







"I agree."







Mr. Liang looked at her.







"Then you are not claiming every payment you made as a personal loan?"







"No. I am claiming only what the documents and circumstances establish."







Shen Guowei seemed relieved.







He had feared that Mingzhao would demand repayment of every coin she had ever spent on the estate.







But the relief did not last.







Sun Qiming turned another page.







"There are also obligations incurred by the estate through commercial arrangements."







He identified three outstanding matters.







The first was a payment due to a grain merchant.







The second concerned a silk shipment purchased for the household.







The third involved repairs to a commercial warehouse.







Shen Guowei looked at the list.







"These are ordinary estate expenses."







"Possibly," Sun Qiming replied. "But the contracts must be examined to determine the exact amount and payment schedule."







Mingzhao looked at the second entry.







"Who negotiated the silk shipment?"







"I believe you did," Shen Guowei said.







"I negotiated it on behalf of the estate."







"Then the contract is yours to explain."







Mingzhao looked at him.







"The contract is the estate's obligation. I am not personally liable merely because I negotiated it."







Mr. Chen nodded.







"That distinction is correct. The identity of the person who negotiated a contract does not, by itself, establish personal liability."







Shen Guowei frowned.







"I did not say she was personally liable."







"Then we agree," Mingzhao replied.







The room became quiet.







Mr. Liang examined the schedule.







"These obligations should be assigned to the estate unless the evidence shows otherwise."







Sun Qiming made a note.







Mingzhao continued reading.







She had no intention of inheriting debts simply because she had managed the transactions.







Nor would she pretend that her departure erased obligations she had properly undertaken in her own commercial capacity.







The distinction had to be made carefully.



The discussion became more difficult when the accountant reached the northern warehouse.







"Until the grain discrepancy is resolved, the value of the remaining stock cannot be finalized."







Shen Guowei frowned.







"Must the entire division wait for this?"







"No," Sun Qiming answered. "The building and other properties can be valued separately. The unresolved inventory should remain a separate item."







Mingzhao nodded.







"That is reasonable."







Mr. Liang looked at the Marquis.







"Then the family need not delay every other matter."







Shen Guowei did not look pleased.







He had hoped the unresolved accounts would provide a reason to postpone the settlement.







Mingzhao noticed.







"Father, the property division does not require every question to be answered on the same day."







"I know."







"Then we should continue with the matters that can be completed."







Shen Guowei looked at her.







"You are very eager to leave."







"I am eager to complete what can be completed."







He had no immediate answer.



After the meeting, Mingyuan found Mingzhao in the side courtyard.







He was carrying another document.







"Second Sister."







She looked up.







"What is it?"







"The carpenter's original receipt."







Mingzhao accepted it.







The paper was old but legible.







It identified the work performed in Shen Mingyuan's study and the payment received.







Sun Qiming examined it.







"This supports the seventy-two taels."







Mingyuan looked relieved.







"Then that amount is settled?"







"Not entirely," Sun Qiming replied. "The receipt supports the payment and the work. We must still confirm the authorization and the classification."







Mingyuan's relief diminished.







"Is that necessary?"







"Yes."







"But the work was done."







"That is not in dispute."







Sun Qiming explained patiently.







"The question is whether the expense was properly charged to the estate, whether it was an ordinary household repair, and whether any portion was a personal improvement beyond the estate's normal obligations."







Mingyuan looked at Mingzhao.







"Do you intend to charge me personally?"







Mingzhao examined the receipt.







"I intend to establish what the payment was for."







He looked frustrated.







"That sounds like the same thing."







"It is not."







She handed the receipt to Sun Qiming.







"Examine the work order when it is found. Compare the materials and the actual repairs."







Mingyuan nodded reluctantly.







He had begun to understand that a receipt was not a magical document that answered every question.







It was merely evidence of a particular transaction.







The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels were still unexplained.







The new receipt did not change that.



In the commercial district, Lin Shuyun was supervising the silk inspection.







Zhou Wenqi's representatives had arrived with measuring tools and sample sheets.







The two sides compared the bolts against the contract.







The first group consisted of silk ready for delivery.







The second group required finishing.







The buyer's representative inspected the texture, colour consistency, and measurements.







Lin Shuyun stood beside the warehouse clerk.







"Record each accepted bolt separately."







The clerk nodded.







The buyer's representative examined one bolt.







"This section has a slight irregularity."







Lin Shuyun inspected it.







"That is within the sample specification."







The representative looked again.







"It is visible."







"It is also visible in the signed sample."







The man considered this.







He compared the cloth with the sample sheet.







Then he nodded.







"Accepted."







The inspection continued.







At the end, the buyer's representative signed the acceptance record for the first group.







The remaining bolts would be inspected after finishing.







Lin Shuyun checked the number.







It matched the reserved quantity.







She sent the record to Mingzhao.







The sale had not yet been fully completed.







Delivery remained.







The balance remained.







But the first inspection had passed.



Mingzhao received the inspection report in the afternoon.







She examined the signed acceptance record.







Qiao Yue stood beside her.







"That is good news."







"Yes."







"Will the payment arrive soon?"







"After delivery."







"Then the business is nearly finished."







"The inspection is nearly finished."







Qiao Yue looked at the report.







"Miss, do you ever allow yourself to be pleased before every document is complete?"







Mingzhao looked at her.







"I am pleased."







"You do not look pleased."







"Would you prefer me to dance?"







Qiao Yue smiled.







"No, Miss."







"Good. The courtyard is too small."







She placed the report with the partnership records.







For a brief moment, she allowed herself to enjoy the progress.







Then she returned to the household budget.







The independent residence still had to be found.



At Chengming Palace, the Empress Dowager requested a private meeting.







Yuru went to the Dowager's residence shortly before evening.







The Dowager was seated beside a low table.







A report lay before her.







"Sit."







Yuru bowed and took her seat.







The Dowager looked at her.







"The Ministry of Personnel has asked you to recommend a successor."







"I have returned the request."







"Why?"







"Because the appointment belongs to the Emperor."







The Dowager's expression remained reserved.







"Do you believe you have no responsibility for what happens after you leave?"







"I believe I have a responsibility to complete the transition properly. I do not believe I should appoint the person who will occupy the position after me."







The Dowager looked at the report.







"The palace cannot remain without an Empress indefinitely."







"I have not suggested that it should."







"Then what do you suggest?"







"That the Emperor make the decision according to the circumstances. Until then, the established offices should continue their duties under the existing regulations."







The Dowager was silent.







Yuru continued.







"The palace must not become dependent on a person simply because that person holds the title. If a future Empress is appointed, she should not inherit every informal responsibility I accumulated."







The Dowager looked at her.







"You have spent many years managing these matters."







"Yes."







"And now you wish to leave them."







"I wish to leave the position."







The Dowager's eyes narrowed slightly.







"Do you believe the two can be separated?"







"They must be."







The Dowager looked away.







For years, she had regarded the Empress's work as part of the natural order of the imperial household.







She had not considered how much of that work existed only because Yuru had taken it upon herself.







The Dowager was not yet convinced.







But she was no longer dismissing the distinction.







"Your ceremony is on the twenty-third day."







"Yes."







"Do not allow the departments to make it disorderly."







"I will not."







The Dowager gave a short nod.







"Then complete your arrangements."







Yuru bowed.







The meeting ended without an argument.







It also ended without a blessing.







That was, perhaps, progress.



That night, the Emperor asked Yuru to join him for a meal.







They sat together in a smaller hall.







There were no ministers, no officials, and no reports on the table.







The Emperor looked at her.







"The Ministry of Personnel asked you to recommend your successor."







"I know."







"You returned the request."







"Yes."







"I returned it first."







"I know that too."







He looked at her.







"Did you think I would allow them to make you responsible for that?"







Yuru considered him.







"I thought you might be amused by the attempt."







The Emperor gave a short laugh.







"I was not."







He poured tea.







"The Ministry has revised the document. It will submit qualifications and possible arrangements to me."







"Good."







He looked at her.







"Do you have any advice?"







Yuru paused.







"About the future Empress?"







"Yes."







She considered her words.







"Do not appoint someone merely because she is willing to work as much as I did."







The Emperor looked at her.







"That is your advice?"







"It is."







He was silent for a moment.







Then he nodded.







"I will remember it."







Yuru looked at the tea.







"Also, do not tell her that the palace cannot function without her."







The Emperor smiled faintly.







"I believe the palace has already demonstrated that it can function without you."







"It has demonstrated that it can function with considerable assistance from people who are still learning."







"That is not the same thing."







"No. It is worse."







He laughed.







Yuru's expression remained composed.







The Emperor looked at her for a long moment.







"Nine days."







"Eight, now."







"Does that make you happy?"







"It makes the schedule accurate."







He shook his head.







She looked at him.







"I am looking forward to it."







He nodded.







He did not ask her to reconsider.







That was perhaps the most meaningful part of the conversation.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao was reviewing the updated liability schedule when Shen Mingxiu entered.







"Second Sister, Father wants to know whether you will accept the family carriage for your departure."







Mingzhao looked up.







"Why?"







"He says it would be inappropriate for you to leave without proper transport."







"I can arrange transport."







"He says the family carriage is available."







"Then it can remain available for family use."







Mingxiu hesitated.







"He also says the family can provide a temporary allowance."







Mingzhao put down her brush.







"Has he specified whether it is a gift, loan, or advance against my share?"







Mingxiu sighed.







"Second Sister, he has not."







"Then the matter is not settled."







"He may simply wish to help."







"Then he can say so clearly."







Mingxiu looked at her.







"Must everything be written down?"







Mingzhao was silent for a moment.







"Not everything. But money that may later be included in a property settlement should be recorded."







Mingxiu nodded.







She understood the practical reason.







She also understood that her elder sister no longer trusted vague promises.







"I will tell Father."







"Thank you."







Mingxiu turned to leave, then stopped.







"Second Sister."







"What?"







"If you do not take the carriage, what will you use?"







"An ordinary hired carriage."







"That will be inconvenient."







"Many things are inconvenient."







Mingxiu smiled faintly.







"That is true."







After she left, Mingzhao returned to the liability schedule.







She had not accepted the carriage.







She had not accepted the allowance.







She had not purchased a residence.







But she had also not agreed to delay her departure.







The family was learning that offering something was not the same as settling an account.



Before retiring, Mingzhao received the latest report from Sun Qiming.







The seventy-two-tael repair payment was now supported by the original receipt and the carpenter's testimony.







The authorization still required confirmation.







The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels were still unverified.







The warehouse transport contractor had agreed to bring his notebook the next day.







The grain discrepancy remained:



- Eighteen sacks confirmed in southern storage.

- Two sacks covered by a movement instruction but without confirmed receipt.

- Twenty-seven sacks still unexplained.



The silk inspection had passed for the first group.







The final delivery and payment remained pending.







The property assessor would inspect the agricultural holdings next.







Mingzhao read the report.







Then she placed it beside the household budget.







There were now enough papers to cover the entire table.







She looked at the space left for her tea.







"Move these."







Qiao Yue shifted the papers.







"Here?"







"Further."







Qiao Yue moved them again.







Mingzhao examined the remaining space.







"Good."







Qiao Yue looked at the table.







"Miss, we may need another table."







Mingzhao looked at her.







"Why?"







"Because the documents no longer fit."







Mingzhao considered this.







Then she looked toward the adjoining room, where the family had stored several unused tables.







"Ask Steward Zhou for one."







Qiao Yue smiled.







"Should I tell him what it is for?"







"Tell him it is for the documents."







"Will he ask which documents?"







Mingzhao looked at the growing piles.







"He should not."







Qiao Yue went to make the request.







Mingzhao returned to her work.







The Empress had eight days left.







The Marquis's eldest daughter still had no final departure date.







The palace was debating who would become Empress.







The Marquisate was debating which debts belonged to whom.







And Mingzhao had just discovered that the greatest obstacle to independence might not be money, family opposition, or property law.







It might be the number of tables required to store the paperwork.







**End of Chapter 23.**
