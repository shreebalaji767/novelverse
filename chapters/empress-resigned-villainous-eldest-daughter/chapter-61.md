# **Chapter 61: The Former Empress Has Returned to Her Parents' Home and Has Been Asked Whether She Is Eating Enough. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Attend a Meeting About Property She Has Already Explained Twice. Both Women Have Discovered That Families Can Repeat Questions Without Improving Them.**

**25th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The morning at the Xu residence began with a disagreement over vegetables.



Duke Xu believed that the kitchen had purchased too few.



Madam Xu believed that the kitchen had purchased enough.



The kitchen steward believed that neither opinion would improve his morning.



"The former Empress is coming tomorrow," the Duke said.



"She is my daughter," Madam Xu replied.



"She is also accustomed to a larger household."



"She has already eaten at this table."



"That was before."



"Before what?"



The Duke hesitated.



He had intended to say *before she left the palace*, but the words seemed unnecessarily troublesome.



Madam Xu looked at him.



"She is coming to eat with her parents. She is not coming to inspect our kitchen."



The Duke cleared his throat.



"I am aware."



"Then stop asking the kitchen to prepare for an inspection."



The steward lowered his head.



He had not known that the Duke was planning an inspection.



He was relieved to learn that it had been cancelled.



The Duke sat down.



"Perhaps we should ask Yuru what she wants."



Madam Xu considered this.



"She has already said she will come."



"That does not tell us what she wants to eat."



"She can eat what we prepare."



The Duke looked toward the kitchen.



The kitchen steward looked at the floor.



The six dishes remained six dishes.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received a message from the family representative.



The meeting scheduled for the twenty-seventh would begin at the usual hour.



The family had requested that she bring the corrected property schedule, the supporting documents for her advances, and any additional records concerning the estate's liabilities.



Mingzhao read the message.



Then she looked at the document case already standing beside her writing table.



"Have they asked for anything else?" Qiao Yue asked.



"They have asked for the same records."



"Perhaps they misplaced the first copy."



"They have not received the first copy."



Qiao Yue blinked.



Mingzhao opened the case.



The corrections were complete.



The supporting documents were arranged by category.



The nine confirmed advances had their own section.



The four payment-confirmed entries with unclear repayment arrangements had another.



The ten uncertain entries remained separate.



The study repairs were recorded as estate maintenance.



The unverified two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The grain discrepancy and the separate transport payment were not combined.



The northern canal property was excluded.



The western field's temporary cultivation agreement was included only as an update to the estate schedule, not as a renewed lease.



Mingzhao closed the case.



"Prepare a second copy of the index."



"Only the index?"



"Yes. The documents themselves are already arranged."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"That should be manageable."



Mingzhao glanced at her.



"Do not sound so pleased."



Qiao Yue lowered her head.



"I am pleased that we are not copying the entire schedule again."



Mingzhao returned to her writing.



That was reasonable.



She allowed it.

At the Imperial Palace, the Emperor had received a report from the Imperial Household Administration.



It concerned the transition of responsibilities formerly handled by Xu Yuru.



The report was orderly.



Several duties had been transferred to the appropriate offices.



Some matters required further clarification.



A few minor procedures were taking longer than expected.



The Emperor read the report.



Then he asked Gao Shun, "Is anything urgent?"



Gao Shun considered the question.



"There are several matters requiring attention, Your Majesty. None is immediately urgent."



"Then they can be handled according to their proper procedures."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor returned to the report.



He had become accustomed to receiving a second opinion from Yuru whenever a matter involved several departments.



Sometimes she would identify an overlooked responsibility.



Sometimes she would ask why two offices were both claiming that the other had authority.



Sometimes she would simply point to a missing signature.



He had not realized how often he had relied on that final step.



Now the reports were being returned to the relevant offices.



Some officials were learning.



Others were complaining.



The Emperor placed the report aside.



"Do not send it to the former Empress."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor looked at him.



"Even if someone suggests that she could resolve it quickly."



"Especially then, Your Majesty."



The Emperor nodded.



Gao Shun had understood.

At the eastern market, the workshop had completed another production group under the revised folding method.



The first twenty bolts had passed inspection.



The next group had also been completed, though one bolt required correction because the edge had not remained consistent.



The material was usable.



The correction was recorded.



No loss had been entered.



Mingzhao examined the corrected bolt.



"Who completed this?"



The trainee raised a hand.



She was the slower of the two trainees.



Mingzhao examined the edge.



"What happened?"



"I folded the corner too tightly," the trainee said.



"Why?"



"I was trying to keep it consistent with the previous bolt."



"Did you compare the sample?"



"Yes."



"Then why did you change the pressure?"



The trainee hesitated.



"I thought the cloth was thicker."



Mingzhao looked at the senior finisher.



"Was it?"



"No, Miss Shen."



Mingzhao turned back to the trainee.



"Then do not correct a difference that does not exist."



The trainee lowered her head.



"Yes, Miss Shen."



Mingzhao placed the bolt beside the sample.



"Look at both again. The purpose is not to make every movement identical. The purpose is to produce the same finished result."



The trainee examined them.



After a moment, she nodded.



Lin Shuyun watched from the other side of the table.



"She is careful."



"She is."



"Perhaps too careful."



"That is why she is being trained."



The senior finisher bowed.



"We will continue supervising her."



Mingzhao nodded.



The revised method had been approved.



It had not removed the need for judgment.



Nor had it removed the need for supervision.



The workshop would continue recording the results.



The new order was still in production.



The buyer had not yet received the completed goods.



No one would announce a profit before the expenses were known.

That afternoon, Shen Mingyuan received the response from the former warehouse steward.



Zhang had agreed to provide further information about the old warehouse records.



He had not claimed responsibility for the missing grain.



He had not identified a recipient.



He had offered to examine the original sheets if they were brought to him.



Mingyuan read the letter.



Steward Zhou stood beside him.



"Should we send the original sheets?"



"No."



The steward looked at him.



Mingyuan considered the matter.



"We should make certified copies first. The originals must remain with the estate records."



"Yes, Young Master."



"Arrange for Zhang to examine the copies."



"Yes."



Mingyuan looked at the letter again.



The former steward might remember the old procedures.



He might recognize a handwriting style.



He might identify an error in the way the figures had been copied.



He might also remember nothing useful.



Mingyuan was learning not to expect a witness to produce an answer merely because the family had summoned him.



"Ask him to state what he remembers separately from what he can confirm from the records."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"That is a sensible distinction."



Mingyuan looked at him.



The steward bowed.



"Young Master."



Mingyuan returned to the letter.



He had begun to appreciate that the steward's praise was not given freely.



He also suspected that the steward was still waiting for him to make a mistake.



This was probably reasonable.

In the western palace residence, Consort Han received a note from the senior palace lady who had organized the proposed gathering.



The gathering would be personal.



No formal household discussion would take place.



Consort Han read the note.



Then she looked at her attendant.



"Did she write that herself?"



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Good."



Consort Han placed the note aside.



She had no objection to meeting other women in the palace.



She had an objection to being invited to tea and discovering that someone expected her to settle a dispute over servants' assignments.



The palace had long relied on informal conversations to handle matters that should have been addressed openly.



That habit would not disappear quickly.



But the former Empress's departure had made it more difficult to pretend that every task could be handled by whoever happened to be available.



Consort Han poured herself tea.



She intended to attend the gathering.



She also intended to leave when the tea was finished.

On the evening of the twenty-fifth, Xu Yuru prepared for her visit.



Her light blue robe was folded.



Her walking shoes were ready.



Fang Ru had packed the small personal items she would need.



The former Empress looked at the arrangement.



"How long will we stay?"



"Madam Xu has not specified."



"Then we will return when it is appropriate."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Should I prepare a second set of clothing?"



"No."



"Should I bring the larger travelling case?"



"No."



Fang Ru paused.



"Then the small case?"



Xu Yuru looked at her.



"We are visiting my parents, not relocating."



Fang Ru bowed.



The former Empress smiled.



She was beginning to enjoy the fact that a visit could remain a visit.



She did not need to decide where she would live permanently.



She did not need to move all her belongings.



She did not need to make the visit into a statement about her future.



Tomorrow, she would eat with her family.



That was enough.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao placed the corrected schedule into its document case.



Qiao Yue checked the index.



The documents were complete.



The supporting records were arranged.



The corrections were marked.



The disputed items were not presented as settled matters.



Mingzhao inspected the case herself.



"Tomorrow, we will take this."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Will you stay for the entire meeting?"



"If necessary."



"And if they ask you to return to managing the estate?"



"I will answer."



"Will you be angry?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"That depends on how they ask."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"That is fair."



Mingzhao closed the case.



She had no desire to spend another afternoon explaining why she had left.



She had already explained.



She had no desire to surrender the property division merely to avoid another argument.



She had already spent six years carrying responsibilities that were not properly assigned.



She would not return to them because the family found the new arrangements inconvenient.



She turned off the lamp.



Tomorrow's meeting would be difficult.



But it would be a meeting about records, not a return to the old household.



She intended to keep it that way.

In the Imperial Palace, Emperor Zhao Jingheng reviewed the next day's schedule.



There was a meeting with two ministers.



There was a report from the Revenue Ministry.



There was a routine audience.



There was no visit from Xu Yuru.



He looked at the empty space where her name might once have appeared in a private consultation schedule.



Then he closed the calendar.



The former Empress was visiting her parents.



He would not interrupt her.



Gao Shun stood nearby.



"Your Majesty, the ministers have confirmed their attendance."



"Good."



"The Revenue Ministry has submitted the figures."



"Good."



"The Imperial Household Administration has requested clarification on one transferred duty."



"Send it to the office responsible for the matter."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor looked at him.



"Do not send it to the former Empress."



Gao Shun bowed.



"I would not presume to do so."



The Emperor nodded.



For the first time in a long while, the schedule did not contain an expectation that Yuru would make the difficult parts easier.



It was not yet comfortable.



But it was becoming normal.

The twenty-fifth day ended with three separate preparations.



At the Xu residence, six dishes were still being planned, and Duke Xu had not entirely abandoned his hope of adding another soup.



At the Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao's document case contained the corrections needed for the next property meeting.



At the palace, the Emperor had decided not to interrupt the former Empress's family visit.



The following day would bring two very different gatherings.



One would begin with a meal.



The other would begin with a document case.



Both would involve people who believed they had already explained themselves.



And both would discover that being understood by one's family was considerably harder than being understood by a ledger.
