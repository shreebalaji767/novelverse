# **Chapter 53: The Former Empress Has Returned to Her Natal Family for Breakfast. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Begun a New Production Order. One Household Has Prepared Porridge. The Other Has Discovered That Hiring Four Workers Requires More Than Finding Four People.**

**15th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first difficulty of the morning was that the Duke of Xu had forgotten which breakfast he was supposed to be eating.



He stood in the dining room looking at two trays.



One contained plain porridge, steamed buns, pickled vegetables, and eggs.



The other contained sweet porridge, pastries, fruit, and several dishes that Madam Xu had ordered for the household.



"Which tray is for tomorrow?" he asked.



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Tomorrow is the sixteenth."



"Yes."



"That is when Yuru is visiting."



"Yes."



"Then the first tray is for tomorrow's breakfast."



The Duke looked at the second tray.



"And this one?"



"For today."



He nodded.



After a moment, he asked, "Should we prepare another dish for tomorrow?"



Madam Xu put down her cup.



"No."



The Duke considered this answer.



"Perhaps a little more fruit?"



Madam Xu stared at him.



He decided that the matter required no further discussion.

At her temporary residence, Yuru was preparing to visit the palace gardens the following week.



The Emperor's personal invitation had been received the previous evening.



She had not accepted a specific date.



She intended to wait until her visit to her natal family was complete before arranging another outing.



Fang Ru was sorting several garments.



"Madam, should I prepare the pale blue robe for the Duke's residence?"



Yuru looked at the garments.



"That one is suitable."



"And the embroidered outer garment?"



"No. It is unnecessary for breakfast."



Fang Ru folded the pale blue robe.



"Will you take a gift?"



Yuru considered the question.



"Some tea from the current year's harvest."



Fang Ru nodded.



"How much?"



"A modest quantity."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Madam Xu may say that you should not have brought anything."



"Then she can say it."



"And keep the tea?"



"Naturally."



Fang Ru laughed.



Yuru returned to her book.



She had once spent entire mornings reviewing ceremonial arrangements for visits that required several departments, multiple lists, and the approval of people who had never met the guests.



Now she was preparing for breakfast with her parents.



The difference was refreshing.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao was having her own morning difficulty.



Four additional workers had been selected for the new silk order.



However, selecting four people did not mean that four people were ready to work.



Two had experience with silk finishing.



One had worked with ordinary woven cloth but had never handled the finer material required for the order.



The fourth was young and had only assisted in a workshop.



Lin Shuyun stood beside the production table.



"The first two can begin with the next batch."



"And the other two?"



"They will need instruction."



"Who will train them?"



"The senior finisher."



"Will training interrupt the existing work?"



Lin nodded.



"Somewhat."



"Then the schedule must include that time."



Lin looked at the production ledger.



"I have already included two days."



"Is that sufficient?"



"Possibly."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Possibly is not a production schedule."



Lin laughed.



"You are becoming difficult."



"I am trying to avoid promising a delivery date that the workshop cannot meet."



Lin considered the figures again.



"Three days would be safer."



"Then use three."



Lin amended the schedule.



The first two workers would begin ordinary preparation.



The other two would observe, practise on lower-grade material, and work under supervision until they were ready.



Mingzhao reviewed the arrangement.



"Keep their training records separate from the production output."



"Why?"



"Because training time is a cost, but it is not completed production."



Lin nodded.



"That is sensible."



Mingzhao opened the new order ledger.



The deposit had been recorded.



The materials had not yet been purchased.



The workers' wages would be recorded from the date they began.



The buyer's first inspection would take place after the initial group was completed.



The order was finally underway.



It was also going to require more supervision than either woman had hoped.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received the revised temporary cultivation agreement.



The family legal adviser, Mr Chen, had reviewed it.



The agreement stated that the tenant could continue cultivating the western field for a limited period while the Marquisate considered its future use.



The payment rate was specified.



The arrangement did not renew the expired lease.



Either side could end the temporary permission according to the written notice period.



The tenant was responsible for ordinary cultivation.



The estate retained responsibility for the land's structural boundaries and any necessary major repairs.



Mingyuan read the document.



"Does this protect the family?"



Mr Chen replied, "It makes the arrangement clearer. It does not remove every possible dispute."



Mingyuan nodded.



"And the tenant?"



"He has a written record of his permission to cultivate and the payment terms."



Mingyuan looked at the steward.



"Send him a copy before he signs."



Steward Zhou bowed.



"Yes, Young Master."



Mr Chen gathered his papers.



"You should also remember that the temporary agreement does not settle the property's long-term classification."



"I know."



"Good. Then do not let anyone describe it as a renewed lease in the next property schedule."



Mingyuan nodded.



He had begun to understand that the same property could have several different kinds of information attached to it.



Ownership.



Cultivation.



Payment.



Permission.



Repair obligations.



A single heading could not replace all of them.

Mingxiu was still waiting for the corrected roof estimate.



The contractor had returned with a revised document.



This time, the number of replacement tiles was separated into confirmed damage and possible breakage during removal.



The labour charge was also listed separately.



Mingxiu checked the arithmetic.



The revised total was lower than the previous estimate.



She called the steward.



"Have the southern building inspected again after the first section is opened. If additional damage is found, the contractor must provide a separate report before expanding the work."



The steward nodded.



"Should we begin?"



"Not until the family confirms the repair expenditure."



"Understood."



Mingxiu signed the inspection note.



She had not approved the repair.



She had only approved the corrected estimate for consideration.



That distinction was becoming familiar.

At the palace, the Crown Prince received the first draft of the Eastern Palace stock reconciliation.



The report separated purchases, issues, and remaining quantities.



However, the clerks had used two different units for one item.



Oil had been recorded by jar in the purchase register and by weight in the distribution register.



The Crown Prince examined the figures.



"Can these be compared directly?"



The clerk shook his head.



"Not without knowing the weight of each jar."



"Then do we know the weight?"



"Some receipts specify it. Others do not."



The Crown Prince looked at the report.



"Separate the entries with confirmed weights from those without them."



The clerk bowed.



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Do not calculate a total from incomplete units."



The clerk accepted the papers.



The Crown Prince watched him leave.



He was beginning to understand why Yuru had insisted that every account identify its units.



A number without a unit was often only a decoration.

At the temporary residence, Mingzhao received a letter from Sun Qiming.



The accountant had located another group of northern warehouse records.



They contained references to grain reserved for seed and damaged stock awaiting disposal.



However, the documents did not include a complete annual reconciliation.



Sun requested permission to compare the original counting sheets with the later movement records.



Mingzhao read the letter.



She wrote her approval.



She also added that the original figures should remain unchanged in the working report until the reconciliation was complete.



Qiao Yue watched her seal the reply.



"Is the missing grain closer to being found?"



"We have more records."



"That sounds encouraging."



"It is."



"Then perhaps the matter will end soon."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Do you believe that?"



Qiao Yue considered it.



"No."



"Then you are learning."



Qiao Yue sighed.

The following morning, the sixteenth day of the fifth month, Yuru arrived at the Duke of Xu's residence.



Madam Xu was waiting in the courtyard.



"You're here."



"I said I would come."



"Come inside. The porridge is still warm."



Yuru entered.



The Duke appeared from the dining room.



"I have prepared something for you."



Madam Xu looked suspicious.



"What?"



The Duke gestured towards the table.



There was a small plate of fresh fruit.



Madam Xu looked at him.



"I said no additional dishes."



"It is fruit."



"It is still an additional dish."



Yuru smiled.



"I can eat fruit."



The Duke looked pleased.



Madam Xu sighed.



"Sit down before he finds another reason to add something."



The breakfast was simple.



There was porridge, steamed buns, eggs, vegetables, and fruit.



Yuru ate slowly.



The Duke asked about her temporary residence.



She told him the arrangements were comfortable.



Madam Xu asked whether she had received any further invitations from the palace.



"One invitation to visit the gardens."



"Will you go?"



"Possibly."



The Duke nodded.



"You need not avoid the palace."



"I know."



Madam Xu looked at her daughter.



"And you need not return merely because someone asks."



Yuru smiled.



"I know that too."



They continued eating.



The conversation moved to ordinary matters.



The Duke had received a letter from a cousin.



Madam Xu had been invited to inspect a neighbour's new garden.



A servant had broken a flowerpot.



The cook had complained that the Duke was requesting too many changes to the breakfast menu.



Yuru laughed.



"Father, perhaps you should leave the kitchen alone."



The Duke looked offended.



"I was only making suggestions."



Madam Xu replied, "You suggested three additional dishes yesterday."



"They were small dishes."



"That is not the point."



Yuru lowered her head to hide her smile.



The breakfast continued without documents.



It was a small thing.



For Yuru, it was becoming increasingly precious.

After breakfast, Madam Xu showed her the eastern courtyard again.



The rooms were quiet.



The furniture had been arranged simply.



The study received good morning light.



Yuru stood beside the window.



"I like this room."



Madam Xu smiled.



"It can remain as it is."



"I do not want you to prepare anything else."



"We will not."



The Duke joined them.



"If you decide to stay here, the courtyard is yours to use."



Yuru looked at him.



"I am grateful."



"You need not decide today."



"I know."



The Duke nodded.



He did not ask again.

Meanwhile, at the workshop, the first day of the new silk order was less peaceful.



One of the newly hired workers had misunderstood the preparation instructions.



A length of material had been handled incorrectly.



It was not damaged beyond use, but it required additional inspection.



Lin Shuyun examined it.



"Who gave the instruction?"



The senior finisher stepped forward.



"I did."



"Did you explain the difference between the two grades?"



"I thought I had."



The new worker looked distressed.



"I understood that both could be handled in the same way."



Mingzhao examined the material.



"Can it still be used?"



The senior finisher checked the edge.



"Yes. But it needs to be examined before proceeding."



"Then record the additional inspection."



Lin nodded.



The worker lowered his head.



"I am sorry."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"You are being trained. You are expected to make mistakes. You are also expected to report them."



The worker looked up.



"Yes, Miss."



"Do not hide damaged material. That would be more expensive than the mistake itself."



Lin added, "And do not attempt to correct something you do not understand."



The senior finisher nodded.



The incident was recorded.



No material was written off.



No payment was reduced.



The schedule lost part of the morning.



Mingzhao looked at the production ledger.



"Add the delay to the training record, not the buyer's delivery report."



Lin smiled faintly.



"You really do separate everything."



"Because everything is different."

That evening, Yuru returned to her temporary residence.



Fang Ru asked whether the breakfast had been pleasant.



"Very."



"Was the food sufficient?"



"Yes."



"Did the Duke add anything?"



Yuru smiled.



"Fruit."



Fang Ru laughed.



"Then he followed the instruction."



"Not entirely."



Yuru removed her outer robe.



She looked at the letter from Madam Xu, which she had placed beside her books.



The next visit did not need to be arranged immediately.



There was no reason to turn affection into a schedule.



At the workshop, Mingzhao reviewed the day's production record.



The new order had begun.



Four workers had been hired.



Two required training.



One preparation mistake had been recorded.



No material had been written off.



The first delivery date remained unchanged, though the workshop would monitor the schedule closely.



She closed the ledger.

The sixteenth day of the fifth month ended quietly.



Yuru had enjoyed breakfast with her parents.



The Duke had successfully added fruit.



Madam Xu had successfully prevented him from adding three more dishes.



Mingzhao's new silk order had begun, although the first morning had not gone perfectly.



Mingyuan had prepared a temporary agreement for the western field tenant.



Mingxiu had obtained a corrected repair estimate.



The Crown Prince had separated oil records by their units rather than pretending that jars and weights were interchangeable.



The palace had continued functioning without the former Empress's intervention.



The Marquisate had continued learning how to function without its eldest daughter.



And in the workshop, four new workers had discovered that silk was considerably less forgiving than the merchant's contract had made it sound.
