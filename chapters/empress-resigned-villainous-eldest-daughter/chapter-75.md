# **Chapter 75: The Former Empress Has Returned with Preserved Fruit and Has Not Been Asked to Write a Report About Her Visit. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Learned That an Old Clerk Remembers the Filing Cabinet but Not the Payment. The Workshop Has Prepared Forty-Eight Bolts and Has Been Reminded That Preparation Is Not Acceptance.**

**9th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress Xu Yuru had returned from her parents' home with three things.



The first was a small box of preserved fruit.



The second was a book her father had selected from his study.



The third was a memory of an afternoon in which no one had asked her to resolve anything.



Fang Ru had placed the box on the table the previous evening.



In the morning, Yuru opened it again.



There were several portions, each wrapped separately.



Her mother had apparently decided that sending a single box was not enough. She had divided the preserved fruit into portions suitable for different occasions, which suggested that she expected Yuru to distribute some of it.



Yuru examined the packages.



"Mother has sent enough for several days."



Fang Ru nodded.



"She said the fruit keeps well."



"That is true."



"Would you like me to arrange some for the attendants?"



"Yes. Give them a portion each. Keep one for yourself."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Your mother also sent a separate packet for me."



Yuru looked at her.



"Then she has anticipated the arrangement."



"She said that I should not be expected to share yours."



Yuru laughed.



"That sounds like her."



Fang Ru placed the separate packet beside her own things.



Yuru then opened the book her father had given her.



It was an account of provincial administration, written several decades earlier. The author described the organization of local granaries, irrigation maintenance, tax collection, and the difficulties of keeping official records consistent across different districts.



Yuru read the preface.



Her father had chosen a book that was not particularly light.



She smiled.



He had probably thought it would interest her.



He had been correct.



She turned the page.



For the next hour, no one interrupted her.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, the retired clerk Chen Rui received the inquiry before midday.



He had left the estate accounts office several years earlier and now lived in a modest house in the southern district.



His son brought the letter to him.



Chen Rui read it carefully.



The letter included the date of the payment, the amount, a copy of the old entry, and the relevant seal-register record.



He read the material twice.



Then he placed it on the table.



His son asked, "Father, do you remember it?"



Chen Rui adjusted his spectacles.



"I remember the period."



"Do you remember the payment?"



"Not yet."



He examined the copied entry again.



The handwriting was familiar. The seal impression was also familiar.



He looked at the reference number.



"Where did they find this?"



"The Marquisate's accounts office."



"Do they still have the original voucher?"



"They have not found it."



Chen Rui frowned.



"Then why are they asking me?"



"Perhaps they think you remember."



Chen Rui tapped the paper.



"I remember thousands of vouchers."



His son said nothing.



Chen Rui continued reading.



"The purchasing seal was in my custody on this date. That part is correct."



"Then you can tell them who received the money?"



"No."



His son looked surprised.



"Why not?"



"Because holding the seal does not mean I personally delivered every payment."



Chen Rui folded the paper.



"The accounts office had clerks who prepared vouchers, assistants who carried payments, and stewards who received goods. The seal was used after the supporting papers were checked. It did not record every person who touched the money."



He stood and went to a cabinet.



After searching through several bundles of old correspondence, he found a small notebook.



The pages were worn, but the writing remained legible.



He turned to the relevant year.



There was a short entry concerning the organization of the accounts office.



Chen Rui read it.



Then he read it again.



His son waited.



"Have you found something?"



"Perhaps."



Chen Rui pointed to a line.



"During that period, the voucher files were arranged by category rather than by the name of the person who received the payment."



"Would that explain why they cannot find it?"



"It may explain where they should search."



He turned another page.



"The purchasing vouchers were stored separately from the household expenditure vouchers. If the entry was filed under a general purchasing category, it may not be in the same box as the cash book."



His son looked at the letter.



"Should we write this?"



"Yes. But make clear that I am describing the filing system. I am not identifying the transaction."



He dictated slowly.



The letter explained that the purchasing seal had been held by Chen Rui on the recorded date. It also explained that the accounts office used separate filing categories for general purchases, household expenditure, maintenance, and payments made through external suppliers.



Chen Rui added that the relevant voucher might therefore have been filed under the purchasing category rather than under the name of the person who authorized it.



He did not claim to remember the payment.



He did not identify a recipient.



He did not accuse anyone.



When he finished, he signed the letter.



His son looked at the pages.



"That is not a complete answer."



"No."



"Will the Marquis be dissatisfied?"



Chen Rui placed the letter in its envelope.



"If they wanted a complete answer, they should have kept complete records."



He handed it to his son.



"Send it."

Mingyuan received Chen Rui's reply late in the afternoon.



He read the letter once, then called Steward Zhou.



The steward arrived with the current accounts file.



"Chen Rui remembers the filing system," Mingyuan said.



Steward Zhou examined the letter.



"That is useful."



"He says the purchasing vouchers were stored separately from the household expenditure vouchers."



"Then the accounts office may have searched the wrong storage section."



Mingyuan frowned.



"Did they search by the date?"



"Yes."



"Then they may have searched the date index but not the category files."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"That is possible."



Mingyuan looked at the old cash book.



The payment had been recorded under an instruction from Mingzhao. The seal register showed that Chen Rui had held the purchasing seal. The original voucher had been indexed, but not yet found.



Now they had a possible filing location.



"Send a request to search the purchasing category files for that date and amount," he said.



"Should we ask Chen Rui to come to the estate?"



"Not yet. He has given us what he remembers. We should search the records first."



Steward Zhou agreed.



Mingyuan placed the letter beside the copied voucher.



There was something else he wanted to ask.



"Steward Zhou, did the accounts office ever keep a separate register for payments made to individual suppliers?"



"Yes, but it was not maintained consistently in every period."



"Could it help?"



"It might. We should ask for it if the voucher search fails."



Mingyuan nodded.



He had learned not to demand every possible record at once.



The accounts office needed a clear request, not an endless list of tasks.



At the other table, Liang Wen was checking the consolidated purchasing register.



He had completed another section.



"Young Master," he said, "the purchasing entries for the same period contain several references to the category files."



Mingyuan turned.



"How many?"



"Seven."



"Do they all concern the same type of purchase?"



"No. Three are for building materials, two for household furnishings, and two are general supplier payments."



Mingyuan considered the matter.



"Mark them separately. Do not assume they are connected to the payment we are investigating."



Liang Wen bowed.



"Understood."



Steward Zhou looked at the new register.



The young master was learning to avoid connecting entries merely because they appeared close together.



This was another improvement.



It was also likely to make the examination take longer.



Steward Zhou decided not to mention that.

Shen Mingzhao received a copy of Chen Rui's letter the following morning.



She read it at her writing table.



Qiao Yue stood nearby, arranging the correspondence.



"Does he remember the payment?" Qiao Yue asked.



"No."



"Then what does he remember?"



"The filing system."



Mingzhao read the relevant paragraph again.



"That may help them locate the voucher."



"Will it?"



"If the voucher still exists."



Qiao Yue glanced at the letter.



"Do you remember how the files were arranged?"



"Yes. The purchasing vouchers were usually kept separately from the household expenditure records."



"Then Chen Rui's recollection is correct."



"It is consistent with what I remember."



Mingzhao placed the letter beside the other records.



She did not need to write another explanation immediately.



Her brother had not asked her to identify the recipient.



The current evidence did not justify another question.



She looked at the papers.



Nine advances remained confirmed.



Four payments had been confirmed, but their repayment arrangements remained unclear.



Ten other claims remained uncertain.



The seventy-two taels for the study repairs were still classified as estate maintenance paid from her personal funds, with reimbursement unresolved.



The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The payment under her authorization was a separate matter.



It should not be added to the confirmed advances merely because her name appeared in the entry.



She took a clean sheet and wrote a brief note for her own records.



*Old purchasing payment: authorization recorded; estate purchasing seal held by Chen Rui; original voucher not yet located; filing category may be general purchasing files. Recipient and purpose remain unconfirmed.*



She placed the note in the relevant folder.



Qiao Yue watched her.



"You have not sent a reply."



"There is nothing to reply to."



"Will you ask them to send the voucher when they find it?"



"They know that."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao closed the folder.



She had spent years making sure that the Marquisate's affairs continued moving.



Now she was learning to let the investigation proceed without her standing beside every table.



It was not effortless.



She still noticed mistakes.



She still wanted to correct unclear wording.



But she was no longer responsible for making the entire estate function.



That responsibility belonged to the people who had accepted it.

At the silk workshop, the forty-eight-bolt group was ready for its final internal inspection.



Lin Shuyun stood beside the inspection table with the production ledger.



The first twenty-four bolts had passed internal inspection the previous day.



The second twenty-four had now completed folding and finishing.



The workers had checked them against the approved sample.



Two required minor adjustments.



Both were corrected before the inspection began.



Mingzhao arrived as the final inspection was being recorded.



"How many have passed?" she asked.



"Forty-eight," Lin Shuyun replied.



"All forty-eight?"



"All forty-eight."



Mingzhao examined the inspection sheet.



The two adjusted bolts were separately recorded.



The remaining forty-six had passed without correction.



"Then the group is ready for the buyer."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



"The buyer is expected tomorrow."



"Has Zhou Jian confirmed the time?"



"Not yet. The message only confirmed the date."



"Then keep the bolts arranged and protected. Do not leave them exposed near the entrance."



Lin Shuyun gave the necessary instruction.



A worker began moving the finished group to the storage area.



Mingzhao examined the production ledger.



"How many have been accepted by the buyer?"



"Ninety-six."



"And how many are ready for inspection now?"



"Forty-eight."



"How many remain outside this group?"



"Fifty-six."



Mingzhao nodded.



The figures were consistent.



Ninety-six accepted.



Forty-eight internally inspected and awaiting the buyer.



Fifty-six still to be produced.



The total was two hundred.



She checked the numbers once more.



Lin Shuyun watched her.



"You are counting again."



"I am checking."



"Is there a difference?"



"Yes."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"Then I will not interrupt."



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



"Have the trainees finished their wage records?"



"Yes. Both signed yesterday."



"Any further mistakes?"



"One trainee folded an edge too tightly this morning. He noticed it himself before the supervisor pointed it out."



"Was it corrected?"



"Yes. The material was usable."



"Record it."



"It has been recorded."



Mingzhao nodded.



She looked toward the worktables.



The trainees were now working more independently, though they still required supervision.



The careful one had learned to complete his work within the allotted time.



The faster one had become more reliable about checking his own output.



Neither was ready to work without oversight.



That was not a failure.



They were still learning.



Lin Shuyun picked up the inspection list.



"The buyer may ask about the revised folding method."



"Then show him the approved sample and the written instruction."



"He may ask why we changed it."



"Explain that the buyer approved the revised method after comparing the samples. Do not describe it as an independent change."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



"And if he asks whether it has improved the production rate?"



"Give him the recorded figures, not an estimate."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"You have become very particular about estimates."



"I have become very particular about people presenting estimates as results."



The two women returned to the office.



The workshop had made progress.



But the order was not finished.



The buyer's acceptance was not a formality.



And the final profit could not yet be calculated.



Mingzhao left the workshop satisfied that the next inspection could proceed without unnecessary changes.

In the palace, the Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an received the revised report from the Eastern Palace household office.



The clerks had separated the oil entries into three groups.



The first contained purchases recorded by weight.



The second contained jars with known capacity.



The third contained jars whose capacity was not stated.



Cheng'an examined the report.



"Have the original descriptions been preserved?"



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Have any estimated conversions been included in the confirmed total?"



"No. The estimated conversions are shown in a separate column."



Cheng'an nodded.



"Good."



He looked at the remaining unresolved entries.



"Has the household office searched the older purchase registers?"



"Not yet. The clerks requested permission to do so."



"Then authorize the search."



The clerk bowed.



Cheng'an put down the report.



"Do not delay the ordinary purchases while the old records are being examined. The current stock must still be monitored."



"Yes, Your Highness."



The clerk withdrew.



Cheng'an turned to the next document.



The palace administration had been operating without the former Empress's direct intervention for several weeks.



It had not become effortless.



Some officials still hesitated when an unfamiliar problem appeared.



Some still wanted to ask Yuru.



But the Crown Prince was learning that asking her was not the same as resolving the matter himself.



He had to understand the records.



He had to make decisions.



He had to accept that some answers would remain incomplete.



The lesson was becoming expensive in time.



It was also becoming useful.

At the guest residence, Yuru was reading the book her father had given her.



She had reached a chapter about provincial granaries.



The author described the difference between grain entered into storage, grain available for immediate issue, and grain recorded as reserved for future distribution.



Yuru paused.



She read the paragraph again.



The distinction was familiar.



She had dealt with similar problems in the palace stores.



For a moment, she considered writing a note to the Crown Prince about it.



Then she stopped.



Cheng'an had already been learning to distinguish stock categories.



The book did not contain a new principle.



It merely explained an old one.



She turned the page.



Fang Ru entered with tea.



"Are you enjoying the book?"



"Yes."



"Your father chose well."



"He did."



Fang Ru placed the cup beside her.



"Would you like to send him a message?"



"Perhaps tomorrow."



"Shall I prepare paper?"



"Not yet."



Yuru continued reading.



She was not refusing to speak with the palace.



She was not refusing to advise the Crown Prince if he asked a personal question.



But she did not need to turn every useful observation into an instruction.



The distinction was still new.



She intended to keep practicing it.

That evening, the Marquisate's accounts office received the request to search the general purchasing files for the old voucher.



The request included the date, amount, seal-register reference, and the category suggested by Chen Rui.



It did not name a suspected recipient.



It did not accuse the retired clerk.



It did not claim that the money had been stolen.



The accounts office began the search.



At the silk workshop, the forty-eight bolts were stored and covered in preparation for the buyer's visit.



At the palace, the Eastern Palace's oil records remained divided into confirmed and unresolved categories.



At the guest residence, Yuru read another chapter of her father's book.



And at the temporary residence near the eastern market, Mingzhao placed the day's correspondence in its proper folder.



Nothing had been settled.



But the next steps were clear.



The old payment might yet be identified if the voucher could be found.



The workshop's next group might be accepted if it met the agreed specifications.



The palace's accounts might become more reliable if the clerks continued to preserve what was known and what was not.



And Yuru's visit to her parents had given her something that no official report could measure.



A pleasant afternoon.



She decided that she would not attempt to calculate its value.
