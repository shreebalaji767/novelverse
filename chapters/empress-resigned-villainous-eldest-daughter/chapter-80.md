# **Chapter 80: The Former Empress Has Received an Invitation to Borrow Another Book. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received the Last Production Report. The Workshop Has Discovered That Twenty-Eight Bolts Are Easier to Count Than Twenty-Eight Different Reasons for Delaying Payment.**

**14th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress had received another letter from her mother.



It arrived shortly after breakfast, carried by a servant from the Xu residence. The envelope was ordinary, the handwriting familiar, and the contents were brief.



Madam Xu had informed her that her father had selected another book.



Yuru read the description.



It concerned the management of household estates, including repairs, tenant agreements, seasonal expenditure, and the responsibilities of stewards.



She looked at the paper for a moment.



Fang Ru, who was arranging the tea service, noticed her expression.



“Another book about administration?”



“Apparently.”



“Your father has a particular idea of what makes suitable reading.”



“He does.”



“Would you like to borrow it?”



Yuru folded the letter.



“I said I would be pleased to borrow another book. I did not say he should choose one that resembles an examination.”



Fang Ru smiled.



“Perhaps he believes it will be useful.”



“Perhaps he believes I have forgotten how to read anything else.”



“Will you refuse it?”



“No. I will read it.”



Yuru placed the letter beside the tea tray.



She was not opposed to reading about household administration. She had spent much of her life studying the subject, and there were still things she found interesting.



What she no longer wanted was the assumption that reading about a problem meant agreeing to manage it.



She could read about repairs without becoming responsible for a roof.



She could read about grain storage without being asked to supervise a granary.



She could discuss a household's accounts without becoming its unpaid accountant.



The distinction was becoming easier to maintain.



Fang Ru poured tea.



“Shall I arrange a visit to the Xu residence?”



“Not today. I have no reason to hurry.”



“Then we can wait.”



Yuru nodded.



She intended to return her father's first book and borrow the second during her next visit. There was no need to turn the matter into an appointment merely because the book had been selected.



She spent the rest of the morning reading.



The western courtyard was quiet. A few attendants crossed the paved path carrying baskets. Somewhere beyond the wall, a servant was speaking to a gardener about a damaged branch.



Yuru heard the conversation, then continued reading.



The gardener would deal with the branch.



That was what gardeners were employed to do.

At the eastern market residence, Shen Mingzhao received a report that was considerably less literary.



The second group of twenty-eight bolts had completed finishing.



The internal inspection had begun before breakfast and continued through the morning.



Twenty-five passed.



Two required adjustments to their edges.



One had a loose fold that needed to be corrected before it could be approved.



Mingzhao read the report twice.



“Was the loose fold caused by the same method as the previous correction?”



“No,” Qiao Yue replied. “The supervisor believes the worker had not secured the inner layer properly before finishing.”



“Was the worker trained?”



“Yes.”



“Experienced?”



“Yes.”



Mingzhao placed the report down.



“Then the instruction was not followed.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



“The supervisor has asked whether the worker should be removed from this order.”



“Not yet. First establish whether this was a single mistake or a repeated problem.”



“If it happens again?”



“Then the supervisor should decide whether the worker is suitable for this stage of production.”



Qiao Yue recorded the instruction.



The workshop had learned that a mistake did not automatically mean a worker was incapable. It also had to learn that experience did not excuse carelessness.



Mingzhao examined the production totals.



One hundred and forty-four bolts had been accepted by Zhou Wenqi's representative.



The first remaining group of twenty-eight had passed internal inspection on the previous day.



The second group was now being corrected.



Once those corrections were completed, the entire remaining quantity could be presented for buyer inspection.



But the buyer had not yet scheduled the visit.



“Has Zhou Jian sent any message?”



“Not yet.”



“Then send him a short note confirming that the first group is ready and the second group is undergoing its final inspection. Ask him to suggest a suitable time for the next inspection.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



“Should we ask him to inspect both groups together?”



“Yes, if his schedule permits. But do not imply that the second group has passed inspection.”



“I will make that clear.”



Mingzhao returned to the accounts.



There was another matter she wanted to examine before the buyer arrived.



The order's expenses had been entered separately from the workshop's ordinary operating costs. Training wages, supervision, materials, corrections, and transport preparations had all been recorded.



She had asked Sun Qiming to review the structure of the accounts when the order was completed.



Until then, she would not announce a profit.



Qiao Yue glanced at the ledger.



“You have become very cautious about declaring success.”



“Success is not a number I can write down whenever I am pleased.”



“Some merchants might disagree.”



“Then some merchants may be surprised when their money runs out.”



Qiao Yue laughed.

The Marquisate's revised purchasing procedure was completed shortly before noon.



Liang Wen had added the final clarification concerning urgent purchases.



An urgent purchase could be made before ordinary authorization only when waiting would cause a demonstrable interruption or further damage. The reason had to be recorded, and the supervising official had to confirm the expenditure afterward.



The document also required that all corrections preserve the original entry, identify the person making the correction, and state the supporting evidence.



Mingyuan read the clean copy.



He checked the five assigned responsibilities again.



He checked the sections concerning partial deliveries.



He checked the correction procedure.



Then he examined the urgent-purchase clause.



“Good,” he said.



Liang Wen waited.



“Is there anything else?”



Mingyuan considered the document.



“Not in the draft.”



Steward Zhou looked at the pages.



“Then we can send it.”



Mingyuan nodded.



A copy was prepared for Mingzhao.



The covering letter was short.



It explained that the Marquisate had completed a draft of the future purchasing procedure and invited her to review it for any factual errors concerning the previous arrangements.



It did not ask her to supervise implementation.



It did not ask her to train the clerks.



It did not ask her to resume managing the estate.



Mingyuan had insisted on that wording.



He knew that his sister had agreed to review the completed document, not to accept responsibility for the work that followed.



The letter was sealed and sent to the eastern market residence.



Afterward, Mingyuan returned to the accounts room.



The procedure had been completed.



The estate's accounts had not.

Mingzhao received the document in the early afternoon.



She read the covering letter first.



Then she examined the procedure.



Qiao Yue brought her a fresh cup of tea and found the table covered with pages.



“Is this the document from the Marquisate?”



“Yes.”



“Is it complete?”



“It appears to be.”



“Will you approve it?”



Mingzhao did not answer immediately.



She read the first section, then the second.



The responsibilities were separated clearly. The authorization stage was distinct from the ordering stage. The receiving steward had to record actual quantities, not simply confirm that an order had been placed. The accounts clerk had to verify supporting documents before payment.



The register keeper had to maintain a consolidated record.



The document also addressed partial deliveries, rejected goods, corrections, and urgent purchases.



It was considerably better than the first draft.



Mingzhao turned to the section concerning discrepancies.



She stopped at one sentence.



“Qiao Yue.”



“Yes?”



“Read this.”



Qiao Yue leaned over the table.



The sentence stated that when a discrepancy arose between the ordered quantity and the received quantity, the receiving steward was to record the difference and notify the supervising official.



Qiao Yue read it twice.



“What is wrong?”



“What happens if the receiving steward is the person who made the incorrect entry?”



Qiao Yue considered it.



“Then the supervising official must examine it.”



“And who records the correction?”



“The register keeper?”



“Possibly. But the procedure does not say that the register keeper must be independent of the original entry.”



Qiao Yue looked at the document again.



Mingzhao took a writing brush and marked the margin.



She did not rewrite the procedure.



She noted the ambiguity and suggested that a discrepancy be verified by someone other than the person responsible for the disputed receipt whenever possible.



Then she turned to the payment section.



The accounts clerk was required to check the order, receipt record, and authorization before payment.



However, the procedure did not explicitly state what should happen if one of those records was missing.



Mingzhao marked that as well.



“A missing document should not become an automatic reason to invent a replacement,” she said.



Qiao Yue nodded.



“Then payment remains suspended until the missing record is examined?”



“Unless a separate lawful authorization applies. The procedure should say what evidence is required and who may approve an exception.”



She continued reading.



There were two more small points.



One concerned the record of goods rejected after delivery.



The other concerned the responsibility for retaining original vouchers.



Neither was fatal, but both required clarification.



Mingzhao finished the document and placed it beside the covering letter.



Qiao Yue looked at the marked pages.



“You have found several issues.”



“Three.”



“Is that a good result?”



“It is better than finding none and discovering them later.”



“Will you write a reply?”



“Yes.”



Mingzhao took a fresh sheet of paper.



Her response was brief.



She thanked Mingyuan for sending the completed draft. She confirmed that the document reflected the general separation of responsibilities she had previously described, but identified three matters requiring clarification.



She also stated that her review concerned the accuracy and completeness of the written procedure only. She would not undertake its implementation, staff supervision, or future purchasing administration.



She signed the letter.



Qiao Yue sealed it.



Mingzhao had fulfilled her agreement.



Nothing more had been promised.

In the Marquisate, Mingyuan received her reply late in the afternoon.



He read it while Liang Wen waited.



The first two observations were straightforward.



The third required some discussion.



Liang Wen examined the marked copy.



“She is right about the missing supporting documents,” he said.



Mingyuan nodded.



“If a voucher is missing, the accounts clerk cannot simply write another one and pretend it is original.”



“We can require a separate explanatory record, dated and signed, with the reason the original is unavailable.”



“And approval from the supervising official?”



“Yes.”



Mingyuan considered the second point.



“Who should verify a disputed receipt?”



“Someone who did not make the original entry.”



“Can we always arrange that?”



“Not always. The estate has limited staff.”



“Then the procedure should distinguish ordinary verification from an exception. If there is no independent person available, the reason must be recorded and a higher official should review it.”



Steward Zhou agreed.



Mingyuan looked at his sister's comments again.



“She has not asked us to change the entire procedure.”



“No,” Liang Wen replied. “She has identified places where it may fail.”



Mingyuan nodded.



That was what she had agreed to do.



He had once expected her to solve every administrative difficulty personally. Now he was learning that she could offer an observation without accepting responsibility for the outcome.



It was an uncomfortable distinction.



It was also a necessary one.



“Revise these sections,” he said. “Then circulate the final procedure to the relevant staff.”



Liang Wen gathered the pages.



“And the old payment?”



Mingyuan looked toward the other stack of documents.



“Continue the search.”



The original voucher had not been found.



The matching amounts remained only possible leads.



The missing grain destination remained unresolved.



The Marquisate's property division remained incomplete.



There was no reason to pretend otherwise.

The buyer's reply arrived at Mingzhao's residence shortly before sunset.



Zhou Jian had received her note.



He would visit the workshop on the following day, provided the second group passed internal inspection in time. He intended to inspect both remaining groups together.



Mingzhao read the message.



“Good,” she said.



Qiao Yue smiled.



“Then the order may finally be finished tomorrow.”



“It may be inspected tomorrow.”



“And if everything passes?”



“Then it may be accepted.”



“And if it is accepted?”



“Then the remaining payment will be settled according to the contract.”



Qiao Yue sighed.



“You have turned a simple sentence into four conditions.”



“Because there are four conditions.”



Qiao Yue laughed.



Mingzhao handed her the message.



“Send confirmation to the workshop. They must not rush the second group merely to meet the inspection.”



“I will.”



“Tell the supervisor to complete the corrections properly and retain the inspection records.”



“Yes.”



“And make sure the buyer's approved sample is available.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



The workshop had been preparing for this moment for weeks.



The first one hundred and forty-four bolts had been accepted. The remaining fifty-six were nearly ready.



The workers were eager to finish.



Mingzhao understood that feeling.



But she had no intention of allowing the last stage of the order to become the least careful.

In the palace, Yuru received no new documents.



She spent the afternoon reading and later sat with Fang Ru near the courtyard window.



The letter from her mother remained on the writing table.



Yuru had decided that she would visit the Xu residence again when it was convenient. She would return her father's book and borrow the next one.



She had no other plans.



Fang Ru brought a small dish of fruit.



“Madam, the kitchen has sent these.”



Yuru looked up from her book.



“Thank you.”



Fang Ru placed the dish beside her.



“Would you like me to arrange the next visit to the Xu residence?”



“Not yet.”



“Very well.”



Yuru selected a piece of fruit.



She had spent many years believing that every available hour ought to be used productively.



Now she was discovering that a quiet afternoon could be complete without producing anything.



She read another page.



Then another.



The palace continued its work beyond the courtyard.

That evening, the Marquisate had a revised procedure, but not yet a final one.



The workshop had a confirmed buyer inspection for the following day, but not yet a final acceptance.



Mingzhao had reviewed the document she had promised to review, but had not resumed her former duties.



Yuru had been offered another book, but had not yet borrowed it.



Every matter had advanced.



None had been forced to become finished merely to satisfy someone waiting for a conclusion.



In the palace, this was becoming a habit.



At the Marquisate, it was becoming a lesson.



And in the workshop, fifty-six bolts were waiting for inspection.



The workers hoped that all fifty-six would pass.



Mingzhao hoped they would too.



But she had already instructed the supervisor not to confuse a deadline with permission to lower the standard.



The following morning would provide the answer.



For once, the Marquisate was also learning to wait for evidence before declaring something complete.
