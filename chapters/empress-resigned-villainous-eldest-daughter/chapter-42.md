# **Chapter 42: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to the Imperial Gardens. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Survey Report. The Imperial Gardens Have Prepared Refreshments, While the Survey Report Has Prepared More Questions.**

**4th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Empress Dowager had requested a quiet morning.



The imperial gardens had interpreted this request as an instruction to prepare for an important occasion.



By the time Xu Yuru arrived, the eastern pavilion had been swept, the stone paths washed, the tea tables arranged, and a small group of servants stationed at intervals along the garden.



There were also two trays of refreshments.



One contained fruit.



The other contained enough pastries to feed a small household.



Yuru stopped at the entrance.



"Mother, you said we were going for a walk."



The Empress Dowager looked at the pavilion.



"We are."



"Then why are there twelve servants?"



"Because I asked for a quiet morning, not an unattended one."



Yuru considered this distinction.



It was not entirely unreasonable.



The Dowager motioned for her to sit.



"Your father-in-law has been asking whether you are comfortable at your natal residence."



"I am comfortable."



"Has he asked you to move there?"



"He has offered the eastern courtyard."



"And?"



"I have not decided."



The Dowager poured tea.



"Your family has been very careful not to hurry you."



"They have been trying."



"That is not the same as succeeding."



Yuru smiled.



The Dowager looked at the garden pond.



"When I was younger, I believed that a woman who left an important position should immediately find another useful position."



Yuru looked towards her.



"And now?"



"Now I suspect that the world has enough people who want to assign work to women without being asked."



Yuru's expression softened.



The Dowager picked up a pastry.



"Do not misunderstand me. I still believe that the imperial household requires order. I do not believe that order must depend upon one person."



"That is a considerable change."



"It is a practical conclusion."



Yuru nodded.



They sat quietly for a while.



The Dowager eventually asked, "Have you considered what you will do with your days?"



"Read. Visit my family. Perhaps travel when the arrangements are suitable."



"Travel?"



"Within the country, at least."



The Dowager looked mildly surprised.



"You have not travelled freely for many years."



"No."



"Where would you go?"



"I have not decided."



The Dowager gave a small laugh.



"You have become very fond of not deciding."



"I have spent many years deciding things that other people should have decided themselves."



The Dowager did not argue.



A servant approached with a tray.



"Your Majesty, the third princess has arrived."



The Dowager looked at Yuru.



"She has brought her account book."



Yuru sighed very slightly.



"Then this is not entirely a quiet morning."



The Dowager smiled.



"I did not promise that it would be."



Third Princess Zhao Minglan arrived carrying two books and a folded sheet of paper.



She greeted the Dowager, then turned towards Yuru.



"Madam, I have finished revising the household expenditure record."



Yuru accepted the sheet.



Minglan had divided the entries into three categories: books purchased for her private study, books held in the shared palace library, and books temporarily borrowed from the Department of Rites.



The categories were sensible.



The descriptions were not.



Yuru read the first line.



"Why have you written 'books that belong to me'?"



"Because they are in my study."



"That is not necessarily the same thing."



Minglan looked down.



"I thought it was."



"Suppose the palace purchases a book for your education but records it as part of the shared library. Where does it belong?"



"In the shared library."



"Suppose it is purchased specifically for your private study and is not intended to circulate?"



"Then it belongs to my private study."



"Good. Now suppose you borrow a book from the Department of Rites and keep it for two months."



Minglan hesitated.



"It still belongs to the Department of Rites."



"Exactly."



The princess took back the sheet and made a correction.



The Dowager watched them.



"Does the Department of Rites know that its books are being borrowed?"



Minglan looked uncomfortable.



"Some of them."



"Some?"



"The librarian has a record."



"Then the record should be complete."



Yuru returned the sheet.



"Ask the librarian to confirm the borrowed books. Do not guess from what is currently in your room."



Minglan nodded.



She was beginning to understand that a record did not describe what someone believed had happened.



It described what could be established.



The Dowager poured more tea.



"Will you continue teaching her?"



Yuru looked at Minglan.



"I can teach her how to examine a record. The responsible offices must still maintain the records."



The Dowager nodded.



"That is acceptable."



Minglan looked between them.



"Then may I ask another question?"



"Yes," Yuru said.



"If an office makes a mistake, should I report it directly to the Emperor?"



Yuru considered this.



"What kind of mistake?"



"A missing entry in the library record."



"Have you asked the librarian?"



"Not yet."



"Then begin there."



"What if the librarian refuses?"



"Then ask the supervising office to review it."



"And if that office refuses?"



"Then you may bring the matter to your father through the proper channel."



Minglan looked thoughtful.



"So I should not immediately go to the Emperor."



"Not unless the matter requires his attention."



The Dowager took a sip of tea.



"That is a lesson several adults in this palace would benefit from."



Yuru did not disagree.



At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, Zhao Jingheng was reading a separate report.



It concerned the palace's annual repair expenditure.



The Imperial Household Administration had identified several items that had previously been recorded under broad headings. The new classification separated ordinary maintenance, ceremonial preparation, replacement of worn equipment, and emergency repairs.



The Emperor examined the figures.



"Have the departments confirmed these totals?"



"Most have, Your Majesty. Two offices have requested additional time to reconcile their records."



"How much time?"



"Five days."



"Grant it."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor looked at the report again.



"Has anyone sent this to the former Empress?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



He turned another page.



A small note had been attached to the report.



It stated that the palace's eastern corridor lamps had been classified as ordinary equipment, except for six lamps reserved for formal processions.



The distinction had taken three days to establish.



Zhao Jingheng read the note.



"Three days?"



"The stores office had one inventory. The Department of Rites had another. The administration needed to compare them."



"How many people were involved?"



"Nine officials, Your Majesty."



The Emperor stared at Gao Shun.



"Nine officials to count six lamps?"



"Not all nine were counting the lamps."



"Then what were the others doing?"



"Comparing the records."



The Emperor put the report down.



"At least the records now agree."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor leaned back.



He was beginning to discover that the palace's old habits had been supported by many people, even when the responsibility appeared to belong to one person.



Yuru had not personally counted every lamp.



She had simply become the person everyone expected to settle disagreements about them.



That was not the same thing as efficient administration.



He turned to another document.



"Where is the Crown Prince?"



"Reviewing the Eastern Palace accounts."



"And the Second Prince?"



"Meeting his household steward."



"The third princess?"



"With the Empress Dowager and the former Empress."



Zhao Jingheng nodded.



"Then the morning is proceeding properly."



Gao Shun waited.



The Emperor picked up his brush.



After a moment, he asked, "Has the kitchen replaced the broken scale?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Good."



He did not ask for a report.



At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received Master Luo's preliminary survey report.



It was longer than she expected.



The first page contained the measurements of the undisputed portion of the northern canal property.



The second described the disputed strip beside the drainage channel.



The third listed the documents required for a formal comparison with the neighbouring parcel.



The fourth contained a note about the old stone marker.



The marker had not been located.



The fifth page contained the survey assistant's observations.



Mingzhao read everything carefully.



Qiao Yue sat opposite her, holding a cup of tea.



"Is the boundary settled?"



"No."



"Is the report useful?"



"Yes."



"That sounds contradictory."



"It is not."



Mingzhao placed the report beside the old registration description.



"The survey establishes the current physical measurements. It does not establish the legal boundary because the old marker is missing and the descriptions are incomplete."



Qiao Yue looked at the pages.



"Can the land office settle it?"



"Possibly. They need the neighbouring registration record."



"Has Mr Qian agreed?"



"He agreed to attend the inspection. He has not agreed to any boundary."



"Then what happens next?"



"We obtain the records."



Mingzhao wrote a letter to Master Luo requesting that he apply for the neighbouring parcel's registration description through the proper office.



She also asked him to identify whether the old drainage channel appeared on any historical map.



She did not instruct him to treat the current channel as the legal boundary.



After sealing the letter, she turned to the cost estimates.



The roof repair remained approximately one hundred and twenty taels.



Drainage work remained approximately thirty.



The kitchen repairs were lower than initially estimated.



The well stones still required attention.



The undisputed land area had now been measured, but the disputed strip could not be included in a reliable purchase calculation.



Mingzhao took out a fresh sheet.



She wrote:



*Purchase decision deferred pending boundary determination and final repair estimate.*



Qiao Yue read it.



"That is all?"



"That is all."



"After all the inspections?"



"Especially after all the inspections."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Perhaps the house should be grateful that we have not purchased it."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Why?"



"Because if we had purchased it, we would now own the drainage channel, the missing marker, and everyone else's arguments."



Mingzhao considered the possibility.



Then she returned to her ledger.



"That would be an expensive collection."



A messenger from the Marquisate arrived shortly before noon.



He carried a letter from Shen Guowei.



Mingzhao recognized her father's handwriting.



She opened it.



The letter was brief.



Her father informed her that the family had received the latest preliminary property schedule. He had also asked Mr Chen to identify which documents were still required before the next settlement meeting.



He did not ask her to review the household accounts.



He did not ask her to return.



He did, however, mention that the northern warehouse records were proving difficult to reconcile.



Mingzhao read the final paragraph twice.



Her father had written:



*Your younger brother has undertaken the review. The matter is proceeding slowly, but it is proceeding.*



She placed the letter on the table.



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Is that good news?"



"It is a factual statement."



"That is not an answer."



"It is a good sign that he is not asking me to do it."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao wrote a reply.



She thanked her father for the update and confirmed that she would attend the next property settlement meeting once the independent accountant had completed the outstanding review.



She also clarified that her participation would concern her own claims and the division of property, not a general review of the Marquisate's expenditure.



She sealed the letter.



Then she returned to the family schedule.



The figures remained unchanged.



Seventy-two taels of study repair expenditure were confirmed as estate maintenance.



Two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.



Nine personal advances were confirmed.



Four were partially confirmed.



Ten remained uncertain.



She had no intention of converting uncertainty into a convenient number merely because the family wanted the settlement completed.



At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received his father's copy of the letter to Mingzhao.



He read it, then placed it beside the warehouse ledgers.



Madam Liang entered.



"Your father has written to your sister?"



"Yes."



"Has she replied?"



"Not yet."



Madam Liang looked at the ledgers.



"Are you still examining the grain records?"



"Yes."



"Have you found the missing sacks?"



"No."



"Then why are you spending so much time on them?"



Mingyuan looked up.



"Because the records are incomplete."



Madam Liang sighed.



"Your sister used to settle these things quickly."



Mingyuan lowered his eyes.



"She had been examining them for years."



Madam Liang looked at him.



He continued.



"I have been examining them for a few days."



The words were not angry.



They were simply true.



Madam Liang sat down.



"Your father says the next settlement meeting may be delayed."



"Until Sun Qiming completes his review."



"That could take weeks."



"Then it will take weeks."



She looked at him with visible dissatisfaction.



"Your sister has always been impatient with the family."



Mingyuan did not answer.



He was no longer certain that the impatience had been entirely unreasonable.



He turned a page.



The northern warehouse discrepancy remained.



The twenty-sack movement instruction still had only eighteen confirmed receipts.



The two unconfirmed sacks had not appeared in any later record.



The remaining twenty-seven sacks had no established explanation.



The separate payment with the blank destination remained separate.



He wrote the figures in the margin.



Then he stopped.



The figures had not changed.



But he had finally learned not to make them change merely because the page looked unfinished.



In the palace gardens, the Empress Dowager had begun to regret bringing the third princess's account book.



Minglan had asked four questions about library expenditure.



The Dowager had answered one.



Yuru had answered two.



The remaining question concerned whether a book purchased by the Department of Rites could be permanently assigned to the princess's private study.



Yuru looked at the record.



"Who authorized the purchase?"



"The librarian."



"Was the purchase made for the princess?"



"Yes."



"Was the book recorded as a personal gift?"



"No."



"Then it remains a palace purchase unless the relevant office formally transfers it."



Minglan nodded.



The Dowager looked at the account book.



"Why are there so many categories?"



"Because the books are used in different ways."



"Would it not be easier to keep them all in one room?"



Yuru looked at her.



"That would make it easier to store them. It would not make the records more accurate."



The Dowager considered this.



"Perhaps I should stop asking questions about books."



Minglan looked alarmed.



"Your Majesty, you may ask."



The Dowager laughed.



"I was joking."



Yuru closed the book.



"Your record is much better than the previous one. Ask the librarian to confirm the borrowed volumes, then submit the revised list to the office."



Minglan bowed.



"I will."



The Dowager looked at Yuru.



"You have become a teacher."



"I have always taught the children."



"Not like this."



Yuru smiled.



"Perhaps I have more time now."



The Dowager did not answer immediately.



Then she said, "That is good."



The three women sat beneath the trees.



The servants remained at a respectful distance.



For a little while, the palace was not a place of petitions, seals, inventories, or arguments over ceremonial precedence.



It was simply a garden.



That evening, Lin Shuyun's reply arrived at Mingzhao's residence.



The corrected silk bolt had passed the finishing inspection.



The two previously rejected bolts were now both acceptable for presentation to the buyer.



Lin had not yet arranged delivery.



She wanted Zhou Wenqi to inspect them together with the next available group, rather than send a separate small delivery and incur additional transport costs.



Mingzhao read the note.



The forty previously accepted bolts remained unchanged.



Thirty-two had been accepted at the full agreed price.



Eight had been accepted at the adjusted rate.



The two newly corrected bolts had not yet been inspected by the buyer.



Their acceptance and payment remained pending.



Mingzhao wrote a short reply approving the combined inspection, provided that the additional finishing costs were recorded separately.



She then opened the partnership ledger.



The silk account was separate from her personal household account.



The money received from Zhou Wenqi remained partnership money.



The cost of production, transport, finishing, and any deductions would need to be recorded before anyone could calculate profit.



She made a note to review the expenses with Lin Shuyun after the next delivery.



Qiao Yue brought in the evening meal.



"Miss, will the silk business earn money?"



Mingzhao put down her brush.



"I hope so."



"Do you think it will?"



"I do not know yet."



Qiao Yue looked at the ledger.



"You have sold some of the goods."



"We have received payment for accepted goods."



"Is that not profit?"



"No."



"Why not?"



"Because the money received is not the same as the money remaining after expenses."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Every answer seems to lead to another account."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"That is because you keep asking financial questions."



Qiao Yue considered this.



Then she sat down to eat.



At the imperial palace, the Emperor received the Crown Prince's revised Eastern Palace reporting forms.



He examined the first page.



The Crown Prince had separated household expenditure, personal expenditure, and ceremonial expenses.



The categories were clear.



The summary was short.



The supporting records were attached.



Zhao Jingheng nodded.



"Acceptable."



Gao Shun bowed.



"The Crown Prince has also requested that the same reporting format be used by the other imperial households."



The Emperor looked up.



"Has he asked the relevant offices to examine whether the forms are suitable?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then let them review it."



He placed the papers aside.



There was a time when he would have sent them to Yuru for confirmation.



He no longer did.



The Crown Prince was learning.



The offices were learning.



The Emperor himself was learning.



It was slower than having one capable person settle everything.



It was also more sustainable.



He turned towards the window.



"Tomorrow, remind me to ask the Empress Dowager whether the former Empress enjoyed the garden."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor paused.



"Not as a formal inquiry."



"Understood."



"Just ask."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Gao Shun withdrew.



The Emperor looked at the papers once more.



He had not asked for the former Empress to approve them.



That was progress.



At the eastern market, Mingzhao placed the survey report in the property file.



She had not purchased the canal house.



She had not rejected it.



She had established the undisputed measurements, identified the missing documents, and recorded the repairs that would be necessary if the purchase eventually proceeded.



The next step belonged to the land office and Master Luo.



Her own task was to wait for the evidence.



She closed the file.



The household was quiet.



The rear drain had carried water properly during the afternoon cleaning. The lower room remained dry. The kitchen shelves were stable. The servants had finished their work.



The rented house was still temporary.



But it was functioning.



Mingzhao looked at the small household ledger.



For the first time, the entries covered several days without any major correction.



There were still ordinary expenses.



There would be more.



There would also be unexpected repairs, delayed deliveries, and questions that could not be answered immediately.



She was beginning to understand that independence did not mean living without problems.



It meant deciding which problems were hers to solve.



She closed the ledger and extinguished the lamp.



In the palace gardens, the former Empress had spent a morning without receiving an official seal.



At the northern canal property, a survey had measured what could be measured and refused to invent the rest.



At the Marquisate, Mingyuan had accepted that an unfinished account could remain unfinished.



And at the eastern market, Mingzhao had discovered that a temporary home did not need to become permanent simply because she had already spent money repairing its drain.



The day had produced no dramatic settlement.



It had produced several accurate records.



In Yongjing, that was becoming a respectable achievement.
