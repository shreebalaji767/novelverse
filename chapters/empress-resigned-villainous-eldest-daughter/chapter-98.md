# **Chapter 98: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Book on Household Management Contains More Instructions About Inspecting Servants Than About Trusting Them. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That Thirty Completed Bolts Are a Useful Result but Not Yet a Reason to Celebrate. The Marquis's Heir Has Asked for an Old Personnel Register and Has Been Informed That the Archive Can Find a Missing Volume More Easily Than It Can Find the Person Who Was Supposed to Keep It.**

**2nd Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had reached the section of the household manual concerning the appointment of senior servants.

The author believed that a household should choose its senior servants according to three qualities: reliability, experience, and obedience.

Yuru read the list.

Then she read it again.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Which of these qualities do you think is most important?"

Fang Ru considered the question.

"Reliability."

"Why?"

"An experienced person may still be careless. An obedient person may follow an instruction even when it is wrong. A reliable person should at least tell you when something has gone wrong."

Yuru looked at her.

"That is a better answer than the author's."

Fang Ru glanced at the book.

"The author may have assumed that a reliable person would also be obedient."

"That is a dangerous assumption."

Yuru turned the page.

The author had described a senior steward who was expected to supervise servants, receive instructions from the household head, and ensure that all household work proceeded without interruption.

He had also advised that the senior steward should never question the household head's decisions in front of other servants.

Yuru frowned.

"That is worse."

Fang Ru leaned closer.

"Why?"

"Because a senior servant who notices an error should be able to report it. If he is afraid to question an instruction, he may allow a mistake to become expensive."

"Perhaps the author means that disagreements should be discussed privately."

"That would be reasonable. But he does not say that."

Yuru placed a small marker beside the passage.

She had spent many years surrounded by people who understood the difference between obedience and agreement.

Some had been willing to disagree with her privately.

Others had remained silent until the consequences became impossible to ignore.

She had not always made it easy for people to challenge her. Her position had carried authority, and her patience had not been unlimited.

That was something she could acknowledge without deciding that every decision she had made was wrong.

She closed the book.

"Perhaps I should ask Father whether he has another book by this author."

Fang Ru looked surprised.

"After finding so many faults?"

"Yes. I would like to know whether the author is equally confident about everything."

Fang Ru lowered her head, smiling.

Yuru returned to her tea.

At the eastern-market workshop, Shen Mingzhao reviewed the production schedule.

Thirty bolts had now completed internal inspection.

The first ten had passed on the twenty-eighth day of the sixth month. The next twenty had passed after the afternoon inspection trial, including two minor corrections and one additional edge examination.

The next twenty were entering production.

The final thirty had not yet been authorized.

Mingzhao read the figures and compared them with the contract.

The delivery period remained twenty-four days from the approval of the sample and receipt of the deposit. Both conditions had been met on the twenty-sixth day of the sixth month.

There was no reason to hurry the workers beyond the agreed schedule.

She turned to the supervisor's note.

The afternoon inspection arrangement had made it easier to examine the edges after the fabric had settled. The supervisor proposed continuing it for the remaining production.

Mingzhao approved.

She also added one instruction: the workshop should retain the original inspection time in the record for comparison, rather than rewriting the schedule as though the new arrangement had always been used.

The supervisor had not asked to change the specifications, and she did not intend to allow the inspection procedure to become an excuse for doing so.

Qiao Yue brought another paper.

"This came from the workshop accounts clerk."

Mingzhao read it.

The clerk had asked whether the wages for the additional inspection time should be recorded under production or inspection expenses.

Mingzhao looked at the note.

"Why has he not classified them?"

"He says the current account has no separate heading for additional inspection."

"Then create one."

"Will that not make the account more complicated?"

"It will make the cost visible."

She took a fresh sheet.

The additional inspection time should be recorded separately for this order. The workshop could decide whether the same heading was useful for future work after comparing the costs.

She signed it.

Qiao Yue took the instruction.

Mingzhao looked at the remaining papers.

The workshop was learning to distinguish production costs from inspection costs. That distinction mattered. If the workers spent more time checking goods, the business should know whether the expense had prevented costly corrections or merely added labour.

A low price achieved by ignoring quality was not necessarily a good price.

A high price achieved by unnecessary work was not necessarily a good price either.

She put the account papers aside.

The first thirty bolts were complete.

That was progress.

It was not profit.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received a reply from the northern administrative office.

The office had located a partial personnel register for the transport department.

It listed two men who had held the position of northern warehouse transport supervisor during the relevant period.

One had been transferred to a southern district.

The other had left government service.

Neither entry stated who had received the movement notebook.

Mingyuan read the copy.

"Can we locate the former supervisor?"

Liang Wen checked the accompanying note.

"The southern district office has been asked to confirm the transfer record. The man who left service has an address recorded in the personnel register, but the address is several years old."

"Has anyone contacted him?"

"Not yet. The office requested permission to verify his current residence first."

Mingyuan nodded.

"That is sensible. We should not send a formal inquiry to an address where he may no longer live."

Steward Zhou examined the register.

"One of these men may have been responsible for the four carts."

"Possibly."

"And the notebook?"

"We still do not know who received it."

Mingyuan looked at the record.

The notebook had been issued to the position of transport supervisor. The personnel register identified two people who had held that position during the relevant period. Neither record established which man had signed for the notebook.

He had to resist the temptation to connect the two documents merely because they belonged to the same office.

"Ask the central transport office whether the notebook issue register includes a signature or personal seal," he said. "If it does, request a copy."

Liang Wen recorded the instruction.

"Should we ask the two former supervisors about the four carts?"

"Once we establish where they are and what duties they held on the relevant date."

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan placed the documents in a separate folder.

The grain figures remained unchanged.

Forty-seven sacks in the original stock record.

Twenty covered by the instruction.

Eighteen confirmed as received.

Two unconfirmed.

Twenty-seven unexplained.

The four-cart dispatch entry remained a separate lead.

The blank-destination transport payment remained separate.

The old payment voucher attributed to Mingzhao remained missing.

Mingyuan looked at the folder.

There were now several people and offices who might be able to explain something.

There was still no one who could explain everything.

The Marquis of Qingyuan was less patient.

When Mingyuan brought him the latest report, Shen Guowei read it with a tightening expression.

"Two supervisors?"

"Two men held the position during the relevant period."

"Then one of them must know."

"One may know."

"Why do you insist on making every answer uncertain?"

Mingyuan remained silent for a moment.

"Because the records are uncertain."

The Marquis put down the paper.

"Your sister has already made this matter difficult enough. Must you also delay the investigation?"

Mingyuan looked at his father.

"The investigation concerns the warehouse records. The property division concerns Mingzhao's claims. They should not be mixed."

"The same years are involved."

"That does not make every record part of the same dispute."

The Marquis's expression hardened.

Mingyuan continued carefully.

"If we use an incomplete transport record to question her about the grain, we may be asking her to explain something she never handled. If we use the old payment entry to reduce her claims without finding the voucher, we may be treating an unverified payment as proof of repayment."

The Marquis did not answer.

Mingyuan knew that his father disliked the direction of the conversation.

The family had been accustomed to Mingzhao explaining the accounts. Her name appeared on many records because she had managed the estate. That did not mean every transaction had been her personal decision, and it did not mean every missing document could be assigned to her.

"Have the schedule corrected," the Marquis said finally.

"It is being corrected."

"And send her the papers when they are ready."

"Yes, Father."

Mingyuan bowed and left.

He did not feel victorious.

He had merely prevented one uncertain matter from being used to settle another.

That was becoming a large part of his work.

At her residence, Mingzhao received a short message from Lin Shuyun.

Lin had asked whether Mingzhao had considered taking another order once the eighty bolts were completed.

The message was friendly, but the question was practical. Lin's own workshop had received inquiries for the coming season, and she wanted to know whether Mingzhao's workshop would be available for a possible partnership.

Mingzhao read the note twice.

The earlier two-hundred-bolt order had closed with a modest positive margin. The new eighty-bolt order was proceeding, but it had not yet been delivered or paid in full.

There was no reason to promise capacity before knowing the result.

She wrote back that she would be willing to discuss future work after the current order was completed and its costs reviewed. She would not reserve production capacity in advance.

Then she considered whether she should add that she was open to a joint order if the terms were clear.

She decided against it.

Lin understood business.

A cautious answer would not offend her.

Qiao Yue watched as Mingzhao sealed the letter.

"Would another order be useful?"

"Possibly."

"Then why not accept the possibility now?"

"Because a possibility does not pay wages."

Qiao Yue smiled.

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Have you become fond of hearing me say that?"

"It is a sensible statement."

"It is also a statement that people seem to forget whenever a merchant offers them a large order."

Qiao Yue took the letter.

Mingzhao returned to the production papers.

The first thirty bolts were complete.

Twenty more were entering production.

She had no intention of promising another order before she knew what the current one had cost.

That afternoon, Xu Yuru received a letter from her mother.

Madam Xu had written about the weather in the eastern district, the repairs to a small garden wall, and a new cook who had been hired to assist the household kitchen.

The letter was ordinary.

It contained no invitation, no request for advice, and no report about a family dispute.

Yuru read it slowly.

Her mother mentioned that Duke Xu had been looking for another book to lend her, but had been told not to select anything until Yuru returned the first two.

Yuru smiled.

She took up her writing brush.

She replied that she was continuing to read the household manual and had discovered that its author believed a household head should personally supervise nearly every task. She had written down several questions about the division of duties and hoped her father would not mistake the notes for a request to inspect the Xu residence.

She added that the rain had interrupted her walk the previous day, but the western path had dried enough for her to go out that afternoon.

She did not write that she missed the household.

She did not write that she wanted to visit immediately.

She simply told her mother what she had been reading and how the weather had been.

When she finished, Fang Ru brought the tea.

"Will you send it today?"

"Yes."

"Would you like to add anything?"

Yuru considered the letter.

"No. It is long enough."

Fang Ru looked at the page.

"It is not very long."

"Then my mother can read it without needing to prepare a second cup of tea."

Fang Ru accepted the letter.

Yuru returned to her book.

The Marquisate's revised property schedule was not completed that day.

The accounts clerk had corrected the wording of the seventy-two-tael repair entry and separated the four payment-confirmed items from the confirmed advances.

However, the supporting documents for several of the ten uncertain entries were still incomplete.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.

The furnishing payment remained estate expenditure.

The northern canal property remained outside the family division.

Mingyuan reviewed the corrections and sent the schedule back for another check.

He did not propose a meeting.

Mingzhao had said she would review the papers when they were ready.

He intended to send her papers that could withstand review.

Near sunset, Mingzhao received the workshop's final report for the day.

The next twenty bolts had entered production.

The workers had followed the approved sample.

No further adjustment was required.

The additional inspection cost had been recorded separately, as she had instructed.

She checked the figures.

Then she placed the report beside the contract.

The business was still small. The profit was still uncertain. The current order had not yet reached buyer inspection.

But the records were becoming more useful.

Mingzhao had once believed that the safest arrangement was to know every detail herself.

She now understood that knowing every detail was not the same as making a business reliable.

A reliable business required people who knew their work, records that preserved what had happened, and procedures that did not depend on one person's memory.

It also required someone to notice when those procedures failed.

She was still learning how to balance those responsibilities.

At the palace guest residence, Xu Yuru placed her father's manual beside her notebook.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan placed the transport records in a folder marked for further investigation.

At the workshop, Mingzhao placed the production report with the contract.

Three different households had accumulated three different sets of unfinished work.

None of them had found a complete answer.

But, for once, none of the three had attempted to pretend otherwise.
