# **Chapter 114: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Day Without an Appointment Can Still Have a Pleasant Beginning and a Pleasant End. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That Six Bolts of Cloth Require More Than One Approval Before They Become Six Saleable Bolts. The Marquis's Heir Has Discovered That the Contents of a Second Box May Be Better Organised Than Those of the First, Which Is Not Necessarily an Improvement.**

**18th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru woke later than she had intended.

This was not because she had slept badly. On the contrary, she had slept well enough that Fang Ru had entered the room twice before deciding not to wake her.

When Yuru finally opened her eyes, the morning light had already reached the edge of the bed.

She looked toward the small clock.

"Has the morning meal been served?"

"Yes, Madam."

"And the hour?"

"Nearly the third quarter of the Chen hour."

Yuru sat up.

"Then I have slept longer than usual."

Fang Ru helped her arrange her outer robe.

"Would you like the meal brought here?"

"No. I will eat in the sitting room."

"Shall I ask the kitchen to prepare something else?"

"Why?"

"The usual morning dishes may have cooled."

Yuru considered this.

"Then warm them."

Fang Ru nodded.

There had been a time when Yuru would have regarded the late start as a disruption. She would have asked what had been delayed, which attendants had been inconvenienced, and whether the day's arrangements needed to be revised.

Today, the only consequence was that breakfast would be warmed.

It seemed a manageable consequence.

At the table, she found the previous day's letters arranged beside her writing materials. Her reply to her cousin remained unfinished. She had begun it the evening before and stopped after writing three lines.

She ate first.

Afterward, she returned to the letter.

Fang Ru poured tea.

"Madam, would you like the writing box brought closer?"

"It is already close enough."

Yuru dipped her brush.

Her cousin had written about an inkstone repairman who charged too much. Yuru could have offered an opinion about the price, but she did not know the repairman's work, the condition of the inkstone, or the cost of the materials.

Instead, she wrote that she hoped the repaired inkstone would serve well and that she wished to see it during her next visit.

She read the sentence once.

Then she added a greeting to the cousin's children.

The letter was finished.

Yuru sealed it.

"Send this with the afternoon post."

"Yes, Madam."

She looked at the remaining correspondence.

The aunt's invitation could wait until she knew when she wished to visit.

The letter about the courtyard had already been sent.

There was no other correspondence.

Yuru put the writing brush away.

"Bring me the garden book."

Fang Ru did so.

Yuru opened it to the section about covered passages and resting places. She read for a while, then closed the book and looked toward the courtyard.

The weather was pleasant.

She decided to take a short walk.

It was not a formal inspection, and she had no intention of asking the gardener to explain the condition of every plant. She simply wished to walk.

Fang Ru followed at a respectful distance.

They crossed the covered passage and reached the small stone path near the western wall. The flowering shrub trimmed two days earlier had settled into its new shape. Its branches no longer obstructed the path.

Yuru passed it without stopping.

At the end of the path, a wooden bench stood beneath the roof.

She sat.

The bench was not particularly comfortable. It had been made for the practical purpose of providing a place to rest, not for displaying the skill of a carpenter.

Yuru found that she liked it.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Would you like to sit?"

Fang Ru looked at the bench.

"Beside you?"

"Is there another person here?"

Fang Ru hesitated, then sat at the far end.

For a little while, they watched the courtyard.

The morning continued without requiring either of them to arrange anything.

At the workshop, Mingzhao rejected the first dyed sample.

The supplier's messenger had arrived shortly after the opening of the eastern market. He carried a small parcel containing the dyed cloth, a written statement of the dyeing charge, and a revised estimate of the material cost.

Mingzhao opened the parcel herself.

The colour was close to the reference piece.

Close was not the same as matching.

She placed the two pieces beside one another.

The reference cloth had a cooler tone. The new sample was slightly warmer, and the difference became more visible when both were placed near the window.

Luo Shun examined them.

"It is not a large difference."

"No."

"The merchant may accept it."

"He may."

The foreman waited.

Mingzhao looked at the sample again.

"But we do not know that he will."

"Should I ask him?"

"Not yet. We have already told him that the trial will be made against the reference specification. If we show him a different colour and ask whether he will accept it, we will have changed the condition before production."

Luo Shun nodded.

Mingzhao turned to the supplier's written statement.

"How much did the dyeing cost?"

The foreman gave her the amount.

She checked it against the quotation.

The charge was reasonable, but the sample had not met the required colour.

"Record the expense separately. We paid for a sample, and we received a sample. The fact that it failed does not make the expense disappear."

"Yes, Miss."

"Tell the supplier that the colour does not match the reference. Ask whether he can produce another sample using the same dye lot as the reference, or provide a written explanation of the difference."

"Should we pay for another sample?"

"First ask what he proposes. We will decide after we see the cost."

Luo Shun gathered the papers.

Mingzhao stopped him.

"One more thing."

"Yes?"

"Do not tell the workers that the six-bolt trial has been approved for production."

"I have not."

"Good. The trial remains conditional."

The foreman left.

Mingzhao placed the failed sample in a separate envelope and wrote the date and reason on the outside.

Qiao Yue, who had been waiting with the household accounts, glanced at it.

"Will the supplier be offended?"

"Perhaps."

"Would that affect future business?"

"It might."

Mingzhao put the envelope aside.

"If he can produce cloth that matches the agreed sample, there is no reason for us to quarrel. If he cannot, there is no reason for us to purchase it merely to preserve his good opinion."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao turned to the household papers.

"Are the expenses checked?"

"Yes. The kitchen account is correct. The household supplies account is also within the amount allocated."

"Any missing receipts?"

"One receipt for lamp oil has not been returned by the supplier."

"Is the payment recorded?"

"Yes. The delivery was confirmed, but the receipt has not been received."

"Then keep it as a missing receipt, not an unpaid account."

Qiao Yue smiled faintly.

"I have written that."

"Good."

Mingzhao checked the entry.

It was properly classified.

She returned the account.

The morning had produced one failed sample and one missing receipt.

Neither required a dramatic response.

She found this more satisfying than she would have admitted.

In the Marquisate, Mingyuan began the examination of the second box.

The second box was more orderly than the first.

It contained bundles tied with similar cord, and most of the papers carried identifiable office headings. There were receipts from the northern transport office, correspondence concerning repairs to two roadside shelters, and several lists of cart drivers.

Mingyuan watched the clerks record the contents.

"Separate the driver lists from the repair receipts," he instructed. "Keep the original bundle order in the inventory."

The clerk nodded.

One of the lists covered the month in which the four-cart dispatch had occurred.

Mingyuan moved closer.

"Is this the same period?"

"Yes, Young Master," the clerk replied.

Mingyuan examined the list.

There were several names, including Sun Shou.

The list recorded the workers assigned to the northern transport office during that month. It did not record which carts they had driven on any particular day.

Mingyuan placed it beside the earlier wage record.

"Compare the names."

The clerk did so.

Sun Shou appeared on both records.

Wei Zhen did not appear on the new list.

"Does this establish that Sun Shou drove the four carts?" Mingyuan asked.

"No, Young Master."

"Why not?"

"The list shows that he was assigned to the office. It does not give his daily cart assignment."

"Correct. Record it as another confirmation of his employment during the period, not proof of the dispatch."

The clerk wrote the distinction.

Steward Zhou looked at the list.

"At least we have one name appearing in more than one record."

"Yes."

"That may help us."

"It may."

Mingyuan turned to the remaining papers.

Another document contained a list of carts temporarily assigned to the northern road. Four cart numbers appeared on the page, but there was no date beside them.

He examined the writing.

"Is this an assignment list?"

The clerk checked the heading.

"It is titled 'Temporary Cart Allocation.'"

"Does it state the period?"

"No."

"Then it cannot yet be connected to the dispatch."

Mingyuan put it aside.

Steward Zhou watched him.

"Young Master, the four cart numbers are the same as those in the movement entry."

Mingyuan looked again.

They were.

For the first time that morning, the evidence appeared to be approaching the same event from two different directions.

But the list had no date.

"Could these be the same carts?"

"They could."

"Could they have been used for another dispatch?"

"Yes."

"Then we need the allocation period."

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan asked the clerk to record the four numbers exactly as written.

"Do not write that they are the four carts from the dispatch. Write that the numbers correspond, while the date and purpose remain unverified."

The clerk complied.

Mingyuan leaned back.

The second box had yielded something more promising than the first.

It had also introduced another uncertainty.

He was beginning to suspect that the old records had been assembled by people who believed that the next person to read them would already know everything.

Unfortunately, the next person had turned out to be him.

At the temporary residence, Mingzhao received a message from the supplier shortly after noon.

The supplier explained that the first dyed sample had been prepared using a new dye mixture. He could attempt another sample using a different proportion, but he could not guarantee an exact match without examining the original reference cloth more closely.

Mingzhao read the message.

"Did he request the reference piece?" she asked Luo Shun.

"Yes. He wants to take it to his dye workshop."

"Will he return it?"

"He says he will return it the same day."

"That is not enough."

Luo Shun waited.

Mingzhao continued, "We will not hand over the only reference piece without a written receipt. Have another copy prepared from the original sample if the material allows it. Keep the original here."

"The copy may not reproduce the colour exactly."

"Then the supplier must examine the original in our presence."

Luo Shun considered this.

"That will require someone to travel with him."

"Yes."

"Should I go?"

"You may, if you have time. Otherwise send the experienced dye worker. I want a written note of the mixture proposed and the price of the second sample."

"Understood."

Mingzhao looked at the production book.

The six-bolt trial had been intended to be small.

It was already producing more correspondence than the previous two hundred-bolt order.

That did not mean it was a bad decision. A small trial was supposed to reveal difficulties before larger quantities were purchased.

Still, she was not going to pretend that a six-bolt order was simple merely because the number was small.

"Do not rush the supplier," she said. "But do not let him keep the reference cloth."

Luo Shun bowed and left.

Mingzhao then opened the Marquisate's latest letter.

It contained a brief update on the second box. The temporary cart allocation list had been found, and the four cart numbers corresponded to those in the movement entry.

The letter asked whether she could identify the carts from the numbers.

Mingzhao read it again.

She knew the estate's general transport records, but the surviving list did not establish that the cart numbers were unique across every office and period. She would need the relevant allocation register and the date range.

She wrote her reply.

*The matching numbers are worth comparing, but the undated allocation list does not establish that the carts were used for the recorded dispatch. Please obtain the original cart register and determine whether the numbers were reused or reassigned during the relevant period. I cannot identify the dispatch from the list alone.*

She paused, then added:

*Please also retain the original list in its present condition and record the box and bundle from which it was recovered.*

She sealed the letter.

Qiao Yue collected it.

"Another inquiry?"

"Another question."

"Will they ask you to attend?"

"They may."

"Would you go?"

"If the records justify a meeting, and the agenda is clear."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao returned to her papers.

She had no intention of travelling to the Marquisate merely to tell them that an undated list was undated.

In the palace, Yuru received a short note from the Dowager's residence.

The Dowager invited her to share tea on the following afternoon.

There was no mention of any household matter.

Yuru read the note and placed it beside her writing box.

Fang Ru was folding a clean cloth.

"Would you like to accept?"

"Yes."

"Shall I ask whether Her Majesty wishes to discuss anything?"

Yuru looked at her.

"Why?"

"To prepare."

"She has invited me for tea."

Fang Ru lowered her eyes.

"Yes, Madam."

Yuru picked up her brush and wrote a brief acceptance.

The note was sent.

Later, when the afternoon light softened, Yuru took another walk through the covered passage. She stopped beside the bench beneath the roof.

The wood had warmed in the sun.

She sat for a short while, then returned indoors.

Fang Ru had selected a book from the residence library.

It was a collection of short accounts concerning different kinds of tea.

Yuru noticed it on the table.

"You chose a book."

Fang Ru looked up.

"Yes, Madam."

"Have you begun reading it?"

"Only the first page."

"Is it interesting?"

"I think so."

"Then continue."

Fang Ru hesitated.

"Would you like me to read it aloud?"

"No. You chose it for yourself."

Fang Ru looked at the book, then at Yuru.

"Yes, Madam."

Yuru returned to her own reading.

For a while, both women sat in the same room, each reading a different book.

It was a small change.

It was also a change that Yuru had not needed to arrange through a formal instruction or an elaborate explanation.

She found it pleasant.

At the Marquisate, the second box was closed before the evening meal.

The inventory had recorded several transport documents, one temporary cart allocation list, and a number of unrelated receipts.

The four matching cart numbers were now recorded as a possible connection to the dispatch.

The date remained unknown.

Mingyuan read the summary.

"Have we found the original cart register?"

"No, Young Master," Liang Wen replied. "The records office says it may have been kept separately from the movement records."

"Where?"

"They have not established that."

Mingyuan looked at the inventory.

"Then add it to the search list."

Liang Wen wrote it down.

Steward Zhou stood beside the desk.

"Young Master, we have examined two boxes. There are four remaining."

"Yes."

"Should we ask the records office to search for the cart register at the same time?"

"Yes. But do not interrupt the box examination. The two searches can proceed separately."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan reviewed the latest property schedule.

The family division remained incomplete. The accounts had improved, but the unresolved entries were still unresolved.

He had not found evidence that Mingzhao received the disputed payment.

He had not found a repayment record for the four payment-confirmed advances.

He had not found the original receipt for one confirmed advance.

The seventy-two taels for study repairs remained a separate question.

The grain discrepancy had not changed.

The four-cart entry had acquired a possible set of matching cart numbers, but not a confirmed date or cargo.

He looked at the page for a long moment.

"Prepare a short report for Father," he said. "State what has been found and what remains unknown."

Steward Zhou nodded.

"Should we include the possibility that the carts carried grain?"

"No."

"Even as a possibility?"

"Not unless there is evidence connecting them to the grain movement. We have enough possibilities already."

Steward Zhou accepted the instruction.

Mingyuan signed the inventory.

He had once believed that a report should present a conclusion.

Now he understood that sometimes the most useful report was one that prevented people from pretending a conclusion existed.

He looked toward the shelves.

Four boxes remained.

The records office had not yet found the cart register.

And the household still had no answer about the twenty-seven sacks.

For the moment, the second box had provided a list of four cart numbers and another reason to search.

It had also provided an important lesson.

A matching number could be a clue.

It could not be made into a date simply because everyone wanted the investigation to move forward.

Mingyuan closed the file.

Tomorrow, they would open the third box.
