# **Chapter 50: The Former Empress Has Attended a Family Dinner. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Revised Property Schedule. One Family Has Served Eight Dishes. The Other Has Produced Twelve Pages of Corrections.**

**12th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Duke of Xu's residence had prepared eight dishes.



Madam Xu had personally checked the menu twice.



The kitchen had been instructed not to add anything without permission.



The dining room had been arranged without ceremonial decorations.



There were no musicians.



There were no additional attendants.



There was no formal announcement.



For once, the household appeared determined to behave like a household.



The Duke stood near the entrance when Yuru arrived.



"You're here."



"I said I would come."



He smiled.



Madam Xu came forward.



"Have you travelled comfortably?"



"Yes, Mother."



"Was the road crowded?"



"Not particularly."



"Have you eaten?"



Yuru looked at the dining room.



"Not yet."



Madam Xu nodded.



"Then let us eat before your father finds another question."



The Duke laughed.



"I have not asked anything."



"You were about to."



Yuru smiled.



The three entered the dining room.



The dinner began peacefully.



The first dish was steamed fish.



The second was braised chicken.



The third was seasonal vegetables.



There was tofu with mushrooms, a light soup, and three other dishes.



The Duke looked at the table.



"Eight dishes. Exactly."



Madam Xu gave him a warning glance.



"Please eat."



Yuru tasted the fish.



"It is very good."



Madam Xu smiled.



"The cook remembered how you liked it."



The Duke picked up his chopsticks.



"Your mother gave him instructions."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Of course I did."



Yuru laughed.



The conversation moved through ordinary matters.



The weather had been warmer.



The garden's osmanthus tree was growing well.



A neighbour had sent a basket of fresh fruit.



The Duke had received a letter from an old acquaintance.



No one asked Yuru to review an account.



No one asked her to settle a household dispute.



No one asked her to decide how many servants should attend a ceremony.



She found herself enjoying the meal more than she had expected.



After the main dishes were cleared, Madam Xu asked about her temporary residence.



"Are you comfortable there?"



"Yes."



"Do you intend to remain?"



"For now."



"Have you considered this courtyard?"



"I have."



Madam Xu waited.



Yuru continued.



"I like it. But I have not decided whether I want to move permanently."



The Duke nodded.



"There is no hurry."



Madam Xu looked at him.



He added, "We have enough time."



Yuru smiled.



"Thank you."



Madam Xu poured tea.



"If you decide to stay elsewhere, you can still visit."



"I know."



The Duke looked pleased.



"Then the courtyard will remain available."



Madam Xu did not object.



For the rest of the evening, they discussed books, relatives, and the condition of the garden.



It was an ordinary family dinner.



The eight dishes had been sufficient.



The absence of documents had been even more satisfying.



At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Mingzhao received twelve pages of corrections.



Qiao Yue brought the papers into the study.



"Miss, the family has sent another document."



Mingzhao looked up.



"From whom?"



"Mr Liang."



"Put it here."



Qiao Yue placed the bundle on the desk.



The revised property schedule had arrived earlier than expected.



The twelve pages contained corrections to land descriptions, building valuations, lease classifications, maintenance expenses, and supporting records.



Mingzhao began reading.



The first page concerned the western agricultural field.



The tenant's payment had been confirmed.



However, the family had not yet established whether the payment renewed the old lease or represented a temporary cultivation arrangement.



The classification remained unresolved.



The second page concerned the southern building.



The structural valuation was complete.



The roof repair estimate remained pending.



The third and fourth pages concerned the northern warehouse.



The building valuation was separate from the grain inventory.



The old stock discrepancy remained forty-seven sacks.



The transfer instruction still covered twenty sacks.



Eighteen had been confirmed received.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



Mingzhao continued.



The fifth page concerned the agricultural holdings.



Several properties had been reclassified.



The sixth concerned maintenance expenses.



The seventy-two-tael study repair remained correctly classified as estate maintenance.



The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels were still unverified.



The seventh and eighth pages concerned personal advances.



The nine confirmed entries and four partially supported entries were correctly separated.



The ten uncertain entries remained uncertain.



The ninth page concerned the northern canal property.



It was not included among the Marquisate's assets.



The tenth page concerned outstanding liabilities.



The eleventh listed missing supporting documents.



The twelfth contained the proposed timetable for the next meeting.



Mingzhao read the last page.



Then she returned to the first.



She found two errors.



The first was a date.



The second was a property reference copied from an older register.



Neither changed the overall classification.



Both required correction.



She marked them.



Qiao Yue watched.



"Are there many mistakes?"



"Two."



"Out of twelve pages?"



"Yes."



"That sounds good."



"It is better than the previous version."



Qiao Yue looked at the papers.



"Should I tell Mr Liang?"



"Send the corrections with the marked pages."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao continued reading.



She was not responsible for making the family's records perfect.



She was, however, entitled to ensure that her own settlement was based on accurate documents.



There was a difference.



At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan was reviewing the warehouse counting method with Sun Qiming.



The accountant had requested access to the original inventory books.



Mingyuan had brought out the oldest volumes.



Sun examined the headings.



"These two records use different categories."



Mingyuan leaned closer.



One record was titled *Total Grain Stock*.



The other was titled *Available Grain for Distribution*.



"Does that explain the forty-seven sacks?" Mingyuan asked.



"It may explain why the figures differ. It does not establish what happened to the sacks listed as missing."



Sun turned the pages.



"The total stock record includes grain reserved for seed, damaged grain awaiting disposal, and grain held for immediate distribution."



Mingyuan looked at the second volume.



"This one only records grain available for distribution."



"Correct."



"Then the two figures should never have been compared directly."



"Not without reconciling their categories."



Mingyuan studied the entries.



"Can we establish the original stock total?"



"Possibly. We need the supporting inventory sheets."



Mingyuan called the steward.



"Find the original counting sheets from that year. Do not replace them with the later summary."



The steward bowed.



"Yes, Young Master."



Sun continued examining the records.



"Even if the categories explain part of the difference, we must still trace the movement instruction and the twenty-seven unexplained sacks separately."



Mingyuan nodded.



"I understand."



Sun looked at him.



"You are beginning to ask the correct questions."



Mingyuan smiled faintly.



"I am beginning to understand how many questions there are."



Sun returned to the ledger.



"That is usually the first improvement."



At the palace, the Emperor was receiving the weekly report from the Imperial Household Administration.



The report was shorter than the previous one.



It listed completed transfers, pending decisions, and unresolved matters.



The ceremonial inventory reference system had been adopted.



The old records would retain their original wording, with new reference numbers added as annotations.



The winter bedding distribution would follow the existing procedure.



The library catalogue remained under the Third Princess's review.



The Emperor read the report.



"Good."



Gao Shun bowed.



"There is one request from the Department of Rites."



The Emperor looked up.



"What request?"



"They wish to know whether the former Empress's personal ceremonial garments should remain listed in the palace inventory until the transfer is completed."



The Emperor considered the question.



"Have the garments been identified as palace property?"



"Some have. Others remain under examination."



"Then keep the palace-owned garments in the palace inventory. Record the personal garments separately. Leave the uncertain items pending."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor paused.



"Has the former Empress been asked to approve the inventory?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



He returned to the report.



He had finally understood that a former administrator could be consulted without being made responsible for the work.



More importantly, she could be left alone.



At the eastern market, Lin Shuyun arrived with the final draft of the proposed two-hundred-bolt contract.



The buyer had accepted the staged payment arrangement.



He had agreed to provide the deposit before production began.



The delivery schedule had been revised to allow for the training of the additional workers.



The contract also included inspection requirements, correction periods, and provisions for delayed delivery.



Mingzhao read it carefully.



"Have the four workers been selected?"



"Two have agreed. The other two are expected to confirm tomorrow."



"Have their wages been included?"



"Yes."



"Does the estimate include training time?"



"Yes."



"Have you separated the proposed order's costs from the existing partnership account?"



"Yes."



Mingzhao examined the figures.



The expected margin was acceptable, though not generous.



The order would require careful supervision.



She placed the contract on the table.



"I approve sending this version for signature."



Lin smiled.



"Then we are close."



"Close to a signed contract."



"That is still progress."



"Yes."



Lin gathered her papers.



"The buyer wants to visit the workshop after signing."



"That is reasonable."



"He also asked whether you would attend."



Mingzhao considered it.



"I can attend if the date is agreed in advance."



Lin nodded.



The proposed order was finally approaching a real commitment.



But the existing order remained separate.



The two corrected bolts had been accepted by the buyer, but payment had not yet arrived.



Mingzhao entered that distinction into the ledger.



At the Duke of Xu's residence, the dinner had ended.



The servants were clearing the table.



Madam Xu walked with Yuru through the eastern courtyard.



The evening air was cool.



"Did you enjoy the meal?" Madam Xu asked.



"Very much."



"Was the food sufficient?"



"Yes."



Madam Xu smiled.



The Duke joined them.



"Your mother was worried that eight dishes might not be enough."



"I was not worried," Madam Xu replied.



"You asked the cook three times."



"Because you kept suggesting additional dishes."



Yuru laughed.



They stopped near the osmanthus tree.



The Duke looked towards the courtyard.



"You may use this place whenever you wish."



"Thank you."



"You need not decide today."



"I know."



Madam Xu looked at her daughter.



"Would you like to stay overnight next time?"



Yuru considered it.



"Perhaps."



"Then we will prepare a room."



"An ordinary room."



Madam Xu smiled.



"An ordinary room."



The Duke looked relieved.



No one mentioned household accounts.



That night, Mingzhao received a reply from Mr Liang.



He had accepted the two corrections to the property schedule.



The corrected date would be amended.



The property reference would be checked against the original register.



The next meeting would focus on the confirmed estate properties and outstanding liabilities.



The family would circulate the supporting documents before the meeting.



Mingzhao placed the letter beside the schedule.



Qiao Yue entered with tea.



"Has the family accepted your corrections?"



"Yes."



"Will the next meeting be easier?"



"No."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Why?"



"Because the next meeting concerns the valuation and liabilities."



"That sounds difficult."



"It will be."



Qiao Yue placed the tea down.



"Perhaps you should have chosen a simpler family."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Was there one available?"



Qiao Yue considered the question.



"Probably not."



Mingzhao smiled.



She opened her personal ledger.



The rental expenses remained within the agreed terms.



The rice supply was adequate.



The servants' wages had been recorded.



The silk partnership account remained separate.



The nine confirmed advances and four partial entries had not yet been included in a final settlement.



The northern canal property remained under consideration.



Her permanent residence remained undecided.



She closed the ledger.



At the palace, Yuru returned to her temporary residence later that evening.



Fang Ru helped her remove her outer robe.



"Was the dinner pleasant?"



"Yes."



"Did the Duke discuss the courtyard?"



"Briefly."



"Did he ask you to review the household accounts?"



"No."



Fang Ru smiled.



"That is good."



Yuru sat by the window.



After a moment, she said, "My father seems happier when I visit without a schedule."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Perhaps he simply wishes to spend time with you."



Yuru looked towards the courtyard.



"Perhaps."



She had spent so long being useful that ordinary affection sometimes felt unfamiliar.



She did not need to solve that feeling tonight.



She simply sat quietly.



The twelfth day of the fifth month ended with two different kinds of progress.



At the Duke of Xu's residence, eight dishes had been served and eaten.



No one had asked Yuru to manage the kitchen.



At the Marquisate, twelve pages of corrections had been reviewed, and two errors had been amended.



No one had asked Mingzhao to resume estate management.



The palace continued to transfer responsibility to its proper offices.



The Marquisate continued to discover how much work remained.



The former Empress had enjoyed a family dinner.



The eldest daughter had received another property schedule.



Neither woman had settled her future residence.



Neither had completed every unfinished matter from her previous life.



But both had begun to spend their days on terms that were increasingly their own.



And for the moment, neither had been asked to count the dishes.
