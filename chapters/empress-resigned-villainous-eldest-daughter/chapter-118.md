# **Chapter 118: The Former Empress Has Discovered That an Afternoon Invitation Can Be Accepted Without Sending a List of Suitable Refreshments. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received Two Samples and Found That Neither Merchant Has Yet Managed to Sell Her the Same Cloth Twice. The Marquis's Heir Has Learned That a Missing Dispatch Book May Have Been Moved More Than Once, Which Is an Unfortunate Habit for a Book That Is Already Missing.**

**22nd Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress Xu Yuru was deciding between two pairs of shoes when Fang Ru entered.



One pair was embroidered with small flowers. The other was plain, with a soft sole and a narrow band around the ankle.



Yuru held them up for comparison.



"Which would be more comfortable for walking in the Dowager's courtyard?"



Fang Ru examined both.



"The plain pair."



"Then I shall wear those."



Fang Ru placed the embroidered shoes back in the cabinet.



"Would you like the carriage prepared for the afternoon visit?"



"Not today. The visit is later in the week."



"Of course."



Yuru set the plain shoes beside her dressing stool.



The invitation from the Dowager had been accepted, but the visit required no elaborate preparation. There would be tea, conversation, and perhaps a walk through the courtyard if the weather permitted. Yuru had no reason to prepare a gift or arrange a formal party.



She had once spent considerable time determining which gifts should be presented to whom, in what order, and with what accompanying words. The arrangements had been necessary in her former position. She had not disliked every part of them, but she had grown accustomed to treating even a simple visit as a matter that could become complicated if someone overlooked a detail.



Now she could decide what to wear without asking anyone to prepare a seating plan.



It was a modest pleasure.



Fang Ru placed a folded cloth on the table.



"This arrived from your aunt's household."



Yuru opened it.



Her aunt had written to confirm the day of the family visit at the end of the month. She also mentioned that several cousins would be present and that one of them had recently begun learning embroidery. The aunt hoped Yuru might look at a piece of her work.



Yuru read the letter again.



"She has not asked me to examine the household accounts."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Perhaps your aunt has learned that you are not an accounting office."



"Perhaps she has simply decided that embroidery is safer."



Yuru took up her brush and wrote a short reply confirming the date.



She did not promise to remain overnight. She did not offer to inspect the young cousin's work before the visit. She simply said that she would be pleased to see it.



When she finished, she placed the letter in the outgoing correspondence box.



"That is settled."



Fang Ru looked at the shoes.



"And the shoes?"



"Also settled."



"Then the morning has been very productive."



Yuru glanced at her.



"Do not tell the palace."

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingyuan had a different kind of morning.



The remaining loose papers from the fifth box had been arranged into four groups: repair accounts, transport office records, unrelated warehouse papers, and documents whose dates or references required further comparison.



Liang Wen had completed the inventory.



Mingyuan reviewed it before allowing the box to be marked as examined.



"Have we checked every paper against the transfer list?"



"Every paper that could be identified, Young Master. Three fragments have no readable date or heading."



"Keep those separate. Do not discard them merely because they are incomplete."



"Yes."



Steward Zhou entered carrying a narrow bundle.



"I have brought the old storehouse transfer index."



Mingyuan looked up.



"Is this the one from the reorganization?"



"Yes. It records the movement of several registers from the northern transport office to the old accounts storehouse."



Mingyuan immediately set aside the inventory.



"Does it mention the northern dispatch book?"



Zhou untied the bundle.



"That is what I wanted you to see."



The index contained a list of records transferred during the reorganization. Some entries were clear. Others had been written in abbreviated form.



Mingyuan found the relevant section.



The movement records were listed first. Beneath them appeared an entry for a northern dispatch register, followed by a reference to a temporary storage shelf.



He read it twice.



"Here."



Liang Wen leaned closer.



The entry named the old accounts storehouse, then identified a shelf and a compartment. A later note stated that the compartment had been cleared during another reorganization.



Mingyuan frowned.



"Where were the contents moved?"



Zhou turned the page.



"That is the difficulty. The later transfer list refers to the shelf number, not the individual books."



"So the dispatch book may have been moved again."



"It may have been."



"Or it may have been removed before the transfer."



"That is also possible."



Mingyuan looked at the index.



"Can we identify who cleared the compartment?"



Zhou pointed to the final column.



"A storehouse clerk's name appears here, but the entry is partly obscured."



Liang Wen read the visible characters.



"Perhaps Chen."



"Which Chen?" Mingyuan asked.



"I cannot tell."



Mingyuan leaned back.



The old accounts storehouse had held papers from several departments. A shelf could contain a transport register one year and unrelated tax records the next. The reorganization had apparently moved entire compartments without preserving every individual title.



He looked at Zhou.



"Was there a separate list of books removed from that shelf?"



"There should have been."



"Should have been?"



"The procedure required one. Whether it survives is another question."



Mingyuan rubbed his forehead.



"Then we search for the list."



Zhou nodded.



"That would be the next step."



Mingyuan turned to Liang Wen.



"Prepare a request for the old accounts storehouse inventory. Ask for the transfer list covering that shelf and the names of the clerks responsible for clearing it. Do not ask them to identify the four-cart dispatch unless the register itself is found."



Liang Wen wrote.



"Should we mention the missing book?"



"Yes. Describe it by its recorded title and the date range we need. Do not tell them what we expect it to contain."



Zhou looked at him approvingly.



"That will prevent someone from finding a different book and declaring the matter settled."



Mingyuan gave a dry smile.



"I have already received enough explanations from people who have not found what I asked them to find."



He returned to the transfer index.



The sixth box had not yet been opened. But now they had a more precise place to search, and a clearer reason for doing so.



That was better than opening another box merely because it existed.

Shen Mingzhao received the second merchant's sample shortly before noon.



It arrived in a small wrapped parcel accompanied by a written quotation.



Qiao Yue placed it on the table.



"The merchant says this is the closest material he can supply without changing the loom arrangement."



Mingzhao untied the wrapping.



The cloth was slightly heavier than the first supplier's sample. Its weave was even, and the threads appeared stronger. The colour was closer to the reference than the first merchant's second attempt, but the surface had a faint sheen that the reference did not possess.



She held the two pieces side by side.



The foreman stood opposite her.



"Which one is better?" he asked.



"Better for what?"



He paused.



"For the proposed order."



"That is not enough. The customer wants a familiar appearance, not merely a cloth that can be woven."



She placed the samples on the table.



"The second merchant's material is stronger. The first merchant's material is closer in weight. Neither matches the reference."



The foreman leaned forward.



"If we adjust the finishing, we might reduce the sheen."



"Would that change the colour?"



"Possibly."



"Would it change the texture?"



"Possibly."



"Would it make the material more expensive?"



"Yes."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"Then we would be changing three things to make a material acceptable that has not yet been accepted in its present form."



The foreman straightened.



"That would be difficult."



"It would also be unnecessary."



She turned to Qiao Yue.



"Ask the merchant whether he can provide a small piece finished in the same manner as the reference. If he cannot, ask him to state that plainly."



Qiao Yue made a note.



"And the first supplier?" she asked.



"Tell him we have not accepted his third sample in advance. We shall examine it when it arrives, but the written terms remain necessary."



Qiao Yue nodded.



The foreman looked at the two samples again.



"Would you like me to prepare a comparison record?"



"Yes. Record the material, weight, weave, colour, surface, and price separately. Do not write simply 'acceptable' or 'unacceptable' without explaining why."



He nodded.



Mingzhao watched him gather the cloth.



The workshop's previous orders had taught her that a written sample could prevent arguments, but only if the description was clear enough to be useful. A vague note that said *same as sample* was little help if the sample itself had not been preserved or if nobody could explain what had been approved.



She wanted the next order to be based on a standard both sides could inspect.



She did not want to spend the next month arguing over what a merchant had meant by *nearly the same*.



After the foreman left, Qiao Yue remained.



"Madam, there is another letter."



Mingzhao took it.



It was from a small trader who had heard that the workshop had completed its previous orders. He wished to discuss a larger quantity of cloth, but had not stated a price, delivery date, or payment arrangement.



Mingzhao read the letter.



"Did he include a sample?"



"No."



"A proposed quantity?"



"No."



"An advance?"



"No."



Qiao Yue looked at the letter.



"He asks whether you are willing to meet."



"Write that he may send the specifications and proposed terms first."



"Should we arrange a meeting if he does?"



"If the proposal is worth discussing."



Qiao Yue took the letter.



Mingzhao returned to her account book.



She had learned that a meeting could consume half a day and still leave everyone agreeing only that another meeting was necessary.



There was no reason to begin with that.

At the western guest residence, Xu Yuru received a visit from a young attendant who had once worked in her former household.



The girl was now assigned to another section of the palace. She had come with permission to deliver a small bundle of sewing thread and a note from an older palace woman who had retired to her home outside the Imperial City.



Yuru recognized the handwriting.



She opened the note.



The retired woman had heard that Yuru was staying in the guest residence. She sent the thread because she remembered that Yuru had once admired its colour. She also mentioned that her knees had become troublesome and that she no longer visited the palace.



There was no request for assistance.



Yuru looked at the thread.



It was a deep blue, wound around a small wooden spool.



"She remembered this?"



Fang Ru examined it.



"It is a lovely colour."



Yuru smiled.



"She used to say that the palace stores never had enough of this shade."



The attendant stood quietly near the door.



Yuru folded the note.



"Please thank her for me. Tell her I remember the thread, and I hope her knees improve."



The girl bowed.



Yuru hesitated.



"How long has she been living outside the city?"



"Since the beginning of spring, Madam."



"Does she have family nearby?"



"Her younger sister lives with her."



"Then I hope they are keeping each other company."



The attendant nodded and departed.



Fang Ru placed the spool beside the tea jar.



"Would you like to use it?"



"Perhaps."



Yuru picked it up.



She had not embroidered anything for a long time. There had always been other things to attend to, and the palace's formal garments were generally prepared by experienced women whose work was more regular than hers.



She turned the spool in her fingers.



"Do we still have the small sewing frame?"



"Yes. It is in the cabinet."



"Bring it out later."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Would you like me to prepare the cloth?"



"No. I shall look for it myself."



Yuru placed the thread carefully on the table.



She had no intention of making anything elaborate. A small piece of embroidery would be enough.



For several years, almost every useful thing she had made had been made for someone else.



She thought it might be pleasant to make something simply because she liked the colour.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan's request for the old accounts storehouse inventory was sent before the afternoon meal.



He then turned his attention to the fifth-box repair records.



Steward Zhou had asked the old yard clerk to compare the surviving repair entry with the handwriting in the main cart register. The clerk had returned with a cautious opinion.



"The same hand may have written the repair note and the return entry," Zhou explained. "But the register has several different clerks' hands, and the writing is not consistent throughout."



Mingyuan examined the comparison.



"Can he identify the writer?"



"No. He believes the entries may have been made by one of two clerks."



"Does he know their names?"



"One may have been the yard clerk who received the three returned carts. The other was a temporary assistant."



Mingyuan looked at the names in the staff list.



"Are either of them still alive?"



"The temporary assistant is not known to the estate. The yard clerk retired several years ago. His present whereabouts are uncertain."



Mingyuan placed the comparison beside the other papers.



"Then we have another possible witness, not a conclusion."



Zhou nodded.



"That is correct."



Mingyuan looked at the record.



"Why did the fourth cart's receiving name appear altered?"



"We do not know."



"Could it have been corrected after the repair?"



"Possibly. The ink differs, but the paper is damaged and the entries may have been written at different times."



"Could it be a different cart with the same number?"



"That is possible too."



Mingyuan sighed.



He had once imagined that old records would be like a series of doors. Open one, and the next would be visible. Instead, the records were more like a courtyard full of half-open shutters. Every time he looked through one, he found another wall.



He stood and walked to the window.



The estate courtyard was quiet. A servant crossed with a tray. Somewhere beyond the wall, a carpenter was working.



Mingyuan returned to the table.



"Have we checked the fifth-box receipt against the general purchasing register?"



"Yes," Liang Wen replied. "The repair payment is recorded separately from the old purchasing voucher under investigation."



"Good. Keep them separate."



Liang Wen nodded.



Mingyuan pointed to the transport office allowance.



"And this?"



"Also separate. It may have funded several repairs."



"Then do not use it to explain the axle payment unless the figures and dates support that."



Liang Wen wrote a note.



Mingyuan was becoming increasingly aware that a record could be accurate and still be misleading if placed beside the wrong document.



The estate had spent years treating papers as though their mere existence settled the questions around them. Now he was discovering that the work of understanding them was much more tedious.



It was also necessary.

That afternoon, Xu Yuru took the small sewing frame from the cabinet.



Fang Ru laid out a piece of pale cloth and placed the spool beside it.



Yuru examined the thread.



"The colour is darker than I remember."



"It may have changed slightly with age."



"Perhaps."



She threaded the needle and made several small stitches.



The first line was uneven.



She looked at it.



Fang Ru did not comment.



Yuru removed the needle and tried again.



The second line was better.



After a while, Fang Ru asked, "What are you making?"



"I have not decided."



"Then how will you know when it is finished?"



Yuru looked at the small pattern.



"Perhaps when I decide that I have made enough."



Fang Ru smiled.



The answer seemed to satisfy her.



Yuru continued stitching.



The work required patience, but not the sort that came from waiting for someone to finish a report or from keeping several people from arguing over an allocation. She could make a mistake, undo it, and begin again. Nobody would suffer because the pattern took another day.



When the afternoon light shifted, she put the frame aside.



The small embroidered line was not particularly impressive.



But it was hers.



She looked at it for a moment, then covered it with a clean cloth.

Mingzhao's final letter of the day came from the first supplier.



The third sample had been prepared, but the merchant had not yet sent it. He wanted to know whether Mingzhao would accept a written statement that the colour might vary slightly in production.



She read the question and placed the letter beside the previous statement.



The merchant was asking her to agree to uncertainty before showing her the result.



Mingzhao called Qiao Yue.



"Reply that we cannot agree to production variation before examining the sample and establishing what variation is acceptable."



"Should I say that we will not proceed?"



"Say that we will not proceed under the proposed terms. If he wishes to provide a sample and discuss a measurable range of variation, we may examine it."



Qiao Yue wrote the reply.



"Would you like to set a precise colour range?"



"Not until we have a sample that can serve as the reference. We cannot describe a range around something we have not approved."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao returned to the notebook.



The trial remained unapproved.



The workshop's ordinary work was complete. The workers had been paid. The reserve was intact. The merchants had not yet provided terms she could accept.



There was no emergency.



She closed the account book and placed the samples in separate envelopes, each labelled with the merchant's name and date.



The first supplier's cloth was not to be confused with the second supplier's cloth. The second supplier's quotation was not to be treated as an agreement. The third sample, when it arrived, would be examined as a new piece of evidence, not as a reward for the merchant's persistence.



She wrote the day's conclusion beneath the earlier entry:



*Two suppliers examined. Neither material approved. No purchase. No production.*



Then she put away the brush.

Before the lamps were lit, Mingyuan received a brief message from the old accounts storehouse.



The storehouse clerk had located a transfer list for the shelf identified in the earlier index. It had not yet been copied or examined, but the clerk confirmed that the list existed.



Mingyuan read the message.



"At last," he said.



Steward Zhou looked over his shoulder.



"That is useful."



"It is a list."



"Yes."



"Not the dispatch book."



"No."



Mingyuan put the message down.



"Then I shall be pleased when the list arrives."



Zhou considered this.



"That is a reasonable expectation."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"Please do not praise my expectations."



Zhou smiled and gathered the papers.



The next step was clear. They would examine the transfer list, identify who had cleared the shelf, and determine whether the northern dispatch register had been transferred elsewhere or removed from the records.



It was not the answer.



But it was a path toward one.



In the western guest residence, Xu Yuru placed her unfinished embroidery beside the tea jar.



In the eastern market, Mingzhao's two rejected samples rested in separate envelopes.



And at the Marquisate, Mingyuan placed the message from the old accounts storehouse on top of the fifth-box inventory.



By the end of the day, none of them had completed what they were doing.



Yuru had made only a few uneven stitches.



Mingzhao had bought no material.



Mingyuan had found only a list that might lead to a missing book.



Yet each had managed to avoid making a small mistake larger merely to give the day a more impressive conclusion.



For the moment, that was enough.
