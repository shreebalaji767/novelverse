# **Chapter 30: The Empress Has Two Days Left. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That Freedom Comes with Rent. The Emperor Has Discovered That Nobody Knows Where to Put the Ceremonial Screens.**



**22nd Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The imperial palace had encountered a problem involving ceremonial screens.



It was not a serious problem.



No one had been injured. No valuable object had been damaged. No official had been accused of misconduct.



The problem was simply that the ceremonial screens had been assigned to three different storage locations.



The Department of Rites believed they should be returned to the ceremonial storehouse.



The Imperial Household Administration believed they belonged in the general palace stores.



The Palace Stores had received an instruction to place them in the eastern warehouse.



All three instructions carried official seals.



None of them identified who had authority to determine the final location.



At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, Emperor Zhao Jingheng read the three documents.



Then he read them again.



"Where are the screens now?"



Gao Shun bowed.



"In the ceremonial hall, Your Majesty."



"Why?"



"The officials have not agreed where to move them."



The emperor stared at him.



"Then why did they issue three storage instructions?"



Gao Shun lowered his head.



"I do not know, Your Majesty."



Zhao Jingheng put down the papers.



"Call the Department of Rites and the Imperial Household Administration."



The two officials arrived shortly afterward.



The emperor placed the documents before them.



"Explain."



The senior official from the Department of Rites spoke first.



"The screens were prepared specifically for the ceremony. We therefore believe they should remain under the supervision of the ceremonial storehouse."



The administrator from the Imperial Household Administration disagreed.



"The screens are palace property. Once the ceremony is complete, their storage should be managed by the Palace Stores."



"Then why did your office issue an instruction to the eastern warehouse?" the emperor asked.



The administrator hesitated.



"That was the designated location in the previous storage arrangement."



"And why does the Department of Rites say they belong in the ceremonial storehouse?"



"Because the ceremonial storehouse maintains similar objects."



The emperor looked from one official to the other.



"Are these screens required for future ceremonies?"



"Possibly, Your Majesty."



"Are they damaged?"



"No."



"Do they require special storage?"



"Not beyond ordinary protection from dampness and insects."



"Then what is the actual disagreement?"



The officials exchanged glances.



At last, the Department of Rites official said, "The question is which office should maintain the record."



The emperor leaned back.



"That is the question?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then decide who maintains the record."



The administrator began to speak.



The emperor raised one hand.



"Do not return with three more documents explaining why the decision belongs to another office. The screens are palace property. The Palace Stores will maintain the physical inventory. The Department of Rites will retain a reference to their ceremonial use. The Imperial Household Administration will retain the purchase and maintenance records."



The officials bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And the screens?"



"They will be transferred to the Palace Stores."



"Good."



The emperor dismissed them.



Gao Shun remained.



Zhao Jingheng looked at the three documents.



"How many people were involved in this?"



Gao Shun answered carefully.



"Seven officials and clerks, Your Majesty."



The emperor closed his eyes.



"Seven people to decide where to put a screen."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"At least the screens have not moved."



Gao Shun did not know whether this was a complaint or praise.



He decided not to ask.



At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru was reviewing the final preparations for the next day's ceremony.



Two days remained before her formal departure.



The ceremonial garments had been prepared. The invited relatives had confirmed their attendance. The official schedule had been approved. The three seals remained in the custody of the Imperial Archives.



There was little left that required her attention.



This was fortunate.



It was also slightly unsettling.



Fang Ru entered with a list.



"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites has completed the final arrangements."



Yuru looked at the list.



"Have the attendants been confirmed?"



"Yes."



"The invited relatives?"



"Yes."



"The ceremonial objects?"



"Yes."



"The transfer records?"



"Prepared."



Yuru nodded.



"Then the arrangements are complete."



Fang Ru did not leave.



"Your Majesty, there is one matter concerning the final announcement."



"What is it?"



"The Department of Rites wishes to know whether the formal announcement should include the full list of administrative responsibilities transferred during the review."



Yuru considered this.



"No."



Fang Ru looked surprised.



"Should the announcement mention the transfer at all?"



"It should state that the duties and records have been transferred to the offices designated by the emperor's order. The full list belongs in the administrative record, not the ceremonial announcement."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Yuru returned the paper.



"Have they prepared the final wording?"



"Yes."



"Then let them use it."



Fang Ru bowed.



At the door, she paused.



"Your Majesty, the attendants have asked whether they should continue addressing you according to the usual forms after tomorrow."



Yuru looked at her.



"The ceremony is tomorrow?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then until the ceremony is completed, the usual forms remain appropriate."



"And afterward?"



"That will depend on the emperor's formal arrangements."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru turned toward the window.



The question was simple.



The answer was not.



She would no longer be Empress after the formal ceremony. The palace would still have an emperor, princes, princesses, consorts, officials, and servants. The inner household would continue to function.



But the name by which people addressed her would change.



She had expected that.



She had not yet decided what she would feel when it happened.



In the eastern market district, Shen Mingzhao was reviewing the first complete household expense statement.



It was a modest document.



The total expenditure was not alarming.



The problem was that several categories had been recorded incorrectly.



The kitchen servant had entered fuel under food supplies.



The second attendant had included the purchase of a replacement water jar under repairs.



The senior maid had recorded the cost of lamp oil as a household provision but had not entered the quantity purchased.



Mingzhao examined the entries.



"Who prepared this?"



The senior maid raised her hand.



"I did, Miss."



"Why is the fuel listed under food?"



"I thought it was part of the kitchen supplies."



"It is used in the kitchen. That does not make it food."



The maid lowered her head.



"Yes, Miss."



Mingzhao looked at the water jar entry.



"Why is this under repairs?"



"The old jar cracked."



"Was the jar repaired or replaced?"



"Replaced."



"Then it is a household purchase, not a repair."



The maid nodded.



Mingzhao turned to the lamp oil.



"How much was purchased?"



"One jar."



"What was the price?"



The maid gave the amount.



"Was the entire jar received?"



"Yes."



"Then enter the quantity."



The senior maid began correcting the report.



Qiao Yue watched from the side.



"Miss, the total amount is still correct."



"The total is not the only thing that matters."



"But the money is accounted for."



"Today. If we need to compare purchases over several months, the categories will matter."



Qiao Yue leaned against the table.



"At the Marquisate, you would have sent this back."



"I have sent it back."



"You are sitting in the same room."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Would you prefer I send it through a messenger?"



Qiao Yue laughed.



The senior maid, who had been worried about the corrections, relaxed slightly.



Mingzhao reviewed the amended report.



The household had spent more than she had expected on small items.



Not enough to threaten her finances.



Enough to make her reconsider the allowance for ordinary provisions.



She adjusted the weekly budget.



The change was small.



It was also the first adjustment she had made based on the actual spending of her independent household rather than an estimate derived from the Marquisate's accounts.



She entered the new amount.



Then she closed the ledger.



"That is sufficient for today."



Qiao Yue looked relieved.



"Truly?"



"Yes."



"Then I suggest we eat before another report arrives."



The report did arrive.



It came from Sun Qiming.



The accountant had completed a further comparison of the old warehouse records and the transport statements.



He had found an additional document in the former northern warehouse steward's papers.



It was a small receiving note, written in an old hand.



The note recorded the arrival of several carts at a temporary stopping point along the southern road.



It did not name the final warehouse.



It did not state the number of sacks.



It did, however, provide a date that corresponded with the three-cart journey recorded in the transport notebook.



Mingzhao read the document carefully.



"Does this identify the receiving location?" she asked.



Sun Qiming shook his head.



"No. It identifies the stopping point only."



"Does it establish the number of sacks?"



"No."



"Does it correspond with the eighteen sacks in the southern warehouse ledger?"



"The date corresponds. The quantities do not."



Mingzhao placed the note beside the other records.



"Then it supports the existence of a stop, not a completed delivery."



"Correct."



"Can the stopping point be identified?"



"I have asked the former steward to examine the description. He may remember the location."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Continue."



Sun Qiming looked at the papers.



"Miss Shen, I should point out that the note may eventually help explain the movement of the grain, but it does not yet establish whether the remaining sacks were delivered, lost, or recorded elsewhere."



"I understand."



"The original discrepancy remains."



"Then the original discrepancy remains."



Sun Qiming nodded.



He had expected more pressure to reach a conclusion.



Instead, Mingzhao returned the receiving note to its protective folder.



"Preserve it with the original records."



The accountant bowed.



As he left, Qiao Yue examined the copy.



"At least we have found something."



"We have found a stopping point."



"That is something."



"Yes."



Mingzhao looked at the note again.



It was a small piece of paper.



It had survived several years of storage, multiple changes of staff, and the general tendency of household records to become less organized whenever no one was actively looking for them.



It did not solve the mystery.



But it gave the investigation another direction.



At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received the same report from Sun Qiming.



He had asked for a copy to be sent to the family because the grain discrepancy remained part of the estate's liabilities.



He examined the new receiving note.



Then he called Steward Zhou.



"Have you seen this before?"



Steward Zhou studied it.



"Not this exact paper."



"Does the stopping point sound familiar?"



"The old steward may know."



"Has he been contacted?"



"Yes. He is expected to visit tomorrow."



Mingyuan nodded.



He looked at the ledger.



"Twenty sacks were covered by the movement instruction. Eighteen were confirmed received. Two remain unconfirmed. Twenty-seven are still unexplained."



"Yes."



"Then this note does not change the figures."



"No."



Mingyuan placed the paper aside.



He had once thought that finding a new document would mean finding an answer.



Now he understood that a new document could simply add another question.



He turned to the next account.



The twelve-tael repair expense remained unverified.



The two hundred and twenty-eight taels still lacked complete supporting records.



The family settlement remained open.



Mingyuan looked at the growing stack.



"Steward Zhou."



"Yes, Young Master?"



"How did Elder Sister manage all of this?"



Steward Zhou considered the question.



"She did not manage it alone."



Mingyuan looked up.



"The stewards, clerks, and household officers all performed their duties. Miss Mingzhao reviewed the work and dealt with the matters that required her authority."



"Then why did it seem as though everything depended on her?"



"Because she was the person who noticed when the separate duties did not fit together."



Mingyuan looked at the records again.



That was the part he had not understood.



The household had always had people to perform the work.



It had depended on Mingzhao to notice when the work failed to connect.



He returned to the account.



"Then we must learn to notice."



Steward Zhou bowed.



"Yes, Young Master."



At the palace, the emperor received a private visit from the Crown Prince.



Zhao Cheng'an carried a report concerning the Eastern Palace's expenditure.



"I have reviewed the lamp oil records," he said.



"And?"



"The additional issue was necessary. The evening study sessions increased, and the reserve stock was used according to the recorded authorization."



"Good."



"The original register did not include the reason for the additional quantity."



"Has it been corrected?"



"Yes. The responsible clerk has added the reference to the issue record."



Zhao Jingheng nodded.



The Crown Prince hesitated.



"There is another matter."



"Speak."



"The Eastern Palace has requested additional bedding for the winter preparation."



The emperor looked at him.



"It is the fourth month."



"The request concerns advance procurement."



"How much?"



The Crown Prince gave the figure.



The emperor read the supporting report.



"Why does the request exceed last year's amount?"



"The number of attendants has increased."



"By how many?"



"Six."



"Then the increase should be supported by the number of new attendants and the expected replacement rate."



"I have asked for that information."



"Good."



The Crown Prince waited.



Zhao Jingheng looked at him.



"What else?"



"Nothing, Father."



"Then why are you standing there?"



The Crown Prince hesitated.



"I wanted to ask whether Mother would have approved the request."



The emperor put down the report.



"Your mother is no longer responsible for approving it."



"I know."



"Then why ask?"



"I wanted to know what she would have thought."



Zhao Jingheng regarded his son.



"She would have asked for the supporting figures."



The Crown Prince nodded.



"That is what I did."



The emperor gave a faint smile.



"Then you already know what she would have thought."



The Crown Prince bowed.



As he turned to leave, the emperor called him back.



"Cheng'an."



"Yes, Father?"



"Do not make your mother's methods into another set of rules that everyone must follow without thought."



The Crown Prince looked puzzled.



"Her methods?"



"She asked questions because the answers mattered. Do not ask questions merely because she would have asked them."



Zhao Cheng'an considered this.



"Understood."



He left.



The emperor returned to his work.



He was beginning to recognize that his wife's influence would remain in the palace long after her administrative authority had ended.



That was not necessarily a problem.



It would become one only if everyone tried to turn her habits into regulations without understanding their purpose.



The Empress Dowager Wei arrived at Chengming Palace before the evening meal.



She had brought a small box of personal ornaments.



Yuru looked at it.



"What is this?"



"Your personal belongings."



The Empress Dowager opened the box.



Inside were several pieces of jewelry that had been kept in the Empress's private store.



"These were given to you by the imperial family. They should be transferred to your personal custody after the ceremony."



Yuru examined the inventory.



The ornaments were listed separately from the ceremonial objects and the official seals.



"Thank you."



The Empress Dowager closed the box.



"I have also reviewed the arrangements for your temporary residence."



Yuru looked up.



"The guest residence?"



"Yes. It has been prepared."



"Then I will use it."



The older woman studied her.



"You have not decided where you will live permanently."



"Not yet."



"Do you intend to remain in the palace guest residence indefinitely?"



"No."



"Then you should begin considering your options."



"I have."



The Empress Dowager sighed.



"You have spent so long telling other people to make decisions that you now seem determined to avoid making one yourself."



Yuru smiled.



"Perhaps I have learned that some decisions benefit from being made slowly."



The Empress Dowager looked unimpressed.



Then she said, "I will not ask you to remain."



Yuru's expression softened.



"Thank you."



"But I will expect you to visit."



"I will."



"And you may still advise the emperor when he asks."



"Personally, yes."



The Empress Dowager nodded.



She seemed satisfied with that distinction, though not entirely comfortable with it.



As she rose to leave, she said, "Tomorrow's ceremony must proceed properly."



"It will."



"And you will not make a speech about how everyone should learn to manage without you."



Yuru looked at her.



"I had not planned to."



"Good. It would be an unnecessary embarrassment."



Yuru laughed softly.



The Empress Dowager left.



In the eastern market district, Mingzhao received a visitor shortly before sunset.



It was Lin Shuyun.



The merchant arrived without a large escort, carrying a small package of fabric samples.



She looked around the receiving room.



"So this is the new residence."



"Temporary residence," Mingzhao corrected.



Lin Shuyun glanced at the room.



"Of course."



She placed the samples on the table.



"The next group of silk is ready for inspection. Zhou Wenqi has agreed to examine forty-two bolts tomorrow."



"Are the two corrected bolts included?"



"No. They are still being finished."



"Good."



Lin Shuyun handed her the production report.



"The first payment remains in the partnership account. The finishing costs for the next group have been recorded. There is no distributable profit yet."



Mingzhao examined the figures.



"Have the workers been paid?"



"Yes."



"All of them?"



"All recorded workers. The final finishing costs remain outstanding."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then the account is correct."



Lin Shuyun looked at her.



"You seem disappointed."



"Why?"



"I expected you to find something wrong."



"Would you prefer that I invent an error?"



Lin Shuyun laughed.



"No. I would prefer that you continue to be difficult about the correct things."



She examined the room again.



"How is the household?"



"Functional."



"That is a cold description."



"It is accurate."



"Have you had any difficulties?"



"A cooking pot was misplaced."



Lin Shuyun stared at her.



"That is your first difficulty?"



"It was listed in the rental inventory."



Lin Shuyun laughed so loudly that Qiao Yue came in to see what had happened.



When she heard the explanation, she laughed too.



Mingzhao looked at both of them.



"The matter has been resolved."



"Of course," Lin Shuyun said. "I would hate to think that the household's first crisis remained unsettled."



Mingzhao returned to the production report.



"Forty-two bolts tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"Then the inspection should proceed as scheduled."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



She did not remain long.



Before leaving, she gave Mingzhao a small piece of fabric.



"A sample from the latest batch. I thought you might want to see the colour."



Mingzhao examined it.



The fabric was a muted blue.



"It is good."



"It is not part of the sale stock."



"I understand."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"Then I will see you tomorrow."



At the imperial palace, the final ceremonial preparations continued into the evening.



The ceremonial screens had finally been moved to the Palace Stores.



The Department of Rites retained a reference record.



The Imperial Household Administration retained the procurement documents.



The Palace Stores retained the physical inventory.



Seven officials had spent much of the day arranging the transfer.



The screens had travelled less than two courtyards.



Gao Shun delivered the completion report to the emperor.



Zhao Jingheng read it.



Then he put it down.



"At least the screens have reached their destination."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"How many people signed the transfer?"



Gao Shun checked the report.



"Six."



The emperor looked at him.



"Only six?"



Gao Shun bowed.



The emperor sighed.



"Tomorrow, I will attend the ceremony. I do not want to hear about another screen."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He looked at the final ceremonial schedule.



Two days remained until the formal departure.



Tomorrow would be the ceremony.



After that, Yuru would no longer be Empress.



The emperor had spent much of the past month thinking about what the palace would lose when she left.



He was beginning to think about what she might gain.



He did not yet know whether she would remain nearby, move to a separate residence, or spend more time with her own family.



He had not asked.



He would ask when the ceremony was over.



For once, he intended to let her decide something without first arranging it for her.



That night, Yuru sat in her private study.



The final ceremonial garments had been laid out.



The personal ornaments had been inventoried.



The guest residence was ready.



The official seals were in the Archives.



The administrative responsibilities had been transferred.



She looked at the last report.



It was a simple confirmation that the Department of Rites had received the final schedule and would conduct the ceremony.



There was no request for her approval.



There was no question for her to answer.



She placed the report aside.



Fang Ru entered.



"Your Majesty, everything is ready for tomorrow."



Yuru nodded.



"Thank you."



"Will you rest now?"



"Yes."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"Your Majesty, after tomorrow, should I continue serving you?"



Yuru looked at her.



"That will depend on your own wishes and the arrangements you choose to make."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



"I have served you for many years."



"I know."



"I would be willing to continue."



Yuru studied her.



"You should not make that decision only because you have always been here."



Fang Ru looked up.



Yuru continued.



"If you wish to remain with me, we can discuss it. If you wish to remain in palace service, you may do so. Your years of service do not oblige you to follow me."



Fang Ru was quiet.



Then she bowed.



"I will consider it."



"Good."



Fang Ru withdrew.



Yuru remained alone.



Tomorrow, the ceremony would take place.



She would leave the position she had held for many years.



The palace would continue.



The emperor would continue.



The princes and princess would continue.



The offices would continue.



And she would have to discover what it meant to live without being the person to whom every unfinished matter was sent.



Across the capital, Mingzhao had completed her first full independent household account.



The total was higher than her estimate by a modest amount.



She had corrected the records, adjusted the weekly allowance, and assigned the household duties.



The cooking pot had been found.



The family accounts remained unresolved.



The silk partnership continued.



The grain investigation had acquired another document but no final answer.



She closed the ledger and placed it on the shelf.



Tomorrow, she would inspect the next group of silk.



The day after that, she would review the household budget.



For once, the two women were not waiting for the same kind of departure.



Yuru had one final ceremony.



Mingzhao had one more day of learning how much an ordinary household cost.



Neither had finished.



But tomorrow, one of them would finally leave the title behind.
