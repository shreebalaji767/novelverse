# **Chapter 63: The Former Empress Has Returned to Her Guest Residence and Has Been Asked Whether Her Parents Fed Her Properly. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Returned to Her Workshop and Has Been Asked Whether the Family Finally Agreed. Both Women Have Discovered That People Who Were Not Present at a Meeting Are Often the Most Confident About Its Results.**

**27th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



Xu Yuru returned to the palace guest residence shortly after sunset.



Fang Ru helped her remove her outer cloak.



The journey had been uneventful.



The visit had been pleasant.



The food had been plentiful.



Duke Xu had attempted to send her home with a basket of fruit, two packets of preserved vegetables, and a small jar of honey.



Madam Xu had reduced the collection to one basket.



The Duke had then tried to put the honey inside the basket.



Madam Xu had removed it.



In the end, Yuru had brought home the fruit and a small packet of preserved vegetables.



Fang Ru placed them on the side table.



"Madam, shall I ask the kitchen to store these?"



"Yes. The fruit can remain here."



Fang Ru nodded.



She had noticed that Yuru was smiling.



"Was the meal enjoyable?"



"It was."



"Madam Xu seemed pleased."



"Mother was pleased."



"And Duke Xu?"



Xu Yuru considered this.



"Father was pleased when he was not being corrected."



Fang Ru lowered her head.



Her shoulders moved slightly.



Yuru looked at her.



"You may laugh."



Fang Ru smiled.



"I would not dare."



"You have already done so."



"Then I have been discovered."



Yuru laughed quietly.



The guest residence felt different when she returned from a family visit.



It was still temporary.



Her belongings were still limited.



The uncertain table and chairs remained palace property, and the disputed robe had not been removed from the inventory.



Yet the room no longer felt like a place where she was waiting for someone to tell her what to do.



She had visited her parents.



She had eaten.



She had returned.



It was an ordinary sequence of events.



For several years, ordinary sequences had been interrupted by matters that someone believed only she could settle.



She was beginning to appreciate the absence of such interruptions.

The following morning, Madam Zhao arrived with a small household report.



She stopped outside the sitting room.



"Madam."



Xu Yuru looked up from her book.



"Please come in."



Madam Zhao entered with a folder.



"I have brought the updated list concerning the guest residence."



Yuru closed her book.



"Is there a problem?"



"No serious problem. The household stores have completed the latest count. The items recorded as belonging to the guest residence have been separated from the items still awaiting ownership confirmation."



"Good."



"There is one question concerning the preserved vegetables."



Yuru looked at her.



Madam Zhao continued.



"The kitchen asks whether they should be entered as a personal gift or as food received by the guest residence."



Yuru considered the question.



"They were given to me by my mother. They are personal."



Madam Zhao wrote it down.



"Understood."



Fang Ru looked toward the basket.



The palace had once recorded the arrival and departure of every food item used by the former Empress's household.



Now a small packet of vegetables had become a question of classification.



Yuru noticed Fang Ru's expression.



"Is something amusing?"



"No, Madam."



"You are looking at the vegetables."



"I was wondering whether your mother would be surprised to learn that they required a separate entry."



Yuru smiled.



"She would probably send another packet to test the system."



Madam Zhao kept her expression composed.



She had worked in the palace long enough to understand that some jokes were safer to ignore.

At the Eastern Palace, Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an was examining a different set of records.



The oil inventory had been divided into three categories.



Oil received.



Oil issued.



Oil remaining.



The quantities had been recorded using different units.



Some entries referred to jars.



Others referred to weight.



A few used the phrase "one container," without specifying its capacity.



The Crown Prince had instructed the clerks to preserve the original descriptions.



He had also asked them to identify which entries could be reconciled without assuming that every container was identical.



The clerk standing beside him presented the latest table.



"Your Highness, the first group can be reconciled by the recorded jar capacity."



"And the second?"



"The second group uses weight."



"Can the quantities be converted?"



"Only where the weight and container capacity are both recorded."



The Crown Prince nodded.



"Then convert those entries only."



"Yes, Your Highness."



He looked at the remaining column.



"And the others?"



"Still unresolved."



"Leave them unresolved."



The clerk bowed.



The Crown Prince had learned that a clean-looking total could be less useful than an honest incomplete one.



He was not pleased by this discovery.



He was, however, becoming accustomed to it.

The Second Prince entered the Eastern Palace shortly afterward.



He had brought a revised estimate for a small gathering.



"Brother."



The Crown Prince looked up.



"Have you submitted the revised request?"



"I have."



"Then why are you carrying another copy?"



"Because I changed one line."



Cheng'an held out his hand.



Chengyu gave him the document.



The revised estimate separated the twelve confirmed guests from the possible additional attendees.



The attendant costs were also listed separately.



The Crown Prince read it.



"This is clearer."



"I know."



"Your previous estimate included twenty attendants for twelve guests."



"Some guests might bring additional attendants."



"Then the estimate should say that they are possible, not confirmed."



Chengyu folded his arms.



"I have already changed it."



"Good."



The Second Prince looked at the table.



"Are you still counting oil?"



"Yes."



"Has the oil become more cooperative?"



"No."



Chengyu sighed.



"Then I am fortunate that my gathering does not involve oil."



The Crown Prince returned the document.



"Your gathering involves money."



"That is why I submitted an estimate."



"And I approved the revised version."



Chengyu accepted the paper.



He had expected another objection.



Instead, he had received approval.



This made him suspicious.



"Are you certain?"



"Yes."



"Do you want me to explain the attendant calculation?"



"No."



Chengyu looked disappointed.



He had prepared an explanation.



The Crown Prince noticed.



"You may explain it if you wish."



The Second Prince immediately began.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, the morning after the meeting began with a revised document request.



Mr Liang had returned to the family representative's office.



The meeting had exposed several problems in the schedule.



He needed to revise the wording, distinguish the confirmed entries from the disputed ones, and prepare a list of supporting documents still required.



He had expected the work to be straightforward.



It was not.



The personal advances occupied three separate sections.



The study repair expenditure required a different classification.



The additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The grain discrepancy required its own supporting records.



The transport payment could not be attached to the grain movement without evidence.



The northern canal property had been removed from the family schedule.



The western field agreement had to be described as temporary cultivation rather than a renewed lease.



Mr Liang read the list twice.



Then he called for the clerk.



"Prepare a revised schedule."



The clerk asked, "Should we use the previous version?"



"Use it as a reference. Do not copy its classifications."



The clerk looked uncertain.



Mr Liang pointed to the papers.



"Every entry must match the evidence. If the evidence is missing, mark it as missing."



The clerk nodded.



The representative had not become fond of Mingzhao's method.



He was beginning to understand why it was necessary.

Shen Mingyuan spent the morning arranging the certified copies for Zhang, the former warehouse steward.



The original counting sheets were held in the Marquisate's records.



Mingyuan had instructed the steward to prepare copies bearing the appropriate certification.



The originals would remain in the estate archive.



He checked the list of pages.



"These are the sheets covering the period in question?"



Steward Zhou De nodded.



"Yes. The relevant entries are marked."



"Have the copies been compared with the originals?"



"Twice."



Mingyuan checked the seals.



"Then send them."



Zhou De hesitated.



"Should we include a request for Zhang to identify the missing grain?"



Mingyuan shook his head.



"Ask him to examine what he can confirm from the records. If he remembers something separately, he can state that as recollection."



The steward looked at him.



"That is a careful distinction."



Mingyuan did not smile.



"It is the distinction we need."



He had once believed that the old steward would simply know where the grain had gone.



Now he understood that a person could remember the warehouse without remembering every transaction.



He also understood that a record could explain one discrepancy while leaving another unanswered.



This was not satisfying.



It was necessary.

At the southern building, Shen Mingxiu examined the roof condition report.



The carpenter had inspected the affected section and provided an estimate.



The roof tiles required repair.



Several wooden supports needed attention.



The report also noted that the damage was not yet severe enough to require immediate structural replacement.



Mingxiu compared the estimate with the earlier version.



The corrected figure was lower.



She placed both documents side by side.



Her mother entered.



"Have you finished?"



"Almost."



Madam Liang looked at the papers.



"Why are there two estimates?"



"The first included work that was not necessary."



"Then why was it submitted?"



"The carpenter included replacement of a section that can be repaired."



Madam Liang frowned.



"Did you ask him to reduce the price?"



"I asked him to explain the work."



"That is the same thing."



"No, Mother."



Mingxiu kept her voice polite.



"If I ask him to reduce the price without understanding the work, he may simply reduce the quality of the repair."



Madam Liang looked at her daughter.



The answer was not entirely what she wanted to hear.



"Your father will decide whether the repairs are approved."



"I know."



"Then there is no need to spend so much time on it."



Mingxiu folded the report.



"If the work is approved, I want the carpenter to repair the damaged supports before the heavy rains."



Madam Liang said nothing.



She had expected Mingxiu to become more responsible.



She had not expected responsibility to involve asking questions that delayed decisions.

At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao returned to her workshop.



The new two-hundred-bolt order was still in production.



Lin Shuyun met her near the worktables.



"The next group is nearly finished."



Mingzhao looked toward the inspection area.



"How many?"



"Twenty-eight."



"Have they been checked?"



"Not all."



"Then they are nearly finished, not finished."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"You have become very particular about that distinction."



"I was particular before."



"Before, you were particular about other people's mistakes. Now you are particular about my vocabulary."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Your vocabulary affects the production report."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



The first twenty bolts had passed inspection under the revised folding method.



The next group had been slower.



One trainee had folded an edge too tightly, requiring correction.



The material remained usable.



The correction had been recorded.



The second trainee had made fewer mistakes but still required supervision.



The senior finisher had kept to the written instructions.



Mingzhao examined the worktable.



"Who checked the revised method today?"



"Zhao checked the first half. I checked the second."



"Did the trainee work under direct supervision?"



"Yes."



"Any change to the edge appearance?"



"Not beyond the accepted sample."



Mingzhao inspected two bolts.



The folds were consistent.



She turned one over and examined the underside.



"Good."



Lin Shuyun watched her.



"Does that mean we can increase the pace?"



"It means the next group can continue under the same supervision."



"That is not an answer."



"It is the answer I have."



Lin Shuyun shook her head.



"Your workers must find you exhausting."



"They are paid to produce goods that meet the contract."



"They are also paid to hear you say that."



Mingzhao placed the bolt back on the table.



"Then they are receiving the full service."

The buyer's representative was expected to inspect the next completed group in several days.



Until then, production would continue according to the agreed specifications.



The four additional workers had been recorded separately.



The two experienced workers were producing at the expected pace.



The two trainees were improving, but their work still required more supervision.



Mingzhao reviewed the wage entries.



The training costs remained separate from the production calculation.



She did not yet know the final profit.



The order had a deposit.



It had a delivery schedule.



It had an approved method.



It did not yet have a completed cost statement.



Lin Shuyun approached with another paper.



"The buyer has sent a short message."



Mingzhao looked up.



"What does it say?"



"He asks whether the next inspection can be arranged before the end of the month."



"Can it?"



"If we finish the current group and complete the checks."



"Then reply that we can propose a date after the group has passed inspection."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



"You will not promise the date first?"



"No."



"Even though it would please him?"



"Especially because it would please him."



Lin Shuyun laughed again.



She took the paper away.

That afternoon, Mingzhao returned to her temporary residence.



The lower room still showed signs of dampness after the previous rain.



The landlord's drainage repair had improved the situation, but the wall had not fully dried.



Mingzhao inspected it.



Qiao Yue stood behind her.



"Will you ask Madam Wu to repair it again?"



"Not yet."



"Why?"



"The drain is working. The wall needs time to dry."



Qiao Yue looked at the affected section.



"If it becomes worse?"



"Then we will document it and ask for another inspection."



Qiao Yue nodded.



She had become familiar with the difference between a repair that had failed and a repair whose result could not yet be judged.



It was a distinction that seemed to appear everywhere.



Mingzhao returned to the main room.



The revised property schedule lay on the table.



She had received no final version from the family representative.



That was expected.



The meeting had not concluded the division.



It had established which questions still required answers.



She opened her own copy and added a note beside the disputed grain entries.



*Awaiting examination of certified warehouse sheets.*



Then she placed the document aside.



Qiao Yue brought tea.



"Did the meeting achieve anything?"



"Yes."



"What?"



"We corrected the schedule."



"Did the family agree?"



"On several classifications."



"Did they agree to the division?"



"No."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Then we have returned to the beginning."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"No. At the beginning, the schedule was wrong."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"That is true."



"And now the family knows which parts are wrong."



"That is also true."



Mingzhao picked up her tea.



"Progress is sometimes an unpleasant document."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Then I hope the next one is shorter."



"So do I."

In the palace, Empress Dowager Wei received a brief account of Xu Yuru's visit to the Xu residence.



She listened while her attendant described the family meal.



"Only six dishes?" the Dowager asked.



"Madam Xu had requested that the meal remain simple."



"And Duke Xu?"



"He wished to add several things."



The Dowager smiled.



"That sounds like him."



The attendant hesitated.



"Madam Xu did not permit it."



The Dowager laughed softly.



"That sounds even more like her."



She looked toward the window.



The former Empress had spent years ensuring that the palace household ran smoothly.



Now she was being fed by her parents and prevented from doing unnecessary work.



The Dowager found the thought unexpectedly pleasant.



"Has Yuru chosen where she will live?"



"Not yet."



"Then do not ask her again."



The attendant bowed.



The Dowager considered the matter.



She still believed that the position of Empress carried responsibilities.



She also understood that those responsibilities had been formally relinquished.



There was no point in inviting Yuru to tea and then asking her to review a household account.



The Dowager had no intention of making that mistake.



At least, not deliberately.

That evening, Xu Yuru sat by the window in the guest residence.



She had finished reading a book and was considering whether to begin another.



Fang Ru brought the fruit basket from the Xu residence.



"Madam Xu sent these."



Yuru looked at the basket.



"She did?"



"Yes. The kitchen says the fruit should be eaten soon."



Yuru smiled.



"Mother always sends enough for several people."



Fang Ru placed the basket on the table.



"Shall I share it with the attendants?"



"Yes."



Fang Ru selected a few pieces.



Yuru picked up one of the smaller fruits.



She thought of her parents' house.



Her father's questions.



Her mother's insistence that she eat.



The six dishes.



The soup that had not been counted as an extra dish.



She smiled again.



For years, people had approached her with requests because they believed she could resolve them.



Her parents had approached her with food because they wanted her to enjoy a meal.



The difference was not complicated.



It was simply important.



Across the capital, Mingzhao was closing her production ledger.



The next inspection had not yet taken place.



The family schedule had not yet been corrected.



The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.



The lower room was still damp.



Neither woman had reached the end of her difficulties.



But both had spent the day refusing to pretend that an incomplete matter was complete.



In the palace, that principle was becoming part of the Crown Prince's education.



In the Marquisate, it was becoming part of a property settlement.



In the workshop, it was becoming part of a production method.



And in the Xu residence, it had taken the form of six dishes.



The number was still being defended.
