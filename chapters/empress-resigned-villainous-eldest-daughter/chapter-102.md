# **Chapter 102: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Household Manual Recommends Keeping an Emergency Reserve but Does Not Explain Whether the Emergency Is a Broken Roof, an Uninvited Relative, or a Servant Who Has Been Asked to Do Three People's Work. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That a Workshop Can Report Progress Without Requesting Her Permission to Breathe. The Marquis's Heir Has Discovered That the Person Who Signed for a Notebook May Be Missing from the Register, Which Is a Different Problem from the Notebook Being Missing.**

**6th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had reached the chapter on emergency expenditure.

The author began with an admirable recommendation.

Every household should maintain a reserve for unexpected expenses.

Yuru nodded.

A roof could leak. A servant could fall ill. A wall could require repair. A delivery could be delayed. A household that spent every coin as soon as it arrived would eventually discover that even a small problem could become expensive.

Then the author wrote that the household head should personally decide whether every emergency was genuine.

Yuru stopped.

She read the sentence again.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Suppose a kitchen servant discovers that the stove chimney has cracked."

"Then the kitchen supervisor should report it."

"And if the supervisor is away?"

"The senior servant present should report it."

"And if the senior servant cannot be found?"

"Then the servant should prevent anyone from using the damaged stove and inform the household office."

Yuru looked up.

"That is already three steps. The author has allowed only one."

Fang Ru glanced at the book.

"Perhaps he expects the household head to be present."

"Then the household head must never leave the house."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru turned another page.

The author had described the emergency reserve as money that should remain untouched unless the household head personally approved its use.

Yuru took her notebook.

She wrote:

*An emergency reserve requires a defined authority for urgent use. The person responsible for preventing immediate harm should not have to wait for the household head to return. Expenditure must be recorded and reviewed afterward.*

She paused.

This was not a new principle.

In the palace, some matters could not wait for a formal review. A broken water pipe, damaged storehouse door, or spoiled food supply could create further losses if no one acted.

The solution had never been to let everyone spend without limits.

It had been to establish which official could authorize an urgent measure, what amount could be used, and what record had to follow.

Yuru wrote the final sentence:

*Urgency should permit action, not erase accountability.*

She put down the brush.

Fang Ru poured tea.

"Madam seems to have found something useful."

"Yes."

"Will you send the observation to the palace?"

"No."

Fang Ru waited.

Yuru smiled faintly.

"I have written it in my notebook. That is sufficient for today."

She turned back to the book.

The next paragraph recommended that the household head inspect all emergency repairs personally.

Yuru closed it.

"Tomorrow."

At the workshop, Shen Mingzhao received a report from the finishing supervisor.

The first ten bolts of the final production group had completed weaving and were now undergoing finishing.

The next twenty remained in production.

The supervisor had included a separate note about the finishing work.

Two bolts required additional attention because the edges had tightened unevenly during folding. The material remained usable, and no loss had occurred. The supervisor had recorded the correction and assigned the work to the experienced finisher responsible for the section.

Mingzhao read the report.

She looked at the correction record.

The supervisor had not written that the bolts were defective beyond repair. He had not concealed the extra work. He had not asked her to decide whether the finisher could correct them.

He had recorded the issue and the action taken.

"That is acceptable," she said.

Qiao Yue stood beside her.

"Would you like to inspect them?"

"Has the supervisor requested an inspection?"

"No."

"Has the correction exceeded the approved specification?"

"No."

"Then the supervisor should complete the examination and submit the record."

Qiao Yue took the report.

Mingzhao returned to the contract folder.

The first fifty bolts had passed internal inspection. The remaining thirty were progressing through production and finishing. The buyer had not yet inspected the order.

She checked the delivery period again.

There was still time.

The workshop did not need to rush.

She placed the report with the others.

For several years, people had treated her attention as a resource that could be used without limit. If a worker had a question, she answered. If a clerk had an unclear entry, she corrected it. If a supervisor was uncertain, she made the decision.

The result had been a workshop in which many people waited for her opinion before acting.

Now she was learning to distinguish between a question that required her judgment and a task that required someone else to do their job.

She did not always enjoy the distinction.

Sometimes she still wanted to take the paper and correct it herself.

But she had begun leaving the paper on the desk.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received the transport office's latest report.

The office had located a fragment of an older notebook-issue register.

It contained several entries from the northern warehouse, but the relevant page was damaged.

The names of two supervisors appeared in the surviving portion.

The date of the four-cart dispatch was visible in the margin.

The column intended for the recipient's signature had been torn away.

Mingyuan examined the fragment.

The record established that the notebook had been issued to the transport supervisor's office.

It did not establish which supervisor had personally received it.

It did not establish whether the notebook had been carried by the first supervisor or passed to the second.

It did not establish whether the notebook had been returned.

Liang Wen stood beside the table.

"At least we have the issue date."

"Yes."

"Can we now question both supervisors?"

"We can ask both about the notebook and the dispatch. But the questions must not assume that either one handled the carts."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan drafted two separate inquiries.

The first was addressed to the former supervisor who had held the position at the beginning of the relevant period.

The second was addressed to the man who had taken over afterward.

Each was asked whether he remembered receiving the notebook, whether he had passed it to another person, and whether he knew where it had been stored.

The inquiry about the four carts was separate.

Neither man was asked to confirm that the carts had carried grain.

That fact remained unknown.

Mingyuan signed the letters.

"Send them through the proper office."

Liang Wen gathered them.

"Should we also ask about the twenty-seven sacks?"

"Not in these letters. The grain inquiry has its own records. If either supervisor provides information that connects the dispatch to the grain, we can examine it then."

Liang Wen hesitated.

"Some of the elders may say we are making the matter unnecessarily complicated."

Mingyuan looked at the damaged register.

"It became complicated before we began asking questions."

He returned the fragment to its protective sleeve.

A damaged record was still evidence.

It was simply not evidence of everything the family wanted to know.

The Marquis of Qingyuan received the draft inquiries later that afternoon.

He read them with visible impatience.

"Two separate letters?"

"Yes, Father."

"Why not ask both men the same questions at once?"

"Because they held the position at different times. We need to know what each person actually remembers and what records each may have handled."

The Marquis put down the papers.

"Will they answer?"

"I do not know."

"Then we may receive nothing."

"We may."

"That is hardly reassuring."

Mingyuan did not answer.

The Marquis looked again at the damaged register.

"Can you not simply have the warehouse office determine what happened?"

"The warehouse office has already searched the surviving records. The movement notebook is missing, and the issue register is damaged."

"Then ask the people who worked there."

"That is what these letters do."

The Marquis leaned back.

He had spent much of his life expecting that a question asked by the head of the family would eventually produce an answer.

Mingyuan was discovering that authority could compel a person to respond, but it could not compel a missing record to reappear or make a witness remember what he had forgotten.

"Very well," the Marquis said at last. "Send them."

Mingyuan bowed.

He collected the papers and left.

The Marquis remained at the table.

Beside the transport inquiries lay the revised property schedule.

The schedule still required supporting documents.

The family still had not completed the division.

He looked at the two piles.

For years, Mingzhao had handled such matters without asking anyone whether the work was convenient.

Now the estate had to learn how much work had been hidden inside the word *handled*.

At Mingzhao's residence, a letter arrived from the family council.

The council acknowledged her latest questions about the property schedule.

It confirmed that the original receipt for one of the nine advances was still being sought in the estate archive. The surviving copy had been retained as supporting material, but the council did not claim that the copy was the original.

Mingzhao read the paragraph.

That was a reasonable answer.

The council had also requested clarification about the seventy-two taels spent on the study repairs.

She examined the attached reference.

The payment had been made from her personal funds. The work had been carried out on the estate's study building. The estate's maintenance records showed that the work had been completed, but the question of reimbursement had not been formally determined.

She wrote that she had no objection to the maintenance expenditure being classified as estate work, provided that the source of payment remained recorded as her personal funds.

She did not demand that the council immediately approve reimbursement.

She did not withdraw the claim either.

Qiao Yue read the reply.

"Will they ask for another meeting?"

"Probably."

"Will you attend?"

"If the meeting concerns the property division and the documents are ready."

"And if they ask you to explain the old accounts?"

"I will answer specific questions supported by records."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao placed the reply in its envelope.

There was a time when she would have read the entire schedule, corrected every entry, and returned it with instructions for the next clerk.

Now she was doing something narrower.

She was identifying which statements concerned her own property, which claims required evidence, and which duties belonged to the estate.

It was not as satisfying as controlling the whole document.

It was also less exhausting.

That evening, Xu Yuru received a small parcel from her mother.

It contained preserved plums and a note asking whether the weather had become too hot for long walks.

Yuru read the note and smiled.

Her mother had also written that Duke Xu had begun another book, but had been told not to choose it for Yuru until she had finished the two already borrowed.

Yuru looked toward the shelf.

The books were still there.

She had not forgotten them.

She had simply discovered that reading at her own pace was more pleasant than treating every book as another task to complete.

She took a preserved plum.

Fang Ru brought the household manual from the table.

"Madam, shall I put this away?"

Yuru looked at it.

"Yes."

"Have you finished the chapter?"

"I have finished enough of it."

Fang Ru placed the book on the shelf.

Yuru took out her notebook and reread the morning's observation.

*Urgency should permit action, not erase accountability.*

She considered it for a moment.

Then she added:

*And the person who acts in an emergency should not be blamed merely because the ordinary procedure could not be followed. The record must explain why the exception was necessary.*

She put the notebook away.

The palace had its own procedures now.

The Marquisate was trying to establish its own.

The workshop was learning to trust its supervisors.

None of these systems would become reliable in a single day.

But they could become more reliable without requiring one person to carry every decision.

Yuru ate another preserved plum.

"Fang Ru, tomorrow I would like to read something other than household management."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Would Madam prefer the book on granaries?"

Yuru looked at her.

"That is also household management, only with larger buildings."

Fang Ru laughed.

Yuru shook her head, amused.

"Perhaps I shall borrow a book about gardens."

"That sounds more restful."

"Unless the author recommends that I personally inspect every tree."

Fang Ru wisely did not answer.
