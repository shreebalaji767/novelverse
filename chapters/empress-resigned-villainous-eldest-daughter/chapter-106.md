# **Chapter 106: The Former Empress Has Discovered That Some Plants Prefer Shade and That a Sensible Gardener Does Not Demand an Explanation from Them. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Sent Eighty Bolts for Buyer Inspection and Has Discovered That a Receiving Warehouse Can Count the Same Goods Without Agreeing with the Carrier's Count. The Marquis's Heir Has Received a Reply from a Former Clerk Who Remembers a Dispatch but Cannot Remember What Was Dispatched.**

**10th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru began the morning with the garden book.

The author had reached the subject of shade.

Some plants, he explained, flourished in direct sunlight. Others required partial shade. A few became unhealthy if exposed to too much heat.

Yuru found the advice sensible.

She read the paragraph about arranging plants according to the conditions available rather than forcing them into a uniform pattern.

Then she reached the next recommendation.

The owner, the author declared, should personally examine the leaves of every plant at least once a week to determine whether the amount of sunlight was appropriate.

Yuru put down the book.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Does the author have a large garden?"

"I do not know."

"He may have one gardener for every tree."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Perhaps."

"Then he should write a book about employing gardeners instead."

Yuru picked up her tea.

She had begun to understand that a practical book could contain good advice and still be written by someone who had never considered the cost of following every instruction personally.

She turned back to the passage about shade.

The useful point was clear: different plants had different needs. A gardener who treated them all identically would damage some while trying to help others.

Yuru wrote a short note:

*An arrangement should respond to actual conditions. Uniform treatment is not the same as fair treatment.*

She read it once and closed the notebook.

The morning was quiet.

She had no palace reports to examine and no household registers awaiting her signature.

She returned to the book.

At the eastern receiving warehouse in River Quarter, the eighty bolts from Mingzhao's workshop arrived shortly before midday.

The carrier presented the dispatch list.

The warehouse clerk counted the bundles.

The first count matched the carrier's list.

The clerk then checked the labels against the contract reference and examined the seals on the containers.

One container had a seal that was difficult to read because the ink had spread during transport.

The clerk did not reject the container.

He recorded the unclear seal impression and asked the carrier to remain while the warehouse supervisor examined the packing record.

The carrier objected.

"The quantity is correct."

"The quantity may be correct," the clerk replied. "The seal is unclear."

"The goods are undamaged."

"Then the inspection should be simple."

The supervisor arrived and compared the container mark with the workshop's packing list.

The order number matched.

The bundle count matched.

The contents were checked against the packing record, and the quantity was confirmed.

The clerk recorded that the seal impression had been unclear but that the container's contents and count had been verified.

Only then did he sign the receiving record.

The carrier left with his copy.

The warehouse retained its own record.

The goods were now at the designated receiving warehouse.

They had not yet been accepted by the buyer.

Gao Wen's representative arrived later that afternoon.

He examined the approved sample, checked the width of the bolts, compared the colour, and inspected the edges.

The first group passed.

The second group passed.

The third group required a closer examination of several edges, but no defect outside the agreed specifications was found.

The representative reviewed the written inspection record.

All eighty bolts were accepted.

He signed the buyer's acceptance document.

The warehouse clerk entered the acceptance against the contract reference.

The inspection was complete.

The remaining payment had not yet been received.

At her residence, Shen Mingzhao received the inspection report.

She read the warehouse receipt first.

The carrier's dispatch count was recorded separately from the warehouse's receiving count.

The unclear seal impression had been explained by the inspection note.

The actual quantity had been confirmed.

She then read the buyer's acceptance document.

Eighty bolts.

All accepted.

No specification defects.

No correction order.

No rejection.

Mingzhao placed the papers side by side.

"Has Gao Wen's representative signed the acceptance?"

"Yes," Qiao Yue replied.

"Is the warehouse receipt complete?"

"Yes."

"Then send a copy of both documents to Sun Qiming."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao examined the contract.

The buyer had completed the inspection within the agreed period. The balance was now payable according to the contract.

She wrote a payment request, attaching the signed acceptance record and the warehouse receipt.

The deposit had already been received.

The remaining balance was now due.

She did not add it to the available funds until it arrived.

"Have the workshop supervisor prepare the final wage and material reconciliation," she said. "We should close the order once payment is confirmed."

Qiao Yue accepted the instruction.

Mingzhao looked at the papers.

The order had finally passed through production, inspection, delivery, and acceptance.

Only payment remained.

She allowed herself a small measure of satisfaction.

It was not a large contract.

It had not made her wealthy.

But the terms had been clear, the goods had passed inspection, and the records showed what had happened at each stage.

That was a respectable result.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received Li Shun's reply.

The former clerk had written that he remembered the four-cart dispatch.

He recalled preparing a movement entry for the carts, but he could not remember the cargo with certainty.

He believed the carts had been sent toward the northern road, though he could not identify the final destination.

He also remembered that the movement notebook had been kept in the transport office for ordinary use.

He did not remember transferring it personally to Zhao Liang.

He did not remember receiving a signed return record.

Mingyuan read the letter twice.

Liang Wen stood nearby.

"At least he remembers the dispatch."

"Yes."

"Does he remember the cargo?"

"No."

"Then we still do not know whether it was grain."

"No."

Mingyuan examined the letter again.

Li Shun's recollection was useful, but it was not a receipt.

The former clerk had confirmed that he prepared a movement entry for four carts. He had recalled a possible direction of travel. He had not identified the cargo or destination.

Mingyuan wrote a summary.

Li Shun remembered preparing the dispatch entry.

He recalled that the carts travelled toward the northern road, but could not confirm the destination.

He could not identify the cargo.

He did not remember transferring the movement notebook to the later supervisor.

No surviving record established the notebook's final custody.

Mingyuan placed the summary in the investigation file.

"Send a second question," he said. "Ask whether he remembers the names of the drivers or the person who ordered the dispatch."

Liang Wen nodded.

"And ask whether he retained any personal copy of the movement entry."

"Yes."

Mingyuan looked at the grain account.

The four-cart dispatch was now a confirmed movement of four carts.

It was not a confirmed movement of twenty-seven sacks.

The distinction had to remain.

The Marquis read Li Shun's reply in the afternoon.

"Four carts went north."

"That is what Li Shun remembers."

"Then perhaps the sacks went with them."

"Perhaps."

The Marquis looked at him.

"Can you not determine whether that is likely?"

"Not without knowing the cargo."

"Would four carts be sufficient?"

Mingyuan considered the question.

"It would depend on the size of the carts, their load capacity, and what else they carried."

"Then find out."

"We can examine the transport records and ask about the carts. But that will not establish their actual cargo by itself."

The Marquis looked displeased.

He wanted the twenty-seven sacks accounted for.

He also wanted the family to stop spending time on the question.

Mingyuan understood both desires.

But a possible explanation could not be entered into the grain account as a fact.

He bowed.

"I will continue the inquiry."

The Marquis waved him away.

Mingyuan returned to the accounts office.

The investigation had advanced by one small step.

The four carts had gone north.

That was more than they had known yesterday.

It was still not enough.

At the eastern-market residence, Mingzhao received the payment confirmation from Gao Wen.

The remaining balance had been transferred according to the contract.

She checked the amount against the agreed price.

It matched.

She then checked the deposit entry.

The deposit and balance together accounted for the full contract price.

Sun Qiming arrived later with the updated final account.

The materials, wages, training, finishing, inspection, packing, and transport costs were all recorded.

The additional inspection expenditure had reduced the margin slightly.

The revised folding method had saved time, but not enough to erase the training and inspection costs.

Even so, the order had produced a modest positive profit.

Mingzhao examined the final figures.

"Have all wages been paid?"

"Yes."

"Carrier?"

"Paid after the warehouse receipt was confirmed."

"Any outstanding supplier balance?"

"None."

She checked the final account again.

Then she signed it.

The Gao Wen order was closed.

Eighty bolts had been produced, inspected, delivered, accepted, and paid for.

No unresolved payment remained.

Mingzhao placed the final account in the completed-contract file.

Qiao Yue smiled.

"Another successful order."

"A completed order."

"Is there a difference?"

"There is always a difference between success and completion. The first is a judgment. The second is a record."

Qiao Yue accepted this answer without further discussion.

Mingzhao looked at the remaining papers on her desk.

The family property division was not complete.

The grain inquiry was not complete.

The old purchasing voucher remained missing.

The northern canal property remained unpurchased.

But the workshop had completed another contract without relying on her to supervise every stage.

She would take that result.

That evening, Xu Yuru reached the chapter about plants near walls.

The author warned that roots could damage old foundations if unsuitable trees were planted too close to buildings.

Yuru read the recommendation carefully.

It was practical.

She glanced at the courtyard wall.

The gardener had already chosen low-growing plants for the narrow space beside it.

Yuru did not call him over.

She trusted that he knew his work.

Fang Ru brought the evening tea.

"Has Madam enjoyed the book?"

"Yes."

"Will Madam continue tomorrow?"

"Certainly."

Yuru closed the book.

"Although I suspect the author will eventually recommend that I personally measure the distance between every tree and every wall."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Then perhaps Madam should stop before that chapter."

"That would be an incomplete book."

"Would that be a problem?"

Yuru considered it.

"No. I am not preparing an examination."

She took her tea.

Outside, the evening light fell across the courtyard.

The gardener was finishing his work. The path was clear, the drainage channel unobstructed, and the plants beside the wall had been watered.

Yuru watched for a moment.

Then she turned away.

The garden did not need her to certify that it was a garden.

It simply needed people who knew how to care for it.

And for once, she was content to be one of its visitors.
