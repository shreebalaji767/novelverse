# **Chapter 19: The Emperor Has Asked the Empress About Her Future. The Marquis Has Asked His Daughter About Her Price. Both Conversations Have Gone Poorly.**

**11th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Emperor had decided to speak with the Empress.

He had also decided not to bring any documents.

This was an unusual decision.

For years, most of their serious conversations had taken place over reports, expenditure summaries, ceremonial schedules, personnel appointments, and the occasional complaint from a senior palace attendant who believed that being ignored by another senior palace attendant constituted a matter of imperial importance.

Zhao Jingheng had therefore entered Chengming Palace with empty hands.

Gao Shun had followed him.

The Empress was in her private sitting room.

She had finished reviewing the morning's transition report and was discussing the next stage with Fang Ru.

When the Emperor entered, Fang Ru immediately rose.

"Your Majesty."

Yuru stood.

"Your Majesty."

Zhao Jingheng gestured for everyone to remain.

"Continue. I have not come to discuss an official matter."

Fang Ru looked briefly at Yuru.

Yuru nodded.

The administrator withdrew.

The Emperor sat opposite his wife.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Zhao Jingheng said, "What do you intend to do after leaving the palace?"

Yuru looked at him.

It was a reasonable question.

It was also a question he had asked eleven days after she submitted her petition.

"I have not decided every detail."

"Do you intend to return to your family?"

"Not immediately."

"Your Xu family?"

"They have their own household."

"Do you intend to establish a separate residence?"

"That is one possibility."

"Where?"

"I have not selected a place."

The Emperor looked at her.

"You have planned the palace's administrative transition in considerable detail."

"That was necessary."

"But you have not planned your own residence."

Yuru considered this.

"I have spent several years planning where other people should live, what they should receive, and how their households should function."

She folded her hands.

"I would like to choose my own arrangements without preparing a report first."

The Emperor looked at her.

There was no anger in her voice.

That made the answer more difficult.

He had been accustomed to asking Yuru for solutions.

Now she was telling him that she wanted the freedom not to have one ready.

"Do you intend to leave the capital?"

"I do not know."

"Do you intend to remain in the imperial family?"

Yuru looked at him carefully.

"That depends on what you mean."

The Emperor was silent.

She continued.

"If you mean whether I intend to treat the years I have spent here as though they never happened, no. If you mean whether I intend to continue performing every duty simply because I have always performed it, also no."

Zhao Jingheng lowered his eyes.

"Do you intend to live separately from me?"

"I have not made that decision."

He looked up.

"Would you?"

Yuru did not answer immediately.

"I want to decide what my days look like. I want to read without being interrupted by an expenditure report. I want to visit places without arranging an official reception. I want to speak to people without wondering whether the conversation will become a palace matter."

The Emperor listened.

"I want to be able to spend an afternoon doing nothing useful."

Zhao Jingheng looked at her.

"Nothing useful?"

"Yes."

"What would that involve?"

Yuru considered the question.

"Perhaps sitting in a garden."

"That is not entirely useless."

"Then I may have to find something less productive."

The Emperor almost smiled.

It was the first time in several days that their conversation had resembled their earlier private exchanges.

But the humour did not remove the seriousness beneath it.

"Yuru," he said quietly, "I do not know whether I can accept your resignation immediately."

She nodded.

"I know."

"I cannot pretend that your position is ordinary."

"I have never asked you to."

"And the consequences will be considerable."

"Then the consequences should be examined."

The Emperor looked at her.

"You make it sound as though this were another administrative matter."

"It is an administrative matter in part. It is also my life."

He fell silent.

Yuru did not fill the silence.

For once, she had no report to offer him.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao was also being asked about her future.

Unfortunately, her father had chosen a very different approach.

Shen Guowei had summoned her to his study.

Madam Liang was present.

So was Mr. Chen.

The family had reviewed the preliminary property schedule the previous evening. Several categories had been accepted, but the valuations and ownership evidence remained incomplete.

Mingzhao had expected another discussion about the documents.

Instead, her father began with a question.

"How much will it cost to establish your own household?"

Mingzhao looked at him.

"I have not completed the budget."

"Then how do you know whether you can afford to leave?"

"I know what my current assets and income are. I am examining the likely expenses."

"Your own household will require servants, maintenance, food, fuel, and security."

"Yes."

"And a suitable residence."

"Yes."

"These expenses will continue."

"Yes."

Shen Guowei leaned forward.

"Then perhaps you should reconsider."

Mingzhao looked at him without expression.

"Reconsider what?"

"Whether leaving is sensible."

Madam Liang spoke before her husband could continue.

"You have been raised in a noble household. You cannot simply take a few boxes and live as though you were an ordinary merchant."

Mingzhao turned toward her.

"I have not proposed living without a budget."

"Then you must understand the expense."

"I do."

Her father looked at her.

"You have not yet purchased a residence."

"Correct."

"You have not settled your commercial accounts."

"Correct."

"You have not completed the property division."

"Correct."

Shen Guowei spread his hands.

"Then why are you so determined to leave?"

Mingzhao regarded him.

"Because none of those matters requires me to continue managing the entire household."

Mr. Chen lowered his eyes.

Madam Liang's expression tightened.

Shen Guowei sighed.

"Your younger brother is still learning."

"Then he should continue learning."

"He cannot manage everything as you do."

"He does not need to manage everything as I do."

Mingzhao looked at the papers.

"He needs to manage what belongs to him."

Her father did not answer.

After a moment, he said, "We should discuss the financial arrangement."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That is what the property schedule is for."

"Your mother and I believe that a suitable allowance should be arranged."

Mingzhao's expression did not change.

"An allowance?"

"An annual sum to support your independent household."

"From which account?"

Shen Guowei hesitated.

"From the family estate."

"Would it be a gift, an advance against my share, or a temporary allowance?"

Her father looked irritated.

"Must you distinguish everything?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because an allowance can become a way of delaying the property division indefinitely."

Madam Liang spoke sharply.

"Your father is trying to help you."

"I did not refuse assistance."

"You are treating every offer as though it were an accusation."

Mingzhao turned to her.

"I am asking what the offer means."

The room fell quiet.

Mr. Chen finally spoke.

"Miss Mingzhao's question is reasonable. The financial arrangement should be recorded clearly."

Shen Guowei looked at him.

Mr. Chen continued.

"If the family provides a temporary allowance, the record should state whether it is a gift, a loan, or an advance against the eventual division."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That is acceptable."

Her father looked tired.

"Very well. We will examine it."

The meeting ended without an agreement.

But Mingzhao had not refused financial assistance.

She had refused to accept an undefined promise in place of a settlement.

There was a difference.

In the palace, the Emperor had not yet left Chengming Palace.

Yuru had asked Fang Ru to bring tea.

Zhao Jingheng looked at the small table.

"You have reduced the number of documents."

"Only those that no longer require my attention."

"How many remain?"

"Enough."

He looked at her.

"Do you intend to keep advising the palace after your resignation?"

"Not as an administrative obligation."

"What if I ask you personally?"

"That depends on the question."

The Emperor looked faintly offended.

"Must I submit a request?"

Yuru took a sip of tea.

"If you ask me to recommend a suitable book, no."

"And if I ask you to review a ceremonial arrangement?"

"That would depend on whether you are asking for my opinion or assigning me the work."

Zhao Jingheng stared at her.

She met his gaze calmly.

"Those are different things."

He sighed.

"Have I become so difficult?"

Yuru considered the question.

"Would you like an honest answer?"

"No."

"Then no."

The Emperor laughed despite himself.

It was a brief sound.

But it loosened something in the room.

After a moment, he said, "I have relied on you too much."

Yuru did not immediately answer.

He continued.

"When a problem reached the inner palace, I assumed you would solve it. When an office could not decide something, I expected you to find the answer. When I received a complaint, I sent it to you because you would know which person was responsible."

"Yes."

"I did not always consider how much work that created."

"No."

He looked at her.

"You could have told me."

Yuru set down her cup.

"I did tell you."

The Emperor stopped.

She continued without raising her voice.

"Not always in the form of a petition. Sometimes I said that the reports were increasing. Sometimes I said that the offices were avoiding responsibility. Sometimes I told you that I was tired."

Zhao Jingheng remembered.

He remembered several conversations in which he had agreed with her, promised to speak to the ministers, and then sent her another report the following morning.

He had not deliberately ignored her.

He had simply assumed that she would continue.

That assumption had been enough.

"I should have listened more carefully," he said.

Yuru looked at him.

"Yes."

There was no elaborate forgiveness.

No dramatic reconciliation.

The Emperor had acknowledged something that should have been obvious.

It was a beginning.

That afternoon, the Ministry of Personnel submitted the revised list of matters requiring an imperial decision.

The list was shorter than the previous report.

It contained three main categories.

First, the formal status of the Empress.

Second, ceremonial functions that depended upon the Empress's position.

Third, the arrangements required if the resignation were accepted.

The Emperor read the document.

He called Minister Han and the Minister of Rites.

They arrived together.

Zhao Jingheng pointed to the first category.

"Has the Empress's petition been properly examined?"

"Yes, Your Majesty," Minister Han replied.

"Are there any legal obstacles to accepting it?"

"None that cannot be addressed through an imperial order and the necessary ceremonial arrangements."

The Minister of Rites added, "The position itself is established by imperial authority. The consequences must be handled carefully, but the regulations do not prevent Your Majesty from accepting the resignation."

The Emperor looked at the second category.

"Which ceremonies require the Empress's personal participation?"

The Minister of Rites named the principal state ceremonies and several rites involving the imperial family.

"Can these be performed by another person?"

"Some may be performed by designated senior members of the imperial family. Others require adjustment to the ceremonial order."

"Then prepare the adjustments."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The Emperor turned to Minister Han.

"What happens to the administrative duties that have already been transferred?"

"They remain with their assigned offices."

"Good."

He closed the document.

The ministers exchanged a glance.

The Emperor had not yet accepted the resignation.

But the legal and administrative obstacles were becoming clearer.

For the first time, the question was no longer whether the Empress could be replaced in the daily work.

It was what the imperial household would become after she left.

At the Marquisate, Mingzhao received an unexpected visitor.

It was her younger brother.

Shen Mingyuan entered carrying a ledger.

"Second Sister," he said.

Mingzhao looked up.

"What is it?"

He placed the ledger on her desk.

"The central clerk found another entry concerning the southern residence."

She examined it.

The entry concerned a payment made for household repairs.

The amount was modest.

The description was incomplete.

"Who authorized it?"

"Steward Zhou."

"When?"

"During the previous month."

"Was the work completed?"

"Yes. The residence manager confirmed it."

"Is there a receipt?"

"There is a payment record, but the contractor's receipt is missing."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Then what did you do?"

"I asked the contractor for a copy."

"And?"

"He said he could provide one."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Good."

Mingyuan remained standing.

She looked up.

"Is there something else?"

He hesitated.

"I wanted to ask about the three hundred taels."

Mingzhao closed the ledger.

"What about it?"

"I have been reviewing my own records."

"That is useful."

"I may have spent some of the money on personal matters."

Mingzhao waited.

"I cannot remember the exact amount."

"Then do not invent one."

He looked at her.

"I thought you would be angry."

"I may be angry when the facts are established."

Mingyuan looked down.

Mingzhao continued.

"If you remember a purchase, record it. If you have no receipt, state that. If the expense was personal, identify it as personal. If it was household maintenance, provide the work details."

"What if I cannot remember?"

"Then say you cannot remember."

He looked at the ledger.

"You make it sound easy."

"It is not easy. It is merely more useful than pretending to know."

Mingyuan nodded slowly.

He had not expected her to make the process comfortable.

He had also not expected her to dismiss him.

"Thank you," he said.

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Do not thank me for asking you to complete your own records."

He gave a small, awkward smile.

Then he left.

Mingzhao reopened the ledger.

She had not forgiven him for every past grievance.

Nor had she decided that the financial matter was settled.

But he had taken another step toward responsibility.

She would not pretend otherwise.

Later, Mingzhao met Sun Qiming in the accounting room.

The accountant had completed a preliminary comparison of the old maintenance register.

He had found several payments made to the same contractor during the period when the northern warehouse repairs were conducted.

One payment corresponded to the transport of timber.

Another corresponded to the movement of grain.

A third had no clear description.

"Could this third payment concern the two sacks?" Mingzhao asked.

"It could. But the description is incomplete."

"Has the contractor been located?"

"Yes. He is still working in the capital."

"Can he be questioned?"

"He has agreed to meet us."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Arrange it."

Sun Qiming placed another paper before her.

"There is also a problem with the seventy-two taels."

"The study repairs?"

"Yes. Carpenter Chen's testimony supports the work, and the estate ledger supports payment. But the original work order has not been located."

"Could the maintenance office have a copy?"

"We have requested its records."

Mingzhao examined the report.

"And the remaining two hundred and twenty-eight?"

"No conclusive records."

"Continue."

Sun Qiming bowed.

The examination remained incomplete.

The family still had no basis for treating the full amount as a personal debt owed by Mingyuan.

Nor did Mingyuan's inability to remember the expenditure make the amount disappear.

The records would have to be completed.

In the evening, Lin Shuyun's reply arrived.

Zhou Wenqi had accepted the revised silk contract.

The visible defects would be addressed during inspection.

Concealed defects would be subject to a limited claim period, provided the buyer supplied evidence and notified the seller promptly.

Transportation damage would be handled according to the carrier's responsibility.

The buyer had agreed to the payment arrangements.

However, the deposit had not yet been transferred.

Mingzhao read the letter.

Then she sent a reply approving the final draft for signature, provided the deposit was received before the stock was reserved.

The sale was close.

It was not complete.

She closed the correspondence.

Qiao Yue brought the evening tea.

"Will the partnership receive the money soon?"

"If the buyer signs and pays the deposit, the stock can be reserved. The balance will be paid upon delivery."

"So not yet."

"Not yet."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao glanced at her.

"You sound disappointed."

"I thought the household might finally receive some good news."

"The household is not entitled to spend money that has not arrived."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Even good news must wait for a receipt."

"Especially good news."

The Emperor spent the final hours of the day reviewing the ceremonial report.

The Department of Rites had identified the ceremonies that required adjustment.

The Empress's administrative responsibilities were being transferred.

The Crown Prince's household was learning to manage its own records.

The Imperial Household Administration had begun operating without referring every routine matter to Chengming Palace.

The Dowager had agreed that the transition should proceed.

There were still questions.

There would be more.

But the Emperor now understood that delaying the decision would not preserve the old arrangement.

It would only leave Yuru waiting.

He put down the report.

Gao Shun approached.

"Your Majesty?"

"Prepare a private audience with the Empress tomorrow."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

The Emperor looked at the petition.

It had been eleven days since she submitted it.

He had asked whether she was ill.

He had asked whether she was offended.

He had asked whether the petition could wait.

He had asked whether the palace could function without her.

He had not asked what it meant to her to continue living in a position she no longer wished to hold.

Tomorrow, he would have to answer her request.

Not with another timetable.

Not with another committee.

Not with a new register.

With a decision.

At Chengming Palace, Yuru was preparing to retire when Fang Ru brought the day's final report.

"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites has completed its preliminary ceremonial assessment."

Yuru read it.

The report was concise.

Several duties would require adjustment if the resignation were accepted.

Others could be transferred to designated members of the imperial family.

The administrative transition could continue regardless of the final decision.

Yuru closed the document.

"Send it to His Majesty."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

Fang Ru hesitated.

"Your Majesty, His Majesty has requested a private audience tomorrow."

Yuru nodded.

"Very well."

"Do you expect a decision?"

Yuru looked at the report.

"I expect a conversation."

Fang Ru lowered her eyes.

Yuru stood and walked toward the window.

The palace gardens were quiet.

For many years, she had passed through them while thinking about ceremonies, household disputes, expenditure, appointments, and the next day's obligations.

Tomorrow, she might still be Empress.

Or she might not.

Either way, the palace would have to continue.

And for the first time in a long while, she intended to let it.

**End of Chapter 19.**
