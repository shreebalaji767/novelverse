# **Chapter 71: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Visit Her Parents and Has Not Been Asked to Bring a List of Palace Expenses. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Explain an Old Payment and Has Requested the Original Receipt. The Workshop Has Discovered That a Buyer Can Accept Silk Without Accepting Every Excuse Made by Its Manufacturer.**

**5th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



Xu Yuru received her mother's letter shortly after breakfast.



The letter was short.



Madam Xu asked whether Yuru had been sleeping comfortably, whether the weather in the western residential section was agreeable, and whether she wished to visit the family residence sometime during the coming days.



There was no mention of the palace.



There was no request to inspect a household account.



There was, however, a complaint about Duke Xu.



According to Madam Xu, he had recently purchased a large quantity of pears despite being told that the price had risen.



He had then declared that the seller must have made a mistake.



Madam Xu had informed him that the seller had not made a mistake.



The seller had simply discovered that Duke Xu liked pears.



Yuru read that portion twice.



Fang Ru stood beside the table.



“Is Madam Xu well?”



“Yes.”



“Does she wish you to visit?”



“She has asked whether I would like to.”



“Would you?”



Yuru folded the letter.



“Yes. Perhaps in a few days.”



Fang Ru nodded.



“Shall I prepare a reply?”



Yuru considered the wording.



“Tell Mother I would be happy to visit. Ask whether she would prefer lunch or an afternoon meal.”



Fang Ru picked up the writing paper.



“And the pears?”



Yuru looked at her.



“Tell her Father should be permitted to buy them if he wishes. Mother can decide how many he is allowed to bring home.”



Fang Ru smiled as she wrote.



Yuru continued:



“Do not suggest that I will help him negotiate with the seller.”



Fang Ru's brush paused.



“Would Duke Xu ask you to?”



“He might.”



“Then perhaps we should warn him.”



“No. Mother can manage Father.”



Fang Ru finished the letter.



Yuru signed it.



The message was sent.



The matter of the pears remained with the Xu family, where it belonged.

At the Imperial Household Administration, Madam Zhao received the first monthly summary prepared under the revised record format.



The report was not especially impressive.



It contained several confirmed figures, a list of unresolved entries, and three references to supporting documents that had not yet been located.



Madam Zhao read it carefully.



The clerk responsible for the summary stood before her.



“Have you checked the original purchase records?”



“Yes.”



“And the issue register?”



“Yes.”



“Are the remaining quantities confirmed?”



“Only where the opening stock and all recorded issues can be reconciled.”



Madam Zhao turned to the last page.



“What about the entries without a recorded unit?”



“They remain separate. We have not converted them.”



“Good.”



The clerk waited.



Madam Zhao looked up.



“Why are you still standing there?”



“I thought you might wish to make corrections.”



“I have made corrections.”



The clerk glanced at the report.



He had expected more.



Madam Zhao closed the file.



“Submit it.”



He bowed.



After he left, a junior administrator approached.



“Madam, should we send a copy to the former Empress for her review?”



Madam Zhao looked at him.



“For what purpose?”



“To ensure that the report is properly prepared.”



“Has the former Empress been appointed to review these accounts?”



“No.”



“Then why should we send it?”



The junior administrator hesitated.



“I thought it might be reassuring.”



Madam Zhao returned the report to its folder.



“An official report should be reviewed by the officials responsible for it.”



“Yes, Madam.”



“Do you believe they cannot do so?”



“No.”



“Then do not send it.”



The junior administrator bowed and withdrew.



Madam Zhao looked at the closed folder.



It was not that she believed the new arrangements were perfect.



They were not.



But sending every uncertain matter to the former Empress would merely recreate the old system under a different name.



The clerks had to learn to take responsibility for their own work.



She returned to the next file.

In the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Mingyuan had begun examining the sixth cash book with greater confidence.



He had already learned that a payment authorization was not a receipt, that a receipt was not necessarily proof of final delivery, and that a person named in a register was not automatically the person who benefited from the expenditure.



These distinctions had made the work slower.



They had also prevented several incorrect classifications.



Steward Zhou placed a new supporting bundle before him.



“This contains the papers for the household maintenance entries.”



Mingyuan opened it.



The first document concerned the eastern study repairs.



The second concerned the southern residence.



The third concerned a payment to a carpenter whose name appeared in another book.



Mingyuan examined the papers one by one.



Then he stopped.



“This receipt is incomplete.”



Steward Zhou leaned forward.



“What is missing?”



“The recipient's acknowledgment.”



“The amount and date are recorded.”



“But the person who received the money has not signed.”



Steward Zhou examined the page.



“That is correct.”



“Can we find the carpenter?”



“He may still be working in Yongjing.”



“Then ask him whether he received the payment.”



Steward Zhou nodded.



Mingyuan continued reading.



Another entry had been marked as an advance to Mingzhao.



The description was brief:



*Funds issued under the eldest daughter's instruction.*



Mingyuan looked at it.



“Where is the original supporting paper?”



“This is the only copy presently attached.”



“Does it identify the purpose?”



“No.”



“Does it identify the recipient?”



“No.”



“Then why is it classified as her advance?”



Steward Zhou considered the question.



“It may have been entered under her account because she authorized the payment.”



Mingyuan looked at him.



“That is not enough.”



“No, Young Master.”



Mingyuan placed the entry in the uncertain group.



“Do not include it among the nine confirmed advances or the four payment-confirmed advances until the original record is found.”



Steward Zhou bowed.



“I will amend the working schedule.”



Mingyuan looked at the growing pile of papers.



He had once believed his sister's strictness was simply a matter of temperament.



Now he was beginning to see that the old records had given her ample reason to be strict.



That did not make every decision she had made correct.



It did make the family's assumptions less defensible.

Mingzhao received a message from Sun Qiming before midday.



The accountant had found a reference to the incomplete payment entry in a separate household expenditure register.



The new record did not establish the recipient.



However, it suggested that the payment might have been intended for a household purchase rather than a personal advance.



Sun had not changed the classification.



He had requested the original supporting paper.



Mingzhao read the report.



Qiao Yue brought tea.



“Have they found something?”



“A reference.”



“Is it useful?”



“It narrows the question.”



“Does it prove the payment was not yours?”



“No.”



Qiao Yue sighed.



“Does anything ever prove anything?”



Mingzhao looked at her.



“Evidence proves what it supports. It does not prove everything else.”



Qiao Yue considered this.



“You should teach the family how to speak.”



“They would probably refuse to attend.”



Mingzhao put down the report.



She was not going to argue against an entry being examined merely because it might weaken one of her claims.



If the payment had been for an estate purchase, it should be classified as such.



If it had been a personal advance, the evidence should establish that.



She wrote a short reply to Sun.



*Continue the search for the original supporting paper. Do not alter the classification based on the secondary reference alone. If the payment proves to be an estate purchase, remove it from my personal claims.*



She signed the letter.



Qiao Yue sealed it.



“Your father may be relieved to hear that.”



“Then he should be relieved by the evidence, not by my willingness to abandon a claim.”



Qiao Yue looked at her.



“You really do not make things easy.”



“I am not trying to.”

At the workshop, Lin Shuyun had received a letter from buyer Zhou Wenqi.



The letter concerned the next inspection of the 200-bolt order.



Zhou Wenqi would send his representative, Zhou Jian, on the following day to inspect the next group of completed bolts.



Lin read the message twice.



“Forty-eight bolts.”



Mingzhao looked up.



“Has he specified the inspection time?”



“Tomorrow morning.”



“Then prepare the completed groups and keep the internal inspection records beside them.”



Lin nodded.



The workshop had forty-eight bolts already accepted by the buyer.



Another forty-eight had passed internal inspection.



The remaining one hundred and four still needed to be produced.



Lin examined the latest production sheet.



“We should have the next group ready before he arrives.”



“Do not rush it.”



“If we finish today, we can have the full group ready.”



“If you finish today.”



Lin looked at her.



Mingzhao returned to the ledger.



The distinction was irritating.



It was also correct.

The senior finisher approached Lin shortly afterward.



He had been less pleased with the inspection rules since his trial group had failed the reduced-check condition.



He was not an incompetent worker.



He had spent many years producing high-quality silk.



He simply disliked being told that his judgment was no longer sufficient on its own.



“Manager Lin,” he said, “I have a suggestion.”



Lin looked up.



“Go ahead.”



“The revised folding method is slower when we follow every measurement exactly. We could make a small adjustment to the edge tension. It would save time.”



Mingzhao, who was checking the day's production tally, raised her head.



“Has the buyer approved that adjustment?”



The worker paused.



“No.”



“Then it is not a production method we can use.”



“It is a minor change.”



“Is it within the specification?”



“I believe so.”



“Have you measured it?”



The worker hesitated.



Mingzhao stood.



“Bring the sample.”



The worker returned with a finished bolt.



Mingzhao compared it with the approved sample.



The difference was small.



It was also visible.



She measured the edge tension.



The result was outside the agreed range.



“This cannot be used.”



The worker frowned.



“It is only slightly outside.”



“The buyer has approved a specific range.”



“The silk itself is not damaged.”



“That is not the question.”



The worker looked toward Lin.



Lin took the sample.



“Could it be corrected?”



“Yes,” the worker replied.



“Then correct it.”



He accepted the bolt and returned to the table.



Lin looked at Mingzhao.



“He is trying to improve the process.”



“Then he should submit a proposed change before using it.”



“You could allow a trial.”



“A trial can be made on a separate sample. It cannot be mixed into the buyer's order.”



Lin considered the distinction.



“That is fair.”



Mingzhao returned to the production sheet.



The workshop could experiment.



It could test faster methods.



It could improve its own procedures.



But the buyer's order was not the place to introduce unapproved variations.

By late afternoon, the next group of bolts was ready for internal inspection.



The group contained one hundred and four bolts in total across the completed and newly finished production stages.



The supervisor checked the first section.



The measurements were consistent.



The trainee's work was slower but improving.



The senior finisher's corrected method remained within the specification.



One bolt required a minor adjustment.



The correction was completed without material loss.



The supervisor recorded the result.



Lin examined the sheet.



“Forty-eight accepted by the buyer. The next forty-eight are ready for inspection. The remaining one hundred and four are still to be produced.”



Mingzhao checked the arithmetic.



“Correct.”



Lin looked toward the finished silk.



“Tomorrow's inspection should go well.”



“It should.”



“You could sound a little more hopeful.”



“I am hopeful.”



“You do not look hopeful.”



“I am looking at the inspection sheet.”



Lin laughed.



The workshop prepared the bolts for the following morning.



The buyer's representative would arrive with the agreed sample and inspection record.



The workshop would present the completed goods.



There was no reason to assume the inspection would fail.



There was also no reason to record the goods as accepted before the buyer had examined them.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received the report that Mingzhao had agreed to clarify specific old payments but refused to resume the purchasing work.



He read it in silence.



Steward Zhou stood nearby.



“My lord, the new clerk has begun taking over the consolidated purchasing register.”



“Has he received the previous records?”



“Yes.”



“Has he been instructed about the unresolved entries?”



“Yes. He has been told not to combine unconfirmed amounts with established expenditures.”



The Marquis nodded.



“Have him report to Mingyuan.”



“Yes, my lord.”



Steward Zhou hesitated.



“There is one more matter.”



“What is it?”



“The old household purchasing papers are not all in the same archive. Some are kept with the warehouse records, others with the household accounts.”



“Then arrange an index.”



Steward Zhou bowed.



The Marquis looked at the papers on his desk.



For years, the family had depended on Mingzhao's memory and personal attention to connect records that were stored in different places.



Now that she had left, they were discovering how much of the estate's organization had depended on one person's knowledge.



He had not understood the extent of it.



He had also not asked.



The Marquis looked toward the courtyard.



“Make sure the new clerk is not given more work than he can manage.”



Steward Zhou bowed again.



“Yes, my lord.”

In the palace, Yuru received a reply from her mother.



Madam Xu preferred an afternoon meal in three days.



The letter also mentioned that Duke Xu had purchased fewer pears than he had originally intended.



He had not been forced to do so.



He had simply been informed that the seller's next delivery would not arrive for several days.



Madam Xu had taken advantage of the delay.



Yuru read the letter and smiled.



Fang Ru noticed.



“Has Madam Xu agreed to the visit?”



“Yes. Three days from now.”



“Shall I prepare your clothes?”



“Not yet. There is time.”



Fang Ru placed the letter aside.



Yuru looked toward the window.



She had been invited to visit her parents.



She had not been asked to inspect their accounts.



She had not been asked to resolve the matter of the pears.



She had not been asked to return to the palace and manage its stores.



It was a family invitation.



She accepted it as one.

The following morning, Zhou Jian arrived at the silk workshop.



He greeted Lin Shuyun and Mingzhao, then examined the production records.



The first forty-eight bolts had already been accepted.



The next forty-eight were presented for inspection.



Zhou Jian compared the samples, checked the edge measurements, and examined the folding.



He stopped at one bolt.



The supervisor stepped forward.



“This one required a correction during internal inspection.”



Zhou Jian examined the corrected edge.



“Was the material damaged?”



“No.”



“Was the correction recorded?”



“Yes.”



He checked the entry.



Then he examined the remaining bolts.



The group passed.



Zhou Jian signed the acceptance sheet.



Lin let out a breath.



Mingzhao checked the signature and date.



The buyer had accepted another forty-eight bolts.



That brought the buyer-accepted quantity to ninety-six.



The next production group was not yet complete.



One hundred and four bolts still remained to be produced.



Zhou Jian placed the signed sheet on the table.



“The next inspection will depend on your schedule.”



Lin nodded.



“We will notify you when the next group is ready.”



Zhou Jian gathered his papers.



“Do that.”



After he left, Lin looked at Mingzhao.



“Ninety-six.”



“Yes.”



“Almost halfway.”



“Almost.”



Lin shook her head.



“You cannot even let me enjoy the number.”



“You may enjoy it.”



“Thank you.”



Mingzhao returned to the ledger.



The buyer's acceptance was good news.



The order was progressing.



But the workshop still had wages, materials, training expenses, supervision costs, and transport to account for.



A signed acceptance sheet was not the same as a completed contract.



And a completed contract was not necessarily the same as a profitable one.



Lin watched her for a moment.



Then she laughed.



“I suppose we should be grateful that at least the buyer has signed something.”



“That is a reasonable conclusion.”

That evening, Xu Yuru walked once more along the path beside the old pavilion.



The weather was warm, but the shade made the walk comfortable.



Fang Ru accompanied her.



They passed the gardener, who bowed and returned to his work.



Yuru did not stop.



At the pavilion, she sat for a short while.



She thought about her mother's letter and the visit planned for three days later.



She thought about the Third Princess's catalogue and the Crown Prince's accounts.



She thought about the officials who were learning to do their own work.



Then she looked at Fang Ru.



“Do you think the path will be crowded tomorrow?”



“I can ask the attendants.”



“No need. We can find out when we arrive.”



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru stood.



They returned to the guest residence at an unhurried pace.



There was no official waiting for her.



There was no report requiring her signature.



And, for once, she had no intention of looking for one.
