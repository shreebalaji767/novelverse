# **Chapter 73: The Former Empress Has Received a Reminder About Her Visit Home and Has Not Been Given an Itinerary. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked Whether She Can Identify an Old Seal and Has Requested the Seal Register. The Workshop Has Discovered That Twenty-Three Accepted Inspections Are Not the Same as Twenty-Four Completed Bolts.**

**7th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress Xu Yuru received a message from her mother shortly after breakfast.



It was not a letter.



It was a small note delivered by a household servant who had come to confirm the arrangements for the following afternoon.



Yuru read it while Fang Ru stood nearby.



*Your father has agreed not to arrange a formal reception. The meal will be served in the eastern courtyard. Come whenever convenient. Do not bring anything unnecessary.*



Yuru read the last sentence twice.



"That is reassuring," Fang Ru said.



"Yes."



"Your mother has specifically mentioned unnecessary things."



"She knows my father."



Fang Ru smiled.



Yuru folded the note.



"Then we should take only the tea."



"I have already prepared it."



"Good."



There was a pause.



Fang Ru looked at the correspondence box.



"Would you like to write a reply?"



"No. The messenger is still here?"



"He is waiting outside."



Yuru considered the matter.



"Tell him I received the message and will come tomorrow afternoon."



Fang Ru bowed and went to deliver the reply.



Yuru returned to her tea.



The visit had been arranged with a simplicity that would have seemed almost impossible several years earlier.



There was no formal carriage procession.



No ceremonial reception.



No schedule requiring her to arrive at an exact hour.



She would go to her parents' home, eat with them, and perhaps sit in the courtyard afterward.



She did not need to bring a report.



She did not need to return with an answer.



The thought was pleasant.



It was also slightly unfamiliar.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingyuan had received another document from the accounts office.



This one concerned the old payment entry that had been authorized under Mingzhao's name.



The clerk had located the relevant seal impression in a secondary register.



The impression was clear enough to establish that the payment voucher had been processed through the estate accounts office.



It did not identify the recipient.



Mingyuan placed the page beside the copied entry.



Steward Zhou examined it.



"The seal is consistent with the one used during that period," he said.



"Can we identify whose seal it was?"



"The accounts office believes it was the estate purchasing seal."



"Not my sister's personal seal?"



"No. The impression is not the same."



Mingyuan looked at the page again.



The distinction mattered.



A payment could have been authorized by Mingzhao and processed through an estate office without being paid to her personally.



It could also have been a payment made to a supplier under her instruction.



The existing evidence did not establish which.



"Can we obtain the seal register?"



Steward Zhou nodded.



"The seal register is kept separately. It records the custody of the principal estate seals and the periods during which they were assigned."



"Then request the relevant pages."



Mingyuan looked at the copied voucher entry.



"Do we know who held the purchasing seal at the time?"



"Not yet."



"Then we should not assume that the person who authorized the payment was the person who processed it."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"That is correct."



Mingyuan wrote the request.



He had once considered such distinctions unnecessary.



If a payment had been made under his sister's instruction, he had assumed she would know where it went.



Now he understood that the authorization, the handling of the money, and the receipt of the goods or funds were separate events.



The distinction was inconvenient.



It was also the reason the family had failed to understand its own accounts.



At the other table, Liang Wen was copying the purchasing register.



He had reached the section concerning household furnishings.



"Steward Zhou," he said, "there are two entries for the same delivery."



Steward Zhou approached.



"Read them."



"The first says that twelve chairs were ordered. The second says that twelve chairs were delivered. The dates are three days apart."



"That is normal."



"But the second entry does not contain a payment reference."



"Then record the delivery and leave the payment reference blank."



Liang Wen hesitated.



"Could it be the same transaction?"



"It probably is."



"Should I connect them?"



"Write that they appear to concern the same order, pending confirmation from the original voucher."



Liang Wen looked at the register.



"Another note."



"Yes."



He lowered his brush.



The new register was becoming rather full of notes.



Mingyuan glanced over.



"Is there a problem?"



Liang Wen looked embarrassed.



"I thought the consolidated register would make the purchasing records easier to understand."



"It will."



"But it seems to be showing more questions than answers."



Mingyuan looked at the copied pages.



"That may be because the old records contained more questions than answers."



Steward Zhou coughed into his sleeve.



Liang Wen returned to his work.



Mingyuan turned back to the cash books.



He was beginning to see that an account system did not become reliable simply because someone wrote the same figures into a cleaner ledger.



It became reliable when the new ledger preserved the difference between what was known and what was merely assumed.

Shen Mingzhao received a separate message that afternoon.



It came from the Marquisate through the usual household correspondence channel.



Her brother had sent a copy of the seal impression and a short explanation.



He had also asked whether she remembered which officials had held the purchasing seal during the relevant period.



Mingzhao read the message at her writing table.



Qiao Yue was arranging the papers from the morning.



"Another question?" she asked.



"Yes."



"About the same payment?"



"Yes."



"Have they found the recipient?"



"No."



"Then the question remains."



Mingzhao examined the seal impression.



She recognized it as the estate purchasing seal.



She also remembered that the seal had been held by the accounts office rather than by her personally.



However, the custody of the seal had changed during her years managing the estate.



She could not identify the exact holder on the date in question without seeing the register.



She placed the page down.



"Prepare a reply."



Qiao Yue brought a clean sheet.



Mingzhao dictated slowly.



"I recognize the seal impression as the estate purchasing seal. I did not personally hold that seal. Its custody was maintained by the accounts office. I cannot identify the custodian on the date shown without examining the seal register."



She paused.



"Add that the seal impression confirms processing through the estate office, but does not establish the recipient or purpose of the payment."



Qiao Yue wrote it down.



"Should we mention that the original voucher is still missing?"



"Yes. Ask them to send the relevant seal-register entry once it is found."



Qiao Yue finished the letter.



"That is all?"



"That is all."



Qiao Yue looked at the page.



"Your brother is asking questions more carefully."



"He is."



"Do you approve?"



Mingzhao considered the matter.



"I approve of the questions. I have not yet seen the answers."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"That is fair."



Mingzhao sealed the reply.



She did not feel particularly pleased.



There was no satisfaction in discovering that the family accounts were more complicated than she had thought. Every unresolved entry represented another matter that would require time and evidence.



But there was a difference between answering a question and accepting responsibility for the entire investigation.



She had agreed to the former.



She would not quietly return to the latter.

At the silk workshop, Lin Shuyun was reviewing the production sheet when Mingzhao arrived.



The corrected bolt from the previous day's inspection had passed its second examination.



It had been returned to the finished group.



The twenty-four bolts were now complete.



"All twenty-four?" Mingzhao asked.



"All twenty-four."



"And the internal inspection?"



"Twenty-three passed yesterday. The corrected one passed this morning."



Lin Shuyun showed her the record.



The original defect had been noted.



The correction had been described.



The second inspection had been signed by a different worker.



Mingzhao examined the entry.



"Good."



Lin Shuyun put the sheet aside.



"The next group is almost ready. The workers are keeping to the approved folding method."



"Any changes?"



"One of the senior finishers suggested moving the inspection table closer to the window. He thinks the light will make it easier to examine the edges."



"That is not a change to the product specification."



"No. It is only a change to the working arrangement."



"Then test it."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"I thought you would say that."



They walked toward the window.



The table had been moved slightly.



The natural light was better, but the position left less room for workers to pass behind it.



A young worker was carrying a bundle of finished cloth when he had to turn sideways to avoid the table.



Mingzhao watched him pass.



"The light is better," she said.



"Yes."



"But the passage is narrower."



"We can move the table another half-step."



"Then test it before deciding."



Lin Shuyun looked at her.



"You have become very cautious about half-steps."



"Half-steps can become full obstructions."



The worker carrying the bundle reached the other end of the room without difficulty.



Lin Shuyun considered the arrangement.



"Let us move it slightly and observe the traffic for the rest of the morning."



"Good."



They returned to the ledger.



"The next buyer inspection has not been scheduled," Lin Shuyun said. "Zhou Jian has not sent a date."



"Has the buyer been informed that the next group is being prepared?"



"Yes. We sent the production update yesterday."



"Then there is no need to send another message today."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



Mingzhao examined the production figures.



The buyer had accepted ninety-six bolts.



The next twenty-four had now passed internal inspection.



Another group was being prepared.



The remaining work was substantial, but the schedule remained manageable.



"Have the trainees received their wage records?" Mingzhao asked.



"Yes. Both have signed."



"Any dispute?"



"One asked why the training hours were listed separately from ordinary production hours."



"What did you tell him?"



"That training time is still paid time, but it is not recorded as completed production."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Correct."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"He seemed satisfied."



"Good. A worker should not have to guess how his wages are calculated."



The workshop continued around them.



A finisher checked an edge against the approved sample.



Another worker folded the next length.



The trainee who had once worked too quickly now examined each bolt before placing it in the completed group.



The careful trainee had become faster without abandoning his method.



There was still room for improvement.



There was also evidence that the training arrangements were working.



Mingzhao left the workshop with the production record in her possession.



She did not take the workshop ledger.



It belonged there.

In the palace, Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an was examining a household expenditure summary.



He had already separated the confirmed entries from the estimates.



The remaining problem concerned a group of purchases for the Eastern Palace.



The record listed several containers of lamp oil, but the descriptions used different units.



Some were recorded by jar.



Some by weight.



One entry simply stated that the oil had been received.



Cheng'an read the page again.



"Who prepared this summary?"



A clerk stepped forward.



"The household office, Your Highness."



"Does the original record specify the capacity of these jars?"



"For some of them."



"And the others?"



"The capacity is not recorded."



"Then do not convert them into a common total."



The clerk bowed.



"Should they remain separate?"



"Yes. Preserve the original descriptions. Put the entries with known capacity in one group and the others in another."



The clerk hesitated.



"That will leave the total incomplete."



"The total is incomplete."



The clerk lowered his head.



Cheng'an looked at the summary.



He had begun to understand that the household's old method of reporting expenditure had often valued a finished total more than a reliable one.



The former Empress had known this.



She had spent years correcting the consequences.



Now he was learning how much work that correction required.



He set the document aside.



"Send the revised summary to the household office. Do not ask the former Empress to review it."



The clerk bowed.



"Yes, Your Highness."



Cheng'an returned to his work.



The decision was ordinary.



It was also important.



He was not going to ask Yuru to solve a problem simply because she had once been better at solving it.

That evening, Yuru received a short note from the Dowager Empress.



It contained no request.



The Dowager had written that she hoped the weather would remain pleasant for Yuru's visit to her parents.



Yuru smiled.



She wrote a brief reply thanking the Dowager for her kind thought.



Then she set the letter aside.



Fang Ru entered with the small tea parcel prepared for the next afternoon.



"Everything is ready, Madam."



"Thank you."



"Would you like me to arrange the carriage for tomorrow?"



"Yes. We will leave after the midday meal."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Should I ask the driver to wait outside the eastern courtyard?"



"No. He can return to the palace and come back when we send word."



"That may be inconvenient."



"It is not necessary for him to wait for several hours."



Fang Ru considered the instruction.



"Very well."



Yuru looked toward the courtyard.



The evening light had softened.



She had spent the day walking, reading letters, and deciding what to take to her parents' home.



It was not a particularly impressive list of accomplishments.



But it was her own day.



She was beginning to understand that a life did not need to be filled with responsibilities to be meaningful.



She also understood that she might still miss parts of the old life.



She missed the certainty of a completed task.



She missed knowing that a difficult matter had been settled.



She did not miss being expected to settle every difficult matter that crossed her path.



Those feelings could exist together.



She did not need to choose one and deny the other.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received the reply from Mingzhao before the evening meal.



He read it carefully.



The seal impression had been identified.



The custodian had not.



The recipient had not.



The purpose had not.



He placed the reply beside the request for the seal register.



Steward Zhou looked at the page.



"Your elder sister has answered."



"Yes."



"She has not identified the person who held the seal."



"She said she could not do so without the register."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"That is correct."



Mingyuan looked at the old cash book.



He had once expected his sister to provide answers from memory.



Now he understood that the records needed to provide answers that could be checked.



He turned to the clerk who had delivered the message.



"Send another request to the accounts office. Ask for the relevant seal-register pages and the original voucher. Do not send a new question to my sister until we have them."



The clerk bowed.



Mingyuan returned to the accounts table.



At the other end of the room, Liang Wen was finishing the day's entries in the purchasing register.



He had recorded the transactions that could be matched.



He had left the others marked for examination.



The ledger remained unfinished.



For once, no one attempted to hide that fact.

By the end of the seventh day of the sixth month, the palace had made another small step toward managing its own records.



The Marquisate had identified the seal used in an unclear payment, but not the person who had held it or the person who had received the money.



The workshop had completed another group of bolts and continued its internal inspections.



The buyer had not yet arranged the next visit.



And the former Empress had prepared to spend the following afternoon with her parents.



The day had produced no grand settlement.



It had, however, produced several useful distinctions:



A seal was not a recipient.



An authorization was not proof of receipt.



A clean register was not proof that the old records had been clean.



A completed inspection was not the same as a completed delivery.



And an invitation to eat with one's parents was not an official appointment.



Yuru intended to remember the last distinction most carefully.



She had already decided that if her father tried to turn the afternoon into a reception, she would ask her mother to serve the meal before he could finish arranging the chairs.
