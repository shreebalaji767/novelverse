# **Chapter 43: The Former Empress Has Received an Invitation to a Family Dinner. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received an Invitation to Another Meeting. One Invitation Contains Food. The Other Contains Eleven Documents.**

**5th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The invitation from the Emperor arrived shortly after breakfast.



It was written on ordinary paper.



There was no official seal, no ceremonial wording, and no list of matters requiring discussion.



It contained only a request that Xu Yuru join the imperial family for dinner that evening.



Yuru read it twice.



Fang Ru, who was arranging the books on the shelf, glanced towards her.



"His Majesty has invited you?"



"Yes."



"Will you attend?"



"I have not decided."



Fang Ru placed a book on the shelf.



"Is there a reason not to?"



"No."



"Then why have you not decided?"



Yuru folded the invitation.



"Because I am considering whether dinner will remain dinner."



Fang Ru understood.



The Emperor had become better at not sending documents to her residence, but he had not entirely abandoned the habit of asking questions whenever she was present.



"Perhaps His Majesty intends to eat without discussing palace administration."



"Perhaps."



"That would be an improvement."



Yuru smiled.



She wrote a short acceptance.



Then she returned to her book.



Fang Ru watched her for a moment.



"Madam, may I ask something?"



"Yes."



"Have you decided whether I will continue accompanying you when you leave the temporary palace residence?"



Yuru looked up.



The question had been expected eventually.



Fang Ru had served her for many years. She was experienced, capable, and accustomed to the routines of the inner palace. She also had her own family responsibilities and a position that would not necessarily follow Yuru into a different household.



"I would like you to consider what you want," Yuru said.



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



"I have considered it."



"And?"



"I would prefer to remain with you for now. But I do not wish to make a permanent decision before you choose where you will live."



Yuru nodded.



"That is reasonable."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"If you move to the Duke's residence, I may accompany you. If you establish another household, I would like to consider the arrangements then."



"Then we shall decide when the time comes."



Fang Ru bowed.



There was no need to settle every future arrangement before the future had arrived.



Yuru returned to her book.



For once, she did not add the matter to a list of duties.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao's invitation arrived in a much larger envelope.



It was from Mr Liang.



The next family settlement meeting had not yet been scheduled because Sun Qiming's review remained incomplete. However, Mr Liang wanted a smaller discussion concerning the classification of several agricultural properties and the treatment of maintenance expenses.



The letter contained a proposed agenda.



Mingzhao read it.



Qiao Yue leaned over her shoulder.



"How many documents?"



"Eleven."



"Eleven?"



"Some are copies."



"That does not make them fewer."



Mingzhao put the letter down.



The agenda included:

- Confirmed landholdings.
- Leased agricultural properties.
- Fallow land requiring restoration.
- Buildings requiring maintenance.
- Properties with incomplete registration records.
- The northern warehouse structure.
- Grain stock and unresolved inventory.
- Maintenance expenses paid from estate funds.
- Maintenance expenses paid personally.
- Disputed boundary records.
- Outstanding property valuations.

Mingzhao read the final item again.



"At least they have separated the warehouse building from the grain stock."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Was that not already clear?"



"It was clear to the accountant. It was not clear to everyone attending the previous meeting."



"Then perhaps the next meeting will be easier."



"It may be."



"Will you attend?"



"Yes."



Qiao Yue looked relieved.



"Good."



"But I will not review the household's ordinary expenditure."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"I did not ask."



"You were about to."



"I was about to ask whether you wanted tea."



Mingzhao glanced at her.



"Then ask."



"Would you like tea?"



"Yes."



Qiao Yue poured it.



Mingzhao returned to the agenda.



She did not object to the meeting.



The family had finally begun preparing documents that separated the different kinds of property and expenditure.



That was useful.



But she would not allow the meeting to become another occasion for everyone to ask her to reconstruct the entire estate's financial history.



She wrote a reply accepting the proposed discussion, subject to receiving the supporting documents in advance.



She also requested that Sun Qiming attend if the meeting concerned figures from his review.



The reply was sent before midday.

At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, the Emperor had received a request from the Department of Rites.



The request concerned the classification of several ceremonial objects that had been stored in the inner palace for years.



Some were used during annual ceremonies.



Some were kept as replacements.



Others had not been used in recent memory.



The Department of Rites wanted to know whether the objects should remain under its inventory or be transferred to the Palace Stores.



Zhao Jingheng read the list.



"Why is this being sent to me?"



Gao Shun answered carefully.



"The Department of Rites believes the transfer requires imperial authorization."



"Does the existing regulation specify who holds the objects?"



"Not clearly."



"Then why has no one checked the original purchase records?"



"The department has requested them from the Palace Stores."



"Have they been found?"



"Some have."



The Emperor placed the list down.



"Then let them finish the comparison."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor looked at the papers again.



"How many objects?"



"Thirty-two."



"How many have been identified?"



"Twenty-seven."



"And the remaining five?"



"Their descriptions are incomplete."



Zhao Jingheng looked towards the ceiling.



He had once believed that the palace's old systems were orderly because they had existed for generations.



Now he was discovering that age and order were not the same thing.



"Tell the Department of Rites to identify the five remaining objects before requesting a transfer."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And do not send the matter to the former Empress."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor picked up another document.



He was beginning to say that sentence less often.



That was another improvement.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received a visit from Lin Shuyun.



Lin arrived carrying a small ledger and a packet of samples.



She placed both on the table.



"The buyer has agreed to inspect the two corrected bolts together with the next group."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Has he confirmed the date?"



"Six days from now."



"Does that give you enough time?"



"It gives the finishing workshop enough time. I have asked them not to rush the dyeing."



Lin opened the ledger.



"The forty previously accepted bolts have been entered. The payment has been received into the partnership account."



"Have you reconciled the transport costs?"



"Partly."



"Production costs?"



"Not yet. The workshop has not sent its final invoice."



Mingzhao examined the figures.



Lin pointed to a separate entry.



"This is the additional finishing charge for the two corrected bolts."



"Has the workshop explained it?"



"Yes. One bolt required another dyeing treatment. The other needed a minor correction along the edge."



"Are those charges included in the original contract?"



"No. The workshop says they resulted from the first finishing failure."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"And do you agree?"



"Not entirely."



Lin's expression was calm.



"The workshop is responsible for the original defect. But the additional work was performed at our request after the buyer's inspection was delayed. I am willing to pay a reasonable amount for the extra work, but not the full charge."



Mingzhao studied the entry.



"Have you negotiated?"



"I have asked for a reduced charge. They have not answered."



"Then leave it pending."



Lin nodded.



She turned another page.



"There is another matter."



Mingzhao waited.



"The workshop wants to accept a larger order next month."



"From whom?"



"A merchant who supplies several northern markets. He has seen our samples."



"Has he offered a price?"



"Yes."



"Has he paid a deposit?"



"No."



"Then he has expressed interest."



Lin smiled faintly.



"That is what I told him."



Mingzhao examined the sample.



The fabric was attractive. The dye was even, and the weave was consistent.



But a larger order would require more working capital, more workshop time, and greater exposure if the buyer delayed payment.



"How many bolts?"



"Two hundred."



Mingzhao looked up.



"That is more than the current order."



"Yes."



"Can the workshop produce that quantity without delaying existing commitments?"



"Not without additional workers."



"Have you calculated the cost?"



"Not yet."



"Then do not accept the order."



Lin leaned back.



"I was not planning to accept it today."



"Good."



"I wanted to know whether you would consider it."



Mingzhao returned the sample.



"I would consider it after we know the full production cost, delivery schedule, deposit, inspection terms, and consequences of delayed payment."



Lin nodded.



"That is what I expected."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Why?"



"Because you do not enjoy buying promises."



"I do not enjoy paying for them either."



Lin laughed.



The two women continued reviewing the partnership ledger.



They did not agree on every expense.



That was not a problem.



The partnership depended on their ability to discuss disagreements before they became liabilities.

At the imperial residence, the Emperor was having difficulty deciding what to wear for dinner.



The problem was not the clothing.



The problem was the question of whether he should bring a document.



Gao Shun stood nearby.



"Your Majesty, the dinner invitation has been accepted."



"Good."



"The former Empress will attend."



"Good."



The Emperor looked towards the table.



There was a sealed report from the Department of Rites.



He glanced at it.



Then he looked at Gao Shun.



"Should I bring this?"



Gao Shun did not answer immediately.



"Is it related to dinner?"



"No."



"Does it require the former Empress's opinion?"



"No."



"Then perhaps Your Majesty should leave it here."



Zhao Jingheng stared at him.



Gao Shun lowered his head.



The Emperor looked at the report again.



"Very well."



He left it on the table.



It was a small victory.



Gao Shun did not smile.

At the Xu residence, Yuru received another message from her father.



The Duke had arranged a family dinner for the following week.



He had not asked her to inspect the courtyard.



He had not asked her to review the household accounts.



He had merely asked whether she would like to attend.



Yuru placed the message beside the Emperor's invitation.



Two dinners.



One with the imperial family.



One with her natal family.



Both were ordinary invitations.



She found that she liked this.



For years, every meal had carried some responsibility. There had been ceremonial arrangements, household disputes, family obligations, and endless questions about expenditure.



Now she could accept a dinner simply because someone wished to see her.



She wrote an acceptance to her father.



Then she placed both invitations in the same drawer.



Fang Ru entered with a small box.



"Madam, the tailor has returned the garments you requested."



Yuru opened the box.



The clothes were modest and suitable for ordinary visits.



There were no ceremonial robes.



No formal accessories.



No insignia.



She examined the stitching.



"These are good."



"Shall I have them stored separately?"



"Yes."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"Would you like the formal garments moved to the palace stores?"



Yuru looked at her.



"Which formal garments?"



"The ones no longer required for official duties."



Yuru considered this.



"Make an inventory first. Some may remain personal property, and some may belong to the palace."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Shall I ask the stores office to identify them?"



"Yes. I do not want anything transferred without a proper record."



Fang Ru smiled.



"That sounds like something the palace would say."



Yuru returned the garments to the box.



"Then perhaps the palace has learned something."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingyuan received a report from the former northern warehouse steward.



The old steward had found another set of papers among his personal records.



He did not know whether they belonged to the northern warehouse or to a later transport arrangement.



Mingyuan examined the papers.



There were three entries.



One referred to a payment for cart hire.



Another mentioned a stop along the southern road.



The third contained a merchant's name that was difficult to read.



Mingyuan called the account assistant.



"Can you identify this name?"



The assistant examined it.



"It may be Zhao Fusheng."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"May be?"



"The handwriting is unclear."



"Do not write his name as confirmed."



The assistant nodded.



Mingyuan placed the paper beside the other transport records.



The former roadside yard operator was still only a lead.



The new paper did not establish that grain had been delivered there.



It did not identify the number of sacks.



It did not establish whether the payment related to the twenty-sack movement instruction or to another journey.



Mingyuan wrote a note:



*Possible reference to Zhao Fusheng. Identification unconfirmed. No quantity or delivery destination established.*



He placed it in the uncertain records folder.



The account assistant looked at the folder.



"It seems to contain more papers every day."



"That is because we keep finding them."



"Will we ever finish?"



Mingyuan looked at the old ledgers.



"I do not know."



He returned to his work.

That evening, the imperial family gathered for dinner.



Yuru arrived shortly before the meal.



The Emperor greeted her without asking whether she had brought any documents.



The Crown Prince was already seated.



The Second Prince was speaking to the third princess about an upcoming archery exercise.



The Empress Dowager sat at the head of the table.



"Sit down," she said to Yuru.



Yuru took her place.



The meal began.



For several minutes, the conversation remained ordinary.



The Crown Prince described a discussion with the Eastern Palace steward.



The Second Prince complained that his household had been asked to distinguish personal expenses from official household expenses.



The third princess asked whether the distinction applied to books.



The Emperor looked at her.



"Have you finished the library record?"



"Almost, Father."



"Then finish it before asking for another lesson."



Minglan lowered her eyes.



The Dowager smiled.



Yuru took a sip of soup.



The Second Prince looked towards her.



"Madam, do you think the new household accounts are unnecessarily complicated?"



Yuru placed down her spoon.



"Which part?"



"The personal expenditure records."



"Why?"



"Because I already know what I spend."



The Crown Prince looked at him.



"Do you?"



The Second Prince frowned.



"Of course."



"Then the record should be easy."



Yuru looked between them.



"Knowing what you spent and being able to demonstrate it are different things."



The Second Prince considered this.



"Does that mean I should keep every receipt?"



"Not necessarily. You should keep records appropriate to the expense and the applicable rules."



The Emperor nodded.



The Second Prince looked dissatisfied.



"That sounds like a complicated answer."



"It is a practical answer."



The Dowager raised her cup.



"Let us eat before the entire meal becomes an account review."



Everyone returned to the food.



The Emperor glanced at Yuru.



He had not brought a document.



He had not asked for approval.



He was beginning to enjoy the fact that dinner could end without a decision.

After the meal, the third princess walked with Yuru along the covered passage.



"Madam, may I ask you something?"



"Yes."



"Will you continue teaching me after you leave the palace?"



Yuru stopped.



"That depends on where I live and what arrangements are possible."



Minglan looked disappointed.



Yuru continued.



"But I will not stop speaking with you simply because I no longer manage the inner household."



The princess looked up.



"Then I can still ask you questions?"



"Of course."



"Even about accounts?"



"Especially if you have tried to understand them first."



Minglan smiled.



They walked a little farther.



"Will you miss the palace?" the princess asked.



Yuru considered the question.



"Some parts."



"Which parts?"



"The gardens. The library. The people I have known for many years."



Minglan looked down.



"Will you miss us?"



"Yes."



The princess's expression brightened.



Yuru added, "But missing someone does not mean I must continue doing their work."



Minglan laughed.



"I suppose not."



At the end of the passage, Yuru turned towards her temporary residence.



The Emperor stood near the doorway.



He had apparently been waiting.



"Was dinner satisfactory?" he asked.



"It was."



"Good."



He looked as though he wanted to say something else.



Yuru waited.



Finally, he said, "The palace administration has made progress."



"I heard."



"Several matters have been settled."



"That is good."



"The reports are shorter."



"That is also good."



The Emperor paused.



"Would you like to hear the details?"



Yuru looked at him.



"Do you want to tell me as your wife, or are you asking me to review the report?"



Zhao Jingheng considered this distinction.



"As your husband."



"Then you may tell me."



He smiled.



They walked together towards the courtyard.



For once, he did not mention the number of lamps, the missing books, or the ceremonial objects awaiting classification.



He told her that the Crown Prince had corrected his own forms.



He told her that the Second Prince had complained about receipts.



He told her that the third princess had asked four questions about library books.



Yuru listened.



She laughed at the appropriate moments.



And when the conversation ended, no document had been approved.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao finished reviewing Lin Shuyun's partnership ledger.



The proposed order for two hundred bolts remained unaccepted.



The buyer's interest was noted.



The production cost was pending.



The workshop capacity required further examination.



The existing order still had two corrected bolts awaiting inspection by Zhou Wenqi.



Nothing had been exaggerated.



Nothing had been promised.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



Qiao Yue entered carrying the evening tea.



"Did the meeting go well?"



"It was useful."



"Will the partnership accept the larger order?"



"Not yet."



"Then what was decided?"



"That we need more information."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"You have attended another meeting and decided not to decide."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"We decided not to accept an order whose costs and schedule are unknown."



"That sounds better."



"It is better."



Qiao Yue placed the tea on the table.



Mingzhao looked at the day's correspondence.



The family settlement meeting would come later.



The northern canal property remained uncertain.



The temporary residence was functioning.



The silk partnership was progressing, but no profit had yet been established.



The grain investigation remained incomplete.



Her life was not suddenly simple.



But the decisions were increasingly hers.



She picked up the tea.



Outside, the eastern market was settling for the night.



The Emperor had managed a family dinner without bringing a document.



The former Empress had enjoyed an ordinary conversation with her children.



Mingyuan had placed another uncertain paper in the uncertain records folder.



Mingzhao had declined to promise two hundred bolts of silk without knowing the cost.



And somewhere in the imperial palace, thirty-two ceremonial objects remained waiting for someone to determine which office should store them.



The objects could wait.



The dinner was over.



The accounts could be examined tomorrow.
