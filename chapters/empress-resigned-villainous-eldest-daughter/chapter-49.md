# **Chapter 49: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Dinner. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Review the Meeting Record. One Invitation Contains Eight Dishes. The Other Contains a Disagreement About the Word "Confirmed."**

**11th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first disagreement of the morning concerned a word.



Mingzhao discovered it while reviewing the copy of the previous day's meeting record.



The seventh document had described nine personal advances as confirmed.



The four partially supported entries had been described as pending further verification.



The ten uncertain entries had been listed separately.



That was correct.



However, the summary at the end of the document stated:



*The family acknowledges thirteen outstanding personal claims.*



Mingzhao read the sentence twice.



Then she called Qiao Yue.



"Who prepared this copy?"



"The clerk assigned by Mr Liang."



"Ask him to come."



The clerk arrived shortly afterwards, carrying the original meeting notes.



Mingzhao showed him the sentence.



"Why does this say thirteen confirmed claims?"



The clerk examined it.



"It says thirteen outstanding claims, Miss."



"That is also incorrect."



He looked confused.



Mingzhao pointed to the supporting list.



"Nine are confirmed as advances. Four have evidence of payment but no settled repayment arrangement. The word 'outstanding' may suggest that all thirteen are established debts."



The clerk considered the distinction.



"Would you prefer 'thirteen entries requiring settlement consideration'?"



"That is more accurate, provided the nine confirmed claims and four partial claims remain separate in the attached schedule."



The clerk nodded.



He corrected the sentence.



Mingzhao read the revised version.



*Nine advances are confirmed. Four additional entries have confirmed payments but unresolved repayment arrangements. All thirteen entries require consideration in the final settlement, subject to their respective classifications.*



She approved the wording.



The clerk left with the corrected copy.



Qiao Yue watched him go.



"Was that necessary?"



"Yes."



"It was only one sentence."



"One sentence can change a claim."



Qiao Yue looked at the papers.



"I am beginning to understand why you dislike summaries."



"I dislike inaccurate summaries."



"That is more specific."



"It is also more useful."



At the palace, the Emperor received a report from the Imperial Household Administration.



The revised ceremonial inventory reference system had been adopted.



The three offices would use the same reference number while maintaining separate records according to their responsibilities.



The Emperor read the report.



"Good."



Gao Shun bowed.



"There is one remaining question."



The Emperor put down the paper.



"What?"



"The offices disagree about whether the old inventories should be rewritten using the new reference numbers or whether the new numbers should be added as annotations."



"Which approach would preserve the original records?"



"Adding annotations."



"Then do that."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor turned to the next report.



It concerned the distribution of winter bedding.



He read the first page.



Then he looked up.



"Why is this being submitted to me?"



"The Imperial Household Administration requests confirmation of the distribution principle."



"What principle?"



"That replacement bedding should be issued according to recorded condition rather than the seniority of the residence."



The Emperor looked at the document again.



"Is that already the approved procedure?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then why do they need confirmation?"



Gao Shun paused.



"They may be seeking reassurance."



The Emperor leaned back.



"Tell them to apply the existing procedure. If they encounter a genuine dispute about authority or expenditure, they may submit it with the relevant facts."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He returned to the report.



The palace was learning to distinguish between a decision and a request for someone important to repeat a decision already made.



It was a slow lesson.



At the Duke of Xu's residence, Madam Xu was checking the dinner preparations.



The kitchen had prepared the first draft of the menu.



Eight dishes had been listed.



There was steamed fish, braised chicken, seasonal greens, tofu with mushrooms, a light soup, and three other dishes.



Madam Xu examined the list.



"Why is there a second chicken dish?"



The cook bowed.



"Because the Duke prefers it."



"Then prepare it for the Duke on another day."



The cook looked uncertain.



"Should we replace it?"



"With the vegetable dish Yuru likes."



The cook nodded.



Madam Xu checked the seating arrangement.



The table was to be placed near the eastern windows.



There would be no ceremonial seating.



No formal reception.



No musicians.



No additional decorations.



The Duke entered.



"Have you finished?"



"Almost."



He looked at the menu.



"Eight dishes?"



"Yes."



"That is enough."



Madam Xu glanced at him.



"I know."



He picked up the list.



"Why is the kitchen asking whether we should prepare a separate meal for the attendants?"



"Because they are being thoughtful."



"Is that a problem?"



"No. But they should eat in the adjoining room, not be included in the family table."



The Duke nodded.



He looked at the seating plan.



"Should Yuru sit beside me?"



"Of course."



"And you?"



"Beside her."



The Duke put down the paper.



"Then the arrangements are settled."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Until the kitchen discovers another reason to prepare twelve dishes."



The Duke laughed.



At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received the corrected meeting record.



He read the amended paragraph.



Then he called Steward Zhou.



"Has the original list of personal advances been copied?"



"Yes, Young Master."



"Does the copy distinguish the nine confirmed entries from the four partial entries?"



"It does."



"Good."



Mingyuan placed the document in the settlement file.



Mingxiu entered carrying a small bundle of receipts.



"I found the lamp oil records."



Mingyuan looked up.



"All of them?"



"Most of them."



She placed the receipts on the table.



"The household account recorded the purchase under kitchen supplies. The maintenance account recorded the delivery under general maintenance."



"Are they the same purchase?"



"Yes. The supplier's name, date, and quantity match."



Mingyuan compared the entries.



The same oil had been recorded twice under different categories.



The payment, however, appeared only once.



"So it is a duplicate classification, not a duplicate payment."



"Correct."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Then the correction should refer to both accounts."



Mingxiu smiled.



"I already wrote the note."



He examined it.



The note was clear.



It identified the two entries, explained why they referred to the same delivery, and stated that only one payment had been made.



"Good."



Mingxiu looked pleased.



Then she noticed another entry.



"What is this?"



Mingyuan leaned over.



A transport charge had been recorded twice.



The amounts were identical.



The dates were close.



The supplier's name was the same.



Mingxiu looked at him.



"Another duplicate?"



"Perhaps."



"Can we correct it?"



"Not yet."



"Why?"



"We need to know whether there were two deliveries."



She sighed.



The accounts room had refused to reward them with an easy answer.



At the temporary residence, Mingzhao received a letter from Sun Qiming.



The accountant had completed another review of the warehouse documents.



He had not found evidence establishing the destination of the twenty-seven unexplained sacks.



However, he had identified a difference between two old inventory descriptions.



One referred to the northern warehouse's full grain stock.



The other referred to grain available for immediate distribution.



The figures were not identical.



Sun did not claim that this explained the discrepancy.



He asked for permission to examine the original warehouse counting procedure and determine whether the two records used different stock categories.



Mingzhao read the letter carefully.



She sent her reply.



Sun was authorized to examine the counting method and compare the original records.



The inquiry was not to assume that the difference explained the missing grain.



It was to establish whether the two records measured the same thing.



She placed the letter in the warehouse file.



Qiao Yue brought tea.



"Has Mr Sun found the grain?"



"No."



"Has he found a new clue?"



"He has found a question worth examining."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Is that good?"



"It is better than inventing an answer."



The former Empress was preparing for the family dinner when Fang Ru entered with the garment inventory.



The two uncertain robes had been examined again.



The first had been confirmed as personal property.



The second remained unresolved because the original purchase order had not been found.



Fang Ru placed the updated list on the table.



"Should we include the second robe in the transfer?"



Yuru shook her head.



"Not until the record is clear."



"It is currently in the palace wardrobe."



"Then leave it there."



Fang Ru nodded.



The remaining garments had been sorted.



Personal clothing would accompany Yuru.



Palace-owned garments would remain in palace custody.



Items with uncertain ownership would be held separately until the records were checked.



Yuru looked over the list.



"That is sufficient."



Fang Ru closed the inventory.



"Your carriage will be ready for tomorrow's dinner."



"Thank you."



Yuru returned to her book.



The question of where she would eventually live remained unresolved.



She was not in a hurry.



For the first time in many years, she could allow a decision to remain undecided without feeling that the entire household would stop functioning.



At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei was discussing the meeting with Mr Liang.



The preliminary review had been useful.



It had also revealed how much work remained.



Mr Liang placed the next proposed agenda on the table.



"The next meeting should focus on the valuation and classification of the confirmed estate properties."



Shen Guowei nodded.



"How many documents?"



"That depends on whether the remaining agricultural records arrive."



Shen Guowei looked at him.



"Can we avoid another meeting with eight documents?"



Mr Liang smiled faintly.



"We can try."



The Marquis sighed.



"Then let us arrange the supporting records first."



Mr Liang nodded.



He had served the clan for many years.



He knew that a family property division could not be settled merely because everyone was tired of discussing it.



He also knew that the Marquis was beginning to understand this.



At the eastern market, Lin Shuyun sent another message.



The buyer had agreed to the revised delivery schedule for the additional two-hundred-bolt order.



The workshop manager had confirmed that four workers could be hired, but two would require training.



The revised estimate included their wages and training costs.



The buyer had also agreed to the proposed deposit.



However, the contract still required final signatures.



Mingzhao reviewed the figures.



She checked the cost of raw silk, dyeing, labour, transport, and expected finishing losses.



The projected margin was narrower than the first estimate.



It was still potentially worthwhile.



But the current order had to be completed first.



She wrote to Lin.



The proposed contract could proceed to final review, but production must not begin until both parties signed and the deposit was received.



She also instructed Lin to keep the accounts for the new order separate from the existing partnership work.



Lin replied later that afternoon.



She agreed.



The additional order was closer to becoming a real contract.



It was not yet a completed agreement.



Mingzhao entered the correspondence in the partnership file.



At the palace, Third Princess Minglan presented the revised library catalogue.



The two unidentified books remained unresolved.



The librarian had obtained an older catalogue from the Department of Rites.



One title could now be identified.



The second still had two possible matches.



Minglan presented the revised catalogue to Yuru.



"I have corrected the first entry."



Yuru examined it.



"How did you confirm the title?"



"The old catalogue contained the full title, and the book's first page matched it."



"Good."



"The second remains uncertain."



"Then leave it marked as uncertain."



Minglan nodded.



"I was worried that the catalogue would look unfinished."



Yuru smiled.



"An unfinished record is not necessarily a bad record."



Minglan looked at the paper.



"It is better than a wrong one."



"Yes."



The princess gathered the documents.



Then she asked, "Will you still teach me after you leave the palace?"



Yuru looked at her.



"I am already no longer responsible for palace administration."



"I know. I meant lessons."



"Yes. If you wish to study, I will teach you."



Minglan smiled.



"Thank you."



Yuru watched her leave.



The princess was learning to ask for instruction without expecting someone else to take responsibility for her work.



That was a more useful habit than memorizing a hundred rules.



The next morning's dinner preparations occupied the Duke of Xu's residence.



Madam Xu inspected the dining room.



The table was ready.



The eight dishes had been confirmed.



The attendants' meal was arranged separately.



The servants had been instructed not to announce Yuru's arrival as though she were attending a formal court ceremony.



The Duke entered.



"Everything is ready?"



"Yes."



"Then why are you still checking?"



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Because you keep asking."



He laughed.



She turned towards the window.



"Do you think she will like the arrangement?"



"She said she would come."



"That was not my question."



The Duke considered it.



"She will like being here."



Madam Xu nodded.



That was what she wanted.



Not a grand welcome.



Not a ceremonial display.



Just a daughter returning for dinner.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao received the corrected record from Mr Liang.



She examined the revised claim summary.



The nine confirmed advances were correctly identified.



The four partially supported entries remained separate.



The ten uncertain entries were not described as debts.



The family meeting record had been corrected.



She signed the acknowledgment.



Qiao Yue looked at the document.



"Is the disagreement settled?"



"The wording is corrected."



"Then yes?"



"That particular disagreement is settled."



Qiao Yue smiled.



"You always leave room for another disagreement."



"Because there will be one."



Qiao Yue laughed.



Mingzhao placed the record in her file.



She then turned to the property schedule.



The next meeting would require further valuations.



The warehouse inquiry would continue.



The canal property remained uncertain.



The silk partnership was approaching a new contract.



Her temporary rental remained suitable for the time being.



There was still no permanent residence decision.



She closed the file.



For today, the work was complete.



That evening, Yuru received a final note from Madam Xu.



It contained no schedule.



It contained no list of duties.



It simply said that the family was looking forward to seeing her.



Yuru read it twice.



Then she placed it beside her book.



Fang Ru asked whether she should prepare anything special.



"Just the ordinary things."



"Yes, Madam."



Yuru looked at her.



Fang Ru corrected herself.



"Yes."



Yuru smiled.



The former Empress had spent much of her life receiving invitations that carried obligations.



Tomorrow's invitation carried dinner.



That was enough.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao's corrected meeting record had been filed.



The family had not completed the division.



The warehouse had not yielded its missing grain.



The northern canal property's boundary had not been settled.



The proposed silk contract had not yet been signed.



But one inaccurate sentence had been corrected.



And for the moment, that was the most useful thing anyone had accomplished.
