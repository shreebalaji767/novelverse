# **Chapter 78: The Former Empress Has Finished Her Father's Book. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Nearly Finished Another Order. The Family Has Not Finished Its Accounts. The Accountant Has Begun to Suspect That Finishing Anything Is a Matter of Procedure.**

**12th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress had finished the book.



This was a small achievement, but she considered it a satisfactory one.



The book had been lent to her by her father during her visit to the Xu residence. It concerned provincial administration, grain storage, transport arrangements, and the difficulties that arose when official records described the same quantity of grain in different ways.



It was not particularly entertaining.



However, it had been written by a man who had spent many years studying the subject, and several passages had been interesting enough that Yuru had read them twice.



She sat at the writing table in the temporary guest residence, the book closed beside her.



Fang Ru placed a cup of tea near her hand.



“Madam, shall I arrange for the book to be returned to the Xu residence?”



“Not yet. I can return it when I visit.”



“Then shall I wrap it?”



“Please.”



Fang Ru picked up the book and examined its condition.



There were no stains, torn pages, or folded corners. Yuru had placed three narrow paper markers between the pages she wished to remember. She had not written in the book itself.



Fang Ru looked at the markers.



“You found several interesting passages?”



“Three.”



“Only three?”



“Three passages worth discussing. There were more than three passages worth reading.”



Fang Ru nodded.



This distinction seemed reasonable.



She wrapped the book in clean paper and placed it inside a small wooden case.



“Your father may be pleased that you read it so carefully.”



“He may be more pleased that I return it without damaging the binding.”



“Those two things are not mutually exclusive.”



“No,” Yuru agreed. “But fathers sometimes have very particular priorities when lending books.”



Fang Ru smiled.



Yuru lifted her tea.



The morning was quiet.



There were no palace expenditure sheets waiting on the table. No servants had arrived to ask whether a particular household should receive additional fuel. No one had requested her opinion about a dispute between two palace departments.



The absence of such matters had become increasingly ordinary.



She was beginning to appreciate that ordinary things did not need to be announced as achievements.



After breakfast, she asked Fang Ru to bring a fresh sheet of paper.



“I will write to my mother.”



“About the book?”



“Partly.”



Yuru considered her words before beginning.



She told Madam Xu that she had finished the book and found the section on provincial granaries particularly interesting. She also mentioned that she would return it during her next visit.



Then she wrote that the preserved fruit sent by her mother had been very good.



She paused.



After a moment, she added that Fang Ru had also enjoyed it.



Fang Ru, who was standing nearby, looked at the letter.



“Madam, you need not mention me every time.”



“My mother sent a separate packet for you.”



“That does not mean I must appear in every letter.”



“It means she will be pleased to know you received it.”



Fang Ru could not argue with that.



Yuru folded the letter and handed it to her.



“Please have it sent.”



“Yes, Madam.”



The former Empress returned to her tea.



She had written a letter, finished a book, and made arrangements for its return.



It was not a particularly remarkable morning.



That was precisely what made it pleasant.

At the eastern market residence, Shen Mingzhao was having a less peaceful morning.



Not because the house was in disorder.



The upper latch worked properly. The kitchen shelves remained stable. The rear drain had been cleaned, and the replacement board no longer shifted underfoot.



The lower room still retained some dampness after rain, but there had been no new rain during the previous night.



For the moment, the house was behaving.



Mingzhao considered this a better arrangement than many human relationships.



She was seated at the small table in the front room, examining the workshop's latest production sheet.



Qiao Yue stood beside her with a second document.



“The first group of twenty-eight has completed finishing,” Qiao Yue reported. “The internal inspection began this morning.”



“How many have passed?”



“Twenty-three passed the first examination. Three require minor adjustment. Two are being examined again because their edges appear slightly uneven.”



“Are the two damaged?”



“No. The finisher believes the folds can be corrected.”



“Then they are not damaged. They are unfinished.”



Qiao Yue looked down at the sheet.



“I will amend the description.”



“Please do. There is no reason to turn a correctable defect into a loss merely because someone used an unfortunate word.”



Qiao Yue made a note.



The revised folding method remained in use, but the instructions were clear. The original buyer-approved sample was attached to the production record, and the revised sample was kept beside it.



The senior finisher had initially complained that the additional comparison was unnecessary.



He had since stopped making that complaint.



This was not because he had suddenly become fond of paperwork.



He had discovered that a written standard made it considerably harder for someone else to blame him for a disagreement about appearance.



The two trainees were still working under separate supervision.



One had become faster without losing much of her carefulness. The other had improved her accuracy, although she still needed more frequent checks.



Mingzhao examined the figures.



“Have the wages been entered?”



“Yes.”



“Training time?”



“Separately recorded.”



“Extra inspection?”



“Recorded under workshop supervision.”



“Material corrections?”



“Listed individually.”



“Good.”



Qiao Yue glanced at the next line.



“The second group of twenty-eight is still being folded. It should enter finishing tomorrow if there are no delays.”



“Do not put tomorrow's expected work into today's completed total.”



“I know.”



“Then why does this sheet say fifty-six bolts remaining in production and twenty-eight nearly complete?”



“Because the first group is nearly complete.”



“Nearly complete is not complete.”



Qiao Yue sighed.



“Would you prefer that I write that twenty-three have passed, three require adjustment, and two require re-examination?”



“Yes.”



“That is a very long sentence for a workshop sheet.”



“It is shorter than an argument with the buyer.”



Qiao Yue accepted this.



The order had reached an important stage.



Of the two hundred bolts contracted by Zhou Wenqi, one hundred and forty-four had already been inspected and accepted by the buyer. Fifty-six remained.



The first twenty-eight were now undergoing internal inspection. The second twenty-eight were still being folded.



No further buyer inspection had been scheduled.



Mingzhao did not intend to treat the remaining quantity as accepted merely because the workshop hoped to finish it soon.



Hope was not a receipt.



She had learned that lesson from the Marquisate's accounts.

The Marquisate's accounts were, meanwhile, continuing to resist a final conclusion.



Shen Mingyuan sat in the eastern accounts room with Steward Zhou and Liang Wen.



Several bundles of documents lay across the table.



The proposed purchasing procedure was still being revised.



Mingyuan had asked for a clearer distinction between the person authorizing an expenditure and the person physically receiving the goods.



Liang Wen had agreed.



He had also added a section concerning partial deliveries, because the previous draft had described payment after receipt without explaining what should happen when only part of an order arrived.



Steward Zhou had suggested that corrections be entered with dates and signatures.



Mingyuan had insisted that the original entries remain visible.



“Otherwise,” he said, “a correction can make it appear that the first entry never existed.”



Liang Wen nodded.



“That is why I have written that the original record must not be erased. The corrected amount should be entered separately, with the reason and the person making the correction.”



“And who verifies it?”



“The accounts clerk checks the supporting document. The register keeper records the change. The supervising steward reviews the completed entry.”



Mingyuan read the paragraph again.



“What if the receiving steward disputes the quantity?”



“Then the discrepancy remains open until it is examined.”



“Who is allowed to close it?”



“The responsible supervising official, after receiving the relevant evidence.”



Mingyuan placed the paper down.



“That is better.”



Steward Zhou looked at him.



“You have become very particular about written procedures.”



Mingyuan glanced at the stacks of documents.



“I have discovered that when something is not written down, everyone remembers it differently.”



“That is true.”



“And when something is written down incorrectly, everyone remembers the written version.”



“That is also true.”



Mingyuan leaned back.



“Then we must be careful about both.”



Liang Wen gathered the pages.



“I will revise the draft once more and prepare a clean copy.”



“Do that. Do not send it to my sister until it is complete.”



Steward Zhou nodded.



Mingzhao had agreed to review a completed written procedure. She had not agreed to help write it, supervise it, or assume responsibility for the Marquisate's purchasing arrangements.



Mingyuan understood the distinction.



He did not entirely like it.



However, disliking a boundary did not make it disappear.



He turned to another bundle.



“Have we found the original voucher for the unclear payment?”



Steward Zhou shook his head.



“The general purchasing files contained a bundle that may be relevant, but the voucher itself has not been identified.”



“Have the entries been checked against the seal register?”



“Yes. The estate purchasing seal was held by Chen Rui on the date recorded.”



“And Chen Rui?”



“He has not remembered the payment. He only explained that general purchasing vouchers were filed separately from household expenditure records.”



Mingyuan looked at the bundle.



“So we know where the voucher might have been filed.”



“We do not yet know whether it was filed there.”



“Quite.”



The distinction was irritating.



It was also important.



Mingyuan had once believed that finding an entry in a ledger meant understanding the transaction.



Now he was learning that an entry might only be the beginning of the question.



He opened the next cash book.



The search continued.

At the eastern market residence, Mingzhao received a short note from the workshop shortly before noon.



The internal inspection of the first group of twenty-eight had been completed.



Twenty-six bolts passed.



Two required further correction.



Both remained usable.



No material had been written off.



Mingzhao read the note and placed it beside the production sheet.



“Ask the workshop to identify why those two failed.”



Qiao Yue wrote it down.



“Do you expect the same folding problem?”



“I do not know. That is why I am asking.”



Qiao Yue smiled.



“You could simply tell them to be more careful.”



“I could also tell the river not to flood.”



“That would probably be less effective.”



“Exactly.”



Mingzhao returned to the order records.



There was another matter.



The buyer's representative had not yet sent a date for the next inspection. That did not necessarily mean a delay. Zhou Jian might be waiting until the next group was ready, or Zhou Wenqi might be occupied with other business.



Mingzhao instructed the workshop to continue according to the agreed schedule, while keeping the accepted and unaccepted quantities separate.



The order was proceeding.



That did not mean it was finished.



Nor did it mean that its profit could yet be calculated.



She had paid the additional workers, recorded training costs, and accounted for supervision. The production expenses were accumulating in the correct places, but the final transport costs and any remaining corrections would have to be included before she could determine the net result.



Qiao Yue looked at the ledger.



“Once the order is completed, we will finally know whether the new arrangement was profitable.”



“Once the order is delivered and the expenses are settled.”



“And once the buyer pays.”



“The buyer has already paid according to the agreed stages.”



“Then once the remaining balance is settled.”



Mingzhao looked at her.



“You are learning to add conditions.”



“I have been working for you for years.”



“Then you should have learned sooner.”



Qiao Yue laughed.



Mingzhao did not.



But the corner of her mouth moved slightly.

In the Imperial Palace, the day passed without any need for the former Empress to intervene.



The Crown Prince continued reviewing the Eastern Palace accounts.



The Second Prince revised an expenditure estimate.



The Third Princess continued her reading in the library.



The senior palace administrators handled their own work.



Gao Shun received the routine reports and sent them to the appropriate offices.



There were still mistakes.



A storehouse clerk had copied one quantity into the wrong column. A minor household purchase had been entered twice. One department had submitted an estimate without identifying whether its figures included transport.



Each matter was returned to the responsible office.



None was sent to Yuru.



Gao Shun reviewed the afternoon dispatch list and found no reason to change that arrangement.



He had once considered it efficient to ask the former Empress for guidance whenever a matter became troublesome.



Now he understood that such efficiency had been built on the assumption that one person would always be available to correct everyone else's work.



The palace was not yet perfect.



It was, however, beginning to become responsible for itself.



In her guest residence, Yuru received no report about any of these difficulties.



She spent part of the afternoon reading a second book from the small collection she had brought from the Xu residence.



It was a collection of essays on household architecture.



The writing was dry.



She read it anyway.



At one point, Fang Ru entered with a small tray of fruit.



“Madam, the kitchen has sent these.”



Yuru looked at the fruit.



“Are they from the guest residence stores?”



“Yes. They were recorded this morning.”



Yuru nodded.



“Then please thank them.”



Fang Ru set down the tray.



“Would you like them cut?”



“Not yet.”



Yuru selected one and examined its skin.



She had once spent years ensuring that the palace kitchens had sufficient supplies, that deliveries were recorded, that expenses were justified, and that every department understood its obligations.



Now someone else had sent her fruit.



She did not need to inspect the storehouse account before eating it.



The realization was small.



She found that she liked it.

That evening, Mingzhao received no new letter from the Marquisate.



There was no completed purchasing procedure.



No final property schedule.



No evidence identifying the destination of the disputed payment.



The northern canal property remained under consideration, and the boundary documents had still not arrived.



The workshop had completed twenty-six of the first remaining twenty-eight bolts to internal standard. Two were still being corrected.



Mingzhao reviewed the day's records, then closed the ledger.



Qiao Yue brought her evening tea.



“Will you write to the Marquisate?”



“About what?”



“They may be waiting for your response.”



“They have not sent the completed procedure.”



“Perhaps they are waiting for you to ask.”



“Then they may continue waiting.”



Qiao Yue placed the tea down.



“You are very patient when refusing something.”



“I have had years of practice.”



“That is unfortunate.”



“It is also useful.”



Mingzhao took her cup.



She had spent six years ensuring that the Marquisate's affairs continued to function.



Now the family was discovering that the work had not disappeared when she left.



It had merely become visible to the people who had previously benefited from not seeing it.



She did not intend to rescue them from that discovery.



Not yet.



Perhaps not ever.



Outside, the eastern market was gradually quieting. A cart passed along the street, its wheels making a familiar sound against the stone road.



Inside the small rented residence, the accounts were in order, the workers had been paid, and the next day's tasks had been recorded.



It was not a grand achievement.



But it belonged to her.



And that, Mingzhao thought, was sufficient for one evening.



In the palace, the former Empress finished her tea.



In the Marquisate, the accountant continued searching for a missing voucher.



In the workshop, two bolts awaited correction.



And somewhere in the capital, a clean copy of a purchasing procedure was being prepared with five separate responsibilities, three stages of verification, and enough written conditions to prevent anyone from claiming that the document had been misunderstood.



The Great Yong Dynasty had survived wars, floods, succession disputes, and several generations of ambitious ministers.



It now faced a more unusual challenge.



People were being asked to do the work assigned to them.



The results remained uncertain.
