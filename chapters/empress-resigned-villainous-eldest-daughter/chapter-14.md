# **Chapter 14: The Empress Has Been Told That Her Resignation Is Still Under Review. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Told That Her Departure Is Also Under Review.**

**6th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The palace had developed a new method of delaying decisions.

It involved acknowledging that a decision was necessary, establishing a committee to examine the consequences of making it, and then requesting another report concerning the committee's findings.

The Emperor had encountered this method twice before breakfast.

By the third occasion, he was no longer willing to admire its consistency.

The report concerning the Empress's resignation had returned from the Ministry of Personnel.

It was a substantial document.

The ministry had identified several questions that required clarification:

Whether the Empress's position could be relinquished while she remained in the imperial family.

Whether the administrative duties associated with the position could be transferred separately.

Whether the existing regulations permitted the appointment of another senior woman to oversee the inner palace.

Whether the transition would affect the status of the Crown Prince, the imperial children, or the established ceremonial order.

Whether a temporary arrangement would be necessary before a final decision.

The Emperor read the report.

Then he turned to the final page.

The Ministry of Personnel had recommended that the matter be referred to the Ministry of Rites.

The Ministry of Rites had already requested that the matter be referred to the Ministry of Personnel.

Zhao Jingheng placed the report on his desk.

"Gao Shun."

"Your Majesty."

"Have the two ministries been informed that they are sending the same question to each other?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"And?"

"The Ministry of Rites says the matter concerns the Empress's position. The Ministry of Personnel says the matter concerns the appointment of a successor."

The Emperor stared at him.

"Then they have identified two questions."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Have they answered either?"

"No, Your Majesty."

Zhao Jingheng picked up his brush.

He wrote beneath the report:

*The questions concerning the status of the Empress and the transfer of administrative duties shall be examined separately. The Ministry of Personnel shall identify the applicable rules concerning appointment and succession. The Ministry of Rites shall identify the ceremonial consequences. Neither ministry is to return the matter merely because the other has a related responsibility.*

He paused.

Then he added:

*The Emperor will determine the final decision.*

Gao Shun accepted the document.

The Emperor leaned back.

"At least the final sentence is correct."

At Chengming Palace, Yuru was not reading the resignation report.

She was reading a report concerning the education of the imperial children.

The Crown Prince's household had submitted a revised schedule for the instruction of younger princes and princesses.

The schedule was sensible.

It contained lessons in classical literature, history, arithmetic, writing, and the proper conduct of official correspondence.

It also contained a request.

The Crown Prince had asked whether the Empress could continue reviewing the children's monthly progress reports after the transfer of her administrative duties.

Yuru read the request.

Then she called Fang Ru.

"Did the Crown Prince write this himself?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Why?"

Fang Ru hesitated.

"He believes Your Majesty's experience would be valuable."

"That is true."

Fang Ru waited.

Yuru looked at the report again.

The Crown Prince had not asked her to manage the education schedule.

He had asked her to continue reviewing the children's progress.

It was a reasonable request.

It was also a request that could quietly become another permanent responsibility.

"How often are the reports submitted?"

"Monthly."

"Who reviews them now?"

"The Crown Prince's educational office, with the senior tutors."

"Then why do they require my review?"

Fang Ru considered the question.

"Perhaps because Your Majesty has traditionally been involved in the children's education."

"Traditionally."

Yuru put down the paper.

"That word is becoming very expensive."

Fang Ru lowered her eyes.

Yuru was not opposed to helping the children.

She was opposed to the assumption that every useful contribution must become an official obligation.

"Send the Crown Prince a reply."

Fang Ru waited.

"Tell him that I will continue to advise the children when necessary. Their monthly progress reports should remain with the tutors and the educational office."

"Should the Crown Prince receive them?"

"Yes. He is responsible for the Eastern Palace and has an interest in the education of his younger siblings."

Fang Ru nodded.

"And the Empress's office?"

"May receive a summary during the transition, if it is useful. But it should not become another approval process."

Yuru looked at the report.

She had spent years believing that a mother's attention and an empress's authority could be used interchangeably.

She was beginning to see that the distinction mattered.

She could remain a mother without becoming the permanent administrator of every child's education.

She could offer advice without requiring every lesson to pass through her office.

And she could care about the palace without being responsible for every document that described it.

The Crown Prince received the reply before noon.

He read it carefully.

Then he went to the Hall of Mental Cultivation.

The Emperor was examining the revised household expenditure register.

"Father."

"Sit."

Cheng'an placed the reply on the desk.

"Her Majesty has declined to review the monthly educational reports."

The Emperor looked at him.

"Has she declined to advise the children?"

"No."

"Then what has she declined?"

"The formal review."

Zhao Jingheng nodded.

"That is reasonable."

The Crown Prince looked down.

"I had thought the reports would benefit from her attention."

"They may."

"Then why—"

"Because your mother is not the only person capable of reading them."

Cheng'an stopped.

The Emperor continued.

"If the tutors cannot assess a child's progress without the Empress, the problem is not that the Empress has stopped reading reports. The problem is that the tutors have failed to perform their duties."

The Crown Prince considered this.

"Then I should review the arrangement."

"You should ask the educational office to explain its responsibilities."

"Yes, Father."

Zhao Jingheng returned to the register.

"Do not turn every useful person into a permanent requirement."

Cheng'an bowed.

He had heard the principle before.

This time, he understood that it applied to people he respected, not only to officials who avoided work.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had received a similar request.

Her father had asked her to review the proposed household transition schedule.

The request was written politely.

It was also accompanied by a note stating that her departure date should remain undecided until the family had reached agreement on all unresolved matters.

Mingzhao read it.

Then she placed it beside the schedule.

Qiao Yue stood near the window.

"Young Lady?"

"Father wants the transition schedule reviewed."

"That seems reasonable."

"He also wants the departure date left undecided."

Qiao Yue glanced at the note.

"That seems less reasonable."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"It is not unreasonable for him to want certainty."

"Then he should establish the transition."

"Yes."

"But he cannot establish certainty by postponing the date forever."

Mingzhao folded the note.

She had expected this difficulty.

The family did not want her to leave before the household could function without her.

But the household had not learned to function without her because she had never been permitted to leave.

The arrangement was circular.

She had identified it weeks ago.

Now she would have to break it without destroying the records, the estate, or the people who depended on the household's income.

She called Steward Zhou.

The transition meeting began in the eastern study.

Shen Guowei sat at the head of the table.

Madam Liang sat beside him.

Shen Mingyuan had brought the household expenditure ledger.

Shen Mingxiu had brought a list of the staff currently responsible for the smaller residential properties.

Steward Zhou sat near the end of the table.

Mingzhao placed the transition schedule in the centre.

"Before we begin," she said, "I want to clarify that the schedule concerns the transfer of responsibilities. It does not require the family to agree on every question of property before the transfer begins."

Shen Guowei frowned.

"The property division and the household management are related."

"They are related. They are not identical."

"How can the household be transferred before the property is divided?"

"Because the household has to continue operating while the property is being examined."

Mingyuan looked at the ledger.

"What happens to the expenditure already charged to my residence?"

"It remains under examination."

"Then who pays for the next repair?"

"That depends on whether it is an ordinary estate repair, a personal expense, or a disputed matter."

Mingyuan looked irritated.

"That is exactly the problem."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Yes. That is why we need a temporary procedure."

She turned to Steward Zhou.

"Ordinary estate repairs should be authorized by the responsible steward within the existing budget. Personal repairs should be charged to the person requesting them. Exceptional expenditure should be recorded and approved by the person responsible for the estate."

Shen Guowei looked at her.

"Who is responsible for the estate?"

Mingzhao did not answer immediately.

The question was not simple.

The Marquisate contained several properties, agricultural holdings, commercial interests, and household expenses.

Some belonged to the family estate.

Some were held separately.

Some were connected to the household but not owned by it.

A temporary administrator could not be given authority over everything merely because he was available.

"Until the property division is complete," she said, "the existing estate manager should continue handling ordinary operations under your authority. The transition should identify which responsibilities are transferred to Mingyuan, which remain with the steward, and which require your approval."

Mingyuan looked at her.

"How many responsibilities are being transferred to me?"

"That depends on what you are willing to accept."

He frowned.

"That is not a proper answer."

"It is the only honest one."

Mingxiu glanced between them.

Mingzhao continued.

"You are the heir. You should learn the estate's finances and obligations. But you do not need to personally inspect every grain sack or approve every kitchen purchase."

Mingyuan looked offended.

"I know that."

"Then we agree."

He opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

Steward Zhou looked down at the ledger.

The meeting had not become peaceful.

It had, however, become useful.

Mingxiu's list revealed another difficulty.

Several members of the household staff had been assigned to more than one responsibility.

The same clerk maintained the household expenditure register and the repair records for the eastern residence.

A senior maid supervised the kitchens and also received deliveries for the family guest rooms.

A steward at the secondary estate was responsible for both agricultural records and the maintenance of the family granary.

These arrangements had developed because the household had relied on Mingzhao to notice when one responsibility interfered with another.

Without her, the overlapping duties could become a source of confusion.

Mingxiu pointed to the list.

"If we transfer the repair records to the estate steward, who checks the payments?"

"The accountant," Mingzhao replied.

"And who checks the accountant?"

"That depends on the authority assigned to the examination."

Mingxiu looked at her.

"You are making everything complicated."

"No. I am separating things that were previously combined."

Mingxiu studied the list.

After a moment, she said, "The eastern residence steward should not approve his own repair payments."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Correct."

"Then the expenditure register should be checked by the central estate clerk."

"Correct."

Mingxiu seemed surprised that her suggestion had been accepted so quickly.

She continued.

"The secondary estate should send its monthly agricultural account to the central office. The granary inventory should be checked separately."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That is also correct."

Mingxiu looked at the schedule.

"Then perhaps I can take responsibility for the monthly summary."

Madam Liang turned toward her.

"You?"

Mingxiu's expression became guarded.

"I already assist with the household records."

"You are not required to take on more work."

"I know."

Mingzhao examined the list.

"Would you be willing to do it?"

Mingxiu nodded.

"Yes. But I do not want to become responsible for every error in the records."

"Then your authority and limits must be written down."

Mingxiu looked relieved.

It was a small distinction.

But she had seen what happened when responsibility was assigned without limits.

She did not intend to inherit the same arrangement merely because her elder sister was leaving.

The meeting continued for another hour.

They identified the first responsibilities to be transferred.

The central estate clerk would maintain the monthly expenditure summary.

Steward Zhou would continue supervising ordinary operations.

Mingyuan would review the estate's principal income and expenditure accounts each month, with the accountant explaining the entries.

Mingxiu would prepare the summary of the secondary residences and agricultural holdings.

The family would retain its existing authority over exceptional expenditure until the property settlement clarified the separate responsibilities.

The schedule was not complete.

But it had a beginning.

At the end, Shen Guowei looked at Mingzhao.

"Would you remain for one month after the transfer?"

Mingzhao considered the request.

"To answer questions about the records?"

"Yes."

"I can agree to a defined period of assistance."

"Then the departure date can be settled afterward."

"No."

Shen Guowei's expression tightened.

Mingzhao continued.

"The period of assistance must have an end date. It cannot depend on whether the family feels ready."

Madam Liang looked at her.

"Your father is trying to protect the household."

"I know."

"Then why are you making this difficult?"

"Because I am not asking the household to stop functioning. I am asking it to function without making my presence a condition."

The room became quiet.

Mingyuan looked at the ledger in front of him.

Mingxiu looked at the transition schedule.

Steward Zhou remained still.

Shen Guowei finally said, "We will discuss the date again."

Mingzhao inclined her head.

"That is acceptable."

She did not say that it was sufficient.

It was not.

But the first responsibilities had been assigned.

And the family had begun to understand that the transition was not a single event.

It was a process that could be measured.

In the palace, the Ministry of Personnel returned its report.

This time, it had answered the question concerning appointment.

The existing regulations did not provide for the Empress to transfer her position to another woman by private agreement.

Any change in the position would require an imperial decision.

The Ministry of Rites had separately identified the ceremonial consequences.

The two reports were now distinct.

The Emperor read them.

He did not immediately summon the Empress.

Instead, he ordered a further review of the proposed administrative transition.

The position and the duties could not be treated as identical.

The Empress's formal status belonged to the imperial household and the dynasty.

The administrative duties associated with her office had developed through regulations, custom, and personal practice.

Some could be transferred to existing offices.

Some required clarification.

Some required the Emperor's decision.

The reports had finally begun to distinguish them.

Zhao Jingheng put them aside.

He had not reached a final decision.

But he was no longer allowing the ministries to pretend that the questions were impossible to separate.

That evening, Yuru received the revised register.

The twelve direct responsibilities had been examined.

Five had been assigned under existing authority.

Three required clarification.

Four remained under review because they involved senior household matters that could not be transferred without considering the consequences for the people concerned.

The eight recommendation duties had also been examined.

The register now identified which recommendations required the Empress's judgment and which could be made by the responsible administrator.

Yuru read the report.

Then she looked at Fang Ru.

"How many matters have been transferred?"

"Five direct responsibilities, Your Majesty."

"How many have been completed?"

"Three."

"Why not five?"

"Two require the revised procedure to be issued."

Yuru nodded.

"Then the report should say that."

Fang Ru made a note.

Yuru looked at the remaining duties.

There were still many.

She had expected the process to be difficult.

She had not expected it to reveal so many small distinctions.

The palace had not been entirely dependent on her.

It had been dependent on a system that made her the easiest person to ask.

That was not the same thing.

It was also not a problem that could be solved by refusing every request.

She would have to continue helping.

But she would have to stop being the only person whose help made the system function.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao received the final inspection report for the northern canal residence.

The second carpenter had confirmed the roof damage.

The kitchen beams required replacement.

The drainage work was more extensive than the first estimate.

The boundary wall needed reinforcement.

The total repair cost had increased again.

The seller was willing to reduce the purchase price slightly.

Not enough.

Mingzhao read the report.

Then she placed it beside the eastern residence's boundary documents.

The northern house was becoming expensive.

The eastern house was becoming uncertain.

She looked at Qiao Yue.

"Neither."

Qiao Yue blinked.

"Neither?"

"Neither house is suitable at the present price and condition."

"Then what will we do?"

"Continue looking."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"Young Lady, we may never find a house."

"Then we will not buy one merely to prove that we can."

She closed the documents.

Her independent household would not begin with a purchase she regretted.

It would begin when the property, the accounts, and the obligations were clear enough to support it.

That was not as dramatic as leaving the Marquisate in a single afternoon.

It was also considerably more sensible.

Before retiring, Mingzhao reviewed the transition schedule.

The ordinary estate responsibilities had been assigned.

Mingyuan had agreed to examine the principal accounts.

Mingxiu had agreed to prepare the monthly summaries.

Steward Zhou would continue supervising the operations.

The independent accountant would complete the examination.

The property division remained unresolved.

The northern warehouse discrepancy remained partially explained.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.

The house search remained open.

Mingzhao looked at the list.

For the first time, she could see a path that did not require her to remain indefinitely.

It was not a perfect path.

It contained delays, disagreements, and several people who still believed that her departure should depend on their comfort.

But it existed.

She placed the schedule in the outgoing tray.

Then she wrote a short note to her father.

*The first transfer of responsibilities may begin tomorrow. I will provide assistance concerning the records for the agreed period. The date of my departure should be established separately and should not depend on the completion of every unresolved matter.*

She signed it.

And sent it.

The palace was learning that the Empress could advise without approving everything.

The Marquisate was learning that the eldest daughter could explain without managing everything.

Neither institution was pleased.

Both were beginning to function.

**End of Chapter 14.**
