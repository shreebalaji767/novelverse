# **Chapter 48: The Former Empress Has Received Eight Invitations to Dinner. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received Eight Documents for Discussion. The Marquis Has Prepared a Family Meeting. The Accountant Has Prepared a Separate List of Things the Family Cannot Yet Agree On.**

**10th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Marquis of Qingyuan had arranged the meeting room before breakfast.



He had ordered the long table to be cleaned, the chairs to be placed evenly, and the windows opened.



He had also instructed the servants to prepare tea.



This was an ordinary meeting.



At least, that was what he had told the household.



By the time Mingzhao arrived, the room contained eleven people, eight documents, three account books, two property maps, and enough tea to serve a small delegation.



Qiao Yue entered behind her mistress carrying a document case.



Sun Qiming followed with his own papers.



Mingzhao looked at the table.



"Father."



Shen Guowei stood.



"You have arrived."



"Yes."



He gestured towards the seats.



"Please sit."



Mingzhao sat beside Sun Qiming.



Across the table were Madam Liang, Old Madam Shen, Mingyuan, Mingxiu, Mr Liang, Master Luo, and Steward Zhou.



Mr Chen, the family's former legal adviser, sat near the Marquis.



The meeting had been arranged as a preliminary review of property classifications.



It was not the final division.



Mingzhao had made that distinction in her written reply.



Mr Liang opened the meeting.



"We are gathered to examine the eight documents circulated in advance. The purpose is to establish which matters can be agreed upon and which require further evidence."



He looked around the table.



"Is everyone in agreement?"



Madam Liang nodded.



Mingyuan nodded.



Mingxiu nodded.



Shen Guowei nodded.



Mingzhao said, "Yes."



Mr Liang turned to the first document.

The first document concerned the western agricultural field.



The register listed it as an active lease.



The lease itself had expired the previous year.



The tenant had continued cultivating the field.



Steward Zhou explained that the tenant had paid rent in the spring.



"Was the payment accepted?" Sun Qiming asked.



"Yes."



"Was a new lease signed?"



"No."



"Was the rent accepted under the old terms?"



Steward Zhou hesitated.



"The steward responsible at the time accepted it."



"Do we have a receipt?"



"Yes."



Sun examined the receipt.



It recorded payment for the spring cultivation period.



It did not identify whether the payment constituted renewal of the old lease or a temporary cultivation arrangement.



Mingzhao looked at the register.



"The field should not be classified as an active lease until the arrangement is established."



Madam Liang frowned.



"But the tenant is still there."



"Physical occupation and a valid lease are not the same thing."



"Then what should the register say?"



"Occupied and cultivated under an arrangement requiring confirmation."



Mr Chen considered the wording.



"That is cautious."



"It is accurate."



Mingyuan wrote the proposed classification.



Shen Guowei looked at his son.



"Can the tenant be asked?"



"Yes, Father. Steward Zhou has already sent a message."



"Good."



The first document was marked:



*Current occupation confirmed. Contractual status unresolved.*



Mr Liang signed the meeting note.



No one objected.



The first matter had been completed in less than half an hour.



Madam Liang appeared disappointed that it had not required a larger argument.

The second document concerned the southern agricultural building.



The estate register described it as usable.



A recent maintenance note stated that the roof required repair.



Master Luo examined the valuation.



"The structure is sound. The roof has several damaged sections. The building can be used after repair, but it should not be described as ready for immediate occupation."



"How much will the repair cost?" Mingyuan asked.



"The estimate is not complete. The carpenter has inspected the roof, but the timber requirement has not been calculated."



"Then the valuation is incomplete?"



"The structural assessment is complete. The repair estimate is not."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Those should be recorded separately."



Madam Liang looked towards her.



"Must everything be separated?"



Sun Qiming answered before Mingzhao could.



"Property value, repair cost, and current usability are different questions."



Madam Liang turned towards him.



"Surely the family can understand that a building needs repairs."



"Yes, Madam. The records should make that clear."



Mingzhao remained silent.



The meeting note was amended.



The building was classified as structurally sound but requiring repairs before ordinary use.



The repair estimate remained pending.



The second document was closed.

The third document concerned the northern warehouse.



Master Luo presented the building valuation.



The warehouse structure was sound.



The northern shelving required repairs.



The rear drainage needed attention.



The building valuation excluded grain stock, equipment, and unresolved inventory.



Sun Qiming explained that the separation was necessary because the value of the building could be established independently of the missing grain.



Mingyuan placed the old warehouse register on the table.



"The original stock discrepancy was forty-seven sacks."



He referred to the movement instruction.



"Twenty sacks were authorized for transfer to southern storage."



He opened the southern ledger.



"Eighteen were confirmed as received."



He looked towards Sun.



"Two remain unconfirmed."



Sun nodded.



"And the remaining twenty-seven?"



"Unexplained."



Madam Liang looked at Mingzhao.



"Could you not tell us where they went? You managed the warehouse."



Mingzhao met her gaze.



"No."



The room became quiet.



Madam Liang frowned.



"You were responsible for the estate accounts."



"I supervised the accounts. I did not personally transport every sack of grain."



"But you should know."



"I know what the records establish."



She pointed towards the documents.



"They establish the original discrepancy, the transfer instruction, and the confirmed receipt of eighteen sacks. They do not establish the destination of the remaining twenty-seven."



Mingyuan looked down at the register.



He had once wanted his sister to provide an answer.



Now he understood why she could not.



Mr Liang intervened.



"The warehouse investigation is a separate matter. It should continue under the current estate management."



Shen Guowei nodded.



"Agreed."



The third document was marked:



*Building valuation established. Grain inventory investigation ongoing.*



No one had found the missing grain.



The meeting had nevertheless made progress.

The fourth document concerned the estate's agricultural holdings.



The properties had been divided into categories:

- Confirmed estate-owned land.
- Land under current cultivation.
- Land with expired or incomplete lease records.
- Fallow land requiring restoration.
- Buildings requiring maintenance.
- Property with disputed boundaries.

Master Luo explained the valuation method.



The land had been assessed according to recorded ownership, current use, and available inspection evidence.



Where documents were incomplete, the valuation had been marked provisional.



Mingzhao examined the list.



"Does the valuation include the tenant's crops?"



"No," Master Luo replied. "The crops are not estate property unless the agreement establishes otherwise."



"Does it include outstanding rent?"



"No. Rent receivables are listed separately."



"Good."



Mingxiu leaned forward.



"Why are the buildings listed separately from the land?"



"Because the condition of a building affects its value and maintenance obligations," Master Luo explained.



Mingxiu nodded.



She had begun to understand that a property was not simply a number written in an estate register.



It consisted of land, buildings, rights, obligations, tenants, repairs, and sometimes arguments that had lasted longer than the people involved.



The fourth document was accepted as a preliminary classification.



Several individual valuations remained subject to verification.

The fifth document concerned the northern canal property.



It was not part of the Marquisate's estate.



It had been under consideration as a possible purchase by Mingzhao.



Mr Liang had included it because the family wanted to know whether she intended to purchase property using funds that might be affected by the division.



Mingzhao objected immediately.



"The property is not owned by the Marquisate."



"That is understood," Mr Liang replied.



"Then it should not be included as an estate asset."



"It is included only as a possible personal acquisition."



"Then it should be recorded separately."



Mr Chen agreed.



"The distinction is necessary."



Master Luo explained the current findings.



The older land registration described the drainage channel as a boundary feature.



A later maintenance record showed that several nearby properties had participated in repairs and widening work approximately twelve years earlier.



The exact extent of the work on each parcel was not established.



The neighbouring registration had not yet been obtained.



The old stone boundary marker was missing.



The current channel extended beyond the line described in the older registration.



Mingzhao listened.



"Has the land office provided the original work order?"



"Not yet."



"Has Mr Qian's registration been obtained?"



"Not yet."



"Then the boundary remains disputed."



Master Luo nodded.



Mingzhao closed the report.



"I will not make a purchase decision until the boundary and repair obligations are established."



Mr Zhou, the owner, had not attended the meeting.



Mr Liang looked towards Mingzhao.



"Would you still consider purchasing it?"



"Possibly."



"Then should we record that?"



"Record that I am considering the property. Do not record it as an agreed purchase."



Mr Liang made the correction.



The fifth document was closed.



The house remained available.



The boundary remained uncertain.

The sixth document concerned maintenance expenses.



The old account included several payments that had been placed under general estate maintenance.



Sun Qiming had separated the supported expenses from those requiring additional evidence.



One payment of seventy-two taels related to repairs to Shen Mingyuan's study.



The original receipt had been found.



The authorization had been confirmed.



The carpenter had testified that the work was carried out.



The payment was correctly classified as estate maintenance.



The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels had no complete supporting records.



Madam Liang looked at the figures.



"Must we continue discussing this amount?"



Sun answered.



"Until the supporting records are found or the amount is classified according to the evidence available."



"Some of it may have been ordinary household expenditure."



"That is possible."



"Then why not classify it as such?"



"Because possibility is not proof."



Madam Liang looked dissatisfied.



Mingzhao did not intervene.



Mingyuan examined the documents.



"I will continue looking for the original receipts."



Shen Guowei nodded.



"Do so."



The sixth document was marked:



*Seventy-two taels supported and classified as estate maintenance. Two hundred and twenty-eight taels remain unverified.*

The seventh document concerned personal advances made by Mingzhao to the family.



Sun Qiming presented the current review.



Of twenty-three uncertain entries:

- Nine were confirmed as advances.
- Four had evidence that money was paid, but the repayment arrangement remained unclear.
- Ten remained uncertain.

The nine confirmed advances could be considered as personal claims in the final settlement.



The four partially confirmed entries required further examination.



The ten uncertain entries could not yet be treated as established debts.



Mingzhao looked at the list.



"Has the evidence for the nine confirmed entries been attached?"



"Yes."



"Have the four partial entries been separated from the confirmed claims?"



"Yes."



"Then the classification is acceptable."



Madam Liang looked towards Shen Guowei.



"These sums were used for the family."



"Some were," Mingzhao said.



"Then surely they should be treated as family expenditure."



"If the records establish that they were family expenditure, they should be classified accordingly. If they were advances made from my personal funds, they should be treated as claims."



Madam Liang pressed her lips together.



Shen Guowei looked at the accountant.



"Will these claims be considered in the final division?"



"Yes," Sun replied. "Once the supporting documents and amounts are accepted, they can be incorporated into the settlement calculation."



Mingzhao nodded.



The seventh document was accepted as a preliminary classification.



The final amounts remained subject to verification.

The eighth document concerned the remaining property division.



Mr Liang explained that the family could not yet calculate each person's final share.



The estate valuation was incomplete.



Some liabilities remained unresolved.



The personal advances required final confirmation.



The agricultural leases needed clarification.



The warehouse investigation was ongoing.



Mingzhao listened.



Then she asked, "Has the family prepared a complete list of assets and liabilities?"



Mr Liang looked towards Steward Zhou.



Steward Zhou opened his ledger.



"Most of the known assets have been listed."



"Most?"



"Several smaller holdings still require confirmation."



"Have the family debts been listed separately from the household expenses?"



"Some have."



"Some?"



Steward Zhou looked uncomfortable.



Mingyuan spoke.



"We are preparing a revised list."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then the final division cannot yet be calculated."



Mr Liang agreed.



The eighth document was marked:



*Final division pending completion of asset, liability, and claim verification.*



The meeting had reached its conclusion.



No one had received a final settlement.



No one had received a new property title.



No one had received the missing grain.



But eight documents had been examined, several classifications had been agreed, and the unresolved matters had been identified.



For a preliminary meeting, it was a respectable result.

Madam Liang was less satisfied.



After the formal meeting ended, she asked Mingzhao to remain.



Shen Guowei, Mingyuan, Mingxiu, and Old Madam Shen stayed as well.



Sun Qiming began gathering his papers.



Mingzhao looked towards him.



"Please remain. If any additional claims are discussed, I want the records available."



Sun nodded and sat down again.



Madam Liang looked at her daughter.



"You have become very formal."



Mingzhao replied calmly.



"The meeting concerned property."



"You are still part of this family."



"That is why I attended."



Madam Liang's expression tightened.



"You could make matters easier."



"Which matter?"



"Your brother is learning to manage the estate. Your sister is helping with the household. Your father has many responsibilities. You know how difficult everything has become."



Mingzhao looked at Mingyuan.



He did not look away.



Then she looked at Mingxiu.



Her younger sister remained quiet.



"I know the estate is difficult to manage," Mingzhao said. "That is why I insisted that the responsibilities be transferred before I left."



Madam Liang frowned.



"You could still advise them."



"I can answer questions about my own claims. I can explain records that concern my personal property. I will not resume ordinary estate management."



"Must you draw such a strict line?"



"Yes."



The answer was quiet.



Madam Liang turned towards Mingyuan.



"Your sister could help you."



Mingyuan took a breath.



"I can ask her about a specific record if it concerns her claims. But I should not ask her to manage the estate for me."



Madam Liang looked surprised.



Mingyuan continued.



"I am responsible for the estate now. If I do not learn, the same problems will remain."



Shen Guowei studied his son.



He did not praise him.



He simply nodded.



Mingxiu spoke next.



"I also need to learn."



Madam Liang looked at her.



"You are helping."



"Yes. But I still need to understand the records instead of asking Elder Sister to correct them."



The room became quiet.



Old Madam Shen, who had listened without interruption, finally spoke.



"Then learn."



Madam Liang turned towards her.



"Mother—"



"Your daughter has left the household management. Your son has inherited the responsibility. Your younger daughter is learning. What more is there to discuss?"



Madam Liang fell silent.



Old Madam Shen looked at Mingzhao.



"Your property claims will be settled according to the records."



Mingzhao bowed.



"Thank you, Grandmother."



The old woman nodded.



"Do not mistake my agreement for approval of every decision you have made."



"I do not."



"Good."



The discussion ended.

At the palace, Yuru received a letter from the Duke of Xu.



It confirmed the family dinner for the twelfth day.



Madam Xu had written a short note beneath it.



She had asked the kitchen to prepare Yuru's preferred dishes and had instructed the household not to make the dinner ceremonial.



Yuru smiled.



Fang Ru was arranging the garment inventory.



"Is the dinner confirmed?"



"Yes."



"Shall I prepare formal clothing?"



"No. Ordinary clothing."



Fang Ru nodded.



Then she showed Yuru the two robes whose ownership records remained unclear.



One had been made before Yuru entered the palace.



The other had been purchased from palace stores, but the original order had described it as a personal garment.



Yuru examined the records.



"Keep the first robe in my personal inventory. The second should remain pending until the original order is found."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Would you like to review the remaining garments?"



"No. The inventory officers can complete the ordinary list."



Fang Ru smiled.



The former Empress returned to her book.

The Emperor received the summary of the day's palace work.



The ceremonial inventory had been revised.



The two unidentified objects remained under investigation.



The shared reference number for ceremonial furnishings had been accepted by Palace Stores and the Department of Rites.



The Imperial Household Administration had agreed to use the same number in purchase and maintenance records.



One office had requested that the reference number be written in red ink.



Another had requested black ink.



The Emperor read the note twice.



"Why?"



Gao Shun answered carefully.



"The first office believes red ink will make the reference more visible. The second believes black ink will be easier to reproduce in ordinary records."



The Emperor put down the report.



"Use whichever ink is already used for the relevant register."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor leaned back.



"Are there any matters requiring my decision?"



"None in this report."



"Then send it to the appropriate offices."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He paused.



"Does the former Empress know about the ink disagreement?"



Gao Shun looked at him.



"Your Majesty, she has not been informed."



"Good."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor looked relieved.

At the Marquisate, the preliminary meeting record was copied before the participants departed.



Mingzhao read the final wording.



She checked that the nine confirmed personal advances were not combined with the four partial claims.



She checked that the two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.



She checked that the northern warehouse's forty-seven-sack discrepancy was not described as resolved.



She checked that the canal property was not listed as purchased.



She checked that the western field's expired lease was not treated as active.



Only after the corrections had been made did she sign the attendance record.



Shen Guowei watched her.



"Are you satisfied?"



"The meeting record accurately reflects what was discussed."



"That is not what I asked."



"It is the answer I can give."



He looked at the papers.



"Will you attend the next meeting?"



"If the family provides the documents in advance and the agenda concerns the property division or my claims."



He nodded.



"Very well."



Mingzhao stood.



Sun Qiming gathered his papers.



Qiao Yue picked up the document case.



The meeting was over.



The family still had much to do.

That evening, Mingyuan returned to the accounts room.



He placed the meeting record beside the agricultural register.



Mingxiu entered with a list of household purchases.



"Did the meeting go well?"



"Some matters were agreed."



"Did Elder Sister settle everything?"



"No."



Mingxiu looked at him.



He continued.



"She was not supposed to."



Mingxiu sat down.



"Then we have to finish the rest."



"Yes."



She opened her list.



"I found another discrepancy."



Mingyuan sighed.



"What is it?"



"The household lamp oil was entered under kitchen supplies in one account and under general maintenance in another."



"Is it the same purchase?"



"I don't know yet."



Mingyuan looked at the list.



"Then we should check the receipts."



Mingxiu smiled faintly.



"You sound like Mr Sun."



"That is not necessarily an insult."



"It was not intended as one."



They began comparing the records.



The two younger members of the Marquisate were learning to manage the household.



They were not yet good at it.



They were, however, beginning to understand that responsibility could not be inherited merely by receiving a title or a room full of account books.



It had to be exercised.

Mingzhao returned to her temporary residence after sunset.



She removed her outer robe and sat at her desk.



The preliminary meeting record was placed in the family claims file.



The silk partnership ledger remained separate.



The household ledger remained separate.



The property inquiry remained open.



The grain investigation remained open.



She looked at the eight documents.



They had not produced a final division.



But they had established something important.



The family could now distinguish between what it knew, what it suspected, and what it had not yet proved.



That was a beginning.



Qiao Yue brought tea.



"Was the meeting difficult?"



"Yes."



"Did anyone argue?"



"Yes."



"Did anyone shout?"



"No."



Qiao Yue looked disappointed.



"Then it was a very restrained family meeting."



Mingzhao took the tea.



"It was a preliminary property discussion."



"That sounds worse."



"It was."



Qiao Yue laughed.



Mingzhao opened her ledger.



The next stage would require further valuations, supporting documents, and calculations.



She would not hurry it.



A settlement that was quick but incorrect would not be a settlement.



It would simply be another account waiting to be corrected.



And she had already spent enough years correcting accounts that other people had not bothered to understand.
