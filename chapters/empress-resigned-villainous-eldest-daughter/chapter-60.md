# **Chapter 60: The Former Empress Has Returned to Her Natal Home for a Family Meal. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Revised Property Schedule. The Duke Has Been Forbidden to Add More Dishes. The Family Representative Has Been Forbidden to Add More Assumptions.**

**24th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



Duke Xu had decided that six dishes were insufficient.



He had reached this conclusion before breakfast.



Madam Xu had reached the opposite conclusion before the servants had finished preparing tea.



"The meal is for family," the Duke explained.



"That is why six dishes are enough."



"The children will be coming."



"They are adults."



"Yuru will be coming."



"Yuru has eaten six dishes before."



The Duke considered this.



"She may have been hungry."



Madam Xu put down her cup.



"Then she may eat more rice."



The Duke looked toward the kitchen steward.



The kitchen steward looked at the floor.



He had been working in the Xu household long enough to understand that a person could lose an argument by asking the kitchen to prepare an additional dish.



The Duke cleared his throat.



"Perhaps we should prepare soup."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Soup is already included."



"Then another soup."



"Why?"



The Duke did not answer.



He had reached the limits of his argument.



Madam Xu returned to her tea.



"Six dishes."



The kitchen steward bowed.



"Yes, Madam."



The Duke looked dissatisfied.



He had commanded armies of servants through several decades of household management.



He had negotiated with officials, supervised estates, and survived family disputes.



He was now being defeated by the number six.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received the revised property schedule.



It arrived in a wooden document case accompanied by a note from the family representative.



The note stated that several corrections had been made and that the family hoped the next meeting would proceed more efficiently.



Mingzhao opened the case.



The schedule was thicker than the previous version.



She looked at it.



Then she looked at Qiao Yue.



"Efficiently?"



Qiao Yue glanced at the document case.



"Perhaps they have used larger paper."



Mingzhao opened the first page.



The headings had been revised.



Some entries had been moved.



Several descriptions had been clarified.



There were also new annotations.



Mingzhao read them carefully.



One entry concerning her personal advances had been changed from *unverified* to *partially acknowledged*.



She marked it.



The classification was not accurate.



Nine advances were confirmed.



Four had payment evidence, but the repayment arrangements remained unclear.



Ten were uncertain.



The four could not be merged with the nine merely because the family had acknowledged that money had changed hands.



Mingzhao wrote a correction in the margin.



Another entry concerning the study repairs had been placed under family liabilities.



She marked that too.



The seventy-two taels were confirmed estate maintenance.



They were not a personal debt owed to her.



The additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.



She turned another page.



The grain discrepancy had been summarized as an unresolved warehouse matter.



That was acceptable.



However, the separate transport payment had been placed beneath the same heading.



Mingzhao drew a line between the two entries.



"These must remain separate."



Qiao Yue leaned forward.



"Because they may not be connected?"



"Because we have not established that they are connected."



Mingzhao continued reading.



The northern canal property had also appeared in a footnote.



She crossed it out.



"It is not part of the estate division."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Should we send the corrections now?"



"No."



Mingzhao closed the schedule.



"We will prepare a complete response. I will not send six corrections today and discover six more tomorrow."



Qiao Yue looked at the thick document case.



"Then we may need another document case."



Mingzhao glanced at her.



"Find one."

At the Imperial Palace, the Second Prince Zhao Chengyu had completed his revised gathering estimate.



The number of confirmed guests remained twelve.



The number of confirmed personal attendants was now recorded separately.



Additional provisions could be prepared if more guests confirmed attendance, but the request did not include those expenses as though they had already been incurred.



He placed the revised estimate before the Crown Prince.



"Read it."



The Crown Prince examined the document.



The first page was clear.



The second page was clearer.



The third page contained a note distinguishing confirmed arrangements from possible additions.



The Crown Prince looked up.



"You have corrected it."



"Yes."



"Good."



Chengyu waited.



The Crown Prince returned the paper.



"Approved for submission."



Chengyu took it.



"That is all?"



"What else did you expect?"



"Perhaps praise."



The Crown Prince considered this.



"The estimate is better."



Chengyu looked at him.



"That is not praise."



"It is an accurate assessment."



The Second Prince stood.



"I hope you never become responsible for arranging a banquet."



The Crown Prince returned to his papers.



"I hope you never become responsible for reconciling one."



Chengyu left.



The Crown Prince looked down at the document.



After a moment, he allowed himself a small smile.



His younger brother was learning.



He was also becoming more difficult to supervise.



The Crown Prince suspected that these developments were related.

In the afternoon, Shen Mingzhao visited the workshop.



The first group produced under the revised folding method had been completed.



The records showed a reduction in handling time.



The edge appearance remained consistent with the approved sample.



The two trainees had completed their assigned work under supervision.



One had improved in speed.



The other had improved in accuracy.



Neither had been allowed to work without inspection.



Mingzhao examined several finished bolts.



She checked the corners.



Then the edges.



Then the labels.



The senior finisher stood nearby.



"Miss Shen, the method is working."



"On this group."



"Yes."



"How many bolts?"



"Twenty."



Mingzhao looked at the record.



"Then record the result for twenty bolts."



The finisher nodded.



"Yes."



Lin Shuyun joined them.



"The workers are asking whether they can use the saved time to begin the next section."



"They may, provided the inspection is completed."



"It has been completed."



"Then they may proceed."



Lin smiled.



"The workshop has gained some time."



Mingzhao handed her the record.



"The workshop has measured some time."



Lin accepted the paper.



"Must every encouraging statement be corrected?"



"Only the ones that become financial claims."



The two women looked at the finished bolts.



The new order was moving forward.



The buyer's deposit had been received.



The revised method had passed its first group.



The trainees were improving.



The existing forty-two-bolt group had been accepted and paid for, though its final production costs still needed reconciliation.



Nothing in the ledger yet established the new order's net profit.



Mingzhao was satisfied.



She did not need to be delighted.



The work was progressing.



That was enough.

At the Xu residence, Madam Xu received a message from her daughter.



Xu Yuru would arrive on the twenty-sixth.



She would bring Fang Ru.



She did not require a formal welcome.



Madam Xu read the message.



Then she gave it to Duke Xu.



He read it.



"She is bringing Fang Ru?"



"Yes."



"Then we should prepare another place setting."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Of course."



"And perhaps another dish."



"No."



The Duke sighed.



"Fang Ru will also eat."



"Then she will eat from the six dishes."



The Duke placed the message down.



He had lost another opportunity.



Madam Xu called the kitchen steward.



"Prepare the six dishes we discussed."



"Yes, Madam."



"And make certain the rice is sufficient."



The Duke looked pleased.



At least the rice had survived the negotiations.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received another report from Steward Zhou.



The western field tenant had begun preparing the soil under the temporary cultivation agreement.



The payment had been recorded separately.



The old lease remained expired.



Mingyuan checked the entry.



He found no mistake.



He signed the acknowledgment.



Then he asked whether the letter to the former warehouse steward had been answered.



"Not yet, Young Master."



"Send a reminder."



"Should it mention the missing grain?"



"Ask him to clarify the old recordkeeping method first."



Steward Zhou bowed.



Mingyuan looked at the papers.



The original count remained forty-seven sacks.



The movement instruction remained twenty.



Eighteen had been confirmed at southern storage.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



The old warehouse steward's letter had explained that some registers separated total stock from immediately available stock.



It had not explained the missing grain.



Mingyuan was beginning to understand the importance of preserving that distinction.



He had once believed that a family heir should be able to make decisions quickly.



Now he was learning that a family heir who made decisions without understanding the records could create expensive problems.



He looked at the steward.



"Has Father reviewed the revised schedule?"



"He has received it."



"Has he read it?"



The steward hesitated.



"I cannot confirm that."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Then I will ask him."



He rose.



The steward watched him leave.



The young master had not become an expert.



But he had begun asking questions before giving instructions.



This was a meaningful change.

That evening, Xu Yuru received a small package from the palace kitchens.



It contained a list of dishes that could be prepared for the following day's family visit.



The former Empress read the list.



Then she put it aside.



Fang Ru looked at her.



"Madam?"



"My mother has already planned the meal."



"Should we inform the kitchens?"



"No."



Xu Yuru opened her book.



"The palace kitchens do not need to know what my mother is cooking."



Fang Ru nodded.



The former Empress continued reading.



After a moment, she added, "And do not send the list to the Xu residence."



"Yes, Madam."



Xu Yuru turned a page.



She had once managed the palace's household provisions.



She knew the importance of meal arrangements.



She also knew that a family meal did not require a second household to supervise it.



The next day, she would go to her parents' home.



She would eat.



She would talk.



She would return.



The thought was simple.



She found herself looking forward to it.

Before the day ended, Mingzhao completed her corrections to the revised property schedule.



She had separated the confirmed advances from the uncertain ones.



She had restored the study repairs to estate maintenance.



She had removed the unrelated transport payment from the grain discrepancy.



She had excluded the northern canal property from the family division.



She had retained the distinction between the expired western field lease and the temporary cultivation agreement.



She had also attached a note stating that the family settlement remained incomplete.



Qiao Yue reviewed the pages.



"Is this everything?"



"For this version."



Qiao Yue looked at the stack.



"It is considerably longer than the previous one."



"Because the previous one was wrong."



"Will the family accept the corrections?"



Mingzhao closed the document case.



"That is not something I can decide."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao placed the case beside her writing table.



On the twenty-seventh, she would attend the meeting.



She would answer questions.



She would present the evidence.



She would not accept unsupported conclusions.



And if the family wanted her to resume managing the estate, they would have to ask directly.



She would not allow the request to be hidden beneath a discussion of property schedules.

In the palace, the Emperor received a short note from the Dowager.



She had enjoyed the garden visit.



She wished to see the former Empress again.



The Emperor read it.



Then he looked at the calendar.



The twenty-sixth was already reserved for Xu Yuru's family visit.



He did not send another invitation.



He placed the note aside.



For once, he allowed another person's time to remain their own.



At the Xu residence, Duke Xu was still considering the possibility of adding a second soup.



At the Marquisate, the family representative was still considering whether the next meeting could settle the property dispute.



At the eastern market, the workshop had completed its first twenty bolts under the revised method.



And in the palace, the Crown Prince had approved a gathering estimate without asking his brother to rewrite it again.



The next two days promised family meals, family arguments, and further attempts to make ordinary arrangements more complicated than necessary.



The former Empress had prepared her walking shoes for the visit.



The Marquis's eldest daughter had prepared her corrected schedule for the meeting.



Both had learned to bring only what was necessary.



Unfortunately, neither had been able to prevent other people from preparing considerably more.
