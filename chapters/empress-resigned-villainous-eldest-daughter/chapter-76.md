# **Chapter 76: The Former Empress Has Been Asked Whether She Enjoyed Her Father's Book and Has Not Been Asked to Review Its Accounts. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Possible Voucher Location and Has Refused to Treat a Possible Location as a Discovery. The Workshop Has Received Its Buyer and Has Learned That Forty-Eight Bolts Must Be Examined Individually.**

**10th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress Xu Yuru had finished the chapter about provincial granaries.



She had also marked two passages.



One concerned the distinction between grain entered into storage and grain available for immediate distribution.



The other concerned the danger of combining records from different storage categories merely to produce a single convenient total.



She placed a small paper marker between the pages.



Fang Ru entered with breakfast.



"Madam, your father has sent a message."



Yuru looked up.



"What does he say?"



"He asks whether you have begun reading the book."



Yuru smiled.



"Tell him I have."



"Would you like to mention the two passages you marked?"



"Yes. Tell him the book is interesting, and that I particularly liked the chapter about granary records."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Shall I tell him you have reached the middle?"



"No. I have not."



Fang Ru smiled.



"Then I will not."



Yuru picked up her tea.



Her father had apparently been waiting for a report on the book.



That was understandable. He had chosen it himself.



But she suspected he was also curious whether she would find it useful.



She would.



She simply did not intend to turn the book into a new administrative assignment.



"Has there been any message from the Dowager Empress?" Yuru asked.



"Only a greeting sent through the usual household channel."



"Then send my thanks."



"Yes, Madam."



Fang Ru arranged the breakfast dishes.



Yuru returned to the book.



The morning was quiet.



She had no official papers to review, no household dispute to settle, and no ceremonial schedule to approve.



She had a book, a cup of tea, and enough time to finish the chapter.



For the moment, that was quite sufficient.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, the accounts office had located a possible reference to the old payment voucher.



It was not the voucher itself.



A clerk had found a category index listing a bundle of general purchasing documents from the relevant period. The bundle number matched the filing system described by Chen Rui.



However, the index did not list individual voucher numbers.



It merely identified the bundle.



Mingyuan read the report.



Steward Zhou stood beside him.



"Does this mean the voucher is there?" Mingyuan asked.



"It means the bundle is likely to contain documents from the relevant category and period."



"That is not the same thing."



"No."



Mingyuan looked at the report again.



"Then we should ask the accounts office to examine the bundle."



"They have already begun."



"How many pages?"



"The bundle contains several sections. They are checking the index first."



Mingyuan nodded.



He was learning that even a search required an orderly process.



If the clerks removed documents without recording their original positions, they might make the records more difficult to reconstruct.



"Ask them to record the bundle's condition before opening it," he said.



Steward Zhou nodded.



"And make sure each document is returned to its original position after examination."



"Yes, Young Master."



Mingyuan placed the report beside the sixth cash book.



He had once expected the accounts office to produce an answer quickly.



Now he was more concerned that it produce the correct answer.



The difference was taking time to learn.



At the other table, Liang Wen was reviewing the consolidated purchasing register.



He had found an entry concerning a delivery of timber.



The original record stated that twenty beams had been ordered.



A separate delivery note recorded eighteen received.



A later maintenance account referred to twenty beams being used.



Liang Wen had marked the discrepancy.



"Steward Zhou?"



"What is it?"



"The timber entry."



Steward Zhou approached.



Liang Wen showed him the three records.



"The order was for twenty. The delivery note records eighteen. The maintenance account records twenty used."



Mingyuan looked up.



"Could the remaining two have been delivered separately?"



"Possibly," Steward Zhou replied.



"Is there a second delivery note?"



"Not in the pages we have examined."



Mingyuan looked at Liang Wen.



"Do not write that two beams are missing. Write that the order and use records each refer to twenty, while the delivery note confirms eighteen. The remaining two require supporting evidence."



Liang Wen wrote it down.



He then hesitated.



"Should I add that the maintenance account may have included timber already in storage?"



Steward Zhou considered the possibility.



"It may have."



"Then should I include it?"



"Put it in a separate note as a possible explanation, not as a confirmed fact."



Liang Wen nodded.



Mingyuan returned to his own papers.



The new register was not making the old records simpler.



It was making the differences visible.



That was a necessary first step.

Shen Mingzhao received a message from the Marquisate before noon.



Her brother had sent a brief account of the possible voucher bundle.



She read it while Qiao Yue arranged the morning correspondence.



"Have they found it?" Qiao Yue asked.



"No."



"Have they found where it may be?"



"Yes."



"That is progress."



"It is a location to search."



Qiao Yue looked at the letter.



"Must every useful development be described so cautiously?"



"Yes, if we want it to remain useful."



Mingzhao set the letter aside.



She did not want the family to declare the matter resolved merely because the accounts office had found a plausible bundle.



The original voucher might be there.



It might have been misfiled.



It might have been moved.



It might not exist at all.



Until the document was examined, none of those possibilities could be excluded.



She took up her brush.



*The identified bundle appears consistent with the filing category described by Chen Rui. Please have the accounts office record its condition and examine the contents in order. If the voucher is found, send a complete copy including any attached receipt, payment acknowledgment, or endorsement. The bundle reference alone does not establish the recipient or purpose.*



She paused.



Then she added:



*Please retain the original order of the documents.*



Qiao Yue read the letter.



"You have included the instruction about the original order."



"Yes."



"Will they think you distrust the accounts office?"



"I distrust careless handling of old records."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"That is different."



"Very."



Mingzhao sealed the letter.



She then returned to her own accounts.



The revised property schedule had not been finalized. The nine confirmed advances, four payment-confirmed but unresolved repayment arrangements, and ten uncertain claims remained separate.



The seventy-two taels in study repairs still required a decision on reimbursement.



The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



She did not intend to let the new voucher investigation obscure those matters.



Nor would she allow the family to use the existence of an unclear payment as a reason to reopen every settled classification.



The property division had to proceed on its own evidence.



The payment investigation had to proceed on its own evidence.



She wrote a short note to Sun Qiming asking him to preserve that distinction in the next revised schedule.



Then she closed the folder.



There was no need to examine the same pages all morning.

At the silk workshop, Zhou Jian arrived shortly after the midday meal.



He brought the buyer's inspection list and a small sample card.



Lin Shuyun received him at the main worktable.



"Manager Lin," he greeted her.



"Manager Zhou."



Mingzhao was present, but she did not interrupt their exchange.



Zhou Jian examined the inspection area.



The forty-eight bolts had been arranged in two groups of twenty-four.



Each group had a separate internal inspection sheet.



He looked at the records.



"These are the forty-eight prepared for today's inspection?"



"Yes," Lin Shuyun replied.



"Have all passed internal inspection?"



"Yes. Two required minor adjustments before passing."



"Are the adjustments recorded?"



"Here."



Zhou Jian examined the entries.



He then looked at the approved folding sample.



The original sample and the revised sample were displayed side by side.



"Which method was used for this group?"



"The revised method approved by Zhou Wenqi," Lin Shuyun said. "The written instruction is attached."



Zhou Jian checked the instruction.



He compared the sample with the first bolt.



Then he began the inspection.



He did not accept the entire group at once.



He examined each bolt individually, checking the edge, fold, tension, and surface.



The first six passed.



The seventh required closer examination.



He turned it under the light.



"Is this the original silk lot?" he asked.



Lin Shuyun checked the production record.



"Yes."



"Was it folded by the same worker as the previous bolt?"



"No. The work was divided among several finishers."



Zhou Jian examined the edge again.



"It is within the approved range."



He marked it accepted.



The inspection continued.



The workers stood at a distance, watching without interfering.



Mingzhao remained near the ledger.



She did not attempt to hurry the buyer.



The agreement had established inspection rights.



The buyer was entitled to use them.



The inspection was not a performance arranged to flatter the workshop.



It was part of the contract.



After some time, Zhou Jian reached the twenty-fourth bolt.



He checked it, then marked the first group complete.



The second group was brought forward.



The next twelve passed without difficulty.



The thirty-seventh required a minor correction.



Zhou Jian marked it for adjustment.



Lin Shuyun recorded the result.



The worker responsible took the bolt back to the finishing table.



The inspection continued on the remaining eleven.



All passed.



The corrected bolt was returned later and examined again.



It passed.



Zhou Jian closed the inspection sheet.



"Forty-eight accepted."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



"Thank you."



Zhou Jian signed the buyer's acceptance record.



Mingzhao examined the document.



The date, quantity, and inspection result were clear.



She compared it with the production ledger.



The total buyer-accepted quantity was now one hundred and forty-four.



The remaining fifty-six bolts had not yet been produced.



She checked the calculation.



Ninety-six previously accepted.



Forty-eight accepted today.



One hundred and forty-four in total.



Fifty-six remaining.



The numbers matched.



"Please give us a copy of the signed acceptance record," she said.



Zhou Jian handed it over.



"Of course."



Mingzhao placed it in the order file.



Lin Shuyun looked toward the workroom.



"The corrected bolt will be recorded separately."



"Good."



Zhou Jian glanced at the sample.



"The revised folding method is producing consistent results."



"Thank you," Lin Shuyun replied.



"However, I will still compare the next group against the approved sample."



"That is understood."



Zhou Jian packed his inspection papers.



He did not promise that the next inspection would be easier.



He did not praise the workshop excessively.



He simply confirmed the accepted quantity and departed.



After he had gone, Lin Shuyun looked at the signed record.



"One hundred and forty-four."



"Yes."



"Only fifty-six remain."



"Yes."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"We are close."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"We are closer."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



"Must you always correct the wording?"



"When the wording changes the meaning."



The workshop returned to work.



The remaining fifty-six bolts would require the same care as the previous groups.



The buyer had accepted the latest forty-eight.



That did not mean the next fifty-six would automatically pass.

In the palace, Third Princess Zhao Minglan was examining the old library catalogue.



She had not forgotten the unidentified book.



The book had remained on the table in the small reading room, with a paper marker indicating the page where the headings began.



She had asked the library attendants to check two other collections.



One had no matching title.



The other contained a similar subject but a different text.



Minglan was now comparing the opening pages.



A young attendant stood nearby.



"Your Highness, should we enter the book under the closest title?"



Minglan looked up.



"No."



"Even though the subject is similar?"



"Similarity is not identification."



The attendant bowed.



Minglan turned another page.



The text contained headings that did not match either catalogue entry.



The paper and binding were old, but the book had not been damaged beyond use.



She wrote a note.



*Title not yet identified. Subject appears related to household instruction, but contents do not match the two catalogue descriptions examined. Further comparison required.*



She paused.



Then she added:



*Do not assign a title based only on similarity of subject.*



The attendant looked at the note.



"Will the former Empress be asked to examine it?"



Minglan considered the question.



She could ask Yuru.



The former Empress had a strong memory and extensive knowledge of palace records.



But Minglan had already learned that Yuru's absence from the administrative office was deliberate.



The Princess did not want to turn every difficult question into a reason to call her back.



"I will continue with the catalogue first," she said.



The attendant bowed.



Minglan returned to the book.



She was beginning to enjoy the work.



It was slow.



It required patience.



And it did not become easier merely because she wanted an answer.

That afternoon, Yuru received a short message from the Dowager Empress.



It was a personal greeting.



The Dowager asked whether the visit to the Xu residence had gone well.



Yuru replied that the meal had been pleasant and that her father had chosen an interesting book for her.



The Dowager sent a brief response.



*Then he has chosen well.*



Yuru smiled.



Fang Ru, who had delivered the message, looked at her.



"Would you like to send the Dowager a description of the book?"



"Not unless she asks."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru returned to reading.



She had reached the section about grain reserves and the difficulty of recording goods that were held in different places for different purposes.



The author warned against adding reserved grain to immediately available grain without identifying the distinction.



Yuru marked the passage.



She did not send it to the palace.



The Crown Prince was already learning to make that distinction.



The Princess was learning to preserve uncertainty in the catalogue.



The household clerks were learning to keep their own records.



They did not need her to turn every page she read into an instruction.



She placed the book aside and looked out into the courtyard.



The afternoon was warm.



She decided to take a short walk before dinner.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received the buyer's inspection report from the workshop through the commercial correspondence file.



He examined the signed acceptance record.



The quantity was forty-eight.



The previous accepted quantity was ninety-six.



The new total was one hundred and forty-four.



He placed the record in the commercial file.



Steward Zhou looked at it.



"The order is progressing."



"Yes."



"Will the remaining fifty-six be ready soon?"



"The workshop has not given a final date."



"Then we should not enter a delivery date."



Mingyuan nodded.



He was no longer inclined to turn a production estimate into a confirmed schedule.



He then returned to the accounts office report.



The purchasing file bundle had not yet been fully examined.



The search was continuing.



Mingyuan looked at the two matters.



One had produced a confirmed result.



The other had produced a possible location.



He kept the records separate.

By evening, the palace had received no new administrative request for the former Empress.



Minglan had preserved the unidentified book's status rather than assigning it a convenient title.



The Crown Prince's accounts review had continued.



The Marquisate had not yet found the original payment voucher.



The workshop had secured acceptance for another forty-eight bolts.



And Mingzhao had added the signed buyer record to her commercial file.



The day's results were clear.



The workshop had one hundred and forty-four accepted bolts and fifty-six still to produce.



The old payment remained unresolved.



The former Empress had finished another chapter of her father's book.



The unidentified library volume remained unidentified.



Several people had resisted the temptation to call something complete before it was complete.



This was becoming a habit.



It was not yet universal.



But it was spreading.
