# **Chapter 11: The Emperor Has Asked for a List of Things the Empress Cannot Delegate. The Marquis Has Asked His Daughter to Stop Counting Things He Cannot Explain.**

**3rd Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Emperor Zhao Jingheng had developed a new method of dealing with administrative problems.

He asked for a list.

This method had produced excellent results when he wanted to know which offices were responsible for the palace's ordinary expenditure.

It had produced less excellent results when he asked for a list of things that could not be delegated.

By the third day of the fourth month, the requested list had arrived.

It was four pages long.

The emperor read the first page.

Then he looked at Wei Zhong.

"Who prepared this?"

"The Imperial Household Administration, Your Majesty."

"Did the empress review it?"

"No, Your Majesty. You instructed that the offices prepare their own assessment."

"Good."

He continued reading.

The first item concerned disputes involving the senior imperial household women.

The second concerned the education and welfare of imperial children.

The third concerned ceremonial precedence when the regulations did not provide a clear answer.

The fourth concerned the personal conduct of senior palace officials.

The fifth concerned the allocation of certain charitable funds.

The sixth concerned the appointment of senior attendants.

The list continued.

By the fourth page, the emperor had discovered that the administration considered almost every difficult decision impossible to delegate.

He put the document down.

"Do they believe the empress is the only person in the palace capable of making a judgment?"

Wei Zhong answered carefully.

"Some decisions require the authority of the empress. Others require her experience."

"Then distinguish them."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"And if the regulation already identifies the responsible office?"

"Then the office should exercise its authority."

"And if the matter requires the emperor's decision?"

"It should be submitted to Your Majesty."

The emperor looked at the list again.

"Why is the empress being asked to decide disputes about the appointment of senior attendants when the appointment regulation identifies the approving authority?"

Wei Zhong glanced at the relevant page.

"The regulation permits the empress to recommend candidates."

"Recommend. Not approve."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Then write that."

Wei Zhong made a note.

The emperor turned to the next item.

"Charitable funds?"

"The Imperial Household Administration believes that the empress's personal supervision ensures the funds are distributed appropriately."

"Who maintains the accounts?"

"The relevant office."

"Who verifies the recipients?"

"The charitable administration."

"Who inspects the accounts?"

"The financial office."

"Then what exactly is the empress supervising?"

Wei Zhong was silent.

The emperor looked at him.

"Do not tell me that the answer is tradition."

Wei Zhong lowered his head.

"No, Your Majesty."

The emperor pushed the list aside.

"Send it back. I want the offices to distinguish between legal authority, practical experience, and the habit of asking the empress because they do not wish to make a decision."

Wei Zhong bowed.

The emperor paused.

"Use more polite wording."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

At Chengming Palace, Yuru was reviewing the proposed transfer of the princes' household responsibilities when Fang Ru arrived with the returned list.

Yuru read the emperor's instruction.

Her expression did not change.

"That is reasonable."

Fang Ru looked relieved.

"The offices may object."

"They may."

"They may also claim that some matters cannot be handled without Your Majesty's personal involvement."

"Then they should explain why."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru turned to the next report.

It concerned the appointment of senior attendants.

"The appointment regulation is clear," she said. "I may recommend. The emperor approves."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Why does the administration say that the matter requires my personal supervision?"

"Because the office has historically waited for Your Majesty's recommendation before beginning the selection process."

Yuru looked at her.

"That is a procedure they created for convenience."

"Yes."

"Then they should begin the selection process before requesting a recommendation."

Fang Ru wrote it down.

Yuru continued.

"The charitable funds are different. I should receive a summary, not every individual request. If there is a serious dispute, it can be brought to me with the relevant records."

"That would reduce the number of documents."

"It should also make the decisions better."

Fang Ru collected the papers.

After a moment, she said, "Your Majesty, the Imperial Household Administration has requested a meeting."

"About what?"

"The transfer of responsibilities."

Yuru looked at the clock.

"Today?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Then arrange it."

Fang Ru bowed.

As she left, Yuru glanced at the four-page list.

She did not dislike the fact that the palace needed her judgment.

She disliked the assumption that her judgment must be requested before anyone else could begin thinking.

The meeting took place in a side hall of Chengming Palace.

Present were Fang Ru, two senior administrators, a representative from the Department of Rites, and a financial officer.

Yuru sat at the head of the table.

The senior administrator, Madam Zhao, opened the discussion.

"Your Majesty, the administration is concerned that the transfer of responsibilities may cause confusion."

"Which responsibilities?"

"Several matters involving the senior palace women and the imperial children."

"Please identify them."

Madam Zhao opened her folder.

"First, disputes concerning the allocation of residence attendants."

"Who has authority over ordinary staffing?"

"The relevant residence administrator."

"Then why is the matter brought to me?"

"In cases of disagreement between residences, Your Majesty has traditionally intervened."

"Does the regulation require that?"

Madam Zhao hesitated.

"Not explicitly."

"Then the first step should be mediation by the supervising administrator. If that fails, the matter should be submitted to the appropriate authority."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Second?"

"Disputes concerning ceremonial precedence."

"The Department of Rites should determine matters governed by ceremony regulations."

The representative from the Department of Rites bowed.

"Your Majesty, some disputes are not covered by existing regulations."

"Then the Department of Rites should provide a recommendation. The emperor decides matters requiring imperial judgment."

The representative hesitated.

"That may create additional work for the department."

Yuru looked at him.

"That is generally what happens when a department receives responsibility."

The financial officer lowered his head.

Madam Zhao continued.

"Third, the education of the imperial children."

Yuru looked at her.

"Which part?"

"The coordination of tutors, household schedules, and educational arrangements."

"Their tutors are appointed through established procedures. Their schedules are managed by the relevant household offices. What requires my personal involvement?"

"The children may have disputes or difficulties."

"Then the supervising adults should address them. Serious matters should be reported to their parents."

Madam Zhao hesitated.

"Your Majesty has often intervened personally."

"I have. That does not mean every matter requires my intervention."

There was a brief silence.

The senior administrator looked down at her papers.

"Your Majesty, we are concerned that without your oversight, the offices may make mistakes."

Yuru folded her hands.

"They will."

Madam Zhao looked up.

Yuru continued.

"Some mistakes will be small. Some will require correction. Some may reveal that a procedure is inadequate. The solution is not to ensure that no one makes a mistake. The solution is to establish how mistakes are identified and corrected."

The financial officer nodded slowly.

Yuru looked around the table.

"I do not intend to withdraw every form of supervision immediately. But I will not preserve an arrangement merely because it allows everyone to avoid responsibility."

The meeting continued for another hour.

By the end, the administrators had agreed to prepare separate procedures for staffing, ceremonial disputes, charitable accounts, and the reporting of serious matters concerning the imperial children.

The work was not complete.

But the four-page list had been reduced to a series of specific questions.

Yuru considered that a better use of paper.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao was facing a different kind of meeting.

Her father had requested that she review the household accounts with him.

He had also asked that she refrain from making any further demands until the family had discussed the matter privately.

Mingzhao had agreed to attend.

She had not agreed to refrain from asking questions.

Shen Guowei sat behind his desk.

Madam Liang was beside him.

Mr. Chen had also been invited.

Mingzhao placed her documents on the table.

Her father looked at the papers.

"Must you bring all of these?"

"They concern the accounts."

"We are having a family discussion."

"Then we can discuss the accounts."

Shen Guowei sighed.

"Your mother believes you are making the matter unnecessarily difficult."

Mingzhao looked at Madam Liang.

"Which part?"

Madam Liang's expression tightened.

"Your father has already agreed to an independent examination. The accountant is reviewing the records. We have not refused to give you what belongs to you."

"I have not said that you have."

"Then why do you keep treating the family as though it intends to cheat you?"

"I am asking for the documents needed to establish the settlement."

"Must everything be examined by an outside accountant?"

"If the settlement is to be accepted by both sides, yes."

Mr. Chen intervened.

"Perhaps we should discuss the immediate arrangements rather than repeat the same disagreement."

Mingzhao nodded.

"That would be useful."

Shen Guowei leaned forward.

"The household cannot function without someone managing the accounts. Your departure must be delayed until your brother is prepared."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"How long will that take?"

"That depends on how quickly he learns."

"Then the family should establish a training schedule."

Her father frowned.

"You are making this sound like an appointment."

"It is a responsibility. It should be transferred properly."

Madam Liang spoke.

"Your brother is the heir. He should not need to be taught how to manage his own family."

Mingzhao looked at her mother.

"Then he should already know how."

Madam Liang fell silent.

Shen Guowei rubbed his forehead.

"Your brother has other responsibilities."

"So did I."

"You have managed the household for six years. You cannot expect him to learn everything immediately."

"I do not. I expect the family to stop treating my continued management as the only possible solution."

Mr. Chen nodded.

"Miss Shen's proposal is reasonable. A transition period can be established without making her departure dependent on the completion of every household matter."

Shen Guowei looked at him.

"What do you suggest?"

"First, appoint a temporary household administrator. Second, transfer the current accounts and records. Third, assign the heir responsibility for reviewing the estate's ordinary operations. Fourth, identify unresolved matters and specify who will handle them."

Mingzhao added, "The temporary administrator should not be the same person responsible for examining the accounts."

Mr. Chen looked at her.

"That is sensible."

Her father seemed increasingly unhappy with the meeting.

"Who do you propose?"

Mingzhao took out a sheet.

"I have listed three possible candidates from the existing staff."

Shen Guowei stared at her.

"You have already considered this?"

"I have known for some time that I would not manage the household indefinitely."

She placed the list on the table.

The first candidate was Steward Zhou.

The second was a senior household manager who had previously supervised the eastern estate.

The third was an experienced administrator from the family's secondary residence.

Shen Guowei examined the names.

"Steward Zhou is too old."

"He is fifty-seven, not seventy-seven."

"He has managed the northern warehouse for years."

"He has also managed household operations and understands the estate's accounts."

"That is precisely why he should not be burdened with another responsibility."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"Then choose another candidate."

Her father stared at the list.

For the first time, he seemed to understand that she had not merely announced a desire to leave.

She had begun preparing for it.

Shen Mingyuan was summoned to the meeting near its end.

He entered with a guarded expression.

His father handed him the proposed transition schedule.

"Read this."

Mingyuan examined it.

The schedule divided the household responsibilities into four groups.

Daily operations.

Monthly accounts.

Estate maintenance.

Commercial and land arrangements.

His eyes moved down the page.

"Why are there so many responsibilities?"

Mingzhao answered before her father could.

"Because they have to be done."

"I know that."

"Then you should not be surprised."

Mingyuan looked at the schedule again.

"Who will handle the commercial contracts?"

"You will eventually oversee them. The current managers will continue their work during the transition."

"And the warehouse accounts?"

"The senior steward will supervise them until the examination is complete."

"And the household staff?"

"The temporary administrator."

Mingyuan put the paper down.

"This is more complicated than I expected."

Mingzhao looked at him.

"That is because you have not been doing it."

His face reddened.

Madam Liang spoke sharply.

"Mingzhao."

Mingzhao did not look away from her brother.

"I am not insulting him. I am explaining why a transition is necessary."

Mingyuan stared at the schedule.

After a moment, he asked, "What happens if I make a mistake?"

Mingzhao considered the question.

"Then it should be corrected."

"And if the mistake costs money?"

"Then the responsibility should be established."

He looked up.

"That is not very comforting."

"It is not intended to be."

Mr. Chen hid a small smile.

Shen Guowei finally spoke.

"You will begin by reviewing the household's ordinary expenditure with Steward Zhou. You will not authorize major transactions until the accounts are transferred."

Mingyuan nodded slowly.

He was not enthusiastic.

But he did not refuse.

Mingzhao watched him.

She had expected resistance.

She had not expected him to look so uncertain.

For years, the family had treated his future authority as an established fact.

Now that the authority was being divided into actual tasks, it looked less like a privilege and more like work.

At midday, Lin Shuyun sent another letter.

The merchant, Zhou Wenqi, had submitted a preliminary offer for two hundred bolts of silk.

He proposed a deposit of thirty percent, with the remaining payment due after inspection at the destination warehouse.

The price was within the partnership's acceptable range, but the delivery schedule was tight.

Mingzhao read the letter carefully.

Qiao Yue stood nearby.

"Is it a good offer?"

"It may be."

"Then why do you look unhappy?"

"The buyer wants the goods delivered within twelve days."

"Can the warehouse do that?"

"Two hundred bolts are ready for immediate delivery, but the finishing checks must be completed and the goods repacked."

"Is twelve days enough?"

"Only if the dyeing workshop finishes on time and the transport arrangements are confirmed."

Mingzhao looked at the payment terms.

"The deposit is acceptable. The balance is not."

"Why?"

"Payment after inspection at the destination warehouse gives the buyer control over the timing of the final payment. If the inspection is delayed, our money is delayed."

"Could we ask for payment upon delivery?"

"We can ask."

She took out a sheet of paper.

"Propose that the buyer inspect the goods before dispatch, or appoint an independent inspector. The remaining payment should be made upon delivery, subject to a short period for identifying genuine defects."

Qiao Yue nodded.

"Will he agree?"

"That is his decision."

Mingzhao wrote the reply.

She did not want to sell merely because the partnership needed cash.

She wanted to sell under terms that would not create another problem.

After sealing the letter, she looked at the account book.

The partnership had several outstanding receivables.

One merchant had delayed payment.

Another had requested a reduction because a shipment had arrived late.

The proposed sale would improve cash flow, but it would not eliminate the partnership's existing obligations.

She added a note to review the receivables.

Independence did not mean that every problem could be solved by having one's own name on a contract.

It meant that the problems would arrive at one's own door.

In the imperial palace, the Empress Dowager had received the latest summary of the resignation review.

She read the report with a thoughtful expression.

The number of responsibilities had not changed.

But several matters had been assigned to existing offices, while others were being separated into defined procedures.

She called her senior attendant.

"Has the emperor spoken to the empress?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Has he agreed to her petition?"

"Not yet."

The Empress Dowager put down the report.

"Of course not."

The attendant waited.

The older woman looked toward the window.

"When I was empress, the palace had fewer offices and fewer written procedures. We relied more heavily on the judgment of the people in charge."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"That was not always a good arrangement."

The attendant lowered her head.

The Empress Dowager picked up the report again.

"Xu Yuru has been doing too much."

The attendant did not respond.

"That does not mean she should be allowed to abandon her responsibilities without a plan."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"But the plan is taking shape."

The Empress Dowager looked at the final page.

She had not agreed with Yuru's decision.

She had also not dismissed it.

For now, she would wait.

That evening, the emperor received the revised responsibilities list.

The four-page document had become a twelve-page report.

The emperor read it slowly.

Some matters were now assigned to the Department of Rites.

Some remained under the Imperial Household Administration.

Some required his approval.

Others could be handled by senior administrators without being submitted to the empress.

He turned to Wei Zhong.

"How many remain under the empress's direct authority?"

"Twelve, Your Majesty."

"How many require her recommendation?"

"Eight."

"How many require only that she be informed?"

"Twenty-one."

The emperor looked at the figures.

"And the rest?"

"Have been assigned to existing offices or are under review."

He nodded.

"Better."

Wei Zhong hesitated.

"Your Majesty, the empress has requested that the transition review include a list of responsibilities that should not be transferred to any single person."

The emperor looked up.

"What does that mean?"

"She believes some duties should be divided between offices rather than assigned to a successor."

The emperor considered this.

"That is sensible."

He returned to the report.

"How long will the review take?"

"At least several weeks."

The emperor looked toward the desk.

Several weeks.

He had been hoping the matter would be resolved quickly.

He was beginning to understand that he had spent years allowing a complicated system to depend on one person, and that undoing the arrangement would require more than signing a document.

He picked up his brush.

"Continue the review."

Wei Zhong bowed.

The emperor added, "And do not ask the empress to supervise the review of her own departure."

Wei Zhong lowered his head.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingzhao was reviewing the proposed transition schedule one final time.

Her father had not yet approved the temporary administrator.

Her brother had agreed to begin reviewing household expenditure.

The accountant was continuing his examination.

The northern canal property was still under inspection.

The silk partnership had received a preliminary offer but had not accepted it.

Nothing had been settled.

Yet the family had finally begun discussing who would perform the work after she left.

Mingzhao placed the schedule in a folder.

Qiao Yue entered.

"Miss, the kitchen has asked whether you will be eating with the family tomorrow."

Mingzhao looked up.

"Why?"

"They say the meal arrangements may change during the transition."

Mingzhao stared at her.

"Have they begun dividing the food?"

"Not exactly."

"Then tell them to continue the existing arrangements."

"Yes, Miss."

Qiao Yue hesitated.

"Miss, are you worried about leaving?"

Mingzhao considered the question.

"Yes."

Qiao Yue seemed surprised.

"You are?"

"Do you believe I have never considered the cost?"

"I thought you had already decided."

"I have."

"Then why worry?"

"Because deciding to leave and knowing what happens afterward are different things."

Qiao Yue nodded slowly.

Mingzhao closed the folder.

"I do not know whether the house will be suitable. I do not know whether the settlement will be generous. I do not know whether the partnership will make enough money this year. I do not know whether the family will continue to treat every problem as my responsibility."

She paused.

"But I know I do not want to continue living as though none of those questions belong to me."

Qiao Yue looked at her.

"Then we will prepare."

Mingzhao nodded.

"Yes."

She turned off the lamp.

In the imperial palace, Yuru was preparing to retire for the night.

The revised list lay on her desk.

She had not completed the review.

She had not received an answer to her petition.

The emperor had not agreed to her resignation.

But the palace was beginning to distinguish between the authority of the empress and the habit of sending everything to her.

That distinction mattered.

Yuru placed the report in a drawer.

For the first time, she did not feel compelled to read the same page again.

The palace could wait until morning.

It would have to learn.

And if it could not, then the problem would no longer be hers to conceal.

**End of Chapter 11.**
