# **Chapter 21: The Empress Has Been Given a Date for Her Departure. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Given Another List of Things She Cannot Take.**



**13th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The palace had received an imperial order.



The Imperial Household Administration had received a copy.



The Department of Rites had received a copy.



The Ministry of Personnel had received a copy.



The Imperial Archives had received a copy.



The Empress had received a copy.



By the middle of the morning, the Emperor had received a request asking whether the Imperial Household Administration should also prepare a copy for the office responsible for maintaining copies of imperial orders.



Zhao Jingheng read the request.



Then he placed it facedown.



"Who sent this?"



Gao Shun examined the document.



"The Imperial Household Administration, Your Majesty."



"Who maintains the copies of imperial orders?"



"The Imperial Archives."



"Then why does the administration need another copy?"



Gao Shun hesitated.



"They wish to ensure that the transition records remain complete."



"The transition records already contain the order."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then the record is complete."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor looked at the request again.



"Tell them that if they make another copy, they must explain which office is responsible for maintaining it."



Gao Shun accepted the paper.



He had begun to understand that the Emperor's patience with unnecessary documents was diminishing.



Unfortunately, the palace's enthusiasm for producing them had not diminished at the same rate.



At Chengming Palace, Yuru was receiving a different kind of document.



The Department of Rites had submitted a proposed ceremonial schedule for the formal completion of her resignation.



It included the necessary announcements, the transfer of ceremonial responsibilities, the return of certain official seals, and the arrangements for her departure from the principal Empress's residence.



Yuru read it carefully.



Fang Ru stood beside her.



"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites proposes that the formal ceremony take place on the twenty-third day of the fourth month."



Yuru looked at the date.



"Ten days from now."



"Yes."



"Why that date?"



"The department believes the preparations can be completed by then. It also allows the necessary notices to be sent to the imperial family and senior officials."



Yuru examined the schedule.



The proposed date was reasonable.



She turned to the next page.



"What is this?"



Fang Ru looked.



"An additional reception following the ceremony."



"Who proposed it?"



"The Department of Rites."



"Why?"



"They believe it would be appropriate for senior members of the imperial family to attend."



Yuru read the description.



The reception included a formal meal, ceremonial gifts, and a presentation of acknowledgments to the departing Empress.



She put down the document.



"I do not want a reception."



Fang Ru looked surprised.



"Your Majesty?"



"The ceremony is necessary. The reception is not."



"The Department of Rites believes it would demonstrate respect."



"Then they may demonstrate respect by completing the ceremony properly."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



"Should I return the proposal?"



"Yes. Ask them to distinguish the required ceremonies from additional celebrations."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru looked again at the date.



The twenty-third day.



Ten days remained.



For the first time, the departure was no longer an uncertain possibility.



It had a date.



She looked toward the garden.



Ten days were not long.



They were also longer than she had expected.



The Crown Prince arrived shortly before noon.



He had heard about the proposed date.



"Mother, is it true that the ceremony will take place on the twenty-third day?"



"If the preparations are completed."



Cheng'an bowed.



"Will the palace remain as it is afterward?"



Yuru considered the question.



"The buildings will remain. The offices will remain. The people will remain."



"I meant the arrangements."



"Some will change. Others will not."



The Crown Prince looked thoughtful.



"Will the new Empress's household be established immediately?"



Yuru looked at him.



"The Emperor has not announced any appointment."



Cheng'an lowered his eyes.



"I did not mean to assume."



"It is sensible to ask what arrangements may be needed. It is not sensible to treat an appointment as though it has already been made."



"Yes, Mother."



She continued.



"The palace must be able to function during the period between my departure and any future appointment. The Imperial Household Administration should not wait for another person to take my place before carrying out its duties."



Cheng'an nodded.



He had understood that the palace needed to become less dependent on one person.



He had not yet fully considered how much the departure of that person would affect the imperial family itself.



"Will you still advise me?" he asked.



"If you ask me something I know, I will answer."



"Even after you leave?"



"Yes."



He looked relieved.



Yuru smiled faintly.



"But I will not examine your household accounts for you."



Cheng'an accepted this.



"I understand."



"Do you?"



"Yes."



She raised an eyebrow.



He corrected himself.



"I will try to understand."



"That is a more honest answer."



At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the question of what Mingzhao could take had become considerably more complicated.



The independent accountant had completed the preliminary inventory of her personal belongings.



The list included clothing, books, writing materials, jewellery, personal ornaments, and several pieces of furniture.



There were also items that had been purchased by the family but had been used in her private rooms.



Those required clarification.



Mingzhao sat with Sun Qiming, Mr. Chen, and Steward Zhou.



The accountant placed two lists on the table.



"The first list contains items supported by your personal purchase records or inherited-property documents."



Mingzhao examined it.



The second list contained items that had been assigned to her rooms by the family estate.



She looked through it.



"These shelves were purchased for the eastern study."



"Yes," Steward Zhou replied.



"And the writing desk?"



"That was purchased by the Marquisate."



"The embroidered screen?"



"Madam Liang arranged it."



Mingzhao looked at the screen's entry.



"Then it does not belong to me."



Mr. Chen nodded.



"Correct. It should remain with the estate unless the family agrees otherwise."



Mingzhao continued reading.



Several books had been purchased from her personal funds.



Others had been presented to her by relatives.



One set had been purchased for the family library but kept in her study because she used it frequently.



She marked the entries.



"These books belong to the family library."



Sun Qiming nodded.



"And these?"



"Personal purchases."



He made a note.



Mingzhao turned another page.



There were several pieces of jewellery.



Some were personal gifts.



Others had been presented during family ceremonies and were traditionally treated as family property until formally assigned.



Mingzhao examined the records.



"This bracelet was given to me by my maternal grandmother."



"Is there a record?" Mr. Chen asked.



"Yes. My mother's family sent a letter when it was presented."



"Then it can be listed as an inherited personal gift, subject to confirmation."



Mingzhao nodded.



She was not interested in taking things simply because they had been kept in her rooms.



She wanted what belonged to her.



Nothing more.



Nothing less.



Sun Qiming closed the first list.



"The inventory can be completed after the remaining ownership questions are confirmed."



Mingzhao looked at the second list.



"Then the disputed items should remain separate."



"Yes."



Steward Zhou hesitated.



"Young Miss, there is also the matter of the servants assigned to your rooms."



Mingzhao looked up.



"What about them?"



"Some have asked whether they will accompany you."



Mingzhao was silent for a moment.



"Have they requested a transfer voluntarily?"



"Several have."



"Then prepare their names and current obligations. I will not take anyone who is unwilling."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"And those who do not wish to leave?"



"They remain here."



Mingzhao looked at the list.



"Do not make anyone choose before they know what the arrangements will be."



Steward Zhou accepted the instruction.



Mingzhao was leaving.



She would not turn her departure into another occasion for the household to decide that everyone else must follow her.



The family property meeting began after midday.



Shen Guowei had requested the presence of the clan representative, Mr. Liang.



The independent accountant was also present.



Madam Liang, Shen Mingyuan, and Shen Mingxiu attended.



Old Madam Shen listened from her usual seat.



Mr. Liang examined the preliminary property schedule.



"The family must distinguish between the estate property and the eldest daughter's personal property."



Mingzhao nodded.



"That has already been agreed."



"The difficulty is determining which assets belong to which category."



"That is why the examination is continuing."



Shen Guowei looked at the accountant.



"How long will it take?"



Sun Qiming answered carefully.



"That depends on the completeness of the records and the availability of the necessary valuations."



"Can you give an estimate?"



"I can provide an estimate for each category. I cannot promise that every disputed entry will be resolved within the same period."



Shen Guowei frowned.



"Then provide the estimate."



Sun Qiming opened his papers.



"The personal-property inventory can be completed within several days, subject to confirmation of disputed items. The commercial accounts require additional examination because some receivables remain outstanding. The family estate requires an independent valuation of land, buildings, stock, and liabilities."



Mr. Liang nodded.



"And the advances made by Miss Mingzhao to the household?"



"Those require payment evidence and confirmation of the circumstances under which the funds were provided."



Mingzhao looked at her father.



"Some of the records are in the estate's own ledgers."



Shen Guowei nodded reluctantly.



"Those will be made available."



The accountant continued.



"The northern warehouse discrepancy remains unresolved. The original difference was forty-seven sacks. Eighteen are confirmed in the southern storage ledger. Two are covered by a movement instruction but lack confirmed receipt. Twenty-seven remain unexplained."



Mr. Liang looked at Shen Guowei.



"Is there evidence of theft?"



Sun Qiming answered immediately.



"No conclusion has been reached."



"Then do not describe it as theft."



"That is correct."



Mingzhao looked at the clan representative.



The old man had been prepared to treat the discrepancy as a family scandal.



At least he was willing to distinguish an unresolved record from an accusation.



Mr. Liang turned to the three hundred taels.



"The same principle applies?"



"Yes," Sun Qiming said. "Seventy-two taels are supported by the payment ledger and testimony concerning repairs, but the original work order and receipt remain missing. The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels are not yet explained."



Shen Mingyuan looked uncomfortable.



Mingzhao did not look at him.



The accountant continued.



"Neither amount should be treated as a final personal liability until the evidence is complete."



The room became quiet.



Shen Guowei finally nodded.



"Proceed with the examination."



Mingzhao looked at the schedule.



The property division had not become easier.



But the family was beginning to accept that evidence had to come before conclusions.



After the meeting, Shen Mingxiu approached her elder sister.



"Second Sister."



Mingzhao stopped.



"What is it?"



Mingxiu held a small document.



"I have completed the list of staff who have asked about transferring with you."



Mingzhao accepted it.



There were six names.



The list included their current positions, years of service, and household obligations.



Mingzhao read it.



"Have they all confirmed voluntarily?"



"Yes. I asked them separately."



"Have you explained that their wages and duties may change?"



"I told them the arrangements have not been finalized."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Good. Do not promise them anything until I have established my own household budget."



Mingxiu hesitated.



"Second Sister, may I ask something?"



"Ask."



"Are you angry with the family?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"That is a broad question."



Mingxiu lowered her eyes.



"I mean, are you leaving because you dislike us?"



Mingzhao considered her younger sister.



The question was not entirely innocent.



Mingxiu had benefited from the arrangements that had burdened Mingzhao.



She had also been young when those arrangements became normal.



Mingzhao did not intend to pretend that the matter had no consequences.



"I am leaving because I want to manage my own life."



Mingxiu looked at her.



"That is all?"



"No. There are many things I am unhappy about. But I am not leaving to punish you."



Mingxiu's shoulders relaxed slightly.



Mingzhao continued.



"You will have your own responsibilities here. Do not spend your time wondering whether I am angry. Learn to manage them properly."



Mingxiu nodded.



"Yes, Second Sister."



She hesitated again.



"Will you visit?"



"If I have reason to."



Mingxiu gave a small, helpless smile.



"That is not a very warm answer."



"It is an honest one."



Mingxiu accepted it.



She left with the understanding that her sister's departure was not a performance designed to make the family regret its treatment of her.



It was a decision.



In the western commercial district, Lin Shuyun received the signed silk contract from Zhou Wenqi.



She examined the seal.



The terms were unchanged.



The inspection date had been confirmed.



The delivery arrangement was clear.



The buyer had also transferred the deposit.



Lin Shuyun sent a messenger to inform Mingzhao.



The sale was now formally underway.



The deposit had been received.



The remaining payment would be due upon delivery.



The stock could be reserved.



Lin Shuyun did not celebrate immediately.



She checked the amount.



Then she checked the contract.



Then she checked the warehouse allocation.



Only after all three matched did she allow herself to smile.



Her business partner had been right to insist on the revised terms.



But the order would still require careful execution.



She wrote to Mingzhao with the details.



Mingzhao received the letter late in the afternoon.



Qiao Yue brought it to her.



"The buyer has signed."



Mingzhao read the letter.



"And the deposit?"



"Received."



Mingzhao checked the amount against the contract.



It matched.



She put the letter aside.



"Good. Tell Lin Shuyun to reserve the agreed stock and arrange the inspection."



Qiao Yue smiled.



"That is good news."



"It is."



"Will the household budget be easier now?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"The partnership's funds are not the same as my personal household funds."



Qiao Yue's smile faded.



Mingzhao continued.



"The partnership must pay its own obligations, reserve operating capital, and divide profits according to the agreement. I cannot treat the entire deposit as money available for my residence."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"I understand."



Mingzhao examined the contract again.



The sale was an achievement.



It was not a personal fortune.



She would not confuse the two.



At Chengming Palace, Yuru received the revised ceremonial schedule.



The Department of Rites had removed the proposed reception.



The formal ceremony would take place on the twenty-third day.



The Empress would return the official seals and complete the necessary ceremonial acknowledgments.



The imperial family would be informed.



The transition of the remaining administrative duties would be completed before that date, except for responsibilities requiring ongoing review by the relevant offices.



Yuru examined the schedule.



Fang Ru waited.



"Is there anything Your Majesty wishes to change?"



Yuru shook her head.



"No."



Fang Ru looked at the date.



"Ten days."



"Yes."



"Does Your Majesty feel relieved?"



Yuru considered the question.



"Somewhat."



"And otherwise?"



"Tired."



Fang Ru smiled gently.



"That is understandable."



Yuru looked at her.



"Do you intend to remain in the palace?"



Fang Ru was taken aback.



"Your Majesty?"



"I am asking whether you intend to remain here after I leave."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



"I have served in Chengming Palace for many years. I have not yet decided."



Yuru nodded.



"Then you should decide according to your own wishes and circumstances."



Fang Ru looked at her.



"Your Majesty, I thought—"



"That you must follow me?"



Fang Ru did not answer.



Yuru continued.



"You have your own responsibilities, your own family arrangements, and your own future. I will not decide them for you."



Fang Ru bowed.



For years, she had believed that serving the Empress meant remaining wherever the Empress went.



Now she was being told that her own life was not an extension of Yuru's.



It was an unfamiliar freedom.



She would need time to consider it.



That evening, the Emperor received the final ceremonial schedule.



He approved the twenty-third day as the date of the formal ceremony.



Gao Shun placed the document beside the imperial order.



"Your Majesty, should the imperial family be informed tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"And the senior officials?"



"Only those whose attendance is required."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor looked toward the window.



Ten days remained.



He had accepted the Empress's resignation.



He had approved the arrangements.



He had also discovered that accepting a decision did not make its consequences easier.



Yuru would leave Chengming Palace.



The palace would continue.



The Crown Prince would inherit more responsibility.



The Dowager would have to adjust.



The imperial household would need to function without the person who had quietly made so many of its arrangements possible.



He wondered whether he would become accustomed to it.



Then he remembered her answer from the previous day.



She wanted to sleep late.



He almost smiled.



Perhaps that was a reasonable beginning.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao completed the first version of her independent household budget.



The figures were not comfortable.



The residence would require rent or purchase expenditure.



Servants would require wages.



Food, fuel, clothing, repairs, transport, and household supplies would all need to be paid for.



Her commercial income was not entirely liquid.



The silk partnership had received a deposit, but most of the sale proceeds remained payable after delivery.



Her other business interests had outstanding receivables.



She had funds of her own.



She also had expenses.



Mingzhao examined the budget.



Then she removed an unnecessary household carriage.



Qiao Yue looked at the revised list.



"Miss, will you not need a carriage?"



"I will need transport. I do not need an expensive carriage immediately."



"But the family has always used one."



"The family has more carriages than I need."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao removed another proposed expense.



She would not purchase expensive furnishings before the residence was selected.



She would not employ more servants than the household required.



She would not spend money merely to imitate the lifestyle of the Marquisate.



Her independent household would be respectable.



It did not need to be impressive.



She looked at the budget.



For the first time, she was not calculating how much money the family could spend.



She was calculating how much she herself could afford.



The result was less comfortable.



It was also hers.



Before retiring, Mingzhao received a message from her father.



He had agreed that her departure should take place after the initial transition period, with the exact date to be fixed at the next family meeting.



The property division would continue separately.



Mingzhao read the message.



Then she wrote a reply.



She accepted the arrangement, provided that the departure date would not be postponed solely because the property division remained incomplete.



She also requested that the remaining household records be handed over to Steward Zhou and Mingyuan according to the transition schedule.



Qiao Yue watched her seal the letter.



"Miss, the family has finally agreed."



"They have agreed to discuss the date."



"Is that not enough?"



"For today."



Mingzhao placed the letter aside.



The palace had a date.



The Marquisate was approaching one.



Neither woman had yet left.



Neither had completed every financial or personal arrangement.



But the direction was now clear.



The Empress would formally relinquish her position on the twenty-third day.



The Marquis's eldest daughter would establish a separate household once the family agreed on a departure date and the essential handover was complete.



The palace was preparing a ceremony.



The Marquisate was preparing an inventory.



And somewhere in the Imperial Household Administration, an official had just requested clarification about whether the Empress's official seals should be returned before or after the final ceremonial acknowledgment.



The Department of Rites had answered that question.



It had also added a note.



The seals were to be returned during the ceremony.



The official had then asked whether the seals should be counted before being returned.



The Department of Rites had answered yes.



The palace had finally discovered the correct order of events.



It had only taken eleven days, several committees, and enough copies of one imperial order to furnish a small archive.



**End of Chapter 21.**
