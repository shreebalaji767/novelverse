# **Chapter 40: The Former Empress Has Inspected a Courtyard. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Inspected a Drainage Channel. Both Women Have Discovered That a Property Is Only Convenient Until Someone Measures It.**

**2nd Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Duke of Xu's eastern courtyard had been cleaned twice.



The first cleaning had been ordered by the steward after receiving the Duke's instruction that the rooms should be made comfortable for his daughter.



The second had been ordered by Madam Xu after she learned that the steward had interpreted comfortable as meaning that every piece of furniture in the courtyard should be polished, every curtain replaced, and every storage chest opened to determine whether its contents were suitable for display.



The third cleaning had not been ordered by anyone.



It had happened because the servants responsible for the first two cleanings had begun arguing about which of them had left dust beneath the western window.



By the time Xu Yuru arrived, the courtyard was spotless.



Almost suspiciously spotless.



She stepped through the doorway and paused.



The courtyard was modest by the standards of the Duke's residence. It had a small central garden, two connected rooms, a covered passage, and a separate study overlooking a narrow pond. The eastern wall received the morning sun. A mature osmanthus tree stood near the southern corner, its branches spreading above the tiled roof.



It was not grand.



That was one of its advantages.



Yuru walked slowly across the courtyard, examining the rooms without touching anything.



Behind her, Madam Xu watched with the careful expression of someone attempting not to make a suggestion before the other person had finished thinking.



"Does the light suit you?" Madam Xu eventually asked.



"Yes."



"The study receives sunlight until midday."



"I noticed."



"The western room is cooler in the afternoon."



"I noticed that too."



Madam Xu looked at her daughter.



"You have inspected the courtyard thoroughly."



"I am considering whether I could live here."



"That is generally why people inspect courtyards."



Yuru turned towards her.



"Then why did Father ask the steward to prepare three different furniture arrangements?"



Madam Xu's expression changed.



It was not quite embarrassment.



It was the expression of a woman who had discovered that a household's attempts at hospitality had developed an administrative structure of their own.



"Your father thought you might prefer to choose."



"He could have asked me."



"He thought asking you would be less convenient than preparing the choices."



Yuru looked towards the study.



Three separate arrangements had indeed been placed there.



One contained a large writing desk, two bookcases, and a cabinet.



Another contained a smaller desk, a couch, and several decorative screens.



The third contained a writing table, a tea table, and a large lacquered chest.



Yuru studied them.



"Who prepared the third arrangement?"



"The steward."



"Why?"



"He said the first two did not account for your possible need to receive visitors."



Yuru considered this.



"How many visitors does he expect me to receive?"



Madam Xu did not answer immediately.



"Your father has not specified a number."



"Then the third arrangement is premature."



Madam Xu laughed.



The sound was quiet but genuine.



Yuru walked into the study and looked through the eastern window. The light was good. The pond was visible without being directly in front of the desk. The room was large enough for her books, but not so large that it would require an army of servants to maintain.



She could imagine reading there.



She could also imagine leaving the room for an entire afternoon without anyone waiting outside to ask her to approve a purchase order.



That thought was unexpectedly pleasant.



"Would you like to use it?" Madam Xu asked.



"I would like to consider it."



"That is not a refusal."



"No."



"It is also not an acceptance."



"Correct."



Madam Xu sighed.



"You have become very difficult to hurry."



Yuru looked at her.



"I spent many years being hurried by other people's requirements. I am trying not to continue."



Madam Xu did not argue.



She had been worried that her daughter's resignation would leave her with too much uncertainty. Yet the more she observed Yuru, the more she understood that the uncertainty was not necessarily a problem.



Yuru had spent years making decisions for everyone else.



She was now attempting to make one for herself.



The household steward approached the doorway carrying a narrow document case.



"Madam, the Duke has asked whether the courtyard arrangement is satisfactory."



Yuru looked at the case.



"Is that another furniture arrangement?"



"No, Madam. It is the household expense estimate."



Madam Xu closed her eyes.



The steward looked confused.



Yuru held out her hand.



"Let me see it."



He gave her the document case.



Inside was a short list of projected expenses: minor roof maintenance, replacement of two window screens, cleaning of the pond, and several items of furniture.



The figures were not unreasonable.



The problem was the final line.



**Additional household preparation: twelve taels.**



Yuru read it twice.



"What does this include?"



The steward answered confidently.



"Various necessary preparations, Madam."



"Which preparations?"



He hesitated.



"Small matters."



"Are they included in the other lines?"



"I do not believe so."



"Then who prepared the amount?"



"The household accountant."



"Has the accountant provided a breakdown?"



"Not yet."



Yuru closed the document.



"Ask him to provide one."



The steward accepted the instruction.



Madam Xu looked at her daughter.



"You are not responsible for these accounts."



"I am aware."



"Then why did you ask?"



"Because someone has written twelve taels into a document without explaining what they are for."



Madam Xu considered this.



"That is a reasonable question."



"It is also a reasonable question if I intend to live here. I would prefer to know what the household is spending."



The steward bowed and withdrew.



Yuru returned to the window.



She did not dislike the courtyard.



She liked its quietness, its light, and the fact that it did not resemble a palace office.



But liking a place and deciding to live there were different matters.



She had learned that distinction rather late.

At the imperial palace, the Emperor was reading a monthly household summary.



He had specifically requested that the report contain only matters requiring his attention.



The first page contained three items.



The second page contained five.



The third page contained a note about the replacement of a broken kitchen scale.



Zhao Jingheng stopped reading.



He looked towards Gao Shun.



"Why is a broken kitchen scale in my monthly summary?"



Gao Shun stood with his hands folded.



"The Imperial Household Administration included it among the completed matters."



"Did they require my approval?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Did the Department of Revenue require my approval?"



"No."



"Did the scale require my approval?"



Gao Shun paused.



"Not to my knowledge, Your Majesty."



The Emperor put down the report.



"Then why am I reading about it?"



Gao Shun had served in the palace long enough to understand that the correct answer was not always the most useful one.



"The office may have wished to demonstrate that the matter was resolved."



"Can it not demonstrate this by keeping the scale?"



"That may be possible."



Zhao Jingheng rubbed his forehead.



He was beginning to understand why Yuru had complained about the number of matters that reached her desk.



It was not merely that the palace had too many responsibilities.



It was that people had become accustomed to treating the person at the top as the final destination of every piece of information.



The Emperor had previously assumed that this was a sign of efficient administration.



He was now less certain.



He opened the report again.



The kitchen scale had been repaired for eight hundred cash.



The replacement would have cost considerably more.



He placed a small mark beside the entry.



"That is a sensible repair. It does not require another report."



Gao Shun nodded.



"Shall I instruct the administration to remove such entries from the monthly summary?"



"Tell them to distinguish between completed routine matters and matters requiring a decision. I do not want a report so short that it conceals problems, but I also do not want to receive a catalogue of every repaired object."



"Understood."



The Emperor continued reading.



The next item concerned the eastern corridor lamps.



The Palace Stores had recorded them as ordinary household equipment. The Department of Rites had included them in a ceremonial inventory because they were used during formal processions.



The Imperial Household Administration had requested clarification.



Zhao Jingheng looked at the three separate annotations.



"Have they been classified by their ordinary use?"



"Yes, Your Majesty. The administration is preparing a revised record."



"Then let them finish."



Gao Shun waited.



The Emperor turned another page.



There was no request for Yuru's approval.



There was no document sent to Chengming Palace.



There was no official asking whether the former Empress could settle the disagreement.



He found this both reassuring and irritating.



The palace was functioning.



It was also discovering that functioning required more effort than simply sending everything to one person.



"Has the Crown Prince reviewed the Eastern Palace reporting forms?" he asked.



"He has, Your Majesty."



"And?"



"He returned two forms for correction."



"Why?"



"One did not distinguish between the expenditure authorized by the Eastern Palace and the expenditure made by the individual household of the Crown Prince."



Zhao Jingheng looked up.



"Good."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor resumed reading.



For the first time in several weeks, he did not consider sending any of the documents to Yuru.



He did, however, make a note to ask her about the eastern courtyard when they next met.



Then he crossed it out.



He could ask her about the courtyard.



He did not need to turn the question into a report.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao was examining a letter from Sun Qiming.



The letter was brief.



The independent accountant had not yet obtained the complete business register connected with the former roadside storage yard. He had located a merchant who had once dealt with the yard's operator, Zhao Fusheng, but the merchant had not agreed to provide his old records.



Sun had asked for permission to approach him again.



Mingzhao read the letter and placed it beside her ledger.



Qiao Yue poured tea.



"Does he have an answer?"



"He has a lead."



"That sounds like an answer that has not arrived."



"It is."



Qiao Yue glanced at the letter.



"Does this Zhao Fusheng know where the grain went?"



"We do not know whether the grain went to his yard."



"Then what does he know?"



"Possibly nothing."



Qiao Yue looked disappointed.



Mingzhao raised her eyes.



"Would you prefer that we invent a destination?"



"No, Miss."



"Then we must wait."



She took a clean sheet of paper and wrote a reply authorizing Sun to approach the merchant again, provided that he recorded the merchant's statements separately from any documents he obtained.



She also instructed him not to treat the existence of a business relationship with Liu Shun as proof of a grain delivery.



The figures remained unchanged.



Forty-seven sacks had originally been recorded as missing from the northern warehouse.



Twenty had been covered by a movement instruction.



Eighteen had been confirmed in the southern storage ledger.



Two remained unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven remained unexplained.



The separate transport payment with a blank destination had not been connected to the twenty sacks.



Nor had the three-cart journey to the southern road established the quantity carried.



Mingzhao placed the letter in the correspondence tray.



"Anything else?" Qiao Yue asked.



"Yes."



She opened the property file.



The northern canal house had become increasingly troublesome.



The roof repair estimate remained approximately one hundred and twenty taels. Drainage work was estimated at thirty. The kitchen required less work than initially expected, but the well stones needed repair.



None of those costs included a final determination of the boundary.



The old maintenance record established that work had been performed near the drainage channel.



It did not establish that the channel belonged to the property.



Nor did it establish that the neighbouring landowner had surrendered any claim.



The inspection had been arranged for the third day of the fifth month.



The owner would attend.



So would Mr Qian, the neighbour disputing the boundary.



Master Luo would bring his survey assistant and the original registration documents if they could be obtained.



Mingzhao checked the appointment.



"Tomorrow," she said.



Qiao Yue looked at the papers.



"Will the owner agree to the measurement?"



"He agreed to attend."



"That is not the same thing."



"Correct."



"Will Mr Qian agree?"



"We shall discover that tomorrow."



Qiao Yue poured more tea.



"Would it not be easier to find another house?"



"Possibly."



"Then why continue examining this one?"



"Because it may still be suitable."



"Even with the roof and drainage?"



"Those are repair costs. A disputed boundary is a different matter."



Qiao Yue considered this.



Mingzhao closed the file.



"If the repairs are expensive, I can calculate whether the property is worth purchasing. If the boundary is uncertain, I cannot even calculate what I am purchasing."



Qiao Yue nodded slowly.



"That makes sense."



"It should. I have explained it three times."



"I understood it the first time."



"Then why did you ask?"



"Because you looked as though you wanted someone to argue with."



Mingzhao gave her a cold glance.



Qiao Yue smiled and returned to arranging the tea things.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan had reached the point in the northern warehouse records where the papers stopped agreeing with one another.



He had spread three ledgers across his desk.



The first contained the original stock count.



The second recorded the movement instruction for twenty sacks.



The third contained the transport payments.



A loose note had been placed between the second and third volumes.



It contained no clear date.



Mingyuan held it against the light.



There were several faded characters, but none established a destination.



He looked at the account assistant standing beside the desk.



"Was this found with the southern storage records?"



"It was found in the same bundle, Young Master."



"That does not mean it belongs to them."



"No, Young Master."



"Has anyone checked the binding marks?"



"The steward is checking."



Mingyuan placed the note to one side.



He was beginning to dislike loose papers.



They were always important enough to interrupt an account review and never clear enough to answer anything.



A knock sounded at the door.



Mingxiu entered with a small account book.



"I found the lamp oil delivery note."



"Which one?"



"The one recorded in the kitchen expenditure ledger but missing from the household purchase register."



Mingyuan looked at her.



"Was the delivery made?"



"Yes. The supplier's receipt matches the payment, and the kitchen storekeeper has signed the receiving record."



"Then the entry is complete."



"Almost."



He waited.



"The quantity differs between the purchase note and the receiving record."



"By how much?"



"One small jar."



Mingyuan frowned.



"Was it a full jar?"



"The storekeeper says the supplier brought one jar with a cracked seal. He replaced it before the goods were accepted."



"Is there a replacement note?"



"Not yet."



Mingyuan looked at the records on his desk.



"Then we should ask the supplier."



"I already sent a message."



He looked at her again.



Mingxiu's expression was carefully neutral.



"What?"



"Nothing."



"You are looking at me as though I have done something unusual."



"You have sent a message without asking me to write it."



"I can write messages."



"I know."



"Then why are you surprised?"



Mingyuan leaned back.



"Because I used to receive every question from the household as though it were an accusation."



Mingxiu placed her book on the desk.



"Perhaps you should stop treating every question as an accusation."



He looked at her.



She looked back.



Neither spoke for a moment.



Then Mingyuan lowered his eyes to the ledgers.



"Perhaps."



Mingxiu did not offer praise.



She had not found the missing jar.



She had merely found the discrepancy.



But that was enough for the day.



She picked up her book.



"Madam has asked whether the next household purchase list should be prepared before the old stock is exhausted."



"Who is responsible for that?"



"The kitchen storekeeper prepares the list. I am checking the quantities."



"Then that is reasonable."



"I thought so."



She left.



Mingyuan returned to the northern warehouse papers.



The records still did not agree.



He no longer expected them to agree merely because he wanted them to.

At the temporary residence, the landlord's worker arrived shortly before midday.



He carried a narrow wooden tool box and a small bundle of cleaning cloths.



Qiao Yue led him towards the rear drainage passage.



The landlord had agreed the previous day to have the drain cleaned and minor repairs completed under the rental arrangement.



Mingzhao followed.



The worker removed the loose covering boards and inspected the channel.



"Leaves and silt," he said. "The water is not moving properly."



"Was it blocked before the last rain?" Mingzhao asked.



"Partly. The rain made it worse."



"Will cleaning it be sufficient?"



"For now. One section needs a small repair, but it does not require rebuilding the channel."



"Then write down what you intend to replace."



The worker looked slightly offended.



"It is only a drain."



"That is why the written note should be short."



Qiao Yue covered her mouth.



The worker glanced between them.



Mingzhao remained perfectly serious.



He wrote down the work: removal of accumulated leaves and silt, replacement of one damaged board, and adjustment of the water outlet.



Mingzhao read it.



"Good."



The worker began.



Qiao Yue watched him work.



"Miss, I think you may have frightened him."



"He has been asked to clean a drain."



"He was asked to clean it by someone who wanted a written description of the work."



"That is not frightening."



"To someone who has spent his life cleaning drains, perhaps it is."



Mingzhao turned away.



The lower room still had a faint smell of damp wood after the previous heavy rain.



The landlord had not promised to eliminate all moisture immediately. He had agreed to inspect the floor and the rear drainage.



Mingzhao would observe the room after the next rain.



She had rented the residence for three months.



She did not need to decide whether it was a suitable permanent home.



She needed to know whether it was suitable for the current period.



That distinction had become increasingly useful.

In the early afternoon, a messenger arrived from the Qingyuan Marquisate.



He carried a sealed packet from Mr Liang.



Mingzhao received it in the front room.



The letter contained an update on the family settlement.



Sun Qiming's review was still in progress. The family representatives had received the preliminary classification of the property, but several supporting records had not yet been verified.



Mr Liang proposed that the next meeting be scheduled after the accountant had completed the relevant review.



Mingzhao agreed.



She did not need another meeting at which everyone sat around a table and repeated that the accounts were incomplete.



The letter also included a copy of the updated household property schedule.



She examined it carefully.



The confirmed study repair expenditure of seventy-two taels had been classified as estate maintenance.



The remaining two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.



The personal advances had not changed.



Nine were confirmed.



Four were partially confirmed, with payment established but repayment arrangements unclear.



Ten remained uncertain.



The family had not attempted to combine the categories.



Mingzhao read the schedule again.



There were fewer errors than in the earlier version.



Not none.



But fewer.



She marked two entries requiring clarification and wrote a short response.



Qiao Yue watched her.



"Is the family improving?"



"The document is improving."



"That is not quite the same thing."



"No."



Mingzhao put the schedule aside.



"People may improve without the documents improving. Documents may improve without the people improving. I prefer to keep the two questions separate."



Qiao Yue looked thoughtful.



"That sounds rather tiring."



"It is."



"Then perhaps you should rest."



Mingzhao looked towards the correspondence tray.



"After I finish this reply."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"That is what you said before lunch."



"And I was correct before lunch."

At the Duke of Xu's residence, Yuru had finished reviewing the courtyard.



She had also finished drinking tea with her mother.



The Duke arrived after the midday meal.



He looked first at the courtyard, then at the document case on the table.



"Have you decided?"



"Not yet."



He nodded.



"That is acceptable."



Madam Xu looked at him.



"You sound as though you expected her to refuse."



"I expected her to consider."



Yuru placed the expense estimate before him.



"Who prepared the twelve-tael additional household preparation charge?"



The Duke looked at the document.



He read the line.



Then he looked at his steward, who had followed him into the courtyard.



"Why is this included?"



The steward bowed.



"The household accountant estimated the additional preparations."



"Which preparations?"



"Various small matters, Your Grace."



The Duke's expression hardened.



"That is not an explanation."



The steward lowered his head.



"I will ask for a breakdown."



"Do so."



Yuru watched the exchange without speaking.



The Duke turned to her.



"I am sorry. The courtyard should have been prepared more carefully."



"It is not necessary to apologize for an unfinished estimate."



"I would rather you not have to inspect every line."



"I am not being asked to approve it."



"Still."



Yuru smiled faintly.



"Father, I am not offended by a question about twelve taels."



The Duke gave a small laugh.



"Your mother says you have become difficult to hurry."



"She is correct."



Madam Xu poured tea.



"She has also become difficult to persuade."



"That is not necessarily bad," the Duke said.



Yuru looked at the courtyard again.



"I like the rooms. I like the study. I would like to visit again before deciding."



"Then visit again."



"I do not want the household to make permanent arrangements yet."



"Very well."



"And please tell the steward that I do not need three furniture arrangements."



The Duke looked towards the steward.



"How many did he prepare?"



"Three, Your Grace."



The Duke closed his eyes briefly.



"One is sufficient."



Yuru's expression remained calm.



Madam Xu laughed into her tea.



The Duke looked at his daughter.



"At least you have found something that can be settled today."



"Yes."



"What is that?"



"The number of furniture arrangements."



The Duke shook his head.



For the first time that day, the courtyard felt less like a possible residence and more like a place where Yuru could simply sit with her parents.



She was not ready to call it home.



But she was no longer in a hurry to leave.

That evening, the Emperor received a brief note from the Duke of Xu.



It was not a political report.



It merely stated that Yuru had visited the eastern courtyard, found the rooms satisfactory, and had not yet made a decision.



Zhao Jingheng read it once.



Then he put it aside.



Gao Shun waited.



"Your Majesty, should a reply be sent?"



"No."



"Shall the Duke be informed that the palace will make no arrangements until a decision is reached?"



"The Duke already knows that."



"Then—"



"Nothing needs to be sent."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor looked towards the window.



He had once believed that every important matter needed an official response.



Now he was discovering that sometimes a matter could remain unfinished without becoming an emergency.



At the eastern market, Mingzhao was also looking at an unfinished matter.



The next day's property inspection.



She checked that the survey assistant had been confirmed, that the owner had agreed to attend, and that the original registration documents had been requested.



She placed the file beside her desk.



Tomorrow would not necessarily settle the boundary.



It might reveal another problem.



It might confirm that the property was unsuitable.



It might establish that the owner and Mr Qian had misunderstood the old drainage arrangement.



She did not know.



She had learned not to mistake an appointment for a conclusion.



Outside, the landlord's worker finished cleaning the rear drain.



Water flowed through the channel without collecting beneath the boards.



Qiao Yue inspected it and returned.



"The water is moving properly now."



"Did the worker replace the damaged board?"



"Yes. He left the old piece beside the wall."



"Good. Keep the work note."



Qiao Yue stared at her.



"Miss, it is a drain."



Mingzhao closed the property file.



"And now it is a drain that has been cleaned, repaired, and recorded."



Qiao Yue considered whether to argue.



She decided against it.



At the palace, a broken kitchen scale had been repaired without reaching the Emperor's desk.



At the Duke's residence, a courtyard had been inspected without being declared a permanent home.



At the Marquisate, two siblings were learning to distinguish between an incomplete record and a completed expense.



And at a modest rented house near the eastern market, a drainage channel had finally begun carrying water in the direction it was supposed to go.



It was not a grand achievement.



But it was a satisfactory one.



For the moment, that was enough.
