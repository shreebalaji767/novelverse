# **Chapter 119: The Former Empress Has Discovered That an Unfinished Embroidery Does Not Require a Progress Report. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Merchant's Explanation That Contains More Conditions Than Cloth. The Marquis's Heir Has Found a Transfer List Whose Most Important Entry Was Written in the Margin, Where Important Things Apparently Go When Nobody Wishes to Be Responsible for Them.**

**23rd Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru's embroidery had acquired a leaf.

It was not a particularly elegant leaf. One side was longer than the other, and the stitches near the stem were tighter than those at the tip. Nevertheless, it was recognizably a leaf, which Yuru considered sufficient progress for the morning.

Fang Ru stood beside the table, arranging the sewing materials.

"Would you like another colour?"

Yuru looked at the dark blue thread.

"Not yet. I should finish this part first."

"Will you make a flower?"

"I have not decided."

Fang Ru smiled.

"Then perhaps the cloth will decide for you."

Yuru examined the small pattern.

"I doubt the cloth has an opinion."

"That is fortunate. Otherwise it might object to the leaf."

Yuru laughed softly.

She made another stitch, then paused to examine the back of the cloth. The thread had crossed itself near the stem.

She carefully loosened the knot.

There was no need to hurry. She could undo the work without inconveniencing anyone, and the cloth was large enough to try again.

After a few moments, she returned to the front and continued.

The embroidered leaf gradually became more regular.

Fang Ru placed a small dish of fruit on the table.

"Your aunt's household sent these. They are dried plums."

Yuru looked up.

"Did the letter say why?"

"Your aunt said the young cousin who is learning embroidery helped prepare them. She was apparently very pleased with the result."

"Then I shall have to praise the plums."

"Would you like to send a reply?"

"Not yet. We are visiting at the end of the month. I can thank her in person."

Fang Ru placed the dish beside the tea.

Yuru picked up one of the plums.

It was slightly too sour.

She considered it, then took another.

The second was better.

"Your aunt's household appears to have acquired a more reliable method of selecting dried fruit."

"Perhaps the young cousin has improved the process."

"Then I hope she will explain it when I visit."

Fang Ru smiled.

Yuru returned to her embroidery.

The small domestic arrangements of the morning were becoming a pleasure precisely because they did not need to become anything more.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Mingyuan received the transfer list from the old accounts storehouse.

It arrived in a sealed packet accompanied by a note from the current storehouse supervisor. The supervisor had explained that the list was a surviving copy made during the later reorganization. The original had not been located, but the copy bore the old storehouse seal and the signatures of two clerks.

Mingyuan examined the seal first.

"Has it been checked?"

"Yes," Liang Wen replied. "The seal pattern matches the other surviving transfer records from the same period."

"And the signatures?"

"The names are legible. We have not yet established whether the handwriting matches other records."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Then record both facts separately."

He opened the packet.

The list contained several entries for records removed from the old accounts storehouse. Most were ordinary: tax summaries, grain purchase registers, repair accounts, and land rent records.

The northern transport records appeared near the middle.

The first entry referred to movement notebooks.

The second referred to a northern dispatch register.

The third referred to a bundle of miscellaneous transport papers.

Mingyuan leaned closer.

The northern dispatch register had been moved to a temporary records room during the reorganization. A marginal note stated that it was to be included with the transport records after classification.

He read the line again.

"Where is this temporary records room?"

Steward Zhou pointed to the next column.

"An old building near the eastern granary."

Mingyuan looked at him.

"Was it used for storage?"

"Temporarily. The building was cleared later."

"Where were the records moved?"

"The next entry should say."

Mingyuan turned the page.

The next entry listed several bundles moved from the temporary room to the central records collection. The northern dispatch register was not named individually.

Instead, the margin contained a short notation:

*Transport papers included in general collection. Final shelf to be assigned.*

Mingyuan stared at it.

"Who wrote this?"

"The clerk who copied the transfer list may not have been the person who made the original note."

"Can we identify the clerk?"

"The signature at the bottom belongs to the supervisor of the temporary room. The marginal hand is different."

Mingyuan placed the list flat.

"So the register was moved from the old accounts storehouse to a temporary room, then the transport papers were moved into a general collection."

"That is what the surviving list indicates."

"But it does not say whether the dispatch register was among them."

"Correct."

Mingyuan rubbed his forehead.

"Then why was it listed separately in the first place?"

"Because someone knew it existed and intended to classify it separately."

"And then someone wrote that it had been included with general papers."

"Yes."

Mingyuan tapped the margin.

"Where is the final shelf?"

"We do not know."

The young heir looked at the note.

The record had survived long enough to tell them that the missing register had once been important enough to receive its own line. It had then been absorbed into a general collection by a clerk who had apparently considered the phrase *final shelf to be assigned* an acceptable conclusion.

Mingyuan was beginning to suspect that the records office had developed a habit of treating unfinished arrangements as completed tasks.

He looked at Liang Wen.

"Prepare a list of every record group transferred from that temporary room. We need the final storage locations, not just the names of the rooms."

Liang Wen nodded.

"Should I request the general collection index?"

"Yes. And ask whether any bundles were later moved to the old western archive."

Zhou looked at him.

"That archive held many records from the Revenue Office."

"Then it may also hold the transport papers."

"It may."

Mingyuan gave him a tired look.

"I know. Nothing is certain until we see it."

He placed the transfer list inside a protective folder.

"Do not mark the dispatch register as found. Record it as last known in the temporary records room, with a possible transfer into the general collection."

Liang Wen wrote the description carefully.

Mingyuan read it over.

The wording was precise.

It was also deeply unsatisfying.

Shen Mingzhao received the first supplier's third sample shortly before midday.

The merchant had sent it in a small wooden case with a letter attached.

This time, the cloth was closer to the reference in colour. The weave was still slightly tighter, but the difference was less obvious than in the previous sample.

Mingzhao examined it under natural light.

Then she compared it with the reference cloth.

The colour was acceptable at first glance. The surface, however, had a faint unevenness near one edge.

She called the foreman.

He examined both pieces.

"The colour is closer."

"Yes."

"The weave is still tighter."

"Yes."

"We might be able to adjust the loom."

"Would the adjustment affect the width?"

"It might."

"Would it affect the weight?"

"Possibly."

Mingzhao placed the samples side by side.

"Then we still do not know whether the finished material will match."

The foreman studied the cloth again.

"Could we ask the merchant to produce another sample using the adjusted loom?"

"We could. But before asking him, we need to know whether he is willing to guarantee the width and colour within an agreed range."

She opened the accompanying letter.

The merchant had written that the third sample was the closest match he could provide under ordinary production conditions. He was willing to discuss a limited variation in colour, but he wanted Mingzhao to accept the weave as it stood.

Mingzhao read the final paragraph.

It was not a refusal, but it was not an agreement either.

"Prepare a comparison record," she told the foreman. "Include the third sample. Record the colour, weave, width, and surface separately."

He nodded.

"Shall we send it back?"

"No. We keep it with the other samples. Send the merchant a written question about the loom adjustment and the width. Ask him to state what he can guarantee."

The foreman hesitated.

"Will this not delay the trial further?"

"Yes."

He looked disappointed.

Mingzhao folded the letter.

"If the merchant cannot give us a stable specification, we cannot promise a stable product. A delay is inconvenient. A disputed delivery is worse."

The foreman nodded.

He had worked with her long enough to know that once she had decided a condition mattered, repeating the cost of waiting would not change her mind.

After he left, Qiao Yue entered with the trader's earlier inquiry.

"The trader has sent a second letter. He asks whether you will meet him next week."

"Has he sent the specifications?"

"No."

"Quantity?"

"No."

"Price?"

"No."

Mingzhao put the letter down.

"Then he has not answered the first letter."

Qiao Yue looked at the paper.

"He says he would prefer to explain his proposal in person."

"That is convenient for him."

"Shall I decline?"

"Tell him that we are willing to consider a written proposal. If he does not wish to send one, we shall leave the matter there."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao turned back to the samples.

There were now three pieces from the first merchant and one from the second.

The growing pile did not make the decision easier.

She labelled the newest sample with its arrival date and placed it in its own envelope.

The trial remained unapproved.

In the western guest residence, Xu Yuru received a short visit from Empress Dowager Wei's personal attendant.

The attendant carried a small parcel and a message.

The Dowager had heard that Yuru had taken up embroidery again. She sent a piece of fine cloth that had been stored in her private cabinet for many years. It was suitable for small decorative work, though the Dowager did not specify what Yuru should make from it.

Yuru opened the parcel.

The cloth was pale cream, with a subtle woven pattern along the edge.

It was finer than the cloth she had been using.

Fang Ru examined it.

"This would make a lovely handkerchief."

"It would."

"Or a small cover for the tea jar."

Yuru looked at the jar.

"The tea jar does not need a cover."

"Then a handkerchief."

"Perhaps."

The attendant waited.

Yuru smiled and asked her to convey her thanks.

"Tell the Dowager I shall use it when I find a suitable pattern."

The attendant bowed.

After she departed, Fang Ru placed the cloth beside the sewing frame.

"Would you like to use it for the leaf?"

Yuru shook her head.

"The cloth I have already begun is sufficient. I do not want to abandon it merely because another piece is finer."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru examined the cream cloth once more.

She had been given many beautiful things during her years in the palace. Some had been intended as honours, others as obligations, and still others as gifts that required a carefully measured response.

This piece seemed to have been sent because the Dowager had heard she was sewing.

Yuru liked that.

She folded it neatly and placed it in the cabinet.

"I shall decide later."

Fang Ru smiled.

"That is becoming your usual answer."

"It is a useful answer when there is no reason to decide today."

Yuru returned to her stitching.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan's inquiry about the temporary records room produced an unexpected complication.

The building had not been cleared in one operation.

According to the surviving records, some papers had been moved to the central collection, while others had been transferred to the western archive. A smaller group had been sent to the estate's old legal records room because the central collection lacked space.

The transfer list did not specify which papers had gone to which destination.

Mingyuan read the explanation.

"Then the dispatch register could be in any of three places."

"Possibly," Zhou replied.

"Could it have been discarded?"

"That is also possible."

Mingyuan put the paper down.

"Is there any record of damaged or duplicate books being removed?"

"There should be."

"Another list?"

"Yes."

Mingyuan looked at him.

Zhou remained calm.

"Old records are often moved more than once."

"I understand that. I am beginning to wonder whether anyone ever moved them directly from one place to another."

Liang Wen looked down.

Mingyuan continued.

"Prepare three requests. One for the central collection index, one for the western archive transfer list, and one for the legal records room inventory."

Zhou considered the workload.

"That will take time."

"Then begin with the central collection. It is the most likely destination according to the transfer note."

"Very well."

Mingyuan looked at the fifth-box records.

"Keep the repair documents separate from the dispatch inquiry. They explain a possible return and repair, not the destination of the carts."

Liang Wen nodded.

Mingyuan had begun to see how easily the two questions could become entangled. A cart had been repaired. A register had been moved. Neither fact established what the cart carried.

The estate's old papers were full of plausible connections.

He intended to require more than plausibility.

Later that afternoon, Shen Mingzhao received the foreman's comparison report.

The third sample's colour was closer to the reference, but the weave was tighter. The width varied slightly near the edge. The second merchant's material was stronger but had a different surface.

The foreman had included measurements and a short note about possible loom adjustments.

Mingzhao read the report.

"Did you measure both samples twice?"

"Yes."

"Who made the second measurement?"

"One of the senior workers."

"Did the figures agree?"

"Within a small difference."

"Record both measurements."

The foreman nodded.

"Would you like us to ask the first merchant for a fourth sample?"

"Not yet. First ask whether he can meet the width and colour conditions without changing the agreed price."

The foreman looked at the report.

"If he cannot, we may have to abandon the trial."

"Yes."

He hesitated.

"Would that be a loss?"

"It would be a small disappointment, not a loss. We have not bought the material or begun production."

The foreman nodded slowly.

Mingzhao put the report aside.

She understood his reluctance. A workshop depended on work, and a possible order gave the workers something to anticipate. Yet she had not built the workshop by treating every inquiry as an obligation to produce.

The six-bolt trial had been proposed because it might reveal whether a new product was worth pursuing.

It was not worth pursuing if the sample itself could not be defined.

She wrote a note in the order book:

*Third sample examined. Colour closer; weave and width remain outside agreed reference. Await supplier's written response. No purchase or production authorized.*

Then she closed the book.

As evening approached, Xu Yuru finished the small embroidered leaf.

She held the cloth up and examined the result.

The stem was slightly crooked. One side of the leaf was fuller than the other.

Fang Ru looked at it.

"It is lovely."

Yuru tilted her head.

"It is uneven."

"It is still lovely."

Yuru smiled.

"Those are not the same thing."

"No. But they can both be true."

Yuru considered this, then placed the embroidery on the table.

She did not remove the uneven stitches.

The small piece of work had taken several days, and she had enjoyed the time spent on it. There was no reason to turn it into an examination of her skill.

Fang Ru began putting away the thread.

"Would you like to start another leaf tomorrow?"

"Perhaps."

"And the cream cloth?"

"Later."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru looked toward the courtyard.

The light was fading. A servant had placed fresh water beside the plants, and the stones along the path were darkening with the evening damp.

She picked up the embroidery once more.

"Find me a small wooden frame tomorrow. This one is a little loose."

Fang Ru smiled.

"I shall look for one."

Yuru placed the cloth in a shallow box.

It was not a grand accomplishment.

It was simply something she had chosen to do.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received the first reply from the central records collection.

The supervisor had acknowledged the request and promised to search the relevant index. He also asked for a more precise description of the missing register.

Mingyuan read the message and immediately dictated a response.

"The title as recorded in the old transfer list, the period covered if known, and the office from which it originated. Include the phrase 'northern dispatch register.' Do not describe it as a book containing the four-cart entry."

Liang Wen wrote.

"Why not include the four carts?" Zhou asked.

"Because we do not know whether the register contains that entry. If we tell them what we expect to find, they may search for the wrong thing or report a result that fits our description."

Zhou nodded.

"That is sensible."

Mingyuan gave him a warning look.

Zhou raised his cup.

"I shall say nothing more."

Mingyuan returned to the message.

The search had become slower than he wanted, but the records were being handled more carefully than before. The difference was beginning to show.

He looked at the three requests waiting to be copied.

"Send the central collection request first. The other two can follow after we receive its index."

"Yes, Young Master."

Mingyuan gathered the documents.

The dispatch register had not been found. The four-cart movement remained unexplained. The repair records offered a possible sequence but no cargo or destination.

Still, the investigation had moved beyond searching boxes at random.

Now they knew where the book had last been recorded, which transfers might have followed, and which collections could contain it.

That was not a conclusion.

It was, at least, an organized uncertainty.

And Mingyuan was discovering that organized uncertainty was considerably more useful than an impressive answer written too early.
