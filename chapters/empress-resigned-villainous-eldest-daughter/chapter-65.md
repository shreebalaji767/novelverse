# **Chapter 65: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Tea Without an Agenda. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to a Family Discussion Without a Corrected Agenda. The Dowager Has Discovered That Invitations Can Be Pleasant. The Marquisate Has Discovered That Invitations Can Also Be Used to Conceal Work.**

**29th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



Xu Yuru received the Dowager's invitation shortly after breakfast.



It was written on a small sheet of fine paper.



The wording was brief.



The Empress Dowager wished to have tea with her, if she had time.



There was no mention of household matters.



No reference to documents.



No request for advice.



No attached schedule.



Fang Ru read it once and looked at Yuru.



"Her Majesty has invited you for tea."



"Yes."



"Would you like to go?"



"Tomorrow, perhaps."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru picked up the invitation again.



The Dowager had been careful with the wording.



That care was itself meaningful.



"Send a reply thanking Her Majesty. Tell her I would be pleased to visit tomorrow afternoon."



Fang Ru prepared the response.



Then she asked, "Shall I arrange a formal robe?"



Yuru looked at her.



"No."



"A simpler one?"



"Yes."



Fang Ru smiled.



The distinction was becoming familiar.

At the Dowager's residence, the reply arrived before noon.



Empress Dowager Wei read it and placed it beside her tea.



"Tomorrow afternoon," she said.



Her attendant nodded.



"Shall we prepare the usual tea room?"



"Yes. And do not prepare a table of documents."



The attendant looked at her.



"Of course, Your Majesty."



The Dowager glanced toward the side table.



There was a small stack of papers waiting for her own review.



She considered them.



Then she moved them to the adjoining room.



She did not want the tea gathering to become a meeting by accident.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingzhao received another letter.



This one came from Shen Guowei.



It requested her attendance at a family discussion the following afternoon.



The subject was described as the remaining matters concerning the property division.



Mingzhao read the letter twice.



Then she placed it on the table.



Qiao Yue was arranging the correspondence.



"Another meeting?"



"Yes."



"Is the revised schedule ready?"



"No."



Qiao Yue stopped.



"Then what will they discuss?"



"That is what I intend to discover."



She took out the previous schedule and compared it with the new correction list.



The furnishing payment had been removed from her personal advances.



The classifications of the thirteen payment entries remained separate.



The seventy-two-tael repair expenditure had been placed under estate maintenance paid from her funds.



The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained disputed.



The grain discrepancy and blank-destination transport payment were still separate.



The family had not yet supplied the additional evidence required for several entries.



Mingzhao turned to the invitation.



There was no agenda.



No list of documents.



No statement of whether the meeting would be attended by the family representative.



No explanation of what had changed since the previous day.



She picked up her brush.



"Prepare a reply."



Qiao Yue brought the paper.



Mingzhao wrote:



*I will attend if the purpose of the discussion and the documents to be considered are circulated beforehand. Matters already classified at the previous meeting should not be reopened without new evidence.*



She read it once.



Then she signed it.



Qiao Yue took the letter.



"Will they be pleased?"



"They do not have to be pleased."

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei read the reply.



Madam Liang sat nearby.



"She has asked for an agenda?" he said.



"She has asked for the purpose of the meeting," Madam Liang replied.



"She knows the purpose."



"Then it should not be difficult to write it down."



The Marquis looked at his wife.



Madam Liang continued reading.



"She also wants the documents circulated beforehand."



"She has already seen the schedule."



"She has seen the schedule from the previous meeting. The revised one has not been completed."



Shen Guowei put the letter down.



He had hoped to speak with Mingzhao directly.



There were several matters he wanted to settle.



He also wanted to ask whether she would agree to a more practical arrangement for the family accounts.



He had not written that in the invitation.



He had assumed the conversation would naturally reach it.



Madam Liang noticed his expression.



"What did you intend to discuss?"



"The remaining property matters."



"Only those?"



The Marquis did not answer immediately.



He had also wanted to discuss the family estate's continuing administrative needs.



Mingyuan was learning.



Mingxiu was assisting.



But neither had the experience Mingzhao possessed.



The family still needed someone who understood the old accounts.



He had considered asking her to help until the division was complete.



He had not written this because he knew she might refuse before hearing him out.



Madam Liang looked at him.



"Then you should not be surprised that she wants to know."



Shen Guowei frowned.



"She is still our daughter."



"That does not tell her what the meeting is about."



The Marquis looked toward the window.



The family had repeatedly expected Mingzhao to understand what they wanted without being told.



They had rarely extended the same courtesy to her.

At the workshop, the next production group was underway.



The forty-eight accepted bolts had been moved to the completed section.



Their inspection records were filed.



The remaining order was still being processed.



Mingzhao arrived before the midday break.



Lin Shuyun met her beside the storage shelves.



"The workers have begun the next group."



"How many?"



"Twenty-four."



"Why twenty-four?"



"The material available at the table was enough for that group."



Mingzhao looked toward the workers.



"Have they been assigned supervisors?"



"Yes."



"Are the trainees working separately?"



"Yes. One is working with Zhao. The other is with the senior finisher."



Mingzhao examined the production sheet.



"Any corrections?"



"One edge needed adjustment."



"Material lost?"



"No."



"Record the correction."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"It has already been recorded."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Good."



Lin Shuyun crossed her arms.



"Do you ever intend to praise someone before checking the record?"



"When the record is checked, the praise is more accurate."



"Then I shall make sure every compliment receives a supporting document."



"That would be efficient."



Lin Shuyun laughed.

A little later, the senior finisher approached them.



"Miss Shen, the revised folding method is saving time."



"How much?"



He presented the latest comparison.



The time required for handling each bolt had decreased.



The difference was not dramatic.



Across a large order, however, it could become meaningful.



Mingzhao examined the figures.



"Are these measurements from the same number of workers?"



"Yes."



"Same material?"



"Yes."



"Same inspection standard?"



"Yes."



"Then the comparison is useful."



The finisher looked pleased.



"We should use this method for future orders."



"Not automatically."



His expression changed.



Mingzhao continued.



"The buyer approved this method for this order under the recorded specifications. A future buyer may have different requirements."



The finisher nodded.



"Yes, Miss."



"The method can be kept as a production option. It is not a universal instruction."



Lin Shuyun looked at him.



"That means you cannot change every order because you found a faster fold."



The finisher sighed.



"I understand."



Mingzhao returned the comparison sheet.



"Good work. Keep recording the results."



He bowed and left.



Lin Shuyun watched him go.



"You could have simply said that."



"I did."



"After explaining the conditions."



"Those conditions are the reason."



Lin Shuyun shook her head.



The workshop was gradually learning to improve its methods without confusing improvement with permission to ignore a contract.



It was a slow lesson.



It was also becoming useful.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received a reply from the former warehouse steward Zhang.



Zhang had agreed to examine the certified copies.



He had also requested that the pages be arranged by date rather than by the order in which the estate had found them.



Mingyuan read the note.



"That is reasonable," he said.



Steward Zhou nodded.



"Shall I rearrange the copies?"



"Yes. Keep the certification attached to each page."



"Should the originals be sent as well?"



"No. They remain in the archive."



Zhou De bowed.



Mingyuan looked at the note again.



Zhang had added that he might be able to distinguish between the total warehouse stock and the grain available for immediate dispatch.



He had not promised to identify the missing twenty-seven sacks.



Mingyuan placed the letter beside the grain records.



"Send the copies in chronological order."



"Yes, young master."



"And ask Zhang to mark anything he remembers separately from anything shown in the documents."



The steward nodded.



Mingyuan looked at the account.



He was learning that the right question was not always the one that produced the fastest answer.



Sometimes it was the one that prevented a wrong answer from being accepted.

At the southern building, Mingxiu received her father's decision.



The roof repairs were approved in principle.



However, the carpenter was required to provide a final work schedule and confirm that the damaged supports would be repaired rather than replaced unnecessarily.



Mingxiu read the instruction.



"That is acceptable."



Madam Liang looked at her.



"Why are you pleased? The work has not begun."



"Because the repair scope is clearer."



"Your father has approved it."



"He has approved the work subject to the final schedule."



Madam Liang looked at the papers.



"Must everything have conditions?"



Mingxiu smiled faintly.



"If the conditions are not written down, people remember them differently."



Her mother did not reply.



Mingxiu placed the papers in the maintenance file.



The roof was not repaired yet.



But the family had moved one step closer to a decision that could be carried out properly.

The next afternoon, Xu Yuru visited the Empress Dowager.



The tea room was arranged simply.



There were two small tables, a pot of tea, fruit, and several pastries.



No clerks were present.



No documents had been placed on the table.



The Dowager smiled when Yuru entered.



"Come and sit."



Yuru greeted her respectfully.



"Your Majesty."



"Today, you are not required to review anything."



Yuru sat down.



"That is reassuring."



The Dowager smiled.



"Have you been well?"



"Yes."



"Did you enjoy your visit to your parents?"



"Very much."



"Your mother prepared the meal?"



"Yes."



"And your father?"



Yuru considered.



"Father wanted to prepare more food than Mother permitted."



The Dowager laughed.



"That sounds familiar."



"He believes that a daughter who visits must be fed as much as possible."



"And your mother?"



"She believes that six dishes are sufficient."



The Dowager looked amused.



"Which of them won?"



"Mother."



The Dowager laughed again.



The conversation moved to ordinary matters.



The weather had become warmer.



The gardens would require more attention after the next rain.



The Dowager mentioned that one of her old acquaintances had sent a new tea blend.



Yuru asked whether she liked it.



"It is acceptable," the Dowager said. "Not particularly good."



"Then why are you drinking it?"



"Because the person who sent it will ask."



Yuru smiled.



"That is a difficult reason to drink tea."



"It is a difficult life to receive gifts."



The Dowager looked at her.



"Do you still find yourself waiting for someone to bring you documents?"



Yuru thought about the question.



"Sometimes."



"Do you miss the work?"



"Some of it."



"Which parts?"



Yuru considered.



"I miss knowing that a problem has been resolved. I do not miss being expected to resolve every problem."



The Dowager nodded slowly.



"Those are not the same thing."



"No."



The Dowager poured more tea.



"You were very capable."



"I was."



The Dowager looked at her.



Yuru smiled slightly.



"I do not deny that I was capable. I only no longer wish to be responsible for everything that happened because I was capable."



The Dowager remained silent for a moment.



Then she nodded.



"That is fair."



It was not an easy admission for her.



For many years, she had believed that the palace's stability depended on the Empress's willingness to carry burdens beyond her formal duties.



She had praised Yuru for doing so.



She had also benefited from it.



Now she was learning that praise could become another way of asking someone to continue.



The Dowager did not want to do that again.

When the tea gathering ended, the Dowager did not ask Yuru to stay.



She did not present a household problem.



She did not mention the unfinished inventory.



She simply said, "Come again when you wish."



Yuru bowed.



"I will."



As she left, Fang Ru followed with a small box of pastries.



The Dowager had insisted that she take them.



Yuru looked at the box.



"Another gift?"



Fang Ru smiled.



"At least this one has been identified."



Yuru laughed.

At the Marquisate, Mingzhao received her father's second message.



The revised schedule was still being prepared.



The next discussion would be delayed until the family representative had finished correcting the documents.



The letter also asked whether she would be willing to attend a separate conversation about the estate's administrative transition.



Mingzhao read the wording.



This time, the subject was stated clearly.



She placed the letter on the table.



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Will you go?"



"Not to discuss the estate's future management."



"Will you reply?"



"Yes."



Mingzhao took out paper.



She wrote that she would attend discussions concerning the property division and her own documented claims. She would not accept responsibility for the estate's continuing administration.



She added that any questions concerning past management should be submitted in writing with the relevant records.



Then she signed.



Qiao Yue took the letter.



"That is very direct."



"It needs to be."



"Do you think they will ask anyway?"



"Yes."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Then why do they keep asking?"



Mingzhao looked at the unfinished family schedule.



"Because asking costs them less than finding someone else."



Qiao Yue thought about that.



Then she smiled.



"At least it costs them a letter."



Mingzhao returned to her accounts.

That evening, the Marquis received Mingzhao's reply.



He read it slowly.



Madam Liang sat across from him.



"She has refused the administrative discussion?"



"She has refused to take responsibility for the estate's continuing administration."



"That is what we wanted to discuss."



"Yes."



Madam Liang looked at him.



"Then the matter is clear."



Shen Guowei folded the letter.



He had hoped that Mingzhao might agree to help until the family found a suitable replacement.



He had not considered how many times she had already heard that request.



The family had depended on her for six years.



It had treated that dependence as ordinary.



Now, when she asked for a lawful division and a separate life, they were still looking for ways to retain her knowledge.



He looked toward the account room.



Mingyuan was there with the steward.



Mingxiu was reviewing the maintenance papers.



Both were working.



Neither had the experience Mingzhao possessed.



But experience could be learned.



Responsibility could be divided.



The family had simply delayed doing so.



The Marquis sighed.



"Then we will have to arrange the work ourselves."



Madam Liang did not answer.



She had no immediate solution.

In the palace guest residence, Yuru placed the Dowager's pastries beside the fruit basket.



Fang Ru looked at the growing collection.



"Madam, we may need another table."



Yuru glanced at the uncertain writing table.



"That one is still in the palace inventory."



Fang Ru nodded solemnly.



"Then we must not use it."



Yuru laughed.



"Perhaps we should ask Madam Zhao to determine whether the pastries belong to the guest residence."



Fang Ru smiled.



"I believe the Dowager's attendant has already recorded them as a personal gift."



"Good."



Yuru selected one pastry.



She had spent the afternoon drinking tea with the Dowager.



No documents had been discussed.



No work had been assigned.



It had been a pleasant visit.



At the workshop, Mingzhao had spent the day ensuring that a faster production method did not become an excuse for inconsistent goods.



At the Marquisate, her family had begun considering how to distribute work without her.



None of these matters was finished.



But the direction was becoming clearer.



The former Empress was learning to accept invitations that required nothing from her.



The Marquis's eldest daughter was learning to reject invitations that concealed obligations.



And the people around them were slowly discovering that a person's usefulness did not grant them permanent ownership of that person's time.



It was a lesson the palace had taken years to learn.



The Marquisate was still arguing over it.



The workshop, at least, had written its instructions down.
