# **Chapter 72: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Eat with Her Parents and Has Not Been Asked to Bring a Report. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Explain an Old Payment and Has Requested the Original Receipt. The Workshop Has Discovered That a New Clerk Can Keep a Register Without Becoming Its Owner.**

**6th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress Xu Yuru had received three letters from her mother in the space of eight days.



The first had contained a question about the price of pears.



The second had contained preserved vegetables and fruit.



The third had been an invitation to an afternoon meal.



Yuru considered the third letter the most dangerous.



Not because her mother was likely to cause trouble.



Her mother was, in fact, very unlikely to cause trouble deliberately. She was far too experienced for that.



The danger lay in the possibility that Duke Xu would hear about the invitation and decide that an afternoon meal required preparations suitable for receiving a visiting governor.



Yuru had therefore replied promptly, accepting the invitation and adding one sentence.



*I will come for the meal. There is no need to prepare anything elaborate.*



She had written the sentence carefully.



She had even underlined nothing.



Unfortunately, she knew her father.



At the guest residence, Fang Ru was arranging the correspondence box while Yuru sat beside the window with a cup of tea.



"Madam," Fang Ru said, "your mother's invitation says the meal will be served in the eastern courtyard."



"Then we will eat in the eastern courtyard."



"Your father may be there."



"He lives there."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes.



"That is true."



Yuru took a sip of tea.



"Is there anything else?"



"Your mother has written that she will prepare two dishes you liked when you were younger."



"That sounds pleasant."



"She also says that if the weather is good, you may sit in the courtyard afterward."



"That sounds pleasant too."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"Your father has added a note."



Yuru put down her cup.



"What does it say?"



Fang Ru handed her the small folded sheet.



The handwriting was broad and unmistakably Duke Xu's.



*The weather may be warm. I will have the servants prepare a shaded place.*



Yuru read it once.



Then she read it again.



"That is reasonable."



Fang Ru waited.



Yuru folded the note.



"Why are you looking at me like that?"



"I am waiting to see whether you have noticed the second line."



Yuru unfolded it.



There was, indeed, a second line.



*I have also asked the kitchen to prepare a few additional dishes in case you are hungry.*



Yuru stared at the paper.



"How many is a few?"



"I do not know."



"Did your mother write anything about it?"



"She says she has already spoken to him."



Yuru looked toward the window.



The morning courtyard was quiet. A servant was watering the plants, and the leaves moved slightly under the falling droplets.



"Then my mother has probably prevented the worst."



Fang Ru nodded.



"That is what I thought."



Yuru returned the note to the table.



She was beginning to understand that visiting her natal family required a different sort of preparation from attending an official banquet.



At a banquet, she had to know the order of precedence, the seating arrangements, the ceremonial wording, and the expected duration.



At her parents' home, she had to know how many dishes her father had secretly requested.



The second task was proving more difficult.



"Have you prepared the small gift for my mother?" Yuru asked.



"Yes. The preserved fruit she sent last time has been divided into two small packets. One is for her, and one is for the household."



Yuru looked at her.



"Those are her own preserved fruits."



"She sent them to you."



"Then I should not return them to her."



Fang Ru considered this.



"Would a different gift be better?"



"Perhaps some tea."



"I will arrange it."



Yuru nodded.



There was no need for anything expensive. Her mother had not invited her to inspect a gift list. She had invited her to eat.



That distinction was becoming easier to accept.



For many years, a visit to her natal family had been arranged around palace schedules. Even when the visit was personal, it had carried the weight of formal courtesy. Her mother had asked whether she was tired; Yuru had answered that she was well. Her father had asked about the Emperor; Yuru had replied with the proper degree of discretion.



Now, for the first time in many years, she could visit without having to return to the palace at a particular hour to settle a particular matter.



She did not yet know what to do with all that freedom.



But she was beginning to use it.



"Madam," Fang Ru said, "would you like to walk this morning?"



Yuru glanced outside.



"Yes. The western path."



"The longer path?"



"Not today. The shorter one will do."



Fang Ru smiled faintly.



"Then I will prepare your shoes."



Yuru rose.



She had no papers waiting for her.



No list of expenditures.



No request to settle a disagreement between two palace departments.



No one had sent a message asking whether a servant's appointment should be approved or whether a ceremonial expense could be reduced.



The palace was still operating.



That was, after all, the point.

In the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingyuan had reached the sixth cash book.



He had discovered that the sixth book was not especially different from the first five.



It contained payments, authorizations, receipts, corrections, and several entries whose handwriting appeared to have been produced by people who believed that ink was a limited resource.



The difficulty was not that the records were impossible to read.



The difficulty was that reading them did not automatically explain what had happened.



Mingyuan sat at the side of the accounts table while Steward Zhou stood beside him.



A second table had been set up near the window.



Liang Wen, the newly appointed clerk responsible for maintaining the consolidated purchasing register, sat there with a clean ledger.



He had been given the task the previous afternoon.



This morning, he had discovered that a clean ledger was not the same as a simple task.



"Steward Zhou," Liang Wen said, "the purchasing records contain several entries that do not state whether the goods were ordered, delivered, or merely approved."



"Then write down what they state."



"But the register is supposed to show the purchasing position."



"It can show the position that is known."



Liang Wen looked at the ledger.



"Should I leave the other columns blank?"



"Write that the information is missing."



"Would that not make the register look incomplete?"



Steward Zhou looked at him.



"It is incomplete."



Liang Wen lowered his brush.



Mingyuan, who had been listening, gave a quiet cough.



He had once believed that an account book should present a complete picture. If something was missing, someone should fill it in. If a total did not match, someone should find a way to make it match.



The past several weeks had taught him that a number written in a blank space did not become true merely because the page looked better.



"Use separate columns," Mingyuan said.



Liang Wen turned toward him.



"Which columns, Young Master?"



"Order authorization, delivery confirmation, payment, and remaining quantity. If the original record does not establish one of them, mark it as unconfirmed."



Liang Wen considered the suggestion.



"Then the register will have many unconfirmed entries."



"That is better than having confirmed entries that are not confirmed."



Steward Zhou glanced at Mingyuan.



The young master had said something sensible.



This was encouraging.



It was also slightly inconvenient, because Steward Zhou had become accustomed to the family heir saying sensible things only after someone else had explained them twice.



Liang Wen dipped his brush.



"Should I copy the original wording as well?"



"Yes," Steward Zhou answered. "Do not replace an unclear description with your own interpretation. You may add a separate note, but the original wording must remain."



Liang Wen nodded and began writing.



Mingyuan returned to the sixth cash book.



He had reached a payment entry from several years earlier.



The amount was recorded.



The authorization was recorded.



The recipient was not.



The description was brief: *Payment made according to the eldest young lady's instruction.*



Mingyuan read it again.



He had seen the entry before.



It was among the records that had been mentioned in the family's recent correspondence with Mingzhao.



The wording did not prove that the payment had been made to her. It did not prove that it had been made on her behalf. It did not even establish what the payment had been for.



It established that someone had recorded a payment under her authorization.



That was all.



"Steward Zhou."



"Young Master?"



"This entry. Do we have the supporting receipt?"



Steward Zhou leaned over.



He examined the page.



"There should be a corresponding voucher if the payment was made through the accounts office."



"Should be?"



"The old system was not always consistent."



Mingyuan looked at the entry.



"Can we ask the accounts office to search?"



"Yes. But we should give them the date and amount."



Mingyuan wrote both down.



"Then send a request for the original voucher and the name of the recipient."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"Would you like to ask your elder sister?"



Mingyuan paused.



It was a natural suggestion. Mingzhao had authorized many payments during the years she managed the estate. She might remember this one.



But he also remembered her latest letter.



She had agreed to answer specific questions about past authorizations. She had not agreed to become the estate's general reference book.



"Not yet," he said.



Steward Zhou waited.



"We should first find out whether the supporting record exists. If it does not, we can send her the entry and ask whether she recognizes it."



"That is reasonable."



Mingyuan returned to the ledger.



He did not enjoy the process.



It was slower than asking Mingzhao.



It was also more appropriate.



At the other table, Liang Wen was now copying an old purchasing entry into the new register.



He stopped halfway through.



"Steward Zhou?"



"What is it?"



"This record says that thirty lengths of cloth were purchased."



"Yes."



"The delivery column says twenty-eight."



"Then record twenty-eight."



"But the payment column says thirty."



Liang Wen looked up.



"Should I write that two lengths are missing?"



Steward Zhou shook his head.



"Write that the recorded payment covers thirty lengths, while the delivery record confirms twenty-eight. The difference is two. Do not write that the two lengths are missing until we establish that they were supposed to be delivered and were not recorded elsewhere."



Liang Wen looked at the page.



"That is a rather long note."



"It is a rather unclear transaction."



Liang Wen wrote it down.



Mingyuan glanced at the new register.



For the first time, he began to appreciate why Mingzhao had insisted that an account was not simply a collection of numbers.



A number could be correct and still fail to answer the question.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao was reading a letter from her family.



She had received it shortly before breakfast.



The letter was accompanied by a copied page from the sixth cash book and a short statement from Steward Zhou.



Mingzhao read the statement first.



Then she examined the copied entry.



The payment amount was clear.



The date was clear.



The authorization was attributed to her.



The recipient was not named.



The description was incomplete.



She placed the page beside the letter.



Qiao Yue entered with a tray.



"Another letter from the Marquisate?"



"Yes."



"Have they discovered that the accounts have been waiting for their attention for several years?"



"They have discovered that the accounts require attention."



"That is a less satisfying discovery."



Mingzhao picked up the letter again.



Her younger brother had written that the accounts office was searching for the supporting voucher. He had asked whether she remembered the payment.



The wording was restrained.



It did not ask her to take over the search.



It did not ask her to return to the estate.



It did not claim that the payment had been repaid.



Mingzhao found this improvement worth acknowledging.



She read the entry once more.



"Do you remember this?" Qiao Yue asked.



"I remember approving many payments. I do not remember this one."



"Then you cannot answer."



"I can answer that I do not remember it."



Qiao Yue placed the tea beside her.



"That seems a rather short answer."



"It is a short fact."



Mingzhao took a clean sheet of paper.



She wrote:



*I have reviewed the copied entry. The amount and date are legible, but the recipient and purpose are not recorded. I do not presently recall the transaction with sufficient certainty to identify either. Please obtain the original voucher, including any attached receipt or recipient's signature. Once the supporting record has been located, I can review it and answer whether I recognize the authorization.*



She paused.



Then she added:



*The entry alone does not establish that the amount was paid to me or that it was a repayment of any advance.*



Qiao Yue leaned slightly closer.



"That last sentence is important."



"Yes."



"Will they be offended?"



"If they are offended by an accurate description of the record, they should consider why."



Qiao Yue smiled.



Mingzhao folded the reply.



She had no intention of being difficult for the pleasure of being difficult.



But the family had spent years treating her familiarity with the estate as a substitute for proper records. Whenever a figure was unclear, someone had asked her to explain it. Whenever a payment was missing, someone had asked whether she remembered it. Whenever a steward could not identify a delivery, someone had suggested that she must know.



She had been expected to remember everything because she had managed everything.



That expectation had not been reasonable when she lived at the Marquisate.



It was even less reasonable now that she no longer did.



"Send it today," she said.



Qiao Yue collected the letter.



"Should I also remind them that you are not returning to manage the accounts?"



"No. They have already been told."



"Some people require repeated instruction."



"Then they can read the previous letters again."



Qiao Yue nodded.



She had learned that repeating an instruction was sometimes necessary.



She had also learned that Shen Mingzhao considered repetition a form of punishment best reserved for people who deserved it.

The silk workshop was quieter than usual when Mingzhao arrived.



Not because production had stopped.



The workers were busy.



The noise was simply more organized than it had been during the first days of the new order. The apprentices no longer needed to ask where every tool was kept, and the experienced finishers had become accustomed to the revised folding instructions.



The approved sample remained displayed beside the worktable.



The original sample and the revised sample were kept together.



No one was permitted to substitute one for the other.



Lin Shuyun was checking a bundle of finished bolts when Mingzhao entered.



"You're early," she said.



"The accounts at the Marquisate were slow."



"That sounds like a reason to arrive later."



"It is a reason to avoid going there again."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



She set the bundle aside.



"Today's group is nearly ready for internal inspection. The trainees are keeping up, though one of them still takes too long to check the edges."



"Which one?"



"The careful one."



"And the other?"



"Still quick. Still needs to be reminded that speed is not a measurement of quality."



Mingzhao looked toward the worktables.



The careful trainee was examining a folded edge, turning the bolt slightly under the light.



The faster trainee was checking a finished length while an experienced worker stood beside him.



"Have their supervisors changed the arrangements?"



"No. The same workers are supervising them. The careful one has been given a little more time, and the quick one must finish his inspection before moving to the next bolt."



"Has the production schedule been affected?"



"Not yet. We have enough time within the current schedule, but I would not recommend reducing the inspection period."



"I did not ask you to reduce it."



Lin Shuyun gave her a look.



"I know. I am reminding the workshop."



Mingzhao examined the completed group.



The bolts were cleanly folded, with consistent edges and no visible defects.



"How many?"



"Twenty-four ready for internal inspection. Another group is being finished."



Mingzhao nodded.



The buyer had accepted the previous ninety-six bolts.



The remaining one hundred and four still had to be produced, inspected, and accepted under the agreed schedule.



The workshop had made progress, but the work was not finished.



"Any change in the silk?" she asked.



Lin Shuyun shook her head.



"The latest delivery is consistent with the previous lot. We checked the thread strength and surface before assigning it to the worktables."



"Record the result."



"It has already been recorded."



"Then keep the sample."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"You say that as though we might suddenly forget what the silk looks like."



"I say it because someone may later claim that the sample was never approved."



"That is true."



They walked toward the rear worktable.



A senior finisher was reviewing the folding instructions with one of the trainees.



He pointed to the edge of a sample.



"The approved tension is here. Not tighter. If it is tighter, the edge changes."



The trainee nodded.



"Understood."



"Do not rely on memory. Compare it."



The trainee picked up the sample and examined it again.



Mingzhao watched for a moment.



The instruction was being followed.



That was more useful than hearing the worker say that he understood.



"How are the extra wages being recorded?" she asked.



Lin Shuyun brought out the small production ledger.



"Four workers remain on the additional order. Their ordinary wages are recorded separately from the training hours. The two trainees' supervision time is also listed."



"And the material costs?"



"Separate."



"Corrections?"



"Separate."



"Transport?"



"Separate."



Mingzhao examined the page.



The figures were not yet sufficient to calculate the final profit. The workshop had recorded several expenses, but the entire order had not been completed. Some transport costs would only be known after the next delivery.



Still, the records were in order.



"Good."



Lin Shuyun closed the ledger.



"Are you going to ask when the order will be finished?"



"No."



"Why not?"



"Because you have already told me the production schedule."



"And?"



"And I know that asking the same question again will not make the workers faster."



Lin Shuyun looked at her for a moment.



Then she laughed.



"You are becoming a very reasonable business partner."



"I have always been reasonable."



"That is not what your family says."



"My family has confused reasonable with convenient."



Lin Shuyun did not answer immediately.



She knew enough about the Marquisate's dispute to understand that the remark was not merely a joke.



After a moment, she said, "The buyer has not requested another inspection date yet. Zhou Jian said they would send notice once their own schedule was confirmed."



"Then we wait for the notice."



"That is all?"



"That is all."



Lin Shuyun shook her head.



"You have become unusually fond of waiting."



"I have become unusually unwilling to manufacture urgency."



The two women returned to the main table.



A worker approached with the inspection sheet.



"Manager Lin, the first twenty-four are ready."



Lin Shuyun took the sheet.



"Bring them forward."



Mingzhao stepped aside.



The inspection began.



One bolt was found to have a slightly uneven fold at the edge. The material was sound, but the fold did not match the approved sample.



The worker marked it for correction.



The other twenty-three passed.



No one argued that the difference was too small to matter.



No one suggested that the inspection should be shortened.



The bolt was taken back to the finishing table.



Mingzhao watched the correction being entered into the record.



"Was the worker who folded it one of the trainees?" she asked.



"No," Lin Shuyun replied. "It was an experienced finisher."



"Then do not put it under trainee correction."



"I won't."



"Record the cause if he can identify it."



Lin Shuyun nodded.



The mistake was small.



The correction would not delay the entire order.



But the record should still be accurate.



Mingzhao had spent too many years watching small inaccuracies become large arguments.



She had no intention of repeating the habit in a business that belonged to her.

At the palace, the former Empress had returned from her walk.



She sat near the window while Fang Ru poured tea.



The morning had been warm, but the western path was shaded. Yuru had walked slowly, stopping once to look at a flowering shrub near the wall.



She had not needed to decide whether the shrub should be moved, whether the gardener should be reprimanded, or whether the expense of its care was justified.



She had simply looked at it.



Fang Ru placed the tea before her.



"Your shoes are ready for tomorrow."



"Thank you."



"Would you like the longer path tomorrow?"



"Perhaps."



Fang Ru waited.



Yuru looked at the tea.



"Did the Dowager Empress send any message?"



"Only the usual greetings. There was no request for a visit."



"Good."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru smiled slightly.



"You sound relieved."



"I am relieved that no one has asked you to attend a meeting disguised as a visit."



"That has happened before."



"More than once."



Yuru lifted her cup.



The palace had not forgotten how to send invitations.



It was merely learning that not every invitation needed to carry a task.



She had not severed her relationships with the people who lived there. She still cared about the Emperor, the Dowager Empress, and the imperial children. If they asked for personal advice, she could choose to answer.



But she was no longer the person responsible for making every answer into an order.



That was a distinction the palace would need time to understand.



"Fang Ru."



"Yes, Madam?"



"Have the tea prepared for the visit to my parents."



"I will."



"And please make sure my father has not arranged a formal reception."



Fang Ru looked down.



"I cannot promise that."



Yuru sighed.



"Then ask my mother to stop him."



"I believe she has already done so."



"Then we may yet have a peaceful afternoon."



Fang Ru smiled.



The former Empress took another sip of tea.



She had three days before the visit.



There was no need to spend all three preparing for it.

That afternoon, at the Marquisate, Mingyuan received a reply from the accounts office.



The original voucher had not yet been found.



The clerk had located a reference to the payment in a secondary index, but the index did not identify the recipient. It merely confirmed that a voucher had once been filed under the relevant date.



Mingyuan read the note.



Steward Zhou stood beside him.



"At least we know there was a voucher," Mingyuan said.



"We know there was an index entry referring to one."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"That is not the same thing."



"No."



Mingyuan placed the note beside the cash book.



"Then the search continues."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"Should we ask the accounts office to search the old storage boxes?"



"Yes. Give them the reference number and ask them to record which boxes they examine."



"That may take several days."



"Then it will take several days."



Steward Zhou regarded him with mild approval.



The young master was learning to tolerate the time required by proper work.



Mingyuan glanced at the new purchasing register.



Liang Wen had entered the thirty-length cloth transaction with a note distinguishing payment from delivery.



The register looked less tidy than he had expected.



It was also more useful.



"How is the new register?" he asked.



Liang Wen looked up.



"I have copied the first section. There are eleven entries where the delivery quantity differs from the payment quantity, and four where the delivery is recorded but the payment reference is missing."



Mingyuan frowned.



"Eleven?"



"Yes, Young Master."



"Are they all from the same period?"



"No. They are spread across several years."



Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou.



"Have these differences been reviewed before?"



"Some may have been. I cannot say whether all were."



"Then do not treat them as new losses."



"I would not."



"List them separately for examination."



Liang Wen bowed his head.



"Understood."



Mingyuan looked again at the ledger.



The new register was already showing that the estate's purchasing records were not as complete as the family had assumed.



That did not mean eleven losses had occurred.



It meant eleven differences required explanation.



There was a considerable distance between those two statements.



Mingyuan had begun to understand that distance.

By evening, the former Empress had finished her tea and read her mother's invitation once more.



She placed it in the correspondence box.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao's reply to the family had been dispatched, and the workshop had recorded twenty-three bolts passing internal inspection, with one returned for correction.



In the accounts office, Liang Wen continued entering the purchasing records.



Mingyuan had not found the missing voucher.



The old warehouse records had not yet explained the twenty-seven sacks of grain.



The family property division remained unfinished.



The new silk order was still in production.



The palace had not appointed a new Empress.



The former Empress had not chosen a permanent residence.



None of these matters had been resolved by the end of the sixth day of the sixth month.



But several people had made small decisions that would make the next day more manageable.



Yuru had decided to visit her parents without turning the visit into an official occasion.



Mingzhao had decided not to answer an unclear financial entry with an uncertain memory.



Mingyuan had decided that a missing record should remain missing until it was found.



Liang Wen had discovered that a register could be useful even when it contained blank spaces.



And the workshop had corrected one uneven fold without declaring the entire production schedule a disaster.



It was not a dramatic day.



For once, that was a considerable achievement.
