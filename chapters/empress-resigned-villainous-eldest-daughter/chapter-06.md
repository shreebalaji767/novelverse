# **Chapter 6: The Second Prince Has Discovered That Personal Expenditure Is Not a Household Expense. The Marquis Has Discovered That His Daughter Keeps Copies.**

**28th Day of the Third Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Second Prince Zhao Chengyu was not opposed to accounting.

He was opposed to being questioned about his accounting.

There was, in his opinion, a considerable difference.

He stood in the eastern reception room of his residence, holding the emperor's instruction in one hand while his steward, Sun De, waited beside the desk.

“Read it again,” Chengyu said.

Sun De lowered his head.

“His Majesty has ordered Your Highness to review the household accounts, including the records of supplies, expenditure, and outstanding payments. Any expenditure charged to the household must be supported by the appropriate records.”

The Second Prince frowned.

“I heard you the first time.”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

“Then why are you reading it again?”

“Your Highness asked me to.”

Chengyu placed the instruction on the desk.

“Does Father believe I am unable to manage my own residence?”

Sun De did not answer.

“Or does he believe the Empress has been concealing something?”

The steward looked even more uncomfortable.

“His Majesty did not make such an accusation.”

“Then why has he ordered an examination?”

Sun De considered his words carefully.

“The imperial household administration is reviewing its procedures. The Eastern Palace has already begun examining its records.”

Chengyu looked at him.

“So the Crown Prince has agreed.”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

“Of course he has.”

The Second Prince walked to the window.

His elder brother was diligent, obedient, and very good at appearing reasonable. He could spend an entire afternoon reviewing a document without showing the slightest sign of irritation.

Chengyu had never considered that a particularly admirable quality.

He turned back.

“Bring me the expenditure ledger.”

Sun De immediately sent for it.

When it arrived, Chengyu opened the book.

The first pages contained ordinary expenses: kitchen supplies, servants' allowances, repairs, clothing, lamps, and other household requirements.

He turned several pages.

Then he stopped.

“What is this?”

Sun De leaned forward.

“Which entry, Your Highness?”

“Three hundred taels for furnishings and reception arrangements.”

Sun De examined the entry.

“That payment was made during the seventh month.”

“I know when it was made. Why was it charged to the household?”

“It was recorded as an expense for Your Highness's residence.”

“Was the money used for the reception?”

“Part of it.”

“Part?”

Sun De bowed.

“The remaining amount was used for several purchases arranged during the same period.”

“What purchases?”

Sun De hesitated.

“I will have to examine the receipts.”

Chengyu looked at him.

“Do you not know?”

“I know some of them, Your Highness. I do not know whether the receipts account for the entire amount.”

The Second Prince stared at the ledger.

He had not realized that a single entry could contain so many unanswered questions.

“Bring the original receipts.”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

“And separate the expenditure for the reception from the other purchases.”

Sun De bowed.

Chengyu returned to his chair.

He was beginning to understand why the emperor had ordered the examination.

He was not yet convinced that the emperor had chosen the best possible method of making the point.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had a similar collection of papers before her.

The independent accountant, Sun Qiming, had agreed to examine the estate accounts.

He had sent his terms that morning.

His fee was based on the number of account books and the time required for verification. He would not accept payment from either party personally; the fee would be paid from the agreed examination fund and recorded as a settlement expense.

He had also requested access to the original ledgers, supporting receipts, warehouse records, land registers, and outstanding debt documents.

Mingzhao read the terms carefully.

“Reasonable,” she said.

Steward Zhou stood beside the table.

“Mr. Sun has asked whether he may begin with the commercial accounts.”

“He may begin with the accounts that are ready. The grain records still require the original receipts from the receiving estate.”

“Should we provide him with the copies?”

“Yes. Mark them as copies and explain that the originals have been requested.”

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingzhao took another document from the bundle.

It was a list of her independently held commercial interests.

The first entry concerned a silk warehouse partnership with Lin Shuyun. The second concerned a smaller investment in a cloth distribution business. The third concerned an outstanding loan to a merchant who had borrowed money through a contract signed in Mingzhao's own name.

She examined each entry.

The silk warehouse partnership had generated a modest profit in the previous year. The cloth business had performed less well because of delayed deliveries and higher transport costs.

The loan remained outstanding.

Mingzhao marked the interest payments that had been received and the principal still owed.

Qiao Yue looked at the list.

“Eldest Miss, these are all yours?”

“They are interests held in my name. Their exact ownership and liabilities must still be verified.”

“But you have the contracts.”

“Yes.”

“And the payment records?”

“Most of them.”

Qiao Yue smiled.

“Then the family cannot claim that everything belongs to the marquisate.”

Mingzhao looked at her.

“The family can make whatever claim it wishes. Whether the claim is supported by law and evidence is another matter.”

Qiao Yue's smile faded.

Mingzhao continued, “Do not assume that a document automatically settles a dispute. The contract may be incomplete. A payment may have been made through another account. An asset may have a liability attached to it.”

She returned to the papers.

“I want Sun Qiming to examine these separately from the family estate accounts.”

Steward Zhou made a note.

“Should I inform the marquis?”

“Yes. The independent interests must be identified before any discussion of division.”

He bowed.

Mingzhao paused.

“And Steward Zhou?”

“Yes, Eldest Miss?”

“Make a separate copy of every document we provide.”

“We already have copies of the account books.”

“I mean every document. Every receipt, contract, correspondence, valuation, and acknowledgment.”

Steward Zhou nodded.

“I will arrange it.”

Mingzhao looked at the bundles.

She had learned something over six years of managing the estate.

A person who controlled the accounts controlled the explanation of the accounts.

She would not allow the explanation to disappear when she left.

The Marquis of Qingyuan received the news of the independent accountant's appointment shortly before noon.

Shen Guowei read the letter from Steward Zhou.

“Sun Qiming?”

“Yes, my lord,” the steward replied.

“Who recommended him?”

“His references were provided through the commercial association. He has examined several large estates and merchant accounts.”

“Is he reliable?”

Steward Zhou answered carefully.

“His previous clients have given satisfactory references.”

Shen Guowei looked at him.

“Have you met him?”

“No, my lord.”

“Then how do you know?”

“I do not know personally. That is why his qualifications and previous work have been checked.”

The marquis put down the letter.

He was not opposed to an independent examination.

He was opposed to the possibility that the examination might expose problems he had not been aware of.

For years, the estate had appeared stable. Its granaries were full, its tenants paid their dues, its servants performed their duties, and the family maintained its position in the capital.

He knew there had been expenses.

He knew his son had borrowed money from time to time.

He knew Mingzhao had often complained about incomplete records.

But he had assumed that these were ordinary difficulties in managing a large household.

He had not expected his eldest daughter to turn them into the central questions of a property settlement.

“Where is Mingzhao now?” he asked.

“In her eastern courtyard.”

“Send word that I wish to speak with her.”

Steward Zhou bowed.

Mingzhao arrived at her father's study shortly afterward.

Shen Guowei gestured toward the chair opposite him.

“Sit.”

She sat.

The marquis placed Sun Qiming's terms on the table.

“You have appointed an independent accountant.”

“I have agreed to his terms. The family has not yet formally confirmed the appointment.”

“I will confirm it.”

Mingzhao nodded.

Shen Guowei studied her.

“Do you intend to examine every account in the estate?”

“Every account relevant to the settlement.”

“That could take months.”

“Then it will take months.”

“You cannot expect the household to suspend its ordinary business while an accountant examines every receipt.”

“I have not asked it to.”

“Then what do you expect?”

“I expect ordinary business to continue, with the accounts preserved and supporting documents made available.”

Shen Guowei leaned back.

“You have always been meticulous.”

Mingzhao did not respond.

He continued, “Perhaps too meticulous.”

“Perhaps.”

Her agreement seemed to irritate him more than an argument would have.

“You understand that the family cannot simply divide everything into separate portions. Some assets must remain together.”

“I understand.”

“The ancestral estate cannot be divided according to convenience.”

“I have not requested that it be.”

“The family land, ancestral property, and certain household assets are subject to restrictions.”

“I have not requested those either.”

Shen Guowei frowned.

“Then why does every conversation become so difficult?”

Mingzhao looked at him.

“Because we keep discussing what I have not requested instead of identifying what I have.”

The marquis was silent.

She opened her document case and placed three sheets on the table.

“The first is a preliminary list of my personal property. The second is a list of commercial interests held in my name. The third is a list of funds advanced to the household for which I have supporting records.”

Shen Guowei examined the pages.

The figures were not insignificant.

He looked at the second sheet.

“This silk warehouse partnership was established through the family trading house.”

“The initial introduction was made through the family trading house. The investment was made from my own funds.”

“Where did you obtain those funds?”

“From the income of my inherited property and the returns on my previous investments.”

“Did the family approve the arrangement?”

“The family was informed.”

“That is not the same as approval.”

“The contract does not require the family's approval. It was signed by the parties to the partnership.”

Shen Guowei looked at her.

“Are you saying the family has no interest in it?”

“I am saying that the family's interest must be established by the contract, not by the fact that I once lived in this house.”

He put the pages down.

“You have become very formal.”

“I have become very careful.”

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Shen Guowei asked, “What do you intend to do after you leave?”

“Establish my own household.”

“And then?”

“Continue my commercial interests.”

“Will you remain in the capital?”

“Yes.”

“Will you attend family ceremonies?”

“That depends on the circumstances.”

The answer was not disrespectful.

It was simply not a promise.

Shen Guowei looked away.

“You are still my daughter.”

Mingzhao's expression did not change.

“I have never disputed that.”

The marquis did not know what to say.

After a while, he returned the documents.

“I will confirm the accountant's appointment. But I expect the examination to be conducted fairly.”

“So do I.”

Mingzhao rose.

At the door, Shen Guowei called her again.

“Mingzhao.”

She turned.

“Do not make this more difficult than it needs to be.”

She looked at him for a moment.

“Then let us not make it more difficult than it needs to be.”

She bowed and left.

In the imperial palace, the Second Prince was having a different conversation with his steward.

Sun De had brought the original receipts.

Chengyu examined them one by one.

The first receipt concerned a set of furniture purchased for the reception room.

The second concerned repairs to a courtyard wall.

The third concerned several decorative objects.

The fourth concerned food and wine for a gathering.

Chengyu looked at the final receipt.

“Why is this included?”

Sun De answered, “The purchase was made through the household office.”

“Was the gathering an official household function?”

“No, Your Highness.”

“Then why was it charged to the household?”

Sun De lowered his head.

“The payment was made from the household account.”

“That is not an answer.”

“No, Your Highness.”

The Second Prince placed the receipt aside.

“Move this expenditure to my personal account.”

“Yes, Your Highness.”

“And the furniture?”

“It remains in the residence.”

“Then it should remain in the household inventory.”

“Yes.”

“The repairs?”

“They were made to the courtyard wall.”

“Then they are household expenses.”

Sun De bowed.

Chengyu continued through the receipts.

Some expenses were legitimate. Some belonged to his personal account. Some required clarification.

The work was tedious.

It was also not particularly complicated once the documents were arranged properly.

He looked at the steward.

“How long have these accounts been prepared in this manner?”

Sun De answered, “For several years.”

“And no one separated personal and household expenditure?”

“The accounts were consolidated before being submitted.”

“By whom?”

“The household office.”

Chengyu frowned.

“Then who was responsible for checking them?”

Sun De hesitated.

“Previously, the Empress's office reviewed the consolidated reports.”

The Second Prince looked down.

He remembered his mother asking him about certain expenses when he was younger. At the time, he had considered the questions unnecessary.

He had assumed that the palace administration simply functioned.

Now he was discovering that the Empress's office had been doing far more than he had realized.

“From now on,” he said, “personal expenditure must be recorded separately before it enters the household account.”

Sun De bowed.

“Should I send the corrected ledger to Chengming Palace?”

“No. Send it to the reviewing office when the procedure is established.”

Chengyu paused.

“Has the Crown Prince completed his examination?”

“Not yet, Your Highness.”

“Then I will complete mine before he does.”

Sun De lowered his head.

He did not comment on the sudden competitiveness.

At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru received a request from the Ministry of Rites.

The ministry wanted clarification concerning the Empress's authority over the annual ceremonies conducted within the inner palace.

The request was lengthy.

The question was simple.

The Ministry of Rites wanted to know whether it would be responsible for determining ceremonial requirements while the inner palace administrators handled the arrangements.

Xu Yuru read the document.

Then she read the attached regulations.

The distinction had already been established.

The Ministry of Rites governed the ceremonial standards and requirements. The inner palace administration arranged the internal performance of ceremonies according to those standards. The Empress's office had supervised the internal arrangements and approved certain exceptional expenditures.

There was no need for a new regulation merely to restate the existing division.

She wrote a short reply.

The Ministry of Rites should continue to determine the ceremonial requirements under existing regulations. The inner palace administration should manage the internal arrangements. Expenditure should be examined according to the approved budget and relevant financial procedures. Exceptional matters should be submitted through the designated authority.

She signed the reply.

Madam Fang read it.

“Should we send it directly to the Ministry of Rites?”

“Yes.”

“Without submitting it to His Majesty?”

“The emperor has already authorized the review of these responsibilities. This is an administrative clarification within the existing rules.”

Madam Fang nodded.

Xu Yuru put the document aside.

Then she looked at the remaining papers.

There were still matters that required her decision.

There were also matters that did not.

She had begun marking them separately.

The distinction was becoming clearer.

It was not becoming easier.

That afternoon, Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an arrived at Chengming Palace.

Xu Yuru looked up from her documents.

“Cheng'an.”

“Mother.”

He bowed and sat after she gestured.

“I heard that you have begun examining the Eastern Palace accounts.”

“Yes.”

“How is it going?”

Cheng'an considered his answer.

“More slowly than I expected.”

Xu Yuru smiled faintly.

“That is usually a good sign.”

He placed a small report on the table.

“I found several incomplete records. The household office has begun comparing its inventory with the purchase records.”

“Have you identified any actual losses?”

“Not yet. There are differences, but we have not established their causes.”

“Good.”

Cheng'an looked at her.

“Mother, did you examine all these reports yourself?”

“Not every one.”

“But you reviewed the consolidated accounts?”

“Usually.”

“How often?”

“When necessary.”

He looked at the papers on her desk.

“That must have taken a great deal of time.”

“It did.”

“Why did you never ask me to examine the Eastern Palace accounts?”

Xu Yuru considered the question.

“Because I did not think it necessary.”

“Was that because I was inexperienced?”

“Partly.”

Cheng'an looked down.

She continued, “I also believed that if I reviewed the reports myself, the work would be completed more quickly.”

“That was true.”

“Yes.”

He waited.

Xu Yuru put down her brush.

“But speed is not the only measure of whether an arrangement is sound.”

The Crown Prince nodded slowly.

“Father has asked me to continue the examination.”

“He is right.”

“Should I send you a copy of the results?”

“You may send the report to the designated reviewing office. I do not need to receive a separate copy simply because I am your mother.”

Cheng'an accepted the correction.

Then he asked, “Will you really leave the palace administration?”

Xu Yuru looked at him.

“I intend to relinquish the responsibilities that do not properly belong to the Empress's office. Whether I formally leave the position itself remains under review.”

“Would you still advise us?”

“If you ask me for personal advice, I will answer as your mother. But I do not intend to remain the person who settles every administrative dispute.”

Cheng'an was silent for a moment.

Then he bowed.

“I understand.”

Xu Yuru regarded him.

“Do you?”

“I believe I am beginning to.”

She smiled.

“Then you are already further along than some of the officials.”

The independent accountant arrived at the Qingyuan Marquisate shortly before the evening meal.

Sun Qiming was a man in his early fifties, with a composed face and a habit of listening carefully before speaking.

He was accompanied by one assistant.

Mingzhao received him in the eastern study.

“Mr. Sun.”

“Eldest Miss.”

They exchanged formal greetings.

Sun Qiming placed his credentials on the table.

“I have reviewed the terms of the examination. I understand that the work concerns both the marquisate's estate accounts and your personal claims.”

“Yes.”

“I would like to clarify the limits of my authority before beginning.”

Mingzhao gestured for him to continue.

“I can examine the records, compare accounts, verify payments, identify discrepancies, and prepare a report of assets and liabilities. I cannot determine legal ownership where the documents are disputed. That must be decided through the appropriate legal process.”

“Agreed.”

“I will not make an accusation of fraud based solely on a difference in the books.”

“Agreed.”

“If original documents are unavailable, I will identify the limitation in the report rather than treat an unsupported entry as verified.”

Mingzhao nodded.

“That is what I expect.”

Sun Qiming opened his document case.

“I will also require a written record of the documents provided to me, with both parties acknowledging the transfer.”

“Certainly.”

He glanced at the shelves.

“Which accounts are ready for examination?”

Mingzhao indicated three bundles.

“These concern my personal commercial interests. The family estate accounts are in the larger case. The northern warehouse records are incomplete, and the receiving estate has supplied copies but not the originals.”

Sun Qiming examined the first bundle.

“These contracts appear to be separately signed.”

“They are.”

“Were the funds paid from your personal account?”

“Yes. The relevant payment records are attached.”

“Then I will examine those first.”

Mingzhao nodded.

He turned another page.

“This loan to the merchant is still outstanding?”

“Yes.”

“Is there a repayment agreement?”

“Yes. The original is included.”

“Has the borrower been informed that the loan is part of the settlement examination?”

“Not yet. I intend to send a formal notice once the principal and interest have been verified.”

Sun Qiming looked at her.

“That is sensible.”

Mingzhao did not smile.

“I would rather not discover a missing payment after the settlement has been signed.”

Sun Qiming returned to the papers.

“I understand.”

The examination began the following morning.

The first task was not to calculate the value of the marquisate.

It was to establish what documents existed.

Sun Qiming's assistant prepared a register of the books and papers received. Each item was given a number. The person providing it was recorded, and the condition of the document was noted.

Steward Zhou watched the process carefully.

Mingzhao sat nearby, checking the entries.

When the assistant reached the commercial partnership records, he paused.

“This contract has two attached schedules, but only one is included.”

Mingzhao examined it.

“The second schedule is held by Lin Shuyun.”

“Should we request it?”

“Yes. The partnership accounts cannot be fully verified without it.”

The assistant made a note.

Sun Qiming examined the next document.

“This land register contains a later amendment.”

Mingzhao leaned forward.

“Which amendment?”

“The eastern field boundary was adjusted three years ago.”

“That concerns the family estate.”

“Was the adjustment registered?”

“It should have been.”

Sun Qiming looked at the document again.

“Then the registration record should be obtained.”

Mingzhao nodded.

The examination was proceeding more slowly than she had hoped.

But it was proceeding properly.

By the end of the morning, the accountant had established that the family accounts contained several categories of records that could not be compared directly without further work.

Some expenses had been recorded by date.

Others had been recorded by the household responsible for them.

Some payments had been entered when authorized, while others had been entered when the money was actually transferred.

The same amount could therefore appear in more than one book without necessarily representing two separate payments.

Sun Qiming explained this to Mingzhao.

She listened carefully.

“Then we must identify the original transaction before calculating the total.”

“Precisely.”

“Otherwise we may count one payment twice.”

“Or overlook a payment because it appears under a different heading.”

Mingzhao nodded.

She was pleased to have found someone who understood the difference between a neat ledger and an accurate one.

That evening, Shen Guowei received the first brief report from the accountant.

The report did not contain a final valuation.

It did not contain accusations.

It contained a list of records requiring further examination.

The marquis read it.

Then he looked at Steward Zhou.

“How many documents are missing?”

“Several supporting records have not yet been located.”

“Which accounts?”

“The northern warehouse, the commercial partnership records, certain household repair expenses, and the three hundred taels charged to the Second Young Master's residence.”

Shen Guowei looked up.

“Again?”

“The receipts have not been submitted.”

“Has Mingyuan been informed?”

“Yes, my lord.”

“What did he say?”

“He has been asked to provide the details of the expenditure.”

The marquis pressed his fingers against his forehead.

For the first time, the three hundred taels seemed less like a small household matter and more like a warning that the estate's records were not as complete as he had believed.

“Have the old accounts been preserved?”

“Yes.”

“All of them?”

“As far as we can establish.”

Shen Guowei looked at him.

“What does that mean?”

Steward Zhou answered carefully.

“There are records from several years ago that were damaged by dampness. Some pages are difficult to read. There are also receipts that were kept separately from the main ledgers.”

“Why was I not informed?”

Steward Zhou lowered his head.

“Your Lordship, the records were previously managed by the household office.”

The marquis looked at him.

It was not an accusation.

It was an explanation.

An explanation that he had no convenient way to dismiss.

“Find the damaged records,” he said. “And have them examined.”

“Yes, my lord.”

Shen Guowei returned to the report.

He had wanted the matter settled quietly.

He was beginning to understand that quietness and completeness were not the same thing.

At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru received the revised responsibility register.

The number remained ninety-four.

But the distribution was beginning to take shape.

Several matters had been assigned to existing offices. Others required written procedures. A smaller number remained under direct imperial authority.

The Empress's office would no longer serve as the automatic destination for every disagreement concerning the inner palace.

She reviewed the final column.

There were still questions concerning the supervision of senior servants, the handling of exceptional ceremonial expenditure, and the relationship between palace household accounts and the Ministry of Revenue.

Those matters would require further discussion.

She put the register down.

Madam Fang asked, “Your Majesty, do you think the review is progressing?”

“Yes.”

“Will it be completed soon?”

Xu Yuru looked at the stack of documents.

“No.”

Madam Fang did not seem surprised.

“Then we must continue.”

“Yes.”

Xu Yuru rose and walked to the window.

The palace courtyard was quiet.

A servant crossed the path carrying a small bundle of supplies. Another followed with a document case.

The palace continued its ordinary work.

It was not collapsing.

It was not thriving independently either.

But it had begun to notice the difference between an instruction and a responsibility, between a purchase and an inventory, between a person who approved a document and a person who was expected to solve every problem connected to it.

Xu Yuru returned to her desk.

“Prepare the next set of transition notes.”

Madam Fang bowed.

“And Madam Fang?”

“Yes, Your Majesty?”

“Include the matters that are still unresolved. Do not make the report appear more complete than it is.”

“Understood.”

Xu Yuru picked up her brush.

Across the capital, the Marquis of Qingyuan was learning that his household accounts required more attention than he had given them.

His eldest son was learning that a payment charged to his residence needed an explanation.

His eldest daughter was learning that leaving a family required more than deciding to leave.

And the emperor was learning that an imperial household could not be reorganized merely by ordering everyone to take responsibility.

It required people to know what they were responsible for.

That lesson was proving expensive in paper, ink, time, and patience.

The palace had plenty of all four.

The marquisate, unfortunately, was beginning to discover that it had not kept enough of the first.

**End of Chapter 6.**
