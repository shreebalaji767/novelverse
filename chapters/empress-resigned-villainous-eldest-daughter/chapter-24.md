# **Chapter 24: The Empress Has Been Told That Her Successor Will Be Decided Later. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Told That Her Debts Must Be Decided First.**



**16th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**







The Emperor had received three recommendations concerning the future of the Empress's position.







The first recommended appointing a new Empress as soon as possible.







The second recommended allowing a temporary period without a new appointment while the inner palace's administrative transition was completed.







The third recommended establishing a special supervisory office to oversee the inner palace until a new Empress was appointed.







Zhao Jingheng read all three.







Then he looked at Minister Han.







"Who proposed the third recommendation?"







Minister Han bowed.







"The Ministry of Personnel, Your Majesty."







"What authority would this office possess?"







"It would coordinate the existing responsibilities."







"Which responsibilities?"







"The responsibilities currently handled by the Empress's office."







The Emperor looked at the document.







"Have those responsibilities not already been assigned?"







"Some have, Your Majesty."







"Then why does this office need to coordinate them?"







Minister Han paused.







"To ensure continuity."







"Who would supervise the office?"







"That would require further consideration."







"Who would appoint its officials?"







"That would also require further consideration."







"Who would pay for it?"







Minister Han hesitated.







The Emperor set down the report.







"You have recommended an office without defining its authority, personnel, or expenditure."







Minister Han bowed lower.







"Your Majesty, the proposal is preliminary."







"Then it should have remained preliminary."







The Emperor returned the document.







"Reject the third recommendation. The existing offices will continue their responsibilities. If a specific gap is identified, submit a proposal addressing that gap."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







The Emperor examined the other two recommendations.







He did not immediately approve either.







"Keep the position vacant until I make a decision. The ceremony will proceed as scheduled. The administrative arrangements will not be changed merely to fill an empty title."







Minister Han bowed.







The Emperor added:







"And stop sending me proposals for offices that do not yet have a purpose."







Minister Han accepted the instruction.







He had begun to suspect that the Emperor's current administrative philosophy could be summarized in a sentence:







If something did not need to exist, it should not be created.







This was an inconvenient philosophy for officials whose careers depended on creating things.



At Chengming Palace, Yuru received the Emperor's decision.







Fang Ru placed the written notice before her.







"The position will remain vacant until His Majesty decides otherwise."







Yuru read it.







"Good."







"The Ministry of Personnel has also been instructed not to submit another proposal for a supervisory office."







"That is also good."







Fang Ru hesitated.







"Your Majesty, some senior palace officials are concerned about the absence of an Empress."







"Are they concerned about the absence of an Empress or the absence of someone to whom they can send their documents?"







Fang Ru lowered her head.







"I cannot say."







Yuru smiled faintly.







"Then they should identify the concern before submitting another report."







Fang Ru took the notice.







"Shall I inform the senior administrators?"







"Yes. They should understand that the existing offices remain responsible for their assigned duties."







"Understood."







Yuru looked toward the garden.







Eight days remained until the ceremony.







She had expected the final days to involve packing, arrangements, and perhaps a little rest.







Instead, the palace continued to discover new ways to ask her to solve problems that would remain after she left.







She would have to be careful.







A person could spend an entire life preparing to leave and still find herself working on the day of departure.



The Crown Prince arrived shortly afterward.







He carried the latest Eastern Palace accounts.







Yuru looked at the documents.







"Have you completed the reconciliation?"







"Yes, Mother."







"Who examined it?"







"The Eastern Palace accountant and the financial clerk."







"Did they agree?"







"Not initially."







"What was the disagreement?"







"The accountant included the remaining lamp oil from the previous month in the current month's stock. The clerk counted only the new delivery."







Yuru looked at him.







"And how did you resolve it?"







"We compared the opening inventory, new purchases, distribution, and remaining stock."







"Good."







The Crown Prince placed the corrected report before her.







Yuru examined the figures.







They were consistent.







She handed the report back.







"Submit it to the designated office."







Cheng'an nodded.







He did not immediately leave.







"Mother, I have a question."







"Ask."







"If the palace has no Empress for some time, should the Crown Prince take responsibility for the inner palace?"







Yuru looked at him.







"No."







"Even temporarily?"







"Your responsibility is the Eastern Palace and your duties as Crown Prince. The inner palace has established offices. The Emperor retains authority over matters that require his decision."







"But if there is a dispute?"







"Then the responsible office should follow the proper procedure. If the dispute exceeds its authority, it should be referred to the Emperor."







Cheng'an thought about this.







"So I should not become the person everyone sends their reports to."







Yuru looked at him.







"Have you not learned anything from the last three weeks?"







He gave a small, embarrassed smile.







"I have."







"Then do not repeat the palace's mistakes."







Cheng'an bowed.







He was beginning to understand that becoming a ruler did not mean accepting every responsibility offered to him.







Sometimes it meant refusing responsibilities that belonged elsewhere.



At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the additional table had arrived.







It was placed in Mingzhao's study.







Qiao Yue stood beside it, looking pleased.







"Miss, the documents can now be separated."







Mingzhao examined the table.







One side held personal-property records.







The other held family-estate accounts.







A smaller section was reserved for commercial interests.







The remaining space contained unresolved claims and liabilities.







Mingzhao looked at the arrangement.







"Better."







Qiao Yue placed the latest report on the commercial section.







"The silk buyer has confirmed the delivery schedule."







Mingzhao read it.







The first inspected group would be delivered the following day.







The remaining stock would be inspected after finishing.







The buyer had agreed to pay the outstanding balance upon delivery, according to the contract.







"Has the carrier been appointed?"







"Yes. Lin Shuyun has confirmed the arrangements."







"Then the delivery record must be signed by both sides."







Qiao Yue nodded.







Mingzhao examined the report again.







The transaction was progressing properly.







She placed it with the partnership records.







Then she turned to the liabilities schedule.







Sun Qiming had arrived and was waiting with a new report.







"Miss Shen, I have reviewed the documents you provided yesterday."







"Have you identified the twenty-three uncertain entries?"







"Several have been clarified. Others remain unresolved."







"How many?"







"Nine are now supported by sufficient evidence. Four have been partially confirmed. Ten remain uncertain."







Mingzhao examined the list.







"Which nine?"







Sun Qiming pointed to the entries.







They included payments for grain seed, several household repairs, and medical expenses.







The evidence showed that Mingzhao had paid the amounts from her personal funds.







In two cases, the estate had later reimbursed her.







In the others, the original payment remained outstanding.







Mingzhao checked the figures.







"These should be listed as confirmed advances, subject to the final settlement."







"Yes."







"And the four partially confirmed entries?"







"The payments are established, but the repayment arrangements are not."







"Keep them separate."







Sun Qiming nodded.







Mingzhao examined the ten remaining entries.







Some were old.







Some had no receipts.







Others had only a brief notation in the household ledger.







She recognized several.







One concerned a payment to a travelling physician.







Another involved a shipment of grain.







The third was a contribution to repairs at a secondary residence.







She could not establish whether those amounts had been repaid.







"Do not include them in the confirmed total."







"Understood."







Mingzhao placed the documents down.







The confirmed claims were smaller than the amount she had initially expected.







She was not pleased.







But the evidence was clear.







She would not turn uncertainty into a claim simply because she wanted a larger settlement.



The property assessor arrived later that morning.







Master Luo had completed his inspection of the agricultural holdings.







He presented his preliminary findings to Shen Guowei, Mr. Liang, Mingzhao, and Sun Qiming.







"The family owns several categories of agricultural land. Some parcels are directly cultivated, others are leased, and some have been left fallow."







Shen Guowei examined the report.







"Why are the fallow parcels valued differently?"







"Because their current productivity is different."







"They are still land."







"Yes, Marquis. But their value is affected by soil condition, water access, location, and the cost of bringing them back into cultivation."







Mingzhao looked through the details.







One parcel had been unused for two years because of drainage problems.







Another had been leased at a fixed rate.







A third was productive but had a disputed boundary with a neighbouring estate.







"Have you inspected the boundary markers?" she asked.







"Not yet. The neighbouring owner must be notified before the survey."







"Then do not include a final valuation for that parcel."







Master Luo nodded.







Shen Guowei looked displeased.







"Must every property have a separate qualification?"







"Yes," Mingzhao replied.







He turned toward her.







"You make the process sound endless."







"It would be shorter if the records were complete."







Mr. Liang coughed.







It was not clear whether he was suppressing a laugh.







Master Luo continued.







"The final valuation will distinguish confirmed property, property requiring further inspection, and assets whose ownership or boundaries remain disputed."







Mingzhao nodded.







"That is acceptable."







Shen Guowei looked at the report again.







He had expected the assessor to provide a single figure.







Instead, he had received a document full of conditions.







The Marquisate was discovering that its wealth was not as simple as the number of fields it owned.



At midday, the transport contractor returned with his notebook.







Liu Shun placed the small book before Sun Qiming.







The pages contained dates, destinations, cart numbers, and payment entries.







The handwriting was inconsistent.







Several pages were stained.







Sun Qiming examined the relevant dates.







"Here. This entry."







Liu Shun leaned forward.







"That was during the warehouse repairs."







"What does this notation mean?"







"Three carts went to the southern road."







"Does it mean three carts delivered grain to the southern storage facility?"







Liu Shun studied the page.







"I believe so."







"How many sacks?"







"There is no number."







"Did you count them?"







"No."







"Who received the goods?"







"I cannot remember."







Mingzhao examined the notebook.







There was another entry later that day.







It recorded a journey to the outer road.







She pointed to it.







"What does this refer to?"







Liu Shun looked.







"I remember that journey. One cart had a damaged wheel."







"Was the cart carrying grain?"







"I do not know."







"Was it the same cart?"







"I cannot say."







Sun Qiming wrote down the answer.







The notebook established that the contractor had made several journeys during the repair period.







It did not establish the quantity of grain transported.







It did not establish the destination of every load.







It did not confirm the two sacks.







Mingzhao looked at the records.







"Can you identify the cart drivers?"







Liu Shun thought.







"Two of them were my regular drivers. One has since left the district."







"Can you provide their names?"







"Yes."







"Do so."







Liu Shun bowed.







After he left, Sun Qiming closed the notebook.







"The transport records are useful, but they do not resolve the discrepancy."







Mingzhao nodded.







"Then the report should say so."







Steward Zhou looked at the documents.







"Could the two sacks have been unloaded at the outer road?"







"They could have been," Sun Qiming replied. "But we have no evidence that they were."







Mingzhao looked at him.







"Do not write that they were."







"I will not."







The investigation continued.







The original difference remained forty-seven sacks.







Eighteen had been confirmed in southern storage.







Two were covered by the movement instruction but lacked confirmed receipt.







Twenty-seven remained unexplained.







The contractor's notebook had added information.







It had not produced a miracle.



That afternoon, Mingyuan arrived with another set of documents.







He had been examining the old household accounts with Steward Zhou.







He placed the records before Sun Qiming.







"I found the original authorization for the study repairs."







Sun Qiming examined it.







The document bore the steward's mark and the approval of Shen Guowei.







The amount corresponded to the seventy-two-tael payment.







Sun Qiming compared it with the carpenter's receipt.







The figures matched.







He examined the description of the work.







The repairs were limited to roof beams, wall plaster, and damaged flooring.







There was no mention of decorative additions.







He looked up.







"This supports the classification as ordinary estate maintenance."







Mingyuan seemed relieved.







"Then the seventy-two taels can be removed from the personal-expense inquiry?"







"Yes. It can be recorded as a verified estate expense."







Mingzhao nodded.







"Update the schedule."







Sun Qiming made a note.







Mingyuan looked at his sister.







"Then the amount under examination is now two hundred and twenty-eight taels."







"Correct."







He hesitated.







"I still cannot remember what all of it was used for."







"Then continue examining the records."







"What if some of it was spent on me?"







"Then the evidence will show that."







Mingyuan lowered his eyes.







"I know."







Mingzhao studied him for a moment.







He had begun to take the matter more seriously.







That did not erase the years in which he had avoided responsibility.







It did, however, make the current work easier.







She returned to the accounts.







"Ask the household clerk to provide the original vouchers for the remaining entries."







Mingyuan nodded.







"I will."







He took the instruction and left.



In the afternoon, Shen Mingxiu came to the study with a question about the secondary residences.







"Second Sister, I have completed the monthly summary."







Mingzhao accepted the document.







The report contained the income and expenditure of the secondary residences.







It also separated ordinary repairs, agricultural expenses, staff wages, and household supplies.







Mingzhao examined the figures.







"Why is the eastern residence's repair expenditure higher this month?"







Mingxiu pointed to the relevant entry.







"The drainage channel collapsed after the rain. The steward ordered repairs."







"Was the work completed?"







"Yes."







"Is there a receipt?"







"Yes."







"Was the expense within the approved allowance?"







Mingxiu checked her notes.







"It exceeded the ordinary repair allowance."







"Then who authorized the additional amount?"







"Father."







Mingzhao nodded.







"Good. The report should include his authorization."







Mingxiu looked relieved.







"Is the summary correct?"







"Mostly."







Mingxiu's expression fell slightly.







"What is wrong?"







"You have included the grain used for household consumption under agricultural sales."







Mingxiu looked at the entry.







"I thought it was part of the harvest."







"It is part of the harvest. It is not a sale."







Mingxiu checked the figures.







She had included the same quantity in two places.







"I see."







"Correct it."







"Yes, Second Sister."







Mingxiu took the report back.







Then she looked at Mingzhao.







"Second Sister, when you leave, who will check these summaries?"







"Your brother and the estate accountant."







"Will they notice mistakes?"







"They should."







"What if they do not?"







"Then the mistakes will remain until someone notices them."







Mingxiu frowned.







"That is not reassuring."







"It is not supposed to be."







Mingzhao returned to her own work.







Mingxiu stood there for a moment.







Then she took the report and left.







She was beginning to understand that taking responsibility did not mean receiving constant reassurance.







It meant learning to check one's own work.



At Chengming Palace, the Empress was preparing for her final official audience with the senior palace administrators.







The meeting would review the responsibilities that remained under her direct authority until the ceremony.







Madam Zhao presented the list.







There were twelve responsibilities still recorded under the Empress's direct authority.







Five had already been examined.







Seven remained pending.







Yuru read the list.







"Why is the annual review of ceremonial gifts still here?"







Madam Zhao answered.







"The Department of Rites has accepted responsibility for the ceremonial register. The final review is pending because the current year's gifts have not yet been reconciled."







"Then mark it as transferred, with the current reconciliation pending."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







Yuru examined the next entry.







"Disputes concerning senior attendants?"







"Those remain under the Empress's authority according to the existing regulation."







"Are all disputes submitted to me?"







"No, Your Majesty. Routine matters are handled by the senior administrators."







"Then the register should identify which disputes require my decision and which do not."







Madam Zhao nodded.







Yuru continued.







She was not going to spend the final days approving every ordinary decision simply because the old paperwork still carried her name.







The meeting continued for nearly two hours.







At the end, the list had been reduced to matters that genuinely required her authority before the ceremony.







Madam Zhao gathered the papers.







"Your Majesty, the departments are making progress."







"They are."







"Some officials remain uncertain."







"They will become less uncertain when they begin making decisions."







Madam Zhao bowed.







Yuru looked at her.







"Do not let them believe that every mistake means the arrangement has failed."







"Yes, Your Majesty."







"Nor should they believe that every mistake can be blamed on the person who previously held the responsibility."







Madam Zhao nodded.







The Empress looked at the remaining list.







It was shorter.







Not perfect.







But shorter.



The Emperor visited Chengming Palace that evening.







Yuru was reading a book.







He looked at the table.







There were no administrative reports.







"Have you finished your work?"







"For today."







"That is unusual."







"I have been practicing."







He smiled.







"Practicing what?"







"Leaving."







The Emperor sat opposite her.







He looked at the book.







"What are you reading?"







"A record of agricultural taxation."







He stared at her.







"That is your idea of rest?"







"It is a book."







"It is a taxation record."







"It is still a book."







The Emperor laughed.







Yuru closed it.







He looked at her.







"The ceremony arrangements are nearly complete."







"I know."







"The Imperial Archives will receive the seals."







"Yes."







"The Ministry of Rites has prepared the necessary announcements."







"Yes."







He paused.







"Is there anything else you require?"







Yuru considered the question.







"An uninterrupted morning."







He looked at her.







"That can be arranged."







"Without reports."







"Yes."







"Without officials."







"Yes."







"Without someone asking whether the palace should create a special office to supervise the absence of the Empress."







The Emperor laughed again.







"I will make certain of it."







Yuru looked satisfied.







For once, the palace might allow her to have a morning without administrative responsibility.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao received the updated settlement schedule.







The personal-property inventory was nearly complete.







The independent commercial assets were being examined.







The family estate valuation was in progress.







The confirmed advances had been separated from disputed claims.







The liabilities schedule remained incomplete.







The warehouse discrepancy remained unresolved.







The property division could proceed in stages, but the final settlement could not yet be calculated.







Mingzhao read the document.







Then she turned to the departure schedule.







The transition of ordinary household responsibilities had largely been completed.







Steward Zhou was managing daily operations.







Mingyuan was reviewing principal accounts.







Mingxiu was preparing the secondary residence and agricultural summaries.







The estate clerk was producing monthly expenditure reports.







The remaining work involved unresolved records and the transfer of authority over certain commercial and property matters.







Mingzhao looked at the proposed date.







The family had suggested the twentieth day of the fourth month.







She examined it.







Three days from now.







It was earlier than she had expected.







She read the conditions.







The transition of ordinary duties would be completed before that date.







Outstanding accounts would remain under examination.







The property division would continue separately.







She looked at Sun Qiming.







"Can the handover be completed by the twentieth?"







"Yes, if the remaining records are transferred according to the schedule."







"Will the unfinished accounts prevent the handover?"







"No. The accountant can continue examining them under the settlement process."







Mingzhao looked at the document.







"Then I accept the date."







Qiao Yue, who stood nearby, looked at her.







"Miss, that means you will leave in three days."







"Yes."







"Have you decided where you will live?"







"Not yet."







Qiao Yue stared at her.







"Miss."







"What?"







"You are leaving in three days."







"I am aware."







"And you have not purchased a residence."







"I am also aware."







"Where will we go?"







Mingzhao looked at her.







"An inn, if necessary. Or a temporary rented residence."







Qiao Yue looked horrified.







"The Marquis's eldest daughter staying at an inn?"







"The Marquis's eldest daughter will be staying somewhere she can afford."







Qiao Yue opened her mouth.







Then closed it.







Mingzhao returned to the schedule.







She had spent weeks searching for a suitable residence.







The northern house needed more repairs than expected.







The eastern property still had a boundary dispute.







She would not purchase either simply because the departure date had arrived.







A temporary residence was inconvenient.







It was also preferable to buying the wrong property.







She signed the acceptance.



That evening, Shen Guowei read the signed schedule.







He looked at the date.







"The twentieth?"







"Yes," Mingzhao replied.







"You have agreed?"







"Yes."







He looked at her for a long moment.







"Three days."







"Yes."







"Your property division is not complete."







"The schedule states that it will continue separately."







"The household accounts are still being examined."







"Sun Qiming will continue the examination."







"You have not found a residence."







"I will arrange temporary accommodation."







Shen Guowei frowned.







"That is not suitable."







"It is sufficient."







He looked at her.







"You have managed this household for six years. I did not expect you to leave without a proper residence."







Mingzhao was silent.







Then she replied.







"I did not expect to spend six years managing the household without a clear agreement about my own property."







Her father looked away.







The statement was not loud.







It did not need to be.







After a moment, he nodded.







"Very well."







Mingzhao stood.







"I will complete the handover on the twentieth."







She bowed and left.







Shen Guowei remained seated.







For the first time, the date was no longer a subject for negotiation.







His eldest daughter was leaving.







The property division would continue.







The household would remain.







And the family would have to discover whether it could manage its affairs without her.



That night, Yuru reviewed the final ceremonial schedule.







Seven days remained.







Mingzhao reviewed the signed departure schedule.







Three days remained.







One woman was leaving a palace after years of responsibility.







The other was leaving a family estate after years of management.







Neither had completed every matter.







Neither intended to wait until every other person was comfortable.







The Emperor had decided not to appoint a new Empress immediately.







The Marquis had agreed that his daughter could depart before the property settlement was complete.







For the first time, both departures had become unavoidable facts rather than proposals.







Unfortunately, the palace still had one question.







The Imperial Archives had asked whether the seals should be counted before being placed in their ceremonial cases.







And at the Marquisate, Steward Zhou had asked whether Mingzhao's departure inventory should include the additional table.







Mingzhao had answered immediately.







"The table belongs to the Marquisate."







Steward Zhou had nodded.







Then he had asked whether the documents on it belonged to her.







Mingzhao had stared at him.







For several seconds, she had not answered.







Finally, she said:







"The documents belong to whoever is responsible for the matters recorded in them."







Steward Zhou considered this.







Then he asked whether the table could remain where it was.







Mingzhao closed her eyes.







"Yes."







The table would stay.







The documents would remain.







And Shen Mingzhao would leave without taking the furniture required to store the problems she had spent six years managing.
