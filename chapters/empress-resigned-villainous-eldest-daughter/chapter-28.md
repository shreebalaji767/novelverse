# **Chapter 28: The Empress Has Four Days Left. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Finally Left. The Marquisate Has Discovered That a Departure Requires More Signatures Than an Arrival.**

**20th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the departure began before sunrise.



This was partly because Shen Mingzhao preferred to finish important work early.



It was also because the cart contractor had informed Steward Zhou that he had another delivery scheduled after midday.



Mingzhao had no intention of allowing her departure to be delayed because someone else had arranged two journeys for the same vehicle.



The four carts stood in the outer courtyard.



Two were for personal trunks and furniture. One was for books, correspondence, and household goods. The fourth carried provisions and the servants' belongings.



The drivers had received written instructions.



The servants had received their new duty arrangements.



The personal inventory had been copied and signed.



The family property inventory had been separated from it.



The departure was, by every reasonable measure, prepared.



And yet, before the first cart could leave, a clerk arrived with another document.



"Miss Shen, the household administration requests your confirmation of the number of trunks."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"The number is written in the inventory."



"The office requires a separate confirmation."



"Why?"



The clerk hesitated.



"Because the departure record and the household inventory are maintained separately."



Mingzhao looked toward Steward Zhou.



Steward Zhou looked at the clerk.



The clerk lowered his head.



Mingzhao took the document.



There were eleven trunks, three wooden boxes, two book chests, one writing desk, and several smaller packages.



She checked the list.



"Eleven trunks. Three boxes. Two book chests. One writing desk."



The clerk wrote down the confirmation.



"Thank you, Miss."



"Does the household administration also require a separate confirmation that the carts have wheels?"



The clerk looked startled.



Steward Zhou coughed.



The clerk bowed and withdrew.



Qiao Yue, who had been standing beside the luggage, waited until he was gone.



"That was unnecessary."



"It was."



"Would you like me to tell him?"



"No. He did not invent the procedure."



Qiao Yue looked at the departing clerk.



"Someone did."



Mingzhao picked up her final personal inventory.



"Someone should also improve it."

The Old Madam Shen received Mingzhao in her private courtyard.



She was already dressed, seated beside a low table with a cup of warm tea.



At seventy-three, she had little patience for people who made a performance of sadness.



She had even less patience for people who pretended that a difficult decision had no consequences.



Mingzhao entered and bowed.



"Grandmother."



"Come here."



Mingzhao sat opposite her.



The Old Madam looked at her granddaughter's hands.



"You are leaving today."



"Yes."



"Have you arranged your lodging?"



"Yes. A three-month rental near the eastern market."



"Only three months?"



"It is temporary."



"Have you found a permanent residence?"



"Not yet."



The Old Madam frowned.



"Why not?"



"The northern canal property requires repairs. The eastern property has an unresolved title and boundary issue. I will not purchase either until the records are clear."



"Your father could have arranged another residence."



"He could have."



"And he did not."



"He did not."



The Old Madam studied her.



"You have become very careful about what you expect from this family."



Mingzhao did not answer immediately.



"I have learned that an expectation is not the same as an agreement."



The Old Madam gave a short laugh.



"That sounds like something an accountant would say."



"It is something an accountant would write."



The older woman shook her head.



Then she reached for a small wooden box.



"I intended to give this to you when you married."



Mingzhao looked at it.



Inside lay a pair of gold hair ornaments, carefully wrapped in silk.



They were not extravagant, but they were finely made.



"I do not know when you will marry," the Old Madam said. "Perhaps you will. Perhaps you will not. I have decided that waiting for an occasion is unnecessary."



Mingzhao looked at the ornaments.



"Grandmother, I cannot accept another gift without a record."



The Old Madam stared at her.



Then she laughed.



"Even now?"



"It should be recorded as a personal gift."



"Then record it."



Mingzhao took out a small sheet of paper.



The Old Madam watched her write.



"Gift from Old Madam Shen to Shen Mingzhao. Two gold hair ornaments. Presented on the twentieth day of the fourth month, eighteenth year of Yonghe."



Mingzhao signed it.



The Old Madam took the paper, examined it, and handed it back.



"Keep it."



Mingzhao placed it inside the personal inventory.



The Old Madam looked at her for a long moment.



"You have always been proud."



"Yes."



"Too proud, sometimes."



"Yes."



"You have also been useful."



Mingzhao's expression remained composed.



The Old Madam continued.



"That is not the same as being loved."



The room became quiet.



Mingzhao looked down at the box.



"I know."



The Old Madam sighed.



"I do not approve of everything you have done. You have spoken harshly to people who did not deserve it. You have sometimes treated the household as though it were an account book and everyone in it an entry to be corrected."



Mingzhao listened.



"But the family also became accustomed to taking your work for granted. Your mother allowed it. Your father allowed it. Your brother benefited from it. I allowed it."



The Old Madam looked at the courtyard.



"I should have said something earlier."



Mingzhao did not know what to answer.



She had imagined many conversations with her grandmother.



Most involved disagreement.



She had not expected this one.



The Old Madam reached for her tea.



"Do not make your new household another place where everyone must obey you because you know how to count."



Mingzhao looked up.



"I will try."



"Do more than try."



The Old Madam's expression softened.



"Go, then."



Mingzhao rose and bowed.



At the door, the Old Madam called her back.



"Visit me."



"I will."



"And do not wait until every account is settled."



Mingzhao turned.



For a moment, she looked as though she might argue.



Then she bowed again.



"Yes, Grandmother."

In the main courtyard, Shen Guowei was waiting beside the carts.



Madam Liang stood nearby.



Shen Mingyuan had arrived with the final transfer records.



Shen Mingxiu was holding a small parcel.



Mingzhao approached them.



"Father."



Shen Guowei nodded.



"Everything is prepared?"



"Yes."



"Have the servants received their arrangements?"



"Yes."



"Have the original family records been returned?"



"The originals belonging to the Marquisate have been returned to Steward Zhou. I have retained only copies and documents concerning my own claims and the partnership."



Steward Zhou confirmed the transfer.



"All original estate records under Miss Shen's custody have been returned or assigned to the responsible office. The receiving signatures are complete."



Mingzhao handed the final document to her father.



Shen Guowei read it.



It was a receipt acknowledging the return of household keys, documents, and administrative records.



He signed.



Then Mingyuan signed as the receiving family representative.



Mingzhao took the completed copy.



"Thank you."



Shen Guowei looked at the carts.



"You are leaving with less than I expected."



"I am taking what belongs to me."



"And the furniture?"



"Family property remains here."



"The books?"



"Two volumes from Grandmother's collection, which she gave to me. The rest remain."



Mingzhao's father nodded.



He had expected a dispute.



Instead, she had made a list.



It was difficult to argue with a list that had already been checked by three people.



Madam Liang stepped forward.



"Your servants have packed everything?"



"Yes."



"Do you have sufficient provisions?"



"For the first several days."



"You should take more."



"I have purchased what is necessary."



Madam Liang looked dissatisfied.



She had intended to make a practical suggestion.



Mingzhao had answered as though it were an account query.



Shen Mingxiu approached with her parcel.



"Elder Sister."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"What is it?"



"A small gift."



Mingxiu held out the parcel.



Inside was a set of plain embroidered handkerchiefs.



They were neatly made, though not particularly elaborate.



"I made them," Mingxiu said.



Mingzhao examined the embroidery.



The stitching was uneven in two corners.



"They are good."



Mingxiu looked relieved.



"You do not have to use them."



"I know."



Mingzhao folded them carefully.



"Thank you."



Mingxiu hesitated.



"Will you be busy after moving?"



"Yes."



"With the accounts?"



"Among other things."



"Then perhaps I can visit."



Mingzhao looked at her younger sister.



"You may."



Mingxiu nodded.



There was no promise of reconciliation.



There was no declaration that everything would be different.



But there was an invitation.



For the moment, it was enough.

The carts departed shortly after the morning meal.



Shen Mingzhao did not look back immediately.



She had spent too many years in the Marquisate to believe that a gate could be made unfamiliar by walking through it.



The household servants stood in the courtyard.



Some bowed.



Some looked relieved.



Some looked uncomfortable.



A few had worked under Mingzhao for years and did not know how to greet her now that she was no longer their immediate superior.



She returned their bows.



Then she entered the carriage.



Qiao Yue sat opposite her.



The wheels began to turn.



For several moments, neither spoke.



Then Qiao Yue said, "We have left."



"Yes."



"That is all?"



"What else should there be?"



"I expected something more dramatic."



Mingzhao looked out through the carriage window.



"Would you prefer that I throw a ledger through the gate?"



Qiao Yue considered it.



"Only if it is an old one."



Mingzhao almost smiled.



The carriage passed through the eastern gate and entered the busy road.



The Marquisate remained behind them.



Its accounts were still unfinished.



Its grain discrepancy was still unresolved.



Its property division was still incomplete.



Its household had not suddenly become harmonious.



But the responsibility for managing its ordinary affairs no longer rested on Shen Mingzhao.



For the first time in six years, she was not expected to return that evening to check the kitchen accounts.



She was not expected to settle a servant's complaint, authorize a repair, or explain why a storehouse receipt had not been signed.



She had other work.



She would still examine the family records.



She would still attend the settlement proceedings.



She would still have to answer questions about the claims she had made.



But she would do so from a different address.



The distinction mattered.

At the temporary lodging house, Madam Wu was waiting at the entrance.



She had arranged for the carpenter to finish the window latch before the carts arrived.



The kitchen shelves had been repaired, though one board was slightly crooked.



Qiao Yue noticed it immediately.



Mingzhao noticed it a moment later.



Madam Wu saw their expressions.



"The carpenter said it was secure."



"It may be secure," Mingzhao replied. "It is not level."



Madam Wu sighed.



"Would you like me to call him back?"



"Yes."



The owner stared at her.



Mingzhao looked at the shelf.



"Before we place anything heavy on it."



The carpenter returned.



He examined the shelf, adjusted the support, and corrected the angle.



This time, it was level.



Mingzhao inspected it.



Then she signed the condition record.



The trunks were carried inside.



The servants began arranging the rooms.



The writing desk was placed near the window. The book chests went into the smaller room. Personal correspondence was stored separately from the partnership records.



The two gold ornaments from the Old Madam were placed in the locked personal box.



The handkerchiefs from Mingxiu were folded into a drawer.



Qiao Yue inspected the kitchen.



The senior maid checked the water jars.



The correspondence worker arranged the writing materials.



The second attendant began unpacking the bedding.



The new household was small.



It was also disorganized.



One trunk had been placed in the wrong room.



A box of kitchen utensils had been carried upstairs.



The carpenter had left a small pile of wood shavings near the courtyard wall.



The rear room smelled faintly of damp plaster.



Mingzhao looked around.



"Who arranged the trunks?"



Qiao Yue raised her hand.



"Me."



"Why is the book chest in the receiving room?"



"Because the book room was not ready."



"Then why was it not placed in the upper room temporarily?"



"Because the upper room had the writing desk."



"Move the chest."



Qiao Yue stared at her.



"We have been here for less than an hour."



"And the chest is still in the wrong room."



The servants exchanged glances.



Mingzhao looked at them.



Then she stopped.



She was about to begin assigning every movement.



She could see the entire process in her mind: the trunks, the rooms, the shelves, the kitchen, the storage arrangements.



She could make it efficient.



She could also spend the entire afternoon managing a household that had only just begun to exist.



She took a breath.



"Qiao Yue, arrange the rooms as you think best. Tell me when the important documents are secured."



Qiao Yue blinked.



"Are you certain?"



"Yes."



"And the book chest?"



"Put it wherever you think it belongs."



Qiao Yue looked almost suspicious.



Then she turned to the servants.



"You heard Miss Shen. Move the chest."



Mingzhao went to her room.



It was smaller than the one she had occupied at the Marquisate.



The furniture was less impressive.



The window overlooked the narrow street.



She placed her writing materials on the desk.



For a moment, she stood still.



There was no household office waiting for her.



No steward waiting for an instruction.



No one had brought her a report.



The silence was unfamiliar.



Then a cart driver knocked.



"Miss Shen, where should we place the remaining provisions?"



Mingzhao closed her eyes briefly.



Independence, she was discovering, did not mean that people stopped asking questions.



It meant that she had to decide which questions were worth answering.

At the Marquisate, the first morning without Mingzhao produced a minor crisis.



The kitchen reported that the household's regular supply of cooking oil was insufficient for the coming week.



The steward's clerk had brought the issue to Steward Zhou.



Steward Zhou examined the stock record.



The current quantity was correct.



The expected delivery date was not.



The supplier had changed the delivery schedule, but the notice had been placed in the commercial correspondence file rather than the kitchen supply file.



Steward Zhou sent for the notice.



It was found.



The delivery would arrive the following morning.



The kitchen could use the reserve stock until then.



The problem was solved in less than half an hour.



The clerk looked relieved.



Steward Zhou looked at him.



"Why was the notice not entered in the kitchen supply register?"



"The correspondence clerk filed it under the supplier's account."



"Then the supplier's account should have been copied to the supply register."



"Yes, Steward."



Steward Zhou returned to his desk.



He had not needed to send a message to Mingzhao.



This was good.



He had also discovered that the household had been relying on one person to notice when two separate registers needed to be compared.



This was less good.



He made a note to revise the filing procedure.



Then he looked at the stack of documents waiting on his desk.



He understood why Mingzhao had often complained about the number of things that required attention.



He also understood that complaining about them did not make them disappear.

In the imperial palace, Xu Yuru was preparing for the final rehearsal.



Four days remained before the formal ceremony.



She had chosen a restrained set of ceremonial garments, and the Department of Rites had already confirmed the order of proceedings.



The Empress Dowager had requested that the family elders of the Xu clan be given a place among the invited relatives.



Yuru had agreed.



The emperor would attend.



The Crown Prince would attend.



The Second Prince and Third Princess would also attend.



The senior palace officials responsible for the transfer would be present.



The ceremony would not be a celebration.



It would not be a public humiliation.



It would formally record the end of Yuru's tenure as Empress and the completion of the transfer arrangements.



Fang Ru entered with the final schedule.



"Your Majesty, the Department of Rites has confirmed the rehearsal."



Yuru examined it.



"Who will direct the proceedings?"



"The responsible ceremonial official."



"Who will manage the palace attendants?"



"The Imperial Household Administration."



"Who will confirm the final personnel list?"



"The Department of Personnel."



Yuru nodded.



"Good."



Fang Ru remained standing.



"Your Majesty, the officials have asked whether you will give an instruction before the ceremony."



"What kind of instruction?"



"They wish to know whether you have any final guidance concerning the inner palace."



Yuru considered this.



"They have already received the transfer records."



"They are asking whether there is anything else."



"Then tell them that the existing regulations remain in force."



Fang Ru waited.



"Anything more?"



"Yes."



Yuru looked at her.



"Tell them not to create a new rule simply because I am leaving."



Fang Ru bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Yuru returned to her schedule.



She had spent many years correcting the palace's habits.



She could not correct every one of them before leaving.



That was not a reason to remain indefinitely.

The emperor received the report of Mingzhao's departure through an ordinary family notice.



It was not a matter of state.



The Marquis of Qingyuan had informed the relevant officials that his eldest daughter had moved to temporary accommodation in the eastern market district while the property settlement remained under examination.



Zhao Jingheng read the notice because it had been included among the day's household correspondence.



He raised an eyebrow.



"She has left?"



Gao Shun answered, "Yes, Your Majesty."



"Was the property division completed?"



"No. The family has agreed that the departure date is separate from the settlement."



The emperor considered this.



"That is sensible."



Gao Shun did not comment.



Zhao Jingheng looked at the notice again.



"How old is she?"



"Twenty-six, Your Majesty."



"She managed the Marquisate's household for six years?"



"That is what the report states."



The emperor set the paper down.



"Then the Marquisate has lost an experienced administrator."



Gao Shun bowed.



"Perhaps the family will learn to manage its own affairs."



The emperor looked at him.



"Perhaps."



He turned to the next report.



It concerned the ceremonial rehearsal.



The emperor read it.



Then he asked, "Has the Empress seen this?"



Gao Shun paused.



"The Department of Rites sent the final schedule directly to Chengming Palace, Your Majesty."



The emperor looked at him.



"Why?"



"The Department of Rites believed Her Majesty should be informed."



"She has already approved the transfer of responsibility."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then why are they sending her the schedule?"



Gao Shun lowered his head.



The emperor picked up his brush.



"Return it to the Department of Rites. They may keep the final schedule in their own records."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Zhao Jingheng looked toward the window.



Four days remained.



He wondered whether the palace would finally stop sending his wife documents after the ceremony.



Then he remembered that the ceremony itself would generate documents.



He put down his brush.



Perhaps he should not expect too much.

That evening, Mingzhao sat at her new writing desk.



The household had been arranged sufficiently for the first night.



The kitchen was usable.



The water jars were filled.



The servants had settled into their rooms.



The important papers were locked away.



The rental agreement was stored with the personal expense records.



Mingzhao opened a fresh ledger.



At the top, she wrote:



**Independent Household — Initial Accounts**



She listed the deposit, rent, transport expenses, carpenter's work, provisions, servant wages, and other immediate costs.



Then she added a separate section for partnership funds.



The silk partnership's capital and proceeds would not be mixed with her household expenses.



She added another section for claims against the Marquisate.



The nine confirmed advances remained confirmed.



The four partially confirmed entries remained partially confirmed.



The ten uncertain entries remained uncertain.



The family estate valuation remained separate.



She paused.



Then she wrote one more heading.



**Personal Household Decisions.**



Below it, she wrote:

- Room arrangements.
- Servant duties.
- Kitchen provisions.
- Household repairs.
- Correspondence schedule.

She looked at the list.



It was short.



She had expected it to be longer.



Qiao Yue entered carrying tea.



"Have you finished?"



"For today."



"Will you continue tomorrow?"



"Yes."



Qiao Yue put down the tea.



"Then you have not escaped the accounts."



Mingzhao looked at the ledger.



"No."



"Or the household."



"No."



"Or the family."



"Unfortunately, no."



Qiao Yue smiled.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



"But I have changed the address."



Qiao Yue laughed.



It was a small joke.



It was also the most accurate description of the day.

At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru stood by the window after the evening meal.



The courtyard was quiet.



Fang Ru entered with a final message from the Department of Rites.



"Your Majesty, the rehearsal is scheduled for tomorrow morning."



Yuru nodded.



"Thank you."



"The officials ask whether you will attend."



"I will attend as the person whose departure is being formally recorded. I will not direct the rehearsal."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"Your Majesty, the attendants have asked whether they should prepare your usual administrative desk for the rehearsal."



Yuru looked at her.



"No."



"Then what should they prepare?"



"Nothing beyond the ceremonial arrangements."



Fang Ru bowed.



Yuru turned back toward the courtyard.



Four days remained.



Across the capital, Shen Mingzhao had finally moved out of the Marquisate.



The family had not settled its accounts.



The palace had not completed its ceremony.



Neither woman had finished the work that had brought her to this point.



But one had left the house she had managed for six years.



The other was preparing to leave the office she had occupied for many years.



And both households were beginning to discover the same inconvenient truth:



A person could leave without taking every problem with her.



Unfortunately, the problems could not leave without someone else taking responsibility for them.
