# **Chapter 82: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Borrow a Book and Has Not Yet Agreed to Attend a Lecture. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Completed Her First Large Order and Has Discovered That a Finished Contract Can Still Occupy an Accountant for Several Days. The Marquisate Has Introduced a New Procedure and Is Waiting to See Whether Its Clerks Can Read It.**

**16th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress received another message from the Xu residence.



This time, it was not a letter from her mother.



It was a small note from her father, written in a hand that was considerably less formal than the letters he sent to officials.



He had selected the second book and placed it aside for her. He also suggested that she might enjoy discussing one particular chapter concerning household stewards and the limits of their authority.



Yuru read the note.



Then she looked at Fang Ru.



“My father has selected a chapter for discussion.”



“Does he expect you to read it before visiting?”



“I suspect he expects me to read it whether I visit or not.”



Fang Ru smiled.



“Will you go?”



“Not today.”



“Would you like me to send a reply?”



“Yes. Tell him I will come when convenient and that I look forward to borrowing the book.”



Yuru paused.



“Also tell him that I am not promising to agree with his interpretation of the chapter.”



Fang Ru looked amused.



“Should I write that exactly?”



“Yes.”



“Your father may prepare an argument.”



“He probably already has.”



Fang Ru took the note away.



Yuru returned to her reading.



She had begun to appreciate the difference between an invitation and an obligation. Her father could invite her to discuss a chapter. She could accept the invitation without agreeing to spend the entire afternoon defending her opinions.



It was a small freedom.



She intended to keep it.

The workshop accounts occupied Mingzhao's morning.



The two-hundred-bolt order had passed inspection, and the goods were being prepared for dispatch.



The buyer's representative had confirmed that the first consignment could leave the following day, with the second to follow after the carrier completed the necessary arrangements.



The final payment would be released according to the contract after the delivery records were confirmed.



Mingzhao had received the preliminary cost summary from the workshop clerk.



She did not like it.



Not because the total was necessarily wrong, but because several categories had been combined.



Training wages appeared under ordinary labour.



Additional supervision had been grouped with general workshop expenses.



The correction costs were listed as one total without identifying which batch had required adjustment.



Mingzhao placed the summary on the table.



“Who prepared this?”



“The workshop clerk,” Qiao Yue replied.



“Did he use the original wage sheets?”



“Yes.”



“Then why are the training wages not separate?”



“He said it would be simpler to combine them.”



Mingzhao looked at the document.



“Simple for whom?”



Qiao Yue did not answer.



Mingzhao continued.



“Have him prepare the summary again. Training time and ordinary production time must be distinguished. Supervision should be listed separately. Corrections must be linked to their batch records.”



“Will that delay the final calculation?”



“It will delay an inaccurate calculation.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



She took the document away.



Mingzhao opened the original wage ledger.



The additional workers had been hired for the new order. Two were experienced, while the other two had required training. Their work had improved steadily, but their early production had taken longer.



The costs were not excessive merely because they were inconvenient.



They were part of the cost of producing the goods.



If Mingzhao wished to judge whether the order had been profitable, she needed to know what had actually been spent.



She turned another page.



There was also a small discrepancy between the amount paid for packing materials and the amount recorded as used.



She marked it for confirmation.



The order was finished.



The accounting was not.

At the Marquisate, the new purchasing procedure was introduced to the relevant staff.



Mingyuan attended the meeting with Steward Zhou and Liang Wen.



The purchasing clerks sat along one side of the room. The receiving stewards sat opposite them. The accounts office had sent two clerks, and the register keeper had brought the consolidated purchasing book.



Mingyuan stood beside the table.



“The purpose of this procedure is not to create additional work,” he began. “It is to ensure that each stage has a responsible person and that the records can be examined afterward.”



One of the purchasing clerks raised a hand.



“Young Master, if the authorizing official has approved the purchase, does the purchasing clerk still need to keep the original order?”



“Yes.”



“Even if the accounts office already has a copy?”



“Yes.”



The clerk looked slightly disappointed.



Mingyuan continued.



“The authorization proves that the expenditure was approved. The order proves what was requested. The receiving record proves what arrived. The payment voucher proves what was paid. These are not interchangeable.”



Steward Zhou watched the clerks.



Several were listening carefully.



One appeared to be calculating how much additional paperwork the procedure would create.



Mingyuan understood the concern.



He had examined enough records to know that a procedure could become burdensome if every clerk created unnecessary copies.



But the answer was not to remove essential evidence.



“The records should be clear and proportionate,” he said. “We do not need five copies of every page. We do need the original authorization, the order, the receiving record, and the payment evidence to be traceable.”



Liang Wen distributed the written procedure.



The meeting continued through partial deliveries, rejected goods, urgent purchases, and corrections.



When they reached the section on missing vouchers, one of the accounts clerks asked a question.



“If the original voucher cannot be found, may we prepare a replacement?”



“No,” Mingyuan replied.



“Then how can the payment be accounted for?”



“A separate explanatory record may be prepared. It must identify the missing document, the reason it is unavailable, the supporting evidence, and the official approving the exception.”



The clerk nodded.



“Would the replacement be marked as an original?”



“No. It is an explanatory record.”



The clerk wrote that down.



Mingyuan looked around the room.



He had once thought that a new procedure would be difficult because the staff might resist it.



Now he was discovering that the more difficult part was explaining why a document could not simply be treated as something it was not.

The meeting ended after midday.



The clerks returned to their departments with copies of the procedure.



Steward Zhou remained with Mingyuan.



“How do you think they will manage?”



“They will make mistakes.”



“That is likely.”



“We will need to examine the first few weeks of records.”



Steward Zhou looked at him.



“Will you personally examine every purchase?”



“No. The supervising steward and accounts office will conduct the regular checks. I will review the reports and examine exceptions.”



“That is a better arrangement.”



Mingyuan glanced at him.



“Do you think I am learning?”



“I think you are learning not to become the person who does every job.”



Mingyuan gave a tired smile.



“I have had an excellent teacher in my sister's absence.”



Steward Zhou did not reply immediately.



Then he said, “Sometimes people understand the value of work only when they must perform it themselves.”



Mingyuan looked toward the door.



He did not know whether his sister would ever forgive the family for how it had treated her.



He no longer assumed that she should.

At the eastern market residence, Mingzhao received the corrected workshop summary.



The clerk had separated the training wages, ordinary wages, and supervision costs.



The correction entries were now linked to their respective batch records.



The packing-material discrepancy had also been explained.



A portion of the materials had been purchased for the first consignment and had not yet been used because the second consignment required a different packing arrangement.



The remaining materials were still in the workshop store.



Mingzhao examined the explanation.



“Has the unused material been counted?”



“Yes,” Qiao Yue replied. “The storekeeper has recorded the remaining quantity.”



“Then it should not be entered as consumed expenditure.”



Qiao Yue made a note.



Mingzhao continued reading.



The summary was considerably clearer.



However, the final transport charge was not yet available, and the buyer's remaining payment had not been received.



Sun Qiming would review the figures once the dispatch records were complete.



Mingzhao placed the summary aside.



“Send the clerk a note confirming that the revised categories are correct. Tell him to retain the original wage sheets with the summary.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



“Will you ask Sun Qiming to come tomorrow?”



“After the first delivery record arrives.”



“Then we wait.”



“Yes.”



Qiao Yue looked at the papers.



“You seem to spend a great deal of time waiting for documents.”



“I spend a great deal of time ensuring that the documents describe something real.”



“That sounds less pleasant.”



“It is more useful.”

The first consignment was packed in the afternoon.



The workshop workers checked the bolts against the dispatch list. The packing clerk recorded the quantity, the condition of the bundles, and the number of containers.



The carrier's representative examined the seals.



Mingzhao was present when the dispatch record was signed.



The first consignment contained twenty-eight bolts.



The remaining one hundred and seventy-two were scheduled for the next dispatch.



The buyer had accepted all two hundred bolts, but only the quantity physically dispatched could be recorded as delivered.



Mingzhao checked the record.



“Does the carrier have a copy?”



“Yes.”



“Does the buyer's office have the dispatch notice?”



“Yes.”



“Then send the notice and retain the signed carrier record.”



The clerk nodded.



The goods were loaded.



The carts departed.



Qiao Yue watched them leave.



“One hundred and seventy-two bolts remain.”



“Prepared for the second consignment,” Mingzhao corrected.



Qiao Yue looked at her.



“You really cannot help yourself.”



“I can. I simply choose not to.”

The former Empress spent the afternoon in the western courtyard.



Fang Ru had received a reply from Madam Xu confirming that Yuru could visit whenever convenient. Her father had added a separate note saying that he would keep the selected book available.



Yuru read both messages.



She did not arrange a date.



Instead, she asked Fang Ru to place the first book in its wooden case so that it would be ready for the next visit.



The afternoon was warm, and the courtyard was quiet.



Yuru sat near the window with her book.



She read a few pages, then set it aside.



For several years, she had been accustomed to measuring a day's success by the number of matters settled.



Today she had read, written two letters, and made no decision about her next visit.



It was a pleasant day.



She had not become less capable.



She had simply stopped treating every capability as a duty.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received a report from the accounts office before evening.



The clerks had begun using the new purchasing procedure.



One purchase had already been recorded under the new format.



The authorization, order, receipt, and payment records were correctly linked.



There was, however, a small problem.



The receiving steward had recorded the quantity in bundles, while the purchasing order had specified individual pieces.



Mingyuan examined the documents.



The actual quantity could be calculated, but the unit difference had not been explained.



He sent the record back for clarification.



Steward Zhou watched him.



“The procedure has been in use for less than a day.”



“And already we have a discrepancy.”



“Would you prefer to wait until there are twenty?”



Mingyuan looked at him.



“No.”



He marked the entry for correction.



The receiving steward would have to identify the number of pieces in each bundle and sign the revised record. The original description would remain visible.



It was not a serious problem.



But it demonstrated why the procedure existed.



The estate had spent years allowing different departments to use different descriptions without ensuring that their records could be compared.



Now the clerks were beginning to discover that a unit of measurement was not a decorative detail.

That evening, Mingzhao received the first dispatch confirmation.



The twenty-eight bolts had left the workshop and were in the carrier's custody.



The buyer's remaining payment would be settled after the agreed delivery conditions were met.



She placed the record with the order file.



The second consignment was still pending.



She then reviewed the workshop accounts one final time before sending them to Sun Qiming.



The figures were clearer, but the net result remained provisional.



She did not mind waiting another day.



She had already completed the difficult part of the production.



The remaining work was to establish the cost accurately.



Qiao Yue brought her tea.



“Will you take a rest now?”



“In a moment.”



“You have said that three times.”



“Then this is the fourth.”



Qiao Yue placed the tea down.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



She had no intention of spending the entire evening examining a document that was already ready for review.



She drank her tea.

The day had produced several small results.



The palace had remained quiet.



The former Empress had received permission to borrow another book and had chosen not to hurry.



The Marquisate had introduced its new purchasing procedure.



The clerks had already discovered that bundles and individual pieces were not the same unit.



The workshop had dispatched its first twenty-eight bolts.



The remaining one hundred and seventy-two were still awaiting dispatch.



And Mingzhao's accounts had become clearer, though the final profit remained unknown.



In the past, the Marquisate might have asked Mingzhao to correct the unit discrepancy herself.



Now the record had been returned to the responsible receiving steward.



The palace had once relied on Yuru to notice every problem.



Now its administrators were expected to notice their own.



Neither transition was effortless.



But the capital was beginning to discover that a capable woman did not need to remain responsible for everything simply because she had once been good at it.



Unfortunately, the clerks had also discovered that the new procedure required them to write down what they had actually received.



Several of them were already regretting the invention of writing.
