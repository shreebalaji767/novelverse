# **Chapter 103: The Former Empress Has Decided That a Book About Gardens May Be Safer Than a Book About Household Management, Although She Has Not Yet Considered the Possibility That Its Author Might Demand Personal Inspection of Every Leaf. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Workshop Report That Contains Fewer Opinions and More Measurements. The Marquis's Heir Has Discovered That Two Former Supervisors Remember the Same Notebook Differently, Which Has Made the Missing Notebook More Interesting and the Missing Grain No Less Missing.**

**7th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru's decision to read something other than household management lasted until breakfast.

After breakfast, she asked Fang Ru to find a book about gardens.

Fang Ru looked at the shelf.

"Would Madam prefer a book about flowers, or one about garden arrangements?"

"Flowers."

"Then I shall ask the residence library."

"Something practical, please."

Fang Ru paused.

"Practical?"

"How to care for plants. When to prune them. Which plants tolerate shade. How to keep a tree alive without appointing a committee to inspect its leaves."

Fang Ru smiled.

"I shall see what can be found."

Yuru returned to her seat.

The household manual remained closed.

She had no intention of reading another page until she had rested from its advice.

A little later, Fang Ru returned with two books.

One was a general guide to garden plants, compiled by a scholar who had apparently spent several years maintaining a family garden.

The other was a collection of seasonal planting notes.

Yuru examined their titles.

"These seem promising."

"The residence library had several books, but these were the only two that concerned ordinary gardens rather than the gardens of famous estates."

"Good. I have no intention of maintaining a famous estate."

Fang Ru placed the books on the table.

Yuru opened the first.

The opening chapter discussed soil, drainage, sunlight, and the importance of choosing plants suited to the local climate.

She approved.

The author recommended observing the garden before deciding what to plant.

Yuru turned a page.

There was no instruction to personally inspect every leaf.

She looked pleased.

"At last."

Fang Ru poured tea.

Yuru continued reading.

The author had written that a garden should be arranged according to its actual conditions, not according to the wishes of the person who owned it.

It was a sensible beginning.

Yuru settled into her chair.

For once, she was reading without considering who would have to carry out the instructions.

At the eastern-market workshop, Shen Mingzhao received the next production report.

The ten bolts that had completed weaving had passed finishing and internal inspection.

The remaining twenty were still in production.

The report included the measured width of the ten finished bolts, the recorded colour comparison against the approved sample, and the result of the edge examination.

All ten passed.

Mingzhao examined the measurements.

The width was consistent.

The colour remained within the approved range.

The finishing supervisor had recorded the two earlier edge corrections and confirmed that the material had not been damaged.

Mingzhao set the report down.

"How many have now passed internal inspection?"

"Sixty," Qiao Yue replied. "The first fifty, and these ten."

"And the remaining twenty?"

"Still being woven."

"Then the report should say sixty passed, twenty in production."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao looked at the cost sheet.

The additional inspection costs had increased slightly, but not beyond the allowance she had included in the revised estimate.

She did not want to reduce the inspection merely to protect the original cost estimate.

A cost estimate was useful because it helped a business plan.

It was not a promise that every unexpected expense could be eliminated.

She wrote a note for Sun Qiming, asking him to include the additional inspection expenditure in the final account rather than burying it in general workshop costs.

Qiao Yue read the instruction.

"Will the profit be lower?"

"It may be."

"Would that be a problem?"

"It would be a problem if the cost were unnecessary. If the inspection prevents a larger loss, then the expenditure has a purpose."

Mingzhao returned the paper.

She had not yet earned a profit on the order.

The deposit was not profit.

The goods had not been delivered.

The buyer had not inspected them.

The balance had not been paid.

She would wait.

At the Marquisate, the two former transport supervisors replied on the same day.

Mingyuan read the first letter.

The older supervisor, He Shun, remembered that a movement notebook had been kept in the northern warehouse office. He believed it had been used by the transport clerk and not carried personally by him.

He did not remember signing for it.

He also remembered that four carts had been prepared for dispatch during the relevant period, but could not recall their cargo.

Mingyuan read the second letter.

The later supervisor, Zhao Liang, remembered receiving several records from the previous officeholder when he took over the position. He believed that the movement notebook might have been among them.

However, he could not confirm this.

He had not found the notebook in the office when he left the post.

Neither letter identified the person who had received the notebook.

Neither letter established what the four carts had carried.

Mingyuan placed the two letters side by side.

Liang Wen examined them.

"They remember different arrangements."

"They remember different parts of the same office."

"Could the notebook have remained with the transport clerk?"

"It could have."

"Could it have been passed to the second supervisor?"

"It could have."

"Could it have been misplaced during the change of office?"

"Yes."

Liang Wen looked at the letters.

"Then we have three possibilities."

"At least three."

Mingyuan took a fresh sheet.

He wrote a summary distinguishing the statements from the evidence.

He Shun remembered the notebook being kept in the office and used by the transport clerk.

Zhao Liang remembered receiving records during the transfer of duties but could not identify the notebook.

The surviving issue register confirmed that a notebook had been issued to the northern warehouse transport supervisor's office, but its recipient column was damaged.

No surviving record established who physically held the notebook.

Mingyuan added that neither man's recollection identified the cargo or destination of the four-cart dispatch.

He placed the summary in the investigation file.

"Now we should ask whether the transport clerk's personnel records survive."

Liang Wen nodded.

"And the four carts?"

"That remains a separate question."

Mingyuan looked at the grain account.

Twenty-seven sacks remained unexplained.

The letters had added information about the notebook.

They had not changed the grain figures.

The Marquis entered the accounts office shortly before noon.

He had heard that both former supervisors had replied.

"What did they say?"

Mingyuan handed him the summary.

The Marquis read it.

"One says the clerk used the notebook. The other thinks it may have been transferred to him."

"Yes."

"Then the second supervisor may have had it."

"He may have."

"Why not ask him where he put it?"

"We did. He does not remember receiving it personally."

The Marquis frowned.

"Then ask the clerk."

"We are checking whether the personnel records identify the clerk who served during that period."

"Why does every answer require another inquiry?"

Mingyuan looked at the documents.

"Because the first record is incomplete."

The Marquis placed the summary on the table.

He was not satisfied.

But he could not point to a specific statement that was wrong.

"Do not allow this matter to continue indefinitely."

"I will continue the inquiry."

"And the grain?"

"The twenty-seven sacks remain unexplained."

The Marquis looked at him.

"Can you not determine whether they were sent with the carts?"

"Not from the records we have."

The Marquis sighed.

Mingyuan did not add anything.

He had learned that repeating an unsupported possibility more confidently did not turn it into evidence.

At Mingzhao's residence, the property schedule arrived with a new attachment.

The family council had found a second reference concerning one of the confirmed advances.

It was a ledger entry showing that money had been issued from the estate accounts to a household office.

However, the entry did not identify whether the money had been handed directly to Mingzhao, paid to a supplier at her instruction, or used for another estate expense.

The council had not reclassified the advance.

It had marked the entry for further verification.

Mingzhao examined it.

The amount matched one of the nine confirmed advances.

The date was plausible.

But the recipient was not named.

She wrote a question asking whether a corresponding payment voucher or receipt existed.

She did not reject the advance.

She did not accept the ledger entry as proof of repayment.

Qiao Yue looked at the document.

"The council has made another correction."

"Yes."

"Does that mean they are finally taking the schedule seriously?"

"They are taking this entry seriously."

Mingzhao put down the brush.

"That is enough for today."

She folded the attachment and placed it with the other records.

There were still four payment-confirmed entries with unclear repayment arrangements.

There were ten uncertain entries.

The seventy-two taels for study repairs remained a separate claim.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.

The northern canal property remained outside the division.

No final settlement had been reached.

The council had made progress, but progress did not mean that the questions had disappeared.

In the afternoon, Mingzhao visited the workshop.

She did not arrive to inspect every bolt.

She wanted to see whether the revised reporting arrangement was working.

The supervisor met her near the finishing tables.

"Madam."

"How many have passed?"

"Sixty. The remaining twenty are still in production."

"Have the finishing workers received the approved sample?"

"Yes. The original sample and the revised working instructions are both kept at the inspection table."

"Who checks the measurements?"

"The measuring clerk records them. The finishing supervisor checks the result. I review any discrepancy."

"Who records a correction?"

"The clerk who finds it records the issue, and the supervisor signs the correction."

Mingzhao looked toward the tables.

The workers were occupied with their tasks.

One of the trainees was comparing a finished edge with the sample.

The other was speaking with an experienced worker about the folding pressure.

Neither was waiting for Mingzhao to decide what to do.

She watched for a moment.

"Continue."

The supervisor bowed.

Mingzhao walked through the workshop and left.

Qiao Yue followed her.

"Madam did not inspect the goods."

"The report was sufficient for the questions I had."

"Would you have inspected them if something had been wrong?"

"If the report showed a problem that required my decision."

Qiao Yue considered that.

Mingzhao did not explain further.

She was still learning the difference between being informed and being involved in every action.

The workshop was still learning the same thing.

That evening, Xu Yuru read the seasonal planting notes.

The author recommended pruning certain trees after the flowering season and warned against cutting branches merely because they grew in an inconvenient direction.

Yuru found the advice unexpectedly interesting.

She read the passage twice.

A tree that grew unevenly was not necessarily a badly maintained tree.

Sometimes its shape reflected the direction of sunlight, the condition of the soil, or the space available to it.

The gardener's task was not to force every branch into the same shape.

Yuru turned the page.

The author recommended removing diseased branches, supporting weak growth, and allowing healthy branches room to develop.

She smiled.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"This book is better."

"Because it has not asked Madam to inspect every leaf?"

"Because it understands that different branches require different treatment."

Fang Ru glanced at the passage.

"That seems reasonable."

Yuru closed the book for the evening.

Outside, the courtyard had grown quiet.

She had spent years ensuring that every part of the inner palace received attention.

She had sometimes mistaken uniform attention for fairness, and constant intervention for care.

A household could not be managed by pretending every person, room, and problem was identical.

Nor could it be managed by expecting one person to hold every branch in place.

Yuru looked at the two books on the table.

Tomorrow she would continue the garden notes.

The household manual could wait.

It had already received more attention than it deserved.
