# **Chapter 35: The Former Empress Has Visited Her Natal Family. The Duke Has Prepared a Welcome Feast. The Household Has Accidentally Prepared Three Different Schedules for Her Arrival.**

**27th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Xu family residence had prepared for Yuru's arrival with considerable care.

The eastern courtyard had been cleaned.

The garden had been inspected.

The guest rooms had been aired.

The kitchen had prepared a selection of dishes.

The steward had checked the carriage entrance.

The Duke had personally inspected the sitting room.

Unfortunately, three different schedules had been prepared for the visit.

The first stated that Yuru would arrive before noon.

The second stated that she would arrive shortly after the midday meal.

The third stated that she would arrive in the afternoon.

All three schedules had been circulated.

None had been cancelled.

At the Xu family residence, the steward stood in the main courtyard holding the documents.

"Your Excellency, which schedule should we follow?"

Duke Xu looked at him.

"Which one did my daughter confirm?"

"Her letter stated that she would arrive today."

"Did she give a time?"

"No."

"Then why have you prepared three schedules?"

The steward looked down.

"The kitchen requested a meal time. The carriage attendants requested an arrival time. The eastern courtyard servants requested a room preparation time."

The Duke stared at him.

"Then each department should have prepared its own arrangements according to the information it required. Why have you combined them into three separate schedules?"

The steward had no answer.

Madam Xu, seated nearby, put down her embroidery.

"Perhaps you should simply ask the kitchen to keep the food warm."

The Duke looked at her.

"That is what I intended."

"Then do it."

The steward bowed and withdrew.

Madam Xu returned to her embroidery.

"She has been away for only a short time, and you have already made her arrival more complicated than necessary."

The Duke sighed.

"I am trying to ensure everything is comfortable."

"Then let her arrive without having to attend a meeting."

He looked at the three schedules.

After a moment, he ordered them all removed.

Yuru arrived shortly after the midday meal.

She had travelled in an ordinary carriage, accompanied by Fang Ru and two attendants.

There was no ceremonial procession.

The Duke and Madam Xu received her at the main entrance.

"Father. Mother."

Madam Xu stepped forward and took her hand.

"You have come."

"I said I would."

The Duke looked at the carriage.

"Was the journey comfortable?"

"Yes."

"Was the road crowded?"

"Not particularly."

"Did the carriage—"

Madam Xu touched his arm.

"Let her enter first."

The Duke stopped.

Yuru smiled.

She followed her parents into the residence.

The eastern courtyard was pleasant.

The garden had been carefully maintained, and the rooms were furnished with comfortable but not excessive furniture.

The desk had been replaced with a smaller one.

The sitting room contained flowers, tea utensils, and several books.

There were no account books.

Yuru noticed.

"Thank you for preparing the rooms."

Madam Xu smiled.

"Do you like them?"

"Very much."

The Duke looked satisfied.

He had personally prevented the household from placing its accounts in the study.

He considered this a meaningful accomplishment.

After tea, the family sat together.

The Duke asked about her new residence.

Yuru described it.

The guest residence was comfortable. The servants were sufficient. The arrangements for her personal belongings had been completed.

She had not yet decided where she would live permanently.

Madam Xu listened.

"Do you wish to remain in the palace?"

"For the time being."

"And afterward?"

"I have not decided."

The Duke nodded.

"Your rooms here will remain available."

"Thank you."

"You may stay as long as you wish."

Yuru looked at him.

"I appreciate that."

The Duke hesitated.

Then he asked, "Would you like to inspect the garden?"

Yuru smiled.

"Yes."

He rose immediately.

Madam Xu looked at him.

"You do not need to escort her through every path."

"I know the garden better than anyone."

"That is not the point."

Yuru laughed.

"Father, I would be happy to walk with you."

The Duke looked pleased.

He led her outside.

The garden had been repaired during the previous month.

Several stone paths had been levelled. The pond had been cleaned. The pavilion had received new roof tiles.

The Duke pointed out the improvements.

"The western path was uneven. We had it repaired."

Yuru examined the stones.

"Good."

"The pond was also cleaned."

"That is good."

"The gardener suggested replacing the old trees near the eastern wall."

Yuru looked at him.

"Why?"

"They are old."

"Are they unhealthy?"

"No."

"Then why replace them?"

The Duke paused.

"The gardener said newer trees would make the garden look better."

Yuru examined the trees.

Their branches were uneven, but they appeared healthy.

"Perhaps they should be maintained rather than replaced."

The Duke nodded.

"I thought so too."

They continued walking.

At the pavilion, he stopped.

"There is something I wished to discuss."

Yuru looked at him.

"Yes?"

"The household has considered your future arrangements."

Yuru waited.

The Duke continued.

"If you wish to establish a separate residence, the Xu family can assist with the initial arrangements. If you prefer to remain here, we can prepare additional rooms."

Yuru considered his words.

"Thank you. I will think about it."

The Duke nodded.

He did not press her.

After a moment, he said, "You have worked very hard for many years."

Yuru looked at the garden.

"Yes."

"You should not feel obliged to make another decision immediately."

She turned toward him.

"That is what I intend to do."

The Duke smiled.

They continued walking.

For once, no one asked her to approve a purchase, settle a dispute, or review an inventory.

The garden remained a garden.

It was a pleasant change.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao received an unexpected visitor.

It was Mr. Liang, the clan representative.

He had come to discuss the next stage of the property settlement.

Mingzhao received him in the modest sitting room of her rented residence.

Qiao Yue served tea.

Mr. Liang examined the room.

"You have settled in."

"Temporarily."

"Your rental agreement has three months remaining?"

"Yes."

He nodded.

"Your father wishes to arrange the next family meeting."

"Has the independent accountant completed his review?"

"Not entirely."

"Then what is the purpose of the meeting?"

Mr. Liang placed a document on the table.

"The family wishes to discuss the confirmed property categories and the division of ordinary estate assets."

Mingzhao read the first page.

The proposal separated the estate into several groups:

- Confirmed family-owned land and buildings.
- Property requiring maintenance.
- Property subject to boundary or ownership disputes.
- Commercial assets and partnership interests.
- Personal assets belonging to individual family members.
- Outstanding liabilities requiring verification.

Mingzhao examined the categories.

"These are reasonable headings."

Mr. Liang nodded.

"Your father believes the discussion can proceed even while some figures remain under review."

"That depends on whether the confirmed assets can be separated from the unresolved claims without affecting their valuation."

"That is why I have come."

Mingzhao returned the document.

"Then I will attend."

Mr. Liang looked relieved.

He had expected resistance.

Mingzhao continued.

"But I will not agree to a final division before the liabilities and valuations are properly recorded."

Mr. Liang nodded.

"That is understood."

"Also, I want Sun Qiming present for the accounting discussion."

"The family legal adviser will attend."

"Mr. Chen may attend as legal adviser. He is not a substitute for the independent accountant."

Mr. Liang considered this.

"I will convey your request."

Mingzhao nodded.

The meeting was scheduled for the following day.

Mr. Liang finished his tea.

Before leaving, he looked at her.

"You have established your own household quickly."

"It is a rented house."

"That does not make it less independent."

Mingzhao did not answer.

Mr. Liang rose.

"I will inform your father that you have agreed to attend."

"Please do."

He left.

Qiao Yue closed the door.

"Miss, another meeting."

"Yes."

"At least this one has a purpose."

"It does."

Qiao Yue looked at the proposal.

"Will it take long?"

"Probably."

"Then I should prepare more tea."

Mingzhao gave her a look.

Qiao Yue smiled and withdrew.

At the Xu residence, Yuru was having lunch with her parents.

The meal was simple.

The Duke had specifically asked the kitchen not to prepare an elaborate feast.

Madam Xu had also instructed the servants not to treat the visit as a formal reception.

The result was a comfortable meal with several familiar dishes.

Yuru tasted the soup.

"It is good."

Madam Xu smiled.

"The kitchen remembered what you liked."

Yuru nodded.

She had not eaten this particular dish in a long time.

The Duke looked at her.

"Would you like to have the recipe sent to your residence?"

"Perhaps."

Madam Xu glanced at him.

"Do not send the kitchen."

The Duke looked offended.

"I did not say I would send the kitchen."

"You were thinking about it."

Yuru laughed.

The Duke sighed.

His wife knew him too well.

After the meal, Madam Xu asked about the imperial children.

Yuru described their recent activities.

The Crown Prince was learning to oversee the Eastern Palace's accounts.

The Second Prince was working on transport arrangements.

The Third Princess was studying financial records.

Madam Xu listened with interest.

"Will they manage well?"

"They will make mistakes."

The Duke looked at her.

"That is not reassuring."

"It is realistic."

Yuru smiled.

"They must learn to correct their mistakes without expecting someone else to do everything for them."

Madam Xu nodded.

"That is true."

The Duke considered this.

Then he asked, "Do you think the emperor will appoint another Empress?"

"That is his decision."

"Would the imperial household remain stable?"

"It should, provided the responsibilities continue to be managed through the proper offices."

The Duke looked at her.

"You sound very confident."

"I am hopeful."

She did not say that the palace had already sent several unnecessary reports to the emperor.

She did not need to.

The Duke had not asked for an administrative assessment.

He had invited his daughter to lunch.

In the palace, Emperor Zhao Jingheng received a short update from Gao Shun.

The former Empress had arrived safely at the Xu family residence.

The emperor read the message.

"Good."

Gao Shun waited.

"Does she intend to stay overnight?"

"That has not been confirmed."

The emperor nodded.

"Then do not ask."

Gao Shun bowed.

The emperor returned to his work.

A moment later, he looked up.

"Has the Imperial Household Administration completed the revised reporting arrangement?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Are routine matters being sent to the responsible offices?"

"Most are."

"Most?"

Gao Shun hesitated.

"One report concerning the allocation of kitchen firewood was sent to the Hall of Mental Cultivation."

The emperor looked at him.

"Why?"

"The office was uncertain whether the purchase belonged under household provisions or general maintenance."

"Who is responsible for the purchase?"

"The kitchen administration."

"Then why did they send it here?"

Gao Shun lowered his head.

"I do not know, Your Majesty."

The emperor put down his brush.

"Return it."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Tell the kitchen administrator to classify the purchase according to its purpose and retain the supporting records."

Gao Shun accepted the document.

The emperor looked at him.

"And do not send me a report explaining that it has been returned."

Gao Shun bowed.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

He withdrew.

The emperor resumed writing.

The palace was improving.

The emperor was beginning to suspect that the improvement would require more patience than the original resignation.

At the rented residence, Mingzhao was reviewing the latest silk partnership report.

The accepted delivery had been recorded.

The payment had been received.

The remaining stock was being prepared for inspection.

Lin Shuyun had sent an updated production schedule.

The next group would be ready within several days.

Mingzhao checked the figures.

She then separated the partnership records from her personal household accounts.

Qiao Yue entered.

"Miss, a letter has arrived from the Marquisate."

Mingzhao accepted it.

The letter contained a short update from Mingyuan.

The former northern warehouse steward had identified the roadside storage yard operator as Zhao Fusheng.

Sun Qiming was attempting to locate the merchant's remaining business records.

The original grain discrepancy remained unchanged.

Mingzhao read the note.

Then she placed it in the investigation folder.

Qiao Yue asked, "Does this mean the grain has been found?"

"No."

"Does it mean we know where it went?"

"No."

"Then what does it mean?"

"It means we have another person to investigate."

Qiao Yue sighed.

"That is less satisfying."

"Investigations are not required to be satisfying."

Mingzhao returned to the silk report.

After a moment, she looked up.

"Has the second attendant returned from the market?"

"Yes."

"Did she bring the missing receipt?"

Qiao Yue nodded.

"She did."

"Good. Put it with the household expenses."

Qiao Yue looked surprised.

"That is all?"

"Yes."

"No questions?"

"The receipt matches the purchase."

Qiao Yue smiled.

"Then we have solved one mystery."

Mingzhao gave her a dry look.

"It was a vegetable purchase."

"Every investigation begins somewhere."

At the Xu residence, Yuru spent the afternoon with her mother.

They walked through the garden, examined the new roof tiles, and visited the small room where Yuru had studied as a child.

The room had changed.

The shelves were different.

The desk had been replaced.

But the window still overlooked the courtyard.

Madam Xu stood beside her.

"You used to sit there for hours."

Yuru looked at the desk.

"I remember."

"You were always reading."

"I still read."

Madam Xu smiled.

"You were also very stubborn."

Yuru looked at her.

"Was?"

Madam Xu laughed.

They sat together.

For a while, they spoke about family matters.

Then Madam Xu asked, "Were you happy in the palace?"

Yuru did not answer immediately.

She considered the question.

"I had meaningful work. I also had responsibilities that became too numerous."

"That is not quite the same as happiness."

"No."

Madam Xu looked at her.

"Do you regret resigning?"

Yuru shook her head.

"No."

"Then that is enough."

Yuru looked out the window.

For years, she had been surrounded by people who needed her to make decisions.

Now her mother had asked a question and allowed her to answer without trying to change the answer.

It was a small kindness.

Yuru appreciated it.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei reviewed the proposal for the following day's meeting.

He had asked Mr. Chen to prepare the legal framework.

Sun Qiming would attend as the independent accountant.

Mr. Liang would observe on behalf of the clan.

Master Luo would provide the latest property valuations where available.

The unresolved boundary dispute would remain separate.

The grain discrepancy would remain an open liability.

The two hundred and twenty-eight taels would remain unverified.

The confirmed seventy-two-tael repair expense would remain classified as estate maintenance.

The nine confirmed advances, four partially confirmed claims, and ten uncertain entries would remain separately listed.

Shen Guowei examined the summary.

He was beginning to see that the settlement would not be completed by one dramatic agreement.

It would require a series of decisions.

Some would be straightforward.

Others would require further evidence.

He looked at the empty chair beside his desk.

For years, Mingzhao had sat there reviewing the accounts.

He had regarded her work as a normal part of the household.

Now he was beginning to understand how much effort had been concealed by the apparent ease of the finished reports.

He called Steward Zhou.

"Have the records been prepared for tomorrow?"

"Yes, Marquis."

"Are the original documents available?"

"Yes."

"Then we will proceed carefully."

Steward Zhou bowed.

Shen Guowei added, "And do not ask Mingzhao to review the records before the meeting."

The steward looked surprised.

"Should she not receive the documents?"

"She should receive the documents as a party to the settlement, not as the person responsible for correcting them."

Steward Zhou nodded.

"Understood."

At dusk, Yuru prepared to return to the palace residence.

The Duke and Madam Xu accompanied her to the entrance.

"Will you visit again?" Madam Xu asked.

"Yes."

"Then come whenever you wish."

Yuru smiled.

"I will."

The Duke looked at the carriage.

"Would you like to stay longer next time?"

"Perhaps."

He nodded.

He did not ask her to make a promise.

The carriage departed.

Yuru looked back once at the family residence.

It had been a pleasant visit.

No one had asked her to approve the garden expenses.

No one had brought her an account book.

No one had attempted to appoint her as the household administrator.

The Duke had nearly placed the records in her sitting room, but he had corrected the mistake.

That was also progress.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao prepared for the following day's settlement meeting.

She arranged the relevant documents.

Personal assets.

Inherited property.

Commercial interests.

Loans and advances.

Confirmed liabilities.

Unverified expenses.

Disputed land.

She placed the original records in separate folders.

Qiao Yue watched.

"How many documents will you take?"

"Those relevant to the meeting."

"How many is that?"

Mingzhao looked at the table.

"Twenty-three."

Qiao Yue stared at her.

"Twenty-three?"

"Some are short."

"That does not make them lighter."

Mingzhao ignored her.

She checked the list again.

Then she placed the rental agreement in a separate folder.

Qiao Yue pointed at it.

"That is not part of the family settlement."

"No. It is part of my current household arrangements."

"Then why bring it?"

"To keep it separate from the family property records."

Qiao Yue nodded slowly.

"That makes sense."

Mingzhao closed the folder.

Tomorrow, the family would discuss the division of property.

There would be arguments.

There would be disagreements over valuations.

There might be new documents.

But the meeting would proceed according to the evidence.

She would not agree to figures that had not been verified.

She would not claim property that did not belong to her.

And she would not resume managing the estate simply because the family found the process inconvenient.

That night, Yuru returned to her study.

A small note from the emperor awaited her.

It was not an administrative request.

He had written only:

*I hope your visit was pleasant. The palace is functioning. The kitchen has finally learned where to send the tea.*

Yuru laughed.

She wrote a short reply.

*I am pleased. Please congratulate the kitchen administrator.*

She sealed the note and gave it to the attendant.

At the Marquisate, Mingzhao placed her twenty-three documents beside the door.

Her meeting would begin the next morning.

Two women had spent the day in very different households.

One had been welcomed as a daughter.

The other had been invited to negotiate as an independent property claimant.

Both had been offered arrangements that others considered convenient.

And both had discovered that a convenient arrangement was only useful when the people involved had actually agreed to it.
