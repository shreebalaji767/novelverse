# **Chapter 32: The Former Empress Has Received Her First Invitation Without an Official Seal. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received Her First Rent Reminder. Both Women Consider the Documents Unnecessarily Urgent.**

**24th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first morning after Xu Yuru's resignation began with a mistake.



It was not a serious mistake.



No one had confused the emperor's breakfast with the Empress Dowager's breakfast. No ceremonial object had disappeared. No official had submitted three contradictory instructions concerning a single piece of furniture.



The mistake was considerably smaller.



A palace attendant had brought the morning tea to the wrong residence.



The attendant stood in the courtyard of Yuru's temporary residence, holding the tray with both hands.



"Your Highness, the tea has arrived."



Yuru looked up from her book.



The attendant immediately realized something was wrong.



He had addressed her incorrectly.



He lowered his head.



"Please forgive this servant. This servant meant—"



"Former Empress," Fang Ru supplied gently.



The attendant looked even more nervous.



"Former Empress, this servant apologizes."



Yuru put down her book.



"Why was the tea brought here?"



The attendant glanced at the tray.



"The morning tea order still lists Chengming Palace as the destination."



"Then the order has not been updated."



"Yes, Your Highness—"



He stopped.



Fang Ru looked at him.



The attendant swallowed.



"Yes, Madam."



Yuru smiled.



"Do not worry. The tea is not at fault."



The attendant bowed.



Yuru examined the tray.



"Is this the tea intended for Chengming Palace?"



"Yes, Madam."



"Then it should be taken there."



The attendant hesitated.



"Would Madam like to keep it?"



"No. The people in Chengming Palace have been expecting it."



The attendant bowed again and withdrew.



Fang Ru watched him leave.



"Should I inform the kitchen?"



"Yes. The order should be corrected."



"Would you like me to send the instruction?"



Yuru looked at her.



"Send it to the kitchen administrator."



Fang Ru nodded.



She did not ask whether Yuru wished to approve the new order.



That was progress.



A small amount of progress.



But progress.

The kitchen administrator received the correction before the morning meal ended.



He read it carefully.



The tea order had been prepared for Chengming Palace for many years. The destination had not changed because the kitchen staff had not received a revised instruction.



He called the responsible clerk.



"Why was the former Empress's tea order not updated?"



The clerk looked through the register.



"The previous order remained active."



"Who was supposed to cancel it?"



The clerk hesitated.



"The Imperial Household Administration."



"Did they issue a cancellation?"



"No."



"Then send them a notice."



The clerk prepared the notice.



The administrator examined it.



"Do not write that the former Empress has refused the tea."



The clerk looked confused.



"Then what should I write?"



"Write that the tea order is to be delivered according to the revised residence arrangement."



The clerk nodded.



The administrator returned to his work.



He had not considered that changing a residence would require the cancellation of an old tea order.



He suspected there would be more such matters.



He was correct.

At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, Emperor Zhao Jingheng was reviewing the previous day's reports.



The ceremonial departure had been completed.



The seals were in the Archives.



The administrative transfers had been recorded.



The palace had survived its first morning without an Empress managing its ordinary affairs.



The emperor was pleased.



He was less pleased when Gao Shun presented him with a report concerning the tea.



"Why am I receiving this?"



Gao Shun bowed.



"It was submitted as a record of the first-day transition."



The emperor looked at him.



"Did the former Empress receive her tea?"



"No, Your Majesty. The tea was delivered to the wrong residence and returned to Chengming Palace."



"Was the matter corrected?"



"Yes."



"Then why does it require a report?"



Gao Shun hesitated.



"The clerk believed the first-day transition record should include it."



Zhao Jingheng put down the document.



"Does every household matter require a report to me?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Then why does a tea delivery?"



Gao Shun lowered his head.



The emperor sighed.



"Return it to the kitchen administrator. He can update the order."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Gao Shun took the report.



The emperor looked at the next document.



It concerned the arrangement of the former Empress's personal servants.



He read it.



"Why are they asking whether Fang Ru should remain with her?"



"The servant has expressed a willingness to continue."



"Then the two of them should discuss the arrangement."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Why has this been submitted to me?"



Gao Shun was silent.



The emperor stared at the report.



He was beginning to understand that the palace had not merely depended on Yuru's decisions.



It had become accustomed to sending every uncertainty to her.



Now that she was no longer the person responsible, the uncertainty had started travelling upward.



The emperor placed the report aside.



"Return this one too."



Gao Shun bowed.



Zhao Jingheng looked at the growing pile.



"Perhaps I should issue an order prohibiting reports about tea."



Gao Shun did not answer.



He was not certain whether the emperor was joking.

At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received a letter from her landlord.



The letter was short.



The rent for the next period was not yet due.



However, the landlord wished to confirm whether Mingzhao intended to extend the rental agreement beyond the initial three months.



Mingzhao read it twice.



Qiao Yue stood beside her.



"Is it bad news?"



"No."



"Then why are you reading it twice?"



"Because the landlord has asked about an extension before the first month has ended."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Perhaps the landlord likes having us as tenants."



"Perhaps the landlord likes knowing whether the house will remain occupied."



"That is less flattering."



"It is also more likely."



Mingzhao placed the letter beside the rental agreement.



The house was acceptable.



The rooms were sufficient.



The kitchen was inconvenient but functional.



The rear drain had been checked.



The upper latch had been repaired.



The dampness in the lower room remained manageable.



The rent was reasonable for a temporary arrangement.



There was no reason to make a permanent decision.



She wrote a brief reply.



*The present agreement will be observed for its full term. A decision concerning extension will be made after further inspection of the property and consideration of future requirements.*



Qiao Yue watched her write.



"That sounds very formal."



"It is a business arrangement."



"Could you not simply say that we have not decided?"



"I have said that."



"With many more words."



Mingzhao folded the letter.



"That is why written agreements are useful."



Qiao Yue sighed.



She was beginning to suspect that every ordinary conversation could become a contract if Mingzhao had enough paper.

The next task was the household's provisions.



Mingzhao had spent the previous evening reviewing the first complete expenditure statement.



She now wanted to compare the actual stock with the quantities recorded.



The senior maid brought the kitchen ledger.



The provisions were stored in a small room beside the kitchen.



There were sacks of rice, dried vegetables, oil, salt, tea, and several jars of preserved food.



Mingzhao examined the inventory.



"How much rice was purchased?"



"One sack."



"How much remains?"



The senior maid checked.



"Approximately half."



"When was it purchased?"



"Four days ago."



"How many people have eaten from it?"



The maid listed the household members.



Mingzhao checked the figures.



The quantity was reasonable.



She moved to the oil.



The jar had been recorded as full when purchased.



It was now slightly more than half full.



"Has it been used for cooking only?"



"Yes, Miss."



"Has any been issued to the servants separately?"



"No."



Mingzhao checked the salt.



Then the tea.



Then the dried vegetables.



The inventory was not perfect.



But it was substantially better than the first report.



She made several corrections.



Qiao Yue leaned against the doorframe.



"Miss, are you satisfied?"



"Not yet."



"Of course."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"What?"



"Nothing."



The senior maid tried not to smile.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



"Tomorrow, we will compare the kitchen purchases with the market receipts."



Qiao Yue straightened.



"Tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"Miss, we have already counted the provisions."



"We have counted the stock. We have not compared the prices."



Qiao Yue looked toward the senior maid.



The senior maid lowered her head.



Neither offered assistance.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingxiu had begun reviewing the household purchase records.



She did not enjoy the work.



The first ledger contained several dozen entries for ordinary goods.



Rice.



Vegetables.



Lamp oil.



Firewood.



Cleaning materials.



Kitchen utensils.



Cloth.



Soap.



Small repairs.



She had expected the work to be straightforward.



It was not.



Some entries recorded quantities but not prices.



Others recorded prices but not quantities.



Several purchases had been entered under general household expenses without identifying the receiving department.



She called the clerk responsible for the purchases.



"Why is this entry listed as general expenditure?"



The clerk examined the page.



"That was the usual practice, Second Miss."



"Who approved it?"



"The household steward."



"Which steward?"



The clerk looked uncomfortable.



"The former steward."



"Then how do we know what was purchased?"



The clerk hesitated.



"We have the total amount."



Mingxiu looked at him.



"That is not what I asked."



The clerk lowered his head.



She turned the page.



"These two entries are both for lamp oil. Were they separate purchases?"



"Yes."



"On the same day?"



"Yes."



"Why?"



The clerk checked the dates.



"One was for the main residence. The other was for the servants' quarters."



"Then why are they listed under the same heading?"



The clerk did not know.



Mingxiu sighed.



"Separate them."



"Yes, Second Miss."



"And do not change the totals."



The clerk bowed.



Mingxiu returned to the ledger.



She had once thought Mingzhao was unnecessarily strict.



Now she understood that the strictness had been directed at a real problem.



The family had been treating the existence of a total as proof that the underlying expenditure was understandable.



It was not.

Shen Mingyuan arrived later with the latest note from the former northern warehouse steward.



The old man had examined the receiving note found among his papers.



He remembered the location.



It had been a temporary roadside storage yard used by several transport contractors.



The yard had stood near a branch road leading toward the southern agricultural properties.



It had not been owned by the Marquisate.



The old steward could not remember which merchant had operated it.



Nor could he establish whether the grain had remained there overnight.



Mingyuan placed the note before Mingxiu.



"Does this help?"



She read it.



"It identifies the stopping point."



"Yes."



"Does it establish who received the grain?"



"No."



"Then it helps the investigation, but it does not settle it."



Mingyuan nodded.



He looked at the account summary.



"Sun Qiming intends to ask whether the yard's former operator can be identified."



"That may take time."



"Yes."



Mingxiu returned the paper.



"Then we should not make a conclusion before he finds the answer."



Mingyuan looked at her.



"You have been speaking like Elder Sister."



Mingxiu frowned.



"Is that an insult?"



"No."



He paused.



"It is an observation."



Mingxiu returned to her ledger.



"Then observe quietly."



Mingyuan laughed.



For the first time in several days, the two siblings had spoken without an argument about Mingzhao.

At the former Empress's residence, Fang Ru received a visitor.



It was Third Princess Zhao Minglan.



The princess entered carrying two books and a small account ledger.



"Mother."



Yuru looked up.



"Minglan. You have come early."



"I brought the books you asked for."



Yuru glanced at them.



"Those are the Eastern Palace expenditure records."



"Yes. The Crown Prince said I could use the copies."



"Good."



Minglan sat down.



"I also brought a question."



"What is it?"



"The Eastern Palace has separate records for household provisions and study materials. But some items are purchased together and divided afterward."



Yuru listened.



"Give me an example."



"Paper. The same order includes paper for official correspondence, study practice, and private reading."



"How are the quantities divided?"



"They are recorded when issued."



"Then what is the problem?"



"The purchase record shows one total. The issue record shows several separate quantities. I am not certain whether the two records can be compared directly."



Yuru smiled.



"That is a good question."



Minglan opened the ledger.



Yuru did not take it from her.



Instead, she asked, "What do you think?"



Minglan considered.



"Perhaps the purchase record should include the total quantity and the issue records should identify the receiving departments."



"That is one method."



"Would it be sufficient?"



"It depends on whether the issue records are complete and whether the total quantity received matches the purchase record."



Minglan nodded.



Yuru continued.



"Do not assume that a difference between two records proves an error. First determine what each record is meant to show."



Minglan wrote this down.



Then she looked at her mother.



"Will you still teach me?"



"Yes."



"Even though you have resigned?"



Yuru smiled.



"Teaching you is not the same as administering the palace."



Minglan looked relieved.



She turned to the next page.



For the next hour, they examined the paper records.



Yuru asked questions.



Minglan calculated quantities.



When she made a mistake, Yuru pointed it out.



When Yuru's explanation became too complicated, Minglan asked her to simplify it.



At one point, the princess said, "Mother, I think you are making this more difficult than it needs to be."



Yuru paused.



Then she laughed.



"Perhaps I am."



Minglan smiled.



It was the first lesson in which the teacher had admitted that the student might have a point.

At the Hall of Mental Cultivation, the emperor received the daily summary from the Imperial Household Administration.



It was shorter than yesterday's.



He read it.



The first item concerned the former Empress's residence.



The second concerned the tea order.



The third concerned the personal servants.



The fourth concerned the transfer of a small number of remaining household objects.



Zhao Jingheng looked at the fourth item.



"Have they found the box?"



Gao Shun nodded.



"Yes, Your Majesty. It was transferred yesterday."



"Then why is it listed as incomplete?"



"The record was prepared before the transfer was confirmed."



The emperor put down the paper.



"Update it."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He continued reading.



The report also included a note that the former Empress had received a visit from Third Princess Minglan.



"That is not an administrative matter," he said.



"No, Your Majesty."



"Then why is it here?"



Gao Shun examined the document.



"It was included in the residence activity record."



The emperor stared at him.



"Does the palace now require a record every time a princess visits her mother?"



Gao Shun lowered his head.



"I will ask the office to revise the reporting arrangement."



The emperor leaned back.



"Do so."



He was silent for a moment.



Then he asked, "Has the former Empress requested anything?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Has she sent any instructions to the offices?"



"No."



"Has anyone asked her to approve anything?"



Gao Shun hesitated.



"One office sent a question concerning the tea order."



The emperor's expression changed.



"What did she say?"



"She directed them to the kitchen administrator."



Zhao Jingheng nodded.



"Good."



He looked at the report again.



"Then the arrangement is working."



Gao Shun bowed.



The emperor returned to his papers.



He was pleased.



He was also beginning to suspect that the palace's greatest difficulty would not be learning to operate without Yuru.



It would be learning not to send her every minor problem simply because she had once solved it.

In the evening, Mingzhao received a letter from her father.



It was not a demand.



It was not a summons.



It was an update concerning the ongoing property division.



Shen Guowei wrote that the independent accountant had been permitted to continue examining the records. The clan representative would attend the next meeting. The family had agreed to keep the unresolved liabilities separate from the confirmed assets until further evidence was available.



He also informed her that Mingyuan had begun reviewing the estate accounts and Mingxiu had taken responsibility for examining ordinary household purchases.



The letter ended with a sentence that Mingzhao read twice.



*The household is adjusting to your departure.*



She placed the letter on the table.



Qiao Yue watched her.



"Does your father want you to return?"



"No."



"Does he want you to reply?"



"He has not asked."



"Then what does he want?"



Mingzhao considered.



"Perhaps he wants me to know that the household has not collapsed."



Qiao Yue looked around the modest room.



"And?"



Mingzhao folded the letter.



"Then it has not collapsed."



She did not smile.



But she did not look displeased either.



She took out a sheet of paper and wrote a reply.



She acknowledged the report.



She thanked him for allowing the independent review to continue.



She requested that the original records remain preserved and that no confirmed asset or liability be reclassified without supporting documentation.



She did not offer to return.



She did not criticize Mingyuan.



She did not give Mingxiu instructions.



When she finished, she sealed the letter.



Qiao Yue looked at it.



"That is all?"



"That is all."



"You have written to your father and said nothing about the family."



"I have written about the family accounts."



"That is not what I meant."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Then perhaps you should be more precise."



Qiao Yue gave up.

That night, Yuru sat in her new study.



The tea order had been corrected.



The residence arrangements were complete.



The Crown Prince had not sent any documents.



The emperor had not sent any requests.



The Third Princess had visited.



Yuru opened her historical book again.



She read several pages.



Then she stopped.



A thought had occurred to her.



For many years, she had measured the end of a day by the number of matters resolved.



There had always been another list.



Another report.



Another person waiting outside the door.



Now she had finished reading several pages without interruption.



She looked at the book.



It was not a remarkable achievement.



But it was the first evening in a long time when she had not been required to turn a quiet hour into useful work.



She continued reading.



At the eastern market, Mingzhao was doing something rather different.



She was calculating the cost of extending the rental agreement.



The landlord had asked too early.



The household had not yet completed its first month.



The permanent residence search was still open.



The family settlement was unfinished.



And the silk partnership had not completed its deliveries.



She calculated the cost of remaining in the rented house for another three months.



Then she calculated the likely cost of moving.



Then she calculated the cost of repairing the northern canal property, including the roof, drainage, kitchen, well, and boundary issues.



She stopped.



The figures were not sufficient to make a decision.



She closed the ledger.



Tomorrow, she would ask Master Luo for an updated assessment.



Tonight, she would sleep.

The next morning, the palace would receive another report.



The Marquisate would receive another account.



The former Empress would continue learning how to live without administering the imperial household.



The Marquis's eldest daughter would continue learning how to live without administering the Marquisate.



Both had left their former positions.



Neither had escaped paperwork.



And somewhere in the palace, a kitchen clerk was still trying to determine whether the tea order should be marked as cancelled, transferred, or simply redirected.



He had decided to ask his supervisor.



His supervisor had decided to consult the Imperial Household Administration.



The Imperial Household Administration had decided that the matter belonged to the kitchen.



The tea, meanwhile, was already being delivered to the correct residence.
