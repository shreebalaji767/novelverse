# **Chapter 115: The Former Empress Has Discovered That an Invitation to Tea Does Not Require a Discussion of Palace Affairs. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That a Supplier May Be Willing to Improve a Sample Without Being Willing to Accept Responsibility for Its Colour. The Marquis's Heir Has Discovered That a Third Box Can Contain a Register, Although the Register May Have Been Kept for a Purpose Entirely Different from the One He Needs.**

**19th Day of the Seventh Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The Dowager had ordered two kinds of tea.

One was a mild green tea that she preferred in the morning. The other was a darker tea sent by a provincial official, which she had not yet decided whether she liked.

Xu Yuru arrived shortly after the afternoon meal.

The Dowager received her in a small sitting room overlooking an enclosed courtyard. There were no officials present, no household clerks waiting outside, and no documents placed on the table.

Yuru noticed the last detail before she sat down.

The Dowager noticed her noticing it.

"Are you searching for something?"

"No, Your Majesty."

"Then why did you look at the table?"

"I was admiring its simplicity."

The Dowager glanced at the table.

It was a plain wooden table with two tea cups, a small plate of fruit, and a folded cloth.

"That is a polite way of saying there is nothing on it."

"It is a very useful quality in a table."

The Dowager laughed.

"Sit down. I have no intention of asking you to arrange anything."

Yuru took her seat.

"Then I am particularly pleased to be here."

The Dowager gave her a sideways glance.

"Have you become so suspicious of invitations?"

"Only of those that arrive with a list of matters to discuss."

"Then this one should satisfy you."

The attendant poured the first tea.

The Dowager tried it and made a small expression of uncertainty.

Yuru waited.

"Well?"

"It is not unpleasant."

"That sounds like a judgment from an official report."

"It is a judgment from someone who has not yet decided whether she wants another cup."

The Dowager set down the cup.

"Perhaps I should send it back."

"That would be difficult. It has already been poured."

"Then I shall endure it."

Yuru took a small sip.

The tea was stronger than she preferred, but not bad.

"Would you like the other one?" the Dowager asked.

"Perhaps after this."

The Dowager smiled.

They spoke about the courtyard, the weather, and the difficulty of keeping fruit fresh in the heat. The Dowager mentioned that one of her attendants had recently purchased a new tea kettle without consulting the kitchen steward.

"Was it unsuitable?" Yuru asked.

"It was too small."

"Did the attendant know how many people would drink from it?"

"She said it was attractive."

Yuru looked at the kettle placed on a side table.

It was indeed attractive.

It was also too small.

"Then she purchased it for the wrong reason."

"That is what I told her."

"Did she return it?"

"She tried. The merchant refused."

Yuru considered the matter.

"Then perhaps it can be used for serving one or two people."

The Dowager looked at her.

"That is what the attendant suggested."

"Then she has found a use for it."

"She also suggested that I buy a second kettle."

Yuru smiled.

"That may be more expensive than admitting the first purchase was unnecessary."

The Dowager laughed again.

For some time, they continued discussing ordinary things.

At no point did the Dowager ask about the palace household.

At no point did Yuru volunteer to discuss it.

When the tea was finished, the Dowager offered her some fruit.

Yuru accepted a small portion.

"You seem more rested," the Dowager observed.

"I have been sleeping well."

"Are you still undecided about your residence?"

"Yes."

"Do you intend to remain in the palace guest residence for the full period?"

"I have not decided."

The Dowager nodded.

"That is an answer."

Yuru looked at her.

"Some people find it an unsatisfactory one."

"Some people ask questions because they want to hear a decision. I asked because I was curious."

Yuru lowered her eyes briefly.

"Then I am glad you asked."

The Dowager reached for her cup.

"Stay as long as you find the place comfortable. When you decide to leave, you may leave. There is no need to turn a residence into another duty."

Yuru listened.

The words were simple.

She did not mistake them for an order.

After another short conversation, she rose to take her leave.

The Dowager did not ask her to remain.

Yuru returned to her residence before the evening meal.

It had been a pleasant visit, and there was nothing else to report.

At the workshop, Mingzhao received the supplier's reply.

He was willing to prepare another dyed sample, but he did not agree that the first sample had failed to meet the specification.

His explanation was that the reference piece had been stored for some time and might have changed slightly in colour.

Mingzhao read the statement twice.

Luo Shun stood beside her.

"Does the supplier believe the difference is caused by the age of the reference cloth?"

"He says it is possible."

"Is it?"

"It is possible."

Mingzhao placed the two samples beside one another again.

The reference cloth had been stored in a closed box, away from direct sunlight. It was not new, but it had not been exposed to the conditions that might produce a substantial change.

She did not know whether the difference came from the dye mixture, the cloth, or the storage.

She could not prove the supplier was wrong.

She could also not accept the claim that the reference had changed merely because he had written it down.

"Ask him to provide the second sample using the same material batch," she said. "He may adjust the dye mixture, but he must record the adjustment."

"Should we tell him that we do not accept his explanation?"

"We do not need to argue about it. We need a sample that meets the agreed colour."

Luo Shun nodded.

"Would you like to send the reference piece to him?"

"No. He may examine it here."

"That may be inconvenient for him."

"It may."

The foreman waited.

Mingzhao continued, "If he wishes to supply the material, he must be able to compare his work with the reference. If he cannot, we can ask another supplier."

"Would that delay the trial?"

"Yes."

"Would it cost more?"

"Possibly."

Luo Shun lowered his eyes.

Mingzhao looked at the production book.

"Do you believe we should accept the first sample?"

The foreman thought carefully.

"No. The difference may be small, but if the buyer compares the finished cloth with the reference, he may reject it."

"Then we agree."

She wrote the decision into the pending production record.

*First dyed sample not accepted. Supplier's explanation of colour variation noted but not established. Second sample requested. No material purchase authorized.*

She signed it.

The foreman read the entry.

"Should I tell the workers that the trial may be delayed?"

"Tell them that the sample has not been approved. They already know production has not begun."

"Yes."

He took the paper and left.

Mingzhao looked at the failed sample once more.

It was not ugly cloth.

It was not even badly dyed.

It was simply not the cloth she had agreed to produce.

That was sufficient reason to refuse it.

She moved the sample into the pending file.

Then she turned to the workshop's ordinary accounts.

The previous orders were closed. The operating reserve remained intact. The possible trial-production funds had not been spent on materials. The sample expense was small and properly recorded.

She checked the figures and closed the ledger.

There was no profit to celebrate and no loss to conceal.

That, too, was useful.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan opened the third box.

The third box was heavier than the first two.

Its label was clearer, although the writing had been faded by moisture. The remaining characters indicated that the papers had once belonged to the northern transport office.

Mingyuan watched the storehouse keeper remove the lid.

Inside were several bundles of receipts, two damaged notebooks, a collection of duty rosters, and a thick register wrapped in oilcloth.

Mingyuan looked at the register.

"Record the outer wrapping before opening it."

The clerk did so.

The register was then placed on the table.

Its cover had no title, only a number written in black ink.

The first pages contained a list of cart repairs.

The next several pages recorded payments for wheel fittings, rope, and replacement harnesses.

Mingyuan turned through them carefully.

"This is not the northern dispatch book," Liang Wen observed.

"No."

The entries were arranged by expense rather than by dispatch.

Mingyuan turned to the last section.

There were several pages of cart assignments, followed by a list of drivers.

He stopped.

"These entries have dates."

The clerk leaned forward.

"Yes, Young Master."

Mingyuan examined the dates.

They covered a period of several months, including the month of the four-cart dispatch.

He found the relevant section and began reading.

The register recorded the assignment of individual carts to transport offices. It also recorded the dates on which carts were inspected, repaired, or returned to the main yard.

One entry referred to the same four cart numbers found in the temporary allocation list.

This time, a date was written beside them.

Mingyuan compared it with the date of the four-cart movement entry.

The dates were separated by several days.

He read the entries again.

"Do the numbers match exactly?" Steward Zhou asked.

"Yes."

"Then these are likely the same carts."

"They may be."

Mingyuan turned to the clerk.

"Does the register state whether the carts were assigned permanently or temporarily?"

The clerk checked the heading.

"Temporarily. The entry says they were assigned to the northern transport office until the end of the month."

"Does it record their return?"

The clerk searched the following pages.

There was an entry showing that three carts had returned to the main yard. The fourth was marked as undergoing repairs.

Mingyuan looked at the date.

The repair entry came after the four-cart dispatch.

"Does it say where the fourth cart was repaired?"

"No, Young Master. It only records the repair cost."

Mingyuan turned the page.

There was no reference to cargo.

No destination.

No receiving office.

No grain.

He sat back.

The register had established that the four carts were assigned to the northern transport office during the relevant period.

It had not established what they carried.

It had not established who drove them.

It had not established where they went on the day of the movement entry.

It had, however, confirmed that the four numbers in the temporary allocation list were not accidental.

"Copy the relevant pages," he instructed. "Include the pages before and after the entries. We need the full sequence."

The clerk nodded.

"Should we ask the former transport supervisors to identify the repair entry?"

"Not yet. First establish the date and the cart numbers. Then ask a specific question."

Steward Zhou looked at the register.

"This is more useful than the first two boxes."

"Yes."

"Perhaps the dispatch book is in this box as well."

"Perhaps."

He did not look pleased or disappointed.

The third box had finally produced a record that connected two previously separate lists.

That was progress.

It was not the answer.

Mingzhao received the Marquisate's new report in the late afternoon.

The message contained copies of the cart assignment register's relevant pages.

She examined the entries.

The four carts had indeed been assigned to the northern transport office during the relevant period. Their numbers matched the movement record.

She checked the dates.

The register established the assignment period, but not the exact journey.

Mingzhao read the repair entry.

Three carts had returned to the main yard. The fourth had been sent for repairs.

She set the papers down.

Qiao Yue watched her.

"Have they found the destination?"

"No."

"The drivers?"

"No."

"Then what have they found?"

"That the four carts were assigned to the northern transport office."

Qiao Yue looked at the pages.

"Is that useful?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"It tells us the carts were available to that office during the relevant period. It also gives us a later record of three returns and one repair."

"Does that mean the carts were used for the dispatch?"

"It makes the connection more likely. It does not prove that every cart on the assignment list was used in the recorded movement."

Qiao Yue nodded.

Mingzhao took up her brush.

She wrote a reply to Mingyuan.

*The assignment register confirms that the four cart numbers were allocated to the northern transport office during the relevant period. The later return and repair entries may assist in identifying their movements, but they do not establish the cargo or destination of the recorded dispatch. Please compare the dates of the return entries with the movement notebook and any surviving repair receipt.*

She paused.

Then she added:

*The assignment register should be retained as a separate source. It should not be copied into the dispatch record as though it were a receipt.*

She sealed the letter.

Qiao Yue collected it.

"Would you like me to send it immediately?"

"Yes."

Qiao Yue left.

Mingzhao looked again at the report.

The Marquisate had found a real connection.

It had not found a solution.

She could understand why Mingyuan might be encouraged by the discovery. He was learning how to separate evidence from conclusions, and the register gave him something solid to work with.

But she would not allow the family to turn a useful record into an excuse to revive old assumptions.

She placed the report in the property file.

Then she returned to the workshop's pending sample account.

The household dispute could wait until the evidence was ready.

The cloth could not be made until the colour was right.

Both matters required patience, although she preferred the one that could be settled with a measuring rule.

In the palace, Yuru received a small package from the Dowager's residence.

It contained a different tea from the one they had tasted the previous afternoon.

The note explained that the Dowager had found the first tea too strong and had decided to send Yuru the milder variety instead.

Yuru read the note and laughed softly.

Fang Ru looked up from the table.

"What is it, Madam?"

"Her Majesty has decided that the first tea was unsuitable."

"Has she sent another?"

"Yes."

Fang Ru opened the package.

The tea was wrapped carefully and labelled by the attendant who had prepared it.

"Would you like me to brew it?"

"Not yet."

"Shall I put it away?"

"Yes. We can try it tomorrow."

Fang Ru placed the package in the tea cabinet.

Yuru looked at the writing table.

Her garden book lay beside the letter she had received from her aunt.

She picked up the letter.

It contained an invitation to visit the Xu family at the end of the month.

Yuru read it once more.

There was no urgency in the invitation.

She could go if she wished.

She could also decline.

She placed the letter beside the others.

"Fang Ru, prepare a reply tomorrow."

"Would you like to accept?"

"Yes. I would like to visit."

Fang Ru nodded.

"Shall I ask the Duke's residence to prepare a room?"

Yuru considered the question.

"No. I will be visiting for the day. There is no need to arrange an overnight stay."

"Yes, Madam."

The matter was settled.

Yuru returned to her book.

She had made a plan for the end of the month.

It was a small plan, and it did not involve the palace household.

She found that she rather liked it.

That evening, Mingyuan read Mingzhao's reply.

He placed it beside the copied pages from the cart assignment register.

Steward Zhou stood nearby.

"She has confirmed the assignment period."

"Yes."

"She has also warned us not to treat the register as a dispatch receipt."

"That is correct."

Mingyuan looked at the pages.

"We should now compare the return dates with the movement entry."

"Should we ask the old transport supervisors?"

"Yes. But ask them whether they recognize the cart numbers and the repair sequence. Do not ask whether the carts carried grain."

Steward Zhou nodded.

Mingyuan continued, "The question about cargo must be answered from a receipt, a dispatch instruction, or a receiving record. Their recollection may help us find one, but it cannot replace one."

"Understood."

Mingyuan reviewed the investigation list.

The missing northern dispatch book remained missing.

The movement notebook's return record remained incomplete.

The four carts were now confirmed as assigned to the northern transport office.

Their exact journey remained uncertain.

The grain discrepancy remained separate.

He looked at the next box number.

"Tomorrow we open the fourth."

Steward Zhou bowed.

Mingyuan signed the day's inventory and closed the file.

For once, he had found a record that answered a question without immediately creating a dispute about whether the record existed.

It had created other questions instead.

He supposed this was progress.

After all, the old records had been kept for years without anyone examining them carefully. It would have been unreasonable to expect them to become cooperative simply because the household had finally become interested.

The register had been found.

The dispatch book had not.

And somewhere among the remaining boxes, there might be another document that could establish what the four carts had carried.

There might also be six more receipts for cart repairs.

Mingyuan had begun to suspect that the old transport office had repaired carts more often than it had recorded where they went.

He was not yet prepared to call that a conclusion.
