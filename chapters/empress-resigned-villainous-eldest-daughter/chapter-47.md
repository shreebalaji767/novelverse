# **Chapter 47: The Former Empress Has Agreed to Take a Walk. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Agreed to Attend a Meeting. One Has Been Told Not to Discuss Government. The Other Has Been Told Not to Discuss the Kitchen.**

**9th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Emperor arrived at the imperial gardens without a single document.



This was unusual enough that Gao Shun noticed it.



It was also unusual enough that the two attendants accompanying the Emperor checked their hands twice to make certain they had not accidentally carried anything.



Zhao Jingheng noticed their behaviour.



"Why are you looking at your sleeves?"



One attendant bowed.



"Your Majesty, I was ensuring that no papers had been brought."



The Emperor looked at Gao Shun.



Gao Shun lowered his head.



"The Empress Dowager's instruction was quite specific, Your Majesty."



"She instructed me not to bring documents. She did not instruct the attendants to search themselves."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor sighed.



He had once commanded armies.



He had negotiated with powerful ministers.



He had faced provincial officials who could turn a simple tax question into a three-hour argument.



Now he was being supervised on a garden walk.



He decided not to complain.



Yuru was already waiting beneath a covered walkway.



She wore a simple pale-green robe, and Fang Ru stood several paces behind her.



The Emperor approached.



"You came."



"You invited me."



"I thought you might be occupied."



"I was reading."



"Then I interrupted you."



"Not particularly."



The Emperor smiled.



They began walking.



For several minutes, neither spoke.



The gardens were quiet. The gardeners had recently trimmed the shrubs, and the stone path was clean. A small pond reflected the pale sky.



The Emperor glanced at Yuru.



"Do you miss the palace?"



She considered the question.



"I am still in the palace."



"You know what I mean."



"I miss some people. I do not miss the work."



He nodded.



"That is fair."



They continued walking.



After a while, he said, "The departments are improving."



"I am glad."



"The inventory records are still troublesome."



"They will be."



He looked at her.



"You are not going to ask what the problem is?"



"No."



"Not even out of curiosity?"



"I am curious. I am simply not responsible for correcting it."



The Emperor laughed.



"I see."



They reached the pond.



Yuru stopped to watch a fish move beneath the water.



The Emperor stood beside her.



"Sometimes I think the palace became accustomed to having you solve everything."



"It did."



"Perhaps I allowed that to happen."



Yuru looked at him.



"Yes."



He turned towards her.



She did not soften the answer.



After a moment, he nodded.



"I should have noticed earlier."



"Perhaps."



The Emperor sighed.



"You are unusually generous with your agreement today."



Yuru smiled.



"You asked for honesty."



He laughed again.



They continued along the path.



For once, the Emperor did not ask her to review a report.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Mingzhao was examining a document from Master Luo.



The neighbouring property's old registration record had not yet been obtained.



However, Master Luo had found a reference to the twelve-year-old drainage work in a local maintenance ledger.



The entry identified the work as a joint repair involving several nearby properties.



It did not state that the channel had been widened under a formal boundary adjustment.



It also did not identify who had paid for the additional excavation.



Mingzhao read the entry carefully.



Qiao Yue stood beside her.



"Does this mean the channel was widened by the neighbours?"



"It means several properties were involved in the repair."



"Could the owner of the northern canal house have paid for it?"



"Possibly."



"Could Mr Qian have paid?"



"Possibly."



Qiao Yue looked disappointed.



"Everyone could have done it."



"That is not what the document says."



Mingzhao placed it beside the older map.



"The record establishes that the work was shared. It does not establish the extent of the work on each property or whether the boundary was changed."



"Then what do we need?"



"The original work order, if it survives. The neighbouring registration. And a clearer account of the land office's measurements."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"The house has become a government office."



"No. A government office would have more people arguing about which document belongs to which desk."



Qiao Yue laughed.



Mingzhao wrote a note to Master Luo.



She asked him to continue the inquiry and not to arrange another inspection until the relevant records were available.



She had no intention of paying for repeated measurements of the same uncertain strip of land.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan was reviewing the old agricultural leases.



He had discovered that the estate register contained three different descriptions of the same western field.



One described it as leased.



Another described it as temporarily cultivated.



A third listed it among the fields under direct estate management.



He called Steward Zhou.



"Which description is correct?"



Steward Zhou examined the papers.



"The field was leased several years ago. The tenant's agreement expired last year."



"Was it renewed?"



"I have not found a renewal agreement."



"Was it cultivated this spring?"



"Yes. The tenant planted crops."



"Under what arrangement?"



The steward hesitated.



"I do not know."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"Then we need to ask."



Steward Zhou nodded.



The question seemed simple.



The answer would require speaking to the tenant, checking the rent receipts, and establishing whether the cultivation had been authorized.



Mingyuan wrote the matter into the review list.



Mingxiu entered with a separate account book.



"Have you finished the western field?"



"No."



"Why?"



"Because the records disagree."



She looked at the papers.



"Could we not simply mark it as leased?"



"Under what agreement?"



"The old one."



"It expired."



"Then mark it as temporarily cultivated."



"Under what arrangement?"



Mingxiu paused.



She looked at the three descriptions again.



Then she sighed.



"I see."



Mingyuan smiled faintly.



"Welcome to the accounts room."



She gave him an irritated look.



"I have been here for days."



"Then you should be accustomed to it."



She placed her account book on the table.



"Do not become pleased with yourself."



He returned to his work.



The western field remained unresolved.

In the imperial gardens, the Emperor and Yuru reached a small pavilion.



A servant had prepared tea.



There were no documents.



There were also no officials waiting behind the pillars.



Yuru sat down.



The Emperor looked at the tea.



"Do you remember when we first came here after our wedding?"



"Yes."



"You complained that the pavilion had too many steps."



"It did."



"You said the gardeners had designed it for people who never needed to carry anything."



"They had."



The Emperor laughed.



"That was a long time ago."



Yuru poured tea.



"It was."



He watched her for a moment.



"Do you regret resigning?"



"No."



"Not even a little?"



"I regret that it took so long."



The Emperor was silent.



Yuru continued.



"I should have insisted earlier that the palace's responsibilities be distributed properly. I should not have allowed so many ordinary matters to become dependent on my approval."



"That was not entirely your fault."



"No. But I participated in it."



He considered her answer.



"Then perhaps we both learned something."



Yuru raised her cup.



"Perhaps."



The Emperor looked towards the pond.



"Your father has offered you a residence."



"He has."



"Will you move there?"



"I have not decided."



"Would you prefer to remain here?"



"For the moment."



The Emperor nodded.



He did not ask again.



They finished their tea.



When they stood, the Emperor glanced towards the garden path.



"Would you like to walk further?"



"Yes."



They continued.



Fang Ru followed at a respectful distance.



She noticed that the Emperor had not once looked for a document.



It was, in her opinion, a considerable achievement.

At the eastern market, Lin Shuyun arrived with an update.



The buyer had accepted the proposed payment structure for the additional two-hundred-bolt order.



However, he still wanted the first delivery earlier.



The workshop manager had calculated the labour requirements.



Four additional workers would be necessary.



The dyeing schedule could be shortened by several days if the workshop operated an extra shift, but doing so would increase labour and fuel costs.



Lin placed the estimate before Mingzhao.



"These are the revised figures."



Mingzhao examined them.



"Are the four workers available?"



"Two are available immediately. The other two may be available after the tenth day."



"Have their wages been included?"



"Yes."



"Training?"



"Not fully."



"Why?"



"The manager assumes they will already know the work."



Mingzhao looked up.



"Have they worked with this particular dyeing process?"



"One has. The other three have not."



Lin folded her hands.



"That is why I have not accepted the schedule."



Mingzhao nodded.



"The buyer's deadline is not our production capacity."



"That is what I told him."



"Then we should not promise it."



Lin smiled.



"I thought you would say that."



Mingzhao turned to the estimate.



The proposed order might be profitable.



It might also place too much pressure on the workshop and jeopardize the existing contract.



She asked Lin to obtain a more realistic training estimate and a revised delivery schedule.



The buyer could wait or find another supplier.



Lin agreed.



Before leaving, she mentioned that the two corrected bolts from the existing order had been inspected by the buyer.



Both had been accepted.



The buyer had not yet paid for them.



Mingzhao entered the acceptance in the partnership record but did not mark the payment as received.



The existing contract had now advanced.



The money had not.

At the palace, Third Princess Minglan was waiting outside the library.



Yuru returned from the garden walk and stopped when she saw her.



"Have you finished the book catalogue?"



"Not entirely."



"Why?"



"Two books remain unidentified."



Yuru nodded.



"Have you marked them as unresolved?"



"Yes."



"Good."



Minglan hesitated.



"I thought I should ask you something."



"Go ahead."



"If an office cannot identify an object, should it ask another office to decide?"



"Only if that office has the relevant records or authority."



"And if neither office knows?"



"Then they should say so and investigate."



Minglan looked down at the notes she carried.



"I think I understand."



Yuru smiled.



"Do you?"



"Yes. I should not ask the Department of Rites to identify a book merely because it has an old ceremonial label."



"Exactly."



Minglan smiled.



Then she asked, "Would you look at the catalogue?"



Yuru considered it.



"I can look at it as your teacher. I will not approve it as an administrator."



"I know."



Minglan handed her the papers.



Yuru examined the entries.



She noticed that one book's title had been copied from a damaged page.



She pointed it out.



"Do you see this character?"



Minglan leaned closer.



"Yes."



"It may be part of the author's name rather than the title."



Minglan examined the original record.



"Then the catalogue entry may be arranged incorrectly."



"May be. You will need to compare the book itself."



Minglan nodded.



Yuru returned the papers.



The princess thanked her.



She did not ask Yuru to correct the catalogue.



That was progress.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received a message from Mr Liang.



The preliminary property meeting was confirmed for the tenth day.



Sun Qiming would attend.



Master Luo would provide the available property valuations.



Steward Zhou would bring the lease records and warehouse documents.



Shen Guowei read the list.



Then he called Mingyuan.



"Are the records ready?"



"Most are."



"What remains?"



"The western field's current arrangement is unclear. The southern building needs an inspection. The warehouse repair estimate is separate from the grain inventory."



Shen Guowei frowned.



"Can these matters be resolved before the meeting?"



"Not all of them."



"Then what will we discuss?"



"What is supported by the records."



The Marquis looked at his son.



Mingyuan continued.



"We can agree on some property classifications. We can identify which matters require further evidence. We should not pretend that the rest is settled."



Shen Guowei was quiet.



He had once expected his son to inherit the estate without needing to understand every detail.



Now he was discovering that inheritance came with a considerable amount of reading.



"Very well," he said.



Mingyuan bowed.



As he left, Shen Guowei glanced at the letter from Mingzhao.



She would attend the meeting.



She would bring her accountant.



She would discuss her claims.



She would not be responsible for making the estate's records complete.



He folded the letter.



The family had not yet reached a settlement.



But the meeting would at least begin with a clearer understanding of what remained unsettled.

That evening, Yuru returned to her temporary residence.



Fang Ru helped her remove the outer robe.



"Was the walk pleasant?"



"Yes."



"Did His Majesty discuss the palace?"



"Briefly."



"Did he bring documents?"



"No."



Fang Ru smiled.



"That is good."



Yuru sat beside the window.



After a while, she asked, "Have the personal garments been separated from the palace inventory?"



"Yes. The remaining pieces are being checked against the old transfer lists."



"Any disagreement?"



"Two robes have unclear records. One was made before Your Majesty entered the palace, but the fabric was purchased from the palace stores."



Yuru considered this.



"Then the garment may be personal, while the fabric cost belongs in the palace account."



Fang Ru nodded.



"That is what the inventory officer suggested."



"Let them record the distinction."



"Yes."



Yuru looked towards the garden.



She had once believed that a well-managed household required her to know every detail.



Now she was learning to let other people establish the details themselves.



It was not always easy.



But it was possible.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao prepared for the following day's work.



She reviewed the eight documents scheduled for the preliminary property meeting.



The western field's lease status remained uncertain.



The southern building required inspection.



The northern warehouse building had a separate valuation.



The grain inventory remained unresolved.



The northern canal property's boundary remained disputed.



The repair estimates were not final.



The personal advances required supporting evidence.



The silk partnership remained separate from the family estate.



She placed the documents in order.



Qiao Yue entered with tea.



"Will tomorrow's meeting settle anything?"



"Some matters may be classified."



"Will the property be divided?"



"Not tomorrow."



"Will the missing grain be found?"



"Not tomorrow."



"Will the northern canal house be purchased?"



"No."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"Then what will happen tomorrow?"



"We will discuss what can be established."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"That sounds very little."



Mingzhao closed the file.



"It is more than pretending."



Qiao Yue considered this.



Then she smiled.



"Should I prepare an extra pot of tea?"



"Yes."



"For the meeting?"



"For you. You will need it."



Qiao Yue laughed.

The ninth day of the fifth month ended without a dramatic announcement.



The Emperor had taken a walk without reviewing a single document.



The former Empress had advised the Third Princess without taking responsibility for the library.



Mingyuan had discovered that an expired lease could not be renewed by changing the wording in a register.



Mingxiu had discovered that the same field could be described in three different ways without becoming three separate fields.



Lin Shuyun had secured agreement on most of a proposed contract without accepting an unrealistic delivery schedule.



And Mingzhao had arranged eight documents for a meeting that would not settle everything.



Tomorrow, the Marquisate would discuss property.



The palace would continue its ordinary work.



Neither institution would collapse.



Neither would become perfectly efficient.



Both would have to learn to function without expecting one woman to correct every mistake.



And somewhere in the palace, a clerk had finally discovered that the same ceremonial screen did not need three different names.



He was very pleased with himself.



Unfortunately, he had not yet informed the other two offices.
