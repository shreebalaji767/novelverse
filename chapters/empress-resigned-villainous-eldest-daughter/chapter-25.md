# **Chapter 25: The Empress Has Seven Days Left. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Three Days Left. The Household Has Finally Asked Who Will Keep the Keys.**



**17th Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first sign that Shen Mingzhao was actually leaving was that Steward Zhou asked for the keys.



Not all the keys.



Only the keys to the rooms, storerooms, and offices that would no longer fall under her personal supervision.



Mingzhao looked at the wooden tray he had placed before her.



There were twenty-seven keys.



She counted them.



Then she counted them again.



"Why are there twenty-seven?"



Steward Zhou stood opposite her.



"These are the keys currently registered under your name."



"Currently?"



"Some were issued to you personally. Others were placed in your custody when you assumed responsibility for the estate."



Mingzhao examined the labels.



The eastern account room.



The household correspondence room.



The northern warehouse record cabinet.



The secondary residence account chest.



The commercial correspondence cabinet.



The old family archive room.



She picked up one key.



"This belongs to the northern warehouse."



"Yes."



"Why is it registered under my name?"



"Because you were responsible for the warehouse accounts."



"Then it should be transferred to the warehouse steward."



Steward Zhou nodded.



Mingzhao examined another.



"This is the key to the household correspondence room."



"That room contains your old correspondence and several family records."



"Then separate the contents before transferring the key."



"Yes, Miss."



She continued.



The keys were not difficult to return.



The difficulty lay in determining what each room contained, who had authority over its contents, and which records were still needed for the property settlement.



She placed the keys into three groups.



**Personal custody.**



**Estate custody.**



**Unresolved records.**



Steward Zhou watched her work.



After a while, he asked, "Miss, should I prepare a separate list of every key transferred?"



Mingzhao looked at him.



"Yes."



He nodded.



"And the person receiving each key should sign."



"Yes."



He bowed.



Then he looked at the remaining pile.



"Should the receiving person also sign that the room's contents have been checked?"



Mingzhao stopped.



She looked at the keys.



Then at Steward Zhou.



"Yes."



He nodded with the satisfaction of a man who had discovered a necessary administrative safeguard.



Mingzhao returned to the inventory.



She had created a system that required signatures for every transfer.



It was a sensible system.



She was beginning to suspect that the system would require more paper than the rooms contained.



At Chengming Palace, the Empress was facing a similar problem.



Her official seals had been prepared for inspection.



The Imperial Archives had sent an official to examine them.



The Department of Rites had sent a representative to confirm the ceremonial arrangements.



The Imperial Household Administration had sent Madam Zhao to witness the transfer.



Gao Shun stood nearby.



Yuru looked at the officials.



"Are we ready?"



The Imperial Archives official bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He opened the first case.



The seal was examined.



Its inscription was checked.



Its condition was recorded.



The second seal was examined.



The third followed.



The official compared each one with the existing register.



Then he placed the seals back into their cases.



"All three are accounted for, Your Majesty."



Yuru nodded.



"Good."



The Department of Rites representative bowed.



"Your Majesty, should the cases be sealed now?"



"Yes."



The official prepared the sealing material.



Then he paused.



"Should the seals be counted again after the cases are sealed?"



Yuru looked at him.



The room became quiet.



Gao Shun lowered his eyes.



Madam Zhao appeared to be studying the floor.



Yuru considered the question.



"Do you intend to open the cases again?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Then why would you count the seals again?"



The official hesitated.



"To confirm the inventory."



"The inventory has already been confirmed."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then seal the cases."



The official bowed.



The cases were sealed.



The Imperial Archives official signed the inventory.



The Department of Rites representative signed the acknowledgment.



Madam Zhao signed as witness.



Gao Shun signed the transfer record.



Yuru watched the final document being completed.



Three seals.



Four officials.



Five signatures.



One ceremony still to come.



She leaned back.



"Is there anything else?"



The officials exchanged glances.



The Imperial Archives official bowed.



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



For once, the answer was final.



The Emperor received the completed inventory before noon.



He examined the signatures.



"All three seals have been verified?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Where are they now?"



"Under the custody of the Imperial Archives."



"Good."



Gao Shun stood beside the desk.



The Emperor placed the inventory aside.



"How are the remaining duties progressing?"



"Madam Zhao reports that the administrative transfers are proceeding according to schedule."



"Any difficulties?"



"Several offices have requested clarification about their new reporting arrangements."



"Have they been answered?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then why are they still listed as unresolved?"



Gao Shun checked his notes.



"The offices have acknowledged the instructions, but their first monthly reports have not yet been submitted."



The Emperor nodded.



"That is a pending verification, not an uncompleted transfer."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He considered the matter.



"Make certain the report distinguishes between responsibilities already transferred and responsibilities awaiting their first review."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor had become increasingly particular about the difference between a decision being made and a decision being carried out.



He had discovered that a palace could produce an impressive number of documents without necessarily completing the work described in them.



He did not intend to repeat the mistake.



At the Marquisate, the key inventory was taking longer than expected.



The household correspondence room contained letters, account notes, old contracts, and several documents that had been placed there because nobody had known where else to keep them.



Mingzhao examined a bundle.



"This is a family land contract."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"It was placed here when the old archive cabinet was repaired."



"Then it belongs in the family archive."



"Yes."



She picked up another document.



"This is a personal letter from Lin Shuyun."



"Should it remain?"



"No. It is part of my commercial correspondence."



Steward Zhou made a note.



Mingzhao continued.



The next bundle contained receipts from merchants.



Some concerned the estate.



Others concerned her personal business.



Several had been filed together because the payments had been made during the same month.



She separated them.



"These belong with the estate accounts."



"Yes."



"These belong with my commercial records."



"Yes."



"And these?"



Steward Zhou examined the documents.



"Those concern the silk partnership."



"Then send copies to Sun Qiming and retain the originals with the partnership records."



Steward Zhou nodded.



Mingzhao looked at the growing piles.



"Who arranged this filing system?"



Steward Zhou hesitated.



"Several people, Miss."



"That is not an answer."



"It was a gradual arrangement."



Mingzhao stared at him.



"Gradual?"



"Over several years."



She looked at the documents again.



The filing system had apparently been created by everyone who had needed to put something away and had not wanted to decide where it belonged.



She closed the bundle.



"Prepare a proper index before the records are transferred."



Steward Zhou bowed.



He did not ask whether the index required a separate index.



He had learned.



In the family hall, Shen Guowei was discussing the departure arrangements with Madam Liang.



"The twentieth day is approaching," he said.



Madam Liang looked at him.



"I know."



"She has arranged temporary accommodation."



"Where?"



"She has not decided."



Madam Liang frowned.



"How can she leave without a proper residence?"



"She says she will rent a place temporarily."



"That is unnecessary. She can remain here until the northern house is repaired."



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"The northern house has not been purchased."



"Then she can remain here."



"She has already agreed to leave."



Madam Liang's expression tightened.



"Must everything happen according to her wishes?"



Shen Guowei did not answer immediately.



For years, the family had treated Mingzhao's work as an ordinary part of household life.



Her absence had seemed inconvenient.



Her departure had seemed negotiable.



Now the date was approaching, and the family was discovering that she had not been asking permission to want a different life.



She had been arranging one.



Madam Liang looked away.



"What about the servants?"



"Those who wish to accompany her may do so if the arrangements are approved."



"She will take people from this household?"



"Only those who volunteer."



Madam Liang frowned.



"Then the household will have fewer experienced servants."



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"That is why we are preparing the handover."



Madam Liang was silent.



She did not like the situation.



But she could no longer pretend that the household would remain unchanged.



Mingyuan was in the account room when Steward Zhou brought him the updated key inventory.



He examined the list.



"Why are there so many?"



Steward Zhou replied, "Because the keys have been held by several people over the years."



Mingyuan frowned.



"Then who is responsible for them now?"



"That is what we are establishing."



Mingyuan examined the entries.



The northern warehouse key would return to the warehouse steward.



The household correspondence room would remain under the central clerk.



The commercial correspondence cabinet would be transferred according to the property settlement.



The old family archive would remain under the Marquis's authority.



Mingyuan pointed to the account room.



"Why is this key still assigned to Second Sister?"



"Because the current inventory has not been checked."



Mingyuan looked at the record.



"Then I will check it."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"Very well."



Mingyuan hesitated.



"Should I ask her to explain the filing arrangement?"



Steward Zhou considered this.



"She can answer questions about the existing records during the agreed handover period. But you should first examine the inventory yourself."



Mingyuan looked at him.



"She will not be here after the twentieth."



"Correct."



Mingyuan nodded.



He had begun to understand the meaning of the date.



After the twentieth, his sister would not be available whenever he encountered a problem.



He would have to read the records.



He would have to ask the relevant people.



He would have to make decisions.



And if he made mistakes, he would have to correct them.



He returned to the account book.



For once, he did not send a messenger to Mingzhao.



At midday, Mingzhao received a report from Lin Shuyun.



The first group of silk had been delivered.



The buyer had accepted the goods.



The delivery record had been signed.



The outstanding payment was transferred to the partnership account.



Mingzhao checked the figures.



The payment matched the contract.



The carrier's charges were also recorded.



She compared the amount with the partnership's outstanding obligations.



After deducting the agreed costs and the amount reserved for the finishing of the remaining stock, the partnership had a positive balance.



She did not immediately calculate her personal profit.



The partnership was still responsible for completing the remaining delivery.



She wrote to Lin Shuyun, confirming receipt of the report and asking for the final inspection date.



Qiao Yue looked pleased.



"Miss, the first delivery is complete."



"Yes."



"And the payment has arrived."



"Yes."



"That is excellent."



"It is."



Qiao Yue waited.



Mingzhao continued writing.



"Is there something else?"



Qiao Yue smiled.



"No, Miss."



Mingzhao sealed the letter.



"Then stop waiting for me to celebrate."



Qiao Yue laughed.



The business was progressing.



Mingzhao was pleased.



She simply did not intend to confuse one successful transaction with financial independence already achieved.



That afternoon, the property assessor returned with a revised valuation schedule.



Master Luo had completed the inspection of the agricultural holdings that did not have disputed boundaries.



The report included:



- Confirmed agricultural land.

- Leased land with existing agreements.

- Fallow land requiring restoration.

- Buildings requiring maintenance.

- Properties awaiting boundary verification.



Mingzhao examined the schedule.



"Has the neighbouring owner been notified about the boundary survey?"



"Yes. Their representative will attend tomorrow."



"Then that parcel should remain provisional."



Master Luo nodded.



Shen Guowei examined the valuation.



"The figures are lower than I expected."



"Some properties require expenditure before they can be used productively," Master Luo explained.



"That expenditure will improve their value."



"Possibly. But the current valuation should reflect their present condition. Future improvements should not be treated as though they have already been completed."



Mingzhao nodded.



"That is correct."



Shen Guowei looked at her.



"You agree with every reduction?"



"I agree with the method."



"Even if it reduces the estate's value?"



"If the property is worth less in its current condition, then the valuation should show that."



He looked dissatisfied.



Mr. Liang examined the report.



"The family should not argue with the assessor simply because the figures are inconvenient."



Shen Guowei did not reply.



The valuation would continue.



The property division would take time.



But the family could no longer pretend that its assets had a value determined by pride.



At Chengming Palace, the Empress Dowager requested another meeting.



Yuru arrived in the Dowager's residence shortly before evening.



The Dowager looked at her.



"The seals have been transferred."



"Yes."



"The ceremony is in six days."



"Yes."



The Dowager studied her face.



"You seem calm."



"I have known the date for several days."



"That is not what I meant."



Yuru waited.



The Dowager looked toward the courtyard.



"When you leave, the palace will be different."



"It will."



"Some people will not know where to turn."



"Then they should learn where to turn."



The Dowager looked at her sharply.



"You speak as though it is simple."



"It is not simple."



"Then why do you insist on leaving?"



Yuru considered the question.



"Because difficulty is not a reason to continue doing something forever."



The Dowager was silent.



Yuru continued.



"I have served as Empress for many years. I have carried responsibilities that were mine and others that gradually became mine because I was willing to handle them. I do not regret every decision. But I cannot spend the rest of my life proving that I can continue."



The Dowager looked at her.



For a moment, she seemed ready to argue.



Then she looked away.



"Your father-in-law would have called that an impractical answer."



Yuru smiled faintly.



"Then I am fortunate he is not here to ask me."



The Dowager gave a short, unwilling laugh.



She looked at the report before her.



"Your ceremony should be conducted properly."



"It will."



"Do not allow the officials to turn it into a disorderly affair."



"I will not."



The Dowager nodded.



The conversation ended without affection or hostility.



It was not reconciliation.



It was an acknowledgment that the decision had been made.



That evening, the Emperor visited Yuru.



She was in her private study, examining a small collection of personal books.



He looked at the shelves.



"Are these the books you intend to take?"



"Some of them."



"Only some?"



"The rest belong to the palace library."



He picked up one volume.



"This one?"



"Mine."



He examined the title.



"You have read it many times."



"Yes."



"Why take it if you already know it?"



"Because I may wish to read it again."



He returned it to the table.



"That is reasonable."



Yuru looked at him.



"Have you come to discuss something?"



"No."



"Then why are you standing beside my books?"



He smiled.



"Must I have a reason?"



"Usually."



He sat opposite her.



For a while, neither spoke.



Then he said, "The Crown Prince has completed his latest account review."



"I know."



"He did not ask you to examine it."



"I know."



"He sent it directly to the designated office."



"Good."



The Emperor looked at her.



"He is learning."



"Yes."



"So is the palace."



Yuru smiled.



"Slowly."



"Very slowly."



"That is still progress."



The Emperor looked at her for a moment.



"Do you regret submitting the petition?"



Yuru thought carefully.



"No."



"Not even now?"



"No."



He nodded.



He did not ask again.



At the Marquisate, the departure preparations had become a matter of practical arrangements.



Qiao Yue had prepared a list of the servants who wished to accompany Mingzhao.



There were four.



Two were attendants.



One was responsible for personal correspondence.



The fourth was a senior maid who had worked in Mingzhao's rooms for many years.



Mingzhao examined the list.



"Have they been informed of the temporary accommodation?"



"Yes."



"Have they been told their wages may be different?"



"Yes."



"Have they agreed?"



"All four."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then prepare their transfer arrangements."



Qiao Yue looked at the list.



"Only four?"



"That is enough for a small household."



"What about the other servants?"



"They remain here."



Qiao Yue nodded.



Mingzhao looked at the budget.



The cost was manageable, though not generous.



She had reduced unnecessary expenses.



She had not reduced the wages below what was fair.



Her new household would be small.



It would also be hers.



She examined the final line.



Temporary accommodation.



The arrangements were not yet confirmed.



She called for Qiao Yue.



"Contact the lodging house near the eastern market. Ask whether it has rooms available for a short stay."



Qiao Yue looked uncertain.



"An ordinary lodging house?"



"Yes."



"Miss, you have never stayed in one."



"Then I will learn."



Qiao Yue opened her mouth.



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Do not tell me it is unsuitable before you have inspected it."



Qiao Yue closed her mouth.



"Yes, Miss."



Before retiring, Mingzhao reviewed the next day's schedule.



The boundary survey would take place.



The transport contractor's two former drivers would be asked to provide statements.



The remaining silk stock would undergo inspection.



The key inventory would be finalized.



The property assessor would complete the next stage of valuation.



There were also several family documents requiring her signature.



She looked at the list.



Three days remained until departure.



She crossed out the completed tasks.



The list became shorter.



Not empty.



But shorter.



She put down her brush.



Qiao Yue brought her tea.



"Miss, the lodging house has rooms available."



"Have they been inspected?"



"Not yet. I will go tomorrow."



"Good."



Qiao Yue hesitated.



"Miss, are you nervous?"



Mingzhao looked at her.



"About what?"



"Leaving."



Mingzhao considered the question.



"Yes."



Qiao Yue seemed surprised.



Mingzhao continued.



"I know how to manage the Marquisate. I know its people, its accounts, its properties, and its routines. I do not yet know how to manage a household that belongs entirely to me."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Then we will learn."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Yes."



She returned to the schedule.



The next three days would be busy.



But after the twentieth, the work would be different.



She would no longer be responsible for making the Marquisate function.



She would be responsible for making her own household function.



That was a smaller responsibility.



It was also a completely new one.



At the palace, the final report of the day arrived.



The administrative transfers were progressing.



The seal inventory was complete.



The ceremonial schedule was confirmed.



The remaining duties had been assigned to the proper offices or identified as matters requiring the Emperor's decision.



The Emperor read the report.



Then he looked at Gao Shun.



"How many days?"



"Six days, Your Majesty."



The Emperor nodded.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao closed her account book.



Three days.



One woman was preparing to leave a palace.



The other was preparing to leave a family estate.



Both had discovered that the final preparations were not about the grand decisions.



They were about keys, documents, servants, rooms, and the people who would inherit the ordinary work.



The Empress would leave behind three official seals.



The Marquis's eldest daughter would leave behind twenty-seven keys.



Neither would take the institution with her.



Unfortunately, the Marquisate had not yet found anyone willing to take responsibility for the old correspondence room.



Steward Zhou had suggested the central clerk.



The central clerk had suggested the archive keeper.



The archive keeper had suggested Steward Zhou.



Mingzhao read their written responses.



Then she wrote one sentence at the bottom.



**The person responsible for the archive shall receive the archive room key.**



She signed it.



The matter was settled.



The room would finally have an owner.



**End of Chapter 25.**
