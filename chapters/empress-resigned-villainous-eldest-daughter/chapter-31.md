# **Chapter 31: The Empress Has Finally Resigned. The Emperor Has Lost His Most Experienced Administrator. The Palace Has Prepared a Grand Ceremony and Forgotten to Decide Who Will Carry the Last Box.**



**23rd Day of the Fourth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The palace woke before sunrise.



This was not unusual.



The palace often woke before sunrise.



However, on this particular morning, the servants of Chengming Palace had begun preparing for a ceremony that would formally end an arrangement which had lasted for many years.



The corridors had been swept. The ceremonial carpets had been inspected. The invited officials had received their instructions. The members of the imperial family had been informed of their places.



The Department of Rites had checked the schedule twice.



The Imperial Household Administration had checked it three times.



The Palace Stores had checked the ceremonial objects.



And the ceremonial screens, after yesterday's administrative ordeal, were still in their designated positions.



Everyone considered this a promising beginning.



At Chengming Palace, Xu Yuru sat before her dressing table.



Her attendants dressed her in the formal robes required for the occasion. The garments were magnificent, heavy with embroidery and carefully arranged ornaments.



Fang Ru checked the final details.



"Your Majesty, the ceremonial carriage is ready."



Yuru nodded.



"The invited relatives?"



"All have arrived."



"The Crown Prince?"



"Already waiting."



"The Second Prince and Third Princess?"



"Both have taken their places."



Yuru looked at the mirror.



For years, she had worn these garments for ceremonies marking births, marriages, appointments, mourning periods, seasonal observances, and imperial celebrations.



Today, she would wear them for the last time as Empress.



She did not feel particularly sad.



She did not feel particularly relieved.



She felt that the collar was slightly too tight.



"Fang Ru."



"Yes, Your Majesty?"



"Has the collar always been this uncomfortable?"



Fang Ru looked alarmed.



"Would Your Majesty like it adjusted?"



"No. It is too late to alter the ceremonial garment."



"I will ask the seamstress to check it."



"Do not trouble her. I can endure it for a few hours."



Fang Ru lowered her head.



Yuru rose.



"Let us go."



The ceremony took place in the Hall of Supreme Harmony.



The emperor was already seated.



The Crown Prince stood in his designated position. The Second Prince and Third Princess stood behind him, followed by the imperial relatives and senior officials invited to witness the occasion.



The Empress Dowager Wei occupied her customary seat.



The hall was quiet.



When Xu Yuru entered, the officials bowed.



She walked forward at a measured pace.



The ceremony proceeded according to the approved schedule.



The Department of Rites announced the formal order.



The emperor confirmed the acceptance of the resignation previously submitted by the Empress and the completion of the administrative transfer.



The Empress's official responsibilities had been reassigned under the imperial order.



The records had been transferred.



The seals had already been placed in the custody of the Imperial Archives.



The ceremonial declaration was read aloud.



Xu Yuru listened without interruption.



There was no mention of the ninety-four responsibilities that had occupied so many officials during the previous month.



There was no mention of the winter blankets, the lamp oil, the ceremonial screens, the kitchen inventories, or the palace brooms.



That was appropriate.



A formal ceremony was not the place to discuss the location of household objects.



When the declaration ended, the emperor rose.



He looked at Yuru.



For a moment, he seemed to have prepared something to say.



Then he spoke simply.



"Xu Yuru has served the imperial household with diligence for many years. Her resignation has been accepted, and the responsibilities formerly administered through her office have been transferred according to the established arrangements."



The emperor paused.



"Her service is acknowledged."



The officials bowed again.



Yuru returned the formal salute.



There was no dramatic exchange.



No one wept.



No official collapsed.



The ceremony ended.



The Empress had resigned.



The palace remained standing.



This was considered a success.



The first person to approach Yuru afterward was the Empress Dowager.



She held Yuru's hand.



"You have completed your duty."



Yuru bowed slightly.



"I have completed the formal arrangements."



The older woman looked at her.



"You always have to distinguish between the two."



Yuru smiled.



"Because they are not always the same."



The Empress Dowager sighed.



"Go and rest."



"I will."



"And do not spend your first day outside this office reviewing the palace accounts."



"I had no intention of doing so."



The Empress Dowager narrowed her eyes.



Yuru added, "I might read a book."



"That is acceptable."



The Empress Dowager released her hand.



The Crown Prince approached next.



"Mother."



Yuru looked at him.



"Cheng'an."



He bowed.



"Congratulations."



Yuru smiled faintly.



"Thank you."



He seemed uncertain what to say next.



At last, he asked, "Will you still be available if I need advice?"



"Of course."



"Even after you leave the palace?"



"Advice is not an administrative appointment."



The Crown Prince nodded.



"I understand."



"Do you?"



He considered the question.



"I will try."



"That is sufficient."



The Second Prince approached with a less formal expression.



"Mother, the ceremonial arrangements were completed without any errors."



Yuru looked at him.



"That is good."



"The Department of Rites handled the schedule."



"I know."



"The Imperial Household Administration handled the objects."



"I know."



"The Palace Stores received the screens."



Yuru paused.



"That is particularly good."



The Second Prince smiled.



"Father says the screens caused considerable trouble."



"Then perhaps you should avoid becoming responsible for them."



The Second Prince laughed.



The Third Princess approached last.



She bowed, then looked up.



"Mother, will you still teach me accounts?"



"If you wish to learn."



"I do."



"Then bring your books when you visit."



Minglan smiled.



Yuru touched her shoulder.



"Remember, you must learn to understand the figures yourself. Do not merely memorize the questions I ask."



"I will."



The four of them stood together for a moment.



Then the emperor approached.



"Everything is complete," he said.



Yuru looked at him.



"Yes."



"Your residence has been prepared."



"I have heard."



"You may move there whenever you wish."



"I will go today."



Zhao Jingheng nodded.



He looked as though he wanted to say more.



Yuru waited.



At last, he said, "You may return to visit."



"I know."



"And you may ask for anything you require."



"I know."



He paused.



"Then I suppose there is nothing else."



Yuru looked at him.



"There is always something else. You simply have to decide whether it is necessary."



The emperor laughed quietly.



For the first time that morning, he seemed less like the ruler of Great Yong and more like a man whose household arrangements had just changed.



The temporary residence prepared for Yuru was located within the palace's western residential section.



It was not a grand new palace.



It was a comfortable residence previously used by senior imperial relatives during extended visits.



It contained a private courtyard, several rooms, a study, and a small garden.



The furnishings were already in place.



The servants assigned to assist her had received instructions not to route ordinary palace administrative matters to the residence.



That instruction had been repeated three times.



Yuru suspected it would be tested before the week ended.



Fang Ru accompanied her.



The senior attendant carried the personal inventory.



Two servants followed with the boxes.



A third servant carried the ceremonial garments that would be stored separately.



At the entrance, a clerk approached with a final document.



"Your Majesty, the transfer of personal belongings requires one additional acknowledgment."



Yuru looked at the paper.



"What is it?"



"The last storage box."



She examined the record.



It contained a list of personal books, correspondence, clothing, ornaments, and several household objects.



The final line read:



*One wooden storage box, contents recorded separately.*



Yuru looked up.



"Who is responsible for carrying it?"



The clerk hesitated.



"The Palace Stores believes it belongs to Chengming Palace."



"And Chengming Palace?"



"They believe it should be transferred with Your Majesty's personal belongings."



Yuru closed the document.



"Where is the box?"



"In Chengming Palace."



"Then ask the assigned attendants to bring it."



The clerk bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



Yuru turned toward Fang Ru.



"Do you see?"



Fang Ru looked worried.



"Your Majesty?"



"The palace has already begun testing whether I am still available to resolve matters."



Fang Ru lowered her head to hide a smile.



At the Qingyuan Marquisate, the ceremony was not discussed.



The family had other matters.



A messenger from Sun Qiming had arrived with the latest accounting summary.



Shen Guowei read it in his study.



Madam Liang sat opposite him.



Shen Mingyuan stood beside the desk.



Shen Mingxiu had also been invited.



The report was divided into several sections.



The first concerned Mingzhao's personal claims.



The second concerned the family's divisible property.



The third concerned unresolved liabilities.



The fourth concerned the northern warehouse discrepancy.



The fifth concerned the agricultural boundary dispute.



Shen Guowei looked at the summary.



"How much has been confirmed?"



Mingyuan answered.



"The study repair expense of seventy-two taels is supported by the original receipt and authorization. It is estate maintenance, not a personal debt."



"And the remaining two hundred and twenty-eight?"



"Still unverified."



Madam Liang frowned.



"Why has this matter taken so long?"



Mingyuan looked at her.



"Because the records are incomplete."



"Surely someone remembers what the money was spent on."



"Someone may remember. That does not make the expenditure verified."



Madam Liang's expression tightened.



Shen Guowei looked at the next page.



"The grain?"



Mingyuan answered.



"The original discrepancy remains forty-seven sacks. Twenty were covered by the movement instruction. Eighteen were confirmed in the southern warehouse. Two remain unconfirmed. Twenty-seven are unexplained."



"Has the new receiving note helped?"



"It establishes that three carts stopped at a point along the southern road. It does not establish the number of sacks or their final destination."



Shen Guowei rubbed his forehead.



"So we still do not know where the grain went."



"Yes."



Madam Liang looked at her son.



"Could we not simply settle the estate first and investigate the rest later?"



Mingyuan shook his head.



"The liabilities affect the valuation. We cannot divide property accurately while pretending the unresolved accounts do not exist."



Madam Liang turned toward her husband.



"Your daughter has made this unnecessarily complicated."



Shen Guowei did not immediately respond.



He looked at the papers.



Then he said, "The records were complicated before she requested the division."



The room fell quiet.



Mingxiu lowered her eyes.



She had been listening carefully.



For years, she had regarded Mingzhao's demands for documentation as an unpleasant habit.



Now the same documentation was being used to establish what the family owned, what it owed, and what remained uncertain.



It was no longer possible to dismiss the work as unnecessary.



She looked at her brother.



"Have you reviewed the estate's regular expenditure?"



Mingyuan nodded.



"I have begun."



"Then perhaps we should divide the work."



Mingyuan looked surprised.



"What do you mean?"



"You are reviewing the accounts. I can review the household purchases and compare the regular orders with the actual quantities received."



Madam Liang looked at her.



"Why should you be involved?"



Mingxiu remained composed.



"Because the household still needs to function. If we expect the accounts to be accurate, someone must examine the purchases."



Shen Guowei regarded his younger daughter.



"Can you do it?"



"I can try."



"Then discuss the arrangement with Steward Zhou."



Mingxiu nodded.



It was not a dramatic transformation.



She had not suddenly become an expert.



But she had recognized that the family could not continue to operate by waiting for Mingzhao to correct every mistake.



That realization was uncomfortable.



It was also useful.



At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao was inspecting silk.



The forty-two bolts were arranged in the merchant's warehouse.



Zhou Wenqi examined the first group with two assistants.



He checked the colour, weave, length, and finish.



Lin Shuyun stood beside him.



Mingzhao reviewed the inspection list.



The first ten bolts passed.



The next twelve passed.



The following eight required additional examination because the dye was slightly uneven near the edges.



Zhou Wenqi inspected them again.



"These can be accepted at the agreed reduced rate."



Lin Shuyun considered the proposal.



"The difference is small, but it should be recorded."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Record the original rate, the adjusted rate, and the reason for the adjustment."



Zhou Wenqi looked at her.



"That is acceptable."



The remaining twelve bolts were examined.



All passed.



The two bolts returned for correction were not included.



At the end of the inspection, thirty-two bolts had been accepted at the agreed price, while eight were accepted with a minor adjustment.



The remaining two had not yet passed inspection.



Mingzhao reviewed the figures.



"Payment should follow the contract."



Zhou Wenqi nodded.



"The payment will be transferred after the delivery record is signed."



Lin Shuyun handed him the delivery documents.



The merchant signed.



Mingzhao checked the signature against the contract.



Only then did she close the file.



Qiao Yue, who had been waiting near the warehouse entrance, approached.



"Miss, the inspection is finished."



"Yes."



"Does that mean we have earned money?"



"It means a delivery has been accepted."



"Is that not the same thing?"



"No."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"I knew there would be another distinction."



Mingzhao handed her the documents.



"Bring these to the partnership office."



Qiao Yue accepted them.



"Yes, Miss."



Lin Shuyun watched her leave.



"You are teaching her to become an accountant."



"I am teaching her to carry documents without losing them."



"That is how it begins."



Mingzhao ignored the remark.



She examined the remaining silk report.



There were still one hundred and thirty bolts from the original group that had not yet been inspected or delivered.



The two corrected bolts remained in finishing.



The partnership had made progress.



It had not finished.



By late afternoon, Yuru's final personal belongings had been transferred.



The wooden storage box had arrived.



It had taken three clerks, two attendants, and one supervisor to establish that the box contained only books and correspondence.



Yuru examined the contents.



Everything was present.



She signed the acknowledgment.



The clerk bowed with visible relief.



"Your Majesty, the transfer is complete."



Yuru looked at him.



"Thank you."



He withdrew.



Fang Ru stood beside the table.



"Your Majesty, would you like tea?"



"Yes."



The tea was prepared.



Yuru sat in the study.



For the first time in many years, no administrative report had been placed on the table before her.



There was no request for a signature.



No urgent dispute.



No question about the number of blankets in storage.



No disagreement about ceremonial screens.



There was only tea.



Yuru took a sip.



It was slightly too hot.



She set the cup down.



Fang Ru waited.



"Your Majesty?"



Yuru looked at her.



"Yes?"



"Is there anything else you require?"



Yuru considered the question.



"At present, no."



Fang Ru nodded.



She did not leave immediately.



Yuru noticed.



"You may sit if you wish."



Fang Ru looked startled.



"Here?"



"Yes."



The attendant hesitated before taking the chair opposite her.



For a while, neither spoke.



Then Fang Ru said, "It is strange."



"What is?"



"Not receiving instructions."



Yuru smiled.



"You will become accustomed to it."



"I hope so."



"So do I."



That evening, Emperor Zhao Jingheng visited the new residence.



He found Yuru reading.



The book was a collection of historical accounts that she had set aside several months earlier.



He glanced at the table.



"Are you not reviewing tomorrow's documents?"



Yuru looked up.



"Which documents?"



"The palace reports."



"They should be sent to the responsible offices."



The emperor nodded.



Then he looked at the book.



"Is it interesting?"



"So far."



"What is it about?"



"A minister who spent ten years trying to persuade the court to repair a bridge."



The emperor sat down.



"Did they repair it?"



"Eventually."



"How?"



"The minister stopped writing petitions and presented the court with the cost of rebuilding the road after the bridge collapsed."



Zhao Jingheng laughed.



"An effective method."



"An expensive one."



He glanced toward the window.



"The palace has managed today."



"I am glad."



"The Department of Rites completed the ceremonial records. The Imperial Household Administration accepted the transfer. The Palace Stores received the remaining objects."



"Good."



"The screens are in their proper place."



Yuru looked at him.



"Excellent."



The emperor laughed again.



Then his expression became more serious.



"Are you comfortable here?"



"Yes."



"Do you need anything?"



"Not at present."



He nodded.



A silence settled between them.



After a while, he said, "I have not decided what arrangement should follow your departure."



Yuru closed the book.



"Then take your time."



"I do not intend to appoint a new Empress immediately."



"That is your decision."



"I will not create a new office to supervise the inner household."



"That is also your decision."



"And I intend to review the remaining responsibilities after the next month."



Yuru smiled.



"That is sensible."



Zhao Jingheng looked at her.



"You are not going to tell me what to do?"



"You have just described a reasonable plan."



He looked almost disappointed.



Yuru raised an eyebrow.



"Would you prefer that I find a problem?"



"No."



"Then you should be careful what you ask for."



The emperor laughed.



He stayed for tea.



For once, the conversation did not turn into a meeting.



At the Marquisate, Mingzhao received the first payment confirmation from the silk partnership.



She entered it into the partnership ledger.



The amount was recorded as partnership revenue.



The agreed costs and outstanding obligations remained separate.



She did not transfer the money into her personal account.



Qiao Yue watched her.



"Miss, the payment has arrived."



"Yes."



"Then the business is profitable?"



"Not yet established."



Qiao Yue looked at the ledger.



"How many more documents must be written before you can say that?"



"Enough to establish the costs."



Qiao Yue sighed.



Mingzhao continued writing.



After a moment, she stopped.



She looked around the room.



The furniture was still sparse.



The household was still adjusting.



The permanent residence question remained unanswered.



The Marquisate's property division was still incomplete.



The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.



The boundary dispute had not been settled.



Her independent life had not become easy simply because she had left the estate.



But there was something different.



No one had entered the room to tell her that she must review the household accounts because her brother had other priorities.



No one had asked her to prepare a report for a family meeting without giving her authority to make the decisions.



No one had treated her personal time as an unlimited household resource.



The room was modest.



The rent was due.



The kitchen still needed improvements.



And tomorrow there would be more work.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



"Qiao Yue."



"Yes, Miss?"



"Tomorrow, we will review the rental agreement and the household provisions."



Qiao Yue stared at her.



"Tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"Miss, today was a long day."



"Tomorrow is another day."



Qiao Yue looked toward the ceiling.



She had followed Mingzhao out of the Marquisate.



She had not realized that freedom would include so many ledgers.



On the same night, the palace received a routine report concerning the location of the ceremonial screens.



The report stated that all screens had been received, counted, and placed in the designated warehouse.



It had been signed by six officials.



The emperor read it.



He put it down.



Then he called Gao Shun.



"Why has this report been sent to me?"



Gao Shun examined it.



"Your Majesty, it is a completion report."



"Does it require my approval?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Does it require my instruction?"



"No."



"Then why is it here?"



Gao Shun hesitated.



"The clerk may have believed Your Majesty should be informed."



The emperor looked at him.



"Inform me when something requires my attention."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Do not send me a report merely because six people have successfully moved a screen."



Gao Shun bowed.



He withdrew with the document.



The emperor looked at the empty space where the report had been.



Then he smiled.



The palace was learning.



Slowly.



Expensively.



And with an extraordinary number of signatures.



**The Empress had resigned.**



**The Marquis's eldest daughter had left.**



And in two different households, the people who remained were discovering that responsibility could not be transferred simply by placing a document on someone else's desk.
