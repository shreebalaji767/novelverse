# **Chapter 83: The Former Empress Has Been Offered a Book and Has Not Yet Been Asked to Explain Its Contents. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Dispatched the First Consignment and Is Waiting for the Second. The Marquisate's Clerks Have Discovered That a New Procedure Does Not Make Old Habits Disappear Overnight.**

**17th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress had received a second letter from the Xu residence.



Her father had added a short note to her mother's reply.



He had apparently reconsidered the book he intended to lend her.



The first selection concerned household estates and the duties of stewards. The second was a collection of essays about provincial storehouses and the movement of grain.



Her father had now placed both books aside.



Yuru read the note and raised an eyebrow.



Fang Ru, who was arranging the writing materials, noticed.



“Has he selected another book?”



“Two.”



“Perhaps he cannot decide which one you would prefer.”



“Perhaps he has decided that I ought to read both.”



Fang Ru smiled.



“Would that be troublesome?”



“No. It is simply a considerable amount of reading.”



“Will you borrow them?”



“I will.”



Yuru folded the letter and placed it beside the wooden case containing the first book.



She intended to return the earlier volume and borrow the two new ones during her next visit. She would not arrange a formal meeting merely to collect them.



Her father could wait.



He had waited years to lend her books.



He could wait a few more days.



Yuru spent the morning reading an essay about provincial storehouses.



It discussed the difficulty of maintaining accurate grain records when different offices used different units of measurement.



She read a passage twice.



Then she smiled faintly.



Apparently, the problem of people recording the same thing in different ways had existed long before the present generation of clerks.



She turned the page.

At the eastern market residence, Mingzhao received a message from the carrier.



The first consignment of twenty-eight bolts had reached the designated transfer point without damage.



The carrier had provided a signed receipt.



The goods had not yet reached the buyer's final warehouse.



Mingzhao examined the document.



“Has the buyer received the delivery notice?”



“Yes,” Qiao Yue replied. “The merchant's office confirmed receipt of the notice.”



“Then file the carrier's receipt separately from the final delivery confirmation.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



“Twenty-eight bolts have arrived at the transfer point.”



“Correct.”



“Should we record them as delivered?”



“No. We should record them as received by the carrier at the transfer point.”



Qiao Yue sighed.



“Another distinction.”



“A useful one.”



Mingzhao placed the receipt beside the contract.



The first consignment was progressing as planned.



The second consignment, containing one hundred and seventy-two bolts, was being prepared for dispatch.



The workshop had completed its production obligations. The remaining work involved packing, transport, and the final settlement of the buyer's payment.



Mingzhao had no reason to interfere with the workshop's ordinary packing arrangements, but she wanted the dispatch records checked before the carts left.



She instructed Qiao Yue to obtain the final packing list and compare it with the accepted quantity.



“Do not let the clerk use the total accepted quantity as the total dispatched quantity.”



“I will remind him.”



“And make sure the two consignments have separate records.”



“Yes.”



Mingzhao returned to the accounts.



She was beginning to understand that a successful business required a great deal of patience with documents.



This was unfortunate.



She had never particularly enjoyed waiting.

The Marquisate's new purchasing procedure encountered its first disagreement.



The receiving steward had returned the record that Mingyuan had sent back for clarification.



The original order specified individual pieces.



The delivery had been recorded in bundles.



The steward had now supplied the number of pieces in each bundle and signed the correction.



However, the purchasing clerk objected to the correction being entered in the consolidated register.



He argued that the register had already been completed and that changing it would make the page untidy.



Mingyuan listened to the explanation.



Then he looked at the clerk.



“Is the register intended to be attractive or accurate?”



The clerk lowered his head.



“Accurate, Young Master.”



“Then the correction must be entered.”



“But the original page—”



“Must remain visible. The corrected quantity should be recorded with the date, the reason, and the person making the correction.”



The clerk nodded reluctantly.



Mingyuan looked at Liang Wen.



“Is the procedure clear on this point?”



“Yes. The original entry is not to be erased.”



“Then there is no need to debate it.”



The clerk accepted the instruction.



Steward Zhou waited until the clerk had left.



“He was concerned about the appearance of the register.”



“He was concerned about having to correct his work.”



“That may also be true.”



Mingyuan looked at the document.



The new procedure had been introduced only yesterday.



Already, some of the staff were trying to preserve their old habits by finding reasons to avoid its requirements.



He understood that habits were not easily changed.



But the estate could not return to its previous methods simply because the new ones required attention.



“Have Liang Wen prepare a short explanation for the clerks,” he said. “The register is a record of transactions, not a finished painting.”



Steward Zhou nodded.



Mingyuan returned to the accounts room.

At the workshop, the packing clerk discovered that the second consignment required more containers than originally estimated.



The problem was not the number of bolts.



The problem was the size of the bundles.



The approved packing arrangement required each bundle to be secured in a way that protected the edges. The first consignment had used slightly more packing material than expected because the bundles had been arranged with additional separation.



The clerk had assumed that the same number of containers would be sufficient for the remaining goods.



It was not.



The workshop supervisor reported the matter to Mingzhao.



She examined the packing list.



“How many additional containers?”



“Four.”



“Can the existing containers be rearranged safely?”



“The supervisor says no. It would compress the bundles.”



“Then use four additional containers.”



“The packing cost will increase.”



“By how much?”



The clerk provided the estimate.



Mingzhao examined it.



The increase was modest.



She approved the additional packing materials.



However, she instructed the clerk to record the reason for the difference.



The first consignment had established a more accurate packing estimate. The second could therefore be prepared without guessing.



Qiao Yue watched her sign the authorization.



“Another unexpected expense.”



“An identified expense.”



“Does that make it better?”



“It makes it manageable.”



Mingzhao handed back the paper.



The goods had to reach the buyer in acceptable condition. Saving a small amount on packing would be pointless if the silk arrived damaged.



She had no intention of reducing the quality of the delivery merely to make the transport estimate look better.

The former Empress received a small basket of fruit from the palace kitchen shortly before midday.



She thanked the attendant and placed it beside the window.



Fang Ru brought a knife and a small plate.



“Would you like me to cut the fruit?”



“Please.”



While Fang Ru worked, Yuru read another page of the essay.



The writer had described an official who had insisted that a granary contained more grain than it actually did because the account books had been copied from an earlier year.



Yuru stopped reading.



She looked at the page for a moment.



Then she continued.



She did not send a message to the palace administrators.



She did not request a review of the granary accounts.



The essay was several decades old.



The author was describing a problem that had occurred in another province.



Yuru could read it without assuming that the palace was experiencing the same difficulty.



Fang Ru placed the sliced fruit before her.



“Madam, would you like more tea?”



“Yes, thank you.”



Yuru ate a piece of fruit.



The afternoon remained quiet.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received a new report concerning the old grain discrepancy.



The former warehouse steward Zhang had examined another set of certified copies.



His response distinguished between what he remembered and what the surviving records confirmed.



The warehouse books showed that the original recorded quantity had been forty-seven sacks.



A later instruction referred to twenty sacks.



The receipt record confirmed eighteen.



Two sacks remained unconfirmed.



The difference between the original quantity and the documented receipt was still twenty-seven sacks.



Zhang's explanation concerning the distinction between total warehouse stock and immediately available stock accounted for differences in the way some figures had been written.



It did not explain the missing twenty-seven sacks.



Mingyuan read the statement carefully.



“Has Zhang identified a destination?”



“No,” Steward Zhou replied.



“Has he identified anyone who received the grain?”



“No.”



“Then the matter remains unresolved.”



“Yes.”



Mingyuan placed the statement beside the earlier records.



The search had produced useful clarification, but no conclusion.



He looked at the blank destination field on the original transport record.



“Continue examining the related transport payments,” he said. “But do not connect them unless the dates, quantities, and supporting documents match.”



Steward Zhou nodded.



The separate payment with a blank destination remained a separate matter.



It was possible that the two matters were connected.



It was also possible that they were not.



Mingyuan would not turn a possibility into a fact simply because he wanted the accounts to make sense.

At the eastern market residence, Sun Qiming arrived to review the completed production accounts.



He examined the original wage sheets, the material records, the training expenses, the supervision entries, and the correction sheets.



Mingzhao sat opposite him.



Qiao Yue served tea.



Sun Qiming read silently for some time.



At last, he placed the papers in order.



“The categories are now clearer,” he said.



“Are the totals correct?”



“The recorded payments agree with the supporting sheets in the sections I have checked.”



Mingzhao waited.



Sun Qiming continued.



“There are still two matters to settle. The final transport cost is not yet available, and the buyer's remaining payment has not been received.”



“The first consignment has reached the transfer point.”



“That is not the same as final delivery.”



“I know.”



Sun Qiming nodded.



He turned to the production expenses.



“The training costs are higher than the original estimate.”



“Because the trainees required more supervision during the first stages.”



“Yes. The expense is properly recorded.”



“Was the revised folding method more efficient?”



“It reduced the time required for some stages, but the additional inspection and training costs offset part of the saving.”



Mingzhao considered this.



“Then we should not assume that the revised method automatically increases profit.”



“Correct. It may improve production efficiency over a larger order, once the workers become familiar with it. But this contract alone does not prove that.”



Mingzhao nodded.



She had suspected as much.



The method had been approved by the buyer and had helped the workshop maintain its schedule. That was valuable.



But a useful production method was not necessarily a cheaper one in every circumstance.



Sun Qiming continued reviewing the records.



“The order appears properly accounted for so far. I would recommend that we prepare the final profit statement only after both consignments have been delivered and the remaining payment has been settled.”



“Agreed.”



He gathered the documents.



“I will prepare a preliminary cost statement now and update it when the final expenses arrive.”



“Please do.”



Sun Qiming paused.



“You have kept the records considerably more carefully than many merchants.”



Mingzhao looked at him.



“Many merchants are not paying for four additional workers and two trainees.”



“That is true.”



“And many merchants do not have the luxury of discovering the cost of an order after the money has been spent.”



Sun Qiming smiled faintly.



“That is also true.”



He left with the original copies required for his review.



Mingzhao looked at the remaining ledger.



The order had been a success in production.



Whether it had been a success in profit would be established later.



She could accept that.

The second consignment was packed by late afternoon.



The final quantity was one hundred and seventy-two bolts.



The packing clerk recorded the number of bundles and containers. The workshop supervisor checked the seals and the condition of the goods.



The carrier's representative examined the dispatch list.



This time, the number of containers matched the revised estimate.



Mingzhao inspected the paperwork before signing.



The goods were ready.



The carrier was scheduled to collect them the following morning.



Qiao Yue stood beside the table.



“Then the entire order will soon be delivered.”



“Once the second consignment reaches the buyer.”



“Do you ever allow yourself to celebrate before every document has arrived?”



“I celebrated when the buyer accepted all two hundred bolts.”



Qiao Yue looked surprised.



“You did?”



“Briefly.”



“When?”



“When you were looking at the inspection record.”



Qiao Yue laughed.



“I did not notice.”



“That was because you were already celebrating.”



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



She had allowed herself a moment of satisfaction.



She simply did not intend to confuse satisfaction with the completion of every obligation.

That evening, Yuru received a message from her mother confirming that the Xu household would be pleased to see her whenever she chose to visit.



Her father had also written a short line.



He promised not to question her about the book unless she wished to discuss it.



Yuru read the note and smiled.



Fang Ru looked at her.



“Your father has made a concession?”



“He has.”



“Will you visit soon?”



“Perhaps in a few days.”



“Would you like me to prepare the book case?”



“It is already prepared.”



“Then there is nothing else to arrange.”



Yuru nodded.



She placed the letters in her writing box.



For once, an invitation from her family did not come with a schedule, a list of duties, or a request for her judgment.



She was simply welcome to visit.



She found that she preferred it that way.

The capital settled into evening.



At the palace, the former Empress continued reading.



At the eastern market residence, Mingzhao reviewed the second consignment's dispatch papers.



At the Marquisate, the clerks began learning that corrections were part of maintaining accurate records rather than evidence that a register had failed.



Mingyuan continued examining the old grain accounts.



The missing twenty-seven sacks remained unexplained.



The unclear payment remained under investigation.



The northern canal property remained outside the family division.



And the workshop's second consignment was ready for collection.



The day had not produced a dramatic revelation.



It had produced a corrected packing estimate, a clearer cost statement, a more precise grain record, and a new understanding among the Marquisate's clerks.



The former Empress considered the day satisfactory.



Mingzhao considered it productive.



The Marquisate's purchasing clerk considered it unnecessarily complicated.



Steward Zhou considered that a promising beginning.
