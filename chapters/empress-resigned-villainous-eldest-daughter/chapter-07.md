# **Chapter 7: The Empress Has Been Asked to Approve a New Regulation. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Explain an Old One.**

**29th Day of the Third Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

The first document Xu Yuru received that morning was a proposed regulation.

The second was a request to explain why the proposed regulation was necessary.

The third was a letter from the emperor asking her not to spend the morning answering documents that could be answered by the departments themselves.

She read the third document first.

It was short.

It was also rather unusual.

*The review is intended to establish proper administrative responsibility. Matters already governed by existing regulations should be handled by the appropriate offices. The Empress need not personally respond to every request for clarification.*

The emperor's signature appeared at the bottom.

Xu Yuru read it twice.

Then she put it beside the proposed regulation.

Madam Fang, who had been waiting for instructions, glanced at the papers.

“His Majesty has been considerate.”

“He has.”

“Shall I return the other two documents?”

Xu Yuru looked at the proposed regulation again.

“Not yet.”

She opened it.

The draft concerned the approval of exceptional expenditure within the inner palace. It proposed that all such expenditure should be submitted to the Empress's office before payment.

Xu Yuru's expression became still.

“Who prepared this?”

“The Department of Palace Stores, Your Majesty. It was sent through the Ministry of Revenue.”

“Why?”

“They believe the revised procedures require an office to authorize exceptional expenditure.”

Xu Yuru turned the page.

The regulation would effectively preserve the Empress's office as the final authority over a broad range of expenses, even after the administrative responsibilities had been redistributed.

She set down the paper.

“This is not a new regulation.”

Madam Fang looked at it.

“It appears to be a clarification.”

“It is a clarification that restores the existing arrangement under a different name.”

“Should it be returned?”

“Yes. Ask the department to identify which exceptional expenditures cannot be handled under the existing financial rules.”

Madam Fang accepted the document.

Xu Yuru added, “And ask why they believe the Empress's office must approve them.”

Madam Fang nodded.

The Empress looked at the third document again.

The emperor had understood the problem.

The departments were beginning to understand it too.

But understanding that responsibility must be distributed was not the same as wanting to receive it.

In the Hall of Mental Cultivation, Zhao Jingheng was having a conversation with Minister Lu of Revenue.

The minister had arrived with a proposal concerning the revised financial procedures for the imperial household.

He placed it before the emperor.

“Your Majesty, the Ministry of Revenue believes that exceptional expenditure should be subject to a clearly defined approval process.”

“That is reasonable.”

“The Ministry proposes that all exceptional expenditure be submitted to the Empress's office for approval.”

The emperor looked at him.

“Why?”

Minister Lu appeared prepared for the question.

“The Empress's office has traditionally supervised the inner palace's financial arrangements. Maintaining this authority would preserve continuity during the transition.”

“Continuity of what?”

“The existing approval process.”

Zhao Jingheng leaned back.

“Is the existing approval process the reason we are reviewing the responsibilities?”

Minister Lu paused.

“Your Majesty, the review is intended to improve administrative clarity.”

“Then why are you proposing to preserve the arrangement that created the confusion?”

“The Ministry believes that a single approving authority would prevent disputes.”

“Would it?”

Minister Lu hesitated.

“It would establish a final authority.”

The emperor looked at the document.

“You have proposed that the Empress continue approving expenditure after the review is completed.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

“Then what has changed?”

Minister Lu lowered his head.

“The departments would have clearer procedures.”

“Except that they would still send the final decision to the Empress.”

The minister did not answer.

Zhao Jingheng tapped the document.

“Does the Ministry of Revenue approve expenditure for every other department?”

“No, Your Majesty.”

“Does the Ministry of Rites approve every purchase connected to a ceremony?”

“No, Your Majesty.”

“Then why should the Empress's office approve every exceptional purchase within the inner palace?”

Minister Lu answered carefully.

“Because the Empress's position has traditionally carried responsibility for the inner palace.”

“The Empress has not asked to abandon every responsibility associated with her position. She has asked to relinquish the work that has accumulated beyond its proper limits.”

The emperor paused.

“Return the proposal. The Ministry should distinguish between financial authorization, verification of expenditure, and administrative supervision. These are not the same thing.”

Minister Lu bowed.

“As Your Majesty commands.”

“And do not send the revised proposal to Chengming Palace merely because the Ministry cannot agree among itself.”

The minister bowed again.

Zhao Jingheng watched him withdraw.

Then he looked toward Wei Zhong, who had been standing near the side of the room.

“Am I being unreasonable?”

Wei Zhong replied, “Your Majesty has asked the Ministry to distinguish three separate functions.”

“That was not my question.”

Wei Zhong considered his answer.

“Your Majesty is asking the departments to perform work they have long relied upon the Empress to perform.”

“Which is unreasonable?”

“No, Your Majesty.”

“Then why does everyone behave as though it is?”

Wei Zhong did not answer immediately.

Finally, he said, “Because an arrangement that is inconvenient to change can become familiar enough to appear necessary.”

The emperor looked at him.

“That sounds like a polite way of saying they do not wish to change.”

“It may be.”

Zhao Jingheng sighed.

“Continue the review.”

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingzhao had been summoned to explain an old regulation.

The regulation concerned the use of family funds for repairs to residences occupied by members of the household.

It was not a particularly complicated document.

It stated that ordinary maintenance of family-owned residences could be charged to the estate, while improvements made for an individual's personal benefit required separate authorization or reimbursement, depending on the circumstances.

The problem was that the regulation had not been applied consistently.

Sun Qiming had found several payments charged to the family estate for improvements made to Shen Mingyuan's residence.

Some appeared to be ordinary repairs.

Others involved new furnishings, decorative work, and alterations to rooms used primarily by the Second Young Master.

The total was not yet known.

Mingzhao had been asked to explain the method by which these expenses had been recorded during her six years managing the household accounts.

She sat across from Sun Qiming in the eastern study.

Steward Zhou had placed the relevant ledgers on the table.

Sun Qiming pointed to an entry.

“Eldest Miss, this payment was recorded as a household repair.”

“Yes.”

“Was it made for the entire residence?”

“No. The work concerned the Second Young Master's private study and adjoining rooms.”

“Why was it recorded as a general household expense?”

“Because the work order was submitted through the household office, and the estate paid the contractor.”

“Did you approve the payment?”

“I approved the payment after the work was completed and the contractor's account was submitted.”

“Did you approve the classification?”

Mingzhao examined the entry.

“No. The classification was entered by the estate clerk before the account reached me.”

“Did you question it?”

“I questioned several similar entries. The household office informed me that the work had been included under the annual maintenance allocation.”

Sun Qiming looked at the ledger.

“Was the annual allocation sufficient?”

“It was sufficient for ordinary repairs. I cannot say whether it was intended to cover private improvements.”

“Did you request clarification?”

“Yes. The correspondence is in the household records.”

Steward Zhou immediately retrieved a folder.

Mingzhao opened it.

The first letter was dated three years earlier.

It requested a breakdown of the maintenance expenditure for the Second Young Master's residence.

The reply stated that the work had been necessary to preserve the condition of the building.

The second letter asked whether the decorative alterations were included.

The answer had not been preserved.

The third letter was a note from the previous estate manager stating that the matter could be settled during the annual accounts review.

Mingzhao placed the documents in order.

Sun Qiming examined them.

“You raised the question.”

“Yes.”

“But the classification remained unchanged.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because the estate manager did not amend the account, and the marquis did not issue a separate instruction.”

Sun Qiming looked at her.

“Did you continue to approve similar payments?”

“Only where the work had been completed and the supporting documents were present. I did not determine whether the expense should ultimately be borne by the family or charged to an individual account.”

“Why not?”

“Because the final classification was not within my authority.”

Sun Qiming nodded.

“That distinction should be included in the report.”

Mingzhao looked at him.

“Include the correspondence as well.”

“Certainly.”

She turned another page.

“There are also payments for the northern courtyard.”

Sun Qiming followed her finger.

“Those appear to concern the roof and drainage.”

“They do.”

“Were they completed?”

“Yes.”

“Were they inspected?”

“By the estate steward.”

Steward Zhou nodded.

“I inspected the work myself.”

“Then include your inspection record,” Mingzhao said.

Sun Qiming made a note.

The examination continued.

By the time it ended, they had established that several expenses had been classified under general household maintenance even though the work had benefited individual residences.

They had not established that the payments were improper.

That question depended on the original authorization, the terms of the estate's maintenance allocation, and the family rules governing such expenses.

Mingzhao was not satisfied.

But she was not surprised.

The problem had existed long before she had been asked to explain it.

At midday, Madam Liang visited the eastern courtyard.

Mingzhao was reviewing the documents concerning the silk warehouse partnership when her mother entered.

She rose and bowed.

“Mother.”

Madam Liang gestured for her to sit.

“I heard that the accountant has begun examining the household expenses.”

“Yes.”

“Your father says you have raised questions about the repairs to Mingyuan's residence.”

“I provided the records requested.”

“Were those expenses not necessary?”

“Some were. Others require clarification.”

Madam Liang looked at the papers.

“You have been managing the accounts for six years. Surely you know why the work was done.”

“I know why some of it was done. I do not know whether every expense should have been charged to the family estate.”

“Your brother lives in the family residence.”

“That does not mean every improvement to his private rooms must be paid for by the estate.”

Madam Liang's expression tightened.

“He is the heir.”

“Then the estate should record what it spends on him as the heir.”

“What difference does that make?”

“It makes a difference when the accounts are divided.”

Madam Liang looked away.

“You are determined to separate everything.”

“I am determined to identify everything.”

“Must you examine even your brother's rooms?”

“The accountant is examining the estate accounts. The expenses are recorded there.”

Madam Liang sighed.

“Your brother feels that you are treating him like a stranger.”

Mingzhao put down her brush.

“Has he submitted the receipts for the three hundred taels?”

Madam Liang looked at her.

“He has been trying to find them.”

“Then I will wait for the records.”

“You could simply accept his explanation.”

“I could. But then the amount would remain unsupported.”

“Your brother is not a dishonest person.”

“I have not called him one.”

Madam Liang was silent.

Mingzhao continued, “If the expenditure was legitimate, the records will show it. If the records are missing, we can identify what remains uncertain. There is no need to turn a financial examination into a judgment of his character.”

Madam Liang studied her daughter.

“You speak as though you are already no longer part of this family.”

Mingzhao's face became still.

“I am part of this family. I am also asking to establish my own household.”

“Those things do not have to be separated.”

“They do if the family expects me to continue managing its affairs while I am trying to leave.”

Madam Liang did not answer.

For several moments, the room remained quiet.

Then she rose.

“Your father will want to speak with you again after the accountant has finished the preliminary examination.”

“I will attend.”

Madam Liang nodded and left.

Mingzhao watched the door close.

Qiao Yue entered shortly afterward.

“Would you like tea, Eldest Miss?”

“Yes.”

Qiao Yue poured it.

After a moment, she said, “Madam Liang looked unhappy.”

“She is unhappy.”

“Does that trouble you?”

Mingzhao took the cup.

“It would trouble me more if she were happy about it.”

Qiao Yue looked at her.

Mingzhao drank her tea.

“Do not misunderstand me. I do not want to quarrel with my mother.”

“Then why does every conversation become an argument?”

“Because she wants me to remain, and I do not.”

Qiao Yue lowered her eyes.

Mingzhao put down the cup.

“That is not a problem a few pleasant words can solve.”

In the palace, the Second Prince had completed the first stage of his account review.

He arrived at the emperor's study carrying a report.

Zhao Jingheng looked at the document.

“You have finished?”

“I have completed the initial examination.”

The emperor opened it.

Chengyu stood before the desk.

The report separated personal expenditure from household expenditure, identified several purchases requiring further verification, and listed the inventory of furnishings and supplies.

It also included a request for clarification concerning the authority of the Eastern Palace steward.

The emperor read the final page.

“Why have you asked for clarification about your steward's authority?”

Chengyu answered, “The steward has been approving ordinary expenditure, but certain purchases have been made under instructions from my personal attendants. The records do not clearly distinguish which payments required my approval.”

“Then what do you propose?”

“The steward should continue to approve ordinary household expenditure within the allocated budget. Personal purchases should be charged to my own account. Large purchases and exceptional expenses should require my written approval.”

The emperor looked at him.

“Can you define a large purchase?”

Chengyu paused.

“I have not yet determined the appropriate threshold.”

“Then do not create a regulation before you know what it needs to regulate.”

The Second Prince bowed.

“I will review the previous year's expenditure and propose a reasonable limit.”

The emperor nodded.

“Good.”

Chengyu looked at the report.

“Father, is this examination necessary for every prince?”

“Yes.”

“Even for the Crown Prince?”

“Especially for the Crown Prince.”

The Second Prince looked mildly dissatisfied.

The emperor continued, “Your brother has already begun his review.”

Chengyu's expression changed.

“How far has he progressed?”

“He has identified several incomplete records.”

Chengyu looked down.

“Then I will complete my examination before he completes his.”

Zhao Jingheng regarded his second son.

“This is not a competition.”

“I understand.”

“Do you?”

Chengyu did not answer.

The emperor closed the report.

“Your household accounts are your responsibility. The Crown Prince's household accounts are his. You are not being asked to defeat each other.”

Chengyu bowed.

“I understand, Father.”

He left with his report.

The emperor looked at the door.

Gao Shun stood nearby.

“His Highness seems motivated.”

“By the wrong thing.”

“Perhaps he will discover the right reason while doing the work.”

Zhao Jingheng glanced at him.

“You have become very optimistic.”

Gao Shun bowed.

“I have learned that the imperial household has enough difficulties without my adding pessimism to them.”

The emperor gave a quiet sigh.

The independent examination at the Marquisate continued into the afternoon.

Sun Qiming had begun reviewing the commercial partnership accounts separately from the family estate ledgers.

Mingzhao sat beside him, answering questions when necessary.

He reached the silk warehouse partnership.

“The original investment was made from your personal funds?”

“Yes.”

“Were any funds later provided by the marquisate?”

“No.”

“Was the warehouse property purchased by the partnership?”

“No. It is leased.”

“Then the partnership's assets consist primarily of stock, receivables, cash, and the leasehold interest.”

“Yes.”

“Are there outstanding liabilities?”

“Payments owed to two suppliers and one transport company.”

“Are they recorded?”

“Yes.”

Sun Qiming examined the ledger.

“The transport company has charged a late fee.”

Mingzhao leaned forward.

“Which delivery?”

“The shipment from the tenth month.”

“That delay was caused by the river closure.”

“Is there a written notice?”

“There should be correspondence with the transport company.”

“Then we will need it.”

Mingzhao looked at the entry.

“Do not accept the late fee as a valid liability until the contract terms and correspondence have been examined.”

Sun Qiming nodded.

He made a note.

After a while, he looked up.

“Eldest Miss, your commercial interests are not as simple as the preliminary list suggests.”

“I did not expect them to be.”

“The partnership is profitable, but its available cash is limited. The outstanding receivables are significant.”

“Then the valuation should distinguish between assets and money immediately available.”

“Precisely.”

Mingzhao nodded.

She had understood that before.

But hearing it confirmed by an independent accountant was useful.

A business could have a positive value without having enough cash to settle every obligation at once.

She would need to plan accordingly.

Sun Qiming closed the ledger.

“I will require a meeting with your partner, Lin Shuyun, to confirm the partnership records.”

“I will arrange it.”

“Until then, I will not issue a final valuation.”

“Agreed.”

He gathered his papers.

Mingzhao looked at the growing list of unresolved matters.

The settlement was becoming more complicated.

But it was also becoming more precise.

She preferred that to a quick agreement built on assumptions.

At the palace, Xu Yuru received the Ministry of Rites' revised explanation.

It was shorter than the previous document.

The ministry had accepted that it would determine ceremonial standards, while the inner palace administration would manage internal arrangements. The Ministry of Revenue would review expenditure according to the approved financial procedures.

The Empress's office would no longer be the automatic final authority for all ceremonial expenses.

Xu Yuru read the document.

Then she signed the acknowledgment.

Madam Fang watched her.

“Your Majesty, the Ministry has accepted the revised arrangement.”

“Yes.”

“Does that mean the responsibility can be removed from the register?”

“No. It means the responsibility has been divided properly.”

Madam Fang nodded.

Xu Yuru added, “The office must still record what it does during the transition. We cannot simply remove an entry because the wording has changed.”

“Understood.”

The Empress looked at the register.

Several responsibilities had now been clarified.

The number remained ninety-four, but the number of matters requiring her direct attention was decreasing.

It was a modest achievement.

It had taken three days, several meetings, and enough documents to fill a small cabinet.

Xu Yuru found herself wondering whether the palace would eventually become efficient.

She decided not to expect miracles.

That evening, the Marquis of Qingyuan received Sun Qiming's preliminary report.

It contained no final valuation, but it identified a problem with the estate's repair expenses.

Several payments had been charged to the family maintenance account without a clear distinction between ordinary repairs and improvements to individual residences.

The report also noted that some supporting documents were missing.

Shen Guowei read it slowly.

“Does this mean the estate has paid for Mingyuan's private improvements?”

Sun Qiming answered, “It means the existing records do not allow me to determine the classification of every payment.”

“Can the payments be separated?”

“With the original work orders, contractor records, and any written approvals.”

“Have they been found?”

“Some have. Others remain outstanding.”

The marquis looked toward Steward Zhou.

“Find the remaining records.”

“Yes, my lord.”

Shen Guowei turned back to the accountant.

“Will this affect the division?”

“It may affect the calculation of the estate's liabilities and the amount of any individual claims. I cannot determine the final effect until the examination is complete.”

The marquis nodded.

He was beginning to regret the years in which he had accepted summary reports without examining their supporting documents.

But regret did not produce missing receipts.

Nor did it make the ledgers any easier to read.

On the same evening, Mingzhao received a letter from Lin Shuyun.

Her business partner had agreed to provide the original partnership schedule and attend a meeting with the independent accountant.

The letter also mentioned that the silk warehouse had received an inquiry from a merchant interested in purchasing part of its stock.

Mingzhao read the letter twice.

The offer was not especially generous.

However, it was not unreasonable.

She wrote a reply asking for the merchant's identity, the proposed quantity, payment terms, and delivery arrangements.

She did not authorize a sale.

The partnership's financial position was being examined. A large transaction during the valuation could complicate the records.

She placed the letter in the commercial folder.

Qiao Yue looked at it.

“Is that good news?”

“It may be.”

“Will you sell the silk?”

“Not until the terms are examined.”

Qiao Yue sighed.

“Everything must be examined.”

Mingzhao looked at her.

“Yes.”

“Even when someone offers to buy something?”

“Especially then.”

Qiao Yue shook her head.

Mingzhao returned to the documents.

The independent household she wanted would require money.

The business interests she intended to retain would require careful management.

The family estate would require a settlement that did not leave her responsible for debts she had never agreed to assume.

She had no intention of exchanging one burden for another.

Outside, the evening wind moved through the courtyard trees.

Mingzhao looked at the papers before her.

She had once believed that leaving the marquisate would be a matter of making a decision.

Now she understood that a decision could be made in a moment.

Making it real required months of work.

At the imperial palace, Zhao Jingheng received the day's final report.

The Ministry of Rites had accepted the revised ceremonial arrangement.

The Crown Prince had begun a detailed examination of his household accounts.

The Second Prince had submitted his initial report.

The Third Princess had corrected her accounting exercises.

And the Department of Palace Stores had still not explained the missing brooms.

The emperor read the final entry.

He put the report down.

“Gao Shun.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

“Tomorrow, I want a report on the progress of the inventory examination.”

“Yes.”

“Not a report explaining why the inventory cannot be completed.”

Gao Shun bowed.

“I understand.”

Zhao Jingheng looked at the documents on his desk.

The palace was beginning to move.

Slowly.

Unevenly.

With considerable resistance.

But it was moving.

He picked up the Empress's resignation petition, which remained in the file beside the review reports.

He had not accepted it.

He had not rejected it.

He had told himself that he needed time to consider the consequences.

That remained true.

But another question had begun to trouble him.

If the Empress's work could be redistributed, if the departments could learn to function independently, and if the imperial household could continue without her personal supervision, then what exactly was he asking her to remain for?

The question had no easy answer.

For now, he returned the petition to its file.

The palace still had work to do.

So did the marquisate.

And both were discovering that the hardest part of relinquishing responsibility was not the act of leaving.

It was making certain that the people who remained could no longer pretend the responsibility belonged to someone else.

**End of Chapter 7.**
