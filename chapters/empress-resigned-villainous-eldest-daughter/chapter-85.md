# **Chapter 85: The Former Empress Has Been Asked Whether She Would Like Another Book and Has Not Been Asked to Write a Review. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Carrier's Report and Has Discovered That a Cart Can Arrive Before Its Paperwork. The Marquisate's New Procedure Has Encountered a Clerk Who Believes a Neat Register Is More Important Than an Accurate One.**

**19th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress received a message from her father shortly after breakfast.



It was not a formal invitation.



It was not an administrative request.



It was a short note informing her that the two books she had been offered remained available, and that the library attendant had placed them together on a table in the eastern courtyard.



Her father had added one sentence beneath the message.



*There is no hurry. You may borrow them when you wish.*



Yuru read it twice.



Fang Ru, who was arranging the morning tea, glanced at the paper.



"Your father is being very considerate."



"He is."



"Will you go today?"



Yuru looked at the wrapped book on the table.



She had finished reading it several days ago. It was ready to be returned, and the two new books were waiting. A visit would be pleasant. There was no reason to avoid one.



But there was also no reason to turn a pleasant possibility into an appointment simply because the opportunity had been offered.



"Not today," she said.



Fang Ru nodded.



"Then shall I prepare a reply?"



"Yes. Tell him I am grateful that he has kept them ready. I will visit when I have finished my current reading."



Fang Ru took the note.



"That may take several days."



"It may."



"Or several weeks."



"That is possible."



"Your father may begin selecting a fourth book."



Yuru looked at her attendant.



"That would be his decision."



Fang Ru lowered her eyes, though the corners of her mouth moved.



"Of course."



Yuru poured tea.



She had once believed that a free afternoon ought to be used well. If there were no ceremonies, there were accounts. If there were no accounts, there were petitions from the inner household. If there were no petitions, there were disputes to anticipate before they became disputes.



A quiet afternoon had been a space waiting to be filled.



Now she was learning that it could remain empty.



The lesson was not difficult in theory.



It was simply unfamiliar in practice.



After breakfast, she walked along the western path with Fang Ru. The weather was warm, and the stone paving had dried after the previous night's light rain. They passed the pavilion without stopping.



Yuru did not ask whether any reports had arrived.



Fang Ru did not mention the palace administration.



They discussed the fruit sent by the Dowager, the condition of the garden, and whether the pear trees near the western wall would bear fruit later in the season.



At the turn of the path, Yuru stopped briefly.



"Do you think the trees are being watered enough?"



Fang Ru looked at the nearest one.



"I believe the gardeners know how to water trees."



"That is reassuring."



"Would you like to ask them?"



Yuru smiled.



"No."



Fang Ru looked pleased.



They continued walking.



The trees were not her responsibility.



She could enjoy them without inspecting the soil.

At the eastern market, the first consignment of silk bolts arrived at the designated transfer point shortly before noon.



The carrier's report reached Mingzhao's temporary residence later than the carts themselves.



Qiao Yue brought it into the main room.



"The first twenty-eight bolts have been received at the transfer point. The receiving clerk has signed the count."



Mingzhao looked up from the account book.



"All twenty-eight?"



"Yes. The report says the outer containers were intact. The receiving clerk counted the bundles and recorded the seals."



"What about the second consignment?"



Qiao Yue unfolded the remaining paper.



"The carrier says the one hundred and seventy-two bolts are still on the road. They departed yesterday morning. The route supervisor expects them to reach the transfer point today, but there is no receipt yet."



"Then record the first as received at the transfer point and the second as in transit."



"Already done."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Send a copy of the first receipt to Lin Shuyun. Keep the original with the dispatch records."



Qiao Yue placed the report on the table.



"Will you inform Zhou Wenqi?"



"Yes. Send a short notice that the first consignment has arrived at the transfer point and the second is in transit. Do not describe the first as final delivery."



Qiao Yue wrote the instruction.



"Do you think the buyer will complain about the two separate deliveries?"



"The contract permits staged dispatch."



"Then there should be no complaint."



"That does not mean there will be none."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Business appears to involve a great deal of waiting for people to complain."



"Only when the documents are not clear."



"That sounds like another way of saying people will complain."



Mingzhao did not disagree.



She reviewed the signed receipt.



The receiving clerk had confirmed twenty-eight bundles. The container seals were intact. The transfer point had accepted custody, but the buyer's warehouse had not yet received the goods.



The record was correct.



That was enough for the moment.



Lin Shuyun arrived shortly afterward.



She read the report and sat down.



"At least the first consignment has reached the transfer point."



"Yes."



"And the second is expected today."



"Expected."



Lin looked at her.



"You are very fond of that word."



"It is useful."



"It is not pleasant."



"Most useful words are not pleasant when someone wants a conclusion."



Lin placed the report beside the cost statement.



"Sun Qiming asked me to tell you that he has nearly finished the preliminary review. He wants the carrier's final charges before he closes the transport account."



"That is reasonable."



"He also asked whether we have decided how to treat the additional packing containers."



"They are a direct packing expense."



"He agrees. He merely wants the entry confirmed against the workshop authorization."



"Then send him the authorization."



Lin nodded.



"Anything else?"



"Ask him to keep the training costs separate from ordinary wages."



"He already has."



"Then ask him to keep them separate in the final summary as well."



Lin smiled.



"You do not trust anyone to remember what they have already done."



"I trust records more than memory."



"That is why your accounts are so long."



"And why we can still understand them."



Lin laughed.



They spent the next hour comparing the dispatch records against the original contract.



The buyer had accepted all two hundred bolts after inspection. That acceptance had established that the goods met the agreed standards. It had not established that the buyer had received them. The carrier's responsibility and the buyer's responsibility were separated by the transfer point.



The remaining payment was due under the contract's delivery terms.



Until the delivery documents were complete, the account remained open.



Mingzhao placed the contract back in its folder.



"Once the second consignment arrives, we can arrange the transfer to the buyer's warehouse."



Lin nodded.



"And once the buyer confirms receipt?"



"Then we can settle the remaining payment and transport charges."



"And after that?"



"Sun can complete the account."



Lin leaned back.



"One might think we were trying to construct a bridge out of documents."



Mingzhao replied, "A bridge made of documents would be safer than one made of promises."



Lin looked at her.



"That is a terrible saying."



"It is an accurate one."



"Those are often the worst."

At the Marquisate, Liang Wen had begun to understand that the new purchasing register would not make every purchase straightforward.



It would merely make the complications easier to find.



That morning, a clerk had entered a delivery of charcoal as twenty baskets. The receiving steward had recorded twenty baskets received, but the storehouse keeper had entered the quantity as two hundred pieces.



Liang Wen had marked the entry for clarification.



The clerk who had made the original purchase looked annoyed.



"Everyone knows a basket contains ten pieces," he said. "There is no need to write another note."



"Does everyone know that?"



The clerk frowned.



"The baskets are standard."



"Are they?"



"Of course."



"Then the storehouse record should state the number of baskets and the number of pieces."



"That will make the register untidy."



Liang Wen looked at the open page.



"The register is already untidy."



The clerk blinked.



Liang Wen pointed to the entry.



"Twenty baskets received. Two hundred pieces recorded. If the conversion is standard, write the conversion. If the baskets are different sizes, write the actual count. We cannot leave the units unexplained."



"The old register did not require this."



"The new one does."



The clerk folded his arms.



"Then the new register will be twice as long."



"Possibly."



"And the accounts office will spend all day writing explanations."



"Only when the entries require explanations."



The clerk muttered something about unnecessary labour.



Liang Wen did not respond.



He had been appointed to maintain the consolidated register, not to ensure that every clerk found the task pleasant.



He added a note identifying the discrepancy and sent the entry back for confirmation.



Steward Zhou arrived while the clerk was still complaining.



"What is the problem?"



"The new register requires both baskets and pieces to be recorded."



Zhou looked at the entry.



"How many pieces were delivered?"



"Two hundred."



"How many baskets?"



"Twenty."



"Then write both."



The clerk opened his mouth.



Zhou raised one hand.



"Do not tell me the old register did not require it. We are not repairing the old register. We are recording today's purchase."



The clerk bowed reluctantly.



Zhou turned to Liang Wen.



"Keep the correction visible. Do not erase the first entry."



"Yes, Steward."



When the clerk had left, Zhou remained beside the desk.



"How many entries have been returned today?"



"Three."



"How many were serious?"



"None so far."



"Then perhaps the procedure is working."



Liang Wen looked at the register.



"It is working slowly."



"Most things worth keeping work slowly."



Zhou paused.



"Except a kitchen fire."



Liang Wen looked up.



"Has there been a kitchen fire?"



"No."



"Then why mention it?"



"Because I do not want you to become so fond of procedures that you forget what an emergency looks like."



Liang Wen nodded.



The new procedure had a separate provision for urgent purchases. It allowed necessary action to be taken immediately, provided the reason, authorization, and subsequent records were completed afterward.



Zhou had insisted that the exception remain clear.



He did not want a clerk delaying the purchase of medicine or emergency repairs because the usual purchasing official was unavailable.



He also did not want every ordinary purchase described as urgent because someone disliked waiting for approval.



The distinction was important.



At the far end of the room, Mingyuan was examining a report from the old warehouse.



Zhang had sent another written statement. It repeated the distinction between total grain stock and immediately available stock, but it did not identify the destination of the twenty-seven sacks.



Mingyuan read the report and set it down.



"Has he found any additional document?"



"No," the clerk said.



"Any name of a receiving official?"



"No."



"Any connection to the separate transport payment?"



"No."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Then the report changes nothing."



The clerk looked relieved.



"Shall we close the inquiry?"



"No."



"Because the twenty-seven sacks remain unexplained?"



"Yes."



Mingyuan returned the report to the folder.



The figures remained what they had been.



Forty-seven in the original record.



Twenty in the instruction.



Eighteen recorded as received.



Two unconfirmed.



Twenty-seven unexplained.



The old payment voucher remained missing as well.



There were now two unresolved matters in the estate accounts, and the new procedure had not made either one disappear.



Mingyuan was beginning to suspect that a good accounting system did not make old problems vanish.



It merely prevented them from being forgotten.

In the late afternoon, a messenger from the workshop arrived at Mingzhao's residence.



He carried a sealed note from the transfer point.



The second consignment had arrived.



One hundred and seventy-two bolts had been received. The outer containers were intact. The receiving clerk had counted the bundles against the dispatch list and confirmed the total.



The first consignment of twenty-eight and the second consignment of one hundred and seventy-two were now both at the designated transfer point.



The transfer point had custody of all two hundred bolts.



Mingzhao read the report carefully.



"Any damage?"



"None recorded."



"Any difference in the count?"



"None."



"Has the buyer's warehouse been informed?"



"The transfer point has sent notice. The buyer's representative is expected to arrange collection."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Send the buyer our confirmation. State that both consignments have arrived at the transfer point and are available for collection."



The messenger bowed.



Qiao Yue watched him leave.



"All two hundred are now in one place."



"Yes."



"That sounds very much like completion."



"It is completion of the transfer-point stage."



Qiao Yue looked at the ceiling.



"I see."



Mingzhao signed the receipt confirmation.



"Do not look so disappointed. The goods have arrived safely."



"I am pleased."



"Then why that expression?"



"Because I suspect that when they reach the buyer's warehouse, you will tell me they have not yet been paid for."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"They have not."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Of course."



Lin Shuyun arrived just as the messenger was leaving.



She read the confirmation and smiled.



"That is good news. All two hundred bolts have reached the transfer point without damage."



"Yes."



"Now we can arrange collection."



"Yes."



"And then the buyer can confirm final receipt."



"Yes."



Lin placed the paper down.



"Do you ever allow yourself to be happy about anything before the last document arrives?"



Mingzhao considered the question.



"I am happy the goods arrived safely."



"That was not what I asked."



"It was the answer I have."



Lin laughed.



"Very well. I shall celebrate the arrival of two hundred bolts. You may celebrate the arrival of the receipt."



Mingzhao allowed herself a small smile.



"That is acceptable."



Sun Qiming arrived later with his preliminary cost review.



He had compared the original production estimate with the recorded expenditure and separated the additional costs that had emerged during the order.



The training wages were higher than anticipated. The revised folding method had saved time in some stages, but the additional instruction and inspection had reduced the benefit. The four additional packing containers had increased the final packing cost. Transport remained provisional until the buyer's warehouse collection and final charges were confirmed.



"Your preliminary expenditure is now more reliable," Sun said. "But I will not call it final."



"Because the buyer has not received the goods."



"And because the remaining payment has not arrived."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Then continue."



Sun placed the statement before her.



"One point should be noted. The training costs should not be treated as an expense that produced no benefit. The two trainees can now complete the assigned folding work under ordinary supervision."



"That is true."



"But the cost belongs to this order."



"Yes."



"Even though the skill may be useful for later orders."



Mingzhao considered this.



"We should record the cost where it was incurred. If the workers remain, their future work may benefit from the training. That does not make the first order's expense disappear."



Sun nodded.



"That is how I have treated it."



"Good."



Lin read the statement over his shoulder.



"So we have completed production, passed inspection, dispatched all goods, reached the transfer point, and trained two workers. Yet we still cannot say whether the order made a profit."



"Correct," Sun said.



Lin shook her head.



"Business is a remarkable invention."



Mingzhao folded the preliminary statement.



"It is not finished."



"That is precisely my complaint."

That evening, in the western guest residence, Yuru sat with a book open before her.



She had chosen a short essay from the palace library, not one of her father's volumes. The subject was the management of provincial granaries during periods of uneven harvest.



The author had argued that a reserve storehouse was useful only if the records distinguished between grain held in storage, grain assigned for distribution, and grain actually delivered.



Yuru read the passage twice.



Then she closed the book.



It was a sensible observation.



She did not need to send it to the palace administration.



She did not need to ask whether the current records followed the same distinction.



She did not need to summon anyone to explain how the matter should be handled.



She had read an essay.



That was all.



Fang Ru entered carrying a small plate of fruit.



"Madam, the evening fruit."



Yuru moved the book aside.



"Thank you."



Fang Ru glanced at the open page.



"Another book about accounts?"



"About granaries."



"Does it require a report?"



"No."



"Then it is a pleasant subject."



Yuru looked at her.



"Why?"



"Because it can be read without anyone asking you to correct the storehouse."



Yuru laughed softly.



She took a piece of fruit.



Outside, the palace was settling into evening. The inner household's officials continued their duties. The Crown Prince was learning to review his own accounts. The Second Prince was discovering that a budget could not be justified by the number of cups someone might possibly drink. The Third Princess had her own studies. The Dowager had her own household.



The palace was not yet perfect.



It had never been perfect.



But it was no longer waiting for Yuru to make every uncertainty disappear.



She returned to her book.



The next chapter could wait until morning.



And, for once, so could everything else.
