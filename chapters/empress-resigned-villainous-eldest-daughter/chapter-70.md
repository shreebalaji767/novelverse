# **Chapter 70: The Former Empress Has Been Offered a Longer Walking Route and Has Not Requested a Map of the Entire Palace. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Question About Her Old Duties and Has Refused to Become Their Temporary Replacement. The Workshop Has Discovered That a Faster Worker Can Still Produce an Incorrect Bolt.**

**4th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The morning began with a question.



It was not an important question.



It did not concern the imperial succession, the palace accounts, or the future of the Great Yong dynasty.



It concerned a walking route.



Fang Ru stood beside the breakfast table with a small note in her hand.



“The western path is clear, Madam. The gardener has finished trimming the hedge. There is also a longer path beside the old pavilion.”



Xu Yuru looked up from her porridge.



“How much longer?”



“About twice the distance.”



“Is it shaded?”



“Partly.”



“Are there steps?”



“Three low ones near the pavilion.”



Yuru considered this.



“Then we can try it.”



Fang Ru looked pleased.



“I will tell the attendants.”



“Tell them to remain at a distance.”



“Yes, Madam.”



Yuru returned to breakfast.



Fang Ru waited for a moment.



“Would you like a map?”



Yuru looked at her.



“Of the path?”



“Yes.”



“I have walked in this palace for more than twenty years.”



“I thought it might be useful.”



Yuru put down her spoon.



“If I cannot find a pavilion without a map, I should not be walking there.”



Fang Ru lowered her head, hiding a smile.



“Yes, Madam.”



The walk was arranged without further discussion.

At the Eastern Palace, Crown Prince Zhao Cheng'an had discovered that the new monthly accounts were producing an unexpected amount of correspondence.



The clerks had separated the oil records into categories based on the units actually written in the original registers.



The result was not a neat total.



It was a series of smaller groups, each with its own confirmed and unresolved quantities.



Cheng'an examined the summary.



“Why has this entry been placed under an estimate?”



The clerk explained.



“The original register records the number of jars, but not their capacity. We have compared it with the purchase records and found two possible capacities.”



“Can we establish which one was used?”



“Not yet.”



“Then why is it an estimate rather than an unresolved entry?”



The clerk looked down.



“We calculated both possibilities.”



“That is useful. But neither calculation establishes the actual quantity.”



The Crown Prince turned the page.



“Keep both calculations in the supporting sheet. The main register should state that the capacity is unconfirmed.”



“Yes, Your Highness.”



The clerk took the paper.



Cheng'an reviewed the remaining entries.



He was becoming more comfortable with the new system.



He was also discovering that the new system required him to read more pages than the old one.



He called for the warehouse records.



The clerk hesitated.



“Your Highness, the warehouse supervisor has already submitted the monthly summary.”



“I want the original issue sheets.”



“Those are in the archive.”



“Bring them.”



The clerk bowed.



Cheng'an looked at the incomplete summary.



He had no intention of approving a total merely because it would allow him to finish before lunch.



Unfortunately, lunch was beginning to look increasingly distant.

In the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Mingyuan had begun the sixth cash book.



This one was in better condition than the previous five.



The ink was clearer, the supporting papers were arranged more carefully, and most entries had recognizable descriptions.



Mingyuan felt encouraged.



Steward Zhou noticed.



“This book should be easier to examine, Young Master.”



“That is good.”



“It was prepared during the later period of the former steward's service.”



Mingyuan nodded.



He read the first few entries.



Then he reached a payment recorded under household maintenance.



The amount was substantial.



The description read:



*Payment for repairs to the eastern study rooms.*



Mingyuan frowned.



“Were these the repairs made to my sister's study?”



Steward Zhou checked the date.



“Yes.”



“Was the payment made from the estate account?”



“The register says so.”



“Then why was seventy-two taels recorded in her personal claims?”



Steward Zhou brought out another sheet.



“The seventy-two taels in her claim concern repairs paid from her personal funds. This entry appears to be a separate maintenance expenditure.”



Mingyuan looked at the supporting record.



The date was different.



The work was different.



The account was different.



He nodded slowly.



“Then they must remain separate.”



“Yes, Young Master.”



Mingyuan placed the document in the estate expenditure group.



He was beginning to see how easily two similar descriptions could be confused.



A room could be repaired twice.



A person could authorize one payment and personally fund another.



The fact that both entries concerned the same building did not make them the same expense.



He wrote a note in the margin.



“Have this distinction added to the schedule.”



Steward Zhou bowed.



Mingyuan continued reading.



The sixth book was easier.



It was not simple.

Mingzhao received the family's latest written question shortly after noon.



It concerned the old household purchasing arrangements.



The letter explained that the family had found several entries bearing her authorization and asked whether she could clarify the original purpose of each payment.



The request was not unreasonable.



However, it included a sentence asking whether she would be willing to assist with the household's current purchasing arrangements until the transition was complete.



Mingzhao read that sentence twice.



Then she put the letter down.



Qiao Yue was arranging the correspondence.



“What is it?”



“My family has found another way to ask me to work for them.”



“Are they asking you to return?”



“Not exactly.”



“Then what are they asking?”



“To assist with purchasing arrangements.”



Qiao Yue looked at the letter.



“Are those part of the property settlement?”



“No.”



“Are they part of your personal claims?”



“No.”



“Then why are they asking?”



“Because they have not yet appointed someone to handle them.”



Qiao Yue sighed.



“Your brother is learning the accounts.”



“He is learning the accounts. He is not yet responsible for every purchasing decision.”



“Then the family should appoint someone.”



Mingzhao looked at her.



“That is what they should do.”



She picked up her brush.



The reply would be brief.



She dictated:



*I will answer specific written questions concerning entries that I personally authorized during my period of management, provided the relevant records are identified. I will not resume the household purchasing duties or act as an interim purchasing officer. The family should assign those responsibilities to its current staff or appoint a replacement.*



Qiao Yue wrote carefully.



Mingzhao continued.



*The property settlement and my personal claims should remain separate from the transition of ordinary household administration.*



Qiao Yue finished the letter.



“Do you want to add that you are willing to explain the records?”



“I have already said so.”



“Perhaps they will think you are being uncooperative.”



“Then they may read the letter again.”



Qiao Yue sealed it.



Mingzhao turned back to her own accounts.



She had no objection to answering questions about her past work.



She had an objection to being made responsible for the work again.



The difference was not complicated.



Her family had simply become accustomed to ignoring it.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received Mingzhao's reply that afternoon.



He read it in silence.



Madam Liang was sitting nearby.



“She has refused again?”



“She has refused the purchasing work.”



“Of course she has.”



Shen Guowei placed the letter on the table.



“She has agreed to answer specific questions about the old records.”



“She could have helped us until the new arrangements were complete.”



“She has already said she will not resume management.”



Madam Liang frowned.



“Does she intend to make everything difficult?”



Shen Guowei looked at the letter.



“No. She intends to make us responsible for our own affairs.”



Madam Liang was silent.



The Marquis did not enjoy the implication.



But he could not honestly deny it.



For six years, the family had treated Mingzhao's ability as a convenient resource. When she left, they had expected the work to continue as before, merely with another name attached.



They had not appointed that person.



They had not prepared a complete transition.



They had simply continued sending questions to the person who had resigned.



He looked toward the door.



“Call Steward Zhou.”



The steward entered shortly afterward.



“My lord.”



“Who is currently responsible for household purchasing?”



“Several purchases are being handled by the individual departments. The general purchasing register is still under my supervision.”



“Can you continue?”



“For the immediate period, yes. But I will need a designated clerk to maintain the consolidated register.”



“Then appoint one.”



Steward Zhou bowed.



“Yes, my lord.”



“And report the new arrangement to Mingyuan.”



“Yes.”



The Marquis leaned back.



It was a simple decision.



It should have been made earlier.

At the workshop, the senior finisher's first group under the reduced inspection arrangement was completed before the afternoon ended.



The supervisor checked the recorded measurements and compared the finished bolts with the approved sample.



The first few were consistent.



The next several were also consistent.



Then one bolt showed a slight difference in edge tension.



The supervisor examined it again.



The difference was small.



It was also outside the recorded standard.



He called the senior finisher over.



“This one needs correction.”



The worker looked at it.



“It is barely different.”



“It is still different.”



“The buyer may not notice.”



“The buyer's specification does not say that the difference is acceptable.”



The worker frowned.



The supervisor placed the bolt beside the sample.



Mingzhao, who had been checking the wage ledger, approached.



“What happened?”



The supervisor explained.



Mingzhao examined the bolt.



The difference was visible when compared directly with the sample.



She looked at the senior finisher.



“Why was the tension changed?”



“It was not changed deliberately.”



“Then what caused it?”



The worker examined the edge.



“I may have pulled it too firmly.”



“Can it be corrected?”



“Yes.”



“Then correct it.”



The worker took the bolt away.



Mingzhao turned to the supervisor.



“Restore full inspection for the next group.”



The supervisor nodded.



Lin Shuyun looked at the record.



“That means the reduced inspection has ended after one group.”



“It means the group did not meet the condition.”



“The first several bolts were correct.”



“The group was not.”



Lin looked toward the senior finisher.



He was already correcting the bolt.



“He will be unhappy.”



“He may be unhappy.”



“He has worked here for twenty years.”



“And he has worked under this order for less than a month.”



Lin sighed.



“You are very difficult to persuade.”



Mingzhao looked at the inspection sheet.



“I am willing to be persuaded by a consistent result.”



The worker returned with the corrected bolt.



The supervisor checked it.



It passed.



The group was accepted internally after the correction.



The reduced inspection arrangement would not be used for the next group.



The senior finisher did not complain again.



He was not pleased.



But he understood the reason.

Later that afternoon, Lin and Mingzhao reviewed the production figures.



The order now stood at:

- Forty-eight bolts accepted by the buyer.
- Forty-eight bolts completed and internally inspected, awaiting buyer inspection.
- One hundred and four bolts remaining to be produced.

The four additional workers had completed another day of production.



The trainees' work was recorded separately.



The senior finisher's trial group had required one correction.



No material had been written off.



Lin examined the figures.



“We are still ahead of the original production schedule.”



“By how much?”



“Less than yesterday.”



“Why?”



“The inspection took longer.”



“Then the schedule should reflect it.”



Lin sighed.



“I know.”



Mingzhao checked the wages.



The training costs were higher than the original estimate, but the trainees were becoming more productive.



The revised folding method had reduced handling time for experienced workers, but the additional inspection and supervision had offset some of the savings.



The overall cost remained uncertain.



Lin placed the ledger down.



“We may need to revise the projected profit.”



“We should not have treated the early estimate as final.”



“It was a projection.”



“Then it should remain a projection.”



Lin looked at her.



“Do you ever allow yourself to be pleased with progress?”



Mingzhao considered the question.



“Yes.”



“When?”



“When the progress is real.”



Lin laughed.



“That is an extremely narrow opportunity.”



“It is sufficient.”

In the palace, Yuru and Fang Ru set out for the longer walking route.



The old pavilion stood beside a quiet section of the western grounds.



Its wooden railings had been repaired recently, and the stone path leading to it was clean.



Yuru walked slowly.



Fang Ru remained beside her.



The two attendants followed at a distance.



They reached the three low steps.



Yuru looked at them.



“They are lower than I expected.”



Fang Ru nodded.



“Would you like to rest?”



“No.”



They continued toward the pavilion.



A gardener was working near the far side of the path.



He bowed when he saw them.



Yuru returned the greeting with a small nod.



The pavilion was empty.



Yuru sat on the wooden bench and looked out over the trees.



For several moments, neither she nor Fang Ru spoke.



Then Fang Ru said, “The path is longer, but quieter.”



“Yes.”



“Would you like to come here again?”



“Perhaps.”



Fang Ru looked toward the garden.



“Madam, do you miss the main palace?”



Yuru considered the question.



“I miss some people.”



“And the work?”



“Some of it.”



Fang Ru waited.



Yuru rested her hands in her lap.



“I miss knowing that a problem has been resolved. I do not miss being expected to resolve every problem simply because I am present.”



Fang Ru nodded.



The distinction had become familiar.



Yuru looked toward the trees.



“I spent many years believing that if I could do something, I should do it.”



“Was that wrong?”



“Not always.”



She paused.



“But it was not always wise.”



Fang Ru did not argue.



They sat for a little longer.



Then Yuru stood.



“Let us return.”



The walk had taken less than an hour.



No official had approached them.



No documents had been delivered.



The palace had continued to function.

That evening, Mingyuan received Steward Zhou's report concerning the new purchasing arrangement.



The general purchasing register would remain under the steward's supervision, with a designated clerk responsible for maintaining the consolidated entries.



Mingyuan read the report.



“Who will be the clerk?”



“Liang Wen, from the accounts office.”



“Has he handled purchasing records before?”



“He has maintained the warehouse issue register for two years.”



“Then he knows the process?”



“He knows the records. He will still require supervision.”



Mingyuan nodded.



“Arrange it.”



Steward Zhou bowed.



Mingyuan looked at the report again.



It was the first time he had seen a household responsibility transferred to someone else through an actual appointment rather than an assumption that Mingzhao would continue doing it.



The arrangement was not perfect.



Liang Wen would need training.



Steward Zhou would have additional work.



Mingyuan would have to review the first few reports.



But it was a beginning.



He closed the paper.



“Send the appointment notice to the household departments.”



“Yes, Young Master.”

At the western guest residence, Yuru received no further messages that evening.



She ate dinner with Fang Ru and two attendants.



The meal was simple.



Afterward, she returned to her room and opened the letter she had begun writing to her mother.



She added a few lines about the longer walking route.



She did not describe the pavilion in detail.



She merely wrote that the path was pleasant and that she might walk there again.



Then she folded the letter.



Fang Ru collected it.



“Shall it be sent tomorrow?”



“Yes.”



Yuru looked toward the window.



The evening was quiet.



In the palace, the Crown Prince was still reviewing records.



At the Marquisate, Mingyuan was learning to appoint people to responsibilities instead of expecting his sister to carry them.



At the workshop, Lin was recalculating costs and the senior finisher was correcting his method.



And Yuru had walked to a pavilion without anyone asking her to bring a register.



She extinguished the lamp.



The day had not been remarkable.



That was becoming one of its better qualities.
