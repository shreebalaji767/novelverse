# **Chapter 81: The Former Empress Has Decided That Her Father's Next Book Can Wait. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Sent Three Corrections to Her Family. The Workshop Has Invited the Buyer to Inspect Fifty-Six Bolts and Has Discovered That an Invitation Is Not the Same as Acceptance.**

**15th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The buyer arrived at the workshop shortly after the morning inspection began.



Zhou Jian had brought two assistants and a written copy of the agreed specifications. He had also brought the original sample approved by Zhou Wenqi.



Mingzhao was already present.



She had arrived before the workers began their final preparations. The workshop floor had been swept, the accepted batches were arranged separately, and the remaining fifty-six bolts were displayed in two groups.



The first group of twenty-eight had completed internal inspection the previous day.



The second group had undergone its final corrections that morning.



The supervisor stood beside the inspection table.



“Both groups are ready,” he reported.



“Have the records been checked?” Mingzhao asked.



“Yes.”



“Are the correction sheets attached?”



“Yes.”



“Has every bolt been marked with its batch number?”



“Yes.”



Mingzhao examined the arrangement.



The buyer's approved sample lay on a clean piece of cloth. The written instructions were placed beside it, along with the original and revised folding samples.



Zhou Jian entered the workshop.



“Miss Shen.”



“Mr. Zhou.”



He greeted her and looked at the prepared goods.



“I received your message yesterday. I am glad the remaining quantity is ready.”



“We have completed the internal inspection. The goods are available for your examination.”



Zhou Jian nodded.



He did not immediately begin inspecting.



Instead, he asked to see the production records.



Mingzhao handed them over.



He examined the batch numbers, the internal inspection sheets, and the correction entries. His assistant compared the recorded quantities with the goods displayed on the tables.



The first group contained twenty-eight bolts.



The second group also contained twenty-eight.



The numbers matched.



Zhou Jian then began the physical inspection.



The revised folding method had been approved by Zhou Wenqi on the condition that its specifications remained consistent throughout the order. The buyer's representative compared the edges, folds, tension, and finishing against the approved sample.



He examined the first bolt.



Then the second.



Then several more from different positions in the group.



The first group passed.



The second group took longer.



One bolt had a slightly uneven edge. It was not damaged, but its appearance differed from the sample enough to require adjustment.



The workshop supervisor stepped forward.



“This one was corrected during internal inspection,” he said. “The edge was adjusted afterward.”



Zhou Jian examined the correction sheet.



“Was the bolt inspected again?”



“Yes.”



“By whom?”



The supervisor identified the worker and the person who had checked the correction.



Zhou Jian examined the bolt once more.



After a moment, he placed it beside the approved sample.



“This is acceptable.”



The inspection continued.



Another bolt had a fold that was slightly tighter than the others. Zhou Jian compared it with the sample and asked whether the variation had been intentional.



“No,” the supervisor replied. “It was a finishing error. The worker corrected it.”



“Was the same worker responsible for the earlier uneven fold?”



“No. This was a separate correction.”



Zhou Jian looked at the record.



He did not appear entirely satisfied with the frequency of corrections, but he did not reject the goods merely because corrections had occurred.



He continued examining the remaining bolts.



By midday, the inspection was complete.



All fifty-six passed.



Zhou Jian signed the acceptance record.

Buyer accepted: 56 bolts

Mingzhao examined the signed document.



The previous one hundred and forty-four bolts had already been accepted.



The final fifty-six were now accepted as well.



The full two-hundred-bolt order had passed the buyer's inspection.



Qiao Yue, standing behind Mingzhao, allowed herself a quiet smile.



“Finished,” she whispered.



“Inspected and accepted,” Mingzhao corrected.



Qiao Yue looked at her.



“Must you?”



“Yes.”



The remaining payment still had to be settled according to the contract. The goods had to be prepared for dispatch, and transport arrangements had to be completed.



But the production and inspection stage was finished.



Mingzhao signed the workshop's acknowledgment.



She did not congratulate herself.



She thanked the supervisor and the workers, then instructed the accounts clerk to prepare the final production summary.



The order had been completed without losing any material.



That was worth recording accurately.

The buyer's representative remained for a short discussion about transport.



The original contract had specified the delivery arrangements, but the final dispatch date depended on the carrier's availability.



Zhou Jian suggested that the goods be packed in two consignments to reduce the risk of handling damage.



Mingzhao asked whether the buyer would accept two separate delivery records.



“Certainly,” he replied. “The quantities can be listed separately.”



“And the payment?”



“The remaining balance will be released according to the agreed delivery terms.”



Mingzhao nodded.



“Then please confirm the dispatch dates in writing.”



Zhou Jian agreed.



He did not object.



The discussion took less time than the inspection.



When he left, Qiao Yue looked at the signed acceptance document.



“Two hundred bolts.”



“Two hundred bolts accepted.”



“After weeks of production.”



“Yes.”



“Four additional workers.”



“Yes.”



“Two trainees.”



“Yes.”



“Several corrections.”



“Corrected and recorded.”



“Then we have done well.”



Mingzhao looked at the workshop.



The workers were beginning to clear the inspection tables. The supervisor was collecting the records. The finished bolts were being arranged for packing.



“Yes,” she said. “We have done well.”



It was not necessary to avoid acknowledging a success simply because the accounts had not yet been closed.



She could recognize what had been accomplished without inventing a final profit.

The former Empress had spent the morning deciding which book to read next.



Her father's new selection had not yet arrived.



She had no intention of making a special request for it.



Instead, she chose a collection of essays from the small stack she had brought from the Xu residence.



The subject was the management of charitable granaries.



She read the first essay, then stopped to consider a passage about the difference between grain stored for emergency relief and grain allocated for ordinary distribution.



It reminded her of several discussions from her years in the palace.



For a moment, she considered whether the imperial household's own storage records might benefit from a similar classification.



Then she closed the thought.



The imperial household had its administrators.



They were responsible for their own records.



She was no longer the person who needed to improve every system she encountered.



Fang Ru entered with a small tray.



“Madam, the kitchen has prepared a light lunch.”



Yuru looked at the tray.



“Thank you.”



Fang Ru set it down.



“Would you like to take a walk after lunch?”



“Perhaps.”



“Along the western path?”



“Perhaps somewhere else.”



Fang Ru smiled.



“Then we shall decide when the time comes.”



Yuru returned to the essay.



She had not decided whether she would visit her parents again this week. She had not chosen a permanent residence. Her personal belongings remained partly in the guest residence and partly at the Xu estate.



These matters could be decided when necessary.



There was no deadline attached to every choice.

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan received the workshop's acceptance report through the commercial correspondence office.



He was pleased to hear that the two-hundred-bolt order had passed inspection.



He also noticed that the report was unusually precise.



It identified the total quantity, the two production groups, the corrections made, the internal inspections, and the buyer's final acceptance.



Mingyuan read it once, then placed it beside the purchasing procedure.



For several moments, he considered how different the document was from the old estate records.



The workshop had recorded what had been produced.



It had recorded what had been corrected.



It had recorded who had checked the corrections.



It had recorded what the buyer had accepted.



There was little room for confusion.



He wondered how many disputes in the Marquisate might have been avoided if its records had followed similar standards.



Steward Zhou entered.



“Young Master, the revised procedure is ready for circulation.”



Mingyuan looked up.



“Have Liang Wen included the three changes?”



“Yes. The receiving discrepancy must be checked by someone other than the original recorder whenever possible. Missing documents require a separate explanatory record. Exceptions must be approved and documented.”



“Good.”



“The staff will need to be informed.”



“Arrange a meeting with the relevant clerks and stewards.”



Steward Zhou nodded.



“Should we invite Miss Mingzhao?”



“No.”



The answer came more quickly than Steward Zhou expected.



Mingyuan continued.



“She reviewed the document. She has already made her contribution. We should not turn that into an obligation to supervise the meeting.”



Steward Zhou looked at him carefully.



“You have understood her position.”



“I am trying to.”



The steward nodded.



Mingyuan took the final copy.



The purchasing procedure was now ready to be introduced.



It would not immediately solve every problem.



The clerks would need to learn the new requirements. The stewards would need to follow them. The accounts office would need to maintain the records.



And the old payment, the grain discrepancy, and the property division would still require separate examination.



But the estate would at least have a written framework for future transactions.



Mingyuan placed the document in the outgoing correspondence tray.



“Send Miss Mingzhao a copy of the final version,” he said. “Inform her that her corrections have been incorporated.”



Steward Zhou nodded.



Mingyuan added, “Do not ask her to attend the implementation meeting.”



“I will make sure the letter says so.”

Mingzhao received the final procedure late in the afternoon.



She read the covering note first.



Mingyuan had thanked her for the corrections and confirmed that all three had been incorporated.



He also stated that the procedure would be introduced by the Marquisate's own staff.



Mingzhao read the note again.



Qiao Yue stood nearby.



“Has he asked you to attend?”



“No.”



“Good.”



Mingzhao examined the final document.



The revised clauses were clear.



The receiving discrepancy would be independently checked where possible. Missing vouchers would require a separate explanatory record, not a fabricated replacement. Exceptions would be documented and approved by the appropriate supervising official.



She placed the document aside.



“That is acceptable.”



“Will you send a reply?”



“I will acknowledge receipt.”



Qiao Yue brought the writing paper.



Mingzhao wrote a short note thanking Mingyuan for incorporating the corrections. She stated that the final document reflected the points she had raised and wished the staff success in implementing it.



She did not offer to attend the meeting.



She did not offer to train the clerks.



She did not offer to review the first month's records.



The letter was sealed.



Qiao Yue carried it to the outgoing tray.



When she returned, Mingzhao had already opened the workshop's final cost ledger.



The order's production stage had ended.



Now she needed to determine what it had actually cost.

The workshop's accounts were brought to the residence the following morning's preliminary summary, but Mingzhao began examining them that evening.



The clerk had separated the figures into categories.

- Silk and other raw materials.
- Regular workshop wages.
- Additional workers' wages.
- Training time.
- Supervision.
- Corrections and reinspection.
- Packing materials.
- Transport preparations.

Mingzhao checked the totals against the original records.



She found one training payment entered under ordinary wages.



It was not a major error, but it affected the calculation of the additional labour costs.



She marked it for correction.



Qiao Yue looked at the page.



“Does that change the total expenditure?”



“It changes the classification. The total payment remains the same.”



“Then the amount is still correct.”



“The amount is correct. The category is not.”



Qiao Yue considered this.



“Will it affect the profit?”



“It may affect how we compare this order with future orders. We need to know what training actually costs.”



She turned to the supervision expenses.



The four additional workers had required more attention than the original estimate suggested. The two trainees had improved, but their early production had been slower, and the supervisor had spent more time checking their work.



The experienced workers had also required additional oversight during the transition to the revised folding method.



The buyer had accepted the method.



That did not mean the method had been free to implement.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



“Sun Qiming should review the summary before we calculate the final result.”



Qiao Yue nodded.



“Then we still do not know the profit.”



“Not yet.”



Qiao Yue sighed.



Mingzhao gave her a brief look.



“Would you prefer that I invent one?”



“No.”



“Then we wait.”

In the palace, Yuru received a small message from the Xu residence.



Her father had added a note to her mother's letter.



He had written that the second book was ready whenever she wished to borrow it. He had also mentioned that the first book should not be returned in haste.



Yuru smiled as she read it.



Fang Ru looked over.



“Has your father changed his mind?”



“No. He has simply decided that I need not hurry.”



“Then perhaps he understands.”



“He usually does.”



Yuru folded the note.



She would visit the Xu residence again.



She would return the first book, borrow the second, and perhaps stay for a meal.



There was no need to settle the date immediately.



She had spent many years arranging visits around ceremonies, reports, and official obligations.



Now she could visit her parents because she wanted to see them.



That was a pleasant reason.

The day ended with two different kinds of completion.



The workshop had completed its two-hundred-bolt order and obtained the buyer's acceptance for every bolt.



The Marquisate had completed its purchasing procedure and prepared to introduce it without relying on Mingzhao.



Neither had completed every remaining task.



The workshop still had to dispatch the goods, settle the final payment, and calculate the net result.



The Marquisate still had to examine its old accounts, resolve the grain discrepancy, and complete the property division.



Mingzhao had completed the review she had promised.



Yuru had completed another quiet day without being asked to manage the palace.



The capital continued its business.



The palace continued its administration.



The Marquisate continued its accounting.



And somewhere in the eastern market, two hundred bolts of silk waited to be packed and delivered.



For the first time in weeks, the workshop had nothing left to manufacture for that particular contract.



The workers were pleased.



The supervisor was relieved.



The accounts clerk was nervous.



Mingzhao, after examining the records, was simply waiting for Sun Qiming to tell her how much the entire undertaking had cost.



She had discovered that finishing an order was considerably easier than proving that it had been profitable.
