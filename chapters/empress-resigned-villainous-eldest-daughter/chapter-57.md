# **Chapter 57: The Former Empress Has Arrived at the Imperial Gardens Without a Single Report. The Emperor Has Arrived Without a Single Report. The Empress Dowager Has Arrived Without a Single Report. The Chief Eunuch Has Begun to Wonder Whether the Palace Can Survive an Entire Afternoon Without One.**

**21st Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The Imperial Gardens had been prepared for an ordinary visit.



This was not an easy achievement.



The pavilion had been swept. The paths had been checked. The tea had been arranged. Fruit had been placed on a small table. The cushions had been inspected and then inspected again by someone who had been told not to inspect them.



No ceremonial banners had been hung.



No musicians had been summoned.



No officials had been invited to stand in a line and demonstrate that the visit was informal.



Chief Eunuch Gao Shun had personally confirmed all of this.



He had also confirmed that the garden maintenance records remained in the Works Office.



At the appointed hour, Emperor Zhao Jingheng arrived.



He wore an ordinary robe.



He had no memorials in his hands.



Gao Shun bowed.



"Your Majesty."



The Emperor glanced toward the pavilion.



"Has anyone brought documents?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



The Emperor walked forward.



A moment later, he turned.



"Has anyone brought documents disguised as a personal message?"



Gao Shun's expression did not change.



"No, Your Majesty."



"Good."



The Emperor continued toward the pavilion.



Gao Shun followed at a respectful distance.



He had learned that the Emperor's greatest danger during an informal visit was not the absence of documents.



It was the possibility that someone might discover an opportunity to discuss them.

Empress Dowager Wei arrived shortly afterward.



She wore a dark green robe and carried no ceremonial accessories beyond those appropriate to her rank.



Her attendants remained at a distance.



She looked at the pavilion, the path, and the tea table.



Then she asked Gao Shun, "Where are the reports?"



Gao Shun bowed.



"In the Works Office, Your Majesty."



The Dowager looked at him.



"Why?"



"The Emperor instructed that no documents be brought."



She considered this.



Then she nodded.



"Very well."



Gao Shun had survived the first question.



The second was more difficult.



"Has the former Empress arrived?"



"Not yet, Your Majesty."



The Dowager sat down.



"Then we shall wait."



She looked at the fruit.



"Who selected it?"



"The palace kitchens prepared it."



"Was the selection made according to the usual list?"



Gao Shun paused.



The Emperor, who had been examining the garden path, turned his head.



"Mother."



The Dowager looked at him.



"I was asking about fruit."



"Today we are not reviewing fruit procurement."



The Dowager's expression became thoughtful.



Then she picked up a piece of pear.



"Then I shall eat it without reviewing procurement."



The Emperor nodded.



Gao Shun lowered his eyes.



He had not expected the afternoon to proceed so smoothly.



He was already suspicious.

Xu Yuru arrived without a ceremonial procession.



Her carriage stopped near the garden entrance.



Fang Ru helped her down.



The former Empress wore a light blue robe and comfortable walking shoes. Her hair was arranged simply, and there were no phoenix ornaments.



She carried a small packet of dried fruit.



The Emperor saw it immediately.



"What is that?"



"My mother's gift."



The Dowager looked at the packet.



"Your mother sent you fruit?"



"Yes."



"Then it must be good."



Xu Yuru smiled.



"It is ordinary dried fruit."



The Dowager nodded.



"Ordinary things are often better than ceremonial ones."



The Emperor looked toward the pavilion.



"Shall we walk?"



Xu Yuru glanced at the path.



"Yes."



Gao Shun moved forward.



The Emperor raised one hand.



"No attendants need to lead us."



Gao Shun stopped.



The Dowager stood.



Xu Yuru began walking.



For several steps, the three imperial family members proceeded without speaking.



The garden was quiet.



A small pond lay beyond the path. The water reflected the trees and the pale morning sky. A gardener was working at a distance, but he had been instructed not to approach unless required.



Xu Yuru looked at the pond.



"The water is clear."



The Emperor nodded.



"The Works Office has maintained it well."



The Dowager glanced at him.



"That is a report."



The Emperor stopped.



Xu Yuru looked at both of them.



Then she said, "The water is clear. That is an observation."



The Dowager considered the distinction.



"Very well."



They continued walking.



The Emperor had ruled for eighteen years.



He had attended ceremonies, received ministers, inspected military reports, heard disputes, and negotiated with men who considered silence a weakness.



He was not accustomed to walking beside two women who could identify an administrative discussion before it had fully begun.



The Dowager was equally unaccustomed to being prevented from turning a pleasant afternoon into a review of institutional duties.



Xu Yuru, meanwhile, was enjoying the path.



It had no petitions.



It had no ledgers.



It had no one waiting at the end to ask her to resolve a dispute between two departments that had both written the same expense under different headings.



She walked at a comfortable pace.



The Emperor noticed.



"Are the shoes comfortable?"



"Yes."



"Good."



The Dowager looked at him.



"That is a sensible question."



The Emperor did not reply.

They reached the pavilion.



Tea had been prepared.



The fruit was arranged in small dishes.



There were no documents on the table.



Xu Yuru sat down.



The Dowager took the seat beside her.



The Emperor sat opposite them.



For a moment, everyone looked at the tea.



Gao Shun stood nearby.



He had the expression of a man waiting for an emergency that had been forbidden to occur.



The Emperor lifted his cup.



"How have you been?"



Xu Yuru answered honestly.



"Quietly."



The Emperor nodded.



"That is good."



The Dowager picked up a piece of fruit.



"Have you chosen a permanent residence?"



"Not yet."



"Your father's eastern courtyard remains available."



"I know."



"Your mother would be pleased to have you nearby."



"I know that too."



The Dowager paused.



"Then why have you not decided?"



Xu Yuru set down her cup.



"Because I have not decided."



The answer was simple.



It was also complete.



The Dowager studied her.



For years, Yuru had answered questions before they were asked. She had anticipated needs, corrected mistakes, and arranged solutions.



Now she was allowing a question to remain unanswered until she had a reason to answer it.



The Dowager found this difficult.



She also found it understandable.



"Very well," she said.



The Emperor looked at the fruit.



"Your mother sent you something?"



Xu Yuru placed the packet on the table.



"Yes."



The Dowager opened it.



Inside were several pieces of dried fruit, wrapped carefully in plain paper.



The Dowager examined them.



"These are not palace fruit."



"No."



"They are from the Xu residence."



"Yes."



The Dowager selected one.



The Emperor selected another.



Gao Shun watched.



He had spent the morning ensuring that the palace fruit had been prepared according to ordinary arrangements.



Now the Emperor was eating fruit from the former Empress's mother's kitchen.



No official had been consulted.



No record had been required.



Gao Shun felt a strange sense of relief.



Perhaps the palace could survive.

The conversation moved to ordinary matters.



The Dowager asked whether Xu Yuru had been sleeping well.



The Emperor asked whether the guest residence was comfortable.



Xu Yuru answered both questions.



She had slept well.



The residence was comfortable enough for the time being.



She had not decided whether she would remain there.



The Dowager did not press.



The Emperor did not ask for a date.



They drank tea.



A breeze moved through the trees.



The Dowager looked toward the pond.



"When I was young, I disliked gardens."



Xu Yuru looked at her.



"Why?"



"Too many people insisted that I admire them."



The Emperor smiled.



"Mother has always disliked being instructed in what to admire."



The Dowager looked at him.



"Your Majesty has learned this very late."



The Emperor accepted the criticism.



Xu Yuru smiled into her cup.



For a short while, they spoke about the old palace, the changing seasons, and the trees that had grown taller since the Dowager first entered the Imperial City.



The conversation did not require a decision.



It did not require an official record.



It did not even require Gao Shun to stand closer.



This was unfamiliar.



It was pleasant.

At the eastern market, Shen Mingzhao received Zhou Wenqi's reply.



The buyer had examined the revised folding sample after storing it overnight.



He accepted that the method was faster.



He also accepted the altered edge appearance, provided that the workshop maintained the same standard across the entire order.



The approval was conditional.



The method could be used for the new order after the workshop recorded the agreed specifications and confirmed that the trainees could follow them consistently.



Mingzhao read the letter twice.



Lin Shuyun stood beside her.



"Approved?"



"Conditionally."



"That is better than refusal."



"It is also more work than approval."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



Mingzhao placed the letter on the table.



The senior finisher was summoned.



He arrived with the revised sample.



Mingzhao handed him the buyer's reply.



He read it.



His expression changed.



"Then we may use the revised method?"



"After the specifications are recorded."



He nodded.



"And the trainees?"



"They must demonstrate consistency."



The finisher considered this.



"They have improved."



"Improvement is not yet consistency."



He bowed.



Mingzhao looked at the sample.



The buyer's condition was reasonable.



The method was faster, but speed alone did not establish quality.



If the workshop adopted it without recording the details, the next batch might be folded differently. If the trainees were instructed verbally, one might remember the corner while the other remembered the edge. If the specifications were written after production began, the record might describe what had happened rather than what had been agreed.



Mingzhao had no intention of repeating that mistake.



"Prepare a written instruction," she said. "The original sample remains attached. The revised sample is marked separately. Record the handling time and the edge standard."



The finisher bowed.



"Yes, Miss Shen."



Lin Shuyun looked at the letter.



"The buyer has given us a useful answer."



Mingzhao nodded.



"Yes."



"And the workshop has a method that saves time."



"Yes."



"And the new order may now proceed more efficiently."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Do not call it profit."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



"I did not."



"You were about to."



"I was about to call it progress."



Mingzhao considered the word.



"That is acceptable."

At the Marquisate, Shen Mingxiu received the revised condition report for the southern building.



The roof required repairs before the heavier summer rains.



The estimate had been divided into materials, labour, and transport.



The report also recorded the condition of the supporting beams and the areas where water had entered during the previous rain.



Mingxiu read it carefully.



She had initially wanted the repair completed immediately.



Now she understood why the steward had requested a condition report.



A roof could be repaired badly.



A roof could be repaired unnecessarily.



A roof could be repaired at the correct time and still cost more than expected if the materials had not been measured.



She placed the report before Madam Liang.



"The work should be scheduled before the next heavy rain," she said.



Madam Liang examined the estimate.



"Has your father approved it?"



"Not yet."



"Then it cannot begin."



Mingxiu nodded.



She did not argue.



The report was complete enough to support a decision.



The decision had not yet been made.



She was learning that the two stages were different.



Across the estate, Shen Mingyuan received the same day's updated property schedule.



The western field was listed under temporary cultivation.



The old lease was not marked renewed.



The payment arrangement was recorded separately.



He checked the wording.



Then he signed the acknowledgment.



The steward took the document away.



Mingyuan looked at the empty table.



He had spent years believing that his sister's work was simply a collection of decisions.



Now he was beginning to understand how much effort had gone into making those decisions distinguishable from one another.



He still did not understand all of it.



But he had stopped assuming that he did.

In the Imperial Gardens, the Emperor rose after the tea had been finished.



The Dowager remained seated.



Xu Yuru looked toward the path.



"Shall we walk a little farther?"



The Emperor nodded.



They left the pavilion.



Gao Shun followed at a distance.



The former Empress walked beside the Dowager.



The Emperor walked on the other side.



No one carried documents.



No one asked about the Works Office.



No one discussed the palace's inventory.



At the edge of the pond, the Dowager stopped.



She looked at the water.



Then she said, "The garden is pleasant."



Xu Yuru smiled.



"Yes."



The Emperor glanced at Gao Shun.



Gao Shun bowed.



The visit had remained informal.



The palace had not collapsed.



No one had requested a report.



No one had appointed the former Empress to inspect the paths.



No one had asked her to settle a dispute over fruit.



The afternoon had been a success.



A quiet, ordinary, almost suspicious success.

When Xu Yuru returned to the guest residence, Fang Ru helped her remove the walking shoes.



The former Empress sat beside the window.



The garden visit had taken less than half a day.



She had spoken with the Emperor and the Dowager.



She had eaten fruit from her mother's kitchen.



She had walked without being followed by a stack of reports.



For several moments, she did not know what to do next.



Then she picked up a book.



Fang Ru looked at her.



"Madam, shall I prepare the evening tea?"



"Yes."



"Would you like the palace account of today's garden arrangements?"



Xu Yuru looked up.



"No."



Fang Ru bowed.



The former Empress opened the book.



The day had been pleasant.



It had also been entirely unnecessary for her to manage.



She considered this the most important part.



At the eastern market, Mingzhao was preparing the written instructions for the revised folding method.



At the Marquisate, Mingyuan was learning to distinguish temporary cultivation from a renewed lease.



Mingxiu was waiting for approval before ordering roof repairs.



In the palace, the Crown Prince was preserving unresolved entries instead of inventing totals.



The Dowager had eaten fruit without reviewing its procurement.



The Emperor had walked through a garden without bringing documents.



And Xu Yuru had returned to her room without being asked to approve the afternoon.



The Great Yong Dynasty had not become more efficient in a single day.



It had, however, managed to spend several hours without requiring the former Empress to prove that she was still useful.



For the moment, this was enough.
