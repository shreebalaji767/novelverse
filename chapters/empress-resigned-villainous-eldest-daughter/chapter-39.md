# **Chapter 39: The Former Empress Has Been Asked to Choose a Permanent Residence. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Asked to Choose a Permanent Property. Both Women Have Requested More Information Before Making an Expensive Decision.**

**1st Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The fifth month began with two letters.



One arrived at Yuru's temporary residence from the Duke of Xu.



The other arrived at Mingzhao's rented house from Master Luo.



Both contained proposals.



Both required decisions.



Neither woman was particularly pleased to receive them before breakfast.

Yuru read her father's letter first.



The Duke had written that the eastern courtyard at the Xu family residence could be prepared for her permanent use if she wished.



The rooms were comfortable.



The garden was private.



The servants could be arranged according to her preferences.



The Duke had also mentioned that a separate household account could be established for her personal expenses.



It was a generous offer.



It was also a permanent arrangement.



Yuru read it again.



Fang Ru placed the morning tea beside her.



"Has the Duke asked you to decide?"



"Not immediately."



"Then perhaps you can consider it."



Yuru nodded.



She looked at the letter.



The eastern courtyard was pleasant. Her parents were welcoming. She would have privacy and familiar surroundings.



But she did not know whether she wanted to live permanently in her natal family residence.



She had spent most of her adult life in the palace.



Leaving the position did not mean she had to exchange one household for another.



She folded the letter.



"Send my father a reply. Tell him I am grateful for the offer, but I would like to visit the courtyard again before deciding."



Fang Ru nodded.



"Should we ask whether the rooms can remain available?"



"No. If another family member needs them, they should use them."



Fang Ru looked at her.



"Then you do not wish to reserve them?"



"I do not want to reserve a place I may never use."



Fang Ru accepted the instruction.



Yuru returned to her tea.



She had spent years ensuring that other people had somewhere to live, somewhere to work, and enough supplies.



Choosing a room for herself ought to have been simple.



She was discovering that it was not.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao opened Master Luo's letter.



The property assessor had received an additional document from the owner of the northern canal house.



It was an older maintenance record.



The document described repairs to the drainage channel and referred to a boundary marker that no longer existed.



It did not establish the disputed boundary.



Master Luo proposed another inspection with the neighbouring owner present.



Mingzhao read the letter.



Qiao Yue, who was sorting correspondence, asked, "Is it the canal property?"



"Yes."



"Have they found the boundary?"



"No."



"Have they found the missing marker?"



"No."



"Then what have they found?"



"A maintenance record."



Qiao Yue looked disappointed.



Mingzhao continued reading.



The record might help establish the history of the drainage channel. It might also identify the period during which the boundary markers were moved or replaced.



But it did not establish ownership.



Mingzhao wrote a reply.



She agreed to another inspection, provided that the owner and neighbouring party both attended and that the findings were recorded separately from the valuation.



She also asked Master Luo to obtain a copy of the original land registration before making any recommendation.



Qiao Yue watched her seal the letter.



"Are you still interested in buying it?"



"Possibly."



"Even after all this trouble?"



"Especially because I now know there is trouble."



Qiao Yue blinked.



Mingzhao explained.



"If I buy a property without understanding its defects, I pay for the house and inherit the dispute. If I understand the dispute first, I can decide whether the property is worth the risk."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"That is sensible."



"Good."



"Then perhaps we should inspect the next house before deciding whether we like it."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"That is generally how property purchases work."



Qiao Yue smiled.

In the palace, the Emperor had received the first monthly summary prepared under the revised reporting arrangements.



The document was shorter.



It was also less decorative.



The Imperial Household Administration had recorded its expenditure by category. The Palace Stores had attached the inventory summary. The Department of Rites had provided the ceremonial requirements separately.



The emperor examined the report.



Gao Shun stood nearby.



"Has the information been reconciled?"



"Yes, Your Majesty. There are two minor discrepancies, both identified by the responsible offices."



"Have they been corrected?"



"One has. The other is awaiting confirmation from the Palace Stores."



The emperor nodded.



"Then leave it with them."



Gao Shun bowed.



The emperor turned a page.



He stopped at the section concerning household repairs.



"Why is the replacement of the eastern corridor lamps included under ceremonial expenditure?"



"The lamps were used during the previous month's ceremony."



"Are they used only for ceremonies?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Then why are they listed there?"



Gao Shun examined the entry.



"The department may have classified them according to their most recent use."



The emperor put down the report.



"Ask them to classify the purchase according to its ordinary purpose, not according to whichever ceremony happened to use it last."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



He returned the document.



Then he added, "Do not send this question to the former Empress."



Gao Shun bowed.



"I had not intended to."



The emperor looked at him.



"Good."

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Shen Mingyuan had found another problem.



It concerned a payment to a transport contractor.



The entry was dated several months before the northern warehouse discrepancy had been discovered.



The payment description referred to the movement of grain from the northern warehouse.



However, the destination field was blank.



Mingyuan compared the entry with the old warehouse ledger.



The amount did not match the later transport instruction.



He called Steward Zhou.



"Was this payment related to the twenty sacks sent toward southern storage?"



The steward examined the date.



"I cannot say."



"Could it have been a separate delivery?"



"Possibly."



"Is there a receiving record?"



"Not among the documents I have."



Mingyuan looked at the ledger.



"Then we should not combine it with the other movement."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"That is correct."



Mingyuan made a note.



*Separate transport payment. Destination and quantity unverified. Do not include in the twenty-sack movement without supporting record.*



He then placed the document in the investigation folder.



It was not an answer.



But it prevented a new assumption from being mistaken for an established fact.

Mingxiu entered the study carrying a small bundle of receipts.



"Brother, I found the missing purchase record."



Mingyuan looked up.



"Which one?"



"The kitchen oil delivery."



She placed the receipts on the table.



The first recorded the purchase.



The second recorded delivery.



The third was a payment acknowledgment.



Mingyuan examined them.



"These match."



"Yes."



"Why were they separated?"



"The kitchen clerk filed the payment acknowledgment under general household expenditure."



Mingyuan looked at her.



"Can you ask him to attach a copy to the purchase record?"



"I already did."



He nodded.



Mingxiu sat down.



"Are you still examining the transport records?"



"Yes."



"Have you found the missing destination?"



"No."



She looked at the papers.



"Could the contractor remember?"



"He was asked. He remembers several journeys, but not the exact number of sacks or the final destination."



"Then we need another record."



"Yes."



Mingxiu nodded.



She had once believed that managing a household meant deciding which expenses were acceptable.



Now she understood that decisions were only one part of the work.



The records had to support them.

At Yuru's residence, Fang Ru brought a second letter.



This one came from the Crown Prince.



He asked whether Yuru would be willing to discuss the revised Eastern Palace reporting format.



Yuru read it.



She smiled.



"At least he has asked properly."



Fang Ru looked at the letter.



"Will you meet him?"



"Yes. He is asking for personal advice, not asking me to approve an office report."



She wrote a short reply inviting him to visit that afternoon.



Fang Ru accepted it.



Then she asked, "Would you like the study prepared?"



"Yes."



"Should I bring the old Eastern Palace accounts?"



"No."



Fang Ru looked at her.



Yuru smiled.



"We are discussing his proposed format. He should bring the information he needs to explain his question."



Fang Ru nodded.

The Crown Prince arrived in the afternoon.



He carried a notebook and several sample forms.



After greeting Yuru, he placed them on the table.



"I have asked the Eastern Palace offices to prepare separate reports, but the formats differ."



Yuru examined the papers.



"Show me how they differ."



The Crown Prince explained.



The food office recorded quantity, unit price, and delivery date.



The clothing office recorded the recipient, material, and issue date.



The transport office recorded route, purpose, and payment.



The maintenance office recorded repairs, materials, and labour.



"These are different kinds of work," Yuru said.



"Yes."



"Then they should not necessarily have identical forms."



The Crown Prince looked at the documents.



"But the monthly summary needs to be consistent."



"That is where you need a common summary, not identical original records."



She took a blank sheet.



"Each office should maintain the details relevant to its own work. The monthly summary should collect the information necessary for comparison."



The Crown Prince considered this.



"Which information?"



"Total expenditure, category, responsible office, supporting record, and any outstanding discrepancy. You may also need quantities or delivery totals where relevant."



He wrote it down.



Yuru continued.



"Do not remove useful information merely to make the forms look alike."



"Understood."



"And do not ask every office to submit a report simply because you enjoy receiving reports."



The Crown Prince looked at her.



"I do not enjoy receiving reports."



Yuru raised an eyebrow.



He paused.



"Not all reports."



She laughed.



The Crown Prince smiled.



He had learned that a well-organized report was useful.



He was still learning that an unnecessary report was merely a well-organized inconvenience.

At the Marquisate, Shen Guowei received a message from Sun Qiming.



The accountant had reviewed the latest transport entries.



The separate payment identified by Mingyuan could not yet be connected to the twenty-sack movement.



The destination remained unknown.



Sun Qiming also confirmed that the roadside storage yard register had not yet been located in full.



A former business associate had agreed to provide information, but no delivery record had been produced.



Shen Guowei read the report.



He looked at the open account book.



For years, he had expected a question about the estate to be answered quickly.



Now every answer seemed to produce another question.



He called Steward Zhou.



"Has Mingyuan seen this?"



"Yes, Marquis."



"What does he propose?"



"He wishes to keep the payment separate until the supporting records are found."



Shen Guowei nodded.



"That is correct."



He placed the report aside.



Then he looked at the next document.



It concerned a routine repair to the western granary.



He called the steward again.



"Who is responsible for this repair?"



"Steward Zhou's office, under the estate maintenance allocation."



"Then why is it awaiting my approval?"



The steward looked at the document.



"The repair estimate exceeds the clerk's usual approval limit."



"Is it within the approved maintenance allocation?"



"Yes."



"Then follow the existing procedure."



Steward Zhou bowed.



Shen Guowei returned to his work.



He had begun to understand that a household did not need its Marquis to approve every repair simply because he was the Marquis.

In the evening, Mingzhao received a message from the landlord.



The rear drainage channel had been inspected.



The landlord agreed that a section required cleaning and minor repair.



He proposed completing the work within three days.



Mingzhao examined the note.



The work was part of the landlord's maintenance responsibility under the rental agreement.



She replied that the repair could proceed, provided that the workers did not disturb the tenant's stored belongings.



Qiao Yue read the message.



"Will the drain be fixed?"



"It should be."



"And the lower room?"



"We will observe it after the next rain."



Qiao Yue looked at the damp patch near the wall.



"At least the landlord is responding."



"Yes."



"Perhaps we should stay here."



Mingzhao glanced at her.



"We have not decided."



"But the drain may be repaired."



"That does not resolve the other questions."



Qiao Yue nodded.



She had forgotten that the residence was temporary.



Mingzhao returned to her ledger.

At the Xu family residence, the Duke was examining a revised household account.



The accountant had corrected the classification of the ornamental stones.



The twelve-tael purchase was now recorded under garden purchases rather than repairs.



The supporting receipt was attached.



The Duke examined it.



Then he looked at the next page.



There was a payment for the replacement of several roof tiles.



He called the steward.



"Who approved this repair?"



"The household maintenance office."



"Was it necessary?"



"The old tiles were damaged."



"Was the cost within the approved allocation?"



"Yes."



"Then why was it delayed?"



The steward hesitated.



"The office was waiting for your confirmation."



The Duke frowned.



"Was my confirmation required?"



"No, Your Excellency."



"Then why did they wait?"



The steward lowered his head.



"I believe they wished to be cautious."



The Duke put down the document.



"Being cautious does not mean waiting for an unnecessary signature."



The steward bowed.



The Duke looked at the corrected accounts.



He was slowly discovering that the problem Yuru had faced in the palace was not unique to the palace.



Whenever one person became responsible for too many decisions, everyone else gradually stopped making the decisions that belonged to them.

The following morning, Yuru received a reply from her father.



He thanked her for considering the eastern courtyard and said she could inspect it again whenever convenient.



He also mentioned that the household accountant had completed the revised monthly summary.



There was no request for her to review it.



Yuru smiled.



She placed the letter beside her books.



Fang Ru noticed.



"Is the Duke's household improving?"



"Perhaps."



"Will you visit again?"



"Yes."



"Will you stay?"



"I have not decided."



Fang Ru nodded.



Then she asked, "Do you wish to inspect the courtyard again?"



Yuru considered it.



"Yes. But I would like to see the rooms in the morning. The light is different then."



Fang Ru accepted the instruction.



Yuru returned to her reading.



She was beginning to consider her future in terms of what she wished to do, rather than what other people expected her to continue doing.



It was an unfamiliar way of making plans.



She intended to become accustomed to it.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received a second letter from Master Luo.



The neighbouring owner had agreed to attend another inspection of the northern canal property.



The proposed date was the third day of the fifth month.



Mingzhao accepted.



She also asked Master Luo to bring the survey assistant and the available registration documents.



Qiao Yue looked at the date.



"That is soon."



"Yes."



"Do you think the boundary will be established?"



"I do not know."



"Would you buy the house if it is?"



"That depends on the final valuation and repair costs."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"Then we still have several decisions."



"Yes."



Mingzhao closed the letter.



She had learned something from the first inspection.



A house could be attractive.



A price could be reasonable.



A seller could be agreeable.



None of those things established the legal boundary.



She would not buy a disputed property merely because she was tired of searching.

On the first day of the fifth month, the palace had completed another round of routine reporting.



The Qingyuan Marquisate had separated another transport payment from the unresolved grain movement.



The Xu household had corrected its maintenance accounts.



Mingzhao had received confirmation of the next property inspection.



Yuru had postponed her permanent residence decision.



No one had been forced to make an expensive choice before obtaining the necessary information.



This was not a particularly dramatic beginning to the fifth month.



It was, however, a useful one.



The former Empress had not accepted a permanent residence merely because her family had prepared one.



The Marquis's eldest daughter had not purchased a property merely because its owner had offered it.



And both women had discovered that the ability to say *not yet* was sometimes more valuable than the ability to make an immediate decision.
