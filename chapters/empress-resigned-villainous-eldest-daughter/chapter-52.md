# **Chapter 52: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Choose a Day for Breakfast. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Approve a Contract. One Family Has Asked Whether She Prefers Sweet Porridge. The Other Has Asked Whether She Prefers Payment in Two Installments.**

**14th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The first letter Yuru received that morning came from her mother.



It contained three possible dates for breakfast.



The second letter came from the palace.



It contained a request for clarification about the transfer of several personal furnishings.



Yuru read both.



She placed the palace letter on the left side of her desk and her mother's letter on the right.



Fang Ru watched her.



"Which one will you answer first?"



"The breakfast invitation."



Fang Ru smiled.



"That seems reasonable."



"The palace furnishings can wait until the responsible office has checked the inventory."



Yuru picked up her brush.



She selected the second proposed date.



Her reply was brief.



She would visit the Duke of Xu's residence on the sixteenth day of the month.



Madam Xu could prepare whatever breakfast she wished.



Yuru added one sentence asking her mother not to trouble the kitchen unnecessarily.



Fang Ru read the reply.



"Madam Xu may interpret that as a request to prepare more dishes."



"Then I will eat them."



Fang Ru laughed.



Yuru sealed the letter.



Only then did she turn to the palace correspondence.

The palace furnishings question concerned a writing table and two chairs.



The former Empress's personal inventory listed them as items transferred from her previous residence.



The Palace Stores inventory listed the same furniture as palace property.



The two records had been created at different times.



One entry described the furniture by location.



The other described it by the person who had used it.



Neither record established ownership.



The Imperial Household Administration had therefore requested clarification.



Yuru read the attached descriptions.



The writing table had been purchased several years earlier for Chengming Palace.



The two chairs had been moved from a storage room before the renovation.



There was no personal purchase record.



She wrote:



*The items should remain under palace inventory until ownership is established. Their previous use in my residence does not establish personal ownership.*



She returned the papers.



Fang Ru looked at the answer.



"You could have kept them."



"I could have."



"They are familiar."



"That does not make them mine."



Yuru closed the file.



"Someone must be able to distinguish between what belongs to a person and what belongs to an office."



Fang Ru nodded.



The distinction was simple.



The palace had spent several months discovering that simple distinctions were often the ones most frequently ignored.

At the Qingyuan Marquisate, Mingyuan received a revised statement from the western field tenant.



The tenant had written down the terms as he understood them.



He had continued cultivation after the previous lease expired.



The estate had accepted the spring payment.



He had received temporary permission to remain until the family decided the property's future use.



He wished to continue cultivating the field.



He was willing to discuss a new agreement.



Mingyuan read the statement.



Then he handed it to Sun Qiming.



"Does this settle the classification?"



"It clarifies the tenant's account of the arrangement. It does not establish the final terms."



Mingyuan nodded.



"We should not call it a renewed lease."



"No."



"Nor should we call it unauthorized occupation."



"Not on the evidence currently available."



Mingyuan looked at the steward.



"Prepare a temporary written agreement, subject to the family's decision about the property's future use."



Sun considered the proposal.



"Make certain it does not accidentally create a longer lease than intended."



Mingyuan frowned.



"How?"



"Specify the duration, payment, permitted cultivation, and the conditions under which either side may end the arrangement. Have the legal adviser review it."



Mingyuan nodded.



He had expected the problem to be solved by asking the tenant whether he wanted to stay.



Instead, the answer had produced another document.



He was beginning to suspect that every answer in estate management eventually produced another document.

Mingxiu was having a similar experience.



The contractor had delivered the revised estimate for the southern building.



The report listed sixty roof tiles requiring replacement.



It also stated that several additional tiles might break during removal.



The estimated cost included labour, materials, and transport.



Mingxiu compared the figures with the earlier estimate.



The new total was higher.



She called the contractor back.



"Why has the cost increased?"



"The earlier estimate was based on the visible damage. The revised estimate includes the tiles that may break during removal."



"How many?"



"Between five and twelve."



"Then why is the estimate calculated for twenty?"



The contractor looked at the document.



He checked the figures.



After a moment, he admitted that the clerk had copied the wrong quantity from another repair schedule.



Mingxiu placed the two papers side by side.



"Please correct it."



The contractor bowed.



"I will."



She handed the report to the steward.



"Do not approve the payment until the corrected estimate arrives."



The steward nodded.



Madam Liang, who had been listening from the doorway, entered.



"You have become very strict."



Mingxiu looked up.



"Is that a problem?"



"It may delay the repairs."



"It may also prevent us from paying for twelve unnecessary tiles."



Madam Liang had no immediate answer.



Mingxiu returned to the papers.



She was discovering that a reputation for being pleasant could be useful in society.



It was less useful when a contractor had written the wrong number.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Mingzhao was examining the final version of the two-hundred-bolt contract.



Lin Shuyun had arrived shortly after breakfast.



The buyer's representative was expected later that afternoon.



The revised clause concerning inspection delays had been accepted.



The deposit amount was specified.



The payment schedule was divided by accepted delivery groups.



The four additional workers were named in the workshop's separate staffing schedule.



Two would receive training before beginning independent work.



Mingzhao read the contract from the beginning.



Lin waited.



"You have already read it twice," she said.



"I am reading it a third time."



"Is there something wrong?"



"I am checking that the changes were made consistently."



Lin poured tea.



"Sometimes I think you trust a contract more than a person."



"I trust a properly written contract to explain what two people have agreed to."



"That is not quite the same thing."



"No."



Mingzhao turned another page.



"A contract cannot guarantee that someone will behave honestly. It can establish what was promised, what was delivered, and what happens if the agreement is not fulfilled."



Lin smiled.



"That sounds like something Sun Qiming would say."



"It is something he would write in a report."



The buyer's representative arrived before noon.



He was a middle-aged merchant named Zhou Wenqi, who had previously purchased the accepted silk groups.



He greeted both women.



"The amended terms are acceptable," he said. "I have brought the deposit."



Mingzhao examined the payment document.



She checked the amount against the contract.



She then asked Lin to verify the receipt.



The payment was counted.



The receipt was prepared.



Both parties signed the final agreement.



Only after the deposit had been received did Mingzhao authorize the workshop to begin production.



Zhou Wenqi looked satisfied.



"We can begin immediately?"



"After the workers receive their instructions and the workshop confirms the production schedule."



"That should not take long."



"It should take as long as necessary to do it correctly."



The merchant laughed.



"Miss Shen, I see that your reputation is accurate."



Mingzhao looked at him.



"Which reputation?"



"That you do not agree to anything until every page has been read."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"That is why I brought you the contract."



Mingzhao entered the deposit in the new order's separate ledger.



The earlier partnership remained untouched.



The forty previously accepted bolts had been paid for.



The two corrected bolts had been accepted by the buyer, but payment was still outstanding.



Production costs for the existing order had not yet been fully settled.



The new two-hundred-bolt order had its own deposit, production schedule, and cost record.



Mingzhao closed the ledger.



The order had finally begun.



It had not yet made a profit.

At the palace, the Emperor received a report from the Department of Rites.



Two ceremonial objects remained unidentified.



The department had located an old reference to one of them in a storage record.



However, the description was incomplete.



The second object had no matching entry.



The Emperor read the report.



"Has the Palace Stores inventory been checked?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And the Imperial Household Administration?"



"They have checked the purchase and maintenance records."



"Then continue examining the original descriptions. Do not assign a new name merely to complete the list."



The official bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Emperor placed the report aside.



Gao Shun brought another document.



"This concerns the autumn palace clothing allocation."



The Emperor glanced at it.



"Has the procedure been approved?"



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"Then why is it here?"



"The office requests confirmation of the distribution date."



"Who is responsible for the distribution?"



"The Imperial Household Administration."



"Then they should set the date within the approved schedule."



Gao Shun bowed.



The Emperor returned the document.



"Send it back."



Gao Shun accepted it.



The Emperor looked at the growing stack of papers.



He had once believed that the greatest difficulty in governing the palace was the number of matters requiring attention.



He was beginning to suspect that the greater difficulty was persuading people not to bring him matters that did not require his attention.

The Crown Prince visited the Imperial Archives in the afternoon.



He had requested access to several old Eastern Palace records.



The archivist brought out three volumes.



The first contained household supply records.



The second contained expenditure summaries.



The third contained corrections and annotations.



The Crown Prince examined them.



"Why are there two totals for the same month?"



The archivist explained.



"One records the total amount purchased. The other records the amount issued during the month."



"Then the remaining quantity should be recorded separately."



"Yes, Your Highness."



"Was it?"



The archivist turned several pages.



"No."



The Crown Prince looked at the volume.



"Then we cannot calculate the remaining stock from these records alone."



"That is correct."



He nodded.



"Add a note. Do not reconstruct a number without supporting records."



The archivist bowed.



The Crown Prince left the Archives with three documents and one new question.



He did not ask Yuru to solve it.



Instead, he requested that the Eastern Palace office prepare a new stock reconciliation.

That evening, Mingzhao received a message from the Marquisate.



It came from her father.



The message informed her that the next property meeting would be delayed until the revised valuations and supporting records were complete.



Mingzhao read it.



There was no request for her to review the accounts.



There was no suggestion that she return to the estate.



The message simply stated the position.



She placed it beside the corrected property schedule.



Qiao Yue brought tea.



"Has the family arranged another meeting?"



"Not yet."



"Is that good?"



"It is better than arranging one without the necessary documents."



Qiao Yue sat down.



"Will the settlement be finished soon?"



"No."



"How long?"



"I do not know."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"At least the contract is signed."



"Yes."



"And the workshop can begin."



"Yes."



"And the two hundred bolts will eventually be delivered."



"Eventually."



Qiao Yue looked at her.



"You could try sounding pleased."



Mingzhao lifted her teacup.



"I am pleased."



"You do not look pleased."



"I am drinking tea."



"That is not the same thing."



Mingzhao gave her a look.



Qiao Yue smiled and withdrew.

At the Duke of Xu's residence, Madam Xu received Yuru's reply.



She read the selected date.



Then she called the household steward.



"Prepare breakfast for the sixteenth."



The steward bowed.



"How many dishes should be prepared?"



Madam Xu considered the question.



"Enough."



The steward waited.



"How many is enough, Madam?"



Madam Xu looked at him.



"Do you think my daughter will arrive with a guest list?"



"No, Madam."



"Then prepare an ordinary breakfast."



The steward bowed again.



"Yes, Madam."



He left.



The Duke entered a moment later.



"Have you arranged the breakfast?"



"Yes."



"How many dishes?"



"An ordinary number."



The Duke looked relieved.



"Good."



Madam Xu turned towards him.



"Do not ask the cook to add anything."



"I would not."



"You asked yesterday whether we should prepare a separate tray for her attendants."



"That is a reasonable question."



"It is. The attendants will eat separately."



The Duke nodded.



Then he paused.



"Perhaps we should prepare a little more fruit."



Madam Xu closed her eyes.



The breakfast had not yet begun.

At the temporary residence, Yuru finished reading a book and placed it beside her tea.



Fang Ru entered.



"Madam, the palace has acknowledged your reply about the furnishings."



"Good."



"They will retain the uncertain items in the palace inventory until the records are examined."



"That is correct."



Fang Ru hesitated.



"The Emperor sent a personal message."



Yuru looked up.



"What does it say?"



"He hopes you are comfortable and asks whether you would like to visit the palace gardens sometime next week."



Yuru considered it.



"Was it an invitation or a request for an official visit?"



"A personal invitation."



"Then send my thanks. I will consider the date."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru returned to her book.



The palace gardens had been familiar to her for many years.



She did not need to avoid them merely because she had resigned.



But she no longer needed to visit whenever a department required an answer.



That distinction mattered.

On the fourteenth day of the fifth month, two important arrangements had finally become clearer.



The Duke of Xu's residence had selected a date for breakfast.



The Marquis of Qingyuan had postponed its next property meeting until the records were ready.



The new silk order had been signed, and its deposit had been received.



The western field tenant's temporary arrangement was being documented.



The southern building's repair estimate was being corrected.



The palace had retained uncertain furniture under its own inventory rather than assigning ownership by convenience.



The Crown Prince had begun a stock reconciliation without asking the former Empress to do it.



None of these matters was extraordinary.



That was precisely why they mattered.



The former Empress had chosen a day for breakfast.



The Marquis's eldest daughter had approved a contract.



One would soon be served porridge.



The other would soon be required to pay wages.



Both had discovered that accepting an invitation and accepting an obligation were very different things.
