# **Chapter 45: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Inspect a Garden. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Inspect a Warehouse. Both Invitations Have Included the Word "Brief," Which Has Made Both Women Suspicious.**

**7th Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The invitation from the Duke of Xu arrived before breakfast.



It contained only three sentences.



The Duke wished to show his daughter the eastern courtyard again. The gardener had completed the seasonal pruning. The household had also finished repairing the western window.



He hoped she would visit that afternoon.



Yuru read the letter.



Fang Ru was arranging the breakfast dishes.



"Will you go, Madam?"



"Yes."



"Shall I prepare the same carriage?"



"Yes."



"Should I bring the household inventory?"



Yuru looked up.



"Why?"



"The Duke may ask about the courtyard arrangements."



"He can ask me."



Fang Ru nodded.



After a moment, she asked, "Would you like me to bring the furniture list?"



"No."



"The expense estimate?"



"No."



"The household plan?"



Yuru put down the letter.



"Fang Ru."



"Yes, Madam?"



"I am visiting my father."



Fang Ru lowered her head.



"I understand."



Yuru looked at her for a moment.



Then she smiled.



"Bring a book. If my father wants to discuss furniture, he can do so without us preparing a meeting."



Fang Ru smiled back.



"Yes, Madam."



The breakfast proceeded without further documents.



It was a successful morning.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao received a letter from Master Luo.



The land office had not yet released the neighbouring parcel's registration record. However, Master Luo had obtained a more complete copy of the old drainage map.



The map showed that the channel had been widened approximately twelve years earlier.



It did not identify the person who had ordered the work.



It did, however, contain a reference number.



Master Luo had also found an old maintenance entry from the same period.



Mingzhao examined the letter.



Qiao Yue poured tea.



"Does the new map settle the boundary?"



"No."



"Does it help?"



"Yes."



"How?"



"It establishes that the channel was altered after the older property description was recorded."



Qiao Yue looked at the map.



"Then perhaps the boundary was altered too."



"Perhaps."



"Could that be why Mr Qian and the owner disagree?"



"Possibly. But the map does not establish a land transfer."



Mingzhao placed the papers beside one another.



The old registration described the drainage channel as a boundary feature.



The later map showed a widened channel.



The maintenance record referred to repairs and widening work.



None of the documents established that the legal boundary had been moved.



She wrote to Master Luo, asking him to obtain the original maintenance order and determine whether the work had been authorized by the local authority or carried out privately.



She also asked whether the reference number could be traced through the land office's archive.



Qiao Yue watched her write.



"Will that be the last document?"



"No."



"How do you know?"



"Because we have not found the neighbouring registration record."



Qiao Yue sighed.



Mingzhao sealed the letter.



"At least we know which document to look for next."

The former northern warehouse was quiet when Shen Mingyuan arrived.



The building had not been used for ordinary grain storage since the family began reviewing the inventory.



Its doors were open, and the old shelves stood along the walls.



Several workers were removing damaged boards from the northern corner.



Mingyuan had asked the steward to keep the repair work separate from the inventory review.



The steward had agreed.



He was now standing beside the entrance with a list of the repair materials.



"Young Master, the carpenter says the northern shelving can be repaired rather than replaced."



"Has he provided the estimate?"



"Yes."



"Does it include labour?"



"Yes."



"Then keep it with the maintenance records."



The steward bowed.



Mingyuan entered.



The independent accountant, Sun Qiming, was already there.



He stood near the old receiving table, examining a group of documents.



"Young Master."



"Mr Sun."



Sun placed the papers down.



"I have reviewed the warehouse records you assembled."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Have you found anything?"



"Several useful things. Nothing that establishes the final destination of the missing grain."



Mingyuan had expected this.



He looked at the papers.



Sun pointed to the original stock record.



"The initial discrepancy remains forty-seven sacks."



He moved to the next sheet.



"The movement instruction covers twenty sacks for transfer to southern storage."



Then the southern ledger.



"Eighteen sacks are confirmed as received."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Two remain unconfirmed."



"Correct."



Sun placed the remaining records in a separate group.



"The other twenty-seven sacks have no established explanation."



Mingyuan looked at the old transport payment.



"And this?"



"It records payment for transport. It does not establish the number of sacks carried or their final destination."



"The separate southern-road journey?"



"Still unconfirmed."



Sun picked up the possible reference to Zhao Fusheng.



"The handwriting may identify this person, but we cannot treat the name as certain."



Mingyuan looked at the note.



"Can we find him?"



"We are attempting to locate him through the merchant who formerly dealt with the roadside yard. We have not yet obtained the full business register."



"Would the register prove the grain delivery?"



"Not by itself. It might establish whether the yard operated at the relevant time, who controlled it, and whether the transport business dealt with it."



Mingyuan nodded.



He was learning that a document could be useful without proving the conclusion he wanted.



Sun continued.



"There is another issue."



He showed Mingyuan a page from the warehouse maintenance account.



A payment had been made for repairs to the northern shelving during the same period as the missing grain.



The amount was modest.



The description was incomplete.



"Was this payment made from the estate maintenance account?" Mingyuan asked.



"Yes."



"Does it relate to the grain discrepancy?"



"Not necessarily. It may be an ordinary repair expense."



Mingyuan looked at the date.



It was close to the period when the warehouse stock had been counted.



He felt a familiar urge to connect the entries.



Then he stopped.



"Keep it separate until we have more evidence."



Sun nodded.



"That is my recommendation."



The two men continued reviewing the records.



The work was slow.



It was also more orderly than the previous attempts.

At the Duke of Xu's residence, the eastern courtyard had changed.



The western window had been repaired.



The old wooden frame had been strengthened, and the shutters opened without difficulty.



The gardener had also trimmed the osmanthus tree.



Yuru stood beneath it.



The courtyard was pleasant in the afternoon light.



Her father joined her.



"Does it look better?"



"Yes."



"The gardener says the tree will grow more evenly now."



"It looks healthy."



The Duke smiled.



"You have not asked whether the household has completed the expense estimate."



"Have you?"



"Yes."



"And?"



"The twelve-tael additional preparation charge has been removed."



Yuru looked at him.



"That is good."



"The curtain cords were replaced. The cleaning materials were included in the ordinary household supply account."



"That is correct."



"The cushions were not purchased."



Yuru smiled.



"Then the courtyard has survived without them."



The Duke laughed.



He led her into the study.



The furniture had been reduced to a desk, two bookcases, and a small couch.



The room was much better.



Yuru walked to the window.



"Father, this arrangement is suitable."



"Would you like to use the courtyard?"



"Perhaps."



"That is not an answer."



"It is the answer I have today."



The Duke nodded.



He had learned not to hurry her.



They sat together.



After a while, he asked, "Would you like to remain at the palace guest residence until you decide?"



"Yes."



"And if you decide to move here?"



"I will tell you."



"Then the rooms will remain available."



Yuru looked at him.



"Thank you."



The Duke poured tea.



"I have also instructed the steward not to send you household accounts."



Yuru looked at him over her cup.



"Did he send them again?"



"Only a small list."



"What list?"



"Garden maintenance."



Yuru smiled.



The Duke sighed.



"I have explained that you are visiting as my daughter."



"Then perhaps the steward will eventually understand."



"I hope so."



He paused.



"Your mother says you have been reading more."



"I have."



"Are the books suitable?"



"They are books, Father."



He laughed.



The conversation moved to ordinary matters.



For a while, the Duke did not ask his daughter to solve anything.

At the silk workshop, Lin Shuyun received a revised cost estimate for the proposed two-hundred-bolt order.



The estimate included materials, labour, dyeing, transport, and an allowance for ordinary finishing losses.



It did not include the cost of additional workers who had not yet been hired.



Lin examined the figures.



The workshop manager stood opposite her.



"Now can we accept the order?"



"Not yet."



He frowned.



"What is missing?"



"The deposit arrangement."



"The buyer will pay."



"How much?"



"He has not specified."



"Then the payment arrangement is not settled."



The manager sighed.



"The buyer is reputable."



"That does not tell us when he will pay."



"He has traded for years."



"Then he should have no difficulty agreeing to written terms."



The manager looked at the estimate.



"What terms do you want?"



"An initial deposit sufficient to cover the first material purchase, staged inspection, and payment for each accepted delivery group."



"That may discourage him."



"It may."



Lin closed the ledger.



"I would rather lose an order than finance someone else's business without knowing the repayment date."



The manager looked at her.



Then he laughed.



"You are difficult."



"I am a merchant."



He shook his head.



They agreed that Lin would send the buyer a proposed contract.



The order remained unaccepted.



At the end of the meeting, Lin wrote to Mingzhao.



She explained the estimate and the proposed payment arrangement.



Mingzhao received the letter that evening and approved the terms for negotiation.



She did not authorize production.



The current order still had to be completed first.

At the palace, the Emperor received the latest report on the ceremonial objects.



The Department of Rites had identified three of the five uncertain items.



Two remained unmatched.



One of the newly identified objects had been stored under an incorrect name.



The other two had been recorded in the old inventory using descriptions that no longer matched their current forms.



Zhao Jingheng read the explanation.



"How did the name become incorrect?"



Gao Shun replied, "The original description referred to the material rather than the object's use. A later inventory copied the name without the accompanying note."



The Emperor looked at the paper.



"Then correct the record."



"Yes, Your Majesty."



"And the two remaining objects?"



"The Department of Rites is comparing the old measurements with the stores inventory."



"Good."



The Emperor placed the report aside.



He had begun to notice that the palace's old records contained many small errors that had survived because nobody had needed to examine them closely.



Now that the offices were responsible for their own records, those errors were becoming visible.



He did not enjoy the inconvenience.



He did, however, prefer it to pretending that the records were correct.



The Crown Prince arrived shortly afterwards.



He carried a revised expenditure summary.



"Father."



Zhao Jingheng looked up.



"Have you finished the Eastern Palace report?"



"Yes."



"Any unresolved matters?"



"Two entries require supporting receipts. The steward has requested copies from the suppliers."



"Then why have you brought it to me?"



The Crown Prince hesitated.



"I wanted to show you the revised format."



The Emperor examined it.



The summary was concise.



The supporting details were attached.



The unresolved entries were clearly marked.



He nodded.



"This is better."



The Crown Prince looked relieved.



"Thank you, Father."



"Continue using it. Have the relevant office review whether the same format can be adapted for the other imperial households."



"Yes."



The Crown Prince turned to leave.



The Emperor stopped him.



"Cheng'an."



"Yes, Father?"



"Do not assume that a report is correct merely because it is well arranged."



The Crown Prince bowed.



"I understand."



"And do not assume it is wrong merely because one entry is incomplete."



"I understand."



The Emperor nodded.



The Crown Prince departed.



Zhao Jingheng looked at the report again.



Yuru would have approved of the format.



He did not send it to her.

At the eastern market, Mingzhao's temporary household had developed a new problem.



The kitchen servant had discovered that the rice jar was nearly empty.



This was not unusual.



The difficulty was that the purchase list had been prepared two days earlier, and the supplier had not yet delivered the replacement stock.



Qiao Yue informed Mingzhao.



"The rice will last until tomorrow morning."



"Did the supplier confirm the delivery?"



"He said it would arrive today."



"Has he sent a message?"



"No."



Mingzhao put down her book.



"Send someone to ask."



Qiao Yue looked towards the kitchen.



"Should we buy from another supplier?"



"Not yet. Ask whether the delivery is delayed or cancelled."



A servant was sent to the market.



He returned shortly afterwards.



The supplier had delayed delivery because his cart had broken a wheel.



He expected to deliver the rice the following morning.



Mingzhao considered the situation.



"Do we have enough for breakfast?"



"Yes."



"Then wait until tomorrow. If the delivery does not arrive, purchase from the nearby shop."



Qiao Yue nodded.



The matter was recorded in the household provisions book.



It was not a major problem.



It was also not something that required a family meeting, a formal petition, or a report to the Emperor.



Mingzhao returned to her reading.



Qiao Yue watched her.



"Miss, the supplier may fail to deliver tomorrow too."



"Then we will buy elsewhere."



"That is all?"



"That is all."



Qiao Yue seemed disappointed.



She had expected a longer discussion.

That evening, Mingzhao received the preliminary valuation of the northern warehouse building.



The valuation was separate from the grain stock.



The building was structurally sound.



The northern shelving required repair.



The drainage around the rear wall needed attention.



The roof was in acceptable condition.



The valuation did not include the value of grain, equipment, or any unresolved inventory.



Mingzhao examined the report.



The distinction was correct.



She wrote a note asking that the repair estimate be attached separately.



She then placed the valuation beside the eleven-document family schedule.



The property meeting was still expected on the tenth day.



She would attend.



She would discuss her own claims and the division of property.



She would not take responsibility for completing the Marquisate's warehouse investigation.



That work now belonged to the people still managing the estate.



She closed the file.

At the Duke of Xu's residence, Yuru was preparing to leave.



Her father walked with her to the courtyard entrance.



"Will you come again next week?"



"Yes, if the arrangements are convenient."



"They will be."



Yuru paused.



"Father, you do not need to keep the courtyard empty indefinitely."



"I know."



"If I decide not to move here, you may use it for another purpose."



The Duke looked at her.



"I will not decide that before you have had time to consider."



Yuru smiled.



"Thank you."



He watched her carriage depart.



Then he turned towards the steward.



"Has the household accountant completed the garden expense report?"



The steward bowed.



"Yes, Your Grace."



"Have it reviewed by the proper person."



The steward hesitated.



"Should it be sent to Madam Yuru?"



The Duke looked at him.



"No."



"Then—"



"Send it to the household accountant responsible for the garden."



The steward bowed.



The Duke entered the residence.



The eastern courtyard remained available.



The household accounts remained elsewhere.

At the temporary residence near the eastern market, Mingzhao reviewed the next day's schedule.



The rice delivery was expected in the morning.



Master Luo was awaiting the neighbouring registration record.



Lin Shuyun was negotiating the proposed silk order.



Sun Qiming was continuing the warehouse review.



The family settlement meeting remained scheduled for the tenth day.



There were several things she could not control.



She had stopped treating that fact as a personal insult.



She placed the schedule beside her ledger.



Qiao Yue brought in the evening tea.



"Will you be busy tomorrow?"



"Yes."



"With what?"



"Ordinary matters."



Qiao Yue looked at the schedule.



"Those ordinary matters seem to occupy the entire day."



"They do."



"Perhaps independence is more expensive than we expected."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Independence is not free."



Qiao Yue sighed.



"Neither is tea."



Mingzhao glanced at the teapot.



"Have you recorded the tea purchase?"



"Yes."



"Good."



Qiao Yue stared at her.



Then she laughed.

The day ended without a major decision.



The former Empress had visited her father's residence and enjoyed an afternoon without being asked to manage its accounts.



The northern warehouse records had become better organized, but the missing grain had not been found.



The proposed silk order had acquired a cost estimate but still lacked settled payment terms.



The palace had identified three more ceremonial objects, leaving two unresolved.



The temporary household had nearly run out of rice, but the supplier had promised delivery the following morning.



And the Marquisate's next settlement meeting was approaching.



The world had not become simple.



It had merely become more honest about what remained unfinished.



For the former Empress, that was a relief.



For Mingzhao, it was a working condition.



For the Emperor, it was an administrative improvement.



For the Marquisate, it was an inconvenience.



And for the supplier who had broken his cart wheel, it was probably a very unfortunate day.
