# **Chapter 94: The Former Empress Has Discovered That a Household Servant Can Have Three Different Supervisors and Still Receive No Clear Instruction. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Discovered That the First Ten Bolts of an Order Can Reveal More Than Eighty Promises Made in Advance. The Marquis's Heir Has Found a Name in an Old Personnel Register and Has Learned That Even a Confirmed Clerk Cannot Explain a Missing Delivery.**

**28th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**

Xu Yuru had reached the chapter on household servants.

She was beginning to understand why her father had selected this particular book.

The author had devoted several pages to the duties of stewards, attendants, cooks, gatekeepers, and household clerks. His descriptions were practical enough, but his proposed arrangement was less convincing.

According to him, the head of a household should appoint a senior steward to supervise daily work, while retaining the authority to intervene whenever necessary. The steward, in turn, should consult the household head before making decisions of consequence.

Yuru read the paragraph twice.

Then she placed the book on the table.

"Fang Ru, how many people are responsible for preparing my meals?"

Fang Ru considered.

"The kitchen steward is responsible for the kitchen. The cook prepares the meals. The storehouse attendant issues the ingredients. The residence steward reviews the household supplies."

"And who decides whether the ingredients are sufficient?"

"The kitchen steward submits the requirement. The residence steward checks the available stores."

"And if the kitchen steward and the residence steward disagree?"

"They discuss it. If they cannot agree, the senior steward decides."

Yuru nodded.

"And who is responsible for the meal arriving on time?"

Fang Ru smiled.

"The cook."

"Good."

Yuru opened the book again.

"The author would have the household head intervene in every disagreement."

"Perhaps he has had troublesome stewards."

"Perhaps he has not appointed a good one."

Fang Ru lowered her eyes.

Yuru continued reading.

The problem was not that the household head should never intervene. There were circumstances in which intervention was necessary. A serious dispute, an unusual expense, a risk to the household, or a failure of duty might require a decision from above.

But if every ordinary disagreement travelled upward, the household would eventually have a steward who could not make a decision without permission.

Yuru marked the passage.

"An office should know what it is permitted to decide."

Fang Ru looked at her.

"That seems reasonable."

"It is. Yet many people prefer to ask for permission rather than accept responsibility."

"Because they fear being blamed?"

"Sometimes. And sometimes because asking someone else to decide is easier than deciding themselves."

Yuru closed the book.

She remembered the palace's early attempts to distribute her former duties. Some officials had hesitated over decisions that were well within their authority. Others had attempted to send questions upward simply because they were accustomed to receiving her answer.

She had once found it easier to answer than to insist that they decide.

The result had been an administration that knew where to find her but did not always know where its own authority ended.

She did not regret every intervention.

She regretted allowing so many ordinary decisions to become dependent on her presence.

Fang Ru placed tea beside her.

"Would you like to continue?"

"Later."

Yuru looked toward the window.

"I have read enough about household servants for one morning."

At the workshop, the first ten bolts of Gao Wen's order were approaching completion.

Lin Shuyun had arranged for the first group to be produced under the approved trial settings. The dye mixture had been recorded, the loom tension marked, and the approved sample hung beside the work area.

The workers were not permitted to alter either without consulting the senior weaver.

Mingzhao arrived shortly before the afternoon inspection.

Lin Shuyun showed her the production record.

"Ten bolts have been completed. Eight passed the initial internal examination. Two require further inspection."

Mingzhao looked at the two marked bundles.

"What is the issue?"

"One has a slight variation in colour near the edge. The other has an uneven fold."

"Are they defects in the finished silk?"

"We have not decided. The colour difference may be within the permitted variation, but it needs to be compared with the sample. The fold can be corrected without damaging the material."

Mingzhao examined the first bolt.

The variation was visible only when the silk was placed beside the approved sample and turned toward the light.

She checked the written specification.

The difference appeared to be within the permitted range, but she wanted the senior weaver and dyer to examine it independently.

The second bolt had a loose fold at one edge.

"Can it be corrected?"

"Yes. The silk itself is sound."

"Then correct it and record the work."

Lin Shuyun nodded.

Mingzhao examined the remaining eight.

They were consistent in colour, width, and weave.

"Do the internal records distinguish between the eight passed bolts and the two requiring further inspection?"

"Yes."

"Good. Do not record the first ten as fully passed until both are resolved."

Lin Shuyun looked at the bundles.

"That may delay the next stage by half a day."

"Then it will be delayed by half a day."

Mingzhao did not sound displeased.

The first ten bolts were not being produced to satisfy a ceremonial deadline. They were being produced to confirm that the workshop could maintain the agreed standard.

A delay at this stage was inconvenient.

A defect discovered after all eighty bolts had been completed would be much worse.

The senior weaver returned with the dyer.

They examined the colour variation together. Both concluded that it was within the permitted range, though close to the limit.

The uneven fold was corrected and reinspected.

The two bolts were then marked as passed.

All ten had passed internal examination.

Mingzhao reviewed the record.

"Authorize the next twenty."

Lin Shuyun signed the production instruction.

The workshop had now moved beyond trial preparation.

It had begun the actual order.

The Marquisate's archive clerk brought Mingyuan the personnel records shortly before midday.

Several old lists had been preserved in a separate cabinet. The relevant entries identified Li Shun as a warehouse clerk assigned to the northern warehouse during the period under examination.

Mingyuan read the page carefully.

"So Li Shun was employed there?"

"Yes, Young Master."

"Was he responsible for dispatch records?"

"The personnel list does not state his exact duties."

"Is there another record?"

The clerk searched through the remaining pages.

There was a duty assignment sheet from the same period. Li Shun appeared under the heading for warehouse correspondence and dispatch entries.

Mingyuan looked at it.

"This confirms that he could have written the record."

"Yes."

"Does it prove that he wrote this particular entry?"

"No."

Mingyuan nodded.

"Then record the distinction."

The clerk prepared a certified copy of the assignment sheet.

Mingyuan placed it beside Zhang's handwriting report.

There were now three separate pieces of information:

- Li Shun had worked at the northern warehouse during the relevant period.
- His duties included dispatch records.
- Zhang believed the handwriting on the four-cart entry resembled Li Shun's.

Together, these facts made Li Shun a plausible author of the entry.

They did not establish the cargo or destination.

Mingyuan wrote an instruction to search for Li Shun's later employment records and any surviving personal correspondence from the warehouse office.

He also asked whether the transport notebook might have been transferred to another archive when the warehouse changed its administrative arrangement.

Steward Zhou watched him write.

"You have found the clerk."

"We have identified a likely clerk."

"Is that not the same?"

"No."

The steward sighed.

"Your generation has made even finding a clerk difficult."

Mingyuan put down his brush.

"Finding him was easy. Establishing what he knew is the difficult part."

The search for the transport notebook produced a second complication.

The archive clerk discovered that the northern warehouse had once used two separate books: a dispatch register maintained by the warehouse office and a transport notebook carried by the person supervising the carts.

The dispatch register recorded the release of goods.

The transport notebook was supposed to record the movement between the warehouse and the destination.

The two books had not been stored together.

Mingyuan listened as the clerk explained.

"Where was the transport notebook kept?"

"We do not know. It may have been returned to the warehouse office after the journey, or retained by the transport supervisor."

"Was there a rule?"

"There was a practice, but I have not found a written regulation."

"Then we should not assume the notebook was returned."

Steward Zhou looked at the clerk.

"Can you identify the transport supervisor?"

"Not from the dispatch register."

Mingyuan considered this.

"Search the duty assignments for transport supervisors during that period. Also check the payment records for cart hire."

"That may take time."

"Then take the time."

The clerk bowed.

Mingyuan added another note to the inquiry.

The four-cart entry had identified a dispatch movement.

The personnel records had identified a likely clerk.

The missing transport notebook might contain information about the journey.

But the investigation still had no confirmed destination for the twenty-seven sacks.

He did not allow the archive clerk to write otherwise.

At the Shen residence, Mingzhao received a letter from her father.

It was brief.

Shen Guowei informed her that the family was preparing a list of documents required for the property division and hoped to arrange another discussion once the records had been gathered.

He also mentioned that Mingyuan was reviewing the old warehouse accounts.

Mingzhao read the letter.

She did not object to the proposed meeting.

She did, however, notice that it contained no specific question about her personal claims.

She folded it and placed it beside the revised property schedule.

Qiao Yue looked at the letter.

"Will you reply?"

"Yes."

"What will you say?"

"That I am willing to review the documents when they are ready. The meeting should address the property division and my outstanding claims. Questions about my previous authorizations should be submitted with the relevant records."

Qiao Yue wrote the draft.

Mingzhao read it before signing.

She added one sentence:

*I will not resume responsibility for the estate's present administration.*

She handed the letter back.

Qiao Yue sealed it.

"Your father may dislike that sentence."

"Then he should read it carefully."

Mingzhao returned to the property schedule.

She had no interest in allowing the family to turn a discussion about her property into another request that she manage the estate.

The two matters were separate.

They would remain separate.

That afternoon, the workshop completed the first ten bolts.

The internal inspection record was signed by the senior weaver and the receiving clerk. The approved sample was attached to the order file, and the two corrected or closely examined pieces were separately identified.

Lin Shuyun sent Mingzhao the final report.

Mingzhao checked the quantities.

Ten completed.

Ten internally passed.

Twenty more authorized.

The next group had begun production.

She placed the report with the contract.

There was no buyer inspection yet. The merchant would inspect the completed order according to the agreed terms.

For now, the workshop had established that its first production group met the standard.

Mingzhao looked at the approved sample.

The first trial had failed.

The second had passed.

The first ten bolts had required additional examination.

All ten had eventually met the specification.

That was not wasted work.

It was information that had prevented a small inconsistency from becoming a larger problem.

Lin Shuyun came to the table.

"The workers are asking whether the next group will be inspected in the same way."

"Yes."

"Some of them think the first ten received too much attention."

"Tell them that the first ten established the method. The next group must demonstrate that the method can be repeated."

Lin Shuyun nodded.

"That should satisfy them."

Mingzhao looked at her.

"Does it?"

"It is a better explanation than telling them to work faster."

Mingzhao smiled.

"Then use it."

The former Empress spent the latter part of the afternoon reading.

She had returned to the household management book, but she was no longer reading it in the same way as before.

The author's advice about stewards and servants was not entirely wrong. A household head did need to understand the work being done. A person could not delegate a responsibility properly without knowing what it involved.

But knowledge and execution were different.

Yuru had once believed that if she understood every detail, she should also be prepared to handle every detail.

She could now see how that belief had narrowed the authority of the people working under her.

She marked another paragraph.

Then she looked at the book's title.

"Fang Ru."

"Yes, Madam?"

"Do you think my father selected this book because he agrees with the author?"

"Perhaps."

"Or because he wanted me to disagree with him?"

Fang Ru smiled.

"That is also possible."

Yuru considered it.

"Then I shall ask him when I return the book."

"Will you visit soon?"

"Not yet. I have only just borrowed these."

Fang Ru nodded.

Yuru placed the book aside.

There was no need to hurry to the Xu residence merely because another book might be waiting.

Her father could keep his library.

Her mother could keep him from sending the entire library.

And Yuru could read at her own pace.

By evening, the first ten bolts of Gao Wen's order had passed internal inspection.

The next twenty were underway.

The Marquisate had identified a likely clerk for the old dispatch entry, but the transport notebook remained missing.

The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.

The family property division had not been completed.

Yuru had marked several passages in a book she would eventually return.

None of these matters had been settled completely.

But each had become clearer.

For the first time in years, Yuru could read about responsibility without being expected to assume it.

For the first time in years, Mingzhao could negotiate an order without first having to rescue the workshop from an arrangement she had never agreed to.

And Mingyuan was learning that an heir could inherit an archive full of records without inheriting the answers.
