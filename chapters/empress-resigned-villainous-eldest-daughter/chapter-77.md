# **Chapter 77: The Former Empress Has Finished Her Father's Book and Has Discovered That Returning It May Require More Discussion Than Borrowing It. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Received a Request for Her Opinion and Has Answered Only the Question Asked. The Workshop Has Fifty-Six Bolts Remaining and Has Discovered That the Last Part of an Order Is Still Part of the Order.**

**11th Day of the Sixth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The former Empress Xu Yuru finished her father's book before breakfast.



She had intended to read only one more chapter the previous evening.



Then she had reached a section about the management of provincial granaries, which led into a discussion of transport records, which led into a description of how officials had once confused grain stored for emergency distribution with grain available for ordinary issue.



By the time she noticed the hour, she had nearly finished the book.



She closed it and placed it on the table.



Fang Ru entered with tea.



"Madam, have you finished reading?"



"Yes."



"Was it interesting?"



"Very."



Fang Ru looked at the book.



"Would you like to send it back to your father?"



Yuru considered the question.



"I should return it when I visit."



"Would you like me to arrange a message?"



"No. I can tell him myself."



Fang Ru nodded.



Yuru opened the book again to check whether she had left any paper markers inside.



There were three.



She removed them and placed them together.



"Your father chose well," Fang Ru said.



"He did."



"Perhaps he will send another book."



Yuru looked at her.



"That is possible."



Fang Ru smiled.



Yuru placed the book in its wrapping cloth.



She had enjoyed reading it.



She had also enjoyed not being expected to turn every useful passage into an official instruction.



The distinction was becoming increasingly important to her.



She had once believed that if she noticed a problem, she should help solve it.



Now she was learning that noticing something did not automatically make it her responsibility.



She could still care.



She could still offer advice.



But she did not need to take ownership of every difficulty.



"Would you like to walk today?" Fang Ru asked.



"Yes. The western path."



"The short route?"



"The longer one, if the weather remains pleasant."



Fang Ru glanced toward the courtyard.



"The sky is clear."



"Then the longer route."

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingzhao received a letter from her mother.



The letter was brief.



Madam Liang had written that the family was continuing its review of the property schedule and that Mingyuan had been examining the old accounts.



She also mentioned that the household had begun using Liang Wen's consolidated purchasing register.



Then came the actual question.



*Your father asks whether you would be willing to review the revised household purchasing procedure before it is formally adopted.*



Mingzhao read the sentence twice.



Qiao Yue, who was sorting the morning correspondence, looked up.



"Is it another request for you to return?"



"No."



"Another request to manage the accounts?"



"Not directly."



Qiao Yue waited.



Mingzhao handed her the letter.



Qiao Yue read it.



"Your father wants you to review the procedure."



"Yes."



"Will you?"



Mingzhao considered the request.



The revised purchasing procedure was not the same as the old estate accounts. It concerned the future organization of purchasing orders, delivery records, payment confirmation, and responsibility for maintaining the register.



She had managed the system for years.



She could identify weaknesses.



But the Marquisate had appointed Liang Wen to maintain the register and Steward Zhou to supervise it.



If she reviewed the procedure, the family might begin treating her as its unofficial supervisor again.



That was not acceptable.



She took a clean sheet.



"I will answer the question," she said.



Qiao Yue brought the inkstone closer.



Mingzhao wrote:



*I am willing to review a written copy of the proposed procedure and provide comments on its wording and record classifications. This review will not constitute an acceptance of responsibility for purchasing, supervision, or future administration. The procedure should identify the person responsible for each stage, including authorization, ordering, receipt, payment, and register maintenance.*



She paused.



Then she added:



*Please send the complete proposed procedure, together with any forms that will be used. I cannot review a document that has not yet been prepared.*



Qiao Yue read the reply.



"That is clear."



"It should be."



"Will they send the documents?"



"If they want a review."



Qiao Yue smiled.



Mingzhao folded the letter.



She was willing to provide professional advice.



She was not willing to become the person everyone consulted whenever a clerk hesitated.



Those were different arrangements.



The family would have to learn the difference.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received Mingzhao's reply shortly before noon.



He read it at the accounts table.



Steward Zhou stood beside him.



"She has agreed to review the procedure," Mingyuan said.



"With conditions."



"Reasonable conditions."



Steward Zhou nodded.



"She wants the responsibility for each stage to be identified."



"That is necessary."



Mingyuan looked toward Liang Wen.



The new clerk was copying another section of the purchasing register.



"How is the register progressing?"



Liang Wen put down his brush.



"The first six sections are copied. I have marked entries requiring supporting documents and separated confirmed deliveries from payment references."



"Have you prepared the proposed procedure?"



"I have drafted a basic version."



Mingyuan looked at Steward Zhou.



"Is it complete?"



"Not yet. We still need to decide how to record goods received in partial deliveries and how to handle corrections."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Then finish those sections before sending it to my sister."



Liang Wen bowed.



"Yes, Young Master."



Mingyuan looked at the draft.



"Who will approve orders?"



"The Marquis or an authorized steward, depending on the amount."



"Who will confirm delivery?"



"The receiving steward."



"Who will confirm payment?"



"The accounts office."



"And who will maintain the register?"



"I will."



"Who will supervise your work?"



"Steward Zhou."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Write those responsibilities clearly."



Liang Wen looked at the page.



"Should the register keeper also check the original receipts?"



"Yes, but checking a receipt is not the same as authorizing a payment."



Steward Zhou added, "And the person receiving goods should not certify a delivery that he did not witness."



Liang Wen wrote down the instruction.



Mingyuan considered the draft.



He had once thought that the estate needed someone who could manage everything.



Now he understood that the estate needed several people who knew exactly what they were responsible for.



That arrangement was less convenient.



It was also less likely to collapse when one person left.

The search for the old payment voucher continued.



The accounts office had opened the first section of the general purchasing bundle.



The documents were arranged in small packets, each tied with string.



A clerk recorded the condition of the bundle before removing the first packet.



The pages were examined in sequence.



Several concerned repairs to storage buildings.



Others concerned timber purchases and payments to local suppliers.



None matched the payment amount under investigation.



The clerk recorded the result.



The second packet contained general household purchases.



Again, there was no matching voucher.



The third packet had a damaged outer label.



The clerk did not open it immediately.



He called the senior accounts officer.



The officer examined the label and compared it with the category index.



"This may be the correct section," he said.



"Should we open it?"



"Yes, but record the condition first."



The clerk did so.



Inside were several documents, some with faded writing.



The first pages concerned payments for building materials.



The next concerned a delivery of cloth.



Then came a voucher with an amount close to the one being sought.



The clerk stopped.



He compared the date.



It was the same month.



Not the same day.



He compared the amount.



It was similar, but not identical.



He checked the reference number.



It did not match.



The clerk marked the document as unrelated and continued.



The search had not yet produced the original voucher.



But the accounts office was now examining the correct category.

At the silk workshop, the final fifty-six bolts were being prepared.



Lin Shuyun had divided them into two groups of twenty-eight.



The first group was already in finishing.



The second was still being folded.



Mingzhao arrived while the workers were comparing the current production against the approved sample.



Lin Shuyun greeted her.



"The first group should pass internal inspection tomorrow."



"And the second?"



"Two days later, if there are no corrections."



Mingzhao examined the schedule.



"Is that a confirmed completion date?"



"It is the current estimate."



"Then mark it as an estimate."



Lin Shuyun smiled.



"I have."



Mingzhao inspected the production sheets.



The workers had used the approved revised folding method.



The two trainees were now completing more work without constant intervention, though their supervisors continued to check the results.



One trainee was examining a bolt with the sample beside him.



The other was recording the finished lengths.



Mingzhao looked at the wage record.



"Any changes?"



"None. Their wages are current."



"Training hours?"



"Recorded separately."



"Corrections?"



"Recorded separately."



Lin Shuyun closed the ledger.



"The buyer has accepted one hundred and forty-four. We have fifty-six left."



"Yes."



"If the next groups pass, the order will be complete."



"If they pass."



Lin Shuyun laughed.



"You are determined not to celebrate early."



"I will celebrate when the buyer has accepted the entire order and the accounts have been settled."



"That may be some time."



"Then we have time."



Lin Shuyun shook her head.



"Your family must have found you difficult to manage."



Mingzhao gave her a level look.



"My family did not manage me particularly well."



Lin Shuyun's expression softened.



She did not ask for further explanation.



Instead, she pointed toward the first group.



"Would you like to examine the folding?"



Mingzhao stepped closer.



She checked the edges against the sample.



The first few were consistent.



One bolt had a slightly uneven corner.



The worker noticed it before she spoke.



He marked it for adjustment.



Mingzhao nodded.



"Good."



The worker returned to the table.



The mistake was small.



It would be corrected.



No one needed to turn it into a larger matter.

In the palace, Third Princess Minglan received the results of the latest library search.



The second collection had produced one possible match.



The subject was similar, but the first page was different.



Minglan examined the description.



"Does the catalogue identify the author?"



"No, Your Highness."



"Does it identify the title?"



"It gives a title, but the opening text does not match."



Minglan put down the paper.



"Then it is not the same book."



The attendant bowed.



"Should we continue searching?"



"Yes, but not through every collection at once. Start with the older household instruction books and compare the opening headings."



"Very well."



Minglan looked at the unidentified book.



She had now examined three possible references.



None was conclusive.



She wrote another note.



*Three catalogue references examined. None matches the complete text. The book remains unidentified.*



She placed the note with the earlier record.



The attendant looked at the growing stack.



"Your Highness, there are several unresolved catalogue entries."



"Then the catalogue should say so."



"Would that not make the library appear poorly organized?"



Minglan considered the question.



"An unidentified book is not the same as a missing book."



The attendant lowered her head.



"Yes, Your Highness."



Minglan returned to the text.



She had begun to understand that the purpose of a catalogue was not to make the library look orderly.



It was to help people find what was actually there.

That afternoon, the former Empress walked the longer western path.



Fang Ru accompanied her.



The path passed the small pavilion and continued along the garden wall.



The weather was warm, but a light breeze moved through the trees.



Yuru walked without hurry.



At the pavilion, she paused.



A gardener was trimming a low shrub near the path.



He stepped aside and bowed.



Yuru nodded in return.



She did not ask why the shrub had grown unevenly.



She did not ask whether the gardener had permission to trim it.



She simply continued walking.



Fang Ru glanced at her.



"Would you like to sit for a while?"



"Yes."



They sat beneath the pavilion roof.



Yuru looked at the garden.



"I used to think that if I stopped watching something, it would eventually be neglected."



Fang Ru remained quiet.



"Sometimes that was true," Yuru continued. "Sometimes it was only because no one else had been given the opportunity to watch it."



Fang Ru looked toward the gardener.



"He seems to be managing the shrub well."



Yuru smiled.



"Yes."



They sat for several minutes.



Then Yuru stood.



"Shall we return?"



Fang Ru rose.



"Yes, Madam."



The walk had taken longer than the previous one.



Yuru was not tired.



She was pleased that she had gone.

At the Marquisate, Mingyuan received the next accounts office report.



The third packet had not produced the payment voucher.



The fourth packet had not yet been examined.



The search continued.



Mingyuan read the report and placed it in the investigation file.



Steward Zhou looked at him.



"Would you like to ask the accounts office to search another bundle?"



"Not yet. They should finish this one first."



"That may take another day."



"Then it will take another day."



Mingyuan turned toward Liang Wen.



"How is the procedure draft?"



Liang Wen handed over the pages.



The draft now identified the responsibilities of the authorizing official, purchasing clerk, receiving steward, accounts clerk, and register keeper.



It also contained a section for partial deliveries.



Mingyuan read it carefully.



"Who records a correction?"



"The clerk who discovers it, with confirmation from the person responsible for the relevant transaction."



"And who approves the correction?"



"The supervising steward."



Mingyuan nodded.



"Good. Add that the original entry must remain visible. Corrections should be dated and signed."



Liang Wen wrote it down.



Steward Zhou examined the draft.



"This will be longer than the old procedure."



"It needs to be clear."



"Some of the servants may find it difficult."



"Then train them."



Steward Zhou looked at him.



The young master had said it without hesitation.



Mingyuan returned to the document.



He had begun to understand that a procedure was not useful merely because it was short.



It had to explain what people were expected to do when something went wrong.

By evening, Yuru had finished her walk and returned to the guest residence.



Her father's book was wrapped and ready to be returned the next time she visited.



Mingzhao had received a request for a review of the purchasing procedure and had agreed to examine it only after the full draft was prepared.



Mingyuan had received another report from the accounts office, but the original voucher remained unfound.



The workshop had begun the final fifty-six bolts.



Minglan had kept the old library book classified as unidentified.



The day had produced no final settlement.



But several responsibilities had become clearer.



Yuru was free to read a book without becoming responsible for the records it described.



Mingzhao could review a procedure without managing the people who used it.



Mingyuan could supervise an investigation without assuming that every possible lead was a discovery.



The workshop could prepare a group without counting it as accepted.



And Minglan could preserve an unanswered question without inventing an answer.



The former Empress had once been expected to hold all these distinctions in her head for everyone.



Now other people were learning to hold them themselves.



It was a slow process.



It was also the point.
