# **Chapter 59: The Former Empress Has Been Invited to Attend a Family Gathering. The Marquis's Eldest Daughter Has Been Invited to Attend a Family Settlement Meeting. One Invitation Contains Food. The Other Contains Twelve Pages of Corrections. Neither Woman Has Agreed to Bring Her Own Work.**

**23rd Day of the Fifth Month, 18th Year of Yonghe.**



The invitation from the Xu residence arrived shortly after breakfast.



It was written in Madam Xu's familiar hand.



Her parents intended to have a family meal on the twenty-sixth day of the month. Her younger brother and his family would attend. There would be no formal guests, no ceremony, and no particular occasion.



Xu Yuru read it twice.



Fang Ru stood beside the table.



"Madam Xu has asked whether you would like to come."



Xu Yuru smiled.



"She has asked whether I would like to eat."



Fang Ru looked at the invitation.



"That appears to be the meaning."



"Then tell her I will come."



Fang Ru nodded.



Xu Yuru picked up her tea.



There was something reassuring about an invitation that did not require an explanation.



Her mother wanted her to come home.



Her father would probably attempt to add another dish.



Her younger brother would likely arrive late and claim that the roads had been crowded.



No one would ask her to review an inventory.



At least, she hoped not.



She took another sip of tea.



Then she looked at Fang Ru.



"Do not tell my father that I have agreed to come."



Fang Ru blinked.



"Madam?"



"If he hears too early, he will have three days to plan the meal."



Fang Ru understood immediately.



She folded the invitation.



The former Empress had not yet decided whether she was prepared to face another breakfast involving excessive fruit.

At the Marquisate of Qingyuan, Shen Mingzhao received a different invitation.



The family representative had requested a meeting to discuss the revised property schedule.



The letter stated that the corrected schedule had been circulated and that the family now wished to review the remaining disputed entries.



It also stated that Shen Mingzhao's attendance was necessary.



She read the letter.



Qiao Yue stood beside the desk.



"Another meeting?"



"Yes."



"Will they have prepared the documents?"



"They claim to have prepared them."



"Will they have read them?"



Mingzhao folded the letter.



"That remains to be seen."



The meeting was scheduled for the twenty-seventh day of the month.



It was not the final settlement.



The nine confirmed advances still required separate treatment from the four payment-confirmed entries whose repayment arrangements remained unclear. The ten uncertain advances had not been accepted as debts.



The seventy-two taels for study repairs remained estate maintenance.



The additional two hundred and twenty-eight taels remained unverified.



The grain discrepancy remained unresolved.



The northern canal property was still not part of the family estate division.



The partnership with Lin Shuyun remained independent of the Marquisate.



Mingzhao placed the invitation beside the corrected schedule.



She would attend.



She would not resume estate management.



She would not accept responsibility for records that the family had failed to produce.



And she would not allow a meeting about disputed property to become a discussion about whether she ought to return home.



Qiao Yue looked at the letter.



"Should I prepare your documents?"



"Yes."



"All of them?"



"Only those relevant to the schedule."



Qiao Yue nodded.



"That will still be a considerable number."



Mingzhao looked at her.



"Then prepare a considerable number."

At the Imperial Palace, the Crown Prince was dealing with a different kind of invitation.



His younger brother, the Second Prince Zhao Chengyu, had requested funds for a small gathering.



The request was modest.



The explanation was not.



The Second Prince had listed the expected guests, the household attendants, the refreshments, the transport arrangements, and several incidental expenses.



The Crown Prince read the request.



Then he looked at his brother.



"How many guests?"



"Twelve."



"How many attendants?"



"Twenty."



Chengyu sat opposite him.



"The guests are important."



"Are the attendants also guests?"



"No."



"Then why are there twenty?"



Chengyu leaned back.



"Some guests may bring their own attendants."



"Have they confirmed that?"



"Not all of them."



"Then why are they included in the request?"



The Second Prince sighed.



"Because they may come."



The Crown Prince placed the paper on the table.



"Then the estimate should distinguish confirmed attendance from possible attendance."



Chengyu looked at him.



"Brother, if we wait for every guest to confirm, we may not be able to prepare anything."



"That is why we can prepare for the confirmed number and reserve additional provisions if necessary."



Chengyu considered this.



He had already reduced the original estimate once.



He had also returned an inflated amount after discovering that the number of attendants had been counted twice in one section.



He was beginning to find the Crown Prince's questions exhausting.



Unfortunately, they were usually reasonable.



"Fine," he said. "I will revise it."



The Crown Prince nodded.



"Thank you."



Chengyu picked up the request.



"Will you attend?"



"I have not been invited."



The Second Prince stopped.



Then he looked at his brother.



"I assumed you would be."



"That is not an invitation."



Chengyu stared at him.



The Crown Prince returned to his papers.



After a moment, the Second Prince said, "You have become very difficult."



The Crown Prince did not look up.



"I have been told that this is an improvement."

At the Xu residence, Madam Xu was already discussing the family meal.



Duke Xu had suggested preparing eight dishes.



Madam Xu had approved six.



Duke Xu had proposed adding fish.



Madam Xu had asked whether the fish was necessary.



Duke Xu had answered that it was a family meal.



Madam Xu had replied that the family had eaten fish before.



The discussion had ended without a decision.



The Duke looked at the kitchen steward.



"Prepare the fish."



Madam Xu turned toward him.



"Did I approve it?"



The Duke paused.



"Not yet."



"Then why are you instructing the kitchen?"



"To save time."



"Whose time?"



The Duke considered the question.



The kitchen steward lowered his head.



He had learned that the safest position during a discussion between the Duke and Madam Xu was to remain completely still.



Madam Xu returned to her tea.



"Six dishes are sufficient."



The Duke looked toward the kitchen.



"Prepare six dishes."



The steward bowed.



Then the Duke added, "And fruit."



Madam Xu closed her eyes.



The steward pretended not to hear.

At the eastern market, the new silk order had entered its first full production stage.



The written folding instructions had been distributed.



The original sample remained attached to the buyer's specification.



The revised method was being used under the agreed conditions.



The senior finisher supervised the experienced workers.



The two trainees worked under separate supervision.



The first trainee had improved in speed.



The second had improved in consistency.



Both still required inspection.



Mingzhao reviewed the morning's record.



Lin Shuyun stood beside her.



"The revised method has saved time on the first group."



"How much?"



Lin handed her the sheet.



Mingzhao examined the figures.



The reduction was measurable, but the group was still too small to establish the final effect on the whole order.



"Continue recording," she said.



Lin nodded.



"The workers are pleased."



"Good."



"They believe the new method will make the order easier."



"Good."



"They also believe the additional time will allow them to finish earlier."



Mingzhao looked up.



"Has the buyer agreed to an earlier delivery?"



"No."



"Then do not promise one."



Lin smiled.



"I did not promise anything."



"Good."



Mingzhao returned to the figures.



The order had a deposit.



The production had begun.



The method had been approved.



The remaining work still needed to be completed.



The workshop's confidence was welcome.



It was not a delivery date.

That afternoon, Mingzhao received a short note from Sun Qiming.



He had completed another portion of the estate account review.



The note confirmed that the original counting sheets supported the distinction between total stock and stock available for immediate distribution.



It did not resolve the missing grain.



Sun had also found a reference to a transport payment with no destination entered.



The payment was recorded in a separate account.



There was no evidence yet connecting it to the grain movement.



Mingzhao read the note carefully.



Qiao Yue waited.



"Is there progress?"



"Some."



"Have they found the grain?"



"No."



"Have they found who received it?"



"No."



"Have they found the person who paid for the transport?"



"Not yet."



Qiao Yue considered this.



"Then what have they found?"



Mingzhao folded the note.



"That the records are more complicated than we hoped."



Qiao Yue looked disappointed.



Mingzhao placed the note beside the other documents.



"That is still useful."



She did not want an answer merely because the family was tired of waiting.



The grain discrepancy would remain open until the records supported a conclusion.



The separate transport payment would remain separate.



She would not combine two uncertain matters simply because they were inconvenient to investigate individually.

In the palace, Empress Dowager Wei received a report about the former Empress's garden visit.



It was brief.



The visit had been pleasant.



The former Empress had walked comfortably.



The Emperor had remained for the entire visit.



The Dowager had enjoyed the tea.



The report ended there.



She read it.



Then she asked her attendant, "Did Yuru say anything about returning to the palace?"



"No, Your Majesty."



"Did the Emperor ask?"



"Not in my hearing."



The Dowager looked at the paper.



She had once assumed that the former Empress would eventually return to her old responsibilities.



Not necessarily to the throne.



Not necessarily to the title.



But to the work.



The palace had been accustomed to her.



The servants trusted her judgment.



The Emperor relied on her.



The princes had learned from her.



The Dowager herself had become accustomed to knowing that a difficult matter could be brought to Yuru and would eventually be resolved.



Now the work was being divided.



Some matters were being handled well.



Some were being handled poorly.



Some were taking longer than before.



The palace was not collapsing.



It was learning.



The Dowager placed the report aside.



"Do not send her any new instructions."



The attendant bowed.



"Yes, Your Majesty."



The Dowager looked toward the window.



"Send her a personal invitation for the next time I wish to see her."



The attendant nodded.



This was a small distinction.



The Dowager intended it to remain one.

On the twenty-third day of the fifth month, two invitations had been accepted.



Xu Yuru would visit her family on the twenty-sixth.



Shen Mingzhao would attend the property settlement meeting on the twenty-seventh.



One event would involve a family meal.



The other would involve a family dispute.



Neither woman had any intention of allowing the event to determine the direction of her life.



Xu Yuru had begun to understand that she could remain connected to the palace without managing it.



Mingzhao had begun to understand that she could remain connected to her family without managing its estate.



The distinction was not yet comfortable for everyone around them.



But it was becoming clearer.



At the Xu residence, Duke Xu was still considering whether six dishes and fruit were enough.



At the Marquisate, the family representative was preparing twelve pages of disputed entries.



And in the Imperial Palace, the Second Prince was revising an estimate for twelve guests and an uncertain number of attendants.



The Great Yong Dynasty had several important matters before it.



Unfortunately, none of them involved a person willing to approve the fruit in advance.
