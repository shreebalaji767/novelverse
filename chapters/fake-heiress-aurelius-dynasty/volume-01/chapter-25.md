# **Chapter 25 — The Woman Who Met Both Mothers**

Edward did not sleep well.

The visitor register remained open on his laptop long after midnight.

He had enlarged the scanned page, compared the handwriting, checked the dates and read the accompanying notes until he could practically recite them.

**VISITOR: EFO-SL/07**

**DATE: SEVEN DAYS AFTER BIRTHS**

**PURPOSE: MATERNAL IDENTITY VERIFICATION**

**ACCOMPANIED BY: MARTIN HALE**

And beneath the entry:

**Neither mother was to be told which child had been assigned to her.**

The signature had been identified provisionally as Eleanor Vale.

That was the part Edward could not reconcile.

Eleanor's letters had suggested she was trying to protect the children. She had warned Margaret Ellis about the misuse of Alexander Aurelius's authority. She had instructed the hospital to keep both records separate. She had insisted that neither child be moved without independent verification.

Yet she had also met both mothers and apparently withheld information about the children's assignments.

Was she protecting them?

Was she protecting someone else?

Or had she been involved in the original arrangement before realising what had happened?

Edward closed the laptop.

There was no point drawing conclusions from one signature.

He needed the original records.

And he needed to speak to someone who remembered that day.

### **1. The Morning After the Discovery**

At the Valmont Estate, breakfast began with a disagreement about toast.

"You took the last two pieces."

Nicholas looked at Elena.

"They were on the plate."

"They were on my side."

"There are no sides to a breakfast plate."

"There are when someone has arranged their food."

Isabelle glanced at the plate.

"Didn't you say you weren't hungry?"

Nicholas looked betrayed.

"You're supposed to support me."

"I've known you for a short time. I'm still evaluating the situation."

Elena laughed.

Nicholas leaned back.

"Wonderful. I'm being judged in my own home."

Sebastian continued reading his tablet.

"That has been happening for years."

Richard lowered his newspaper.

"Can we have one breakfast without an argument?"

Nicholas considered the question.

"Possibly."

Elena looked at him.

"When?"

"Not today."

Catherine smiled despite herself.

For a little while, the room felt familiar.

Then Edward entered.

He looked as though he had spent the night working.

Nicholas noticed immediately.

"You look terrible."

"Good morning to you too."

"Did you sleep?"

"Some."

"How much?"

Edward ignored him and took his seat.

Catherine looked concerned.

"Is something wrong?"

Edward glanced at Richard.

"We found another hospital record."

Richard put down his newspaper.

"What kind?"

"A visitor register from the week the children were in the private nursery."

Sebastian's expression sharpened.

"Who visited?"

Edward hesitated.

"Eleanor Vale."

The table became quiet.

Isabelle looked from Edward to Catherine.

"Who is Eleanor Vale?"

"The wife of Charles Vale," Sebastian explained. "She was involved in the legal correspondence connected to the hospital."

Edward continued.

"Her signature appears beside a visit seven days after the births. She met both mothers separately."

Richard frowned.

"Both?"

"Victoria Aurelius and Catherine."

Catherine's hand stopped beside her teacup.

"She met me?"

"According to the register."

Catherine tried to remember.

A hospital corridor.

The soft noise of medical equipment.

A nurse bringing her baby to her.

Visitors coming and going.

She remembered Richard.

She remembered the doctor.

She remembered a woman from the hospital administration asking her to sign a form.

But Eleanor Vale?

Nothing.

"Did she speak to me?" Catherine asked.

"The register says the meetings were conducted separately."

"That doesn't tell us what she said."

"No."

Richard looked at Edward.

"What else?"

Edward glanced at the girls.

"The note says neither mother was to be told which child had been assigned to her."

Elena looked up.

"Assigned?"

Edward realised he had used a word that required explanation.

He chose his next sentence carefully.

"The hospital was following an arrangement involving two infants. The records suggest someone was trying to verify their identities before deciding where they should be placed."

Isabelle frowned.

"But why wouldn't the mothers be told?"

"That's what we're trying to find out."

Elena looked at Catherine.

"Do you remember meeting her?"

Catherine slowly shook her head.

"Not clearly. It was a long time ago."

Elena nodded.

She did not ask anything else.

But the word *assigned* stayed in her mind.

It made the whole situation sound less like a mistake and more like a decision someone had made.

### **2. What Catherine Remembered**

After breakfast, Catherine went to her sitting room.

Richard followed her.

He found her standing near the window, looking at the garden.

"You're thinking about the hospital."

She nodded.

"I remember that week."

"Of course."

"I remember the nurses bringing Elena to me. I remember being told that everything was fine."

Richard waited.

"But there were so many people. Doctors, administrators, representatives from the hospital. I was exhausted."

"Do you remember Eleanor Vale?"

Catherine shook her head.

"No."

"Could she have met you without introducing herself?"

"Possibly."

Richard moved closer.

"We can ask the hospital for the visitor schedule."

Catherine looked at him.

"What if she did meet me?"

"Then we'll find out what she wanted."

"What if she told me something?"

Richard was silent.

Catherine turned back towards the window.

"I've spent eighteen years believing that I brought my daughter home from that hospital."

Her voice was steady, but her hands were clasped tightly.

"I remember holding Elena. I remember her crying when the nurse placed her in my arms. I remember thinking she was very small."

Richard approached her.

"You loved her."

"I still do."

"I know."

Catherine looked at him.

"But what if someone knew she wasn't ours? What if someone knew the records were wrong and let us take her home anyway?"

Richard had no easy answer.

He placed a hand on her shoulder.

"We don't know that yet."

Catherine nodded.

She wanted to believe him.

But the visitor register had introduced a new possibility.

Someone had met both mothers before the infants were discharged.

Someone had known the placements were uncertain.

And someone had made sure the mothers did not know.

### **3. A Name in Eleanor's Correspondence**

At Vale International Legal Group, Jonathan Pierce was reviewing the remaining documents from Eleanor Vale's estate.

The discovery of her hospital visit had changed the direction of the investigation.

Until then, Eleanor had appeared mainly as a person who had written letters and preserved documents.

Now she was directly connected to the hospital.

Pierce requested the original visitor register and arranged for a handwriting specialist to compare the signature.

He also contacted Margaret Vale.

"Did your aunt ever mention visiting the hospital?"

Margaret was quiet for a moment.

"Yes."

Pierce sat forward.

"When?"

"Not specifically. She said she had once been involved in a matter concerning two newborn children."

"Did she mention their mothers?"

"No."

"Did she say why she became involved?"

Margaret hesitated.

"She said Charles had asked her to help verify something."

"What?"

"I don't know. But I remember her saying that she had been asked to make sure two women were given the same information."

Pierce stopped writing.

"The same information?"

"Yes. She said one woman must not be told something that the other had not been told."

"Did she explain what?"

"No. I was younger. I didn't ask."

Pierce looked at the documents on his desk.

"Did she ever say she regretted getting involved?"

Margaret's voice became quieter.

"Yes."

"When?"

"Near the end of her life. She said she had made a decision in a hospital corridor that she could never properly explain."

Pierce leaned forward.

"What decision?"

Margaret took a breath.

"She said she had believed she was buying time."

### **4. The First Independent Examination**

The handwriting specialist's preliminary report arrived shortly after noon.

Edward, Adrian and Pierce attended a secure video conference.

The specialist displayed two documents.

The first was the visitor register.

The second was a letter from Eleanor Vale dated several days after the births.

"The signatures are consistent," the specialist explained. "The letter and the visitor register were almost certainly written by the same person."

Edward nodded.

"So it was Eleanor."

"That is the current conclusion. A full forensic report will follow, but there is no obvious discrepancy."

Adrian looked at the visitor entry.

"Can you tell whether the note beneath the signature was written at the same time?"

The specialist enlarged the handwriting.

"No. It appears to be a separate entry. Different pen, different pressure and possibly a different writer."

"Can you identify the writer?"

"Not from this image alone."

Pierce displayed the handwritten instruction.

**Neither mother was to be told which child had been assigned to her.**

The specialist studied it.

"I would not attribute this to Eleanor based on the current material."

Edward frowned.

"Why?"

"The handwriting is different."

Adrian leaned forward.

"Could it belong to the hospital administrator?"

"Possibly. But there isn't enough material to compare."

The specialist moved to another document.

A small handwritten note from the hospital's maternity archive.

It contained a short instruction:

**Both mothers have been informed that identity verification remains incomplete. No further explanation is authorised.**

Edward looked at it.

"That sounds like they were told something."

"Yes," the specialist replied. "But it does not establish who informed them or what was said."

Pierce examined the date.

"Same day as Eleanor's visit."

Adrian looked at Edward.

"Then we need to know what each mother was actually told."

Edward nodded.

"And whether the statements were identical."

### **5. The Two Conversations**

Victoria Aurelius agreed to meet Catherine.

The meeting was arranged through the legal representatives, with Richard and Alexander present.

They chose a private room at a neutral legal office rather than either family's residence.

Catherine arrived first.

She sat beside Richard, her hands folded in her lap.

When Victoria entered, Catherine stood.

For a moment, neither woman seemed certain what to say.

They had never met before.

Yet their names had appeared together in documents for eighteen years.

Victoria approached.

"Mrs. Valmont."

"Mrs. Aurelius."

They exchanged polite greetings.

Alexander and Richard shook hands.

Adrian and Edward sat at the far end of the table.

The meeting began with the documents.

Pierce explained the visitor register and the confirmation that Eleanor Vale had visited the hospital seven days after the births.

Victoria listened carefully.

"Did she meet me?"

"According to the register, yes," Pierce replied.

Victoria looked at Catherine.

"She may have met both of us."

Catherine nodded.

"Apparently."

Victoria turned back towards Pierce.

"Do you know what she told me?"

"No. We are trying to establish that."

"Then I would like to tell you what I remember."

Everyone became quiet.

Victoria folded her hands.

"I was told that my daughter was undergoing additional medical observation. The nurse said it was routine. I was not particularly concerned because I had been told the baby was healthy."

Alexander looked at her.

"You never mentioned that."

"I didn't think it was important. They told me she would be returned to me after the checks were complete."

Edward leaned forward.

"Did anyone say the baby could not be identified?"

Victoria shook her head.

"No."

Catherine looked down.

"When I was asked about the hospital records, I was told there had been an administrative delay. I was also told Elena was healthy."

Richard turned towards her.

"You never told me about an administrative delay."

"I thought it was paperwork."

Edward asked, "Did anyone tell you that the child's identity was uncertain?"

Catherine shook her head.

"No."

Victoria looked at her.

"Then we were given different explanations."

"Perhaps," Catherine replied.

The room became quiet.

Alexander spoke.

"Do you remember Eleanor Vale?"

Victoria closed her eyes briefly.

"I remember a woman visiting my room. I thought she was from the hospital's administration."

Catherine looked up.

"Do you remember what she looked like?"

"Not clearly. She was polite. She asked whether I had been told about the additional verification."

Catherine's expression changed.

"She asked me something similar."

Everyone looked towards her.

Catherine continued.

"She asked whether I had been told that the hospital was checking the infant records. I said yes."

Edward sat forward.

"Did she ask you to sign anything?"

"No."

Victoria shook her head.

"Not me either."

Pierce wrote something down.

"Did either of you receive a document explaining the verification process?"

Both women shook their heads.

Victoria looked towards Catherine.

"What happened after that?"

Catherine hesitated.

"I was told everything was settled."

Victoria's expression changed.

"So was I."

The two women looked at each other.

For the first time, they understood that they might have been given carefully controlled explanations by people who knew considerably more than they did.

### **6. A Question Without an Answer**

The meeting continued for another forty minutes.

They compared memories.

Victoria remembered a woman asking whether she had chosen a name for her daughter.

Catherine remembered being asked whether she intended to return to work soon.

Neither could identify Eleanor with certainty.

Both remembered that the hospital had described the additional checks as administrative.

Neither remembered signing a document authorising a transfer.

And neither had been told that the infants' identities were uncertain.

Finally, Richard asked the question that had been troubling him.

"Did either of you receive a visit from someone claiming to represent the other family?"

Victoria shook her head.

Catherine considered it.

"No. But I remember a man visiting the room after the nurse had left."

Edward looked at her.

"What man?"

"I don't know. He wore a dark suit. He said he was from the hospital administration."

"Did he give you his name?"

"No."

"Did he ask about Elena?"

"He asked whether I was comfortable with the arrangements."

Richard frowned.

"What arrangements?"

Catherine shook her head.

"I didn't understand what he meant. I thought he was referring to the medical checks."

Victoria looked at her.

"Did he mention another child?"

"No."

Edward opened his notebook.

"Can you remember anything distinctive about him?"

Catherine thought for a long time.

"He had a ring."

"What kind?"

"Silver, I think. It had a dark stone."

Edward glanced at the old hospital photograph from Chapter 6.

The man in the corridor had worn a lapel pin.

Not a ring.

But the description was useful.

"Did he say anything else?"

Catherine looked towards Richard.

"He said the hospital had to be certain that the children went to the correct families."

Nobody spoke.

The sentence hung in the room.

Victoria slowly turned towards Catherine.

"Did he use the word children?"

Catherine nodded.

"Yes."

"Not child?"

"No. Children."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

For eighteen years, she had believed she was searching for one missing daughter.

Now she was beginning to understand that the hospital had been dealing with two children from the beginning.

### **7. The Girl Who Wanted to Leave**

At the same time, Elena was standing in the university admissions office.

She had submitted the preliminary exchange application.

The administrative officer reviewed the documents.

"Your academic transcript is complete. We still need the faculty recommendation and the language proficiency confirmation."

"I can submit both by Friday."

"That should be fine."

Elena nodded.

"Thank you."

She stepped outside and checked her phone.

There was a message from Clara.

**Clara:** Did you submit it?

**Elena:** Preliminary application, yes.

**Clara:** Excellent. Emergency biscuits will be delivered in celebration.

**Elena:** I haven't been accepted yet.

**Clara:** We celebrate the application. We panic about the result later.

Elena smiled.

She was about to reply when she noticed Isabelle sitting on a bench near the entrance.

Isabelle was looking at her phone.

"Are you waiting for someone?" Elena asked.

Isabelle looked up.

"Not exactly. I finished early."

Elena sat beside her.

"How did the foundation meeting go?"

"Good. Mrs. Bennett gave me some work to review."

"Are you enjoying it?"

"Yes. It's different from what I expected."

Elena nodded.

They sat quietly for a moment.

Then Isabelle noticed the application folder in Elena's hand.

"Did you submit it?"

"Part of it."

"Singapore?"

"Yes."

Isabelle smiled.

"That's good."

Elena looked at her.

"Have you thought about applying somewhere?"

"I have."

"Where?"

"Possibly the same exchange programme. But I haven't decided."

Elena looked surprised.

"Really?"

"Why not?"

"I'm interested in studying abroad. I just don't know whether I want to go immediately."

Elena considered that.

"You don't have to decide now."

"I know."

Isabelle looked towards the university buildings.

"Do you think it would be strange if we ended up at the same university?"

Elena smiled.

"Not particularly."

"Even after everything?"

"Especially after everything. It would be nice to know someone."

Isabelle looked at her.

"You really don't mind?"

"No."

Elena paused.

"But I don't want you to choose a university because of me."

Isabelle laughed softly.

"I was about to say the same thing."

They both smiled.

For a moment, the complicated circumstances of their lives seemed far away.

### **8. The Second Signature**

At 7:10 p.m., Jonathan Pierce received a call from the legal repository.

The archivist had located a supplementary document associated with Holding 114-B.

It was not part of Box 114.

It was an independent receipt.

The document had been created on the same day Eleanor Vale visited the hospital.

Pierce requested an electronic copy.

When it arrived, he opened it immediately.

The receipt confirmed that two maternal identification pages had been received by Charles Vale.

But beneath his signature was a second signature.

A witness.

Pierce enlarged the image.

He recognised the handwriting.

It was the same signature that appeared beneath the hospital visitor register.

Not Eleanor Vale's.

The specialist had been right.

The handwritten instruction had been added by someone else.

Pierce examined the witness field.

The name was faint but readable.

**DR. SAMUEL WHITMORE.**

The hospital administrator.

Pierce stared at the document.

Whitmore had died eleven years earlier.

He could no longer be questioned.

But the receipt contained one additional detail.

The witness had signed not only to confirm receipt of the pages.

He had also confirmed that the contents were complete.

Pierce immediately called Edward.

"We found the second signature."

"Who?"

"Dr. Samuel Whitmore."

Edward sat up.

"The hospital administrator?"

"Yes."

"What did he confirm?"

"That both maternal identification pages were transferred to Charles Vale."

Edward was silent.

"Anything else?"

Pierce looked at the final section.

"Yes. There's a handwritten statement."

"What does it say?"

Pierce read it carefully.

**The original maternal identities have been verified against the mothers' personal records. The infant assignments remain provisional.**

Edward frowned.

"Then they verified the mothers, but not the children."

"That's what the document says."

"Why?"

Pierce examined the bottom of the page.

There was a final sentence, written in smaller handwriting.

**No infant may be identified through the maternal records alone.**

Edward understood.

The original pages could establish which women had given birth.

They could not establish which child had been delivered to which woman.

The two questions had been separated deliberately.

"Jonathan," Edward said, "who requested that distinction?"

Pierce looked at the signature field.

There was no name.

Only the same reference that had followed them for weeks.

**EFO-SL/07.**

### **9. The Person Who Had Known**

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander read the receipt in silence.

Victoria sat beside him.

Adrian stood near the desk.

Lucien and Damien had joined them.

Julian entered halfway through the discussion, carrying a notebook.

He had written two new items on his list.

**37. Find out what kind of books she likes.**

**38. Ask whether she likes travelling.**

He stopped when he noticed the adults' expressions.

"Is something wrong?"

Alexander looked at him.

"We found another document."

"About my sister?"

"About the hospital."

Julian approached.

Adrian explained the discovery.

The mothers had been independently identified.

The infants had not.

The distinction had been written into the legal record eighteen years earlier.

Julian looked confused.

"But if they knew who the mothers were, why didn't they know which babies belonged to them?"

"Because the hospital records had become unreliable," Adrian explained.

"Couldn't they just check the babies?"

"They tried to establish independent identity. The original identification pages were separated from the infant records."

Julian frowned.

"Who would do that?"

Nobody answered.

Victoria looked at the document.

"Someone who wanted the mothers' identities preserved but didn't want the children assigned based on those records."

Alexander turned towards her.

"That is one possibility."

"Then why keep everything secret?"

"That is what we need to discover."

Julian looked at the receipt again.

"Does this mean we're closer?"

Adrian nodded.

"Yes."

Julian smiled faintly.

"Good."

He looked at his notebook.

"Then perhaps I should add another item."

Lucien raised an eyebrow.

"What now?"

Julian wrote carefully.

**39. Ask whether she wants to see the family library.**

Damien glanced at the list.

"You're assuming she likes books."

"Everyone likes something."

"Not necessarily libraries."

"Then I'll ask."

Victoria smiled.

She wanted to tell him that they did not even know whether the child he imagined was the girl they were searching for.

But she did not want to extinguish his hope.

So she simply said, "That sounds like a good question."

### **10. The Identity of the Originating Representative**

At 10:35 p.m., Edward received one final message from Jonathan Pierce.

It contained a scanned page from the repository's original correspondence index.

The page had been partially damaged, but one section remained readable.

**EFO-SL/07 — ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE**

**AUTHORITY SOURCE: PRIVATE FAMILY INSTRUCTION**

**VERIFICATION OFFICER: CHARLES VALE**

**WITNESS: ELEANOR VALE**

**FINAL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED FROM: ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE**

The name field had been covered by a strip of paper.

Edward enlarged the scan.

There was writing beneath the strip.

The archivist had not noticed it.

He adjusted the contrast.

A few letters emerged.

Not a complete name.

Just an initial.

**M.**

Edward stared at it.

There were several possibilities.

Martin Hale.

Margaret Ellis.

Someone from the hospital.

Someone from either family.

He opened the original hospital photograph.

The man in the dark suit stood beside Dr. Whitmore.

The lapel pin was visible.

Edward zoomed in.

The emblem was not the Aurelius crest.

It was a different symbol.

A circle surrounding a single letter.

The letter M.

He immediately sent the photograph to the archivist.

Then he opened the correspondence index again.

At the bottom of the page, almost invisible beneath the damaged section, was a handwritten note.

It had been added after the file was closed.

**The originating representative has withdrawn authority. No further action is permitted without personal confirmation.**

Edward read the sentence slowly.

The originating representative had not simply disappeared.

At some point, the person behind EFO-SL/07 had withdrawn their authority.

That explained why the children had never been transferred according to the original plan.

It might also explain why the legal file had remained sealed.

But it raised a more important question.

If the originating representative had withdrawn authority, who had been responsible for the children after that?

Edward looked at the date.

The withdrawal had been recorded nine days after the births.

The same day the private nursery register recorded both infants as discharged.

He called Adrian.

"We have the date of the withdrawal."

"And?"

"It was the day both children left the private nursery."

Adrian was silent.

Edward continued.

"Someone withdrew the original authority on the exact day the children were released."

"Do we know who?"

"No."

"Can the repository identify the person?"

"The name has been deliberately obscured."

Edward looked at the final handwritten note.

Then he noticed something else.

The ink beneath the obscured name had left an impression on the page below.

He carefully enlarged the image.

A second initial appeared.

Not M.

Not E.

Not V.

It was the letter **A**.

And beneath it, a fragment of a surname.

**...RELLI.**

Edward's fingers stopped over the keyboard.

He had seen that surname before.

Not in the hospital records.

Not in the Vale archive.

But in the old corporate directory of Aurelius Global.

A former senior officer.

Someone who had worked directly under Alexander Aurelius.

Someone who had left the family office eighteen years ago.

And whose departure had never been explained.

**The name was Adrian Aurelius's predecessor as Director of Family Affairs: Marcus Morelli.**
