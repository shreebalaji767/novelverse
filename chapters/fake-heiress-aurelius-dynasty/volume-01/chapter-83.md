# **Chapter 83 — The Signature That Released the Missing File**

Edward did not immediately call Victoria Aurelius.

He read the release receipt again.

**Annex C — Part One.**

**Receiving authority: Dr. Samuel Hale.**

**Return condition: To be returned only after maternal identity reconciliation.**

And beneath it, the signature.

**Victoria Aurelius.**

There was no obvious reason for the signature to be there.

Victoria had received correspondence concerning Isabella's maternity record. She had preserved the empty identification pouch and the warning from Marianne Cross. She had not received the original IV-204 band.

She had also been kept away from Annex C.

Yet someone had recorded her as the person authorising the release of its first part.

Edward placed the receipt beside the original letter from Marianne.

The handwriting appeared similar.

He examined the signature more carefully.

It might have been genuine.

It might have been copied.

Or Victoria might have signed a document without being told what it authorised.

He picked up his telephone.

"Jonathan?"

"Yes?"

"I need the complete release file for Annex C, Part One. Not just the receipt."

"I'll request it."

"And arrange a meeting with Victoria."

"Should I tell her about the signature?"

"No. I'll show her the document myself."

Edward put down the phone.

For eighteen years, the Aurelius family had been searching for a missing daughter.

Now their mother's signature had appeared on a document that might explain why the investigation had never reached a conclusion.

He wanted to know whether she remembered signing it.

More importantly, he wanted to know what she thought she had signed.

Victoria received Edward at the Aurelius estate the following afternoon.

Alexander was present.

Adrian had joined them after finishing a meeting at the company's headquarters. He had brought a notebook and a copy of the archive register.

Victoria looked at Edward as he entered.

"Have you found Annex C?"

"Part of it."

"Which part?"

"Part One was released from the secure archive on the forty-third day."

Alexander frowned.

"Released to whom?"

"Dr. Samuel Hale."

Victoria looked at the document Edward placed on the table.

She read the heading.

Then she reached the signature.

Her expression changed.

"That's mine."

Edward watched her.

"You're certain?"

"Yes."

"Do you remember signing it?"

Victoria read the document again.

"No."

Adrian leaned forward.

"Mother, are you saying you signed something without remembering?"

"I'm saying the signature is mine. I don't remember signing this particular receipt."

Edward took out another sheet.

"Could you have signed it as part of a larger document?"

Victoria examined the date.

"Perhaps."

"Do you remember meeting Dr. Hale?"

"No."

"Did you authorise him to receive medical records?"

Victoria shook her head.

"Not directly."

Alexander looked at the receipt.

"Could someone have presented this as a routine archive authorisation?"

Victoria remained silent.

Edward placed the second document beside it.

It was the letter from Marianne Cross, dated the seventeenth day.

Victoria looked at it.

"That's the letter I received."

"Do you remember anything else arriving with it?"

"The empty pouch. The identification reference. And a folded instruction."

Edward showed her the note discovered in the archive.

Victoria studied it.

"Yes. This is the note."

"Do you remember signing a separate authorisation around the forty-third day?"

Victoria closed her eyes briefly.

"I remember signing a group of documents concerning the preservation of the hospital records. There were several requests for access and copies. I was told that the material should remain protected until the identity review was completed."

"Did you know Dr. Hale would receive Annex C, Part One?"

"No."

"Did you know the file contained the maternal records of both you and Isabella Vale?"

Victoria looked at him.

"No."

The answer came without hesitation.

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Who presented those documents to you?"

"I don't remember the name. I was not handling the archive correspondence personally."

"Was it someone from the Family Office?"

"Possibly."

Adrian opened his notebook.

"Could we obtain the complete authorisation packet?"

Edward nodded.

"That's what I've requested."

Victoria looked at the signature again.

"I would never have knowingly authorised the release of a file containing both mothers' identities to a doctor who had been involved in the disputed transfers."

Edward did not contradict her.

"But you may have signed a document that was used for that purpose."

Victoria looked up.

"Yes."

The room fell quiet.

For the first time, the Aurelius family had a document showing that Victoria's own authority had been used to release part of the missing identity file.

It did not establish whether she had been deceived.

But it made one thing clear.

Her signature alone could no longer be treated as proof that she had personally approved the action described in a document.

Adrian was the first to speak.

"If the release was made under Mother's authority, who prepared the paperwork?"

Edward answered carefully.

"We don't know yet."

"Could it have been Charles?"

"Possibly."

"Or someone in the Family Office?"

"Also possible."

Alexander looked at Victoria.

"Do you remember anyone explaining why the records had to be separated?"

Victoria nodded slowly.

"Eleanor once told me that the maternal records and receiving records should not be combined until the originating hospitals had completed their verification."

Edward looked up.

"When did she tell you that?"

"Not directly. I remember hearing it during a discussion about the missing identification material."

"Was that before or after the forty-third day?"

Victoria considered the question.

"After."

"Did she mention Annex C?"

"No."

"Did she mention Dr. Hale?"

"No."

Edward made a note.

"Did you ever ask why the file was divided into two parts?"

Victoria looked towards the window.

"No. I was told that one part contained medical material and the other contained restricted receiving information."

"Who told you?"

"I believe it was Helena Brooks."

Alexander turned towards her.

"Helena?"

"She was overseeing the Family Office archive at the time."

Adrian immediately wrote down the name.

Edward closed his notebook.

"Then we should speak to her."

Helena Brooks had retired from the Aurelius Family Office several years earlier.

She agreed to meet Edward at the estate, provided Alexander was present.

She arrived carrying an old document case.

After the introductions, Edward placed the release receipt before her.

Helena examined it.

"I remember this."

"Do you remember preparing it?"

"No."

"Do you remember receiving it?"

"Yes."

Edward looked at her.

"Explain."

Helena placed the paper down.

"I was responsible for ensuring that restricted documents were properly recorded. I remember receiving a copy of the authorisation, but I did not prepare the original release."

"Who did?"

"I don't know."

"Did you verify Victoria's signature?"

Helena hesitated.

"I checked that the document carried the correct authority reference."

"That wasn't my question."

"No. I did not independently verify her signature."

Edward waited.

Helena continued.

"There was an assumption that documents carrying the Family Office authorisation code had already been approved."

"Who made that assumption?"

"Everyone involved in the process."

Alexander's voice was controlled.

"That is not an acceptable answer."

Helena looked at him.

"I agree."

Edward placed the archive register beside the receipt.

"Did you know Annex C had been divided into two parts?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because the originating medical material and the receiving identity material were subject to different access restrictions."

"Who decided that?"

"Charles Vale proposed the arrangement."

Alexander's expression changed.

"Charles proposed how our daughter's identity records should be handled?"

Helena lowered her eyes.

"He presented himself as the administrative coordinator for the investigation. The Family Office was dealing with several competing requests at the time."

"That does not answer the question."

"No, sir."

Edward intervened.

"Was Charles authorised to make that decision?"

"Not independently. He had authority to coordinate the external records process, but he did not have authority to determine the children's identities."

"Did he ever claim that he did?"

"Not openly."

Edward wrote something down.

"Do you remember who requested the release to Dr. Hale?"

Helena thought for several seconds.

"I remember receiving a request from the medical review office."

"Who submitted it?"

"Gabriel Vale."

Edward looked at her.

"Gabriel?"

"Yes."

"Did he say why Dr. Hale needed the records?"

"He said Dr. Hale was conducting an originating identity reconciliation."

"Was that the same comparison that had been suspended earlier?"

"I don't know."

"Did you see a report from Dr. Hale?"

"No."

"Did you ever receive confirmation that the reconciliation was completed?"

Helena shook her head.

"No."

Edward placed the receipt in front of her again.

"Then why was the file released?"

Helena looked uncomfortable.

"Because the request carried Victoria's authorisation."

Victoria spoke quietly.

"But I don't remember authorising it."

Helena looked at her.

For a moment, she appeared genuinely distressed.

"Your signature was on the document."

"I know."

"I believed it had been properly approved."

Victoria looked at the papers.

"So did I."

Edward returned to the question of the two parts.

"Was Part Two ever released?"

Helena opened her document case.

She searched through several old indexes before finding a handwritten entry.

"Part Two remained in the secure archive."

"Until when?"

"Until the facility was closed."

"Was it transferred to the central archive?"

"Yes."

"Was it ever opened?"

"Not according to the custody register."

Edward leaned forward.

"Do you remember who was permitted to request access?"

Helena turned a page.

"Charles Vale. Eleanor Vale. Margaret Voss. Adrian Holt. And the originating medical authority."

"Was Victoria included?"

"She could authorise access, but the archive would not release the contents directly to her without completion of the medical verification process."

Victoria looked at Helena.

"Why?"

"Because the file was classified as restricted identity material."

"But it contained my maternal record."

"Yes."

"And I was not allowed to see it?"

Helena looked down.

"That was the procedure."

Victoria did not respond.

Alexander stood and walked towards the window.

Adrian remained seated, his expression unreadable.

Edward understood why the procedure had been established.

The file contained sensitive information concerning two mothers and two children whose identities had not been verified.

But a procedure intended to protect the records had become a barrier between the mothers and the information about their own children.

And no one had apparently questioned that arrangement.

Before leaving, Edward asked Helena one final question.

"Did you ever see the actual contents of Annex C, Part Two?"

"No."

"Did you know who the receiving identity custodian was?"

"Margaret Voss."

"Was she allowed to open it?"

"No."

"Did she ever request permission?"

"Not to my knowledge."

"Then who knew what was inside?"

Helena looked at the archive register.

"Charles Vale."

"Anyone else?"

"Gabriel Vale may have known some of the contents."

"Eleanor?"

"I don't know."

Edward placed the register back into his folder.

"Thank you."

As Helena stood, Victoria spoke.

"Helena."

The older woman turned.

"Yes, ma'am?"

"If you had known that the records were being used to keep a mother from knowing where her child was, would you have allowed it?"

Helena did not answer immediately.

"No," she said at last.

Victoria nodded.

"Neither would I."

At the Valmont estate, Isabelle had been trying to concentrate on a university course catalogue.

Her interest in business and economics had grown over the previous weeks.

She had begun looking at degree programmes, reading about corporate governance and examining how large companies managed their operations.

She wanted to understand the world her biological family belonged to.

But she did not want to become someone whose only qualification was a famous surname.

Catherine entered the room carrying tea.

"You're still studying?"

"I'm comparing programmes."

"Have you found anything you like?"

"Several."

Catherine sat opposite her.

"That's good."

Isabelle turned the catalogue around.

"This one has a strong economics programme. This one has a business law module. And this one offers a placement year."

Catherine looked at the pages.

"Have you thought about visiting the universities?"

"Not yet."

"There's no need to decide immediately."

"I know."

Isabelle closed the catalogue.

"Mother, can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"If Elena had never been identified as unrelated to the family, would you have encouraged her to study whatever she wanted?"

Catherine looked at her.

"Yes."

"Even if she wanted to study something completely different from the family business?"

"Yes."

"Would Father have agreed?"

Catherine smiled faintly.

"He would have argued about practical considerations, but he would not have forbidden it."

Isabelle looked down.

"I don't want her to think I took her place."

Catherine's expression softened.

"You didn't."

"I know you say that."

"Because it is true."

Isabelle was quiet.

After a moment, she asked, "Does she still speak to Nicholas?"

"Yes."

"And Sebastian?"

"Occasionally."

"What about Edward?"

Catherine hesitated.

"He is busy with the investigation."

Isabelle looked at her.

"Does Elena know how much he's doing?"

"I don't think so."

Isabelle turned the catalogue closed.

"Then perhaps someone should tell her."

Catherine watched her carefully.

"Would you like to?"

Isabelle considered it.

"Not yet."

She did not want to contact Elena simply because she felt guilty.

If they were going to build a relationship, it would have to be something more honest than that.

Elena, meanwhile, was sitting in front of her laptop.

Her interview presentation was finally complete.

She had reduced it to twelve slides.

The first explained the problem.

The second introduced the proposed system.

The remaining slides demonstrated how a small business could record sales, expenses and stock without expensive equipment.

She had also included a section on data recovery.

She knew that a record-keeping system was useless if a shopkeeper lost everything after a phone stopped working or a browser was accidentally closed.

Her solution was deliberately simple.

Local storage.

Exportable backups.

Clear warnings.

No subscription.

No dependence on a paid service.

She reviewed the final slide.

**Technology should reduce work, not create more of it.**

Elena smiled.

That was the sentence she wanted the interviewers to remember.

Her telephone rang.

It was Catherine.

"Hello?"

"Have you finished preparing?"

"Almost."

"Are you nervous?"

"A little."

"That's normal."

Elena leaned back in her chair.

"I think I'm more worried about explaining the technical parts."

"Then explain them as you would to someone who has never used the system."

"That's what I'm planning to do."

"Good."

Elena smiled.

"Thank you."

After the call, she saved another copy of her presentation.

Then she checked her application portal.

The interview confirmation remained unchanged.

Three days.

She closed the laptop and went downstairs.

For once, she was looking forward to something that had nothing to do with the Valmont family, the Aurelius dynasty or the mystery surrounding her birth.

It was a small thing.

A student programme.

A presentation.

A chance to build something useful.

But it belonged to her.

Edward received the full archive release packet two days later.

It contained the original request, the authorisation page, the custody receipt and a covering memorandum.

He read the memorandum first.

**Request submitted by: Gabriel Vale.**

**Purpose: Completion of maternal identity reconciliation.**

**Material requested: Annex C — Part One.**

**Receiving officer: Dr. Samuel Hale.**

The next page was Victoria's authorisation.

It was part of a larger document.

The title was:

**General Preservation and Medical Review Authority.**

Victoria had signed the first page.

The authority covered several categories of records.

But the specific request for Annex C had been attached as a separate schedule.

Edward examined the schedule.

It carried the same authorisation code.

But there was no handwritten approval from Victoria beside the Annex C entry.

Instead, the schedule bore a stamp:

**APPROVED UNDER GENERAL AUTHORITY.**

Edward looked at the date.

The general authority had been signed on the thirty-ninth day.

The Annex C request had been submitted on the forty-second day.

The release had occurred on the forty-third.

The signature was genuine.

The authority reference was genuine.

But the specific release had never been personally approved by Victoria.

Edward turned the page.

The final document was a medical receiving acknowledgement.

Dr. Hale had signed for Part One.

There was a note beneath his signature.

**Maternal material received. Receiving identity schedule not included.**

Edward stared at the sentence.

Part One contained the maternal records.

Part Two contained the receiving identities.

Hale had received only the maternal section.

He had not received the information needed to connect the mothers to the children.

Edward read the acknowledgement again.

The release had not been a completed identity reconciliation.

It had been the release of only one half of the evidence.

And someone had ensured that the doctor conducting the review received the maternal information without the receiving identity schedule.

Jonathan arrived shortly afterwards.

Edward showed him the documents.

"So Hale never received Part Two?"

"Correct."

"Then what was he supposed to reconcile?"

"That is the question."

Jonathan read the covering memorandum.

"Perhaps he was supposed to compare the maternal records with the hospital records he already had."

"Perhaps."

"But without the receiving identity schedule, he couldn't establish which infant had been assigned to which family."

"Exactly."

Jonathan looked at the authorisation page.

"Victoria's signature was genuine, but the specific release was not personally approved."

"Yes."

"That means someone used her general authority to obtain Part One."

Edward nodded.

"And they did it under the stated purpose of completing the identity reconciliation."

Jonathan placed the document down.

"Who benefited from keeping Part Two sealed?"

Edward looked at the list of authorised custodians.

Charles Vale.

Gabriel Vale.

Eleanor Vale.

Margaret Voss.

Adrian Holt.

And the originating medical authority.

"At this stage," Edward said, "we don't know who benefited."

He turned to the final page.

There was another handwritten instruction.

It had been added after Hale acknowledged receipt.

**Part Two must not be released until the receiving family has been notified.**

Edward frowned.

"Jonathan."

"What?"

"Look at the wording."

Jonathan read it.

"The receiving family has to be notified before Part Two can be released."

"Yes."

"Which receiving family?"

"There were two mothers and two receiving identity records."

Jonathan looked at him.

"So the instruction doesn't identify which family."

Edward nodded.

"And it doesn't say who is responsible for notifying them."

He turned the page over.

At the bottom was a small reference.

**Notification authority: R.V.**

Edward stared at the initials.

He had seen them before.

Not in the hospital records.

Not in the Family Office archive.

But in a document recovered from the Valmont legal files.

He opened his notebook and searched through the entries.

There it was.

**R.V. — Receiving Verification.**

Not a person's initials.

A designation.

A role.

Someone had been appointed to verify the receiving identity and notify the relevant family.

But the archive contained no name beside the designation.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"We need the appointment register."

Jonathan nodded.

"I'll find it."

Edward returned to the release packet.

For eighteen years, two mothers had been told different things about their children.

One had been given no confirmed account of where her daughter went.

The other had been told that a child had died, without a complete medical death record.

Now the investigation had uncovered a procedure requiring a receiving family to be notified before the identities could be released.

But no one had recorded who was responsible for carrying out that notification.

Edward closed the folder.

Somewhere in the missing appointment register was the name of the person who had been entrusted with the final step.

And that person might have been the only one authorised to tell two mothers which children had been placed under their names.
