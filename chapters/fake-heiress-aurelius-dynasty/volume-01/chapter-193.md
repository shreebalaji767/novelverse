# **Chapter 193 — The Instruction Daniel Did Not Write**

Daniel Mercer arrived at 8:52.

He carried a thin notebook, a sealed envelope, and no briefcase.

Edward noticed the envelope first.

“Is that related to PA-44/03?”

“Yes.”

“Why wasn't it included in the original response?”

“Because it wasn't part of the routing file.”

Daniel placed the envelope on the table but did not open it.

Sophie sat opposite him, her laptop ready.

Edward glanced at the clock.

“You wrote in your confirmation that you could explain the routing, but did not originate the instruction.”

“That's correct.”

“I hadn't asked whether you originated it.”

“I thought it was important to clarify.”

“Why?”

Daniel considered his answer.

“Because the instruction was circulated under an administrative reference that made it look as though it came from Programme Administration. I handled its routing. I didn't want that to become an assumption about authorship.”

Edward leaned back.

“Then let's start with the document.”

Daniel opened the envelope.

Inside was a printed copy of PA-44/03.

The instruction was familiar, but this copy had a header absent from the gateway extract.

**Preparation status: Restricted working instruction.**

**Originating office: Not displayed on distribution copy.**

**Authorising authority: To be confirmed.**

The final paragraph was identical.

*Please obtain the independence criteria before the originating office is asked to identify a replacement. Do not treat the correspondence contact as the nominating authority.*

Edward examined the page.

“Where did you obtain this?”

“It was in my retained working papers.”

“Why?”

“Because I was asked to route it, and the original instruction was not returned to my desk.”

“Who asked you?”

“A Programme Administration supervisor.”

“Name?”

Daniel opened his notebook.

“Patricia Moore.”

Edward looked up.

“Patricia Moore?”

“Yes. She was supervising the administrative coordination desk at the time.”

“Did she give you the instruction personally?”

“Not personally. It came through the internal task queue.”

“Was it signed?”

“No.”

“Did it carry an approval reference?”

“Only PA-44/03.”

Edward looked at the copy again.

“Then how did you know it was valid?”

“I didn't know it was a substantive instruction. I knew it was a routing task.”

Daniel explained that the task had arrived in the Programme Administration queue on day 44 at 14:08.

It instructed him to forward the independence-criteria query to the restricted correspondence office for transmission through the existing C-19 route.

His responsibility was limited to checking the destination, preserving the reference, and recording the dispatch.

He had not been asked to identify a candidate.

He had not been asked to approve a reviewer.

He had not been asked to make a decision about P-04.

“Did you understand that C-19 was only a correspondence location?” Edward asked.

“Yes.”

“Then why did the later day-46 record describe it as the nomination source?”

Daniel looked at the page.

“I didn't write that description.”

“Did you see it?”

“Not on day 46.”

“When did you first see it?”

“When the closure package was assembled.”

“Who assembled that package?”

“I prepared several sections. The final package was consolidated by the Programme Administration desk.”

“Did you challenge the classification?”

“I asked whether the source field should say correspondence route rather than nomination source.”

Edward's expression sharpened.

“When?”

“During the closure review.”

“Which day?”

“Day 151, I believe.”

“Do you have a record?”

Daniel nodded.

“I requested that the wording be checked. The response was that the original entry should remain unchanged because it reflected the recorded transmission source.”

Edward exchanged a glance with Sophie.

“Who gave you that response?”

“I don't remember the name. It came from a shared account.”

“Which one?”

“Programme Administration Review Desk.”

“Did you accept the explanation?”

“I accepted that the historical entry would remain unchanged. I did not agree that it established a nomination.”

Edward let the silence settle.

“Did you make that distinction in writing?”

“Yes.”

Daniel opened his notebook and removed a folded sheet.

It was a contemporaneous working note, dated day 151.

**The C-19 entry should be read as the correspondence route from which the query was transmitted. It does not establish that C-19 originated, authorised, or submitted a candidate nomination. No alternative candidate is recorded in the appointment register.**

Edward read it carefully.

“Why wasn't this included in the closure package?”

“Because it was a working note, not a formal correction.”

“Who decided that?”

“The desk.”

“Which person?”

“I don't know.”

Edward placed the note beside PA-44/03.

Two documents.

One explicitly warned against treating C-19 as a nominating authority.

The other clarified that the day-46 classification did not establish a nomination.

Neither had corrected the original entry.

“Daniel,” Edward said, “did you ever see an alternative candidate's name?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell you a candidate had been selected?”

“No.”

“Did you receive an appointment instrument?”

“No.”

“Did you send a candidate's details to Marianne Vale?”

“No.”

“Did you believe someone had been appointed?”

“No.”

Daniel's answers were firm.

Edward moved to the next question.

“Who originated PA-44/03?”

Daniel looked at the sealed envelope.

“I don't know.”

“Then why did you bring this copy?”

“Because there's something missing from the version you have.”

He removed a second sheet.

It was a small system printout.

At the top was a reference:

**PA-44/03 — Task Creation History**

The task had been created at 13:51.

The user field was blank.

The originating instruction field was blank.

The task category was listed as *Administrative Coordination — External Correspondence*.

But the final line contained a system note.

**Task generated from prior instruction: GOV/OC19/02.**

Edward read the reference.

“GOV/OC19/02?”

“Yes.”

“Was that included in the register?”

“No.”

“Have you seen it?”

“Only as a reference.”

Edward closed the notebook.

“Why didn't you mention it in your written response?”

“Because I couldn't explain it.”

Edward stared at the new reference.

The investigation had just moved one level further back.

At 10:15, Sophie found GOV/OC19/02 in the restricted governance index.

It was not a completed appointment document.

It was an instruction request.

**Reference:** GOV/OC19/02.

**Date:** Day 43.

**Subject:** Independent original-channel verification — eligibility and authority requirements.

**Originating department:** Governance Secretariat.

**Receiving department:** Programme Administration.

**Purpose:** Establish the required independence criteria and identify the competent authority for any future nomination.

The document had been marked restricted.

Its distribution list included:

- Governance Secretariat.
- Programme Administration.
- Restricted Correspondence Administration.
- Vale Private Counsel.

No individual recipients were listed.

“That's interesting,” Sophie said.

Edward read the next section.

The request had been issued before the day-44 C-19 query.

It explicitly distinguished three responsibilities.

**Original-channel verification:** To establish whether the communication procedure and source record were authentic.

**Personal representation:** To communicate with or respond on behalf of P-04 within separately granted authority.

**Receiving authority:** To accept or assume responsibility for any verified recipient arrangement.

The document stated that these roles must not be treated as interchangeable.

Edward placed it beside the day-44 instruction.

“Who issued GOV/OC19/02?”

“The originating department field says Governance Secretariat.”

“That tells us the department.”

“Not the author.”

Sophie opened the metadata panel.

The author field was restricted.

The approval field was also restricted.

But the system showed the document had passed through a formal review queue.

**Review status:** Accepted for administrative circulation.

**Substantive approval:** Not recorded.

Edward frowned.

“So the request was allowed to circulate, but that doesn't establish that its substantive contents were approved.”

“Correct.”

“Who received it?”

“Four departments.”

“Did any of them respond?”

Sophie opened the response index.

Two responses were listed.

One from Programme Administration.

One from Vale Private Counsel.

The first was the day-44 task that Daniel had routed.

The second was a short acknowledgement.

**Received. No substantive comment at this stage.**

It had been entered by Charles Vale's office.

Edward read the timestamp.

Day 43, 17:26.

“Charles received the original request before the C-19 query.”

“Yes.”

“Did he respond to the independence criteria?”

“No. Only acknowledged receipt.”

“Then why was Vale Private Counsel included?”

Sophie examined the distribution rationale.

“Document preservation and procedural coordination.”

Edward nodded.

“Not appointment authority.”

“Not according to this document.”

He turned to the next page.

There was a line near the bottom that had not appeared in the index summary.

**Any candidate proposal must originate from the competent appointing authority. A correspondence intermediary may transmit a question or supporting material but cannot substitute for that authority.**

Edward read it twice.

The same rule had been stated three separate times.

Once in GOV/OC19/02.

Again in PA-44/03.

And later in Daniel's day-151 working note.

Yet the day-46 record had still labelled C-19 as a nomination source.

This was no longer simply a careless misunderstanding of the address's function.

Someone had repeatedly been warned about the distinction.

The question was whether the incorrect label had been deliberate, or whether a sequence of people had allowed a misleading administrative description to survive because correcting it would require identifying who had made the original entry.

At 11:40, Charles Vale's office responded to Edward's request.

The message was brief.

**Vale Private Counsel acknowledges receipt of GOV/OC19/02 on day 43. No appointment authority was exercised by this office. No candidate was nominated.**

Edward read it.

Then he requested the underlying acknowledgement record.

The response came twenty minutes later.

The acknowledgement had been prepared by a junior administrative clerk and approved through Charles's document-custodian account.

There was no evidence Charles had personally reviewed the instruction.

But his office had received it.

And the acknowledgement was genuine.

Sophie looked at the record.

“That's another boundary.”

“Yes.”

“His office knew the independence question was being examined. But we still can't say Charles knew the day-46 classification was wrong.”

“Not from this record.”

Edward added the new evidence to the chronology.

**Day 43:** Governance Secretariat circulates GOV/OC19/02, distinguishing independent verification, personal representation and receiving authority.

**Day 43:** Vale Private Counsel acknowledges receipt; no candidate nominated.

**Day 44:** Programme Administration creates PA-44/03 and routes an independence-criteria query through C-19.

**Day 46:** Alternative candidate possibility recorded; source classification C-19.

**Day 47:** Adrian Holt's proposal declined; no alternative appointed.

**Day 151:** Daniel records that C-19 was a correspondence route, not a nomination authority.

The sequence was becoming clearer.

The authorship was not.

At 13:05, Edward received a message from Elena.

It was a screenshot of her platform's updated authority panel.

**Application:** Active.

**Review assignment:** Closed.

**Successor appointment:** Pending.

**Decision authority:** Suspended.

Below it, Elena had added a note.

*The system should not let an application proceed to final decision merely because someone has completed an administrative task. It must check whether the person making the decision has current authority.*

Edward read the message again.

He had not told Elena about the latest discovery.

She was working from the general principles they had discussed earlier.

Still, the timing was uncomfortable.

He replied:

**This is a useful distinction. Keep the audit record showing who changed each status and under which authority.**

Her answer came quickly.

**Already added. It records the operator, timestamp, previous value, new value and supporting authority reference.**

Edward smiled faintly.

Then he put the phone down.

At 15:20, Daniel was called back into the room.

Edward had one final question.

“Did you ever receive an instruction to change the C-19 source classification?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell you the classification had been corrected?”

“No.”

“Did you ever see a candidate's name attached to the day-46 alternative proposal?”

“No.”

“Then why did you keep your day-151 note?”

Daniel looked down at the folded paper.

“Because the wording was wrong.”

“Wrong in what way?”

“It made a transmission route look like an authority.”

“And you thought that mattered?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Daniel took a moment.

“Because if someone later looked at the record without understanding the procedure, they might conclude that a person had been nominated by the protected principal's correspondence channel.”

Edward waited.

Daniel continued.

“That could be interpreted as evidence of a personal instruction. It could also be interpreted as evidence that the person behind the channel had authority to nominate a reviewer. Neither conclusion would be justified.”

Edward nodded slowly.

“Did you tell anyone that?”

“I asked for the entry to be clarified.”

“And they refused.”

“They said it was a historical record.”

“Did you escalate?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

Daniel looked uncomfortable.

“I believed the appointment register was sufficient. No candidate had been appointed. The incorrect classification had no legal effect.”

“Yet you kept the note.”

“Yes.”

“Because you were concerned it might later be used as evidence.”

Daniel did not answer immediately.

Then he said, “I was concerned that someone might confuse what the record said with what had actually happened.”

Edward closed his folder.

That was not an admission of wrongdoing.

But it was a clear description of the danger.

At 17:06, Sophie received a new message from the Governance Secretariat.

It contained the release decision for the author and approval metadata of GOV/OC19/02.

The document had been drafted by an officer named Helen Sloane.

Edward recognised the name immediately.

Helen had previously served as control liaison before her position ended on day 148.

But GOV/OC19/02 was dated day 43.

At that time, she had still held the appointment.

The approval record showed a second name.

**Reviewing officer: Marianne Vale.**

Edward checked the appointment register.

Marianne Vale had been appointed on day 38.

Her role allowed her to receive proposed appointment instruments, check authority references, maintain the register and refer incomplete appointments.

It did not allow her to issue substantive instructions concerning the appointment of independent verifiers.

He looked at the approval status.

**Administrative review completed.**

**Substantive authorisation: Not required for circulation.**

Marianne had reviewed the document for procedural consistency.

She had not approved the policy.

Helen had drafted it.

Neither fact established who had decided to initiate the review in the first place.

Sophie opened the metadata history.

A final line appeared.

**Initiating request: Restricted instruction received from originating office.**

The originating office was not named.

Instead, the system displayed a reference.

**OI-07.**

Edward stared at it.

“What's OI-07?”

Sophie searched the index.

No direct result.

She tried the restricted archive catalogue.

A single entry appeared.

**OI-07 — Originating Instruction Register.**

**Access:** Restricted.

**Custodian:** Office of the Programme Authority Chair.

**Release condition:** Joint confirmation by the originating office and the preservation custodian.

Edward sat very still.

The Programme Authority Chair was Dr Eleanor Vale.

The preservation custodian was also Dr Eleanor Vale.

But that did not mean she had written the instruction.

It meant the register was held under her authority.

He looked at the day-43 chronology.

The investigation had begun with a question about who had nominated an alternative independent verifier.

Now they had found an earlier instruction register that might identify who had initiated the entire process.

Sophie closed the archive window.

“Do we request it?”

“Yes.”

“Under what basis?”

Edward looked at the repeated references to C-19, the anonymous instruction, and the unassigned nomination.

“Because someone initiated a review of the authority structure before the candidate was proposed. We need to know who.”

He typed the request.

**Please provide the originating instruction recorded under OI-07, including its author, approving authority, distribution history and any subsequent amendment or withdrawal.**

He added one sentence.

**The request does not seek release of the protected principal's identity or personal communication content.**

Then he sent it.

At 17:39, the acknowledgement arrived.

**Request received. Joint confirmation required.**

Edward read the final line.

**Preservation custodian has been notified.**

For the first time, the next step did not lead to another administrative desk.

It led to the person who had been protecting the sealed register from release.

Dr Eleanor Vale.

And this time, Edward intended to ask her not what the records meant.

He intended to ask who had first instructed them to be created.
