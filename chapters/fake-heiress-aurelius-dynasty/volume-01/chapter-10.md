# **Chapter 10 — For the Child's Mother Only**

Victoria Aurelius had not entered the old nursery in three weeks.

It was not because she had forgotten it.

Quite the opposite.

She remembered every detail of the room.

The pale curtains. The white wooden crib. The small wardrobe Alexander had insisted on assembling himself, despite having absolutely no experience with furniture.

She remembered the afternoon sunlight falling across the carpet.

She remembered holding her newborn daughter beside the window.

And she remembered the night the child disappeared.

The room had remained untouched ever since.

Not because Victoria believed that preserving furniture would bring her daughter home, but because she could not bring herself to decide what should replace it.

There were days when she entered and sat quietly in the armchair.

There were days when she could not even walk past the door.

This morning, she stood outside it with a document in her hand.

**For the child's mother only.**

The words were written on the report concerning the old legal envelope.

Victoria read them again.

Then she opened the door.

Alexander found her there twenty minutes later.

She was standing beside the small wardrobe, looking at the collection of baby clothes arranged inside.

“Victoria.”

She turned.

“You wanted to see me?”

“Yes.”

He noticed the document in her hand.

“You've read the archive report.”

“I have.”

He walked into the room.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Victoria held out the paper.

“What does this mean?”

Alexander took it.

“The original envelope contained a secondary sleeve. The outer seal was broken eighteen years ago. The inner sleeve remains sealed.”

“And it says it is for the child's mother.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“I don't know.”

Victoria looked at him.

“Alexander, someone prepared a document specifically for me. Why did I never receive it?”

He had no answer.

He could explain that the old legal file had been created under his authority. He could explain that Charles Vale had handled the matter. He could explain that the family office had been involved in countless confidential affairs.

None of those explanations answered the question.

Why had a document addressed to the mother of a missing child been kept away from her?

“I'm going to find out,” he said.

Victoria looked back at the wardrobe.

“Was this something you authorised?”

“I don't remember approving a letter addressed to you.”

“Then perhaps someone used your name.”

“Perhaps.”

She turned toward him.

“I don't want another investigation that ends with a report telling me that we couldn't establish what happened.”

Alexander stepped closer.

“I know.”

“No, you don't.”

Her voice remained quiet, but it trembled.

“I have spent eighteen years being told that there is no confirmed lead. Eighteen years of interviews, searches, records, hospitals and people promising to call when they find something. And now there is a letter that was apparently meant for me, written eighteen years ago, and nobody thought I should see it.”

Alexander did not interrupt.

Victoria took a breath.

“I want to read it.”

“So do I.”

“Then don't let anyone delay it.”

“I won't.”

She looked at him for a long moment.

“Alexander, if this letter contains something about our daughter, I want the truth. Even if it is painful.”

He nodded.

“I promise.”

At eight-thirty, the Aurelius family gathered in the main dining room.

Julian arrived last, carrying a tablet and wearing an expression of extreme concentration.

Lucien looked at him.

“Are you studying?”

“No.”

“Then why do you look so serious?”

“I'm choosing a game.”

Lucien nodded solemnly.

“A difficult intellectual exercise.”

Julian ignored him and sat down.

Adrian entered a moment later.

“Father wants everyone in the study after breakfast.”

Damien looked up.

“Everyone?”

“Everyone.”

Lucien placed his fork down.

“That sounds promising.”

“It isn't a social gathering,” Adrian replied.

“Those are usually the most entertaining.”

Victoria entered with Alexander.

She looked composed, but Adrian noticed that she had been holding the same folded piece of paper throughout breakfast.

Julian noticed it too.

“Mother, what's that?”

“Something from the family archives.”

“About my sister?”

Victoria glanced at him.

“Yes.”

Julian straightened.

“Did they find her?”

“Not yet.”

His expression fell.

“But we found something that may help us understand what happened.”

Julian nodded.

“Then that's good.”

Lucien reached across and lightly tapped his shoulder.

“Eat first. Investigations are easier when you're not hungry.”

Julian gave him a doubtful look.

“Is that scientifically proven?”

“Absolutely.”

Adrian sighed.

“Please don't start.”

The family meeting began at nine.

Alexander explained what the archive department had found.

He did not hide the fact that the file had been created under his authority. He also did not pretend to know why it had been created.

When he finished, Adrian placed the archive report on the table.

“The supplementary sleeve is still sealed. Its outer envelope was opened eighteen years ago, but the inner sleeve remains intact.”

Damien looked at the document.

“Who opened the outer envelope?”

“We don't know,” Adrian said.

“Who had access?”

“Three people were authorised. Charles Vale, the originating representative and Father.”

Lucien frowned.

“Could the representative have opened it?”

“Yes.”

“Then why is their name missing?”

“Because the original authorisation document was removed.”

Lucien leaned back.

“So someone opened the envelope, removed the identity record, and left the letter addressed to Mother.”

Victoria's expression tightened.

“That is what the report suggests.”

Julian looked from one person to another.

“Can we open it?”

Alexander answered.

“We can. But we must follow the legal process.”

“Why?”

“Because the document was placed under a legal restriction. If we ignore that restriction, we may damage the evidence or create problems with the archive's custody.”

Julian looked dissatisfied.

“So we have to wait?”

“Not unnecessarily,” Alexander said.

Damien turned toward Adrian.

“Do we have a list of the three authorised people?”

“We have two names. The third is still missing.”

“Who are the two?”

“Charles Vale and Father.”

“And the third?”

“The originating representative.”

Damien nodded.

“I'll work with the security archives.”

Alexander looked at him.

“Keep the investigation internal. I don't want anyone approaching former employees without coordination.”

“Understood.”

Lucien glanced at Victoria.

“Mother, do you want us to find someone to review the letter with you?”

Victoria shook her head.

“I want to read it myself first.”

No one argued.

Julian raised his hand slightly.

Everyone looked at him.

“What?” Lucien asked.

“I have a question.”

“Proceed.”

“If the letter is for Mother, does that mean it was written by someone who knew her?”

Alexander considered it.

“Most likely.”

“Someone who knew our family?”

“Yes.”

Julian looked down at the table.

“Then why would they hide something from us?”

Nobody answered immediately.

It was a question that deserved an answer.

Unfortunately, they did not have one.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was trying to repair a broken bookmark.

It was not an important task.

The bookmark had been a gift from Clara, a small leather strip with a decorative metal clasp. The clasp had come loose, and Elena was attempting to fix it with a tiny screwdriver from her desk.

Nicholas watched her from the doorway.

“You know we have people who can repair things.”

Elena did not look up.

“It's a bookmark.”

“Exactly. A highly specialised object.”

“Go away.”

Nicholas walked into the room.

“Good morning to you too.”

She tightened the small screw.

“Good morning.”

“Did you sleep?”

“Yes.”

“Properly?”

“Yes.”

“Without reading until two?”

Elena finally looked up.

“Do you have a list of questions?”

“I have concerns.”

“You have too much free time.”

Nicholas grinned and sat on the edge of the desk.

“Mother wants us downstairs.”

“Why?”

“Family meeting.”

Elena looked at him.

“About the investigation?”

“Probably.”

She put the screwdriver down.

“Do you know anything?”

Nicholas shook his head.

“Edward hasn't told me much.”

“Is something wrong?”

“Nothing that I know of.”

Elena studied him.

Nicholas was usually terrible at hiding his feelings. When he was worried, his jokes became more frequent.

At the moment, he was making too many jokes.

She closed the bookmark case.

“Let's go.”

The family meeting was held in Richard's study.

Catherine was already seated when Elena and Nicholas arrived. Isabelle stood beside the bookshelves, holding a glass of water.

Sebastian was reading a document.

Edward was speaking quietly with Richard.

Elena took a seat.

“Is something happening?”

Richard turned toward her.

“We have received an update from the hospital investigation.”

Edward stepped forward.

“The investigation has identified an old legal file connected to the same period as your birth.”

Elena listened.

“Is it connected to the hospital?”

“We believe it may be.”

“Does it explain what happened?”

“Not yet.”

Isabelle looked concerned.

“Is it another medical record?”

“Not exactly,” Edward said. “It's a legal archive from an outside firm.”

Richard took over.

“We are trying to establish why that firm was involved.”

Elena glanced at her father.

“Is the firm connected to the Aurelius family?”

The room became still.

Edward looked at her.

Richard's expression tightened slightly.

“How did you know that name?”

Elena looked confused.

“I heard you and Edward discussing it yesterday. I wasn't trying to listen.”

Nobody spoke for a moment.

Then Richard nodded.

“Yes. The file appears to have some connection to the Aurelius Family Office.”

Elena looked at Edward.

“Why would they be involved?”

“We don't know.”

“Could it have something to do with the baby exchange?”

“It's possible.”

Elena absorbed that.

She had known that the investigation was becoming more complicated.

But the name Aurelius meant something different from the hospital, the nurses or the old records.

It belonged to a family whose businesses appeared in newspapers, financial reports and international news.

A family whose wealth was so extensive that even the Valmonts were considered considerably smaller in comparison.

“Have you contacted them?” she asked.

Richard shook his head.

“Not yet.”

Elena nodded.

“Okay.”

Isabelle glanced at her.

“Are you worried?”

“A little.”

“Me too.”

Elena looked at her.

For a moment, they shared the same uncertainty.

Two girls.

One investigation.

And a history neither of them remembered.

Richard cleared his throat.

“There is something else. Until we have more reliable information, I want everyone to avoid discussing the investigation outside the family.”

Sebastian nodded.

Edward agreed.

Catherine looked at both girls.

“Especially at university. There is no reason for rumours to spread.”

Elena understood.

She did not want strangers discussing her birth as though it were entertainment.

“I won't say anything,” she promised.

Isabelle nodded too.

The meeting ended shortly afterward.

Elena returned to the library.

She tried to read, but her attention kept drifting.

Aurelius.

She had heard the name before, of course.

Everyone had.

Aurelius Technologies had developed software used by businesses around the world. Aurelius Medical operated hospitals and research facilities. Aurelius Capital appeared in financial news with impressive regularity.

She had once written a university report about the family's international investments.

She had not thought about them since.

Now the name had appeared in the story of her own birth.

She opened her laptop.

For a few minutes, she considered searching for information.

Then she stopped.

Richard had asked everyone to keep the matter private, but searching public information was not exactly discussing it.

Still, she closed the browser.

There was something uncomfortable about reading articles about a family that might somehow be connected to her.

She did not know whether the connection would be good or bad.

And she did not want to make assumptions.

A knock sounded at the library door.

Isabelle entered.

“Are you busy?”

“No. Come in.”

Isabelle sat opposite her.

“I wanted to ask something.”

“Go ahead.”

“Do you think we should talk to the hospital ourselves?”

Elena blinked.

“Why?”

“I don't know. Maybe if we asked them questions directly, we'd understand more.”

Elena considered it.

“Edward is already handling the investigation. I don't think the hospital would tell us anything different.”

“But it's about us.”

“I know.”

Isabelle looked down.

“I hate waiting.”

“So do I.”

Elena closed her laptop.

“But I don't want to make things harder for Edward by interfering.”

Isabelle nodded.

“You trust him?”

“Yes.”

“Even when he doesn't tell you everything?”

Elena smiled faintly.

“Especially when he tells me he doesn't know something. At least that means he isn't pretending.”

Isabelle seemed to think about that.

Then she stood.

“Would you like to go for a walk?”

Elena looked at the clock.

“Sure.”

They left the library together.

At three in the afternoon, Edward received confirmation from the legal archive.

The Aurelius Family Office had approved the retrieval process.

However, the supplementary sleeve could only be opened in the presence of the originating client or an authorised family representative.

Richard read the message.

“Alexander Aurelius has agreed?”

Edward nodded.

“He has requested a meeting.”

Sebastian looked up.

“When?”

“Tomorrow afternoon.”

Richard considered it.

“Where?”

“The legal archive's conference office. The custodian will supervise the opening.”

“Who is attending?”

“Alexander Aurelius, Adrian Aurelius and Damien Aurelius. Jonathan Pierce will be present. We will attend as well.”

Richard nodded.

“Good.”

Sebastian looked at Edward.

“Do you think Alexander knows what is inside?”

“I don't know.”

“Does he know about the hospital?”

“Apparently, his family has begun reviewing the old records.”

Richard placed the message down.

“Then tomorrow may answer several questions.”

Edward did not look convinced.

“Or create several more.”

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was preparing for the meeting.

She had selected a simple dark-blue dress and placed the archive report on her desk.

Her assistant knocked.

“Madam, the chairman would like to know whether you will attend tomorrow.”

Victoria looked up.

“Yes.”

“Shall I inform the family office?”

“Yes, please.”

The assistant left.

Victoria returned to the report.

The words on the envelope seemed to grow heavier every time she read them.

**For the child's mother only.**

She wondered who had written them.

A nurse?

A lawyer?

Someone from the family office?

Someone who had held her daughter?

She closed her eyes.

For eighteen years, she had imagined countless explanations for what had happened.

Some were ordinary mistakes.

Some were deliberate acts.

Some were possibilities so terrible that she had refused to consider them.

But she had never imagined that someone had written her a letter and hidden it.

She stood and walked to the wardrobe.

Inside was a small box.

She opened it.

A hospital identification bracelet lay inside.

It was tiny, made of soft plastic, with her daughter's birth details printed on it.

Victoria touched it gently.

“I'm still waiting,” she whispered.

That night, the legal archive's security team completed the preparations for the following day's meeting.

The original file was brought from restricted storage.

Its condition was documented.

The outer envelope was placed under a camera.

The broken seal was photographed.

The secondary sleeve was examined without opening it.

The custodian then noticed something that had not appeared in the earlier inventory.

A small handwritten notation was visible beneath the edge of the sleeve.

It was partly obscured by the paper binding.

The technician adjusted the light.

The words became clearer.

**If the mother has not received the child, do not deliver this document to the family office.**

The technician stopped.

He called the senior custodian.

Within minutes, the notation was photographed and sent to the legal team.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was already asleep.

Her university books were arranged on her desk.

Her repaired bookmark lay beside them.

Tomorrow, she planned to attend her lectures, finish her assignment and perhaps visit the bookstore with Clara.

She had no idea that an old document was about to change the investigation.

She had no idea that a woman she had never met was preparing to read a letter written eighteen years earlier.

And she had no idea that the letter had been written under a condition.

**If the mother has not received the child.**

It was a strange sentence.

Because Victoria Aurelius had never received her daughter.

Not after the hospital.

Not after the searches.

Not after eighteen years.

And if the document was genuine, then someone had known that the child had not been returned.

Someone had known the difference between a missing child and a child who had never reached her mother.

Tomorrow, the Aurelius and Valmont families would finally sit across the same table.

But before anyone could ask what had happened to the two newborn girls, they would have to answer a much more disturbing question:

**Who had written a letter to a mother who was never allowed to receive it?**
