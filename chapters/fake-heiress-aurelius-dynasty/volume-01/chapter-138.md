# **Chapter 138 — The Authority That Existed Before Its Approval**

The day-34 amendment had been filed under the wrong section.

That was the first thing Jonathan discovered.

It belonged to the restricted authority archive, but the original index placed it among routine correspondence instructions. Someone had classified it as an administrative clarification rather than a change affecting the duration of a protected representative's authority.

The difference had allowed the document to remain largely unnoticed.

Edward stood beside Jonathan's desk, reading the archive description.

**AV-18 / DURATION CLARIFICATION**

**Received: Day 34**

**Originating designation: Victoria Aurelius**

**Processing status: Accepted for administrative continuity**

“Who accepted it?” Edward asked.

Jonathan scrolled through the entry.

“The receiving clerk recorded it as approved. There is no name beside the approval.”

“Was the clerk authorised to approve changes to representative authority?”

“No. The clerk could receive documents, verify the presence of required fields, and route them to the appropriate officer.”

“Then what did accepted for administrative continuity mean?”

“Apparently, that the document was allowed into the system.”

Edward looked at him.

“Allowed into the system is not the same as approved.”

“Exactly.”

Jonathan opened the document history.

The amendment had three recorded versions.

The first was a draft.

The second was a received copy.

The third was a processed version.

The wording differed between them.

Edward leaned closer.

“Show me the first.”

Jonathan opened the draft.

*Where the protected principal cannot be contacted through the approved correspondence process, the communication representative's authority shall remain available for the limited purpose of receiving further information and preserving the principal's opportunity to respond.*

Edward read it slowly.

“That is not unreasonable.”

“No,” Jonathan agreed.

It did not permit Gabriel to accept a placement or make decisions on behalf of P-04. It simply ensured that the principal's communication channel would not disappear because the first delivery attempt had failed.

“Now the second version,” Edward said.

Jonathan opened it.

*Where the protected principal cannot be contacted, the communication representative's authority may remain administratively active until the receiving identity process is completed.*

Edward's expression changed.

The second version was broader.

The original required an approved correspondence process and limited the representative's continuing authority to receiving further information.

The revised version removed the reference to the approved process.

It also replaced the narrow purpose with the vague phrase *administratively active*.

“Who changed it?”

“The audit history doesn't identify an individual. The amendment was submitted through the shared review desk.”

“And the third version?”

Jonathan opened it.

The processed copy included a final sentence:

*This provision does not constitute authority to accept permanent placement, waive personal acknowledgement, or determine originating or receiving identity.*

Edward read the three versions again.

The limitations had been preserved.

But the central change remained.

The representative's authority could continue even when the process required to exercise it had not been completed.

“Find the person who requested the revision,” Edward said.

Jonathan began searching the routing records.

A few minutes later, he found an attachment.

It was a handwritten cover note.

**For processing before the next scheduled review.**

Beneath it appeared a reference:

**P.I. DIVISION — C.V.**

Edward looked at the initials.

“Charles.”

“Probably.”

“Probably isn't enough. We need the original.”

Jonathan opened the dispatch register.

The physical submission had been delivered to the Vale Private Counsel office on day thirty-three.

The courier record identified Marianne Cross.

The recipient was Charles Vale's private office.

There was no signature from Victoria Aurelius.

No originating-office approval.

No receiving authority acknowledgement.

Edward closed the file.

“Find Marianne.”

Marianne Cross was less surprised than Edward expected.

She met him in a small records office, where she had been helping the successor firm organise several restricted correspondence files.

When Edward showed her the day-34 cover note, she recognised the handwriting.

“That was Charles's instruction.”

“Did he give you the amendment?”

“He gave me a sealed packet.”

“Did you read it?”

“No. I was instructed to deliver it to his private office and obtain a receipt.”

“Was it marked as an originating-office instruction?”

Marianne looked at the cover.

“Yes.”

“Did you know Victoria had authorised it?”

“No.”

“Did you ask?”

“I was a courier and administrative liaison. I wasn't authorised to question the substance of every sealed instruction.”

Edward placed the three versions before her.

“Did you know the wording changed?”

Marianne examined them.

“I did not see these versions at the time.”

“Did Charles explain why the amendment was needed?”

“He said the communication process could not be allowed to collapse merely because the principal was temporarily unavailable.”

“Did he mention P-04?”

“He used the protected reference.”

“Did he say P-04 had been contacted?”

“No.”

“Did he say the principal had personally authorised Gabriel to continue?”

“No.”

Edward pointed to the first draft.

“Did he ever describe the amendment in these terms?”

Marianne read the original wording.

“He told me that the representative should remain available to receive further information. He said the principal should not lose the opportunity to respond.”

“And did he tell you the authority could remain active indefinitely?”

Marianne looked at the second version.

“No.”

Edward studied her.

“Did you deliver the packet to Charles personally?”

“Yes.”

“Who was present?”

“His records assistant. I don't remember the name.”

“Was Eleanor there?”

“No.”

“Was Gabriel?”

“No.”

Marianne returned the papers.

“Mr Vale was very particular about the wording of these arrangements. He frequently said that temporary care must continue even when identity verification remained incomplete.”

Edward did not answer.

Marianne added something.

“But he also knew that the communication representative's authority and the receiving family's authority were separate.”

“Did he ever explain why he wanted the communication authority to remain active?”

“He said the protected principal might still need someone to receive information on their behalf.”

“Even though the principal had not received the original information?”

Marianne looked at him.

“That was the contradiction, wasn't it?”

At V-17, the day-34 packet had been listed among Charles Vale's private instructions.

The archive inventory described it as a sealed administrative amendment, received and preserved under the authority of the originating office.

The original envelope had not survived.

However, the internal receipt did.

It contained a small handwritten notation:

*Authority duration clarification received. Source document not independently verified.*

Edward examined the notation.

“Who wrote this?”

The archive custodian checked the reference.

“Eleanor Vale.”

Edward looked up.

“She received the packet?”

“No. She reviewed the record after it had been filed.”

“On what date?”

“Day thirty-five.”

The custodian retrieved the associated review sheet.

Eleanor's handwriting appeared across the bottom.

*The designation of the originating representative must not be treated as proof of that representative's approval. No originating signature or independent authorisation has been attached.*

A second note followed.

*Retain as an unverified administrative submission. Do not incorporate into the verified authority certificate.*

Edward read it twice.

“Was the amendment incorporated?”

The custodian opened the next page.

“Yes.”

“Then someone ignored this instruction.”

“It appears so.”

The system history showed that the processed amendment had been added to the authority record on day thirty-six.

The original certificate had remained in the archive.

The amendment was stored as an additional page.

There was no replacement stamp.

No signed approval.

Only a processing notation:

**ADDITIONAL AUTHORITY CONDITION — ACTIVE**

Edward stared at it.

“Who entered this?”

The custodian checked the audit record.

“Shared administrative review account.”

“Operator?”

“Not recorded.”

“Approval?”

“None listed.”

Edward turned back to Eleanor's note.

She had explicitly said the document must not be incorporated into the verified authority certificate.

Someone had incorporated it anyway.

And by day forty-seven, the temporary exception had given the altered authority provision a practical purpose.

Victoria read Eleanor's day-35 objection in silence.

Alexander stood behind her chair.

Edward had brought the original review sheet to the Aurelius Estate.

“Did you ever see this amendment?” Victoria asked.

“Not at the time,” Edward said.

“Was my office listed as its source?”

“Yes.”

“But I never signed it.”

“We have found no signature from you.”

Victoria looked at Alexander.

“Could someone have used the designation without using my actual signature?”

“That appears to be what happened,” he replied.

She returned to the document.

“Why would Charles do that?”

Edward considered his answer.

“We don't yet know whether he wanted to keep Gabriel's authority active for a legitimate administrative reason or whether he wanted to make an incomplete arrangement easier to maintain.”

“Could both be true?”

“Yes.”

Alexander moved closer to the table.

“Did Eleanor challenge the amendment?”

“Immediately.”

“And did Charles know?”

“We have not found proof that he received her objection.”

Victoria looked at the date.

“Day thirty-five.”

She placed the paper down.

“Then she objected before Gabriel signed the conditional response.”

Edward nodded.

“That is important.”

Victoria looked toward the window.

“She was trying to preserve the principal's ability to communicate.”

“Yes.”

“And the amendment may have made it possible to continue using a representative's authority without that communication.”

“Yes.”

For a while, none of them spoke.

Victoria finally asked, “Can we establish whether P-04 was the person who appeared at C-19?”

“Not yet.”

“Can we establish whether that person received the original information?”

“No.”

“Then we still don't know whether the principal had an opportunity to make a decision.”

Edward closed his folder.

“Correct.”

Victoria's voice softened.

“Then we must keep looking.”

The original day-34 submission was not the only document that had entered the authority archive.

Jonathan found a second packet.

It had arrived on day thirty-six.

Its reference was:

**AV-18 / PRINCIPAL COMMUNICATION CONTINUITY**

The first page requested that the amendment be added to the protected authority record.

The second page contained a list of supporting documents.

- Original communication authority certificate.
- Proposed duration clarification.
- Conditional response procedure.
- Temporary care continuity instruction.
- Originating-office designation.

Edward examined the list.

The final item was marked:

**ORIGINATING DESIGNATION — RECEIPT ONLY**

There was no signature from Victoria.

No independent approval.

But the submission included a routing certificate stating that the originating designation had been acknowledged.

The certificate carried Eleanor's initials.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Did Eleanor acknowledge this?”

“We asked her.”

“And?”

“She says she never signed the certificate.”

Jonathan opened the audit record.

The initials had been copied from another document.

Dr Mira Sen's preliminary examination had already identified several instances in which genuine signatures or initials were reproduced out of context.

This one appeared to follow the same pattern.

“Who created the certificate?”

Jonathan pointed to the lower section.

“Document assembly desk. Day thirty-six.”

“Which account?”

“FA-ADMIN/REVIEW DESK.”

Edward rubbed his forehead.

“The same account used for the altered status entries.”

“Yes.”

“Was the certificate ever independently verified?”

“No.”

“Then the amendment was added to the authority file using an acknowledgement that Eleanor says she never made.”

Jonathan nodded.

Edward studied the routing certificate.

There was another line beneath the approval field.

**Originating authority confirmed for administrative continuity.**

He read it again.

Not confirmed by the originating authority.

Confirmed for administrative continuity.

A phrase that sounded official without identifying who had made the decision.

“Find out who was responsible for document assembly that day.”

Jonathan opened the staffing register.

Three people had access to the desk.

Martin Keane.

Daniel Mercer.

And a junior records clerk named Helen Ward.

Edward considered the names.

“Martin handled migration access. Daniel changed the status wording. Helen supervised the restricted correspondence index.”

“Do you want to speak to all three?”

“Yes.”

He gathered the pages.

“But start with Helen.”

Helen Ward was visibly uncomfortable when Edward showed her the certificate.

“I remember the file,” she said.

“Did you assemble this document?”

“No.”

“Did you approve it?”

“No.”

“Did you see it before it was entered into the authority record?”

Helen studied the reproduced initials.

“I may have seen a copy.”

“What did you understand it to be?”

“A receipt confirming that the originating designation had been received.”

“Not approved?”

“Correct.”

Edward pointed to the next line.

“Then why does the system say the originating authority was confirmed?”

Helen looked at the document again.

“That wording was not mine.”

“Who used it?”

“I don't know.”

“Was it standard wording?”

“Not in our ordinary receipt process.”

She opened a cabinet and retrieved an old procedural manual.

The relevant section distinguished three stages.

**RECEIVED:** A document had arrived.

**ACKNOWLEDGED:** The receiving office had confirmed receipt.

**APPROVED:** An authorised person had accepted the substance of the instruction.

Edward read the definitions.

“Who changed the language?”

Helen turned several pages.

“There's a migration note.”

It was dated day thirty-six.

*For continuity of restricted authority records, receipt and acknowledgement fields may be consolidated where the originating designation is already present in the protected file.*

Edward frowned.

“Who authorised that?”

Helen checked the bottom.

The note had been issued under the shared administrative account.

No individual name.

No approval reference.

“Was this applied to other records?”

“Yes.”

“How many?”

Helen searched the old index.

“Twenty-three.”

Edward looked at her.

“Twenty-three authority records?”

“Twenty-three restricted authority entries. Not necessarily twenty-three people or cases.”

“Were all of them connected to the same process?”

“No. Some were ordinary correspondence protections.”

“Were any of them connected to PR-06?”

Helen checked.

“Three.”

She opened the first.

It concerned Gabriel Vale.

The second concerned a separate temporary medical representative.

The third concerned a restricted financial correspondence custodian.

Edward looked at the screen.

“Did the same change affect all three?”

“Yes.”

“Did anyone challenge it?”

Helen hesitated.

“There was one objection.”

“From whom?”

She opened the record.

The author field contained Eleanor Vale's name.

The date was day thirty-seven.

Edward leaned forward.

“What did she say?”

Helen read the entry aloud.

“'Receipt of an originating designation does not establish approval of the authority described in the accompanying document. The original distinctions must be retained.'”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

Eleanor had objected again.

And again, her objection had not stopped the administrative change.

That evening, Eleanor examined the migration note herself.

She recognised the wording immediately.

“I remember this,” she said.

“Did you know it had been applied to Gabriel's record?”

“I knew it had been applied to several restricted files. I did not know the exact list.”

“Did you ask for the original distinctions to be restored?”

“Yes.”

“Did anyone respond?”

She opened a correspondence folder.

A reply had been received on day thirty-nine.

It was short.

*The migration adjustment is limited to administrative continuity. No substantive authority is created or expanded by the revised field presentation.*

Edward read it.

“Who signed?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

“Did you accept his explanation?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because the issue wasn't merely how the fields appeared on the screen. The change had allowed an unverified designation to be treated as an active authority record.”

She removed another sheet.

Her reply had been sent the same day.

*If no substantive authority is created, there is no need to alter the distinction between receipt, acknowledgement, and approval. The original record must remain visible.*

Edward looked at the date.

“Was it restored?”

“No.”

“Did you escalate the matter?”

“I sent a copy to Charles.”

“Did he respond?”

“He said the migration team had been instructed to preserve the original documents and that no rights would be affected.”

Eleanor paused.

“That answer was technically correct.”

Edward looked at her.

“But?”

“But the system was still presenting something different from the original record.”

She folded her hands.

“An archive can preserve every original document and still mislead people through the summary it displays.”

Edward thought of Elena's presentation.

The distinction between a missing decision and an actual decision.

A record could be technically preserved while its practical meaning was altered.

“Did you ever tell Gabriel about this?” he asked.

“No.”

“Did you know he had requested a review of his authority?”

“Not until much later.”

Edward placed the two letters beside each other.

For the first time, he could see how the process had divided itself.

Gabriel had asked for his authority to be reviewed.

Eleanor had objected to its administrative expansion.

Neither had known the full extent of the other's actions.

Both had sent their concerns into the same system.

And the system had continued.

In Singapore, Elena's first external prototype test began with a problem.

Two fictional grant reviewers had entered conflicting interpretations of the same application.

One marked the applicant eligible.

The other marked the application incomplete.

The platform displayed both decisions but refused to produce a final status.

Elena examined the audit trail.

The first reviewer had authority to verify supporting documents.

The second had authority to assess eligibility.

Neither had authority to override the other's findings.

Professor Lin watched from the other side of the table.

“What happens now?”

“The system sends the file to the designated decision officer.”

“And if there isn't one?”

“It remains pending.”

“Indefinitely?”

“Until the organisation appoints someone with the right authority or changes its procedure.”

Professor Lin raised an eyebrow.

“Would you ever allow the system to continue using the last available decision?”

Elena considered the question.

“Only if the rules specifically allowed a temporary status, and even then I'd require a review date.”

“Why?”

“Because otherwise people might treat the temporary status as a decision.”

Professor Lin nodded.

“And what would you record if the review date passed?”

“That the review was overdue. Not that the original decision had become permanent.”

She adjusted the settings.

A new warning appeared on the screen.

TEMPORARY STATUS — REVIEW REQUIRED

Elena added another field.

**AUTHORITY SOURCE AND SCOPE**

Then another.

**EXPIRY OR REVIEW CONDITION**

She looked at the prototype.

It was becoming more complicated than she had originally intended.

But the complications were not unnecessary.

They were part of making the system honest.

Jonathan called Edward shortly before midnight.

“I found the day-39 migration instruction.”

“Who issued it?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

“Did he approve the change?”

“He says he only approved the display adjustment. He did not approve the underlying authority amendment.”

“Does he remember the day-34 document?”

“He remembers processing a request to keep the representative's status active during migration.”

Edward sat forward.

“Who requested it?”

“Daniel says the request came from Charles's private office.”

“Did he receive a written instruction?”

“Yes.”

“Where is it?”

Jonathan paused.

“It wasn't filed with the authority record.”

“Where was it filed?”

“In the Personal Instructions Division.”

Edward waited.

“There's a separate index entry,” Jonathan continued. “It is marked as a personal instruction from Charles Vale, dated day thirty-three.”

Edward looked at the documents spread across his desk.

Day thirty-three.

One day before the duration clarification had been submitted.

One day before the amendment entered the system.

“What's the subject?”

Jonathan read the entry.

**CONTINUITY OF REPRESENTATIVE AUTHORITY — PRIOR TO PRINCIPAL CONTACT**

Edward's expression hardened.

“Open the file.”

“I can't.”

“Why?”

“It's not in the ordinary archive. The index says the original was transferred to V-17 under Private Instruction 07.”

Edward looked toward the closed door.

“Is it sealed?”

“Yes.”

“Who can open it?”

Jonathan read the final line.

“Joint authority of the originating representative and the receiving authority.”

Edward went still.

The same condition.

The same unresolved appointment.

The same sealed instruction.

“Does the index identify the person who received it?”

Jonathan checked.

“No recipient is named.”

“Then who was it meant for?”

There was a pause.

Jonathan answered quietly.

“The communication representative.”

Edward looked at Gabriel's conditional response, then at the day-34 amendment.

A private instruction had been prepared before the amendment.

Before the exception.

Before the failed delivery.

It had been preserved under the same joint-authority restriction that had kept Private Instruction 07 sealed.

And its title contained a phrase Edward had not seen before.

*Prior to principal contact.*

He opened his notebook and wrote it down.

Then he added one question beneath it.

**What did Charles Vale intend to instruct Gabriel to do before P-04 had been contacted?**
