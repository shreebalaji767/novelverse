# **Chapter 57 — The Guardian Outside Both Families**

Edward did not open the envelope immediately.

He placed it inside a protective document sleeve and examined the seal.

The paper was old, but the wax remained intact. Eleanor Vale's initials had been pressed into it.

Jonathan stood beside the desk.

"You're not going to open it?"

"Not until we document its condition."

"Margaret has already confirmed that Eleanor intended it for whoever investigated Infant A."

"She confirmed that the envelope was meant to be preserved. She didn't confirm that she had authority to release it to us."

Jonathan glanced towards the door.

"She gave it to you herself."

"Which is why we need her written statement."

Edward had learned to distrust convenient explanations.

An eighteen-year-old investigation had survived because people repeatedly assumed that a document meant exactly what its heading suggested.

He would not make the same mistake.

Margaret Voss signed her statement the following morning.

She confirmed that Eleanor had entrusted the envelope to her shortly before her death.

She also confirmed that she had been instructed to release it only when an investigator could demonstrate that both families understood the provisional nature of the original infant placements.

Edward read the statement carefully.

"Why did Eleanor choose that condition?"

Margaret adjusted her glasses.

"She believed the families would try to identify the children through the original hospital assignments."

"Would that have been wrong?"

"Not necessarily. But the assignments had changed too many times."

"Then why didn't she simply explain what happened?"

Margaret looked at him.

"Because she didn't know everything herself."

Edward paused.

"You're saying Eleanor was also missing information?"

"Yes."

"Even though she was involved in the transfers?"

Margaret nodded.

"She knew what she had arranged. She didn't know everything other people had done afterward."

Edward considered that.

It was an uncomfortable possibility.

For weeks, the investigation had treated Eleanor as one of the central figures who might have controlled the children's movements.

Perhaps she had been trying to correct a situation she no longer controlled.

Margaret continued.

"She told me once that the worst mistake was believing that because she had protected one child, she had protected both."

"Did she explain?"

"No."

"Did she say which child she had protected?"

Margaret looked towards the envelope.

"She never used their names."

The envelope was opened in the presence of both families' legal representatives.

Edward removed three sheets of paper.

The first was a letter.

The second was a copy of a private receiving agreement.

The third was a handwritten note.

Edward began with the letter.

*To the person investigating the original placement of Infant A,*

*I am writing this because the official records no longer reflect what happened.*

*The child recorded as Infant A was not delivered to the guardian named in the original instruction.*

*She was received by a person outside both families.*

*That person was not selected by the hospital.*

*She was not selected by the Valmont family.*

*She was not selected by the Aurelius family.*

Edward stopped.

Jonathan looked at the next paragraph.

*I agreed to the arrangement because I believed it would keep the child safe until the original maternal identity could be verified.*

*I was wrong to believe that temporary protection would remain temporary.*

Edward continued.

*The receiving person was told that the child had no confirmed identity. She was not told which family had originally requested the child.*

*She was instructed not to register the child under either family's name.*

*I did not give her permission to change the child's identity permanently.*

*I did not give her permission to conceal the child's existence from the mother indefinitely.*

The final paragraph was short.

*If you find this child, do not assume she knows she was protected. She may have grown up believing she was unwanted.*

Edward placed the letter down.

For several moments, the room remained silent.

Then Richard Valmont spoke.

"Who received her?"

Edward turned to the second sheet.

The receiving agreement was dated the ninth day after the births.

It contained no full name.

Only a description.

**RECEIVING PARTY: PRIVATE CIVILIAN GUARDIAN**

**RELATIONSHIP TO CHILD: NONE**

**FAMILY AFFILIATION: NONE**

**PLACEMENT STATUS: TEMPORARY**

**IDENTITY DISCLOSURE: RESTRICTED**

The receiving party had signed beneath the conditions.

The signature was partially obscured by a later document stamp.

Edward examined it.

"Can we recover the name?"

"Possibly," Jonathan said. "The original carbon copy may still exist."

"Where?"

"Vale International Legal Group's document archive."

Sebastian looked at the agreement.

"Why would a civilian with no connection to either family agree to take a newborn under such restrictions?"

Nobody answered immediately.

Catherine finally spoke.

"Perhaps she believed she was helping a child."

Richard looked at her.

"Or perhaps she was paid."

"Those possibilities aren't mutually exclusive."

Catherine's voice was quiet.

She had spent weeks watching documents transform people she had known for years into strangers.

She did not want another assumption to become another accusation.

Victoria looked at the agreement.

"Was the person told that the child's mother was looking for her?"

Edward shook his head.

"Not according to this document."

"Then she might have believed the mother didn't want the child."

"That is possible."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

The thought was difficult to bear.

The third sheet was handwritten.

It contained only a few lines.

*Eleanor,*

*I will keep her safe.*

*I will not ask which family she belongs to.*

*But I cannot promise that she will remain with me forever.*

*If her mother comes looking, I want to know before anyone sends her away again.*

There was no signature.

At the bottom, Eleanor had written:

**SHE ASKED FOR THE MOTHER'S NAME. I REFUSED.**

Edward looked at the sentence.

"Why would Eleanor refuse to tell her?"

Jonathan examined the handwriting.

"Perhaps she didn't know."

"She had access to the original maternal records."

"Access isn't the same as certainty."

Edward nodded slowly.

"Then the receiving guardian may have wanted to return the child to her mother."

"Possibly."

"Which means she may have been trying to help."

Jonathan pointed to the first paragraph.

"Or she may have been negotiating for the right to keep the child."

Edward looked at him.

"Either way, she wasn't told the child's identity."

The Aurelius family received copies of the documents that evening.

Alexander read Eleanor's letter twice.

"She believed the child was safe."

Victoria looked at him.

"She believed she had made the right decision."

"Those aren't necessarily the same thing."

Adrian stood beside the window.

"The receiving guardian was outside both families. That means we cannot assume she had any prior connection to the hospital or the Family Office."

"Could she have been a relative of one of the nurses?" Damien asked.

"Possible," Adrian said. "But there's no evidence."

Lucien examined the receiving agreement.

"She took a newborn without knowing which family the child belonged to. That's an extraordinary decision."

"Not necessarily," Victoria said.

Everyone looked at her.

"She may have believed she was temporarily caring for a child whose mother was in danger or unable to receive her."

Alexander considered that.

"You're trying to give her the benefit of the doubt."

"I'm trying not to condemn someone because we don't understand her decision."

Julian looked at the document.

"Does that mean the child might have grown up with her?"

"Yes," Adrian answered.

"Then she might have thought that woman was her mother."

Victoria nodded.

"She might have."

Julian closed his notebook.

He had imagined finding his sister many times.

In most of those imagined situations, she was waiting somewhere, perhaps wondering why nobody had come.

He had never considered that she might already have a family.

Not necessarily a biological one.

But a family nevertheless.

He looked at Victoria.

"If we find her, we shouldn't make her leave the person who raised her immediately."

Victoria reached across the table and held his hand.

"No. We shouldn't."

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was dealing with a much more ordinary problem.

Her university portal had finally updated.

She was sitting at the dining table with her laptop open.

Nicholas was standing behind her.

"Well?"

"Wait."

"Elena."

"Wait."

"You're taking too long."

"You're making it worse."

He stepped back.

Isabelle entered carrying a folder.

"Did the result arrive?"

Elena nodded.

She clicked the notification.

The page loaded.

For a moment, she simply stared at the screen.

Then she read the first sentence aloud.

"Congratulations. Your application has been shortlisted for the Singapore International Academic Programme."

Nicholas raised both hands.

"Yes!"

Elena continued reading.

Her expression gradually became more serious.

"What is it?" Isabelle asked.

"They've offered me a place in the final selection stage. There's a second assessment and a financial documentation review."

"That's good, isn't it?" Nicholas asked.

"Yes. But it's not a final admission."

He lowered his hands.

"Still good."

Elena smiled.

"Very good."

Isabelle pulled out a chair.

"Do you need help with the documents?"

"Perhaps. There are a few financial forms."

"I can look at them with you."

Elena turned towards her.

"Wouldn't you have your own work?"

"I can do both."

Nicholas leaned over the table.

"Should we celebrate?"

Elena closed the laptop.

"After the final selection."

"That's unreasonable."

"That's practical."

Isabelle laughed.

For a little while, the investigation, the old hospital records, and the uncertainty surrounding their birth identities remained outside the dining room.

They were simply three young people discussing university applications.

And Elena was happy.

Edward's next step was the Vale archive.

The original agreement had been filed under a reference that had not appeared in any of the earlier records.

**PRIVATE CIVILIAN PLACEMENT — NINTH DAY.**

The archive contained a scanned index but no original document.

Jonathan searched the associated correspondence.

"Here."

Edward approached.

Jonathan had found a payment voucher.

It was dated the ninth day after the births.

The payment had been made to a private residential address.

The recipient's name was not recorded.

Instead, the voucher stated:

**INITIAL CARE ALLOWANCE — INFANT A.**

Below it was a second payment.

**CONTINUED CARE ALLOWANCE — SUBJECT TO REVIEW.**

The address was unfamiliar.

Edward copied it.

"Let's find out who owned the property."

The property had been sold several times since then.

The original owner was a retired schoolteacher named Helen Brooks.

She had died twelve years earlier.

But the property records showed something interesting.

Helen Brooks had transferred ownership to a younger woman approximately eighteen months after the children's births.

The transfer had been completed through a private trust.

The new owner's name was:

**M. ELLISON.**

Edward stared at the record.

"Ellison?"

Jonathan checked the address again.

"Not Margaret Ellison's current address."

"Could be a relative."

"Or an unrelated person."

Edward requested the original transfer documents.

They arrived two hours later.

The transfer had been authorised by Charles Vale.

The beneficiary was described as:

**THE RECEIVING GUARDIAN OF INFANT A.**

No name.

No relationship.

But there was a handwritten note on the final page.

**PROPERTY TO REMAIN AVAILABLE UNTIL THE CHILD REACHES THE AGE OF SIXTEEN.**

Edward looked at the date.

The property had been maintained for sixteen years.

Then, almost immediately after the child's sixteenth birthday, it had been sold.

"Why sixteen?" Jonathan asked.

Edward examined the remaining pages.

There was no explanation.

But a second document had been attached.

It was a letter from Eleanor Vale.

*The child is old enough to ask questions now.*

*The receiving guardian must decide whether she can continue to answer them.*

*If she cannot, the child must be given access to the original records.*

Edward read the final sentence.

*She should not spend another year believing that nobody knows where she is.*

The following afternoon, Edward returned to Margaret Voss.

He showed her the property transfer.

"Do you recognise this address?"

Margaret examined it.

Her fingers tightened around the page.

"Yes."

"Whose property was it?"

She looked at him.

"That was where Infant A was placed."

"Who lived there?"

She hesitated.

"A woman named Helen."

"Helen Brooks?"

"Yes."

"Was she the receiving guardian?"

Margaret nodded.

Edward sat down.

"Why didn't you tell us this yesterday?"

"Because I didn't know whether the address still existed."

"Did Helen raise the child?"

"For a time."

"How long?"

"Until she became ill."

"What happened then?"

Margaret looked towards the window.

"Someone else took responsibility."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Edward placed the property transfer document before her.

"The property was transferred to an unnamed receiving guardian through a private trust."

Margaret studied the page.

Then she noticed the date.

"Sixteen years..."

"Does that mean something?"

She slowly nodded.

"That was when Eleanor wanted the child to be told the truth."

"Did she tell her?"

"I don't know."

"Who was supposed to tell her?"

"The person who had her."

Edward leaned forward.

"Was that person Helen Brooks?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No."

"Then who?"

Margaret looked at him.

"The woman who came after Helen."

Edward felt the direction of the investigation shifting again.

"Do you know her name?"

Margaret's answer was immediate.

"No."

"Did you ever meet her?"

"Once."

Edward waited.

"Where?"

"At Eleanor's house."

"What did she look like?"

Margaret closed her eyes, searching her memory.

"She was younger than Eleanor. Perhaps in her thirties. She had a quiet voice and wore a dark green coat."

"Did she have any identifying features?"

Margaret opened her eyes.

"She had a small scar across her left thumb."

Edward stopped.

The same scar.

Daniel Mercer had described the woman who collected Eleanor's papers.

The woman who had waited for one of the children.

Margaret continued.

"She told Eleanor she would take care of the girl."

Edward placed the photograph of the elderly Margaret Voss on the table.

"Was it this woman?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No. This was Margaret Voss."

Edward showed her a second photograph.

It was an old image recovered from the estate inventory.

The woman standing beside Eleanor was partially turned away from the camera.

Margaret examined it.

Her expression changed.

"Yes."

"Who is she?"

Margaret looked at the photograph for a long time.

Then she spoke.

"I don't know her name."

Edward felt his frustration return.

"But I know what Eleanor called her."

"What?"

Margaret pointed to the woman.

"She called her **Mrs. Aurelius**."

Edward went completely still.

That evening, the photograph was sent to the Aurelius Estate.

Victoria examined it first.

Then Alexander.

Adrian enlarged the image.

The woman was not clearly identifiable.

Her face was partially hidden, and the photograph was nearly two decades old.

But something about her clothing and posture suggested that she had not been an ordinary visitor.

Victoria looked at the handwritten note attached to the photograph.

**ELEANOR VALE — PRIVATE MEETING.**

**ATTENDEE: MRS. AURELIUS.**

She slowly lowered the page.

"Who was she?"

Alexander looked at the photograph.

"We'll find out."

Adrian turned towards the family records.

"There is one problem."

"What?"

"The Aurelius family had no authorised representative named Mrs. Aurelius at that time."

Victoria looked at him.

"Perhaps she was using the title."

"Or someone gave it to her."

Damien examined the photograph again.

"Could she have been pretending to be a member of the family?"

Alexander did not answer.

He was looking at the woman's left hand.

A small scar crossed the base of her thumb.

And on her right hand was a ring.

A simple silver ring with a dark stone.

The same description Margaret Voss had given of the man who had approached the Valmont family at the hospital eighteen years earlier.

Alexander slowly turned the photograph over.

On the back, Eleanor had written a single sentence.

**SHE CLAIMS TO BE THE MOTHER OF THE CHILD.**
