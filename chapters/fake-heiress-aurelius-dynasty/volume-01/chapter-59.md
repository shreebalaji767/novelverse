# **Chapter 59 — The Man Who Took a Child Home**

The instruction was unusual.

Edward read it again.

**NEITHER FAMILY HAS ANY RIGHT TO ASK ME ABOUT THE CHILD I TOOK HOME.**

The receptionist had printed the instruction from Gabriel Vale's visitor restrictions.

It had been signed eight months earlier.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Why would he phrase it like that?"

"Perhaps he believes he raised one of the children."

"Or perhaps he was only responsible for her temporarily."

"Either way, he doesn't want either family involved."

Edward folded the paper.

"Then we should find out why."

Gabriel Vale lived in a private residential care facility on the outskirts of the city.

The building was quiet, surrounded by a carefully maintained garden.

A nurse led Edward and Jonathan to a small consultation room.

"Mr. Vale has agreed to see you," she said. "But he has asked that you don't discuss his family arrangements without his permission."

"Understood," Edward replied.

A few minutes later, an elderly man entered.

He was thinner than Edward expected.

His hair had turned almost completely white, and he walked with the assistance of a cane.

But his eyes were alert.

He looked at Edward first.

"Valmont?"

"Edward Valmont."

Gabriel gave a short nod.

"I wondered when one of you would come."

Edward indicated the chair.

"Thank you for agreeing to meet."

"I haven't agreed to tell you anything."

"That's your choice."

Gabriel sat down.

His gaze moved to Jonathan.

"And you?"

"Jonathan Pierce. Legal investigator."

Gabriel looked back at Edward.

"Which child are you looking for?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

"Both."

Gabriel gave a quiet laugh.

"Of course."

"What does that mean?"

"It means eighteen years have passed, and nobody has managed to establish which child belongs where."

Edward leaned forward.

"Do you know?"

Gabriel looked at him for a long moment.

Then he said:

"I know which child I took home."

Edward became still.

"Which child?"

"Infant A."

"Was she biologically related to the Aurelius family?"

Gabriel's expression changed.

"I don't know."

"Did you know her identity?"

"No."

"Then why did you take her?"

Gabriel rested both hands on his cane.

"Because someone had to."

The first account Gabriel gave was brief.

He had been working with Vale Private Counsel when Charles Vale contacted him about a confidential arrangement involving two newborn children.

He was told that the hospital had made an administrative error.

Two infants had been given provisional identities.

The families involved were powerful, and the situation could become dangerous if the wrong people learned the details.

Gabriel was asked to act as an intermediary.

At first, he believed he would simply transport documents and supervise a temporary placement.

Then he was asked to take one of the children.

"Who asked you?" Edward said.

"Charles."

"Did Eleanor know?"

"Yes."

"Did Margaret Voss know?"

"Yes."

"Did the Aurelius family know?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"Someone from the Aurelius Family Office knew enough to authorise the arrangement."

"That wasn't my question."

"No. The family itself was not informed."

Edward's jaw tightened.

"Alexander Aurelius has spent eighteen years searching for his missing daughter."

Gabriel looked away.

"I know."

"Then why did you keep a child from him?"

"Because I was told that the child might not be his daughter."

"That doesn't justify concealing her."

"No," Gabriel said quietly. "It doesn't."

For the first time, he appeared genuinely tired.

"I have had eighteen years to think about that."

Edward opened his notebook.

"Where did you take Infant A?"

"To a private residence."

"Whose residence?"

"Mine."

Edward looked up.

"Your home?"

"Yes."

"Did you raise her?"

"For a while."

"How long?"

Gabriel considered the question.

"Not long enough to call myself her father."

"Did you have a family?"

"I had a wife. We had no children."

"Did your wife know?"

"She knew I was bringing home a baby temporarily. She did not know the child's original identity."

"Did she agree?"

"Not immediately."

Gabriel looked towards the window.

"She thought I had lost my mind."

Edward waited.

"Then she saw the child."

"What happened?"

"She held her."

His voice softened slightly.

"After that, she asked me how long we would have her."

Edward wrote down the answer.

"Did you tell her?"

"I said I didn't know."

"Was the child given a name?"

"Yes."

"What name?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"Anna."

Edward's pen stopped.

"Anna?"

"Yes."

"Was this the same Anna who later entered St. Agnes Children's Recovery Centre?"

"I don't know."

"Did you register the child under that name?"

"No. The child was not registered with us."

"Then how do you know she was called Anna?"

"Because that was the name Eleanor suggested."

Edward thought of the rental property, the St. Agnes records, and the girl who had repeatedly asked whether her mother was alive.

"Why Anna?"

Gabriel gave a faint smile.

"Eleanor said the child deserved a name that belonged to her, not a reference number."

Jonathan placed the photograph of the infant from Margaret Ellison's records on the table.

"Is this the child you took home?"

Gabriel examined it.

After a moment, he nodded.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"Are you certain?"

"I remember that hospital band."

"Could you identify the child by her face?"

"No. She was a newborn."

"Then what makes you certain?"

Gabriel pointed to a small mark visible on the infant's blanket.

"My wife embroidered that."

Edward looked closely.

There was a tiny stitched flower near the edge.

"Your wife made this?"

"Yes."

"Do you have the original?"

Gabriel shook his head.

"No. We returned the blanket with the child."

"To whom?"

Gabriel hesitated.

"Her next guardian."

Edward's expression sharpened.

"Who was that?"

"Margaret Ellison."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Margaret Ellison?"

Gabriel nodded.

"She was the person who agreed to care for the child after us."

"But Margaret Ellison received a baby on the ninth day."

"That is correct."

"How could you have taken the child before her?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"Because the ninth-day records don't describe the first placement."

Edward sat back.

The chronology was wrong.

Or incomplete.

"Explain."

Gabriel folded his hands.

"I received the child on the sixth day."

The room became silent.

Edward opened the old hospital records.

"There's no sixth-day release."

"Then your records are incomplete."

"Who authorised your custody?"

"Charles Vale."

"Under what authority?"

"An emergency temporary placement instruction."

"Was the mother informed?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"I was told she had been informed."

"Which mother?"

"I wasn't told."

Edward felt his frustration rising.

"Did you ask?"

"Yes."

"What did they say?"

"That the mother had been advised that her child was receiving specialist care."

"Was that true?"

Gabriel looked down.

"I don't know."

Edward closed the file.

"Did you ever meet the mother?"

"No."

"Did she contact you?"

"Not directly."

"Then how did you know the child was supposed to be returned?"

Gabriel's answer came slowly.

"Because Eleanor told me."

Gabriel remembered the day Eleanor visited his house.

It was the eighth day after the births.

She arrived alone.

His wife was holding the baby when Eleanor entered.

The child was asleep.

Eleanor stood beside the cot for several minutes.

Then she asked Gabriel to speak privately.

"She told me the original arrangement had failed," Gabriel said.

"What arrangement?"

"That the two children were supposed to be placed with their respective families after their identities were verified."

"And that hadn't happened?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the hospital records were compromised."

Edward wrote it down.

"Did Eleanor explain how?"

"She said someone had changed the maternal identification pages."

"Did she know who?"

"She suspected someone within the hospital and someone within the legal office."

"Did she name them?"

"No."

Gabriel paused.

"Then she told me something I didn't understand at the time."

"What?"

"She said the child I was holding might not be the child I had been asked to protect."

Edward looked up.

"Did she explain?"

"She said there were two separate questions. Who had given birth to the child, and who had been assigned to receive her."

"And those answers might be different."

"Exactly."

Edward asked the question that had been bothering him.

"Why did you agree to take the child?"

Gabriel looked at the photograph.

"Because Eleanor said the baby would otherwise be transferred to someone whose identity had not been verified."

"Who?"

"She didn't know."

"Then why didn't you take the child to the police?"

Gabriel gave him a tired look.

"Eighteen years ago, I believed the hospital, the legal office, and the Family Office were all acting in the child's interests. I thought the situation was temporary."

"And when did you realise it wasn't?"

"After the ninth day."

"What happened then?"

"Charles told me the child had been reassigned."

"To Margaret Ellison?"

"Yes."

"Did you agree?"

"I did not have a choice. The temporary authority I held had been withdrawn."

Edward looked at him.

"Did you return the child?"

"Yes."

"To Margaret Ellison?"

"Yes."

"Then why does her account say that she received a child from Charles Vale?"

Gabriel's expression darkened.

"Because Charles collected the child from my house."

Edward stopped.

"Charles physically collected her?"

"Yes."

"And delivered her to Margaret Ellison?"

"That's what I was told."

"Did you see him hand her over?"

"No."

"Then you cannot confirm where she went."

Gabriel looked at him.

"Neither can you."

Edward did not argue.

There was another problem.

If Gabriel had taken Infant A on the sixth day, the ninth-day placement records could not be the first receiving arrangements.

They were later movements.

Edward asked for the original emergency instruction.

Gabriel shook his head.

"Charles took it."

"Did you sign anything?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"A temporary custody acknowledgment."

"Do you have a copy?"

"No."

"Did you keep any records?"

Gabriel looked towards his cane.

"One."

He reached into the inner pocket of his jacket and removed a small folded paper.

It was a hospital discharge note.

The document was dated the sixth day after the births.

It contained no infant name.

Only a reference number.

**INFANT A — TEMPORARY RELEASE.**

The receiving party was listed as:

**G. VALE.**

At the bottom was a handwritten instruction.

**RETURN TO ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE UPON REQUEST.**

Edward examined it.

"Who was the originating representative?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"That's what I never found out."

Back at the Valmont Estate, Elena received another notification.

She opened the university portal.

This time, the message was short.

Her application had passed the second assessment.

She had been offered provisional admission to the Singapore International Academic Programme, subject to the final verification of her academic and financial documents.

Elena read it twice.

Then she called Nicholas.

He arrived in less than a minute.

"What happened?"

"I got it."

"What?"

"The provisional admission."

Nicholas stared at her.

Then his face broke into a grin.

"You got in!"

"Provisionally."

"You got in!"

Elena laughed.

"Yes!"

He pulled out a chair.

"Show me."

She turned the laptop towards him.

He read the message carefully.

Then he stood.

"I'm telling everyone."

"Wait!"

But he was already leaving.

A few minutes later, Catherine, Richard, Sebastian, and Isabelle gathered around the table.

Catherine hugged Elena.

Richard congratulated her.

Sebastian asked about the final documentation requirements.

Isabelle smiled.

"You're going to Singapore."

"Not yet," Elena said. "I still have to submit everything."

"Then we'll help you."

Elena looked around the table.

For a moment, the uncertainty surrounding her birth seemed far away.

She had earned this opportunity.

Whatever her biological identity turned out to be, it did not change that.

Edward returned to the Aurelius Estate that evening.

He gave Alexander the sixth-day discharge note.

Alexander examined it carefully.

"Infant A was released to Gabriel Vale six days after birth."

"Yes."

"Why wasn't this in the hospital records?"

"Because the private nursery records began on the seventh day. The earlier release was handled through a separate emergency instruction."

Adrian looked at the note.

"That means the ninth-day transfer wasn't the first movement."

"Correct."

Damien crossed his arms.

"How many movements were there?"

"We don't know."

Victoria looked at the reference number.

"Was this the child who later went to Margaret Ellison?"

"Gabriel says he returned the child to Charles on the ninth day. Charles supposedly delivered her to Margaret."

"Supposedly?"

"Gabriel never witnessed the handover."

Victoria looked at the document again.

"Then we still don't know where Infant A went after Gabriel."

Edward nodded.

"But we have another lead."

He placed a photograph on the table.

It showed Gabriel and a woman standing outside a modest house.

The woman was holding a baby.

"Who is she?" Victoria asked.

"Gabriel's wife."

Adrian examined the photograph.

"Is she alive?"

"Yes. She lives in a different city. Gabriel says she may remember the child's later placement."

Victoria looked at the infant.

"Then we should speak to her."

Alexander nodded.

"Carefully."

At the residential facility, Gabriel sat alone after Edward left.

He had not told the investigator everything.

There was one more thing he remembered.

On the ninth day, when Charles came to collect the child, Gabriel had asked whether the mother knew.

Charles had replied that the matter was being handled.

Gabriel had not believed him.

He had asked again.

Charles had finally said:

"The mother has already received her child."

Gabriel had looked at the baby in his wife's arms.

Then at Charles.

"Which mother?"

Charles had not answered.

Instead, he had taken the child.

Before leaving, he had placed a small envelope on the table.

It contained a photograph of a young woman.

On the back, there were four handwritten words.

**SHE MUST NOT KNOW.**

Gabriel had kept that photograph for eighteen years.

He had never shown it to Margaret Voss.

He had never shown it to Eleanor.

And he had never told his wife what it meant.

Now, after all these years, he asked the nurse to bring him his personal effects.

The envelope was still there.

The photograph inside was slightly faded.

But the face remained clear.

It was not Victoria Aurelius.

It was not Catherine Valmont.

It was not Eleanor Vale.

It was a young woman Gabriel had never met.

On the back of the photograph, beneath the original instruction, was a name.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**
