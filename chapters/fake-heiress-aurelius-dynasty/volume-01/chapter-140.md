# **Chapter 140 — The Instruction That Prevented Its Own Review**

The instruction had been entered on day forty-eight.

It was not part of the original PR-06 exception.

It was not included in the signed document.

It existed only as a separate administrative entry.

**DO NOT ISSUE THE ORIGINAL PROCEDURE WHILE PR-06 REMAINS ACTIVE.**

Edward read the sentence again.

“Who entered it?”

Jonathan displayed the audit history.

“The record was created through the Personal Instructions Division account. The operator field is blank.”

“Was it approved?”

“No approval reference.”

“Then why was it followed?”

“Because the system classified it as a restricted distribution instruction.”

Edward looked at the screen.

“Who could issue that classification?”

“Three roles. The originating office representative, the Personal Instructions Division supervisor, and the records preservation officer.”

“Which of them authorised this entry?”

“None of the three has an identifiable approval attached.”

Edward leaned back.

“So an unsigned instruction from a shared account prevented the distribution of a document that had already been prepared.”

“Yes.”

“And the reason given was that the temporary exception remained active.”

Jonathan nodded.

Edward closed the audit history.

“Find every attempt to cancel or review PR-06.”

The search produced six records.

The first was Gabriel's day-93 request to review his authority.

The second was Eleanor's day-97 objection.

The third was a day-106 request from the Personal Instructions Division asking whether the exception required renewal.

The fourth was a day-118 review note from the restricted archive.

The fifth was a day-131 query from the Family Allocation Review office.

The sixth had been submitted much later, on day 174.

Edward examined the sixth entry.

It was marked:

**PR-06 — STATUS CLOSURE REQUEST**

The request had been submitted by the records preservation officer.

Its purpose was straightforward.

The original temporary communication exception had no expiry date. The receiving authority remained unappointed. The protected principal's personal acknowledgement had never been received.

The preservation officer wanted to know whether the exception should remain active.

The answer was recorded two days later.

**STATUS TO REMAIN ACTIVE UNTIL RECEIVING AUTHORITY APPOINTED.**

Edward frowned.

“Who approved this?”

Jonathan checked.

“Charles Vale.”

“Did he provide a reason?”

“There is a note.”

Edward opened it.

*The communication position must remain available to prevent interruption of temporary care and correspondence arrangements.*

He read it twice.

“Was the original procedure mentioned?”

“No.”

“Was P-04's missing acknowledgement mentioned?”

“No.”

“Was the failed delivery at C-19 mentioned?”

“No.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Then this was not a review of the communication process. It was a continuation of the administrative status.”

Jonathan nodded.

“And it linked the exception's duration to the appointment of a receiving authority.”

Edward looked at the date.

Day 176.

By then, the original procedure had remained undistributed for nearly five months.

The receiving authority had still not been appointed.

And the temporary exception had no independent review mechanism.

“Find the preservation officer,” Edward said.

The preservation officer was a retired administrator named Helen Ward.

She remembered submitting the day-174 request.

“I was reviewing the restricted archive,” she explained. “The temporary exception had been in place for months. I wanted to know whether it still had a valid administrative purpose.”

“What prompted you to ask?”

“The original communication procedure was still listed as undistributed. I noticed that the restriction preventing its release referred to PR-06.”

“Did you understand the relationship?”

“I understood that the exception was being used to prevent the original procedure from being issued.”

Edward placed the two records before her.

“Did you believe that was appropriate?”

“No.”

“Then why did you ask whether the exception should remain active instead of requesting that the procedure be released?”

Helen considered the question.

“Because the receiving authority had not been appointed. I was concerned that issuing the procedure without the required recipient might create another administrative problem.”

Edward studied her.

“Did anyone tell you that the receiving authority was required to deliver information to the communication representative?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell you that the representative could not receive the procedure directly?”

Helen shook her head.

“I assumed the restriction had been authorised.”

“By whom?”

“That was what I wanted to establish.”

Edward looked at the response.

Charles had approved continuation.

“Did you challenge his answer?”

“Yes.”

“Where is the challenge?”

Helen retrieved a separate file.

Her letter was dated day 177.

*The status continuation does not resolve the outstanding communication issue. The original procedure remains undistributed, and the protected principal's personal acknowledgement remains absent. I request confirmation of the authority under which the original procedure is being withheld.*

Edward read the reply.

It was dated day 179.

*The original procedure is retained pending completion of the receiving identity process. PR-06 remains active for temporary administrative continuity.*

No legal authority was cited.

No new approval was attached.

The same explanation had simply been repeated.

“Did you escalate it?” Edward asked.

“Yes.”

“To whom?”

“Eleanor Vale.”

Eleanor remembered receiving Helen's letter.

She had been concerned about the restriction since day forty-eight, but the day-177 request had forced her to examine the archive more closely.

“I requested the original PR-06 instruction,” she said.

“Did you receive it?”

“Yes.”

“Did you ask who had added the distribution restriction?”

“Yes.”

“Did anyone answer?”

“No.”

Eleanor opened a folder.

Inside was her day-181 objection.

*The existence of an active temporary exception cannot independently justify withholding the original communication procedure. The procedure is the instrument intended to preserve the principal's opportunity to receive information and respond. Its continued non-issuance defeats that purpose.*

Edward placed the letter beside the day-48 instruction.

“Did you send this to Charles?”

“I sent it to the Personal Instructions Division and copied his private office.”

“Did he respond?”

“He returned the matter to administrative review.”

“What did that mean?”

“That the office responsible for maintaining the status would review the status it had already maintained.”

Edward looked at her.

“Did you consider that an adequate response?”

“No.”

She turned another page.

There was a handwritten note beneath the return stamp.

*No change required. Existing procedure remains valid while PR-06 is active.*

Edward looked at the handwriting.

It was not Charles's.

It was the same unknown writer whose notes had appeared on several restricted records.

“Have you seen this handwriting before?” he asked.

Eleanor examined it.

“Yes.”

“Where?”

She took out another file.

“This was attached to the day-118 review.”

The day-118 record was familiar.

It had been created after Eleanor discovered that a copy of Schedule 4 had been used to tell a family they were allocated a child.

She had requested a review of the allocation and the communication process.

The original R-04 register recorded that the receiving representative had declined to acknowledge the proposed allocation.

The later administrative summary had described the notification as complete for administrative purposes.

Now Eleanor showed Edward a separate page from the same review.

It contained the familiar handwriting.

*PR-06 remains active. No personal acknowledgement required for temporary continuity. Original communication procedure to remain sealed pending receiving authority appointment.*

Edward examined the page.

The instruction was dated day 118.

The handwriting was similar to the note on the day-48 record.

Dr Sen had not yet compared them.

“Was this included in the official review?”

“No,” Eleanor said. “It was attached to the restricted copy.”

“Who received that copy?”

“The Personal Instructions Division.”

“Did you challenge it?”

“I asked for the source of the instruction.”

“And?”

“No answer.”

Edward looked at the page.

“Then the same restriction was repeated seventy days later.”

“Yes.”

“And again on day 177.”

“Yes.”

Eleanor's expression was troubled.

“I don't believe the process was being reviewed at all. I believe the office was checking whether the previous restriction could continue.”

Jonathan found the day-106 request in a different archive.

It had been submitted by Daniel Mercer.

Daniel remembered it when Edward interviewed him.

“I was asked to confirm whether the PR-06 exception needed renewal during the migration.”

“Who asked?”

“Charles's office.”

“Did you consider the lack of an expiry date?”

“I did.”

“What did you do?”

“I checked the original instruction.”

“Did you find an expiry condition?”

“No.”

“Did you recommend a review?”

“Yes.”

Daniel produced his old working note.

*Temporary exception remains active without stated expiry. Recommend independent review and confirmation of continuing necessity.*

Edward read it.

“Who rejected the recommendation?”

“I don't know.”

“Was your note forwarded?”

“Yes.”

“Where?”

“Personal Instructions Division.”

“Did you receive a response?”

Daniel searched his own archive.

“Yes. A status instruction.”

He opened the file.

**PR-06 — CONTINUATION CONFIRMED.**

The reason field contained a familiar sentence.

*Receiving authority not yet appointed.*

Edward stared at it.

“The same condition.”

“Yes.”

“Did you question that?”

“I asked whether the lack of an appointed receiving authority was sufficient reason to continue the exception.”

“And?”

Daniel looked uncomfortable.

“I was told the temporary care arrangements depended on administrative continuity.”

“Did you believe that?”

“I believed temporary care should continue. I did not believe that meant the communication procedure should remain withheld.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Did you tell anyone?”

“I sent a note.”

“Who received it?”

“Eleanor.”

Edward made a note.

The pattern was becoming clearer.

Several people had recognised the problem.

They had asked for review.

Their objections had been retained.

But the status itself had never changed.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria read Daniel's note.

“Then this wasn't simply an administrative oversight,” she said.

Alexander stood at the other end of the table.

“Several people identified the problem.”

“Yes,” Edward replied.

“And each time the answer was to continue the same arrangement.”

“Yes.”

Victoria looked at the documents.

“Did anyone ever attempt to contact P-04 again?”

“Not through a verified process that we have found.”

“Did anyone try to release the original procedure?”

“No.”

Alexander's expression hardened.

“Then the principal was effectively excluded from a process that supposedly existed to protect their interests.”

Edward nodded.

“Without a formal decision to exclude them.”

Victoria looked down at the day-48 instruction.

“Whoever wrote this understood the original procedure was still waiting to be delivered.”

“We haven't established who wrote it,” Edward said.

“But the person knew what it did.”

“Yes.”

Alexander turned toward him.

“Can we identify the author?”

“Dr Sen is comparing the handwriting.”

“And the authority?”

“Still unclear.”

Victoria returned to the documents.

“I want the original communication procedure preserved exactly as it is. No one should be allowed to rewrite it or attach another interpretation to it.”

Edward nodded.

“It remains sealed under the examination order.”

She looked at him.

“And the person who was supposed to receive it?”

“We still don't know whether the visitor at C-19 was P-04.”

Victoria's expression softened.

“Then we must not assume.”

Elena's prototype had reached its next stage.

Professor Lin had given the testing group a new scenario.

A temporary decision had been made by an authorised officer. The decision was valid for a limited period, but the system had not recorded an expiry date.

The students were asked to determine what should happen next.

One suggested that the system automatically renew the decision.

Another suggested that it should terminate immediately.

Elena shook her head.

“Neither is necessarily correct.”

“Then what would you do?” Professor Lin asked.

“First, I would check the authority that created the temporary decision. If it specifies a review condition, the system should track that condition. If it doesn't, the system should flag the missing review mechanism rather than invent one.”

“And while the review is pending?”

“That depends on the rules. Some temporary actions may need to continue to prevent harm. But continuing them should not create a permanent decision.”

Professor Lin nodded.

“Can your system distinguish those two things?”

“I've added a separate field.”

Elena opened the prototype.

**TEMPORARY STATUS**

**CONTINUATION BASIS**

**REVIEW AUTHORITY**

**REVIEW DEADLINE OR TRIGGER**

**UNDERLYING DECISION STATUS**

She selected the last field.

It displayed:

UNRESOLVED

“Even if the temporary status continues,” Elena explained, “the underlying decision remains unresolved until the proper authority makes it.”

Professor Lin studied the screen.

“That's a useful distinction.”

Elena smiled.

“It's also a difficult one to explain to people who only want the dashboard to show green.”

The professor laughed.

“Then perhaps the dashboard should sometimes be allowed to show amber.”

After the session, Elena sent the updated prototype to Clara.

Clara replied with a photograph of her handwritten notes.

At the top, she had written:

*Don't let the system pretend it knows something it doesn't.*

Elena laughed.

She added the sentence to her private project notes.

It was not a technical requirement.

But it was a good principle.

Dr Sen completed her handwriting comparison the following afternoon.

Edward met her at the archive office.

The report contained three conclusions.

First, the day-48 distribution restriction and the day-118 note were likely written by the same individual.

Second, the handwriting showed partial similarities to the unknown writer of the AV-18 recipient correspondence.

Third, the available samples were insufficient for definitive attribution.

Edward read the report.

“Could it be Eleanor?”

“No,” Dr Sen replied. “The letter formations differ significantly.”

“Charles?”

“Not consistent.”

“Gabriel?”

“No reliable match.”

“Marianne?”

“Also not consistent.”

“Margaret Voss?”

Dr Sen shook her head.

“Not enough similarity.”

Edward looked at the report.

“Then who?”

“I cannot identify the writer from these samples.”

She placed another document beside it.

“This is more interesting.”

It was the day-48 distribution instruction.

The paper carried an impression from a document that had been placed above it when the entry was written.

Dr Sen had recovered several faint words.

Edward leaned forward.

“What does it say?”

She pointed to the impression.

**PERSONAL REPRESENTATION — CONTROL COPY**

Beneath it:

**RELEASE ONLY AFTER PRINCIPAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT OR INDEPENDENT AUTHORITY REVIEW**

Edward read the words.

“That's the original requirement.”

“Yes.”

Dr Sen showed him the impression from the reverse.

A second line was visible.

**DO NOT DUPLICATE INTO GENERAL ADMINISTRATIVE RECORD.**

Edward looked up.

“Why would the person writing the restriction have a copy of the original release conditions?”

“That is what we cannot establish yet.”

Edward examined the impression again.

The day-48 restriction had been written on top of a document containing the original release conditions.

The writer knew the conditions.

And had still instructed the office not to issue the procedure while PR-06 remained active.

Edward turned to Jonathan.

“Find the control copy.”

Jonathan was already searching.

The archive index showed that a control copy had been prepared on day thirty-three.

It was not in the Personal Instructions Division.

It had been filed under a different reference.

**PR-06 / PRINCIPAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT CONTROL**

The custodian field was not blank.

It contained a name.

**MARGARET VOSS.**

Margaret Voss did not deny holding the control copy.

She retrieved it from a locked cabinet in her private records room.

“I was given this for preservation,” she explained.

“When?”

“Day thirty-four.”

“By whom?”

“Charles's office.”

“Did you open it?”

“It was not sealed.”

Edward examined the document.

It was a complete copy of the original communication procedure, including the separate acknowledgement sections.

The final page contained a tracking table.

**ORIGINAL PROCEDURE — DISTRIBUTION STATUS**

- Prepared: Day 33.
- Verified: Pending.
- Released: No.
- Principal acknowledgement: Not received.
- Independent review: Pending.

At the bottom was an additional instruction.

*The control copy must be retained independently from the administrative continuation record. Any change to the distribution status must be supported by an authorised release decision.*

Edward looked at Margaret.

“Did you receive a release decision?”

“No.”

“Did you receive a request to release the procedure?”

“No.”

“Then why was it kept?”

“Because I was instructed to preserve it.”

“Did you know the general administrative record contained an instruction preventing its release?”

Margaret looked at him.

“I discovered that later.”

“Did you challenge it?”

“Yes.”

She opened another folder.

Her letter was dated day fifty.

*The original communication procedure remains prepared but undistributed. The control copy must not be treated as satisfying the principal's acknowledgement requirement. I request confirmation of the authority under which the procedure is being withheld.*

Edward read the reply.

It was dated day fifty-two.

*The control copy is preserved. The active temporary communication status remains sufficient for administrative continuity.*

Margaret's face was tight.

“I didn't agree with that answer.”

“Did you send another objection?”

“Yes.”

“Where is it?”

She placed another letter on the table.

Day fifty-four.

The final paragraph was underlined.

*Preservation of a document is not delivery of that document. Retention of a control copy cannot satisfy the communication requirement.*

Edward looked at her.

“You understood.”

“I understood that the principal had not received the information.”

“Did you know who P-04 was?”

“No.”

“Did you know whether the person at C-19 was P-04?”

“No.”

“Then what did you believe should happen?”

Margaret looked at the control copy.

“The person should have been given the opportunity to understand what was being asked of them.”

Edward remained silent.

Margaret added:

“I did not know whether they would agree, refuse, ask for more information, or choose someone else to represent them. That was precisely why the original procedure mattered.”

Edward closed the folder.

“Who received your day-54 objection?”

Margaret checked the dispatch copy.

“Charles Vale's private office.”

“Did he respond?”

“No.”

“Did anyone?”

She shook her head.

Then she turned to the back of the control copy.

There was a small handwritten note, almost hidden beneath the tracking table.

It had not appeared in the ordinary archive scan.

Edward leaned closer.

The note read:

*The principal's response must not be inferred from the representative's continued availability.*

Beneath it were two initials.

**M.V.**

Margaret recognised them.

“I wrote that.”

Edward looked at her.

“When?”

“Day fifty-four.”

“Why?”

“Because someone had written that the representative's authority remained active until the receiving authority was appointed.”

She paused.

“I wanted it clear that those were separate matters.”

Edward looked at the control copy again.

The original procedure had been prepared.

The control copy had been preserved.

Eleanor had objected to the altered authority.

Gabriel had asked for his own role to be reviewed.

Margaret had challenged the withholding of the principal's acknowledgement.

And yet the same temporary status had continued.

He gathered the documents.

“Mrs Voss, did you ever receive an instruction to destroy or replace this control copy?”

“No.”

“Did anyone ask to collect it?”

Margaret hesitated.

“Yes.”

Edward stopped.

“When?”

“Day one hundred and thirty-three.”

“Who?”

“I don't know. The request came through the archive office.”

“Was it authorised?”

“No. The person requesting it had no recognised custody authority.”

“Was the request signed?”

Margaret opened the old register.

“No.”

Edward looked at the entry.

The request had been refused.

But the final field contained a reference he had not seen before.

**REQUEST TO COLLECT PR-06 CONTROL COPY — SOURCE: V-17 / PERSONAL CUSTODY.**

Edward slowly raised his eyes.

“Who was the intended collector?”

Margaret turned the page.

The name field was blank.

But a handwritten instruction appeared beneath it.

*Do not release until the principal's identity is independently confirmed.*

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The same unknown writer had appeared again.

And this time, the instruction was not about preserving an administrative status.

It was about preventing someone from obtaining the original record that showed the principal had never acknowledged it.
