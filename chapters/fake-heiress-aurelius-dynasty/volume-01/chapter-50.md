# **Chapter 50 — The Child in the Rental Photograph**

Edward enlarged the photograph.

The girl stood beside a window, holding a book against her chest. Her hair fell just below her shoulders, and her clothes were ordinary: a light sweater, dark trousers, and worn shoes.

She appeared to be around eleven years old.

On the reverse, someone had written:

**She has started asking questions. We cannot keep telling her that nobody is looking for her.**

And beneath it:

**Find out whether the mother is still alive before telling the child the truth.**

Edward placed the photograph beside the property inspection report.

"Eleven years," Jonathan said.

"Yes."

"Someone kept this child in that property for eleven years."

"Apparently."

"Why?"

Edward examined the photograph again.

"That's what we need to establish."

Jonathan pointed towards the handwritten instruction.

"Do you think the occupant wrote this?"

"Possibly. The handwriting is different from the property records."

"Could it be the person who used the name Victoria A.?"

"We don't know whether Victoria A. was the occupant for the entire period."

Edward opened the property ownership history.

The property had been purchased through a trust.

The trust's beneficiary was listed as an unidentified child.

The maintenance payments came from Northbridge Family Services.

The final sale had been arranged through Vale International Legal Group.

Everything pointed towards the same network.

But the identity of the child remained uncertain.

Edward picked up his phone.

"Find the inspector who completed this report."

The inspector was named Robert Ellis.

He had retired eight years earlier and now lived in a small town outside the city.

When Edward contacted him, Robert initially remembered very little.

Then Edward sent him the photograph.

The response arrived twenty minutes later.

**I remember the property. I remember the child.**

Edward arranged a meeting.

Robert Ellis arrived at the Valmont legal office the following afternoon.

He was in his late sixties, with thinning grey hair and a careful manner of speaking.

Edward placed the photograph on the table.

"Is this the child you remember?"

Robert adjusted his glasses.

"Yes."

"Do you remember her name?"

"Anna."

"Did she have a surname?"

"Not that I knew."

"Who lived with her?"

"A woman."

"Victoria A.?"

Robert frowned.

"That might have been the name on the inspection file."

"Was she the child's mother?"

"I don't know."

"Did she tell you?"

"No. She said she was responsible for the child."

Edward showed him another photograph.

This one was an image of the woman identified in the Northbridge records as Victoria A. It had been taken from an old rental application.

Robert studied it.

"That's her."

"You're certain?"

"Yes. She was older than the girl by perhaps twenty or twenty-five years."

"Did you speak to her during the inspection?"

"Several times."

"What was she like?"

Robert considered the question.

"Very careful."

"How?"

"She checked every detail. She wanted to know exactly what information would appear in the report. She asked whether the inspection photographs would be stored permanently."

"Why?"

"She said she didn't want the child exposed to strangers."

Edward made a note.

"Did she ever mention the child's family?"

Robert nodded.

"She once told me the girl had relatives who couldn't be allowed to find her."

Edward looked up.

"Did she say why?"

"No."

"Did she seem afraid of those relatives?"

Robert thought about it.

"Not exactly. She seemed afraid of what would happen if they found her."

"Did she ever receive visitors?"

"One man."

"Who?"

"I don't know his name. He came twice."

"Can you describe him?"

"Middle-aged. Dark hair, expensive clothes. He carried a leather briefcase."

Edward showed him a photograph of Charles Vale.

Robert studied it for several seconds.

"That could be him."

"Could you identify him in person?"

"Possibly."

Edward placed another photograph on the table.

It showed Eleanor Vale.

Robert looked at it.

"No. I don't remember her."

Edward returned to the property inspection report.

"Did the woman ever tell you why the child was called Anna?"

Robert smiled faintly.

"She said the child had chosen the name herself."

"Chosen it?"

"She said the girl had started calling herself Anna after finding an old book. Apparently, the character had the same name."

Edward looked at the photograph.

"Did Anna know she was living under a temporary arrangement?"

Robert shook his head.

"She seemed to think it was her home."

"Did she attend school?"

"Yes. The woman arranged everything."

"Which school?"

Robert remembered the name.

Edward wrote it down.

"Did you ever see a birth certificate?"

"No."

"Any identity document?"

"No. I asked once because the property records required information about the occupant."

"What did the woman say?"

"She said the child's documents were being handled privately."

Edward leaned forward.

"Did that concern you?"

Robert looked uncomfortable.

"It did."

"Why?"

"Because the girl was old enough to attend school, and I couldn't understand how she had no ordinary identity documents."

"Did you report it?"

Robert nodded.

"To whom?"

"The property management company. They said the matter had already been reviewed by their legal department."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

Another review.

Another legal department.

Another missing record.

"Do you remember the last time you visited?"

Robert looked at the photograph again.

"Yes. The girl was about eleven. She asked me whether I knew where her mother lived."

Edward stopped writing.

"What did you say?"

"I told her I didn't know."

"What did the woman say?"

"She ended the inspection immediately."

Robert's expression became distant.

"Before I left, she apologised. She said the girl had been asking questions for some time."

Edward looked at the handwritten note.

**She has started asking questions.**

The dates matched.

The child had begun asking about her mother around the time the inspection report was written.

But someone had been preparing an answer.

And that answer depended on whether the mother was still alive.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander was examining the recovered trust records.

Adrian had obtained the original banking instructions.

The account that funded Northbridge Family Services had been established through an Aurelius private banking arrangement.

The account had been opened under a confidential family trust.

Its initial deposit was substantial.

Additional payments had followed over the next two years.

Alexander examined the signature.

"Again, this isn't mine."

Adrian nodded.

"The bank's verification process was completed using an internal authority code."

"Which code?"

"EFO-SL/07."

Alexander looked at him.

"So the same authority was used for the Family Office transfer and the trust funding."

"Yes."

"Can we identify who accessed it?"

"The bank has provided a partial access log. The request came through the former Family Office administrative terminal."

"Who used that terminal?"

"Marcus Morelli and Margaret Voss."

Alexander put the document down.

"Have you spoken to Morelli?"

"Not yet. Edward Valmont is meeting him."

"Then coordinate with Edward. We need the same evidence."

Adrian hesitated.

"Father, there's another matter."

"What?"

"The trust was not closed when the property was sold."

Alexander looked at him.

"Where did the money go?"

"Into a new account."

"Whose?"

"Unknown. The beneficiary designation was changed."

"By whom?"

Adrian showed him the authorisation.

The name was obscured.

Only the initials remained.

**E.V.**

Alexander looked at the page.

"Eleanor Vale?"

"Possibly."

"Do we have her signature?"

"No."

Alexander leaned back.

The investigation was becoming increasingly difficult to separate from the private decisions of people who were no longer alive.

Eleanor Vale had been involved in the hospital records, the temporary placements, and the later custody arrangements.

Charles Vale had authorised payments, received children, and arranged property.

Marcus Morelli had coordinated the releases.

Margaret Voss had handled confidential records.

And somewhere between them, a child had grown up without an official identity.

Alexander looked towards the window.

"Find out who received the final payment."

Adrian nodded.

"And Adrian?"

"Yes?"

"Do not let this become another exercise in protecting the family name."

Adrian understood.

They were not investigating an embarrassing historical mistake.

They were trying to find a person.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was practising for her interview again.

This time, Sebastian had agreed to help.

It was going badly.

"Explain a weakness you have worked to improve," he said.

Elena looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because they might ask."

"That's not the answer."

"Then give me one."

She thought for a moment.

"I sometimes spend too much time trying to solve problems independently."

Sebastian nodded.

"That's a reasonable answer."

"But I don't want to make it sound like I refuse to work with people."

"Then explain that you have learned to recognise when cooperation is more effective."

Elena wrote it down.

"That sounds better."

Sebastian studied her notes.

"You're preparing seriously."

"I don't want to waste the opportunity."

"Good."

He returned the notebook.

Then he added, "Remember that an interview isn't an examination of your entire personality."

Elena smiled.

"That's reassuring."

"They want to understand how you think. You don't need to impress them with complicated answers."

"Thank you."

Sebastian stood.

"Ask yourself whether you'd be happy studying there even if nobody knew your family name."

Elena looked up.

"I would."

"Then that's your answer."

After he left, Elena returned to her notes.

She read the sentence she had written earlier.

*I want to develop the ability to make informed decisions, understand how organisations affect people, and build something that can stand on its own merits.*

For the first time, she felt confident about the interview.

Whatever happened with the family investigation, she had something to look forward to.

Edward and Jonathan visited the former Northbridge office the following morning.

The building had been converted into a small accounting firm.

Most of the original records were gone.

However, the landlord had retained an old storage cabinet that had never been collected.

Inside were several boxes of correspondence.

Edward examined the labels.

One box was marked:

**CASE 07 — FAMILY ACCOMMODATION.**

He opened it.

The first folder contained invoices.

The second contained property inspection reports.

The third was sealed.

A handwritten note on the cover read:

**FOR THE ATTENTION OF CHARLES VALE ONLY.**

Edward opened it carefully.

Inside was a letter.

It was dated the ninety-second day after the births.

*Charles,*

*I have continued the arrangement as instructed. The child is healthy and has begun attending school. She is asking questions about her mother.*

*I cannot continue giving her the same explanation indefinitely.*

*You told me the mother had been informed. I now believe that was not true.*

*If she is not informed soon, I will have to decide what to tell the child myself.*

There was no signature.

Only the initials:

**V.A.**

Edward read it twice.

Jonathan leaned over.

"Victoria A."

"Yes."

"She knew the mother hadn't been informed."

"Or she had reason to doubt the explanation."

Jonathan pointed to the final paragraph.

"She was considering telling the child."

Edward turned the page.

A reply had been attached.

It was written by Charles Vale.

*The arrangement must continue. You have been paid to provide care, not to investigate the child's family.*

*The mother will be approached when it is safe.*

*Until then, you must not disclose the child's circumstances.*

Edward looked at the date.

The reply had been sent three days later.

There was another handwritten line at the bottom.

**If you are unwilling to continue, the child will be transferred to another receiving party.**

Jonathan's expression darkened.

"That's a threat."

"Yes."

"She was being paid to keep quiet."

Edward looked at the payment records.

The money had not merely covered the child's accommodation.

It had also purchased the woman's silence.

He returned to the original letter.

The woman had written that the child was healthy, attending school, and asking about her mother.

She had not sounded like someone who wanted to abandon the child.

She had sounded like someone who was beginning to feel responsible for her.

Edward placed the letter in a protective folder.

"Find out whether she was still living at the property after the ninety-second day."

Jonathan nodded.

"And whether the child was transferred."

Edward looked at the photograph again.

The girl holding the book.

Anna.

For eleven years, someone had watched her grow up without knowing her identity.

That evening, Isabelle found Elena in the garden.

Elena was sitting beneath the large tree near the western pathway, reading an interview preparation guide.

"You're studying outside?"

"It's quieter here."

Isabelle sat beside her.

"Are you nervous?"

"Less than yesterday."

"That's good."

Elena closed the book.

"How was the foundation meeting?"

"Productive. Catherine approved the revised programme."

"Congratulations."

"Thank you."

Isabelle looked across the garden.

"Do you think I'll be able to manage it?"

"Yes."

"Even though I've never run anything like this before?"

"Especially because you've never run anything like this before."

Isabelle looked at her.

Elena shrugged.

"You'll learn."

Isabelle smiled.

"That's a very simple answer."

"Sometimes simple answers are correct."

They sat quietly for a while.

Then Isabelle asked, "Have you thought about what you'll do if you get accepted?"

"Yes."

"Will you go?"

Elena looked towards the house.

"I think so."

"Even with everything happening?"

"Especially with everything happening."

Isabelle nodded.

"I understand."

Elena looked at her.

"What about you? Are you still considering the exchange programme?"

"Yes. But I haven't decided which one."

"Take your time."

"I will."

A breeze moved through the garden.

For a few minutes, neither girl spoke.

They were both beginning to understand that their futures did not have to be decided by the circumstances of their births.

That was something they could choose for themselves.

At 8:45 p.m., Edward received the final occupancy report.

The property had been occupied for eleven years.

The woman using the name Victoria A. had left with the child after the inspection that produced the photograph.

The property had remained empty for several months.

Then a new tenant had moved in.

The new tenant's name was different.

But the property manager had recorded a note.

**Previous occupant instructed that all correspondence concerning Anna must be forwarded through Vale Private Counsel.**

Edward opened the next page.

It contained a forwarding address.

Not a residential property.

A medical institution.

A private children's care centre that had closed seven years earlier.

Edward stared at the name.

**ST. AGNES CHILDREN'S RECOVERY CENTRE.**

The centre had provided temporary medical care and educational support for children without stable guardianship arrangements.

It had operated for nearly fifteen years.

And according to the old records, one child had been admitted there under the name Anna M.

The admission date was eleven years after the births.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"She's moved."

"To the care centre?"

"Apparently."

"Do we know who took her?"

"No."

Edward opened the admission summary.

The child's age was recorded as eleven.

The medical history was incomplete.

The guardian's name had been left blank.

But the final section contained a note.

**Child has requested access to original birth records. Request denied pending legal review.**

Edward's expression changed.

"She knew."

Jonathan leaned forward.

"She knew what?"

"That the records were being withheld."

He turned the page.

A handwritten note had been added several days later.

**The child has repeated her request. She states that she has a right to know who her mother is.**

Edward closed the file.

For the first time, the investigation had found evidence that the child herself had challenged the secrecy surrounding her identity.

She had asked for her birth records.

Someone had refused.

And then, somewhere along the way, she had disappeared from the institution's records too.

At 11:30 p.m., a message arrived from the archive.

The St. Agnes admission register had been recovered.

Edward opened the scanned page.

There were several ordinary entries.

Names.

Dates.

Medical notes.

Guardian information.

Then he found the relevant line.

**ANNA M. — FEMALE — AGE 11 — TEMPORARY ADMISSION.**

The guardian field was blank.

The emergency contact field contained a number.

Edward enlarged it.

The number belonged to a legal office that had closed seventeen years earlier.

Vale Private Counsel.

But beneath the contact information, someone had written a different name.

Not Margaret Voss.

Not Margaret Ellison.

Not Victoria A.

A name that had never appeared in any previous record.

**MARGARET VICTORIA.**

Edward stared at it.

The handwriting was neat.

The entry had been made in blue ink, unlike the rest of the register.

He checked the date.

It had been entered three days after Anna's admission.

There was another note beneath it.

**Do not contact the family. The child has not been informed.**

Edward slowly closed the file.

The child had been admitted under one name.

Her emergency contact had been entered under another.

And someone had specifically instructed the institution not to contact either family.

He looked at the photograph once more.

The girl holding the book.

Anna.

Whoever she had been, someone had spent years keeping her away from the people who might have known where she belonged.

And now Edward had a new name to investigate.

**Margaret Victoria.**
