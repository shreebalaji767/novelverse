# **Chapter 17 — The File That Contained Two Names**

Martin Hale did not sleep that night.

He sat in the small living room of his house, the lights switched off, watching the clock on the opposite wall.

The sealed folder remained inside his storage unit.

He had not taken it home.

He had not dared.

For eighteen years, he had convinced himself that keeping the documents was safer than destroying them. He had told himself that the situation would eventually resolve itself, that the people involved had made their decisions, and that his own responsibility had ended when he left the Aurelius Family Office.

Those explanations had become increasingly difficult to believe.

Especially now.

The Aurelius family had found the archive.

The Valmont family had discovered the missing package.

And Edward Valmont was asking questions about documents that Martin had once believed would never be seen again.

He picked up his phone.

The call he had made the previous evening was still bothering him.

He had expected the person on the other end to offer reassurance.

Instead, he had received a warning.

*Do not speak to the Aurelius family.*

Martin had spent eighteen years following instructions.

He was beginning to wonder whether that had been his greatest mistake.

At the Valmont Estate, Nicholas was conducting an investigation of his own.

The subject was the disappearance of the last chocolate biscuit.

His first suspect was Elena.

His second suspect was Isabelle.

His third suspect was the household staff.

He had ruled out Edward because Edward had no appreciation for biscuits.

Sebastian had been excluded because he claimed not to eat sweets, although Nicholas considered that claim suspicious.

He finally found Elena in the sitting room.

"Where is it?"

She looked up from her book.

"Where is what?"

"My chocolate biscuit."

"I don't know."

"You were here."

"Many people were here."

"You were the last person near the table."

Elena turned a page.

"Perhaps the biscuit decided to leave."

Nicholas stared at her.

"You're not taking this seriously."

"I'm deeply concerned."

"Good. Then tell me where it is."

She looked at him.

"Have you checked your pocket?"

Nicholas frowned.

Then he reached into his jacket.

His fingers found a small packet.

He stared at it.

Elena smiled.

"Case closed."

Nicholas slowly withdrew the packet.

"I knew that."

"Of course."

"I was testing you."

"Naturally."

He sat beside her and opened the packet.

"Do you want one?"

Elena looked at him.

"Those are your biscuits."

"Yes."

"And you were accusing me of stealing them."

"That was before I found them."

She took one.

"Thank you."

Nicholas shook his head.

"This family is impossible."

Elena smiled and returned to her book.

For a few minutes, the world was pleasantly ordinary.

Then Nicholas's phone rang.

It was Edward.

"Where are you?"

"At home."

"Can you come to the study?"

"Why?"

"We have an update."

Nicholas glanced at Elena.

"Is it about the investigation?"

"Yes."

"I'll be there."

He ended the call.

Elena closed her book.

"What's happened?"

"I don't know yet."

"Should I come?"

Nicholas hesitated.

"Edward didn't ask for you."

"Then I'll stay here."

Nicholas stood.

"Don't worry. I'll tell you what I can."

Elena nodded.

She watched him leave.

Then she looked down at the book in her lap.

She had become very good at pretending that waiting did not bother her.

She was not sure whether that was a useful skill.

Edward's study contained more people than Nicholas expected.

Richard was standing near the window. Sebastian sat at the desk. Daniel, the investigator, had a folder open in front of him.

Edward looked up when Nicholas entered.

"Where's Elena?"

"Upstairs."

"Good. This is still preliminary."

Nicholas sat down.

"What's happened?"

Edward turned the folder around.

"We've located Martin Hale."

Richard nodded.

"The former Aurelius family office administrator?"

"Yes. He's connected to both the original report and the package that was delivered to our residence."

Sebastian leaned forward.

"Has he agreed to speak?"

"Not yet."

Daniel placed a photograph on the desk.

It showed a storage facility with several rows of private units.

"Hale has an old storage unit registered under his former company account. He requested access to it yesterday."

Edward pointed to the photograph.

"The unit contains archived office material. We believe the original maternity identity report may be there."

Nicholas looked at the image.

"Then why haven't we opened it?"

"Because we need the proper legal authority."

Nicholas leaned back.

"Of course."

Edward continued.

"The Aurelius family has been informed. Adrian is arranging a preservation request through their legal team."

Richard looked at the photograph.

"How long before the unit can be secured?"

"Hopefully today."

Sebastian folded his arms.

"What if Hale removes the documents first?"

"That's a possibility. We're monitoring the situation within the limits of the law."

Nicholas frowned.

"He's had eighteen years to explain himself. Why is he suddenly trying to access the storage unit now?"

Edward looked at him.

"Because he knows we're investigating."

"Then he knows we're close."

"Perhaps."

Nicholas looked at Richard.

"Does Elena know?"

Richard shook his head.

"Not yet."

"She should."

"She will be told when we have something reliable."

Nicholas did not argue.

But he did not look satisfied either.

At Aurelius Global Headquarters, Adrian received confirmation that the storage unit had been secured.

The request had been processed through the appropriate legal channels. The facility had been instructed to preserve the contents while the parties established the proper procedure for examination.

Damien stood beside him.

"Can we access it today?"

"The preservation order is in place. The inspection arrangements are being finalised."

"Then we should go."

Adrian nodded.

He called his father.

Alexander answered immediately.

"Is it secured?"

"Yes."

"Good."

"We're arranging a joint inspection with the relevant legal representatives."

"Will Hale be present?"

"His lawyer has been contacted. He has not confirmed whether he will attend."

Alexander looked toward the photograph of Victoria holding their newborn daughter.

"Make sure the inspection is documented. Nothing is to be removed without a record."

"Understood."

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"If the report is there, do not assume it will answer everything."

Adrian paused.

"Why do you say that?"

"Because every document we've found so far has raised another question."

Alexander ended the call.

Adrian looked at Damien.

"He's right."

Damien nodded.

"Then let's hope this one at least tells us who made the decisions."

The inspection took place that afternoon.

Martin Hale arrived with a lawyer.

He looked older than the photograph Adrian had found in the personnel records. His hair had turned grey, and his face appeared thinner.

He avoided looking directly at the Aurelius representatives.

Adrian approached him.

"Mr. Hale."

"Mr. Aurelius."

"Thank you for coming."

"I was advised to attend."

"Then perhaps we can begin."

Hale glanced toward the storage unit.

"I don't know what you expect to find."

Adrian did not respond.

The unit was opened in the presence of the legal representatives and the facility manager.

Inside were rows of boxes, old filing cabinets and several sealed containers.

The facility manager checked the inventory.

Everything was photographed before being moved.

After nearly forty minutes, one of the legal representatives found a brown folder at the bottom of a metal cabinet.

It was sealed with an old adhesive strip.

Adrian recognised the label immediately.

**AURELIUS — MATERNITY IDENTITY REPORT**

Damien looked at Hale.

"Is this yours?"

Hale said nothing.

Adrian turned toward the lawyer.

"Please record the discovery."

The folder was photographed.

The seal was examined.

Then the legal representatives opened it.

Inside were several documents.

The first was a hospital report.

The second was a list of registration numbers.

The third was a handwritten instruction.

And the fourth was a page containing two names.

Adrian stared at it.

The first name was:

**ELENA.**

The second was:

**ISABELLE.**

He read the page again.

The names were followed by their respective birth registration references.

One belonged to the original V. Aurelius record.

The other belonged to the original C. Valmont record.

A note had been written beneath them.

**The children must not be identified through the revised records until the original transfer is confirmed.**

Damien looked over Adrian's shoulder.

"Both girls?"

Adrian nodded slowly.

The report did not merely concern the Aurelius child.

It contained information about both newborns.

Adrian turned toward Martin Hale.

"Why were both names in this file?"

Hale looked at the document.

For a long moment, he said nothing.

Then he answered.

"Because there were two children."

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was sitting with Isabelle in the garden.

They had taken their books outside because the weather was pleasant.

Isabelle was reading a university brochure.

Elena was attempting to understand a complicated section of her economics textbook.

She looked up.

"Do you understand this?"

Isabelle glanced at the page.

"Which part?"

"This paragraph."

She read it.

"Yes."

Elena pushed the book toward her.

"Explain it."

Isabelle smiled.

"You're asking me for help?"

"Yes."

"That's unusual."

"Don't become arrogant."

Isabelle laughed and began explaining the concept.

After a few minutes, Elena nodded.

"That makes more sense."

"Good."

They returned to their work.

Then Isabelle spoke.

"Do you think we'll still study together after the results?"

Elena looked at her.

"Why wouldn't we?"

"Things might change."

"Perhaps."

Isabelle looked down.

"I don't know what I would do if you weren't here."

Elena was quiet for a moment.

"Neither do I."

Isabelle looked surprised.

Elena closed her textbook.

"I've lived here my entire life. You're still getting used to the family. But I think we'd both find it strange if we suddenly stopped talking."

Isabelle smiled faintly.

"That's true."

Elena picked up her pen.

"Besides, I still need someone to explain economics to me."

"Then I suppose you're stuck with me."

"Unfortunately."

They laughed.

Neither girl knew that, at that very moment, two men were looking at a document containing both their names.

Back at the storage facility, Adrian continued examining the report.

The first page contained a summary of the hospital's original registration records.

It listed two births.

The first:

**Female child — 2:17 a.m. — Mother: V. Aurelius.**

The second:

**Female child — 5:46 a.m. — Mother: C. Valmont.**

The transfer details were marked as incomplete.

A later page contained a comparison of the hospital's revised registration records.

The names and identification numbers did not match.

Adrian looked at the original references.

"These numbers are the same as the ones on the identification cards."

The legal representative nodded.

"Yes."

"Then why were the revised numbers different?"

Hale remained silent.

Damien moved closer.

"Mr. Hale, you need to explain what you know."

Hale looked at him.

"I was not involved in the original hospital arrangements."

"Then what were you involved in?"

"I received documents."

"Which documents?"

"The report."

"Who gave it to you?"

Hale looked toward Adrian.

"Charles Vale."

Adrian's expression changed.

"Charles Vale personally?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"Six days after the births."

"Why?"

"To keep the report separate from the family office's regular records."

"Did he explain why?"

"No."

"Did he tell you not to give it to my father?"

Hale looked down.

Adrian's voice became colder.

"Mr. Hale, we have a document specifically instructing someone not to allow Alexander Aurelius to receive the original report."

Hale closed his eyes.

"That instruction came from Eleanor."

"Eleanor Vale?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Hale did not answer.

Adrian placed the handwritten note on the table.

"Did she know where the children were?"

Hale stared at the page.

"I don't know."

"Did she know what happened to them?"

"I don't know."

"Then tell us what you do know."

Hale looked at the two names.

"She told me that the hospital had made a serious mistake."

Adrian leaned forward.

"What kind of mistake?"

Hale's voice dropped.

"She said that the wrong child had been delivered to the wrong family."

The room became completely silent.

Damien was the first to speak.

"Did she tell you which child belonged to which family?"

Hale shook his head.

"Not initially."

"Then when?"

"Several days later."

Adrian's expression hardened.

"What did she tell you?"

Hale looked at him.

"She told me that the Aurelius child had not been delivered to the Aurelius family."

Adrian's hands tightened.

"And the Valmont child?"

Hale looked away.

"She said the same thing."

Damien glanced at Adrian.

"So both children were switched."

Hale nodded.

"That's what she told me."

Adrian took a breath.

"Did you know where the girls were?"

"No."

"Did you try to find out?"

Hale hesitated.

"Yes."

"Then what happened?"

"I was told not to interfere."

"By whom?"

Hale looked at the documents.

"Charles Vale."

Adrian stared at him.

"Why would a lawyer tell you not to interfere with two newborn children who had been sent to the wrong families?"

Hale gave a tired, bitter smile.

"Because he said it was no longer a hospital matter."

Adrian's expression changed.

"What did he mean?"

Hale did not answer.

He reached into the folder and removed a folded sheet.

"This was attached to the original report."

The legal representative immediately documented it.

Adrian opened the sheet.

It contained a short statement.

**The transfer cannot be reversed until the identity of the person who arranged it has been established. Any premature attempt to retrieve either child may expose both families to further harm.**

At the bottom were two initials.

**C.V.**

Adrian stared at them.

Charles Vale.

The man who had handled the original legal file.

The man who had kept the report away from Alexander.

The man who had instructed Martin Hale not to interfere.

But the statement did not say that Charles had arranged the transfer.

It said the person responsible had not yet been identified.

Adrian looked at Hale.

"Did Charles tell you who had arranged it?"

Hale shook his head.

"No."

"Did you ever ask?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

Hale looked at the floor.

"He said that if we identified the person too early, we might lose both children."

That evening, Alexander and Victoria received the report.

They read it together in the family study.

Victoria's hands trembled slightly as she turned the pages.

"Both girls," she whispered.

Alexander nodded.

"Yes."

"They knew there were two."

"Apparently."

Victoria looked at the birth times.

"2:17 a.m."

Her finger rested on the original registration number.

"That was our daughter."

Alexander looked at her.

"It was the child registered under your name."

Victoria closed her eyes.

"Why did they not bring her to us?"

Alexander had no answer.

He turned to the next page.

The report contained a preliminary conclusion, written by Charles Vale.

**The hospital's records are unreliable. Neither child's current family placement can be accepted as proof of biological identity. Independent verification is required.**

Alexander read it carefully.

"They knew the records could not be trusted."

Victoria looked at him.

"Then why did they leave the girls where they were?"

"We don't know."

"Why did no one tell us?"

Alexander remained silent.

Victoria placed the report on the table.

"Did you know any of this?"

"No."

"Did you authorise Charles to handle the matter?"

"I authorised the family office to obtain legal assistance. I did not authorise anyone to conceal my daughter's identity."

Victoria looked at him.

She wanted to believe him.

But the documents were becoming more complicated by the day.

Alexander took her hand.

"I will find out what happened."

Victoria nodded.

Then she looked at the final page.

There was a section titled **Current Placement**.

Both children's names appeared.

But the entries had been covered by a strip of paper.

Only the first few letters of each placement reference remained visible.

The first began with:

**VAL—**

The second:

**AUR—**

Victoria stared at the page.

"Alexander."

He looked down.

The report suggested that someone had recorded the children's current placements.

But the names had been deliberately obscured.

And the person who had hidden them had not merely concealed the truth from the families.

They had concealed it from the very people entrusted with investigating it.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was preparing for bed when Nicholas knocked.

"Come in."

He entered carrying two cups of hot chocolate.

Elena looked at them.

"What's the occasion?"

"Nothing."

"Then why are you carrying two cups?"

"Because I was thirsty twice."

She took one.

"Thank you."

Nicholas sat on the chair near her desk.

"Edward told me there was another development."

Elena looked at him.

"Good or bad?"

"Complicated."

"That's becoming everyone's favourite word."

Nicholas smiled.

"They found a file. It contains both your names and Isabelle's."

Elena became still.

"What does it say?"

"That there were two children and that the hospital records were unreliable."

She looked down at her cup.

"So they still don't know?"

"Not everything."

Elena nodded slowly.

Nicholas leaned forward.

"Listen. Whatever happens, don't start thinking you have to solve everything tonight."

"I'm not."

"You were staring at the same spot for almost a minute."

"That's because you were speaking."

He smiled.

"There's my sister."

Elena gave him a small smile.

Then she asked:

"Do you think they knew?"

Nicholas's expression softened.

"Who?"

"The people who were involved. Did they know where we were?"

He did not answer immediately.

"I don't know."

Elena looked toward the window.

"Someone must have known."

"Perhaps."

"And if they knew, why didn't they tell anyone?"

Nicholas put his cup down.

"I don't have an answer."

She nodded.

For once, she did not try to make the conversation lighter.

Nicholas stood after a while.

"Get some sleep."

"I will."

He reached the door.

"Elena?"

"Yes?"

"You're still my little sister."

She smiled.

"I know."

He left.

Elena sat quietly for several minutes.

Then she opened her university notebook.

Tomorrow, she had a lecture.

The day after that, an assignment was due.

And somewhere in a file cabinet, there were documents that seemed to know more about her life than she did.

She began writing.

At 12:08 a.m., Adrian received one final message from the archive examiner.

The obscured placement references had been examined under magnification.

The paper strip had been placed over the original writing, not added as part of the document's initial printing.

The examiner had recovered a partial impression from the sheet beneath it.

Adrian opened the attached image.

The first name was now almost completely visible.

**VALMONT — ELENA.**

The second name was:

**AURELIUS — ISABELLE.**

Adrian stared at the screen.

Then he read the note beneath the two entries.

**Provisional placement only. Do not disclose until the transfer authority is identified.**

He immediately called Edward.

"Edward, we need to speak."

"What happened?"

"The original report contains a placement record."

Edward sat up.

"What does it say?"

Adrian paused.

"The document identifies Elena as the child provisionally placed with the Valmont family."

Edward said nothing.

"And Isabelle as the child provisionally placed with the Aurelius family."

The silence stretched.

Then Edward spoke.

"Are you certain?"

"The examiner has recovered the original writing."

Edward looked toward the closed door of his study.

The two girls had grown up in the opposite families.

Elena, who had been raised by Richard and Catherine, was apparently the child originally registered under the Aurelius name.

Isabelle, who had recently arrived at the Valmont Estate, was apparently the child originally registered under the Valmont name.

But the word *provisional* remained.

And the report itself warned that neither placement could be accepted as proof of biological identity.

Edward understood why.

The document was not a DNA report.

It was a record of what someone believed had happened eighteen years earlier.

"Adrian," he said quietly, "we still need the genetic results."

"I know."

"And we need to find out who made the placements."

"I know."

Edward looked at the photograph of the hospital corridor.

The man in the dark suit.

The altered records.

The missing report.

The two names.

The investigation was finally beginning to reveal the shape of what had happened.

But the most important question remained unanswered.

**Who had decided that Elena should grow up as a Valmont, and Isabelle as an Aurelius—and why?**
