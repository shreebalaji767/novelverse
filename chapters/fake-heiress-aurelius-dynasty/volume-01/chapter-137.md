# **Chapter 137 — The Request That Kept His Answer Alive**

Gabriel Vale had not expected Edward to contact him again so soon.

He nevertheless agreed to meet.

The appointment was arranged at a private conference room belonging to Vale Private Counsel's successor firm. The firm had inherited several of the old legal archives, although it no longer administered the family arrangements that had occupied so much of the previous eighteen years.

Gabriel arrived carrying a thin leather folder.

Edward noticed that he did not bring a solicitor.

“You asked about the day-ninety-two continuation,” Gabriel said.

“I asked who requested your acknowledgement.”

Gabriel sat down.

“I can tell you what I remember.”

“That's a start.”

Edward placed three documents on the table.

The first was the day-47 temporary communication exception.

The second was the day-92 continuation.

The third was Gabriel's acknowledgement, with his initials at the bottom.

Gabriel studied them.

“I remember receiving this.”

“Why?”

“Because I had already submitted my conditional response. I was concerned that someone might interpret it as something more than it was.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Did you request that your response remain attached to the file?”

Gabriel looked at the acknowledgement.

“Yes.”

“Who asked you to do that?”

“No one.”

“Then why did you?”

“Because the document referred to my response as the current administrative position. I wanted the original wording preserved alongside it.”

Edward remained silent.

Gabriel opened his folder and removed a personal copy of the conditional response.

It was the same document Anna Mercer-Smith had helped prepare.

The same statement that had been signed by Gabriel under his limited communication authority.

*I acknowledge that information concerning a possible receiving arrangement has been presented for consideration. I am willing to receive further information once originating identity and authority are independently established. I do not accept permanent responsibility for an unidentified child. This response must not be recorded as consent to appointment or placement.*

Edward compared the copy with the archived version.

“Did you receive the original from the proposed recipient?”

“No.”

“Then how did you sign it?”

“Margaret Voss gave me the draft. She explained that it reflected the position communicated through the restricted correspondence process.”

“Did you personally speak with P-04?”

“No.”

“Did you see evidence that P-04 had received the information?”

Gabriel hesitated.

“No.”

“Did anyone tell you the protected principal had reviewed the proposed arrangement?”

“I was told that communication had been attempted.”

“That wasn't my question.”

Gabriel looked down.

“No. I wasn't told that the principal had reviewed it.”

Edward let the silence continue.

“Then why did you sign?”

“Because I was authorised to return a conditional response on behalf of the proposed representative.”

“On behalf of whom?”

“The protected principal's appointed communication representative.”

“Who had appointed you?”

Gabriel opened another page.

“The authority certificate was verified by Eleanor Vale.”

“Was P-04's identity independently established?”

“The certificate stated that the principal had been verified through a protected process.”

“Did you inspect the underlying verification?”

“No.”

“Did you know the principal personally?”

“No.”

Edward placed the certificate beside the response.

“So you knew the identity was protected, but you did not independently establish it.”

“Correct.”

“And you knew your authority was limited.”

“Yes.”

“Then why did your response become the current administrative position?”

Gabriel's expression hardened.

“That is precisely why I kept asking for the original to remain attached.”

He described the day-92 meeting.

It had not been a formal hearing.

He had been invited to the Vale Private Counsel office and given a copy of the continuation document. Charles was present, along with a records officer whose name Gabriel could not immediately recall.

The officer explained that the temporary communication status was being continued because direct contact with P-04 remained incomplete.

Gabriel had objected to the phrase *current administrative position*.

He had asked whether the wording would make his conditional response appear to represent the principal's own decision.

Charles told him it would not.

The document, Charles explained, was intended to preserve temporary care and administrative continuity.

Gabriel had asked for that assurance to be recorded.

The records officer made a note.

Then Gabriel requested that his original conditional response remain attached to the continuation.

He had signed the acknowledgement only after the officer added the instruction.

Edward studied him.

“Did you ask for the missing personal acknowledgement to be pursued?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“During that meeting.”

“What did Charles say?”

“That the verification process remained suspended.”

“Did you ask why?”

Gabriel's answer came slowly.

“He said the originating records were still under review and the receiving authority had not been appointed.”

Edward looked at him.

“That was not a reason to suspend contact with P-04.”

“No.”

“Did you say that?”

“I did.”

“And?”

“Charles said another communication attempt would create confusion while the receiving arrangements remained unresolved.”

Edward's expression did not change.

“Did you agree?”

“No.”

“Did you withdraw your authority?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

Gabriel looked at the copy of his response.

“Because I believed it was still possible to correct the process. I thought the missing verification would eventually be completed.”

Edward picked up the day-92 continuation.

“You knew your response was being used to keep the arrangement active.”

“I knew it was being retained.”

“Those are not the same thing.”

“I understand that now.”

“Did you understand it then?”

Gabriel did not answer immediately.

“I understood that the document could be misinterpreted. I did not understand how widely it would be used.”

Edward returned the response to the table.

“Who was the proposed representative?”

Gabriel shook his head.

“I was never given a confirmed name.”

“Did you know Susan Bell had been proposed?”

“No.”

“Did you know she had refused to acknowledge the allocation?”

“I knew someone had declined to accept the proposed arrangement. I did not know who.”

“Did you know the day-40 conditional response had been delivered through Cross Administrative Services?”

“I knew a response had been returned.”

“Did you know it was signed only with an initial?”

Gabriel examined the document again.

“No.”

Edward closed his folder.

“You have described your response as conditional. But your signature helped keep an administrative process moving for months.”

Gabriel looked up.

“I won't deny that.”

“Did you ever ask for a review of your own authority?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Day ninety-three.”

Edward's attention sharpened.

“Where is that request?”

“I don't know. I sent it to Charles's office.”

“Do you have a copy?”

Gabriel opened his leather folder again.

This time, he removed a folded letter.

“I kept the draft. I don't know whether it was formally received.”

Edward unfolded it.

The first sentence was direct.

*I request confirmation that my limited communication authority remains necessary and that my conditional response is not being treated as a substitute for the protected principal's personal acknowledgement.*

The final paragraph was more important.

*If the principal remains unable to receive the original correspondence, I request that the reason for non-delivery be reviewed independently. I do not consider my authority sufficient to resolve that matter.*

Edward checked the date.

Day ninety-three.

“Did you receive an answer?”

“No.”

“Did you send a second request?”

“Yes.”

“Day one hundred and six.”

Gabriel produced another copy.

This one contained a handwritten notation at the top.

**RETAIN WITH PR-06 — NO CHANGE TO CURRENT STATUS.**

No signature.

No explanation.

Edward looked at Gabriel.

“Who wrote this?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you accept it as an answer?”

“No.”

“Then why did you continue?”

Gabriel folded his hands.

“Because no one told me my authority had ended. And no one gave me a way to contact the principal directly.”

Edward studied him for a moment.

“You were allowed to communicate with P-04.”

“Under a verified process.”

“Which never happened.”

“Correct.”

“And you continued to be listed as the authorised representative.”

“Yes.”

“Without having successfully communicated with the person you represented.”

Gabriel's eyes lowered.

“Yes.”

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria read the new findings twice.

Alexander stood near the window, listening as Edward explained the meeting.

“So Gabriel knew his response was conditional,” Alexander said.

“Yes.”

“And he knew the principal had not personally acknowledged it.”

“He says he did.”

Victoria placed the papers on the table.

“Then why did no one simply stop using his response?”

“Because the temporary exception permitted it to remain the current administrative position.”

“But the exception did not appoint him as the principal's decision-maker.”

“No.”

“And it did not establish that the principal had refused further contact.”

“No.”

Victoria looked at the day-92 continuation.

“Then the process had become self-sustaining.”

Edward nodded.

“Every office could point to the previous office. The trust preserved temporary care. The allocation office displayed the current status. The correspondence division retained the conditional response. And the personal instruction remained sealed because the receiving authority was never appointed.”

Alexander's expression grew colder.

“Someone created a system in which the absence of a decision could be treated as sufficient reason to continue the existing arrangement.”

“Without formally declaring that the decision had been made,” Edward replied.

Victoria looked toward the window.

“That is worse than a simple false record.”

Alexander turned.

“Why?”

“Because a false record can be challenged by proving it false. But a series of technically qualified records can make an unauthorised arrangement look ordinary.”

Edward did not disagree.

He placed Gabriel's day-93 request on the table.

“This is important. Gabriel asked for an independent review of his authority. We need to find out what happened to it.”

Alexander read the final paragraph.

“He explicitly said he could not resolve the matter himself.”

“Yes.”

Victoria looked at Edward.

“Did he know who P-04 was?”

“No. Not from anything he has shown us.”

“Did he know whether P-04 was connected to our family?”

“No.”

She nodded slowly.

“Then we must not make that assumption.”

Alexander looked at the records.

“We have spent eighteen years trying to find our daughter. I will not allow the investigation to turn an administrative reference into proof of her identity.”

Edward closed the folder.

“Neither will I.”

The day-93 request had been entered into the correspondence index.

It had not been filed under Gabriel's name.

Instead, it appeared beneath a general reference:

**PR-06 / REPRESENTATIVE AUTHORITY REVIEW**

Jonathan found the index entry in a restricted records directory.

The original document was not present.

Only a scanned receipt and a routing instruction remained.

The receipt confirmed that the request had reached Vale Private Counsel on day ninety-four.

The routing instruction directed it to three departments:

- Personal Instructions Division.
- Originating Records Office.
- Administrative Continuity Review.

There was no entry showing that the request had been forwarded to P-04.

There was no response from the protected principal.

There was, however, a decision.

Day ninety-six.

**REPRESENTATIVE AUTHORITY TO REMAIN ACTIVE FOR TEMPORARY CONTINUITY.**

The authorisation field contained a reference to the day-47 exception.

No fresh appointment had been made.

No new consent had been obtained.

And no independent review had been completed.

Edward read the record.

“Who approved it?”

Jonathan enlarged the authorisation field.

“That's the problem.”

The entry cited:

**PI-07 / ORIGINATING OFFICE DIRECTION.**

“Who held that authority?”

“Charles Vale could issue administrative instructions under the originating office arrangement. But he couldn't appoint the receiving authority or determine the protected principal's personal wishes.”

“Was there a separate approval?”

“No.”

Edward studied the routing record.

“Then this was another administrative continuation.”

“That's what it looks like.”

Jonathan opened the audit history.

The first version of the day-96 entry had a different status.

**REPRESENTATIVE AUTHORITY — REVIEW REQUIRED.**

It had been changed three hours later.

The revised version read:

**REPRESENTATIVE AUTHORITY — ACTIVE FOR TEMPORARY CONTINUITY.**

The change was made through the shared account FA-ADMIN/REVIEW DESK.

Operator identity was blank.

The reason field contained only:

*Existing exception applies.*

Edward leaned back.

“Find the person who submitted the change.”

Jonathan began checking the associated workflow.

The original request had been prepared by the Personal Instructions Division.

It included a note from Charles:

*The representative's conditional response remains the only available communication position. No alteration to the underlying identity or placement status is authorised.*

Edward read it carefully.

Charles had repeated the limitation.

But the status change still allowed Gabriel's authority to remain active.

“Who received the revised record?”

Jonathan checked the distribution list.

“Northbridge Trust, Family Allocation Review, R-04, V-17, and the external liaison office.”

“Gabriel?”

“No.”

Edward looked up.

“Was he told that his authority had been continued?”

Jonathan shook his head.

“There's no acknowledgement from him.”

The next morning, Eleanor confirmed that she had received the day-96 record.

She had not approved it.

In fact, she had written a separate objection.

Edward found the original in her private correspondence file.

It was dated day ninety-seven.

*The continued administrative status of the communication representative must not be interpreted as renewal of personal authority by the protected principal.*

A second paragraph followed.

*The representative's authority was granted for the limited purpose of communication. Where communication has not been completed, the authority cannot be treated as renewed through administrative continuation alone.*

Edward read the final line.

*The principal must remain free to receive information, respond personally, request another representative, or decline further contact after receiving the information required to make that decision.*

He looked at Eleanor.

“You understood the problem.”

“Yes.”

“Did you send this to Charles?”

“I sent it to the Personal Instructions Division and copied the originating records office.”

“Did you receive a response?”

“No.”

“Did you request that Gabriel's authority be suspended?”

“I requested that it not be treated as renewed until the principal's position was established.”

“That is not quite the same thing.”

“No,” Eleanor said. “I did not want the principal to lose the ability to communicate merely because the administrative office had failed to complete the process.”

Edward considered that.

It was a subtle but important distinction.

If Gabriel's authority were simply cancelled, P-04 might be left without an available communication representative.

If it remained active without review, however, the system could continue relying on a representative whose authority had never been exercised through successful contact.

“What did Charles say?”

Eleanor looked at the letter.

“He said the matter was being handled.”

“By whom?”

“He did not specify.”

Edward glanced at the date.

“Did you ever learn whether Gabriel received your objection?”

“No.”

“Did you send him a copy?”

Eleanor shook her head.

“I was not authorised to disclose the protected principal's correspondence arrangements directly to him without the proper routing approval.”

Edward stopped.

“Then the representative may not have known that you were challenging the continuation.”

“Correct.”

“And you may not have known that he was asking for his authority to be reviewed.”

Eleanor's expression changed.

For a moment, she looked less like a records officer and more like someone who had discovered that two people had been asking the same unanswered question from opposite sides of a sealed process.

“We were both waiting for the same office,” she said quietly.

Edward looked at the two documents.

Gabriel's request on day ninety-three.

Eleanor's objection on day ninety-seven.

Both had reached the same organisation.

Neither had received a meaningful answer.

In Singapore, Elena received an update from Professor Lin.

Her prototype had passed the initial technical review.

The next stage would involve a small group of students testing the platform using fictional grant applications.

Professor Lin's message contained one specific suggestion.

*Your system handles incomplete records well. I would now like you to test what happens when two authorised users enter conflicting interpretations of the same event.*

Elena read it carefully.

She opened her project notes.

That was a difficult problem.

Two users might have access to the same file but different responsibilities. One might verify documents. Another might approve eligibility. A third might communicate the final decision.

If one person entered an interpretation outside their authority, the system should not automatically treat it as a valid decision.

She added a new feature request.

**ROLE-BASED DECISION VALIDATION**

Then she wrote beneath it:

*An authorised user can only make decisions within the scope of their assigned role.*

She stared at the sentence.

It seemed obvious.

But making it work would require more than assigning names to different permissions.

The platform would need to preserve the actual source of each decision, the scope of the user's authority, and whether the necessary conditions had been met.

Elena began sketching the process.

A notification appeared on her phone.

Isabelle had sent another message.

*I spoke with the foundation's application team. They would like to see the prototype when you're ready. No pressure. They can wait until you've completed your testing.*

Elena replied:

*Thank you. I'll let you know when it's ready.*

A moment later, Isabelle answered.

*And congratulations again. I know how much work you've put into it.*

Elena placed the phone aside.

She returned to her screen.

For once, the work in front of her had a clear beginning and an achievable next step.

She did not need to know how it would eventually grow.

She only needed to make the next part work properly.

At the Vale archive, Jonathan found the missing day-96 attachment.

It had not been stored with the administrative continuation.

Instead, it was filed under a different reference:

**PR-06 / AUTHORITY SOURCE RECORD**

The attachment was a copy of the original communication authority certificate.

Edward recognised it immediately.

It was the certificate Eleanor had verified.

But this version contained an additional page.

The first page described Gabriel's limited authority.

The second page contained a section titled:

**AUTHORITY DURATION AND REVIEW**

The original certificate had stated that Gabriel's authority remained valid until the protected principal personally acknowledged, amended, or withdrew it, subject to independent review.

The additional page introduced a different condition.

*Where personal acknowledgement cannot be obtained, the representative's authority may remain administratively active until the receiving identity process is completed.*

Edward read it again.

“Who added this?”

Jonathan examined the page.

“It carries the same document reference as the original certificate.”

“Is the signature genuine?”

“The verification stamp is genuine. The page itself appears to have been inserted later.”

Edward looked at him.

“Later than the original certificate?”

“Yes. The paper stock is different. So is the printing format.”

“Who authorised the amendment?”

Jonathan turned the page over.

There was a small reference in the lower margin.

**DAY 34 — AV-18 / DURATION CLARIFICATION**

Edward felt the significance immediately.

The day-34 date placed the amendment before Gabriel's conditional response, before the failed delivery to C-19, and before the day-47 exception.

Someone had altered the apparent duration of Gabriel's authority before P-04 had received the original communication.

He examined the final sentence.

*This provision is administrative only and does not confer authority to accept permanent placement, waive personal acknowledgement, or determine originating or receiving identity.*

The limitations were still there.

But the amendment had created a way for Gabriel's authority to remain active even when the principal could not be contacted.

Edward placed the page beside the original certificate.

“Find the source file for AV-18.”

Jonathan was already searching.

A moment later, he stopped.

“Edward.”

“What?”

“The amendment was not issued by the Personal Instructions Division.”

“Where did it originate?”

Jonathan turned the screen around.

The routing field showed:

**ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE — VICTORIA AURELIUS**

Edward stared at it.

Victoria had never appointed Gabriel.

She had never approved the receiving arrangement.

And her signature on the day-46 notification had acknowledged receipt only.

Yet the new page appeared to have entered the authority file through a route bearing her designation.

“Does it carry her signature?” Edward asked.

“No. It cites her office as the originating authority.”

“Who submitted it?”

Jonathan opened the final audit entry.

The submission had been received on day thirty-four.

The sender field was blank.

But the receiving clerk had recorded one handwritten instruction:

*Do not return this amendment to the originating representative. It has already been approved for administrative continuity.*

Edward read the sentence again.

The amendment had appeared in the system before the temporary exception existed.

And it claimed approval from an office that had never given it.

He looked at Jonathan.

“Find the original day-34 submission.”

Jonathan nodded.

Edward gathered the documents.

For the first time, the question was no longer simply why Gabriel's conditional response had remained active.

It was whether someone had prepared the administrative system to keep him active before he had even signed it.
