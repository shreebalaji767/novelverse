# **Chapter 111 — The Authority Behind the Code**

The reference EFO-SL/07 appeared on forty-three documents.

Edward had counted them twice.

Some concerned hospital transfers. Others involved protected correspondence, financial instructions, restricted identity records, and communications between Vale Private Counsel and the Aurelius Family Office.

The reference was never used consistently.

Sometimes it appeared beside the words *External Family Office Liaison*.

Sometimes it was described as a protected administrative channel.

On several documents, it appeared without any explanation at all.

But the day-37 disclosure memorandum had revealed something new.

Someone had used the reference to request an external liaison review before the originating mothers received their reports.

That request had not been signed.

It had not been entered under an individual's name.

And the restriction had remained in place.

Edward placed the forty-three documents across the conference table.

“Start with the authority records,” he told Jonathan. “I want to know who could use this code, who could approve a restriction, and who could only forward a request.”

Jonathan divided the documents into three piles.

“Some of these were generated by the hospital.”

“Separate them.”

“Some came from Vale Private Counsel.”

“Separate those too.”

“And the Family Office records?”

Edward pointed to the third pile.

“Those are the ones I want first.”

The first useful document was an old access schedule.

It had been prepared before the children's transfers began.

The schedule described EFO-SL/07 as a temporary liaison authority established to coordinate communications between the Aurelius Family Office, medical institutions, and external legal representatives.

It listed four authorised functions:

1. Receive protected correspondence.
2. Forward requests for medical-record verification.
3. Preserve documents pending identity reconciliation.
4. Communicate approved decisions to external representatives.

A separate paragraph stated:

**The liaison authority shall not independently determine biological identity, appoint a receiving family, or approve permanent placement.**

Edward underlined the sentence.

“That's important.”

Jonathan nodded.

“The code was administrative. It didn't give anyone authority to decide where a child belonged.”

“Exactly.”

He continued reading.

The access schedule listed six authorised users.

Alexander Aurelius.

Victoria Aurelius.

Helena Brooks.

Gabriel Vale.

Margaret Voss.

And a final entry:

**EXTERNAL LEGAL REPRESENTATIVE — APPOINTMENT BY CHAIRMAN'S OFFICE.**

No name appeared.

Edward looked at the page.

“Who was appointed?”

Jonathan searched the archive index.

“There are several appointment records.”

“Names?”

“Gabriel Vale appears most frequently. Charles Vale also received temporary access for legal coordination.”

“Did Charles have authority to issue instructions through the liaison code?”

“Not by himself. He could submit instructions for review. The Family Office was supposed to approve them.”

“Who approved them?”

Jonathan turned the page.

The answer was not a person.

It was a procedure.

**CHAIRMAN'S OFFICE OR DELEGATED FAMILY OFFICE AUTHORITY.**

Edward leaned back.

“So Charles could submit a request, but he couldn't authorise it merely by using the code.”

“Correct.”

“And if a document appears to have been authorised through EFO-SL/07, we need the underlying approval.”

Jonathan nodded.

Edward looked again at the six access entries.

“Find every approval record for day 37.”

At the Aurelius residence, Helena Brooks was reviewing the old Family Office authority schedule when Alexander entered.

She looked up.

“Edward has requested the original access register.”

“I saw the request.”

“Some of the older records were never digitised.”

“Then we'll retrieve them.”

Helena closed the folder.

“There is something else.”

Alexander waited.

“EFO-SL/07 was not a permanent office. It was established for a limited administrative purpose. Several people were permitted to use it because the work involved multiple institutions.”

“Did you authorise Charles Vale to use it?”

“No. I approved his temporary access for legal correspondence. I did not delegate authority to approve child placements.”

“Did he ever claim otherwise?”

Helena hesitated.

“He submitted documents that referred to the Family Office's general authority.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

“No,” she admitted. “He never received a formal appointment to make permanent family-allocation decisions.”

Alexander looked at the schedule.

“Then how did the documents come to carry our authority?”

“That is what we need to establish.”

He turned toward the window.

For years, the Aurelius family had been told that their missing daughter had been placed somewhere under an emergency protection arrangement.

The language had sounded reassuring.

A child had been protected.

A family had been assigned responsibility.

Records were being reconciled.

But the original documents now suggested that no one had ever completed the process.

Alexander looked back at Helena.

“Find the original delegation register. Not the summary.”

“I'll arrange it.”

“And make sure Edward receives every relevant record.”

Helena nodded.

Alexander returned to his office.

He did not want another explanation.

He wanted the authority trail.

The original delegation register was recovered that afternoon.

It was a bound ledger with handwritten amendments.

The first entries were straightforward.

Alexander had delegated routine financial and administrative authority to Helena Brooks.

Victoria had authorised the preservation of her maternity records.

Gabriel Vale had been appointed as external liaison for a limited period.

Charles Vale had been granted temporary legal correspondence access.

Margaret Voss had been assigned custody of restricted originating records.

None of these appointments authorised permanent child allocation.

Edward examined the amendment pages.

On day 18, an additional entry had been made.

**External Liaison Review — EFO-SL/07.**

**Purpose: Protected identity correspondence and record reconciliation.**

**Administrative coordinator: Gabriel Vale.**

A second entry, dated day 31, extended the arrangement.

**External legal coordination: Charles Vale.**

But the authority column contained a handwritten qualification.

**No delegation of receiving identity appointment or permanent allocation authority.**

Edward placed the ledger beside the day-37 memorandum.

The wording matched.

Someone had repeatedly restricted the authority of the liaison channel.

Yet the day-37 deferral had still been processed under that reference.

“Who entered the day-31 qualification?” Edward asked.

Jonathan examined the handwriting.

“Helena Brooks.”

“Was it authorised?”

“The amendment carries her signature and the Family Office seal.”

Edward nodded.

“Then the restriction was formally in place before the reports were deferred.”

He turned to the next page.

There was another amendment.

Day 36.

**Any request to defer originating representative disclosure must be referred to the relevant originating authority and legal records officer.**

The signature was Alexander's.

Edward read it twice.

“Alexander personally restricted the ability to defer disclosure without proper review.”

Jonathan nodded.

“Which means the day-37 action should have required additional approval.”

“Find out whether it was ever obtained.”

Daniel Mercer was interviewed again.

He arrived carrying a small notebook and appeared less defensive than during their previous meeting.

Edward showed him the day-36 amendment.

“Were you aware of this restriction?”

Daniel read the page.

“I remember something similar.”

“Did you receive it?”

“I don't think I received the original. But it was circulated through the administrative department.”

“Did the review desk process a disclosure deferral on day 37?”

Daniel nodded.

“I remember the reference.”

“Who submitted it?”

“EFO-SL/07.”

“Who approved it?”

“I don't know.”

“Did the request contain a name?”

“No. It was submitted through the shared liaison account.”

“Did you see an approval attached?”

Daniel looked at the document.

“I remember a status marking.”

“What marking?”

“External review accepted.”

Edward's expression tightened.

“Accepted by whom?”

“I don't know.”

“Was there a signature?”

“No.”

“An approval code?”

Daniel thought for a moment.

“Possibly a delegated-authority reference.”

“What reference?”

“I can't remember the exact characters.”

Edward placed a blank sheet in front of him.

“Write down everything you remember.”

Daniel took the pen.

After a few seconds, he wrote:

**EFO / D-36 / LIAISON CLEARANCE**

He looked at the words.

“That's what I remember seeing.”

Edward glanced at Jonathan.

“D-36 was the day-36 amendment.”

“Yes.”

“Liaison clearance was not the same as originating authority approval.”

Daniel nodded.

“I understand that now.”

“Did you understand it then?”

Daniel lowered his eyes.

“I knew the process required a separate approval. I assumed the clearance marking meant it had been obtained.”

“Did you verify?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because the review desk wasn't responsible for determining who had authority to approve the disclosure. We processed the status once the request came through the authorised channel.”

Edward's voice remained calm.

“And that was how an unverified request became an accepted administrative status.”

Daniel did not defend himself.

“Yes.”

The day-37 liaison record was eventually found in a separate correspondence archive.

It was not a full approval.

It was a one-page receipt.

The top section contained the reference EFO-SL/07.

The middle section stated:

**Request received: Deferral of originating representative disclosure.**

**Reason: Receiving identity information remains under reconciliation.**

**Administrative status: Accepted for restricted handling.**

At the bottom was a line:

**Originating authority approval: NOT ATTACHED.**

Edward stared at it.

The status had been accepted for restricted handling.

But no approval from the originating authority had been attached.

Someone had treated acceptance of a request as acceptance of the request's substance.

The difference was small in wording.

It was enormous in consequence.

“Who signed the receipt?” Jonathan asked.

Edward turned the page.

There was no signature.

Only a system code.

**LIAISON-07 / AUTO-RECEIPT.**

Jonathan exhaled.

“So the shared account accepted the request automatically.”

“It acknowledged receipt,” Edward corrected. “The later system treated it as approval.”

“Who changed the status?”

“That is what we need to find.”

A second page had been stapled behind the receipt.

It contained a handwritten instruction.

**Do not interpret restricted-handling acceptance as consent to withhold originating reports.**

Edward recognised the initials.

E.V.

Eleanor Vale.

The note had been written on day 38.

One day after the request had been acknowledged.

Eleanor had noticed the difference.

She had tried to correct it.

But the correction had not reached the distribution record.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Find out where she sent this.”

Eleanor remembered the note.

“I wrote it after reviewing the liaison receipt,” she said.

Edward placed the copy in front of her.

“You understood that the system had treated an acknowledgement as an approval?”

“Yes.”

“Did you notify Charles?”

“I sent a correction to the legal office.”

“Did you notify Alexander?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“I believed the matter would be corrected through the legal review.”

“Was it?”

“No.”

“Did you receive a response?”

Eleanor looked at the paper.

“I received an acknowledgement.”

“From whom?”

“Gabriel Vale.”

Edward made a note.

“What did he say?”

“That the request would remain under restricted handling until the originating authority had reviewed it.”

“Did he say the reports would be withheld?”

“No.”

“Did he say the reports could be released?”

“No.”

“So the matter remained unresolved.”

“Yes.”

Edward placed another document before her.

It was the day-39 general preservation authority signed by Victoria.

Eleanor examined it.

“This was not an approval to withhold the report.”

“Correct.”

“It was permission to preserve and examine records.”

“Correct.”

“Then it should not have been used to justify the deferral.”

Edward looked at her.

“Was it?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you ever see the day-39 authority attached to the day-37 liaison request?”

“No.”

Edward closed the folder.

The pattern was becoming clearer.

The request to defer disclosure had been acknowledged automatically.

Eleanor had objected to treating that acknowledgement as consent.

The originating authority had not approved the restriction.

Victoria's later preservation authority had not approved it either.

And yet the originating reports had never been delivered.

“Did you know who submitted the original request?”

Eleanor hesitated.

“I suspected Charles's office.”

“Why?”

“Because the request referred to current receiving arrangements, which were being coordinated through his department.”

“Did you have proof?”

“No.”

Edward nodded.

“Thank you.”

At the Valmont Estate, Elena had finished her final academic checklist.

She sat on the floor beside her suitcase, checking the documents one by one.

Passport.

Programme acceptance.

Accommodation confirmation.

Insurance.

Emergency contacts.

Travel itinerary.

She placed each item in a transparent folder.

Catherine entered with a small package.

“This arrived for you.”

Elena looked up.

“What is it?”

“Your programme materials.”

She opened the package.

Inside were printed orientation instructions, a campus map, and a welcome letter.

Elena examined the map.

The campus was larger than she had expected.

She traced the route from her accommodation to the academic building.

“It's quite far.”

“Have you checked the transport arrangements?”

“Yes. There's a student shuttle.”

“Good.”

Elena smiled.

“I think I'm finally ready.”

Catherine sat beside her.

“Are you excited?”

“Yes.”

“Are you nervous?”

“A little.”

“That's normal.”

Elena placed the map back in the package.

“I'm looking forward to having a routine that belongs to me.”

Catherine studied her.

“What do you mean?”

“Classes, projects, people I meet, things I work on. I want to find out what I can do when I'm not constantly thinking about what happened here.”

Catherine's expression softened.

“You deserve that.”

Elena looked down at the welcome letter.

She had not forgotten the Valmonts.

She had not forgotten the questions surrounding her birth.

But she did not want her entire life to become an investigation into something she could not remember.

She wanted to study.

To build her project.

To make mistakes that belonged to her.

And to discover what kind of person she could become without anyone deciding it for her.

Catherine gently touched her shoulder.

“You don't have to leave your family behind to build your own life.”

Elena smiled.

“I know.”

Edward returned to the office with one final request.

He wanted the original EFO-SL/07 access log for day 37.

The archive team found it in a sealed technical file.

The record showed three events.

DAY 37 — ACCESS LOG

| Time  | Event                                                   |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 09:12 | Deferral request received through EFO-SL/07             |
| 09:13 | Automatic restricted-handling acknowledgement generated |
| 11:46 | Manual status update: disclosure deferred               |

Edward examined the final entry.

“Who performed the manual update?”

Jonathan enlarged the operator field.

It contained a code.

**G.V. — EXTERNAL LIAISON.**

Gabriel Vale.

Edward looked at the time.

“Gabriel manually changed the status.”

“Apparently.”

“Was he authorised to do that?”

Jonathan checked the delegation register.

“He could coordinate correspondence and preserve records. He could not independently approve a disclosure restriction.”

Edward read the entry again.

Gabriel had taken the automatic acknowledgement and changed the administrative status to disclosure deferred.

But the original authority approval was missing.

The action had been recorded under his own operator code.

That did not establish why he had done it.

He might have believed a separate approval existed.

He might have been following an instruction from Charles.

Or he might have understood that the request had never been properly authorised.

Edward looked at the access log.

“Find Gabriel.”

Gabriel Vale was located at his current residence.

He agreed to meet Edward the following week.

The interview had not yet begun when another message arrived from Jonathan.

It contained a scan from the original R-04 correspondence register.

Edward opened it.

The entry was dated day 37.

**Originating disclosure restriction — pending authority verification.**

**Correction received: Eleanor Vale.**

**Correction acknowledged: Gabriel Vale.**

**Manual status entered: disclosure deferred.**

Below the entry was a final notation.

**Originating representatives not to be contacted until external liaison authority confirms release conditions.**

Edward read the sentence carefully.

It was not a medical restriction.

It was not a legal prohibition imposed by a court.

It was an instruction about who could communicate with the mothers.

And it had been entered after Eleanor's correction had been received.

He called Jonathan.

“Do we know who wrote the final notation?”

“Not yet.”

“Does it carry an operator code?”

“No. The entry was added to the paper register.”

“Handwriting?”

“Dr Sen is examining it.”

Edward looked at the document again.

The mothers had not been informed.

The review had not been completed.

The receiving authority had not been appointed.

And now the records showed that someone had explicitly instructed the liaison office not to contact the originating representatives until release conditions were confirmed.

The instruction might have been intended to prevent an improper disclosure.

But the authority to impose it was still missing.

Edward placed the scan beside the day-36 amendment.

Alexander had required proper originating-authority approval.

Eleanor had objected to the automatic acknowledgement being treated as consent.

Yet Gabriel had manually deferred disclosure.

Someone had then instructed the liaison office not to contact the mothers.

Edward stared at the final notation.

There was no signature.

Only a reference written in the margin.

**CROSS-REFERENCE: VPC / PRIVATE INSTRUCTION 07.**

He looked up.

“Jonathan.”

“Yes?”

“Find Private Instruction 07.”

Jonathan checked the archive index.

Then his expression changed.

“It isn't in the ordinary correspondence register.”

“Where is it?”

Jonathan read the location twice.

“Vale Private Counsel's sealed personal-instruction archive.”

Edward slowly closed the file.

“Who was authorised to open that archive?”

Jonathan answered quietly.

“Charles Vale.”
