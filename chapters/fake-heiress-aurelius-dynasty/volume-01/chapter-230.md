# **Chapter 230 — The Name She Chose to Carry**

Six weeks later, the final historical review was ready.

It was not a single document.

It was a consolidated report containing the independent biological findings, the original maternity records, the protected identity material, the receiving-route history, and the investigation into the administrative failures.

Elena received the report through Laura Bennett.

She had requested a full copy of every section she was legally entitled to see.

She had also requested an independent explanation of the conclusions.

The report was more than two hundred pages long.

Elena did not attempt to read it all in one sitting.

She divided it into sections.

First, the biological comparison.

Then the original identity record.

Then the maternity record.

Then the receiving process.

Finally, the administrative failures.

The conclusions were consistent.

The independent genetic comparison strongly established that Alexander and Victoria Aurelius were her biological parents.

The original protected identity record contained the historical name Elena Aurelia.

The original St Catherine's clinical source was connected to that protected identity record through the independent documentary examination.

The receiving-family reference, however, had never been finally established.

The Day-6 amendment had introduced a provisional reference.

Later administrative summaries had incorrectly described that reference as confirmed.

The original records did not establish who had authorised the infant's receiving placement with the Valmont family.

The investigation could not identify the individual responsible for the original transfer.

The report also made clear that the administrative failures had delayed the recognition of the identity problem and prevented the originating family's objections from reaching the appropriate review authorities.

It did not identify a single mastermind.

It did not claim to know more than the evidence supported.

Elena appreciated that.

She closed the report.

The truth was substantial.

It was also incomplete.

At the Valmont estate, Edward presented the findings to Richard and Catherine.

Sebastian, Nicholas, and Isabelle were present.

Nobody spoke while Edward explained the conclusions.

When he finished, Richard asked, “So Elena is the biological daughter of Alexander and Victoria Aurelius.”

“Yes.”

“And the records still don't explain how she came to us?”

“Correct.”

Catherine lowered her eyes.

For years, the Valmont family had treated the original DNA result as the end of the matter.

It had established that Elena was not biologically related to them.

But it had not explained who she was.

It had not justified the way they had treated her afterward.

It had not erased their responsibility for raising her.

Catherine looked at Edward.

“Does the report establish that we received her unlawfully?”

“It establishes that the original receiving authority was not documented in the surviving records. It does not establish the full circumstances or the intentions of the people involved.”

Richard leaned back.

“Then we cannot claim that someone deliberately gave us the wrong child.”

“Not on the current evidence.”

Sebastian looked at the table.

“We still raised her.”

Catherine turned toward him.

“Yes.”

“And then we threw her out.”

Nobody answered.

Isabelle had been silent throughout the meeting.

Finally, she spoke.

“The report doesn't change what happened after the DNA result.”

Richard looked at her.

“No.”

“We treated Elena as though the biological result had made every previous year meaningless.”

Catherine's eyes filled.

“We did.”

Isabelle looked down at her hands.

“I don't want to ask her to come back just because another family has been identified.”

Edward glanced at her.

“Then don't.”

“I want to know whether she is willing to keep working with me on the foundation review.”

“That is a separate question.”

“I know.”

Catherine looked toward the empty chair.

“Does she know the report is complete?”

“Yes. Her representative has delivered it.”

“Has she said anything about meeting the Aurelius family?”

“She has not agreed to a meeting.”

Richard nodded.

“Then we respect that.”

It was a simple decision.

It had taken the family a long time to learn how to make it.

Elena finished reading the report on a Sunday afternoon.

She sat at her desk in Singapore.

Beside the report lay her old Valmont identity documents, her current university records, and the laboratory's signed conclusion.

Three sets of papers.

Three different parts of her history.

She opened a blank page.

At the top, she wrote:

**What I know now.**

She listed the facts.

She had been born under circumstances that had not been properly documented.

Her biological parents were Alexander and Victoria Aurelius.

Her original historical name was Elena Aurelia.

She had been raised by the Valmont family.

The original receiving process remained unresolved.

Later administrative records had concealed the uncertainty by describing provisional references as established.

She had not caused any of it.

She read the last sentence.

Then she added another:

**I am not responsible for correcting every mistake made before I was old enough to understand it.**

She saved the document.

The next day, Laura arranged a confidential call.

“Have you finished reviewing the report?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“Would you like to discuss any of the conclusions?”

“I understand them. I have one question about the legal position.”

“Go ahead.”

“If I want to use the historical surname, can I?”

“Yes. Your current legal name remains valid unless you choose to pursue a formal change under the applicable law. The historical record does not automatically alter your civil registration.”

“And if I don't change it?”

“Nothing happens automatically. You remain legally known by your current name.”

Elena nodded.

“Then I won't change it right now.”

“That is entirely your decision.”

“There's something else.”

“Yes?”

“I want the biological report preserved. But I don't want my genetic information used for anything else.”

“The laboratory's retention instructions already restrict secondary use. We can also place a separate written restriction with the records custodian.”

“Please do.”

Laura made a note.

“Anything else?”

Elena looked at the documents.

“Can I request a meeting with the Aurelius family without making it a permanent commitment?”

“Yes. A meeting does not establish a continuing relationship or require you to accept any family arrangement.”

“Can I choose where it happens?”

“Certainly.”

“And can I bring someone?”

“Yes.”

Elena thought for a moment.

“I want to meet them.”

Laura did not react with surprise.

“Would you like me to arrange it?”

“Yes. But I want it to be a single private meeting. No press, no extended family, no business representatives.”

“I'll communicate those conditions.”

“And I want the meeting to happen here in Singapore.”

“Understood.”

Elena paused.

“Please don't describe it as a homecoming.”

“I won't.”

The Aurelius family received the request the following morning.

Alexander read it first.

Then he handed it to Victoria.

“She wants to meet.”

Victoria's eyes filled.

“Here?”

“In Singapore.”

“Just us?”

“Just us, initially.”

Adrian looked at the conditions.

“No press. No extended family. No business representatives.”

Damien nodded.

“That is reasonable.”

Lucien looked at Julian.

“Are you disappointed?”

Julian stared at the message.

“A little.”

Then he shook his head.

“But she said she wants to meet them. That is already more than she had to offer.”

Victoria touched his hair.

“She may want to meet us first and decide about everyone else later.”

Julian nodded.

“I can wait.”

Alexander replied through Laura.

**We agree to every condition. We will travel to Singapore and meet at the location you choose. Thank you for giving us the opportunity.**

The meeting was scheduled for a Saturday afternoon.

Elena chose a private conference room at a quiet hotel near the university.

She did not choose the Aurelius estate.

She did not choose the Valmont estate.

She wanted neutral ground.

She also asked Ms. Bennett to attend as her independent representative.

Edward offered to accompany her, but Elena declined.

“This is not a Valmont investigation meeting,” she told him.

“I understand.”

“I know you have helped establish the historical facts. But I want this conversation to be mine.”

Edward nodded.

“Then it should be.”

On Saturday, Elena arrived early.

She wore a simple blue dress and carried a small folder.

Inside were the final historical report, a copy of the biological comparison, and the list of questions she had prepared.

Laura was already waiting.

“Are you comfortable?” she asked.

“Mostly.”

“Would you like to postpone?”

Elena considered it.

“No. I want to know what they have to say.”

At 2:00 p.m., the door opened.

Alexander Aurelius entered first.

Victoria followed.

They stopped when they saw her.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Elena stood.

Alexander looked at her face.

Victoria's eyes filled immediately, but she did not move forward.

“Hello, Elena,” Alexander said.

“Hello.”

Victoria took a breath.

“Thank you for meeting us.”

Elena gestured toward the chairs.

“Please sit.”

They did.

Laura remained nearby, but she did not interrupt.

For several seconds, the three of them looked at one another.

Alexander finally spoke.

“We don't know where to begin.”

“Then perhaps we should begin with what you know.”

He nodded.

“That is fair.”

He placed a thin folder on the table.

“We brought copies of the original documents we were authorised to retain. Some are the same documents you have already received. Others concern the original request and the circumstances in which we began the search.”

Elena opened the folder.

“Did you know the receiving identity was provisional?”

“We knew that the historical records were incomplete,” Alexander said. “We did not know that later administrative summaries had described the receiving reference as confirmed.”

“Did you know I was with the Valmont family?”

“No.”

“Did you ever receive a confirmation that I had been placed with them?”

“No.”

“Did you know the family had raised a child under the name Elena Valmont?”

“No.”

Elena looked at Victoria.

“Did you stop searching?”

Victoria shook her head.

“We had periods when we had no reliable lead. We were told that the identity records were protected and that the communication route remained unresolved. We were not given a name or a verified location.”

“Did you know that I had been disowned?”

“No.”

“Did you know I was living independently?”

“No.”

Elena looked down at the documents.

There was no answer in the room that could undo what had happened.

She turned to Alexander.

“Why did you write that you couldn't ask me to forgive you?”

He looked at her carefully.

“Because we don't know what happened to you. We don't know how much of your life was difficult. We don't know what you think of us. We cannot ask you to forgive us for the consequences of something we have not yet fully understood.”

“But you are my biological parents.”

“Yes.”

“And that matters.”

“It does.”

“But it doesn't mean I know you.”

Alexander nodded.

“No. It doesn't.”

Victoria spoke softly.

“We would like to know you. But we understand that you have a life that existed before we entered it.”

Elena looked at her.

“I don't want to be treated as though I've been missing from your family for eighteen years and now everything should return to normal.”

“We won't ask that,” Victoria said.

“I don't want a new house, a new schedule, a security team, or people deciding which university I should attend.”

Alexander gave a small nod.

“You will not be required to accept any of those things.”

“And I don't want money offered as an apology.”

Victoria looked down.

“We understand.”

Elena leaned back.

“Good. Because I have my own plans.”

“What are they?” Victoria asked.

Elena considered the question.

“I want to finish my academic programme. I want to develop the grant-management platform. I want to build something useful that belongs to me.”

Alexander listened.

“You can do that.”

“I intend to.”

A faint smile appeared on Victoria's face.

“I would like to hear more about it someday.”

“Perhaps.”

It was not a promise.

But it was not a refusal either.

The conversation lasted nearly two hours.

They discussed the historical records.

They discussed the failed communication attempts.

They discussed the difference between biological parentage and legal identity.

They discussed the fact that no one had established who made the original receiving decision.

Alexander admitted that he had no complete explanation for the administrative failures.

Victoria said she wanted the historical investigation to continue, but not in a way that forced Elena to participate beyond her consent.

Elena asked whether the Aurelius family would attempt to obtain custody or control over her legal affairs.

“No,” Alexander said.

“Would you challenge my current identity?”

“No.”

“Would you insist that I change my surname?”

“No.”

“Would you expect me to move?”

“No.”

Elena looked at Laura.

The answers were consistent with the written commitments.

She closed the folder.

“I think that's enough for today.”

Alexander nodded.

“Of course.”

Victoria stood, then hesitated.

“May I ask one thing?”

Elena looked at her.

“Go ahead.”

“Would you be comfortable if we wrote to you occasionally through Ms. Bennett?”

Elena thought about it.

“Not frequently. And not every message needs to be answered.”

“We can do that.”

“And I don't want gifts sent to my apartment.”

Victoria gave a small, understanding smile.

“All right.”

Alexander stood.

“Thank you for meeting us.”

Elena nodded.

“Thank you for coming.”

There was no embrace.

No dramatic declaration.

No sudden feeling that the past had been repaired.

They said goodbye.

And they left.

A week later, Elena received a message from Julian.

It had been sent through Laura, with the Aurelius family's approval.

It was short.

**Hello, Elena. I'm Julian. I'm the youngest. I know you haven't agreed to meet the rest of us, so I won't ask. I just wanted to say that I hope your university programme is going well.**

Below the message was a photograph of a notebook page.

The page contained a handwritten list.

At the bottom, one line had been added:

**Ask her what she wants to do, instead of deciding everything myself.**

Elena smiled.

She replied:

**Hello, Julian. My programme is going well. I hope school is going well for you too.**

A minute later, another message arrived.

**It is. I have exams. Unfortunately.**

Elena laughed.

She typed:

**Unfortunately, they tend to happen even when you don't want them to.**

Julian replied with a sad face.

It was the beginning of a conversation.

Not a sibling relationship established overnight.

Just two people exchanging messages.

Elena was comfortable with that.

At the Valmont estate, Catherine received a short message from Elena.

**I have completed the historical review. The biological result is confirmed. I am not changing my current legal name at this time. I am willing to continue the limited foundation review, provided we maintain the written scope and boundaries.**

Catherine read it several times.

She showed it to Richard.

“She's willing to continue working with us,” she said.

Richard nodded.

“Then we should respect the agreement.”

Isabelle read the message last.

She did not ask whether Elena would return.

Instead, she replied:

**The revised foundation procedures are ready for your review whenever you are comfortable. Thank you for continuing the work.**

Elena responded later that evening.

**I'll review them next week.**

That was all.

But it was enough.

The Valmont family had not regained the relationship they once had with her.

They had not earned that yet.

And Elena was no longer waiting for them to decide whether she belonged.

Three months later, the Singapore programme concluded.

Elena's grant-management pilot had been expanded to a second supervised group.

The system had processed its first complete cycle of applications, reviews, decisions, and payment confirmations.

The programme coordinator offered her an opportunity to continue developing the platform with institutional support.

Elena accepted the discussion, not the offer itself.

She wanted to examine the terms.

She wanted to understand the intellectual property arrangements.

She wanted to retain control over the project.

The meeting was scheduled for the following week.

Her life was moving forward.

The Aurelius family had begun communicating with her occasionally.

Alexander sent a short message asking about her project.

Victoria sent a note congratulating her on the pilot's successful review.

Adrian offered to share general business resources, but Elena declined the offer of direct investment.

Lucien sent a photograph of a ridiculous cake Julian had attempted to bake.

Damien sent a brief message about a security issue in the platform's proposed hosting arrangement, with no expectation that she would use his advice.

She had not met the other brothers yet.

She was not in a hurry.

At the Valmont Foundation, Isabelle continued improving the grant procedures.

Their working relationship became more professional and less strained.

Sometimes they disagreed.

Sometimes they worked well together.

Neither pretended that the past had disappeared.

And that was healthier than pretending everything was fine.

On the day Elena returned to her apartment after the programme, she placed her university certificate on the desk.

Beside it were two documents.

Her current identity record.

And the historical record bearing the name Elena Aurelia.

She looked at them for a long time.

For years, she had believed that being called a fake heiress was the most important thing that had happened to her.

It had been the beginning of a difficult chapter.

But it had not been the definition of her life.

She had lost a family.

She had discovered another.

She had learned that biological truth could be established through evidence, but trust could not.

She had learned that records could preserve facts and still fail to explain what happened.

She had learned that a person could have two family histories without owing either family complete control over her future.

Elena picked up her pen.

She opened a new notebook.

On the first page, she wrote:

**Elena Aurelia Valmont.**

She looked at the name.

Then she crossed out the final word.

Not because she was ashamed of it.

Not because she wanted to erase the years she had spent in the Valmont household.

She simply did not want to carry a name out of obligation.

She wrote beneath it:

**Elena Aurelia.**

Then, after a moment, she added:

**My name. My life. My decision.**

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Julian appeared.

**I have another list. It's much shorter this time.**

Elena smiled.

**How short?**

**Three things.**

**That's an improvement.**

**One: meet you when you're ready. Two: show you my school project. Three: ask what you want to do next.**

Elena read it.

She replied:

**The third one is the most important.**

Julian answered:

**I know. I finally understood the assignment.**

Elena laughed.

She put the phone down and looked at the notebook.

Outside, the city continued its ordinary evening.

There were no grand announcements.

No public reunion.

No sudden transfer of wealth.

No demand that she return to a family estate and take her place in a dynasty.

There was only a young woman with a finished academic programme, a developing project, unanswered questions about her past, and the freedom to decide what came next.

The Valmont family had raised her.

The Aurelius family had given birth to her.

Neither fact could own her future.

And Elena no longer needed to prove that she deserved a place in anyone's family.

She had already built a place for herself.

**The End.**
