# I Was Kicked Out for Being a Fake Heiress. Why Is an Entire Dynasty Looking for Me?

## Volume 1: The Fake Heiress

# Chapter 1 — An Ordinary Morning in the Valmont Mansion

The Valmont mansion had thirty-seven bedrooms, six reception rooms, three dining rooms, two libraries, an indoor swimming pool, an outdoor swimming pool, a private cinema, a gymnasium, and a garden large enough to make a person forget they were still standing on private property.

It also had a rather peculiar problem.

Nobody in the household could agree on what to have for breakfast.

"I want pancakes."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because you had pancakes yesterday."

"That's discrimination."

"That's called having a balanced diet, Nicholas."

Nicholas Valmont leaned back in his chair, looking deeply offended.

Across the enormous breakfast table, Elena Valmont tried very hard not to laugh.

She failed.

A small giggle escaped her.

Nicholas immediately turned towards her.

"Elena! You're supposed to support me!"

"I support your right to eat pancakes."

"Thank you!"

"But I also support Mother."

His expression collapsed.

"Traitor."

Elena picked up her glass of orange juice.

"An unfortunate consequence of being raised in a household with functioning nutritional standards."

"You're becoming just like Mother."

"That's a compliment."

"Not when she's the one confiscating my desserts."

At the head of the table, Richard Valmont lowered his newspaper.

"Can we have breakfast without turning it into a parliamentary debate?"

Nicholas opened his mouth.

Richard raised one eyebrow.

Nicholas closed it.

Elena quietly pushed the plate of sliced strawberries towards her second brother.

He accepted them without looking up from his tablet.

The morning returned to normal.

Or as normal as a breakfast involving a billionaire family could be.

Elena was eighteen years old.

She had spent her entire life in the Valmont mansion, surrounded by people who had known her since infancy.

Her father, Richard Valmont, was the chairman of Valmont Holdings, a major domestic business conglomerate.

Her mother, Catherine Valmont, managed the family's charitable foundation and was frequently involved in social events.

Her three older brothers had all inherited different aspects of their father's personality.

Sebastian, the eldest, was serious and ambitious.

Nicholas, the second, was sociable and occasionally troublesome.

Edward, the youngest of the three brothers, was quiet, observant and considerably more difficult to fool than Nicholas.

And then there was Elena.

The youngest child.

The only daughter.

The person who had somehow acquired the ability to make three grown men argue over who would drive her to university.

She wasn't particularly interested in the family business.

She liked reading, studying, visiting bookstores, trying new recipes and spending time with her family.

Her life was comfortable.

Perhaps excessively comfortable.

She had never needed to worry about tuition fees, medical expenses, rent or the price of groceries.

But she wasn't particularly extravagant.

Her favourite handbag was three years old, her phone was perfectly functional, and she regularly complained that the family spent too much money on unnecessary decorations.

Her father once purchased an antique chandelier for the eastern reception hall.

Elena had looked at it for approximately five seconds before asking why they needed another chandelier when the previous one had been perfectly capable of producing light.

Richard had not appreciated the question.

Nicholas had laughed for an entire afternoon.

It was, in Elena's opinion, one of her better contributions to family life.

"Elena."

She looked up.

Sebastian was watching her from across the table.

"Yes?"

"Your university examination schedule has been released."

"Oh, really?"

"Your first examination is next Monday."

"I know."

"Are you prepared?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

Elena sighed.

"Sebastian, I've been studying."

"Studying and being prepared aren't necessarily the same thing."

Nicholas groaned.

"Please don't start. It's barely eight in the morning."

Sebastian ignored him.

"Your results last semester were good, but you could improve your performance in economics."

"I got an A."

"You could have received an A-plus."

Elena stared at him.

"Do you want me to study or do you want me to discover a new branch of mathematics?"

Edward finally looked up from his tablet.

"An A-plus would probably be easier."

"You're not helping."

Nicholas burst out laughing.

Even Catherine smiled.

For a moment, the breakfast table was filled with familiar warmth.

Elena had no reason to believe anything would change.

At 8:35 a.m., the household began its usual morning routine.

Sebastian left for the company.

Nicholas departed for an appointment with a business partner.

Edward went to his study.

Catherine was scheduled to attend a foundation meeting.

Richard had already left.

Elena remained in the mansion.

Her first lecture wasn't until ten.

She walked through the eastern corridor, carrying a notebook and a cup of coffee.

The walls were decorated with family photographs.

There were photographs of Richard and Catherine's wedding, Sebastian's graduation, Nicholas's childhood birthday celebrations, Edward's school competitions and countless family holidays.

Elena stopped in front of one particular photograph.

It showed the entire family standing in the garden.

She was approximately five years old.

Nicholas had his arm around her shoulders.

Sebastian was standing behind them, looking thoroughly displeased with the photographer.

Edward was holding a toy airplane.

Their mother was smiling.

Their father looked unusually relaxed.

Elena smiled.

She remembered that day.

Nicholas had accidentally pushed her into the garden fountain.

He had then jumped in after her because he thought she was drowning.

She hadn't been drowning.

She had been standing in knee-deep water.

Their mother had scolded both of them.

Nicholas had received the larger punishment.

Naturally, he still insisted that the entire incident had been her fault.

Elena continued walking.

She had a lecture to attend.

And she was already running late.

At approximately eleven o'clock, something unusual happened inside the Valmont mansion.

A telephone call arrived.

It was received by the household manager, Mr. Harris.

He had worked for the Valmont family for more than twenty years and was familiar with almost every aspect of the household's operations.

He answered politely.

"Valmont residence. Good morning."

The person on the other end introduced herself as a representative of a private medical institution.

She requested to speak with Richard Valmont.

Mr. Harris explained that the chairman was unavailable.

The woman hesitated.

Then she asked whether Catherine Valmont was present.

"Madam is currently attending a meeting."

"I understand. This concerns a confidential medical matter."

Mr. Harris straightened slightly.

"May I ask what matter?"

"I'm afraid I cannot disclose the details over the telephone. We require confirmation from one of the child's legal guardians."

The child's legal guardians.

Mr. Harris glanced at his notes.

"May I have your name and contact information?"

The representative provided them.

He wrote everything down.

Then he transferred the call to the chairman's private office, leaving a message with his secretary.

It was an ordinary administrative interaction.

Nothing about it suggested that the lives of everyone in the mansion were about to change.

At the university, Elena was having a considerably less dramatic morning.

She sat near the window in a lecture hall, listening to her professor explain the fundamentals of corporate financial management.

Her notebook was open.

Her handwriting was neat.

Her phone was on silent.

Everything was proceeding normally.

Until a message arrived.

**Nicholas:**

*Are you coming home for lunch?*

Elena typed a reply.

**Elena:**

*I have classes until two.*

**Nicholas:**

*Unfortunate.*

**Elena:**

*Why?*

**Nicholas:**

*I was going to steal your dessert.*

**Elena:**

*You can do that even when I'm home.*

**Nicholas:**

*Yes, but then you complain.*

**Elena:**

*I will complain either way.*

A few seconds passed.

**Nicholas:**

*That's my little sister.*

Elena smiled and put her phone away.

She returned her attention to the lecture.

Outside the window, the city continued its usual afternoon activity.

Traffic moved through the streets.

Office workers hurried between buildings.

Students gathered in small groups.

Somewhere in the city, people were making decisions that would affect their entire futures.

Elena had no idea that she was about to become one of them.

By the time Elena returned to the mansion, it was nearly three in the afternoon.

She handed her bag to a household attendant and walked into the main living room.

Her mother was sitting on the sofa.

That was unusual.

Catherine generally returned home later in the afternoon.

"Mother?"

Catherine looked up.

"You're back."

"Yes. Was the meeting cancelled?"

"No. I came home early."

Elena put her bag down.

"Is something wrong?"

Catherine smiled.

"Nothing serious."

But the smile didn't reach her eyes.

Elena noticed.

She had grown up watching her mother interact with people. Catherine was remarkably good at maintaining a pleasant expression even when she was unhappy.

This was different.

Her fingers were wrapped tightly around a teacup.

"Did something happen?"

Catherine placed the cup on the table.

"Your father wants to speak with you this evening."

Elena blinked.

"About what?"

"Something concerning your birth."

For a moment, Elena didn't understand.

Then she frowned.

"My birth?"

Catherine nodded.

"There's an issue with some old medical records."

Elena tilted her head.

"What kind of issue?"

"I don't know all the details yet."

That answer was unusual.

Her mother was rarely uninformed about anything involving the family.

Elena sat opposite her.

"Is someone ill?"

"No."

"Is it about my medical history?"

Catherine hesitated.

"Possibly."

Elena studied her face.

She wanted to ask another question.

But Catherine looked genuinely uncomfortable.

So she decided not to press her.

"All right. I'll speak with Father when he gets home."

Catherine nodded.

Then, unexpectedly, she reached across the table and took Elena's hand.

"Don't worry too much, darling."

Elena smiled.

"I'm not."

She squeezed her mother's hand.

"Everything will be fine."

Catherine looked at her for a long moment.

Then she nodded.

Richard Valmont returned home at 6:40 p.m.

Dinner was delayed.

Sebastian arrived shortly afterwards.

Nicholas came home ten minutes later, complaining about a meeting that had lasted considerably longer than expected.

Edward was already in the mansion.

Elena was upstairs when the household manager informed her that the family would be dining together.

She changed into a simple cream-coloured dress and went downstairs.

The dining room was illuminated by a large crystal chandelier.

Dinner had been prepared.

But nobody seemed particularly interested in eating.

Richard sat at the head of the table.

Catherine was beside him.

Sebastian sat opposite his mother.

Nicholas and Edward occupied their usual places.

Elena took her seat.

She glanced around.

"Why does everyone look so serious?"

Nicholas tried to smile.

"Perhaps we've finally realised that your jokes aren't funny."

Elena looked offended.

"My jokes are excellent."

"Your standards are questionable."

"You're just jealous."

"Of what?"

"My superior sense of humour."

"Please."

Richard cleared his throat.

The conversation stopped.

He looked at Elena.

"We need to discuss something important."

Elena straightened.

"All right."

Her father placed a folder on the table.

"I received a report from a medical institution today."

She looked at the folder.

"What kind of report?"

"A report concerning your birth records."

Elena glanced at her mother.

Catherine lowered her eyes.

Sebastian folded his hands.

Nicholas stopped playing with his fork.

Edward was watching his father.

Richard opened the folder.

"Several months ago, the family office requested a review of certain old documents. During that process, some inconsistencies were discovered in the records relating to your birth."

Elena frowned.

"What inconsistencies?"

"Your original hospital documentation contains discrepancies."

"Like what?"

"Names, identification numbers and certain administrative details."

Elena stared at him.

"Could it be a clerical mistake?"

"That was our initial assumption."

Richard turned a page.

"However, a subsequent investigation raised questions that required further examination."

Elena's fingers tightened around her napkin.

"What examination?"

Her father paused.

"A DNA examination."

The room became quiet.

Elena looked at him.

Then at her mother.

Then back at the folder.

She gave a small, uncertain laugh.

"Why would you need a DNA examination?"

Nobody answered immediately.

That silence was considerably more frightening than any explanation.

Finally, Richard spoke.

"Because the results suggest that you are not biologically related to your mother and me."

Elena stopped breathing for a moment.

The words were perfectly understandable.

She knew what they meant.

But her mind refused to arrange them into something that made sense.

"Sorry?"

Richard looked directly at her.

"The examination indicates that you are not our biological daughter."

For several seconds, Elena said nothing.

The clock on the wall continued ticking.

A servant standing near the entrance lowered his eyes.

Nicholas looked at his father.

"Father..."

Richard raised a hand.

"Let me finish."

He turned back to Elena.

"We are still investigating the circumstances. However, there is strong evidence that a newborn exchange may have occurred at the hospital."

Elena stared at him.

"A newborn exchange?"

"Yes."

"Meaning..."

She swallowed.

"Meaning I was switched with another baby?"

Richard nodded slowly.

Elena looked down at her hands.

Her hands were trembling.

She tried to stop them.

"Then..."

Her voice came out quieter than she expected.

"Then where is the other girl?"

Catherine finally spoke.

"We've found her."

Elena raised her head.

"You have?"

"Yes."

"Where is she?"

"She is safe."

Elena looked between her parents.

A strange sensation settled in her chest.

There was another daughter.

Somewhere in the world, another girl had been born on the same day.

A girl who was apparently supposed to have lived Elena's life.

A girl who had been separated from her biological parents.

A girl who had spent eighteen years somewhere else.

Elena pressed her lips together.

"Does she know?"

Richard nodded.

"She has been informed."

"Is she coming here?"

"That is being arranged."

Elena looked down again.

She didn't know what she was supposed to feel.

Sadness?

Fear?

Anger?

Confusion?

All of them seemed to be present at once.

She finally asked the only question she could think of.

"What's her name?"

Richard turned another page.

"Isabelle."

Elena repeated it quietly.

"Isabelle."

It was a pretty name.

She wondered what the girl looked like.

Whether she liked reading.

Whether she had brothers and sisters.

Whether she was happy.

Whether she was frightened.

And whether she was going to hate Elena.

Dinner ended earlier than usual.

Elena barely touched her food.

She excused herself and returned upstairs.

Her bedroom was exactly as she had left it.

The curtains were open.

Her books were arranged on the shelves.

A small collection of photographs stood on her desk.

She sat down in front of them.

One photograph showed her first day of school.

Another showed her with Nicholas at a family holiday.

A third showed her mother holding her when she was a baby.

Elena picked up the last photograph.

She studied it carefully.

A tiny baby.

Her mother smiling.

Her father standing beside them.

She had always believed that this was the beginning of her life.

Now she didn't know what to believe.

Was she still the same person?

Of course she was.

Her memories hadn't changed.

Her childhood hadn't disappeared.

The people in the photographs were still the people who had raised her.

But something fundamental had shifted.

For eighteen years, she had belonged to the Valmont family.

At least, she had thought she did.

She placed the photograph back on the desk.

Then she opened her phone.

She searched for information about newborn exchanges.

There were articles about hospital identification errors, investigations and legal disputes.

She read several of them.

The details were complicated.

Some cases involved mistakes.

Others involved negligence.

A few involved deliberate interference.

Elena closed the browser.

She wasn't going to solve an eighteen-year-old medical mystery by searching the internet at night.

She changed into her nightclothes and climbed into bed.

Sleep did not come easily.

At 1:16 a.m., Elena was still awake.

She turned towards the window.

The mansion grounds were illuminated by small garden lights.

Everything looked familiar.

The trees.

The fountain.

The pathway.

The distant guesthouse.

She had spent eighteen years looking at that view.

Would she still be looking at it a year from now?

She didn't know.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Nicholas.

**Nicholas:**

*Are you awake?*

Elena looked at the screen.

**Elena:**

*Yes.*

**Nicholas:**

*You okay?*

She stared at the message for a while.

Then typed.

**Elena:**

*I don't know.*

A few seconds later, another message arrived.

**Nicholas:**

*Don't overthink things tonight. We'll figure it out.*

Elena smiled faintly.

**Elena:**

*Good night.*

**Nicholas:**

*Good night, idiot.*

She placed her phone on the bedside table.

For the first time in her life, she wondered whether she would still be called that tomorrow.

The following morning, the Valmont family gathered for breakfast.

Elena entered the dining room at her usual time.

Nicholas was already there.

He looked up.

"Morning."

"Good morning."

He pushed a plate towards her.

"Eat. You barely had dinner."

Elena sat down.

"Thank you."

Sebastian arrived shortly afterwards.

He greeted everyone and opened his tablet.

Edward was reading the news.

Catherine entered last.

For a few minutes, the conversation was ordinary.

Then Richard arrived.

He was carrying a document folder.

"I've received an update."

Everyone looked at him.

"The other girl has agreed to meet us."

Elena put down her fork.

"She's coming here?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"Tomorrow afternoon."

Elena nodded.

She looked at the plate in front of her.

Tomorrow.

In less than forty-eight hours, she would meet the girl who was biologically supposed to be the Valmont daughter.

She wondered whether Isabelle was nervous.

She wondered whether Isabelle was excited.

She wondered whether Isabelle had already imagined what it would be like to live in this mansion.

Perhaps she had.

Perhaps she had spent eighteen years dreaming about it.

Elena looked towards her mother.

"Mother?"

Catherine turned.

"Yes, darling?"

"Is there anything I should do?"

Catherine looked surprised.

"What do you mean?"

"Well, she's coming here. Should I prepare something?"

Nobody spoke.

Elena continued.

"Does she have any food preferences? Allergies? Should I show her around the house?"

Nicholas stared at her.

"You're asking whether you should prepare a welcome tour?"

Elena looked at him.

"She's never been here before."

Nicholas opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

Catherine looked down at her plate.

Richard answered.

"That won't be necessary. The household staff will handle the arrangements."

"Oh."

Elena nodded.

"All right."

She returned to breakfast.

Nobody noticed the way her fingers briefly tightened around her fork.

That afternoon, Elena received another message.

This time, it was from the family office.

They requested that she attend a meeting the following morning.

The message was formal.

It mentioned identity verification, legal documentation and the completion of the medical investigation.

Elena read it twice.

Then she placed her phone down.

She had never attended a family office meeting before.

She wasn't particularly interested in legal matters.

But apparently, this was no longer something she could avoid.

She returned to her studies.

For the rest of the afternoon, she tried to concentrate on her university assignments.

She managed three pages.

Then she realised she had been reading the same paragraph for twenty minutes.

She closed her notebook.

Tomorrow, Isabelle would arrive.

And Elena would finally meet the girl who had unknowingly occupied the other half of her identity.

She had no idea what the future would look like.

She only hoped that, whatever happened, nobody would have to be unhappy.

It was a rather optimistic hope.

But Elena had always been an optimist.

Far away from the Valmont mansion, in a quiet medical records office, an archivist was examining an old file.

The file had been retrieved following the recent request from the Valmont family's legal representatives.

It was eighteen years old.

The paper had yellowed slightly around the edges.

Several pages contained handwritten corrections.

The archivist frowned.

Something was wrong.

The original identification numbers did not match the later copies.

One signature appeared on a document dated before the corresponding birth registration.

And a page that should have contained the newborn transfer record was missing.

She checked the archive register.

Then checked it again.

The original record was supposed to be there.

It wasn't.

She called her supervisor.

"There's a problem with the Valmont hospital file."

"What kind of problem?"

"The original newborn transfer record is missing."

A pause.

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

"Check the supplementary archive."

"I already have."

Another pause.

Then the supervisor spoke more quietly.

"Don't make copies. Don't contact anyone outside the records department. I'll come down."

The archivist looked at the file.

She had no idea that the missing page would eventually become one of the most important pieces of evidence in an investigation spanning eighteen years.

She only knew that an old record was incomplete.

And someone, somewhere, had made certain that it stayed that way.

That evening, Elena stood in front of her bedroom mirror.

She brushed her hair and looked at her reflection.

Nothing had changed.

Same face.

Same eyes.

Same person.

She touched the photograph on her dressing table.

Then she turned off the light.

Tomorrow, she would meet Isabelle.

For now, that was enough.

She climbed into bed, pulled the blanket over herself and closed her eyes.

Outside, the Valmont mansion settled into silence.

The family slept beneath the same roof.

But for the first time in eighteen years, the future of that household was uncertain.

And somewhere beyond their world, a much larger family was still waiting to discover that the little girl they had lost was closer than anyone imagined.

**End of Chapter 1.**
