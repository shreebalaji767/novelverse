# **Chapter 45 — The Signature Beneath the Voucher**

Edward placed the property voucher beneath the examination lamp.

The document was old, but its condition was remarkably good. Its edges had yellowed, and a faint crease ran diagonally across the lower half. The paper had been stored flat for years, protected from moisture and direct sunlight.

The handwriting was the more troubling part.

**The child is to remain with the receiving woman until the mother can be approached safely. Do not disclose the identity of the other infant.**

Below it was the partial signature.

**M. V—**

Edward had seen those initials in too many places.

He had also learned that, in this investigation, familiar initials were rarely reliable answers.

"Have you compared it with Martha Voss's handwriting?" Jonathan Pierce asked.

"Preliminary comparison only."

"And?"

"The similarities are sufficient to justify a proper examination. They are not sufficient to identify her as the author."

Jonathan leaned back.

"Could it be Martin Hale?"

"Possibly."

"Marcus Morelli?"

"Also possible."

"Someone else?"

"Certainly."

Edward switched off the examination lamp.

"That's the problem. We have spent weeks discovering that nearly everyone involved had a reason to conceal something. We cannot turn a partial signature into an accusation simply because one name fits."

Jonathan looked at the voucher again.

"Then we need the original."

"We have it."

"No. We have the voucher. I mean the document from which this instruction was copied."

Edward looked at him.

The voucher was attached to a financial record from the account opened under Margaret Ellison's name. The instruction had been copied onto a property payment sheet, probably to justify continued accommodation expenses.

If the wording had been copied from an earlier document, the original might contain a complete signature.

"Find the payment file," Edward said.

Jonathan picked up his folder.

"Already requested."

"Good."

"And there's something else."

He removed a second sheet.

It was a copy of the rental property's maintenance ledger.

Three entries had been highlighted.

- Replacement of nursery curtains.
- Installation of additional window locks.
- Medical cabinet and temperature-monitoring equipment.

Edward read them twice.

"Someone prepared the property for a child."

"Not just prepared it. The work was completed before the tenant arrived."

"How much earlier?"

"Two days."

Edward looked at the address.

The rental property had been leased on the twelfth day after the births. The maintenance work had been authorised on the tenth.

The same day an unidentified infant had returned to the hospital observation room.

"Who requested the work?"

"Vale Private Counsel."

"Who inspected it?"

"An independent contractor. His company no longer exists, but the owner is alive."

Edward closed the folder.

"Let's speak to him."

At the Valmont Estate, the morning had begun with an argument over a university application.

Not a serious one.

At least, Elena hoped it wasn't.

"You've selected the wrong category."

Elena stared at her laptop.

"I'm fairly certain I know what programme I'm applying for."

Nicholas stood behind her chair, holding a cup of coffee.

"That's not what I meant."

"Then explain what you meant."

"You're applying for business administration, but the portal lists business management and economics separately."

"Because they're separate programmes."

"Exactly."

"Which is why I selected the one I wanted."

Nicholas looked at the screen.

Then at Elena.

Then back at the screen.

"Oh."

Elena slowly turned her head.

"Did you just discover that I was right?"

"I was checking."

"You were questioning."

"There's a difference."

"Not when you're doing it every morning."

Nicholas laughed and placed the coffee beside her.

Elena returned to the application.

The Singapore exchange programme had invited her to an online interview the following week. She had already submitted her academic records, personal statement, and a short explanation of why she wanted to study abroad.

She had written the explanation herself.

No family secretary.

No university consultant.

No one had rewritten her words into something more impressive.

She wanted to learn how businesses operated beyond the familiar environment in which she had grown up. She wanted to understand how organisations made decisions, how they handled failure, and how smaller enterprises could survive in competitive markets.

Most importantly, she wanted to discover what she could accomplish without relying on her surname.

The thought made her pause.

She had not expected the recent events to affect her university plans so deeply.

But everything about her life seemed uncertain now.

Her birth.

Her identity.

Her place in the Valmont family.

Even the meaning of the name she had used for eighteen years.

"Are you worried?" Nicholas asked.

Elena looked up.

"A little."

"About the interview?"

"About everything."

Nicholas pulled out the chair opposite her.

For once, he didn't immediately offer to solve anything.

"That's understandable."

Elena studied his expression.

"You aren't going to tell me that everything will be fine?"

"I could."

"Please don't."

"Then I won't."

She smiled faintly.

Nicholas glanced at her application.

"Whatever happens, the interview is yours. You earned the invitation."

"Thank you."

"And if you get accepted, I'll help you pack."

"I haven't even been interviewed."

"I know."

"Then stop planning my departure."

"I'm planning a hypothetical packing schedule."

"That's worse."

He laughed.

Elena returned to her laptop, but her expression had become a little lighter.

Across the room, Isabelle entered carrying a folder.

"Am I interrupting?"

"No," Elena replied. "Nicholas is planning my hypothetical packing schedule."

Isabelle blinked.

"That sounds like him."

Nicholas raised his cup.

"I am being supportive."

"You're being excessive," Elena said.

Isabelle placed her folder on the table.

"Catherine asked me to review the scholarship programme's application guidelines. She wants the first draft ready by Friday."

Elena looked at the pages.

"That's a lot of work."

"It is."

"Are you enjoying it?"

Isabelle considered the question.

"More than I expected."

She opened the folder.

The programme had grown beyond its original proposal. It now included university scholarships, vocational training, examination fees, and assistance for students who lacked the documents needed to complete applications.

Isabelle had also insisted on an independent review panel.

She didn't want the foundation's employees selecting students based on personal connections.

"Sebastian says I'm making the process unnecessarily complicated," she explained.

Nicholas nodded.

"That's because you are."

Isabelle looked at him.

"Thank you for your contribution."

"You're welcome."

Elena examined the guidelines.

"Why does this section say applicants must demonstrate exceptional academic performance?"

"Because the foundation wants to support capable students."

"But what about someone whose grades aren't exceptional because they couldn't afford tutoring or had to work?"

Isabelle looked down at the page.

"I hadn't considered that wording."

"You could assess improvement as well as final results."

Elena pointed to the next paragraph.

"And maybe include financial circumstances separately, so someone isn't rejected simply because they don't have certificates proving everything."

Isabelle picked up her pen.

"That's useful."

"It might make the assessment more complicated."

"It already is."

Nicholas looked between them.

"At least one of you is admitting that."

Neither girl paid attention to him.

For the next twenty minutes, they discussed the application guidelines.

It was the first time their conversation had felt entirely ordinary.

Not a discussion about birth records.

Not a careful exchange of reassurances.

Just two young women disagreeing about how a scholarship programme should work.

And Elena found herself appreciating that more than she expected.

The contractor's name was Peter Hargreaves.

He had retired from property maintenance six years earlier and now lived in a small house outside the city.

He was surprised to receive a visit from Edward Valmont and a legal representative.

"Vale Private Counsel?" Peter repeated after Edward explained the reason for their visit.

"Yes."

Peter adjusted his glasses.

"That was a long time ago."

"We're investigating a property they leased eighteen years ago."

"Which one?"

Edward gave him the address.

Peter's expression changed.

"I remember that place."

"Why?"

"Because of the nursery."

Edward leaned forward.

"What nursery?"

Peter looked at the two men.

"They told me it was for a young child. I assumed a family had hired someone to prepare a temporary residence."

"Who told you?"

"A gentleman from the legal office. Charles Vale, I think."

Edward made a note.

"Did you meet him personally?"

"Twice. He came to inspect the work."

"What work did he request?"

"Window locks, curtains, a medical cabinet, and some repairs to the heating system."

"Anything unusual?"

Peter thought for a moment.

"Yes. He didn't want the room decorated like a nursery."

Edward waited.

"He wanted it plain. No toys, no pictures, no name on the door. He said the child wouldn't be staying long."

"Did he say how long?"

"Four months, perhaps less."

"Did you see the child?"

Peter nodded.

"Once."

"When?"

"After the work was finished. I was called back because one of the window locks wasn't closing properly."

"Who was there?"

"A woman."

"Can you describe her?"

"Not very well. She was young. Maybe in her twenties. Dark hair, pale coat. She was carrying a baby."

"Was the baby newborn?"

"Small. I assumed so."

"Did you hear the woman speak?"

Peter hesitated.

"She asked whether the windows could be opened from the inside."

Edward stopped writing.

"Why?"

"She said she didn't want the child trapped in a room."

The answer was unexpectedly specific.

"What did you tell her?"

"That the locks were safety locks. They could be opened from inside by an adult."

"Did she seem satisfied?"

"No."

Peter looked towards the window.

"She asked me something else."

"What?"

"Whether the room had ever been used by another child."

Edward's pen stopped.

"What did you say?"

"I told her no. It had been prepared for the first time."

"Did she explain why she was asking?"

Peter shook his head.

"No. But she looked at the room for a long time before leaving."

"Did she give you her name?"

"No."

"Did Mr Vale identify her?"

Peter considered this.

"He called her Mrs Voss."

Edward and Jonathan exchanged a glance.

"Martha Voss?" Edward asked.

Peter frowned.

"I don't know her first name. But I remember the surname."

"Are you certain?"

"That's what Mr Vale called her."

Edward opened his folder and showed Peter a photograph of Martha Voss.

Peter studied it.

His expression remained uncertain.

"She looks similar."

"Is that the woman?"

"I can't say. Eighteen years is a long time."

"Could you identify her if you saw her in person?"

"Possibly."

Edward put the photograph away.

"Did the woman return?"

Peter shook his head.

"No. I never saw her again."

"Who paid for the work?"

"Vale Private Counsel."

"Did you retain the payment records?"

Peter gave a short laugh.

"I kept everything. My accountant insisted."

He stood and walked towards a cabinet.

After several minutes, he returned with a large envelope.

Inside were invoices, receipts, and a handwritten work order.

Edward examined the final sheet.

The instructions were dated the tenth day after the births.

At the bottom was a signature.

Not a partial one.

A complete name.

**M. Voss.**

Edward stared at it.

Jonathan leaned closer.

"That's her."

"Maybe."

The signature looked similar to Martha's name on the tenth-day administrative receipt.

But it was not the same handwriting.

The letters were too carefully formed, almost as though someone had copied the name from another document.

Edward turned the sheet over.

There was a second line, written in a different hand.

**Authorisation confirmed by originating representative.**

Below it sat a reference number.

EFO-SL/07.

The same code that had appeared throughout the investigation.

Edward photographed the document.

"Mr Hargreaves, may we keep this?"

Peter nodded.

"Of course. I don't need it."

Edward placed it carefully inside his folder.

He had come looking for the person who prepared a nursery.

Instead, he had found evidence that someone had used Martha Voss's name to authorise the work.

At the Aurelius Estate, Julian was having a disagreement with his eldest brother.

It concerned a list.

More specifically, the list of things Julian wanted to do with his younger sister.

Adrian held the notebook in one hand.

"Why is this necessary?"

"Because I don't want to forget anything."

"You're writing down activities you haven't discussed with someone who doesn't know you exist."

Julian took the notebook back.

"That doesn't make the activities bad."

"It makes the schedule premature."

"It's not a schedule."

Adrian glanced at the page.

"Item forty-six says, 'Don't assume she wants the same things I do.'"

"That's an important rule."

"Then why do you need to write it down?"

"So I remember."

Adrian looked at his younger brother.

For a moment, the usual criticism did not come.

Instead, he placed the notebook on the table.

"You're allowed to be excited."

Julian looked up.

"I know."

"But don't let excitement become an expectation she has to satisfy."

"I know that too."

Adrian studied him.

Julian sighed.

"I don't want her to arrive and feel like she has to become the sister I imagined."

The room became quiet.

Adrian's expression softened slightly.

"Then perhaps you've understood the most important part."

Julian picked up his notebook.

"Can I keep the list?"

"Yes."

"Can I add more things?"

"Within reason."

"Define reason."

"No."

Julian grinned.

Across the room, Victoria listened without interrupting.

She had been holding a small photograph.

It was an old photograph of the nursery, taken shortly before the disappearance of her daughter.

The room had been prepared for a baby.

A crib.

A white blanket.

A wooden shelf.

A small mobile hanging above the cot.

Everything had been waiting.

For eighteen years, she had kept it that way.

She looked at the photograph.

Then at her sons.

"I hope," she said quietly, "that when we find her, she has a place where she already feels comfortable."

Adrian turned towards her.

"Mother, we will make sure she has choices."

Victoria nodded.

"That's what I want."

Not a grand entrance.

Not a perfect reunion.

Not a daughter who immediately understood the scale of the family waiting for her.

Just a young woman who could enter a room without feeling that she had to earn the right to be there.

Alexander entered carrying a report.

"Adrian."

His eldest son stood.

"We received an update from the legal team."

"About Morelli?"

"Partly."

Alexander placed the report on the table.

"The historical account records are being reviewed. The private payment authorisations may have been issued through a delegated office."

"Could the signature have been copied?"

"Yes."

"Do we know who had access to the authorisation system?"

"Not yet."

Damien, who had been reading another document nearby, looked up.

"There's a second concern."

Alexander turned towards him.

"The EFO-SL/07 code was not retired after Morelli left the Family Office."

Adrian frowned.

"What?"

"It remained active in the system for another six months."

"Who maintained it?"

"That is what I'm trying to establish."

Alexander looked at the report.

For the first time that morning, his expression hardened.

Someone had continued using an authority code after the person associated with it had departed.

That meant the investigation was not simply about an old mistake.

Someone had preserved the means to continue making decisions.

And those decisions had affected two children.

That evening, Edward returned to the Valmont Estate.

Richard was waiting in his study.

Catherine sat beside the window, while Sebastian and Nicholas occupied the opposite sofa.

Isabelle was in the adjoining room, finishing a call with the foundation's programme coordinator.

Elena had gone upstairs to complete her university application.

Edward placed the contractor's documents on the desk.

"I found the property contractor."

Richard looked at the papers.

"What did he tell you?"

"The rental property was prepared on the tenth day. A woman carrying a baby visited after the work was completed."

"Who?"

"He was told her name was Mrs Voss."

Catherine looked up.

"Martha?"

"Possibly. He couldn't identify her with certainty."

Edward showed them the invoice.

"The authorisation carries the name Martha Voss, but the handwriting does not match the signature on her administrative receipt."

Sebastian examined it.

"So someone used her name."

"That is one possibility."

Nicholas frowned.

"Why would anyone do that?"

"To make an instruction appear authorised by someone who had not actually given it."

Richard leaned back.

"Could Martha have authorised it through another person?"

"She could have. We haven't established that she did."

Edward opened another document.

"The reference number is EFO-SL/07. The same code remained active in the Aurelius system for six months after Morelli left."

Catherine's expression changed.

"Then Morelli wasn't necessarily the last person who could use it."

"Correct."

Richard looked towards the window.

"Whoever controlled that authority could arrange payments, property leases, and temporary guardianship without appearing personally."

Sebastian tapped the invoice.

"And the woman carrying the baby?"

"She may have been the receiving woman. She may have been an intermediary. We cannot establish that from one witness statement."

Nicholas frowned.

"How many people were involved in this?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

He had asked himself the same question many times.

"More than we know."

Richard looked at him.

"What's the next step?"

"Handwriting examination. We also need the original payment file and the authorisation records."

"Do it."

Edward gathered the documents.

As he reached the door, Catherine spoke.

"Edward."

He turned.

"Did the contractor say anything else about the woman?"

Edward thought about the description.

"She asked whether the child could be trapped in the room."

Catherine looked down.

For a moment, she seemed to be somewhere else entirely.

Then she shook her head.

"No. Nothing."

Edward left.

Catherine remained by the window.

She remembered very little about the days after the birth.

Fragments.

A nurse.

A hospital room.

A small bundle.

The sound of someone saying that everything would be explained.

She had spent eighteen years believing that the missing details were simply the result of exhaustion and confusion.

Now she wondered whether someone had deliberately made sure she would remember as little as possible.

At 10:18 p.m., Elena finally submitted her university application.

She checked the confirmation page twice.

Then she saved a copy of the receipt.

Only after that did she close her laptop.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Clara.

*Did you submit it?*

Elena replied.

*Yes.*

Clara answered almost immediately.

*Excellent. Emergency biscuits are ready for the interview.*

Elena smiled.

She placed the phone beside her pillow.

Tomorrow would bring more questions.

There would be more documents, more meetings, and probably another uncomfortable family discussion.

But for tonight, she had completed something that belonged entirely to her.

She turned off the bedside lamp.

Downstairs, the Valmont family continued discussing a mystery that had begun eighteen years before.

And hundreds of kilometres away, in an archive maintained by a private legal firm, an old payment file was finally retrieved.

Its cover bore the same reference.

**EFO-SL/07.**

Inside was a list of authorised expenses.

The rental property.

Medical supplies.

Temporary accommodation.

Childcare.

And one final payment, dated ninety-second day after the births.

The recipient's name had been blacked out.

But beneath the redaction, someone had written a short instruction in blue ink.

**Do not use the name Voss again. The mother has recognised it.**
