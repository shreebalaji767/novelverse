# **Chapter 19 — The Instruction That Never Arrived**

Adrian Aurelius read the message twice.

Then he called Edward back.

"You're certain the reference code is EFO-SL/07?"

"Yes. It matches the code in the original family office archive."

"And the instruction?"

Edward looked at the photograph on his desk.

*If the original placement cannot be completed, the child must remain where she is until the second instruction is received.*

"The wording is exact. There is no indication of which child it refers to."

"Could the missing page contain the answer?"

"Possibly. The binding has been damaged, and part of the original sheet appears to have been cut away. We have requested a forensic examination."

Adrian stood beside the window of his office.

Below him, the Aurelius Global headquarters continued its usual activity. Employees moved between buildings, vehicles entered the underground parking levels, and meetings proceeded according to schedules prepared weeks in advance.

The company operated across dozens of countries.

Its executives could locate missing financial records, trace international transfers and reconstruct complicated corporate transactions.

Yet an eighteen-year-old instruction from a hospital seemed almost impossible to find.

"Have you spoken to Martin Hale again?" Adrian asked.

"Not since the inspection."

"Then we should."

"I agree."

"Arrange it."

Edward paused.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"The wording suggests the original placement was expected to happen after a second instruction. But we haven't found evidence that it ever arrived."

Adrian looked at the photograph again.

"Perhaps it did."

"And?"

"And perhaps someone made certain it was never recorded."

Martin Hale was not pleased to receive another visit.

He opened the door of his house, recognised Edward and Adrian, and immediately looked towards the street.

"Didn't we finish this?"

"No," Edward replied.

Hale sighed.

"Of course not."

He stepped aside.

His house was smaller than Edward had expected. The furniture was old but carefully maintained, and several cardboard boxes were stacked near the hallway.

Hale noticed Edward looking at them.

"I'm sorting things out."

"Are those from the storage unit?"

"Some of them."

"Have you found anything else?"

Hale gave him a tired look.

"Do you ever ask ordinary questions?"

"Occasionally."

"Not today, apparently."

They sat in the living room.

Adrian placed a photograph of the newly discovered annotation on the table.

Hale's expression changed.

Only slightly.

But Edward noticed.

"Have you seen this before?" he asked.

Hale leaned closer.

His eyes moved across the sentence.

"Where did you find it?"

"In the original hospital report."

Hale looked up.

"I thought that section had been removed."

"By whom?"

"I don't know."

"Mr. Hale."

"I said I don't know."

Adrian spoke for the first time.

"You're aware that this investigation concerns two children, not one. Every incomplete answer creates another problem for both families."

Hale looked at him.

"Do you think I don't understand that?"

"Then help us."

The older man rubbed his forehead.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Finally, he pointed to the reference code.

"That wasn't a hospital code."

"We know," Edward said.

"It belonged to an external liaison office. They handled confidential instructions between the Aurelius Family Office and certain legal representatives."

"Who worked there?"

"I don't remember everyone."

"Who was responsible for EFO-SL/07?"

Hale looked down.

"I never met that person."

"Did you receive instructions from them?"

"Sometimes."

"What kind?"

"Administrative instructions. Document transfers. Requests to delay circulation of certain reports."

"Did they have authority to alter hospital records?"

"No."

Hale answered too quickly.

Edward waited.

Hale looked uncomfortable.

"Not officially," he added.

Adrian leaned forward.

"What does that mean?"

"It means the liaison office could request that records be withheld from circulation. It could not legally change the original hospital records."

"Yet the records were changed."

"Yes."

"Then someone acted outside their authority."

Hale nodded slowly.

"That is what I believe."

Edward took out another photograph.

It showed the original dispatch record for the package intended for Victoria Aurelius.

"You collected this package from the Valmont residence."

Hale's eyes moved to the photograph.

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I was told it had been delivered to the wrong address."

"Who told you?"

"Someone from the liaison office."

"Who?"

"I don't remember."

Edward placed another page beside it.

"The package was addressed personally to Victoria Aurelius. Why did you take it instead of arranging a direct delivery?"

Hale's expression hardened.

"Because I was instructed to."

"By whom?"

"Someone who had authority over the file."

"That isn't a name."

"It is all I can give you."

Adrian watched him carefully.

"Did you open the package?"

Hale did not answer.

Edward leaned forward.

"Mr. Hale."

"I did not read the entire contents."

"That wasn't the question."

Hale looked towards the window.

Then he said, quietly, "I opened it."

The room became still.

"Why?" Adrian asked.

"Because I was instructed to verify that it contained the correct documents before returning it."

"Did you read the letter?"

"Some of it."

"What did it say?"

Hale shook his head.

"I don't remember every word."

"Then tell us what you do remember."

He stared at the photograph for a long time.

"It said that the original arrangement could not proceed. It said the child's identity had not been confirmed. And it said that the mother should not be informed until the second instruction arrived."

Edward felt a chill run through him.

"Which mother?"

"I don't know."

"Was the letter signed?"

Hale hesitated.

"Yes."

"By whom?"

He closed his eyes briefly.

Then he gave them a name.

"Charles Vale."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was sitting in the old nursery.

The room had been preserved for eighteen years.

The furniture had been maintained. The curtains had been replaced when necessary. The walls had been repainted, but the original pale colour had been kept.

A small wooden horse still stood on the shelf.

Victoria had bought it before her daughter was born.

She had never been able to remove it.

On the table lay a photograph of the original hospital identification card.

She had looked at it so many times that she could remember every detail.

Mother V. Aurelius.

Female child.

2:17 a.m.

Transfer Pending.

She had spent eighteen years imagining the moment she would finally learn what happened.

Sometimes she imagined a hospital mistake.

Sometimes a kidnapping.

Sometimes an accident so complicated that no one could explain it.

She had never imagined that people might have deliberately prevented her from receiving information.

The door opened.

Alexander entered.

"You're still here."

Victoria nodded.

"Did you find anything?"

"Adrian and Edward are speaking with Martin Hale."

"About the instruction?"

"Yes."

She turned towards him.

"Do we know who wrote it?"

"Charles Vale signed the package letter."

Victoria looked down.

"Charles."

Alexander sat beside her.

"He was a respected lawyer."

"I remember."

"He handled several confidential family matters. I trusted his judgement."

Victoria's fingers tightened around the photograph.

"Did you trust him enough to let him decide whether I could know what happened to my daughter?"

Alexander did not answer immediately.

"No."

"Then why was he allowed to?"

"I don't know."

She looked at him.

For eighteen years, she had watched her husband arrange searches, interview investigators, review hospital records and contact authorities.

He had never stopped.

But now there was a possibility that the truth had been deliberately kept from him.

It changed something.

Not the grief.

Not the longing.

Something else.

The feeling that their daughter had been missing while people who knew more than they admitted had continued living ordinary lives.

Victoria placed the photograph back on the table.

"I don't want another explanation about why we must wait."

Alexander took her hand.

"Neither do I."

"Then find the instruction."

"We will."

"And if someone knew where she was?"

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Then they will answer for it."

Elena was spending the afternoon at the university library.

She had intended to study economics.

Instead, she had spent nearly forty minutes comparing university programmes and another fifteen checking the application requirements for an international academic exchange.

Clara sat opposite her.

"You've read the same paragraph four times."

Elena looked up.

"Have I?"

"Yes."

"Maybe it's particularly interesting."

"Is it?"

"No."

Clara pushed a small packet across the table.

Elena looked at it.

"What is this?"

"Emergency biscuits."

"I thought you had run out."

"I bought more."

Clara opened her own notebook.

"You're welcome."

Elena smiled.

They studied quietly for a while.

Then Clara glanced at her.

"Any news?"

"Not really. The investigation is still going."

"Are you okay?"

Elena considered the question.

"I think so."

"That's not very convincing."

"I don't have a better answer."

Clara nodded.

She didn't push.

After a few minutes, Elena closed her laptop.

"I've been thinking about something."

"That sounds dangerous."

"I might apply for the academic exchange programme."

Clara looked interested.

"Really?"

"Yes. It would be for the second year, assuming I get accepted."

"Where?"

"There's a university in Singapore with a programme I'm interested in. There are also options in Europe."

"That sounds exciting."

"It does."

Elena opened the application page again.

"I've never really considered studying somewhere else for a semester."

"Why not?"

"I suppose I always assumed I'd stay close to home."

Clara gave her a sympathetic look.

"Things have changed."

"Yes."

Elena stared at the application requirements.

She had no idea where she would be living a year from now.

She didn't know which family she would be biologically connected to.

She didn't know what the investigation would uncover.

But she did know she wanted to study.

And she wanted to make decisions that belonged to her.

"Maybe I should apply anyway," she said.

Clara smiled.

"Definitely."

Isabelle had received a message from Catherine asking whether she wanted to accompany her to the foundation office the following morning.

She was looking at it when Elena entered the sitting room.

"Everything all right?"

Isabelle looked up.

"Mother asked me to visit the foundation tomorrow."

"Are you going?"

"I think so."

Elena sat down.

"Have you been there before?"

"No."

"They have a rather large library of project reports. You might find it interesting."

Isabelle looked at her.

"You've been there?"

"A few times. Mostly because Catherine needed help organising documents."

"You helped?"

"Only with simple things."

Isabelle smiled.

"You make everything sound like a school assignment."

"Some of it was."

Isabelle put her phone aside.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"Do you still feel comfortable here?"

Elena looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because I arrived, and everything changed."

Elena thought about the question.

There were things she missed.

The family photographs that had been moved.

The rearranged furniture.

The awkwardness around certain conversations.

The sudden awareness that her place in the house might be questioned.

But Isabelle had not caused those things.

And Elena knew that.

"Sometimes," she admitted. "Not always."

Isabelle looked down.

"I'm sorry."

"You don't have to apologise for coming home."

"Even if it means you have to share everything?"

Elena smiled faintly.

"I've been sharing everything with Nicholas for years. Trust me, sharing is not the difficult part."

Isabelle laughed.

Then her expression became serious again.

"I don't want to take your place."

Elena looked at her for a moment.

"Then don't try to."

Isabelle nodded.

It was not a complete solution.

Neither girl expected it to be.

But it was a beginning.

The meeting with Martin Hale ended after sunset.

Edward and Adrian returned to the Aurelius Global headquarters with a copy of Hale's statement.

Adrian read it again in the lift.

"Charles Vale signed the letter."

"Yes."

"And Hale opened the package."

"Yes."

"But he says he didn't know which mother it was intended for."

"That's his claim."

Adrian looked at him.

"You don't believe him?"

"I believe he is leaving something out."

"Why?"

"Because he remembers the instruction but not the person who gave it. He remembers opening the package but not what he read. He remembers Charles Vale's signature but claims he doesn't know why the document was important."

Adrian nodded.

"Then we need the original correspondence."

"Agreed."

They reached the executive floor.

A staff member was waiting outside Adrian's office.

"Mr. Aurelius, the archive team has sent the recovered document list."

Adrian accepted the tablet.

There were several entries.

Most were routine administrative records.

One was marked as a restricted attachment.

He opened it.

**EFO-SL/07 — SECOND INSTRUCTION**

ARCHIVE ENTRY

**Status:** Dispatched

**Date:** Four days after original maternity identity report

**Recipient:** External liaison

**Delivery confirmation:** Pending

Adrian stopped walking.

Edward came closer.

"What's wrong?"

Adrian turned the tablet towards him.

Edward read the entry.

"Four days later."

"Yes."

"Then the second instruction existed."

Adrian opened the attached delivery record.

The recipient field had been partially damaged.

The delivery location was still visible.

**Valmont Estate — Private Family Office**

Neither man spoke.

The first package had been delivered to the Valmont residence.

Martin Hale had collected it.

And now the second instruction had apparently been sent to the same estate.

Edward looked at Adrian.

"Who received it?"

Adrian enlarged the scanned signature.

The writing was faint.

But the name was readable.

It was not Catherine Valmont.

It was not Richard Valmont.

It was not Martin Hale.

The signature belonged to someone else.

Someone who had never been mentioned in the investigation.

Adrian stared at the name.

"Do you know this person?"

Edward shook his head.

"No."

Adrian turned the tablet towards him again.

At the bottom of the delivery record was a handwritten note.

*Delivered personally. Recipient instructed that the contents must not be entered into the family correspondence register.*

The name above it was:

**MARGARET ELLIS.**

The former maternity nurse.

The same woman who had first revealed that someone had interfered with the hospital records.

The same woman who had kept the photograph and the original instruction for eighteen years.

And apparently, the person who had received the second instruction.
