# **Chapter 61 — The Signature That Belonged to Nobody**

Edward called Margaret Voss again.

She did not answer.

He waited five minutes and tried once more.

Nothing.

Jonathan, who had been reviewing the Category 17 documents, looked up.

"What's happened?"

"She says she has the original signature from the placement register."

"And?"

"She claims to have proof that Victoria Aurelius didn't sign it."

Jonathan put down his pen.

"Then why isn't she giving us the document?"

"She said she wants to speak to Victoria first."

Jonathan frowned.

"After eighteen years of secrecy, she suddenly wants to speak to the mother?"

"Apparently."

"Do you believe her?"

Edward looked at the telephone.

"I believe she has something."

"That's not the same thing."

"No. It isn't."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was reading a report when Adrian entered.

"Edward called."

She looked up.

"Did he find Margaret?"

"Yes. She has agreed to disclose another document."

"Which one?"

"The original Category 17 signature."

Victoria placed her report aside.

"Why is that important?"

"Because the register contains an instruction bearing your name."

Victoria's expression changed.

"My name?"

"Yes."

"What instruction?"

"Apparently, the person who authorised the original receiving placements used your identity."

Victoria stood.

"I never authorised any receiving placements."

"We know."

"Then the signature is false."

"Margaret says she can prove that."

Victoria looked towards Alexander, who had entered behind Adrian.

"Why would someone use my name?"

Alexander answered carefully.

"To make the instruction appear to come from you."

"But why?"

"Perhaps they believed the children would be easier to move if the receiving guardians thought their mother had authorised it."

Victoria looked horrified.

"Someone made people believe I had given away my children?"

Alexander did not answer.

He had spent eighteen years searching for a daughter whose identity and location had been concealed from him.

Now it appeared that Victoria's name had been used to conceal the same truth from her.

Adrian spoke quietly.

"Margaret wants to meet you."

Victoria nodded.

"Arrange it."

Alexander looked at her.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"She may have information that is difficult to hear."

"I know."

Victoria picked up the report.

"But if someone used my name to decide where my children went, I want to know who."

The meeting was arranged at a neutral legal office.

Margaret arrived with a small leather case.

She looked older than she had during her previous interviews, and her hands trembled slightly as she opened the case.

Victoria sat opposite her.

Alexander and Adrian remained nearby.

Edward and Jonathan were also present.

Margaret removed a folded document.

"I have kept this for eighteen years," she said.

Victoria looked at it.

"Why?"

"Because I was told that if I ever released it, I had to release the entire record."

"What entire record?"

"The original authority, the verification report, and the correction."

Margaret placed three sheets on the table.

Edward examined the first.

It was an authorisation form.

**ORIGINATING AUTHORITY: VICTORIA AURELIUS**

**REFERENCE: EFO-SL/07**

**PURPOSE: TEMPORARY PROTECTION AND SEPARATE PLACEMENT OF TWO INFANTS.**

The signature beneath the name looked convincing.

Victoria stared at it.

"That's not my signature."

Margaret nodded.

"I know."

Edward examined the second page.

It was a verification report from the legal office.

**SIGNATURE VERIFIED AGAINST ORIGINAL FAMILY AUTHORITY.**

The verification date was the same day as the authorisation.

But the report contained an unusual detail.

The verification had not been performed by a member of the Aurelius Family Office.

It had been completed by an external legal representative.

**VERIFIED BY: G. VALE.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Gabriel."

Victoria read the name.

"Gabriel Vale?"

"Yes," Edward replied.

"He's the man who took Infant A."

Edward nodded.

Victoria looked back at Margaret.

"Did he know the signature was false?"

"I don't know."

"Then why did he verify it?"

Margaret looked down.

"Because he was told that it was genuine."

The third document was a correction.

It had been issued six days after the original authorisation.

The heading read:

**AUTHORITY VERIFICATION FAILURE — ORIGINAL SIGNATURE NOT AUTHENTIC.**

Below it was a short statement.

*The person named as the originating representative did not personally authorise the placement.*

*The authority reference was used without the knowledge or consent of the named family member.*

*All receiving instructions issued under this authority must remain provisional until the original representative is independently identified.*

Victoria covered her mouth.

"Someone discovered the forgery."

Edward nodded.

"Six days after the first authorisation."

"Then why did the transfers continue?" Alexander asked.

Margaret looked at him.

"Because the correction was never delivered to the hospital."

"Who received it?"

"Charles Vale."

"And what did he do?"

"He placed it in the restricted archive."

Edward looked at the document.

"Why did you keep it?"

"Because Eleanor told me that one day the families would need to see it."

Victoria looked at Margaret.

"Did Eleanor know who forged my signature?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No."

"Did Gabriel?"

"I don't know."

"Did Charles?"

Margaret hesitated.

"Charles knew the signature was false."

The room became silent.

Alexander stood.

"You're certain?"

Margaret looked at him.

"Yes."

"How?"

"Because I heard him say it."

Edward took out his notebook.

"When?"

"On the sixth day."

"Where?"

"In the private records room at the hospital."

"Who else was present?"

"Gabriel. Eleanor. And me."

"What did Charles say?"

Margaret's voice became quieter.

"He said the authority was never meant to be permanent. He said the signature was only needed to get the children into temporary protection."

Edward looked at her.

"And what did Eleanor say?"

"She asked who had signed it."

Margaret paused.

"Charles told her that the person who signed it had already been promised something."

Victoria stared at her.

"Promised what?"

"I don't know."

"Did he name the person?"

Margaret shook her head.

"He said the arrangement had been made before the births."

Edward leaned forward.

"Then the forgery was planned before the children were born."

Margaret nodded.

"Yes."

The Aurelius family returned home with copies of all three documents.

Alexander immediately requested an independent forensic examination of the signature.

Adrian began reviewing the original Family Office access logs.

Victoria remained in the library.

She had placed the forged authorisation on the table.

For several minutes, she simply looked at it.

Alexander entered.

"Victoria."

She did not turn.

"Someone signed my name."

"Yes."

"Someone used it to move two newborn children."

"We don't yet know whether the instruction was used to move them or only to arrange temporary protection."

Victoria turned towards him.

"Does that distinction matter to the children?"

Alexander had no immediate answer.

She looked back at the document.

"I remember asking the hospital where our daughter was."

Alexander stood beside her.

"I remember."

"They told me she was receiving additional medical care."

"Yes."

"They told me not to worry."

"I know."

Victoria's voice trembled.

"And somewhere in those same days, someone was arranging to send her away under my name."

Alexander placed a hand on her shoulder.

"We'll find out who."

Victoria nodded.

But she did not look reassured.

Edward's examination of the verification documents revealed something else.

The correction had been signed by a senior legal officer at Vale Private Counsel.

The signature belonged to Charles Vale.

That meant Charles had formally acknowledged that Victoria's authority was false.

Yet he had continued to retain the documents under the same reference.

Edward requested every subsequent instruction issued under EFO-SL/07.

The results were extensive.

There were thirty-two separate instructions.

Some concerned medical care.

Some concerned temporary accommodation.

Others concerned identity restrictions, financial support, and the movement of the infants.

The earliest instructions were clearly provisional.

The later ones increasingly treated the placements as permanent.

Edward arranged them chronologically.

Then he noticed a gap.

Between the sixth-day correction and the ninth-day release, there was one missing instruction.

The register contained a reference to it.

But the document itself was absent.

**EFO-SL/07 — ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE AMENDMENT.**

**DATE: NINTH DAY.**

**STATUS: EXECUTED.**

Edward stared at the entry.

"Who executed it?"

Jonathan checked the record.

"No name."

"Who received it?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward looked up.

"Find it."

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was preparing the financial documents for her Singapore programme.

Catherine had offered to review them.

Elena had insisted on completing the forms herself first.

"You're sure you don't want me to fill in the family financial section?" Catherine asked.

"I can do it."

"I know you can."

"Then let me."

Catherine smiled faintly.

"All right."

Elena continued typing.

After a moment, she asked, "Do you think Isabelle will apply for the same programme next year?"

Catherine looked towards the garden.

"She hasn't decided."

"She might enjoy it."

"Perhaps."

Elena paused.

"Would you be comfortable with both of us being away?"

Catherine looked at her.

"That would be difficult."

Elena nodded.

"But I wouldn't want you to stop us."

Catherine approached the table.

"I wouldn't."

Elena looked up.

Catherine gently touched her shoulder.

"You have both spent enough time worrying about what the family needs. I want you to discover what you want."

Elena smiled.

"Thank you."

Catherine returned to her work.

The two girls continued with their applications.

For Elena, the future was becoming something she could plan rather than something that happened to her.

Three days later, the missing ninth-day amendment was located.

It had been stored in a separate legal archive under a different reference.

Edward opened the scanned document.

The first paragraph was familiar.

*The original temporary arrangement is no longer considered sufficient.*

The second paragraph was more concerning.

*The receiving guardians may be informed that the placements have been authorised by the originating family representative.*

Edward stopped reading.

The document was using Victoria's name again.

But the final paragraph contained something new.

*The identity of the person who authorised this amendment must remain confidential until the children reach an age at which they can understand the circumstances of their birth.*

Edward read it twice.

Jonathan leaned closer.

"Does it name the person?"

Edward scrolled down.

There was a signature.

Not Victoria's.

Not Alexander's.

Not Charles's.

It was a short, unfamiliar signature.

**I. VALE.**

Jonathan frowned.

"Who is I. Vale?"

Edward searched the legal archive.

There were several people with that surname and initial.

But one record matched the date.

A former employee of Vale Private Counsel.

A woman who had worked in confidential family administration.

Her personnel file contained a single surviving photograph.

Edward opened it.

The woman was in her early thirties.

Her hair was tied back.

She wore a plain dark blouse.

And on her left hand was a thin scar across the base of her thumb.

Edward looked at the photograph again.

It was the same woman Margaret Voss had described.

The woman who had called herself Mrs. Aurelius.

The woman who had collected Eleanor's papers.

The woman who had claimed to be the mother of one of the children.

Her full name was recorded beneath the photograph.

**ISABELLA VALE.**

Edward called Margaret Voss immediately.

"Do you know Isabella Vale?"

There was a long silence.

Then Margaret answered.

"Yes."

"Who was she?"

"She worked for Charles."

"Was she the woman who visited Eleanor?"

"Yes."

"Was she the person who signed the ninth-day amendment?"

Margaret did not answer.

Edward repeated the question.

"Was she?"

"Yes."

"Did she claim to be the mother of one of the children?"

"She did."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

"Which child?"

Margaret's voice became almost a whisper.

"Infant A."

Edward looked at the photograph.

"Did she have any biological connection to the Aurelius family?"

Margaret answered immediately.

"No."

"Then why did she claim to be the mother?"

Margaret took a breath.

"Because she believed she had given birth to that child."

Edward stopped.

"Are you saying Isabella Vale was pregnant?"

"No."

"Then what are you saying?"

Margaret's answer was quiet but devastating.

"She believed that the child had been taken from her."

Edward looked at the ninth-day amendment.

The document that had used Victoria's authority.

The woman who claimed to be the mother.

The infant who had been moved outside both families.

Suddenly, the question was no longer simply who had taken Infant A.

It was why Isabella Vale believed the child had once belonged to her.

And who had convinced her of it.
