# **I Was Kicked Out for Being a Fake Heiress. Why Is an Entire Dynasty Looking for Me?**

## **Volume 1: The Fake Heiress**

# **Chapter 2 — The Girl Who Was Supposed to Be Their Daughter**

The Valmont mansion had never been particularly quiet.

Even during the early hours of the morning, there was usually some activity taking place somewhere in the enormous residence.

The kitchen staff prepared breakfast. The gardeners tended to the eastern grounds. Security personnel changed shifts, and the household manager reviewed the day's schedule.

But that morning, something unusual had happened.

Everyone seemed to be in a hurry.

"Mr. Harris, have the guest rooms been inspected?"

"Yes, Madam."

"Which room has been prepared?"

"The eastern guest suite, Madam."

Catherine paused.

"The eastern suite?"

"Yes. It has been prepared according to the instructions received from the chairman."

Catherine considered that for a moment.

"Perhaps the blue suite would be more appropriate."

Mr. Harris waited.

"The blue suite has better natural lighting," she explained. "And it's closer to the family sitting room."

"Of course, Madam. I'll arrange it."

Catherine nodded.

She looked towards the staircase.

"Also, please make sure the flowers are fresh."

"Certainly."

"And ask the kitchen to prepare a few different dishes. We don't know her preferences yet."

"I'll inform the head chef."

Catherine hesitated.

"Thank you, Mr. Harris."

The household manager bowed slightly before leaving.

Catherine remained in the hallway.

She looked towards the staircase again.

For eighteen years, she had imagined what it would be like to welcome her biological daughter home.

She had imagined a little girl running towards her.

A small hand holding hers.

A child calling her Mother.

She had imagined birthdays, school performances, family holidays and all the ordinary moments that mothers usually took for granted.

But the girl arriving today was eighteen years old.

She had grown up somewhere else.

She had another childhood, another home and another set of memories.

Catherine pressed her fingers against her forehead.

She needed to remember that.

She couldn't expect an eighteen-year-old stranger to immediately become the daughter she had spent eighteen years missing.

Even if that was exactly what her heart wanted.

### **8:15 a.m. — Elena's Bedroom**

Elena was sitting on the floor, surrounded by books.

There were three open notebooks, a textbook, two highlighters and a half-finished cup of tea beside her.

Her laptop displayed a university assignment.

She stared at the screen.

Then at the notebook.

Then back at the screen.

Finally, she sighed.

"Why is this subject so unnecessarily complicated?"

A knock sounded at the door.

"Come in!"

The door opened.

Nicholas entered carrying a small paper bag.

"Good morning."

Elena looked up.

"Good morning. What's that?"

"Breakfast."

She glanced towards the clock.

"I already ate."

"That's not breakfast."

He placed the bag on her desk.

Inside was a box of pastries from a bakery near his office.

Elena immediately recognised the packaging.

"Did you go there?"

"Yes."

"That's on the other side of the city."

"I had a meeting nearby."

She narrowed her eyes.

"Your office is in the opposite direction."

Nicholas smiled.

"Geography is a complicated subject."

Elena laughed.

She picked up the box.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

He looked at the books scattered around her.

"Studying?"

"Trying to."

"How much have you completed?"

"Two pages."

"How long have you been working?"

"An hour."

Nicholas stared at her.

Elena stared back.

"Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"Your face has."

He laughed and sat on the edge of her bed.

For a few moments, neither spoke.

Then Nicholas glanced at her.

"Are you nervous about today?"

Elena stopped opening the pastry box.

"A little."

"Why?"

She shrugged.

"I don't know what to expect."

"She's just another person."

"I know."

"Then don't worry."

Elena looked at him.

"Are you nervous?"

Nicholas blinked.

"Me?"

"Yes."

He leaned back.

"Why would I be nervous?"

"Because you're meeting your biological sister."

Nicholas looked towards the window.

For the first time that morning, his expression became thoughtful.

"Honestly?"

Elena nodded.

"I don't know."

He rubbed the back of his neck.

"It's strange. We've spent our entire lives believing there were four children in this family. Now we're being told that there were actually five people involved in this situation, and one of them grew up somewhere else."

Elena smiled faintly.

"That's a very complicated way of saying you're confused."

"Thank you for your valuable analysis."

"You're welcome."

Nicholas looked at her again.

"But I suppose she's probably just as nervous."

Elena nodded.

"I think so too."

He stood.

"Anyway, don't spend the entire day studying."

"I have an assignment."

"Your assignment will still exist tomorrow."

"Unfortunately."

Nicholas picked up an empty notebook from the floor and placed it on the desk.

"Come downstairs later. Mother wants everyone to have lunch together."

"All right."

He walked towards the door.

Then stopped.

"Elena?"

"Yes?"

"Nothing changes between us because of a medical report."

She looked at him.

Nicholas gave her a familiar smile.

"You're still my annoying little sister."

Elena laughed.

"You're still my annoying older brother."

"Good."

He left.

Elena looked at the closed door for a few seconds.

Then she opened the pastry box.

The pastries were still warm.

### **9:40 a.m. — The Blue Suite**

Catherine stood inside the newly prepared guest suite.

The room was spacious, decorated in soft blue and cream colours.

There was a large bed, a sitting area, a private balcony and a walk-in wardrobe.

Fresh flowers stood on the bedside table.

A selection of toiletries had been arranged in the bathroom.

Everything was perfect.

And yet Catherine felt strangely dissatisfied.

"Madam?"

She turned.

Mr. Harris was standing near the entrance.

"Yes?"

"Would you like anything else arranged?"

Catherine looked around.

"No. I think this is fine."

She approached the wardrobe.

"Do we know her clothing sizes?"

"Not yet."

"Then don't prepare anything unnecessary. We can arrange shopping later if she needs something."

"Of course."

Catherine touched the edge of the bed.

"Has she been informed about the arrangements?"

"The young lady has been informed that she will be staying with the Valmont family."

Catherine nodded.

Then she asked quietly:

"Did she sound happy?"

Mr. Harris hesitated.

"I couldn't say, Madam."

Catherine looked down.

"Of course."

She understood.

The girl had spent eighteen years away from them.

A beautiful bedroom wouldn't change that.

She turned towards the window.

"Mr. Harris?"

"Yes, Madam?"

"Please don't call her a guest."

He waited.

"She's our daughter."

Catherine's voice trembled slightly.

"Even if everything is complicated, she is still our daughter."

Mr. Harris lowered his head.

"Understood, Madam."

### **11:30 a.m. — The Valmont Family Office**

Richard Valmont was reviewing a confidential report when Sebastian entered his office.

"Father."

Richard looked up.

"Sit."

Sebastian took the chair opposite him.

He placed a folder on the desk.

"The legal department has completed its preliminary review."

Richard opened it.

"Any problems?"

"Several."

Richard looked at him.

"Explain."

Sebastian opened his tablet.

"The hospital records contain multiple inconsistencies. The original birth registration appears to have been altered, and the identification numbers in the supplementary documents don't match the hospital's archived records."

Richard's expression hardened.

"How extensive?"

"Enough that we cannot treat this as a simple administrative error."

"Has the hospital been contacted?"

"Yes. They're conducting an internal investigation."

Richard read through the documents.

"Who authorised the DNA examination?"

"The family office arranged it after the initial discrepancies were discovered."

Richard nodded.

"Good."

Sebastian hesitated.

"There's something else."

Richard looked up.

"The biological daughter."

"What about her?"

"She has been living with another family. Her circumstances are not particularly well documented."

"Have we received the complete background report?"

"Not yet."

Richard closed the folder.

"Then don't make assumptions."

Sebastian nodded.

Richard leaned back.

"This situation is already complicated enough. We need to make sure the girl's transition is handled properly."

Sebastian was quiet.

After a moment, he asked:

"What about Elena?"

Richard looked towards the window.

"What about her?"

"She's been here for eighteen years."

"I am aware."

"She's going to be affected by this."

Richard's expression became unreadable.

"She will continue to be cared for."

Sebastian nodded, but he wasn't entirely satisfied.

He had spent his entire life regarding Elena as his little sister.

The DNA report had introduced a fact.

It had not erased eighteen years of memories.

He stood.

"There's one more thing."

Richard waited.

"Elena has a university examination next week. We should make sure this situation doesn't interfere with her studies."

Richard nodded.

"Agreed."

Sebastian left the office.

Richard remained seated.

He opened the folder again.

Two names were printed on the first page.

Elena Valmont.

Isabelle Valmont.

For the first time, he realised that the situation wasn't going to be resolved by simply correcting a family record.

### **12:45 p.m. — Lunch**

Elena entered the dining room carrying her phone.

Nicholas was already seated.

Edward was reading a report.

Sebastian had returned from the office.

Catherine was speaking to the head chef.

"You're late," Nicholas said.

Elena checked the clock.

"It's twelve forty-five."

"Exactly."

"You're acting like I've arrived at midnight."

"Time management is important."

Elena sat beside him.

"Then perhaps you should stop spending twenty minutes deciding which dessert to order."

Edward didn't look up.

"He's been doing that since childhood."

Nicholas pointed at him.

"Nobody asked you."

Catherine turned towards Elena.

"Did you finish your assignment?"

"Almost."

"Good. Don't forget to take a break."

"I will."

The head chef entered.

"Madam, lunch is ready."

The dishes were served.

For a while, the conversation remained ordinary.

Then Catherine spoke.

"Isabelle will arrive at three."

Elena looked up.

"All right."

"Her representative informed us that she will be accompanied by a family adviser."

"Okay."

Catherine hesitated.

"Would you prefer to meet her privately first?"

Elena considered the question.

"Wouldn't that make things more awkward?"

"Perhaps."

"I think everyone should be there."

Catherine nodded.

"Very well."

Nicholas glanced at Elena.

"You don't have to force yourself to be friendly."

Elena looked confused.

"I'm not forcing myself."

"I mean, if you're uncomfortable, you can simply say so."

She thought about it.

"I don't think she's done anything wrong."

Nicholas didn't respond.

Elena continued eating.

Edward finally put down his report.

"That's true."

He looked at her.

"The circumstances are unfortunate, but she didn't cause them."

Elena nodded.

"Exactly."

Nicholas sighed.

"Both of you are making it very difficult for me to be angry at the situation."

Elena smiled.

"Then don't be angry."

"That's easier said than done."

### **2:18 p.m. — A Car Approaches the Valmont Estate**

The black sedan moved through the private entrance of the Valmont property.

Inside, Isabelle sat beside a middle-aged woman who had accompanied her during the legal proceedings.

She was wearing a light-coloured dress.

Her hands were folded neatly in her lap.

She had barely spoken during the journey.

The woman beside her glanced over.

"Are you all right?"

Isabelle nodded.

"Yes."

"You're very quiet."

"I'm thinking."

"About what?"

Isabelle looked through the window.

The mansion appeared in the distance.

It was enormous.

White stone walls, tall windows, carefully maintained gardens and a long driveway leading towards the main entrance.

She had seen photographs.

But photographs had not prepared her for the scale of the property.

"Is this really where I was supposed to grow up?"

The woman followed her gaze.

"According to the evidence, yes."

Isabelle swallowed.

She had spent eighteen years living in a completely different environment.

Her childhood had not been particularly luxurious.

She had attended ordinary schools, lived in a modest house and learned early that money had to be managed carefully.

She had never considered herself unfortunate.

Her life had been ordinary.

Now she was being told that she was the biological daughter of Richard and Catherine Valmont.

The youngest daughter of a wealthy business family.

She had imagined this moment many times.

She had imagined meeting her parents.

She had imagined having a room of her own.

She had imagined discovering what her life might have been.

But she had not imagined meeting another girl who had lived in her place for eighteen years.

"What is she like?" Isabelle asked.

The adviser turned.

"Who?"

"Elena."

The woman considered her answer.

"I haven't met her personally."

"Is she... upset?"

"I don't know."

Isabelle looked down at her hands.

"I wouldn't know what to say to her."

The car slowed.

The mansion gates opened.

The vehicle continued towards the entrance.

The adviser placed a hand lightly on Isabelle's shoulder.

"You don't need to resolve everything today."

Isabelle nodded.

The car came to a stop.

### **3:02 p.m. — The First Meeting**

Mr. Harris opened the main entrance.

"Welcome to the Valmont residence."

Isabelle stepped out of the car.

She looked up at the mansion.

For a moment, she forgot to move.

The building was even more impressive from the entrance.

A broad staircase led towards the main doors.

The gardens stretched out on either side.

Several staff members stood nearby.

Everything appeared carefully arranged.

Then she noticed the people waiting inside.

Richard Valmont stood at the centre.

Catherine was beside him.

Sebastian, Nicholas and Edward stood behind their parents.

And slightly to the side—

A young woman with long dark hair.

Elena.

Isabelle looked at her.

Elena looked back.

Neither girl spoke immediately.

Catherine stepped forward.

"Isabelle?"

Isabelle turned towards her.

"Yes."

Catherine's eyes filled with tears.

"Oh, sweetheart."

She approached slowly.

Then she stopped, apparently unsure whether she should embrace her.

Isabelle noticed.

For a moment, neither moved.

Finally, Catherine opened her arms.

Isabelle stepped forward.

The embrace was gentle and somewhat awkward.

Catherine closed her eyes.

"Welcome home."

Isabelle's expression changed.

She nodded against her mother's shoulder.

"Thank you."

Richard approached next.

"I'm Richard Valmont."

Isabelle looked at him.

"Father."

Richard paused.

Then he nodded.

"Welcome."

He offered his hand.

Isabelle shook it.

Sebastian introduced himself.

Then Nicholas.

Then Edward.

Each greeting was polite.

Each was accompanied by a slightly different expression.

Nicholas smiled warmly.

Edward nodded.

Sebastian remained reserved.

Then Catherine turned.

"And this is Elena."

Elena stepped forward.

"Hello."

Isabelle looked at her.

For the first time, she was standing face-to-face with the girl who had grown up in the place that should have been hers.

Elena smiled.

"I'm Elena."

Isabelle returned the greeting.

"Isabelle."

There was a brief silence.

Then Elena spoke.

"Would you like something to drink?"

Isabelle blinked.

"What?"

"Tea? Water? Juice?"

Nicholas turned his face away.

He was trying not to laugh.

Elena looked at him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She looked back at Isabelle.

"Sorry. It's just that you've probably had a long journey."

Isabelle nodded.

"Water would be fine."

"All right."

Elena turned towards the staff.

"Could we have some water, please?"

The household attendant nodded.

Catherine watched the exchange.

She had expected tension.

She had expected awkwardness.

She had even prepared herself for tears.

She hadn't expected Elena to behave like a hostess welcoming a visitor.

Perhaps that was simply how Elena handled uncomfortable situations.

### **3:20 p.m. — The Living Room**

The family gathered in the main living room.

Isabelle sat beside Catherine.

Elena occupied an armchair opposite them.

Richard took his usual seat.

The brothers sat nearby.

Tea and refreshments were served.

For several minutes, the conversation remained formal.

Catherine asked Isabelle about her journey.

Richard asked whether she had any immediate needs.

Sebastian explained that the family office would help with the remaining legal formalities.

Isabelle answered politely.

She seemed overwhelmed by the attention.

Elena watched quietly.

She noticed that Isabelle kept glancing around the room.

Perhaps she was comparing the mansion with the place where she had grown up.

Perhaps she was imagining what it would be like to live there.

Elena couldn't know.

Finally, Isabelle looked towards her.

"Can I ask you something?"

Elena straightened.

"Of course."

"How long have you lived here?"

Elena considered the question.

"Since I was born."

Isabelle nodded.

"Do you like it?"

Elena smiled.

"Yes. It's a nice house."

Isabelle looked down.

"Do you have a favourite place?"

"The library."

"Why?"

"It's quiet."

Nicholas interrupted.

"That's because nobody is allowed to enter when you're studying."

Elena frowned.

"That's not true."

"You once locked me out."

"You were making noise."

"I was breathing."

"Very loudly."

Edward shook his head.

"That argument lasted three hours."

Isabelle watched them.

For the first time, she saw something she hadn't expected.

They were comfortable with each other.

Not simply polite.

They had years of shared memories.

Little jokes and habits that couldn't be explained to someone who had just arrived.

Isabelle lowered her eyes.

She had imagined returning to her biological family.

She hadn't considered what it would mean to enter a family that already existed.

### **4:05 p.m. — The Bedroom**

Catherine offered to show Isabelle the prepared suite.

Elena remained downstairs.

Nicholas sat beside her.

"That went better than I expected."

Elena nodded.

"She's nice."

"You think so?"

"She seemed nervous."

Nicholas looked towards the staircase.

"Everyone is nervous."

Elena smiled.

"You're nervous."

"I'm not."

"You've been eating the same biscuit for ten minutes."

Nicholas looked at his hand.

He was, in fact, holding the same biscuit.

He took a bite.

"Problem solved."

Elena laughed.

Upstairs, Catherine opened the blue suite.

"This room has been prepared for you."

Isabelle entered slowly.

She looked around.

The room was spacious.

There was a large bed, a writing desk, a walk-in wardrobe and a balcony overlooking the gardens.

Fresh flowers had been arranged on the bedside table.

Everything looked beautiful.

"Do you like it?" Catherine asked.

Isabelle nodded.

"It's lovely."

Catherine smiled.

"If you need anything, just let me know."

Isabelle looked towards the wardrobe.

"Is this my room?"

"Yes, sweetheart."

She turned back.

"Can I really stay here?"

Catherine's expression softened.

"Of course."

Isabelle looked down.

"Thank you."

Catherine approached her.

"You don't need to thank us for giving you your home."

Isabelle nodded.

But she still felt like a visitor.

### **5:30 p.m. — An Unexpected Conversation**

Elena was in the library when Isabelle entered.

She was returning a book to the shelf.

Isabelle paused near the entrance.

"Sorry. Am I disturbing you?"

Elena turned.

"No. Come in."

Isabelle stepped inside.

Her eyes moved across the shelves.

The library was enormous.

There were thousands of books arranged across several levels.

A spiral staircase connected the upper galleries.

"Do you spend a lot of time here?" Isabelle asked.

"Quite a bit."

Elena placed the book back.

"Do you like reading?"

"Sometimes."

"Any particular genre?"

Isabelle considered it.

"Historical fiction. And biographies."

Elena smiled.

"There's an entire section over there."

She pointed towards the far wall.

Isabelle walked towards it.

For a few minutes, they browsed the shelves.

Then Isabelle spoke.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Sure."

"Are you angry with me?"

Elena looked at her.

"Why would I be?"

"Because I came here."

Elena was silent for a moment.

Then she shook her head.

"No."

"But this was your home."

"It still is."

Isabelle looked at her.

Elena continued.

"I mean, you haven't done anything wrong. You didn't ask for this situation."

Isabelle looked down.

"Neither did you."

Elena smiled faintly.

"Exactly."

There was another silence.

Isabelle ran her fingers along the spine of a book.

"I don't know how we're supposed to behave around each other."

Elena considered that.

"Maybe we don't need to decide immediately."

"What do you mean?"

"We've only just met. We can take our time."

Isabelle looked at her.

Elena picked up a book.

"Besides, I don't think anyone knows what they're doing."

Isabelle let out a small laugh.

It was the first time she had laughed since arriving.

Elena smiled.

"See? That's better."

For a little while, they continued browsing the shelves.

Neither girl noticed Catherine standing outside the library entrance.

She had come looking for Isabelle.

Instead, she found the two girls talking quietly.

She remained there for a moment.

Then she walked away without interrupting them.

### **7:10 p.m. — Dinner**

The family gathered in the main dining room.

Isabelle sat beside Catherine.

Elena sat opposite her.

Richard began the meal by discussing the remaining legal arrangements.

"The medical investigation is still ongoing," he explained. "We will need additional documentation before the identity records can be formally corrected."

Isabelle nodded.

"Of course."

"The legal department will assist you."

"Thank you."

Richard looked towards Elena.

"The same applies to you."

Elena nodded.

"All right."

Sebastian placed his glass down.

"We should also discuss the arrangements for both girls."

Nicholas glanced at him.

"Already?"

"It's necessary."

Catherine looked uncomfortable.

"Perhaps we can discuss that later."

Sebastian shook his head.

"We shouldn't leave it unresolved."

Richard considered the matter.

"He's right."

Elena looked between them.

"What arrangements?"

Richard folded his hands.

"Isabelle will be living here. We need to determine how the household will be organised."

Elena nodded.

"Okay."

Catherine looked at her.

"Elena, your father and I want you to understand that nothing needs to change immediately."

Elena smiled.

"I understand."

Richard continued.

"Both of you are our daughters."

The words sounded simple.

But nobody at the table seemed entirely certain how to respond.

Isabelle looked at her plate.

Elena reached for her water.

Nicholas attempted to change the subject.

"So, Isabelle, do you like desserts?"

Everyone looked at him.

He shrugged.

"What? It's an important question."

Isabelle smiled uncertainly.

"Yes."

"Excellent. The kitchen makes very good chocolate cake."

Elena nodded.

"He's particularly interested in that subject."

"I have refined tastes."

"You once ate an entire cake before dinner."

"It was a small cake."

"It was meant for twelve people."

Nicholas pointed his fork at her.

"Unnecessary details."

For a moment, the atmosphere relaxed.

But beneath the conversation, something remained unsettled.

The family had welcomed Isabelle.

They had begun introducing her to their household.

Yet nobody had fully considered what would happen to Elena's place within it.

And that question would become increasingly difficult to avoid.

### **9:25 p.m. — The Eastern Corridor**

After dinner, Elena walked upstairs.

She was carrying a glass of water.

As she passed the blue suite, she heard a faint sound.

Someone was moving furniture.

She stopped.

The door was partially open.

Inside, two household attendants were carrying a small cabinet towards the wall.

Catherine stood nearby.

"Please put it there."

"Yes, Madam."

Elena glanced inside.

The room looked beautiful.

Isabelle's belongings had begun arriving.

Several suitcases stood near the wardrobe.

A collection of books had been arranged on the desk.

Elena smiled faintly.

She was glad Isabelle was settling in.

Then she noticed something.

The small cabinet being moved was familiar.

It had previously stood in Elena's bedroom.

It contained a collection of old family albums.

Elena paused.

"Mother?"

Catherine turned.

"Oh, Elena."

"What are they moving?"

Catherine glanced at the cabinet.

"It's being placed here for Isabelle."

Elena nodded.

"Wasn't that in my room?"

"Yes. We have another cabinet being arranged for you."

"Oh."

Catherine approached.

"Is that all right?"

Elena smiled.

"Of course."

Catherine seemed relieved.

"Thank you, darling."

Elena looked at the cabinet again.

There was nothing particularly important about it.

It was just furniture.

Still, she wondered why the family albums were being moved.

She decided not to ask.

"Good night, Mother."

"Good night."

Elena continued towards her room.

Behind her, the attendants resumed their work.

### **10:40 p.m. — A Conversation Between Brothers**

Nicholas entered Sebastian's study without knocking.

Sebastian looked up.

"Have you forgotten how doors work?"

"No."

"Then use them properly."

Nicholas closed the door.

"You're in a wonderful mood."

"What do you want?"

Nicholas sat down.

"Something about Elena."

Sebastian put down his pen.

"What about her?"

"Have you noticed how quiet she's been?"

Sebastian frowned.

"She's always quiet when she's studying."

"Not like that."

Nicholas leaned back.

"She's been pretending everything is fine."

Sebastian looked at him.

"She hasn't complained."

"Exactly."

Sebastian was silent.

Nicholas continued.

"She's been here for eighteen years. Isabelle arrived today, and suddenly everyone's discussing arrangements, rooms and documents."

Sebastian sighed.

"We have to handle the situation properly."

"I know."

"Then what are you suggesting?"

Nicholas hesitated.

"Nothing. I just don't want her to feel like she's being replaced."

Sebastian looked towards the window.

"I don't want that either."

"Then tell Father."

Sebastian rubbed his forehead.

"He's already aware."

"Does he understand?"

Sebastian didn't answer.

Nicholas stood.

"Think about it."

He walked towards the door.

Before leaving, he turned around.

"And don't forget that she's still our sister."

The door closed.

Sebastian remained seated.

He looked at the family photograph on his desk.

It showed all three brothers with Elena.

She was sitting on Nicholas's shoulders, laughing.

Sebastian had taken the photograph himself.

He remembered the day clearly.

He had also remembered thinking that their family was complete.

Now he wasn't sure what that word meant anymore.

### **11:18 p.m. — The First Night**

Isabelle sat on the edge of her new bed.

The room was quiet.

She had unpacked most of her belongings.

Her clothes were arranged in the wardrobe.

Her books were on the desk.

Her phone was charging beside the bed.

Everything she had been promised was real.

She was finally living with her biological parents.

She had three older brothers.

A beautiful home.

A family name that carried enormous influence.

Yet she couldn't sleep.

She kept thinking about Elena.

The other girl had been surprisingly kind.

She hadn't behaved like someone who resented her.

In fact, Elena had seemed more concerned about making her comfortable than protecting her own position.

That confused Isabelle.

She had expected hostility.

She had prepared herself for arguments.

Instead, she had received a glass of water, a tour of the library and a conversation about books.

She looked towards the bedroom door.

Somewhere in the same mansion, Elena was sleeping.

Isabelle wondered whether the other girl was thinking about her.

Then she looked at the ceiling.

For eighteen years, she had been told that her biological family was somewhere else.

Now she was here.

But she was also entering a life that already belonged to someone else.

She pulled the blanket over herself.

Tomorrow would be another day.

### **11:47 p.m. — Elena's Bedroom**

Elena was awake.

She was sitting at her desk, looking at the family photographs.

Her room had not changed.

Her books were still there.

Her clothes were still in the wardrobe.

Her university schedule remained on the noticeboard.

But the house felt different.

She had spent the day meeting Isabelle.

She had spoken to her, shown her the library and watched her settle into the mansion.

Isabelle seemed nice.

That was good.

Elena didn't want her to feel unwelcome.

Still, she couldn't ignore the strange sensation that something was shifting around her.

She picked up the photograph from her desk.

The one taken during the family holiday.

Nicholas was smiling.

Edward was holding his toy airplane.

Sebastian looked annoyed.

Her parents stood behind them.

Elena was in the middle.

She studied the photograph for a long time.

Then she placed it back.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from her mother.

**Catherine:**

*Good night, darling. I love you.*

Elena smiled.

**Elena:**

*Good night, Mother. Love you too.*

She turned off the desk lamp.

Then she climbed into bed.

For now, she was still Elena Valmont.

Tomorrow, she would attend university, complete her assignment and continue with her ordinary life.

She had no idea how long that ordinary life would last.

### **Somewhere Else — A Private Medical Archive**

A man in a dark suit stood in front of a filing cabinet.

He held a photocopy of an old hospital record.

The document contained several identification numbers.

One of them had been circled in red.

He examined it carefully.

Then he placed the document inside a separate folder.

His phone rang.

He answered.

"Yes?"

A voice spoke from the other end.

"Have you found anything?"

"Possibly."

"What is it?"

"The original transfer record is missing, but I've located a secondary registration."

"Does it identify the children?"

"Not conclusively."

A pause.

"Continue investigating."

The man looked down at the document.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"The original registration appears to have been modified after the hospital submitted its first report."

Silence.

Then the voice returned.

"Keep this confidential."

The call ended.

The man remained in the archive.

He looked at the date printed on the document.

Eighteen years earlier.

Someone had altered the records.

Someone had removed evidence.

And whatever had happened that night had not been a simple administrative mistake.
