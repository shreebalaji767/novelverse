# **Chapter 186 — The Appointment That Had No Successor**

Marianne Vale's appointment instrument was not in the Governance Secretariat's ordinary personnel archive.

Edward discovered this at 8:12.

The personnel office had a record of her employment, her department, and her position.

It did not have the instrument defining her restricted appointment authority.

Instead, the archive contained a reference.

**GOV/RA/41 — Restricted Appointments Coordination**

The reference had been entered on day 38.

Its status was:

**Appointment instrument held separately.**

Edward read the line.

“Where?”

Sophie checked the archive index.

“Restricted Governance Secretariat records.”

“Can we request it?”

“We already have.”

“And?”

“They've asked us to specify the purpose of the request.”

Edward looked at her.

“Tell them we need to establish whether the coordinator had authority to appoint a successor to the vacant control liaison position.”

Sophie sent the clarification.

Edward returned to the day-155 note.

Marianne Vale's office had received Helen Sloane's warning.

The note stated that the shared desk could not replace the vacant control liaison function.

It also stated that continued access required a competent supervising authority.

If the coordinator had authority to appoint such an authority, her role might have been relevant.

If she did not, her office might simply have been another recipient of the warning.

Either possibility mattered.

But neither could be assumed.

At 9:30, the Governance Secretariat responded.

The appointment instrument could be inspected at its restricted records office.

It could not be copied or transferred without approval from the original appointing authority.

Edward requested an inspection appointment.

The confirmation arrived within minutes.

**Day 186 — 11:00**

**Location:** Governance Secretariat, Restricted Appointments Office.

He and Sophie went together.

The office was on a floor they had visited before, but the reception procedure was different.

The receptionist checked their authorisation letters and asked them to leave their phones in a secure cabinet.

Then she led them into a small records room.

A sealed folder waited on the table.

Its label read:

**GOV/RA/41 — M. VALE**

Edward examined the seal.

It was intact.

A records officer watched as he opened it.

Inside was a six-page appointment instrument.

The first page identified Marianne Vale as the Restricted Appointments Coordinator.

The appointment had been issued on day 38.

Its scope included:

- Maintaining the register of restricted appointments.
- Receiving proposed appointment instruments.
- Checking whether proposed appointments contained the required authority references.
- Recording acceptance, expiry, suspension, and withdrawal.
- Referring incomplete appointments to the originating authority.

Edward read the final paragraph.

**The coordinator shall not appoint, extend, substitute, or revoke a substantive authority unless expressly delegated by the originating appointing office.**

He looked at Sophie.

“So she maintained the register.”

“Not the appointments themselves.”

Edward continued.

A second paragraph was more specific.

**Where an appointed control function becomes vacant, the coordinator shall record the vacancy and notify the originating office. No interim successor shall be presumed from continued administrative activity.**

He turned to the final page.

The appointment had an expiry date.

Day 180.

Marianne's role was still active.

But it was limited.

She could record a vacancy.

She could notify the originating authority.

She could not fill the vacancy herself.

Edward looked at the records officer.

“Was Marianne Vale responsible for notifying the originating office about Helen Sloane's departure?”

“Yes, under the appointment register procedure.”

“Was the notification issued?”

The officer checked a separate index.

“There is a notification record.”

“May I see it?”

The officer produced a single-page extract.

**DAY 149 — VACANCY NOTIFICATION**

**Position:** Control Liaison\
**Former appointee:** Helen Sloane\
**Effective vacancy date:** Day 148\
**Notification recipient:** Originating Governance Authority\
**Status:** Delivered\
**Successor appointment:** Not recorded

Edward read the final field.

“Was a response received?”

The officer checked.

“No response is recorded.”

“Was a follow-up issued?”

“Not in this register.”

Edward looked at Sophie.

The vacancy had been formally recorded.

The originating authority had been notified.

But no successor had been appointed.

The shared desk had continued operating under an administrative chart that described the control function as active.

The appointment register had recorded the vacancy.

The system had preserved the appearance of continuity.

But the authority itself had disappeared.

Marianne Vale agreed to speak with them after the inspection.

She arrived carrying a small notebook.

“I understand you've been reviewing the control liaison vacancy,” she said.

“Yes,” Edward replied.

“Then I should clarify my role before we begin. I maintain the appointment register. I don't make the appointments.”

“We've read your instrument.”

“Good.”

Edward placed the day-149 notification before her.

“You recorded Helen Sloane's departure.”

“Yes.”

“You notified the originating authority.”

“Yes.”

“Did you receive a response?”

“No.”

“Did you follow up?”

Marianne opened her notebook.

“I issued a reminder on day 153.”

Edward looked up.

“Where is it?”

“The register contains the reference.”

She provided it.

**DAY 153 — FOLLOW-UP NOTICE**

**Subject:** Control Liaison Vacancy — Successor Appointment Required

**Recipient:** Originating Governance Authority

**Response:** None recorded.

Edward examined the dispatch record.

The reminder had been delivered.

“Did you notify the shared desk that the position remained vacant?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because that was not part of my appointment function. The shared desk was not an appointing authority. It had no reason to receive a successor-appointment notice.”

“But the shared desk continued performing tasks associated with the position.”

“Then someone should have recognised that the function was vacant.”

Edward considered her answer.

“Did you know the shared desk was being treated as a replacement?”

“I learned about the operational chart later.”

“When?”

“Day 163.”

“Who showed it to you?”

“Peter Langford's office.”

“Did you object?”

“Yes.”

“Where is the objection?”

Marianne opened her notebook again.

“I sent a register clarification.”

She produced a copy.

**DAY 163 — REGISTER CLARIFICATION**

*The control liaison position remains vacant.*

*The administrative chart does not constitute a successor appointment.*

*No delegation has been recorded.*

*The shared Administrative Review Desk shall not be entered as an appointed control authority.*

Edward read the final sentence.

“Was the clarification acknowledged?”

“Yes.”

“By whom?”

“Peter Langford's office.”

“Did they change the operational chart?”

“I don't know.”

“Did they stop using the shared desk for control tasks?”

Marianne looked at him.

“I don't know that either.”

Edward closed the document.

“Then your role was limited to maintaining the register and notifying the originating authority.”

“Correct.”

“Did you have authority to suspend the shared desk's access?”

“No.”

“Could you order the revocation of Marianne Cross's credential?”

“No.”

“Could you approve a temporary extension?”

“No.”

She paused.

“I could identify that an appointment was missing. I could not create the appointment.”

Edward nodded.

That was a clean answer.

It also meant the responsibility for acting on the vacancy lay elsewhere.

At noon, Sophie found the originating authority's name.

The day-149 vacancy notification had been sent to:

**Governance Council — Office of Programme Authority.**

The office had existed throughout the closure period.

It had received the original notice.

It had received the day-153 reminder.

It had not appointed a successor.

Edward looked at the records.

“Was the office still operational on day 155?”

“Yes.”

“Who headed it?”

“Dr. Eleanor Vale.”

Edward paused.

“Eleanor Vale?”

“Yes.”

The same Eleanor Vale who had held the sealed preservation package under PRES-AP-09.

Edward looked at Sophie.

“Different appointment?”

“Different office, different authority. We need to verify the identity and scope before connecting the records.”

“Of course.”

He requested the programme authority office's appointment register.

The response was immediate.

Dr Eleanor Vale had served as Programme Authority Chair until day 166.

Her appointment included oversight of programme governance, control appointments, and administrative closure arrangements.

It did not include personal identity custody or receiving-family decisions.

The record was clear.

The person who had been responsible for appointing a successor to the control liaison position was also the person who had held the sealed technical package under a separate preservation appointment.

That did not make the two matters the same.

But it raised a legitimate question.

Had Eleanor received the vacancy notifications?

And if so, why had no successor been appointed?

In Singapore, Elena was packing her presentation materials.

The programme coordinator had given her a new task.

She had to prepare a short technical note explaining how the platform would handle incomplete applications during an organisational handover.

Clara read the title.

“Your favourite subject.”

Elena smiled.

“Unfortunately.”

Clara sat beside her.

“Why can't the new administrator just inherit the old administrator's account?”

“Because then nobody knows who actually performed the actions.”

“Couldn't they use the same login and change the name?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because an account records access. It doesn't prove appointment authority.”

Clara sighed.

“You've said that about five times this week.”

“And it remains true.”

Elena opened the technical note.

She added a section titled:

**Successor Appointment and Handover**

The first rule was simple.

A departing administrator's account would be disabled according to the end of their appointment.

Unfinished tasks would remain visible.

A successor could receive responsibility only after their appointment had been recorded and verified.

Until then, the system would identify the responsible office as vacant.

Clara read the section.

“So if nobody is appointed, everything stops?”

“Not everything. Routine functions can continue under existing valid permissions. But decisions requiring the vacant authority can't be made.”

“And if that delays the project?”

“Then the delay is recorded.”

Clara leaned back.

“You really don't like shortcuts.”

“I like shortcuts that don't pretend to be decisions.”

She saved the document.

For a moment, Elena watched the status panel.

A test task showed:

**Responsible authority: Vacant.**

**Status: Pending successor appointment.**

She left it that way.

Edward received Eleanor Vale's response at 4:16.

It was a formal statement, accompanied by the original notification records.

Eleanor confirmed that her office had received the day-149 vacancy notice.

It had also received the day-153 reminder.

The original notification had been registered at 15:02 on day 149.

The reminder had been registered at 10:41 on day 153.

Both had been placed in the Programme Authority Chair's correspondence queue.

Neither had been marked as urgent.

Neither had been assigned to an individual officer.

Edward read the explanation.

*The office was handling a substantial number of programme closure matters. The vacancy notice was retained for review with the wider governance transition.*

He looked at the next paragraph.

*No successor appointment was issued because the originating office had not received a completed nomination and supporting appointment instrument.*

Edward frowned.

“Who was expected to submit the nomination?”

Sophie searched the appointment procedure.

“The outgoing control liaison's supervising office.”

“Helen's supervising office?”

“Yes.”

“Did it submit a nomination?”

“No.”

“Did Eleanor ask for one?”

“Not according to the correspondence register.”

Edward returned to the statement.

Eleanor had not appointed a successor because no nomination had been submitted.

The supervising office had not submitted a nomination because the control liaison position was vacant.

The coordinator had notified the originating authority.

The originating authority had waited for a nomination.

The supervising office had not sent one.

The vacancy had remained unresolved.

Edward read the statement again.

It was a circular failure.

But it was not yet clear whether anyone had deliberately created it.

He requested the supervising office's day-148 to day-166 records.

The records arrived at 6:03.

The supervising office was the Governance Secretariat's Programme Administration Division.

Its director had been Peter Langford.

Edward looked at the appointment chart.

Peter's office had been responsible for nominating a successor.

It had also supervised the shared desk.

And it had received the day-154 access limitation.

He placed the documents in chronological order.

Day 148: Helen Sloane's appointment ended.

Day 149: Marianne Vale notified the originating authority.

Day 153: Marianne issued a reminder.

Day 154: Margaret Voss raised the temporary credential issue.

Day 155: Peter Langford received the clarification.

Day 155: Helen Sloane warned Daniel Mercer that the shared desk could not replace the vacant function.

Day 155: Peter asked the shared desk to confirm whether credentials were still required.

Day 155: The shared desk received a note stating that continued access required separate authorisation.

Day 156: The task was closed without a recorded answer.

Day 156: Marianne Cross's credential was retained.

Day 157: The credential was used to request restricted content access.

Edward sat back.

The same office had been responsible for nominating the successor, supervising the shared desk, and handling the warning about continued access.

But the vacancy remained unresolved.

He opened Eleanor's statement again.

There was an additional attachment at the end.

A short letter from the Programme Authority Chair's office, dated day 164.

**To:** Peter Langford\
**Subject:** Outstanding control liaison appointment

*The vacancy notification remains unresolved.*

*Please submit a proposed successor and supporting appointment documentation, or confirm that the control function is to remain suspended pending further authority.*

The letter had been delivered.

Peter had acknowledged it on day 165.

His response was attached.

*The administrative desk is continuing routine work. A successor nomination is not presently required for the remaining closure process.*

Edward read the sentence.

He placed it beside Helen's earlier warning.

The shared desk was not an appointed control authority.

The vacancy remained unresolved.

The originating authority had asked Peter either to nominate a successor or confirm suspension.

Peter had replied that no successor was presently required.

But the desk had continued handling restricted correspondence and credential matters.

Edward looked at Sophie.

“Did Peter have authority to declare the control function unnecessary?”

“No. That required the Programme Authority Chair's decision.”

“Did Eleanor suspend it?”

“There's no suspension instrument.”

“Then what happened?”

Sophie checked the correspondence register.

“Nothing further.”

Edward looked at the day-165 acknowledgement.

Peter had not nominated a successor.

He had not requested a suspension.

He had simply stated that the remaining work did not require one.

The function had remained vacant.

The shared desk had continued.

And no formal authority had replaced the missing appointment.

Edward opened a new investigation note.

**DAY 165 — FAILURE TO NOMINATE OR FORMALLY SUSPEND CONTROL FUNCTION**

He added:

**Determine whether Peter Langford's response was based on a specific instruction from the Programme Authority Chair or on his own administrative assessment.**

Then he stopped.

There was another name on the day-164 letter.

The letter had not been drafted by Eleanor's personal office.

It had been prepared by the Programme Authority's restricted appointments team.

The author field identified:

**Marianne Vale.**

Edward looked at the appointment register again.

Marianne had recorded the vacancy.

She had sent the notification.

She had issued the reminder.

She had prepared the day-164 letter asking Peter to nominate a successor or confirm suspension.

She had not possessed authority to appoint anyone.

But she had continued to document the absence of authority.

And Peter had acknowledged her letter.

Edward closed the file.

For the first time, the investigation had a clear chain of responsibility for the vacant control function.

But it still lacked the most important answer.

Why had Peter Langford decided that the programme could continue without the authority the records required?

And who, if anyone, had told him that the vacancy no longer mattered?
