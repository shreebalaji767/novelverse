# **Chapter 44 — The Sister Who Never Existed**

Edward read the rental application for the fourth time.

The handwriting was faded, but the relevant details remained legible.

**Tenant:** Victoria A.

**Property:** 18 Ashford Lane

**Occupancy:** Four months

**Emergency Contact:** Margaret Ellison

**Relationship:** Sister

He placed the document beside the bank records.

The account registered under Margaret's name had funded the rental property. Charles Vale had controlled the account. Daniel Mercer had arranged the paperwork.

And now Margaret Ellison had appeared as the emergency contact for the woman who had used the name Victoria A.

There were too many connections for coincidence.

But there was one obvious problem.

Margaret had never mentioned a sister.

Edward called Jonathan.

"Have you found any records of Margaret Ellison having a sister?"

"We're checking."

"Check birth records, marriage records, old residential registrations, everything available."

"Do you think the relationship was genuine?"

"I think someone wrote it on a document. That's all we know."

Jonathan paused.

"There's another detail. The landlord remembers the tenant leaving with the baby, but he says someone else collected her belongings."

"Who?"

"He couldn't identify the person. An older man, according to him."

"Charles?"

"He isn't certain."

Edward looked at the application.

"Was there a forwarding address?"

"No. But the landlord kept a note left by the tenant."

"What did it say?"

"Only that future correspondence should be sent to Vale Private Counsel."

Edward closed his eyes.

"Of course."

The care facility was unusually quiet that morning.

Margaret Ellison was sitting at a small table, arranging several old photographs.

When Edward entered, she looked up.

"Have you found something?"

"Possibly."

He sat opposite her.

"I want to ask you about someone."

Margaret adjusted the photographs.

"Who?"

"A woman who used the name Victoria A."

Margaret's hands stopped moving.

Edward noticed immediately.

"Does that name mean anything to you?"

She looked at the photographs.

"Victoria?"

"Yes."

Margaret was quiet for several seconds.

Then she shook her head.

"I don't know anyone called Victoria."

Edward waited.

"She listed you as her sister on a rental application."

Margaret frowned.

"My sister?"

"Yes."

"I don't have a sister."

"Are you certain?"

She gave him a puzzled look.

"I had two brothers. Both died a long time ago. I never had a sister."

Edward took out a copy of the application.

Margaret examined it.

Her expression became increasingly troubled.

"That's my name."

"Yes."

"And this address..."

"Do you recognise it?"

"No."

She looked at the relationship field again.

"Someone wrote that I was her sister."

"Did anyone ever ask you to act as an emergency contact for another woman?"

Margaret thought carefully.

"Charles asked me to sign something once."

Edward leaned forward.

"When?"

"After Anna came to live with me."

"Do you remember what the document was?"

"No. He said it was necessary for the child's arrangements."

"Did you sign it?"

Margaret looked uncertain.

"I might have."

"Did you read it?"

"I was tired. Anna had been crying all night."

Edward lowered his voice.

"Mrs. Ellison, I need you to think carefully. Did you ever meet a woman who collected a baby from Charles Vale?"

Margaret's expression changed.

For a moment, she seemed to be looking beyond the room.

Then she whispered:

"There was a woman."

Edward sat still.

"What do you remember?"

"She came to the house."

"When?"

"After Anna had been there for some time. I don't remember exactly."

"Was she called Victoria?"

Margaret shook her head.

"Charles called her something else."

"What?"

She closed her eyes.

"I can't remember."

Edward waited.

"She was carrying a little bag. She asked whether Anna was sleeping properly. She asked whether she was eating."

"Did she hold the baby?"

Margaret nodded.

"Yes."

"Did she say who she was?"

Margaret looked down.

"She said she was looking after someone."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Edward studied her expression.

"Did she say she was your sister?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No."

"Did you consider her family?"

"No."

Margaret looked at the rental application again.

"Why would someone write that?"

Edward had no answer.

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian received the first results of the search into the account.

He entered Alexander's study carrying a folder.

"We have confirmed that the intermediary account was not opened by Margaret Ellison."

Alexander looked up.

"Who opened it?"

"Daniel Mercer. He used a power of attorney bearing a signature attributed to Margaret."

"Was the signature genuine?"

"The examiner cannot establish that. The original document is missing."

Victoria sat across from Alexander.

"Then Margaret may not have known about the account."

"Correct."

Adrian placed another document on the desk.

"We also found a property lease connected to the same payments. The tenant used the name Victoria A. and listed Margaret Ellison as her sister."

Victoria looked at the document.

"Have they identified the woman?"

"Not yet."

Alexander examined the rental dates.

"Four months."

"Yes."

"Was a child present?"

"The landlord remembers a baby."

Victoria's fingers tightened around the paper.

"Which child?"

"That hasn't been established."

She looked at Adrian.

"Could it have been the infant Charles removed from Room Fourteen?"

"Possibly."

"Could it have been Infant B?"

"Also possible. We have no reliable identification."

Victoria looked down.

For eighteen years, she had been told her daughter was missing.

Now the investigation had uncovered evidence of a woman who had cared for an unidentified infant in a rented property.

It was not enough to offer hope.

But it was enough to make the absence feel more deliberate.

"Find the woman," she said.

Adrian nodded.

"And Adrian?"

He stopped.

"Don't approach her as if she stole a child. We don't know what she was told."

Adrian gave a small nod.

"Understood."

At the Valmont Estate, Catherine was reviewing the foundation's revised scholarship proposal when Elena entered.

"Do you have a minute?"

"Of course."

Elena placed a printed document on the desk.

"I wanted to ask you something about the university interview."

Catherine looked at the paper.

"Have you decided what to wear?"

"That's part of it."

Catherine smiled.

Elena sat down.

"The interview is online. They said I should choose a quiet place with a neutral background."

"That shouldn't be difficult here."

"I know. But I don't want it to look like I'm sitting in a mansion."

Catherine considered that.

"Why?"

"Because I want them to evaluate me, not the house."

Catherine nodded.

"That's reasonable."

Elena looked at the document.

"I was thinking about using the small study near the east garden."

"That room has good lighting."

"Exactly."

"Then use it."

Elena hesitated.

"Mother?"

Catherine looked up.

"Yes?"

"Do you think I'm making the right decision by applying abroad?"

Catherine put down her pen.

"I think you're making a decision about your education. It doesn't have to be a decision about your family."

Elena looked at her.

Catherine continued carefully.

"You can study abroad and still be part of this family. You can stay here and still have an independent life. Neither choice needs to be a declaration of loyalty."

Elena was quiet.

"Sometimes I feel as though every decision I make is being watched."

Catherine's expression softened.

"I know."

"I don't want to hurt anyone."

"You won't always be able to prevent people from feeling disappointed. That doesn't mean you're doing something wrong."

Elena looked down.

Then she nodded.

"Thank you."

Catherine reached for her hand.

"Whatever happens with the interview, I want you to remember that you are allowed to choose."

Elena squeezed her hand gently.

For a moment, the uncertainty surrounding the family seemed less important than the simple fact that they were still capable of having an ordinary conversation.

Isabelle was working in the foundation's records room when Sebastian entered.

He glanced at the folders on the table.

"You're still here?"

"Yes."

"It's nearly seven."

"I know."

"Do you intend to sleep in the office?"

Isabelle smiled.

"Only if the foundation pays for it."

Sebastian raised an eyebrow.

"You're becoming expensive."

"I'm working on the scholarship programme."

He picked up the revised proposal.

"Mother showed me this."

"And?"

"Better than the first version."

"That's a compliment?"

"Yes."

She laughed.

Sebastian read another page.

"You've included an independent review panel."

"I don't want the selection process to depend on who knows whom."

"Good."

He placed the proposal down.

"Have you considered how you'll prevent applicants from submitting false information?"

"Yes. Verification, but with an appeals process."

"That will require staff."

"I know."

"Funding."

"I know."

"Additional administration."

Isabelle looked at him.

"Are you trying to discourage me?"

"No. I'm checking whether you've considered the consequences."

She leaned back.

"I have. I don't expect it to be easy."

Sebastian nodded.

"Then you're learning."

He turned toward the door.

"One more thing."

"What?"

"Don't let the foundation become your only ambition."

Isabelle looked surprised.

Sebastian continued.

"You can work here. You can learn about the business. You can study something entirely different. You don't need to spend your life proving you deserve a place in this family."

His voice remained practical, almost businesslike.

But Isabelle understood what he meant.

"I'll remember that."

Sebastian left.

She looked at the proposal again.

Then she added a new section.

**Long-term programme evaluation and independent impact assessment.**

If she was going to build something, she wanted to know whether it actually helped.

Daniel Mercer agreed to meet Edward the following morning.

The former legal assistant arrived carrying a worn leather folder.

He looked older than he had during their previous meeting, but his manner remained composed.

Edward placed the rental application on the table.

"Do you recognise this?"

Mercer examined it.

"Yes."

"Who was Victoria A.?"

"I don't know her full name."

"Did you meet her?"

"Yes."

"Was she the woman who collected an infant?"

Mercer nodded.

"I saw her with a baby."

"Which baby?"

"I don't know."

"Was she related to Margaret Ellison?"

Mercer looked at the application.

"Not biologically, as far as I knew."

Edward leaned forward.

"Then why did the application say they were sisters?"

"Charles told me to use that relationship."

"Why?"

"Because the property owner required an emergency contact."

"Why not use her actual relationship?"

Mercer hesitated.

"Because she wasn't supposed to have one."

Edward stared at him.

"What does that mean?"

"She wasn't supposed to be connected to the child through ordinary records. Charles said the relationship had to remain separate from the hospital documentation."

"That doesn't explain why you invented a sister."

Mercer looked uncomfortable.

"I was told it was a temporary arrangement."

"Everything was temporary, wasn't it?"

Mercer did not answer.

Edward opened his notebook.

"Who was the woman?"

"I never learned her full identity."

"Did she know the child was connected to the hospital?"

"Yes."

"Did she know there were two infants?"

Mercer shook his head.

"I don't believe so."

"Did she know the child's biological mother?"

"I don't know."

"Did she ask?"

Mercer looked down.

"Yes."

Edward waited.

"She asked whether the child's mother had been informed."

"What did Charles say?"

"That the mother had been told."

"Was that true?"

Mercer did not answer.

Edward repeated the question.

"Was it true?"

"I don't know."

Edward closed his notebook.

"Did the woman believe him?"

Mercer considered it.

"I don't think she did."

"Why?"

"She asked the same question twice."

Edward looked at him.

"What else did she say?"

Mercer hesitated.

Then he spoke quietly.

"She said she would not take the child if the mother had not been informed."

Edward went still.

"Did she take the child?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Charles told her the mother had been informed. He also showed her a document."

"What document?"

"I don't know. It was folded."

Edward stared at the table.

The woman had been told that the mother knew.

She had questioned the claim.

And she had eventually taken the child.

"Was the document signed?"

Mercer nodded.

"By whom?"

"I couldn't see."

"Did the woman keep it?"

"Yes."

Edward wrote that down.

"Do you remember anything else?"

Mercer looked at the rental application.

"She called the child by a name."

"What name?"

"Anna."

Edward's pen stopped.

"Anna?"

"Yes."

"Are you certain?"

Mercer nodded.

"She said the name while holding the baby."

Edward looked at him for a long moment.

Margaret Ellison had called the child she received Anna.

The woman called Victoria A. had also called a baby Anna.

Either the woman had received the same child later, or someone had given two different infants the same name.

Edward stood.

"Thank you, Mr. Mercer."

Mercer looked worried.

"Does this mean she did something wrong?"

"I don't know."

"She wasn't a bad person."

Edward paused.

"Did she seem frightened?"

Mercer nodded.

"Very."

"Of whom?"

"I couldn't tell."

That evening, Edward returned to the study and arranged the photographs again.

The photograph of Margaret holding Infant A.

The photograph of Eleanor holding Infant B.

The tenth-day record.

The rental application.

The account records.

The witness statements.

He placed the rental application beside Margaret's photograph.

There was a possibility he had not considered.

The woman called Victoria A. might not have received a second child.

She might have received the same child Margaret had cared for.

Perhaps Margaret had given the baby to her.

Perhaps Charles had arranged it without Margaret's knowledge.

Perhaps the woman had been told she was receiving a different infant.

Or perhaps the child had never been the same one at all.

The repeated name Anna was not proof.

It was only another question.

His phone rang.

Jonathan.

"Edward, we've found the original property payment voucher."

"Who signed it?"

"Charles Vale."

"Anything else?"

"Yes. The voucher includes a handwritten note."

Edward waited.

Jonathan read it aloud.

"'The child is to remain with the receiving woman until the mother can be approached safely. Do not disclose the identity of the other infant.'"

Edward sat down.

"Who wrote it?"

"Not Charles. The handwriting is different."

"Can you identify it?"

"Not yet."

Edward looked at the photographs.

The woman had been instructed to keep the child until the mother could be approached safely.

But which mother?

And why was the identity of the other infant being concealed?

Jonathan continued.

"There's a final line."

"What?"

"Listen carefully."

Edward waited.

Jonathan read the sentence.

"'If Margaret asks, tell her the child has been returned to the original guardian.'"

Edward slowly lowered his pen.

The woman called Victoria A. had been instructed to keep a child.

Margaret Ellison had been instructed to believe that child had been returned.

And someone had made certain that neither woman knew the full arrangement.

Edward looked again at the rental application.

The word *Sister* had been written in a neat, deliberate hand.

It was not an innocent clerical mistake.

It was a connection created on paper to conceal a connection that someone did not want recorded.

At the Aurelius Estate, Julian was adding another page to his notebook.

Damien passed him and noticed the writing.

"What are you doing now?"

"Updating my list."

"Didn't you say you were waiting?"

"I was. But I thought of something."

Damien looked over his shoulder.

Julian had written:

**46. Don't assume she wants the same things I do.**

Damien read it.

"That's a good addition."

Julian closed the notebook.

"I know."

He looked toward the corridor leading to the preserved family rooms.

"Do you think she'll like the library?"

"Possibly."

"What about the garden?"

"Possibly."

"The cinema?"

"Possibly."

Julian frowned.

"You're not helping."

Damien sat beside him.

"Why don't you wait until you meet her and ask?"

Julian considered that.

Then he nodded.

"That's probably better."

He looked at his notebook again.

"Forty-six things."

"Not instructions."

Julian smiled.

"Suggestions."

Damien shook his head.

"Exactly."

At 10:18 p.m., Edward received the handwriting examiner's preliminary report.

The note on the property payment voucher had been compared with the known handwriting samples.

It did not match Charles Vale.

It did not match Marcus Morelli.

It did not match Eleanor Vale.

And it did not match Daniel Mercer.

The examiner had found one possible similarity with a document already in the investigation.

A short instruction from the hospital's private nursery.

The document that had authorised the original separation of the two infants.

The signature at the bottom was partially obscured.

But the examiner had identified a distinctive feature in the final letter.

The same unusual stroke appeared on the property voucher.

Edward opened the old hospital document.

He looked at the signature.

Then he looked at the new report.

The name was not complete.

Only the first letter and the final portion remained legible.

**M. V—**

Edward stared at it.

The same handwriting had authorised the original nursery arrangement and later instructed that a child remain with the woman called Victoria A.

Someone had been involved from the beginning.

Someone who had not yet been properly identified.

And that person had apparently known about both infants long before either family learned that the hospital records were wrong.
