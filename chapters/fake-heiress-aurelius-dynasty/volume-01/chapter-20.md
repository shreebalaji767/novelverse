# **Chapter 20 — The Woman Who Received the Second Instruction**

Edward read the name again.

**MARGARET ELLIS.**

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Adrian enlarged the delivery record.

The signature was faint, but the letters were unmistakable. Beneath it, the courier had written that the recipient had accepted the document personally and instructed that no entry be made into the household correspondence register.

"She told us she had never received the second instruction," Edward said.

"Did she?"

"She told me she had kept the photograph and the original note. She never mentioned another delivery."

Adrian looked at him.

"Did you ask?"

Edward paused.

He remembered their conversations.

He had asked about the hospital records, the photograph, the man in the corridor and the instructions given to her on the night of the births.

He had asked whether she knew what happened to the children.

He had never specifically asked whether another document had been delivered to her several days later.

"Not directly."

"Then we don't know whether she deliberately concealed it."

"No."

Adrian handed the tablet back.

"Find her."

Edward was already reaching for his phone.

Margaret Ellis was at home when the call arrived.

She had been sitting at her dining table, sorting through old photographs.

Most were ordinary.

Family gatherings. Birthday celebrations. A holiday near the coast. A picture of her younger self standing outside the hospital where she had worked for nearly thirty years.

She had kept very few photographs from the maternity ward.

The one Edward had seen was among them.

When her phone rang, she looked at the screen.

Edward Valmont.

She let it ring twice before answering.

"Hello?"

"Mrs Ellis. It's Edward."

"I know."

"We need to speak."

"About what?"

"An archive record recovered from the Aurelius Family Office."

Margaret became still.

Edward continued.

"A second instruction was dispatched four days after the original maternity identity report. It was delivered to you personally."

There was silence.

Then she asked, "Where did you find that?"

"In the original delivery register."

Margaret closed her eyes.

"I see."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"I didn't think it was relevant."

"It was addressed to you."

"I didn't say I never received it."

"You never mentioned it."

"Mr Valmont, I have told you everything I remember."

"Then help me understand why the delivery record says you instructed the courier not to enter it into the correspondence register."

Margaret looked towards the photographs on the table.

For a long time, she did not answer.

Finally, she said, "I can't discuss this over the telephone."

"Then we'll come to you."

"Not tonight."

"Mrs Ellis—"

"Tomorrow morning. Ten o'clock. And bring the original document, not a photograph."

She ended the call.

Edward lowered his phone.

Adrian watched him.

"She agreed?"

"Tomorrow. Ten o'clock."

"Good."

Edward looked at the delivery record again.

"She sounded frightened."

Adrian's expression did not change.

"Perhaps she has been frightened for eighteen years."

The next morning, the Valmont breakfast table was unusually crowded.

Nicholas had arrived early, which was generally a sign that he wanted something.

Elena noticed immediately.

"Why are you here?"

Nicholas looked offended.

"Good morning to you too."

"You usually come down later."

"I am allowed to change my schedule."

"Is something wrong?"

"Nothing is wrong."

Elena narrowed her eyes.

"Then what do you want?"

Nicholas reached across the table and took a piece of toast.

"Is it necessary to assume I have an ulterior motive every time I enter a room?"

"Yes."

Edward, who had just arrived, laughed quietly.

Nicholas pointed at him.

"See? This is the treatment I receive."

Sebastian continued reading his tablet.

"Perhaps you should become a better person."

"Unhelpful."

Catherine entered with Isabelle.

"What's happening?"

"Your son is stealing breakfast," Elena replied.

Catherine looked at Nicholas.

"There's plenty of food."

"Thank you, Mother. Someone appreciates me."

"That doesn't mean you should steal from your sister."

Nicholas looked at Elena.

"Traitor."

Elena smiled and continued eating.

For a few minutes, the conversation remained ordinary.

Then Edward placed his phone beside his plate.

"Richard, I need to speak with you after breakfast."

Richard looked up.

"About the investigation?"

"Yes."

Catherine noticed his expression.

"Has something happened?"

"We recovered another delivery record."

Sebastian put his tablet down.

"Related to the second instruction?"

Edward nodded.

"Margaret Ellis received it."

The table fell quiet.

Elena looked from Edward to Catherine.

"Margaret? The former nurse?"

"Yes."

"She received the document?"

"According to the archive record."

Isabelle frowned.

"Didn't she already give you everything?"

Edward answered carefully.

"Apparently, there is more to her account."

Nicholas leaned back.

"Then why didn't she tell us?"

"We don't know."

Richard looked at Edward.

"You're meeting her?"

"At ten."

"Take Sebastian."

"I was planning to."

Richard nodded.

"Keep me informed."

Elena returned her attention to breakfast.

She had no intention of interrupting an investigation she barely understood.

But something about the discovery bothered her.

Margaret had been the first person to admit that the hospital records were not what they appeared to be.

She had seemed frightened, but also determined to help.

Why would she keep another instruction hidden?

And what could a document delivered four days after the births possibly contain?

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was preparing to visit the family foundation when Alexander entered the dining room.

She was reviewing a report on a scholarship programme.

"You're leaving?"

"In an hour."

"Good. I'll have Adrian arrange the car."

Alexander sat opposite her.

"There's an update."

Victoria immediately put down the report.

"About the second instruction?"

"Yes."

He told her about the delivery record.

When he mentioned Margaret Ellis's name, Victoria stared at him.

"The nurse?"

"She received the document personally."

"Did she know what it contained?"

"We don't know."

Victoria slowly closed the report.

"Then she may know what happened."

"She may."

"Can we meet her?"

"Edward is meeting her this morning."

Victoria looked towards the windows.

For eighteen years, she had wanted to speak to someone who could tell her what happened after her daughter was born.

Now the possibility was close enough to touch.

Yet every new discovery seemed to create another unanswered question.

"Alexander."

He looked at her.

"If she knows where our daughter went, I want to hear it from her."

"You will."

"And if she doesn't?"

Alexander reached across the table.

"Then we continue searching."

Victoria nodded.

She had learned how to wait.

She had never learned how to stop hoping.

Margaret had prepared the dining room before they arrived.

Three cups of tea stood on the table.

A small wooden box rested beside them.

Edward noticed it immediately.

"Good morning, Mrs Ellis."

"Good morning."

Sebastian greeted her politely.

"Thank you for agreeing to meet."

Margaret gestured towards the chairs.

"Please sit."

Neither man touched the tea.

Edward placed the recovered delivery record on the table.

"Is this accurate?"

Margaret looked at it.

"Yes."

"You received the second instruction."

"Yes."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

She looked at him.

"Because I didn't know what it meant."

"Then why keep it secret?"

Margaret's fingers rested on the wooden box.

"Because the person who delivered it told me that if I discussed its contents with anyone, both children could be placed in danger."

Sebastian leaned forward.

"Who told you that?"

"I never saw his face clearly."

"Another man?"

"Yes. He came to my house four days after the births."

Edward looked at the box.

"Did he bring the instruction?"

"Yes."

"Did you open it?"

"I did."

"What did it say?"

Margaret did not answer immediately.

Instead, she opened the wooden box.

Inside were several folded documents, an old hospital identity badge and a small envelope.

She removed the envelope.

It had no address.

Only a handwritten reference.

**EFO-SL/07 — SECOND INSTRUCTION**

Margaret placed it on the table.

"I kept the original."

Edward looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because I thought one day someone would come asking the right questions."

He carefully examined the envelope.

The seal was broken.

"May I?"

Margaret nodded.

Edward opened it.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

The first paragraph was typed.

*The previous arrangement is suspended until the identities of both infants have been independently verified.*

Edward read further.

*No child is to be transferred, returned, or presented to either family on the basis of the provisional placement record.*

Sebastian glanced at him.

"Continue."

Edward did.

*The children are to remain with their current guardians until further instructions are received. Neither family is to be informed of the provisional placement entries.*

The final paragraph was handwritten.

*If the mother asks about the child recorded under her name, tell her that the original records cannot be verified.*

Edward stopped.

Margaret looked at him.

"That was the instruction I received."

Sebastian's expression hardened.

"Who wrote it?"

"I don't know."

"The typed section?"

"I was told it came from the external liaison office."

"And the handwritten addition?"

Margaret shook her head.

"I couldn't identify the handwriting."

Edward read the document again.

The instruction did not say the children should be separated.

It did not say they should be moved.

It specifically prohibited either family from being informed about the provisional placements.

But there was something else.

The document contained a date.

Four days after the births.

And beneath the date was a reference to an earlier report.

**Maternity Identity Report — Original Copy.**

Edward looked up.

"Did you receive anything else?"

Margaret hesitated.

Then she reached into the wooden box and removed another folded sheet.

"This was attached to the back."

Edward unfolded it.

It was a brief handwritten note.

The writing was different from the instruction.

*The person who arranged the original transfer has not been identified. Until that person is found, neither child can safely be moved.*

Below it was a signature.

Not Charles Vale.

Not Alexander Aurelius.

Not Martin Hale.

The name was unfamiliar.

**ELEANOR VALE.**

Sebastian looked at Edward.

"Eleanor?"

Margaret nodded.

"Charles Vale's wife."

Edward stared at the signature.

The same woman whose earlier correspondence had warned the hospital not to return the child through the usual channels.

The same woman who had instructed the hospital to keep the records separate.

Now her name appeared on a second instruction warning that neither child could safely be moved.

"Did you meet her?" Edward asked.

Margaret nodded slowly.

"Once."

"When?"

"About a week after I received the instruction."

"Where?"

"At the hospital."

"What did she want?"

Margaret looked down at the paper.

"She asked whether both children were still alive."

Edward's expression changed.

"Both?"

"Yes."

"What did you tell her?"

"That they were."

Margaret's voice became quieter.

"She looked relieved."

Sebastian exchanged a glance with Edward.

"Did she tell you who had arranged the transfer?"

"No."

"Did she explain why the children had to remain where they were?"

"No."

"Did she mention the Aurelius family?"

Margaret nodded.

"She asked whether anyone from the family office had contacted me."

Edward leaned forward.

"What did you say?"

"I told her about the first instruction."

"And?"

"She told me not to trust anyone who claimed to be acting on behalf of the family office without an original authorisation."

Edward sat back.

The room seemed suddenly smaller.

"Did she say why?"

Margaret looked directly at him.

"She said someone inside the office had been using Alexander Aurelius's authority without his knowledge."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander was in a meeting when Adrian received Edward's call.

Adrian listened without interrupting.

Then he walked into his father's office.

Alexander immediately noticed his expression.

"What happened?"

Adrian placed his phone on the desk.

"Edward found the second instruction."

Alexander stood.

"And?"

"It confirms that neither child was supposed to be transferred until both identities were independently verified."

"Who issued it?"

"The external liaison office."

"Under whose authority?"

Adrian hesitated.

"That's not clear."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Then what is clear?"

"That Eleanor Vale signed an attached note. She warned the hospital that someone inside the Aurelius Family Office was using your authority without your knowledge."

Silence.

Alexander looked at him.

"She said that?"

"Yes."

"Did she identify the person?"

"No."

Alexander walked towards the window.

For years, he had believed that his daughter's disappearance might have resulted from a hospital error, a criminal act or an elaborate deception.

Now he was being told that someone within his own organisation might have used his name to authorise it.

"Who had access to the family office authorisation system eighteen years ago?"

Adrian answered immediately.

"Several senior administrators. The liaison office, the legal department and the executive secretariat."

"Names."

"I've already requested the historical staff register."

"Find every person who had access."

"Understood."

Alexander turned.

"And find Eleanor Vale."

"She died eleven years ago."

"Then find everything she left behind."

Back at the Valmont Estate, Elena had finished her university work.

She was in the garden when Isabelle joined her.

Isabelle carried two glasses of juice.

"Peace offering."

Elena accepted one.

"What have I done?"

"Nothing. I just thought we could sit together."

They settled on the garden bench.

For a while, they watched the gardeners working near the flower beds.

Then Isabelle spoke.

"Do you ever wonder what your life would have been like if the hospital had never made a mistake?"

Elena looked at her.

"Sometimes."

"Would you have been happier?"

"I don't know."

Isabelle looked down at her glass.

"I think about it quite often."

Elena waited.

"If I had grown up here, perhaps I would have had a different life. Different schools. Different opportunities. Different people around me."

"Probably."

"And you might have grown up somewhere else."

"Probably."

Isabelle gave a small, uncertain smile.

"Do you ever resent me for that?"

Elena considered her answer.

"No."

"Not even a little?"

"Sometimes I resent the situation."

She looked at Isabelle.

"But that's different."

Isabelle nodded.

Elena continued.

"I don't know what happened eighteen years ago. Neither do you. I don't think either of us should spend our lives blaming the other for it."

Isabelle looked at her for a long moment.

Then she smiled.

"You're making it difficult to argue with you."

"That's one of my better qualities."

"Your modesty is impressive."

"Thank you."

They laughed.

Neither knew that, only a short distance away, Edward was preparing to tell the Valmont family that the second instruction had finally been found.

Neither knew that the document contained a warning about someone inside the Aurelius Family Office.

And neither knew that the woman who had delivered that warning had once stood in the same hospital corridor where their lives had been divided.

That evening, Edward placed the second instruction on Richard's desk.

Richard read it in silence.

Catherine stood beside him.

Sebastian was seated opposite them, while Nicholas remained unusually quiet.

When Richard reached Eleanor Vale's note, he stopped.

"Someone used Alexander's authority without his knowledge."

"That is what she claimed," Edward said.

Catherine looked at the signature.

"Why would someone do that?"

"We don't know."

Nicholas leaned forward.

"Does this mean the Aurelius family wasn't responsible?"

Edward shook his head.

"It means we don't have proof that Alexander authorised the transfer. It doesn't establish who did."

Sebastian examined the document.

"And the children?"

"The instruction says neither child was to be moved until their identities were verified."

Richard looked up.

"Then why were they separated?"

Edward did not answer.

Because that was the question the document had failed to explain.

Catherine looked at the date.

"Who was responsible for keeping the children where they were?"

Margaret's earlier statement came back to Edward.

The children had remained with their current guardians.

But who had decided those guardians?

Who had taken them home?

Who had ensured that the hospital records reflected those destinations?

And why had no one informed either family?

Edward looked at the final line of Eleanor's note.

"There's one more thing."

He turned the page.

At the bottom, in smaller handwriting, was an additional sentence.

*If the second instruction is ever discovered, do not assume that the person who received it was the person who was meant to act upon it.*

Richard frowned.

"What does that mean?"

Edward shook his head.

"I don't know."

He turned the document over.

There was a faint impression on the back, as though another sheet had once been placed beneath it while someone wrote.

The forensic team had not examined that side yet.

Edward looked at the others.

"There's more."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was waiting in the old nursery when Alexander entered.

She stood immediately.

"Did they find it?"

"Yes."

"Does it tell us where she went?"

"No."

Her shoulders lowered.

Alexander approached her.

"But it tells us something important. Someone used my authority without my knowledge. Eleanor Vale warned the hospital not to trust instructions from the family office unless they had original authorisation."

Victoria stared at him.

"Then someone inside our own organisation may have been involved."

"Possibly."

"Did they know where our daughter was?"

"We don't know."

Victoria looked towards the small wooden horse on the shelf.

"Alexander, I want to know whether our daughter was safe."

He took her hands.

"We will find out."

She nodded, but her eyes remained on the old photograph beside the window.

Then her gaze shifted to the document he was holding.

"What's that?"

Alexander looked down.

It was a photocopy of the second instruction.

He handed it to her.

Victoria read the first paragraph.

Then the second.

Then the handwritten note.

She stopped at the final sentence.

*Neither child can safely be moved.*

Victoria looked up.

"Why?"

Alexander had no answer.

For eighteen years, she had believed that someone had taken her daughter away.

Now she was beginning to wonder whether someone had been trying to protect her.

And if that was true, she needed to know what danger had been waiting for a newborn child.

At the same moment, in the Valmont Estate's investigation room, Edward received a message from the forensic examiner.

The faint impression on the back of Eleanor Vale's note had been successfully recovered.

It contained a partial name.

A date.

And a handwritten instruction.

Edward opened the attached image.

The date was four days after Margaret Ellis had received the second instruction.

The name was incomplete.

But the remaining letters were clear enough to read.

**MARTIN HA—**

Beneath it was a final line.

*He must not be allowed to deliver the child.*

Edward stared at the screen.

Martin Hale had not merely collected the missing documents.

Someone had once written an instruction specifically warning that he must not deliver a child.

And eighteen years later, Hale had still been the man holding the file that contained both children's names.
