# **Chapter 22 — The Guardians Who Never Signed**

The correction in the nursery register was only one sentence.

**THE CHILDREN WERE NOT RELEASED TO THE GUARDIANS LISTED IN THE ORIGINAL INSTRUCTIONS.**

Beneath it was Charles Vale's signature.

Edward read it several times.

The statement did not identify the original guardians.

It did not identify the actual receiving guardians.

It did not explain why the children had been released to different people.

But it established something important.

The original instructions had contained specific names.

And someone had deliberately changed the arrangements before the children left the hospital.

Edward forwarded the scanned page to Adrian.

Then he called the hospital archive team.

"I need the original document preserved. No additional handling, no annotations, and no further restoration until our forensic examiner arrives."

The archivist agreed.

Edward ended the call.

He looked at the two discharge records again.

Martin Hale had signed both.

But Charles Vale had corrected them.

Which meant that, at some point, both men had known that the children were not being released according to the original plan.

The question was no longer simply who had taken the babies home.

It was who had been supposed to receive them in the first place.

At Aurelius Global, Adrian was already waiting when Edward arrived.

Alexander stood near the conference table.

Sebastian Valmont had joined them through a secure video meeting, while Richard was expected shortly.

Adrian placed the scanned discharge register on the screen.

"Let's establish what we know."

He displayed the first page.

"Two infants were admitted to the private nursery. Their identification references correspond to the original maternity registrations. Both remained there for approximately one week."

The second page appeared.

"Martin Hale signed both discharge entries. Neither identifies the receiving guardian."

Alexander looked at the screen.

"Why would he sign for both?"

"We don't know," Adrian replied.

Edward displayed the correction.

"Charles Vale later amended the records. His statement says the children were not released to the guardians listed in the original instructions."

Sebastian leaned closer to his camera.

"Do we have those original instructions?"

"Not yet."

"Then how do we know there were two different guardians?"

"Because the correction refers to plural guardians, and the original discharge forms contain separate guardian-reference fields."

Richard entered the room.

"Could Hale have taken the children himself?"

Edward shook his head.

"That would require evidence we don't currently have. His signature establishes that he handled the discharge paperwork. It does not establish that he physically received either child."

Alexander looked at the document.

"Did he say anything about the discharge?"

"He admitted seeing both infants in the nursery. He hasn't yet been asked about the signatures."

"Then ask him."

Adrian nodded.

Richard took a seat.

"There is another possibility."

Everyone looked at him.

"Could the original guardians have been hospital-appointed representatives rather than family members?"

Edward considered it.

"Yes. The term 'guardian' may have been used administratively. We need the original instructions before drawing conclusions."

Richard nodded.

"Then find them."

Alexander looked towards Adrian.

"And find out who authorised the private nursery arrangement."

The meeting continued for another hour.

By the end, they had agreed on three priorities:

- Recover the original nursery admission and discharge instructions.
- Identify everyone who accessed the private nursery during the relevant eight days.
- Establish who authorised the release of both infants.

They had not agreed on what to tell Elena and Isabelle.

For the moment, both girls remained unaware of the latest discovery.

And Alexander wanted it to remain that way until there was something reliable to tell them.

Martin Hale did not answer his telephone.

Edward tried twice.

The second call went directly to voicemail.

Adrian looked at the screen.

"He's avoiding us."

"Possibly."

"Or someone has told him not to cooperate."

Edward put his phone away.

"We should check his address."

They did not have to.

A few minutes later, the investigator assigned to the case called.

"Mr Valmont, Martin Hale has left his residence."

"When?"

"Approximately forty minutes ago."

"Do you know where he went?"

"His vehicle was recorded entering the old business district. He visited a storage facility near the railway terminal."

Edward exchanged a glance with Adrian.

"Did he collect anything?"

"We're checking."

"Do not confront him. Keep the location under observation and preserve the relevant records lawfully."

"Understood."

Edward ended the call.

Adrian stood.

"Let's go."

The storage facility was an older building with several rows of privately rented units.

It had no connection to the modern archive facility where the Aurelius and Valmont representatives had inspected Hale's first storage unit.

This one appeared to have been used for years.

The investigator met them near the entrance.

"His unit is on the second floor. He entered approximately twenty minutes ago."

"Has he left?"

"Not yet."

Edward looked towards the corridor.

"Does he have the right to access it?"

"Yes. The rental agreement is in his name."

Adrian nodded.

"Then we wait."

They remained in the small reception area.

A little over ten minutes later, Hale appeared at the end of the corridor.

He was carrying a flat cardboard box.

When he saw them, he stopped.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Hale sighed.

"I suppose this was inevitable."

Edward looked at the box.

"What have you found?"

Hale glanced down.

"Something I should have given you earlier."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because I was afraid."

Adrian stepped closer.

"Of whom?"

Hale looked towards the exit.

"I don't know."

"That isn't an answer."

"I know."

He placed the box on a nearby table.

"I received a call last night. Someone knew about the second instruction. Someone knew I had documents."

Edward watched him carefully.

"Did they threaten you?"

"They told me not to give you anything else."

"And you came here anyway?"

Hale nodded.

"Because I found something."

He opened the box.

Inside were several old folders, a photograph album and a sealed envelope.

Hale removed the envelope.

"This was kept separately from the other records."

Edward examined it.

There was no sender's name.

Only a date.

It was dated two days before the children were born.

Adrian looked at Hale.

"What's inside?"

"I don't know. I never opened it."

Edward carefully inspected the seal.

It appeared intact.

"Why did you keep it?"

"Because I was instructed to."

"By whom?"

Hale looked at him.

"Charles Vale."

The envelope was opened under supervision at the archive office.

Inside were three pages.

The first was a typed list.

The second was a handwritten note.

The third was a photocopy of an authorisation form.

Edward examined the list.

There were two entries.

**INFANT A — RECEIVING REPRESENTATIVE: M. HALE**

**INFANT B — RECEIVING REPRESENTATIVE: C. VALE**

Adrian read the entries over his shoulder.

"So Hale was supposed to receive one child, and Charles Vale the other."

Edward nodded.

"According to this list."

The handwritten note was dated the following morning.

*The original arrangement is to remain unchanged unless the originating authority issues a written amendment.*

There was no signature.

The third page was more interesting.

It contained an authorisation for the private nursery arrangement.

The form had been submitted by the external liaison office.

The authorised representative field was blank.

The approval field contained Alexander Aurelius's name.

Adrian stared at it.

"That is the same authorisation."

Edward examined the photocopy.

"Yes."

"Was the signature verified?"

"Not on this copy."

Adrian turned to Hale.

"Did you ever see the original?"

"Yes."

"Was my father's signature on it?"

Hale hesitated.

"I saw his name."

"That wasn't what I asked."

Hale looked uncomfortable.

"I cannot say whether he signed it."

Edward leaned forward.

"Why not?"

"Because I never saw him sign it. The document was already in the file when it reached me."

Adrian placed the page down.

"Who delivered it?"

Hale looked at the table.

"Charles Vale."

Adrian's expression hardened.

"Did Charles say my father had authorised it?"

"Yes."

"Did you believe him?"

"I had no reason not to at the time."

Adrian looked at the document again.

The form had no verified signature.

No named authorised representative.

And no record of direct approval from Alexander.

Yet it had been used to establish a private nursery arrangement involving two newborn children.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was reading the latest update when Julian entered.

He carried a schoolbag over one shoulder.

"Mother?"

Victoria looked up.

"You're home early."

"Our last class was cancelled."

He put his bag down.

"Is there news?"

She hesitated.

"There is another document."

"Does it tell us where she is?"

"Not yet."

Julian sat beside her.

"How many documents do we need?"

Victoria smiled sadly.

"I wish I knew."

He looked towards the old nursery door.

"Do you think she likes books?"

Victoria followed his gaze.

"I hope so."

"Why?"

"Because I have kept some of your old storybooks."

Julian considered that.

"Those are terrible."

Victoria laughed.

"You used to like them."

"I was five."

"That doesn't make them terrible."

He grinned.

Then his expression became serious again.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"If she comes home, will she have to live here?"

Victoria was quiet.

"She will be welcome here."

"But what if she doesn't want to?"

"Then we will respect that."

Julian frowned.

"I don't understand."

Victoria took his hand.

"Your sister will be eighteen. She will have her own thoughts, habits, friends and plans. We cannot expect her to arrive and immediately feel that this house is home."

"But it is her home."

"It may become her home."

Victoria brushed a hand over his hair.

"That is something we must build with her, not demand from her."

Julian thought about this.

Then he nodded.

"Okay."

He picked up his schoolbag.

"I'll keep the list, then."

Victoria smiled.

"How many things are on it now?"

"Thirty-six."

She laughed.

"Of course."

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was having lunch with Isabelle.

Nicholas had joined them, but he was mostly occupied with his telephone.

Elena looked at him.

"Are you listening?"

"Yes."

"What did I say?"

"Something important."

She stared at him.

Nicholas smiled.

"See? I was listening."

Isabelle laughed.

Elena shook her head.

"I was asking whether you wanted to come with us to the bookstore tomorrow."

Nicholas put his phone down.

"Which bookstore?"

"The one near the university."

"Why?"

"Because Isabelle wants to look at some books."

"I own books."

Elena looked at him.

"Congratulations."

Nicholas sighed.

"Fine. I'll come."

Isabelle smiled.

"You don't have to."

"I know. That's why I'm coming."

Elena finished her lunch.

"Good. We can stop for coffee afterward."

Nicholas raised a finger.

"I have one condition."

"What?"

"No one is allowed to spend two hours comparing notebooks."

Elena looked at Isabelle.

"He's referring to himself."

Nicholas shook his head.

"I am surrounded by unreasonable people."

The conversation continued.

Neither girl knew that the adults were examining documents that might change their understanding of the first week of their lives.

And neither knew that the two men who had been listed as receiving representatives had never been identified as the people who actually took them home.

The following morning, Edward returned to the archive office.

The forensic examiner had completed a preliminary review of the newly recovered envelope.

There was something unusual about the third page.

The photocopy contained a faint impression of an earlier signature.

The examiner placed an enlarged image on the monitor.

"Look here."

Edward leaned closer.

A series of marks appeared beneath the approval field.

They were not part of the typed form.

They appeared to be the remains of a signature that had been erased or covered before the document was copied.

"Can you recover it?"

"Possibly. But the image is incomplete."

The examiner adjusted the contrast.

Several letters became visible.

Edward recognised the first name.

**ALEXANDER.**

But the surname was missing.

He looked at the examiner.

"Does this prove he signed it?"

"No. It proves that a signature resembling his name was present on an earlier version of the document. We cannot establish whether it was genuine."

Edward nodded.

"Continue."

The examiner adjusted the image again.

A second line emerged beneath the signature.

It appeared to be a verification notation.

**VERIFIED AGAINST ORIGINAL AUTHORITY —**

The rest was obscured.

Edward stared at the screen.

"Can you recover the final word?"

The examiner shook his head.

"Not from this copy."

Edward looked at the document again.

Someone had removed the signature.

But the verification notation suggested that the original had once been accepted as authentic.

He took out his phone and called Adrian.

"We've found evidence that the authorisation form originally contained a signature."

"Your father's?"

"Possibly. The signature resembles his, but we cannot verify it."

"Then we need the original."

"Yes."

"Where is it?"

Edward looked at the envelope.

There was one more clue.

The authorisation form contained a reference to a document storage location.

Not the Aurelius archive.

Not the hospital.

A private legal repository.

**VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL — BOX 114.**

Edward sent the reference to Adrian.

Adrian replied almost immediately.

"That repository was transferred when the firm was absorbed into Vale International Legal Group."

"Can they still access it?"

"I'm checking."

Edward looked again at the two receiving representatives.

M. Hale.

C. Vale.

The original arrangement had been explicit.

One man was supposed to receive one child.

The other was supposed to receive the second.

But the later correction had said neither child was released to the guardians listed in the original instructions.

That meant something had changed.

And the change had happened after the children had already been admitted to the private nursery.

Edward returned to the first page.

At the bottom was a final handwritten note.

It had been overlooked because it was written along the edge.

*No direct handover is to occur without the presence of the originating representative.*

Edward read it twice.

Then he looked at the blank authorised representative field.

Someone had created an arrangement that required a third person to supervise the handover.

Yet no one had recorded that person's name.

At 4:30 p.m., Adrian received a reply from Vale International Legal Group.

The old repository still existed.

Box 114 had not been destroyed.

However, its access classification was unusual.

It was marked as a restricted legal holding.

Only the original originating representative could authorise its opening.

Adrian read the message again.

Then he forwarded it to Alexander.

A few minutes later, his father called.

"Have you found out who the originating representative was?"

"Not yet."

"Then how do we open the box?"

"We may need a court-authorised preservation order."

Alexander was silent.

"Apply for it."

"That could take time."

"Then begin now."

Adrian looked at the archive reference.

"There is something else."

"What?"

"The repository record includes a note from the original legal office. It says the box contains the final handover documentation."

Alexander stopped moving.

"Final handover?"

"Yes."

"Meaning?"

"Presumably, the records documenting who actually received the children."

For the first time that day, Alexander sounded hopeful.

"Then we may finally learn where they went."

Adrian looked at the final line of the message.

He had not yet told his father about it.

Because it was the part that made the entire discovery more complicated.

The repository record also contained a warning.

**BOX 114 — ACCESS RESTRICTED. DO NOT RELEASE TO AURELIUS FAMILY OFFICE.**

Alexander waited.

"Adrian?"

Adrian read the warning again.

"The original legal office specifically prohibited the box from being released to our family office."

There was a long silence.

Then Alexander spoke.

"Who did they intend to receive it?"

Adrian looked at the record.

The answer was printed beneath the warning.

**AUTHORISED RECIPIENT: THE MOTHER OF THE CHILDREN.**

He felt a chill.

There had been two mothers.

Victoria Aurelius.

And Catherine Valmont.

And the original legal office had apparently intended the final handover records to be delivered to one of them.

But the archive contained no indication of which mother.

Nor did it explain why the other was excluded.

And until Box 114 was opened, the identity of the intended recipient would remain unknown.
