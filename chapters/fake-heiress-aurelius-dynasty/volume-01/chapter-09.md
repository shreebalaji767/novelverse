# **Chapter 9 — The Missing Page**

The Aurelius family had a rule about secrets.

It was never written down, never discussed at family meetings and never included in the official household guidelines.

Nevertheless, everyone knew it.

If Alexander Aurelius asked for a document, it was brought to him.

If he asked for an explanation, he expected the truth.

And if he asked a question twice, someone was probably about to have a very unpleasant day.

At nine the following morning, the head of the family archives was summoned to his office.

The man arrived carrying a leather folder.

Alexander stood beside the windows, looking down at the busy streets surrounding Aurelius Global Headquarters. Adrian sat at the conference table with a laptop open. Damien stood near the door, having been asked to attend because the old file involved a security liaison.

“Sit,” Alexander said.

The archivist, Mr. Bennett, placed the folder on the table.

“I understand you have questions about a historical family-office record.”

“I have several,” Alexander replied. “Let's begin with the person who submitted it.”

Bennett opened the folder.

“The surviving index identifies the submitting party as an external special liaison. Unfortunately, the name field is blank.”

“Why?”

“The original entry was made using an internal classification code. The identity of the representative was recorded separately.”

“Where?”

“In the supporting authority document.”

“Which is missing.”

“Yes, sir.”

Adrian turned his laptop around.

“The file was transferred to Charles Vale's legal office two days before our daughter disappeared. The supporting authority was retained separately, and access was restricted to the originating representative and senior counsel.”

Alexander looked at the screen.

“Who approved that restriction?”

“The record attributes it to Charles Vale.”

“Did he have the authority?”

“Under the terms of the original legal arrangement, yes.”

Damien folded his arms.

“Who was the liaison?”

Bennett hesitated.

“We have a partial reference code.”

He placed another sheet on the table.

The code consisted of several letters and numbers, followed by a departmental designation.

**EFO-SL/07**

Adrian read it aloud.

“External Family Office, Special Liaison, reference seven.”

“Correct,” Bennett said.

“Does the family office have a list of the liaisons assigned that year?”

“It should.”

“Should?”

“The relevant personnel register is incomplete. Several historical records were transferred during the restructuring of the family office.”

Damien's eyes narrowed.

“Transferred where?”

“To the former security administration.”

“Which no longer exists.”

“Not in its original form, sir.”

Alexander finally turned from the window.

“Find the records.”

Bennett nodded.

“And Mr. Bennett?”

“Yes, sir?”

“I want the original authorisation, not another index.”

“Of course.”

“Do not tell me what the record probably contained. Find out what it actually contained.”

Bennett closed the folder.

“I understand.”

After he left, Adrian remained silent.

Alexander returned to his desk.

“Do you remember authorising an identity-protection matter that year?” Adrian asked.

Alexander did not answer immediately.

“There were dozens of family-office matters.”

“Anything involving the hospital?”

“No.”

“Charles Vale?”

“He handled several legal matters for the family.”

“Do you remember giving him authority to restrict a document?”

Alexander looked at his son.

“No.”

Adrian's expression changed slightly.

“Then someone may have used your authorisation.”

“Or I may have authorised something whose details I no longer remember.”

“That seems unlikely.”

“It is not impossible.”

Damien spoke from the doorway.

“If someone used the family office to arrange something involving the hospital, we need to know whether they acted alone.”

Alexander nodded.

“That's why you're here.”

He looked at Adrian.

“Until we understand the original request, this remains an internal investigation.”

Adrian closed his laptop.

“And the search?”

“Continues.”

Alexander's voice hardened.

“Nothing takes priority over finding your sister.”

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was discovering that Nicholas had a very particular understanding of lunch.

He considered it a sacred family tradition.

He also considered any restaurant that did not serve an adequate dessert selection to be fundamentally unreliable.

“You said we were having lunch,” Elena reminded him as they walked toward the entrance hall.

“We are.”

“You said it would be quick.”

“It will be.”

“Then why did you bring a restaurant guide?”

Nicholas looked down at the booklet in his hand.

“Preparation.”

“For lunch?”

“An important meal deserves proper planning.”

Elena shook her head.

“Where are we going?”

“Somewhere nearby.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It's a surprise.”

“That's worse.”

Nicholas grinned.

They were interrupted by Isabelle, who came down the stairs carrying her handbag.

“Are you both going out?”

Nicholas nodded.

“Lunch.”

Isabelle looked at Elena.

“Can I come?”

Elena answered before Nicholas could.

“Of course.”

Nicholas looked at the two girls.

“I was planning a peaceful outing.”

“Then you should have gone alone,” Elena said.

Isabelle laughed.

“I'm sorry. I can stay here if—”

“No,” Elena said immediately. “You should come.”

Nicholas sighed theatrically.

“Fine. But I reserve the right to complain if either of you takes more than twenty minutes to decide what to eat.”

“That's unreasonable,” Isabelle said.

“I'm already regretting this.”

They left together.

The restaurant Nicholas had chosen was a comfortable place in the business district, popular with university students and office workers.

It was not the most expensive restaurant in the city, which Elena appreciated.

Nicholas had initially suggested a private dining room at a luxury hotel.

Elena had rejected the idea.

“Why?” he had asked.

“Because we're having lunch, not negotiating a merger.”

“Those things can happen at the same table.”

“Not today.”

Isabelle had quietly supported Elena.

Now the three of them were seated near a window, looking over the menu.

Nicholas examined the dessert section first.

Elena noticed.

“You haven't even looked at the main course.”

“Dessert is the main course.”

“Then why is it listed separately?”

“Poor administrative planning.”

Isabelle laughed.

For the first time since arriving at the Valmont Estate, she seemed genuinely relaxed around Nicholas.

She asked him about university, and he told her stories about Elena's habit of correcting his grammar when they were younger.

“I did not correct your grammar.”

“You absolutely did.”

“You used the wrong word.”

“I was seven.”

“You were eleven.”

“That is a technicality.”

Isabelle smiled.

“You two have always been like this?”

“Unfortunately,” Elena said.

Nicholas looked pleased.

“It's called sibling affection.”

“It's called being annoying.”

“Same thing.”

Their food arrived.

For a while, the conversation remained light.

Then Isabelle asked a question.

“Do you think things will go back to normal?”

Nicholas looked up.

“What things?”

“The family.”

Elena put down her glass.

Isabelle continued.

“I know it's only been a few days, but everything feels different. Everyone is trying to be careful around each other.”

Nicholas leaned back.

“That's because we're all trying to figure out how to handle something none of us expected.”

“Do you think they'll treat me differently once the investigation is finished?”

Nicholas considered her question.

“I don't know.”

Isabelle looked down.

“But I don't think you should spend every day worrying about what everyone will think of you.”

Elena nodded.

“You're allowed to have your own life here.”

Isabelle gave a small smile.

“I know. I just don't want to be the reason you lose yours.”

Elena was quiet.

Then she said, “You aren't.”

It was a simple answer.

It did not erase the uncertainty.

But Isabelle seemed to accept it.

Nicholas raised his glass.

“To having lunch without discussing hospital records.”

Elena raised hers.

“To that.”

Isabelle joined them.

“To lunch.”

Nicholas looked satisfied.

“Finally, a sensible family decision.”

Edward spent most of the morning speaking with former hospital employees.

The hospital's official response remained unchanged.

Its administration acknowledged that the birth records had inconsistencies. It also acknowledged that the original transfer documentation was unavailable.

But nobody seemed eager to explain why.

At eleven, Daniel arrived at Edward's office with a new lead.

“We found the records department's old staff register.”

Edward looked up.

“From the hospital?”

“Yes. The year of the births.”

“Who was listed?”

“Margaret Ellis, Dr. Samuel Whitmore and three administrative employees. Two have retired. One is deceased.”

“And the remaining two?”

“One lives outside the city. The other is still working in medical administration.”

“Have they agreed to speak?”

“One has. The other declined.”

“Which one agreed?”

“Former records clerk, Helen Brooks.”

Edward stood.

“Arrange a meeting.”

Daniel placed another document on the desk.

“There is something else.”

Edward glanced at it.

“What?”

“The hospital's original computer system was replaced several years ago. However, a private backup company retained some of the older data during the transition.”

Edward looked at him.

“Did the hospital know?”

“Apparently not. The company was contracted to preserve selected administrative records.”

“Can we access them?”

“We're checking the legal requirements.”

Edward read the company name.

It was unfamiliar.

“Who owns it?”

Daniel answered.

“A private records-management firm. It was acquired several years ago by a larger technology company.”

“Which one?”

Daniel pointed to the page.

Edward's eyes narrowed.

**Aurelius Technologies.**

He looked up.

“Of course.”

Daniel gave him a cautious glance.

“Do you think it's connected?”

“I think we have found another reason not to jump to conclusions.”

Edward placed the paper on the desk.

“Find out whether the company still has the archive. And don't request anything without confirming the legal basis.”

Daniel nodded.

As he left, Edward looked again at the company name.

The Aurelius connection was becoming harder to dismiss.

But he was beginning to suspect that the family itself might not be the only place where answers were hidden.

That afternoon, Isabelle visited the greenhouse again.

Elena was already there, sitting on a small bench with a book open on her lap.

“Am I interrupting?” Isabelle asked.

“No. I was taking a break.”

Isabelle sat beside her.

For a few minutes, they watched the gardener tending to the plants outside.

“I spoke to Mother this morning,” Isabelle said.

Elena looked at her.

“About what?”

“University.”

“Oh?”

“She wants me to consider the university you attend.”

“That would be convenient.”

“Would you mind?”

Elena blinked.

“Why would I mind?”

“I don't know. Maybe you'd prefer some space.”

Elena closed her book.

“Isabelle, you don't need to choose a university based on whether I attend it.”

“I know.”

“But if you like the programme, you should apply.”

Isabelle nodded.

“I suppose I wanted to ask.”

Elena smiled.

“Then you have your answer.”

Isabelle looked toward the greenhouse door.

“Can I ask something else?”

“Go ahead.”

“Do you ever feel angry?”

Elena was silent.

“Sometimes,” she admitted.

“At me?”

“No.”

“At them?”

Elena looked down at her book.

“Sometimes I feel angry at the situation. Sometimes I'm confused. Sometimes I wonder why nobody noticed anything for eighteen years.”

Isabelle listened without interrupting.

“But I don't think anger would make any of this clearer,” Elena continued. “And I don't want to spend every day looking for someone to blame.”

“Even if someone is responsible?”

“Then the investigation should find them.”

Isabelle looked at her.

“You make it sound simple.”

“It isn't simple. I just don't know what else to do.”

A breeze moved through the greenhouse.

Isabelle lowered her gaze.

“I used to imagine meeting my family.”

Elena waited.

“I thought there would be a big moment. Everyone would be happy, and I would finally belong somewhere. I never imagined there would already be another girl in my place.”

Elena's expression softened.

“I don't think you took my place.”

“But the family has to decide what happens now.”

“Maybe they do.”

Elena looked out toward the garden.

“But I don't think being someone's daughter has to be a competition.”

Isabelle said nothing.

After a moment, she leaned back against the bench.

“I hope you're right.”

“So do I.”

At five in the afternoon, Jonathan Pierce received a call from the archive department.

The original file had been located.

It was stored in a restricted legal repository, separate from the general Vale practice archive.

The department could not release it without confirmation from the current legal custodian and the originating client.

Pierce called Edward immediately.

“They've found the file,” he said.

Edward stood up from his desk.

“Does it include the missing authorisation?”

“I don't know. The inventory lists a sealed supplementary envelope.”

“Can it be opened?”

“Not without formal approval.”

“Who can approve it?”

“The original client or their authorised successor.”

Edward paused.

“Alexander Aurelius?”

“According to the index, yes.”

“Then we need to contact him.”

Pierce hesitated.

“Mr. Valmont, I should warn you that the archive department has already notified the Aurelius Family Office about the retrieval request.”

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

“So they know.”

“They know that an old family-office file has been located. I cannot say what they know about your investigation.”

Edward ended the call.

For several seconds, he remained standing.

Then he picked up his phone and called Richard.

“Father.”

“What happened?”

“The original file has been located.”

Richard's voice sharpened.

“And the missing page?”

“There is a sealed supplementary envelope.”

“Who has it?”

“The former Vale legal archive.”

“Can we obtain it?”

“Only with the original client's approval.”

Richard was quiet.

“Then we will have to ask Alexander Aurelius.”

“Yes.”

“Arrange it carefully.”

Edward looked at the window.

“I think they may already know we're looking.”

At Aurelius Global Headquarters, Alexander received the same notification.

He read it twice.

Then he placed the paper on his desk.

Adrian stood opposite him.

“The archive department confirms the original file has been found,” Adrian said.

“Where?”

“Restricted legal storage.”

“And the supplementary envelope?”

“Still sealed.”

Alexander's expression hardened.

“Who requested access?”

“A former associate of Charles Vale's firm. Jonathan Pierce.”

“Who is he working for?”

Adrian paused.

“The Valmont family.”

Alexander looked at him.

“Valmont?”

“Yes. Richard Valmont.”

For the first time that morning, Alexander's composure visibly shifted.

“Why are the Valmonts investigating this?”

“We don't know. Their request refers to a hospital record investigation from the same period.”

Alexander stood.

“Which hospital?”

Adrian told him.

Alexander looked toward the framed photograph on the shelf behind his desk.

It showed Victoria holding their newborn daughter.

The baby was wrapped in a pale blanket.

Her face was turned slightly toward the camera, eyes closed, one tiny hand resting near her cheek.

Alexander had seen the photograph thousands of times.

He remembered the day it had been taken.

He remembered leaving the hospital believing his family was complete.

He remembered returning home without his daughter.

And he remembered the years that followed.

“Get the hospital records,” he said.

Adrian nodded.

“Already in progress.”

“Contact the legal custodian. I want the envelope preserved exactly as it is. No one opens it without my approval.”

“Understood.”

Alexander turned back to the desk.

“Arrange a meeting with Richard Valmont.”

Adrian looked surprised.

“Personally?”

“Yes.”

“Do you want me to attend?”

“You and Damien.”

Adrian nodded.

“And Father?”

Alexander looked at the photograph again.

“Find out why his family is investigating the same night ours lost a child.”

That evening, Elena was sitting at the dining table when Catherine entered.

“Have you finished your work?”

“Almost.”

Catherine looked at the books scattered around her.

“Are you planning to study all night?”

“No. I have one more chapter.”

“That's what you said yesterday.”

Elena smiled.

“I'll finish soon.”

Catherine sat beside her.

For a moment, she simply watched Elena write.

Then she reached over and gently moved a loose strand of hair away from her face.

“You've been quiet lately.”

Elena stopped writing.

“Have I?”

“A little.”

“I'm fine.”

Catherine did not immediately respond.

“Is there anything you want to ask me?”

Elena looked at her.

There were several things.

Why had the family albums been moved?

Why did everyone lower their voices whenever she entered a room?

Why did Richard look at her as though he was trying to solve a problem he could not explain?

Why had the house begun to feel unfamiliar?

But the question that came out was different.

“Do you think we'll still be a family when the investigation is finished?”

Catherine's face changed.

“Elena…”

“I don't mean the legal part. I mean us.”

Catherine reached for her hand.

“Yes.”

“Even if the results are different from what everyone expects?”

“Yes.”

Catherine answered quickly, perhaps too quickly.

Elena studied her mother's face.

“Promise?”

Catherine squeezed her hand.

“I promise that what we have shared will never become meaningless.”

It was not quite the answer Elena had asked for.

But it was the answer Catherine could give.

Elena looked down at their joined hands.

“Okay.”

Catherine kissed the top of her head.

“Finish your chapter. Then get some rest.”

She left the dining room.

Elena looked at the open book.

For several minutes, she did not read another word.

At ten-forty, Alexander Aurelius stood alone in his study.

The sealed-envelope notification was still on his desk.

He had asked the legal department to retrieve the original authorisation from the archive.

The response had arrived moments earlier.

There was a complication.

The supplementary envelope had two seals.

One belonged to Charles Vale.

The other belonged to a representative of the Aurelius Family Office.

The legal custodian had confirmed that the first seal was intact.

The second, however, had been broken.

Not recently.

Not during the archive transfer.

The seal had been broken eighteen years earlier.

Alexander read the report again.

Someone had opened the envelope before it was placed into storage.

Someone had removed its contents.

And the person who had done so had left the outer file untouched.

He picked up his phone.

“Adrian.”

His son answered immediately.

“Yes, Father?”

“Come to my study.”

“Now?”

“Now.”

A few minutes later, Adrian entered.

Alexander handed him the report.

Adrian read it.

“The envelope was opened?”

“Eighteen years ago.”

“Who had access?”

“That's what we need to find out.”

Adrian turned the page.

A final note had been added by the archive custodian.

**The envelope contains a secondary document sleeve. The sleeve remains sealed.**

Adrian looked up.

“Then the missing page may still be inside.”

“Possibly.”

“Why was the outer seal broken?”

Alexander's expression was grim.

“Someone needed access to the contents.”

“And the secondary sleeve?”

“Either they did not know it existed, or they were unable to open it.”

Adrian looked at the report again.

“Who was authorised to access the envelope?”

Alexander answered quietly.

“Only three people.”

“Who?”

“Charles Vale.”

Alexander paused.

“The originating representative.”

“And?”

“Myself.”

Adrian's eyes narrowed.

“Do you remember opening it?”

“No.”

“Then the third person is the one we need.”

Alexander nodded.

He looked toward the photograph of his daughter.

“Find the name.”

At the Valmont Estate, Elena finally closed her book.

She turned off the bedside lamp and settled beneath the covers.

Tomorrow would bring another lecture, another breakfast, another day of trying to understand the unfamiliar shape her life was taking.

She hoped it would be an ordinary one.

She had no idea that two powerful families were preparing to meet.

She had no idea that a sealed document had survived eighteen years.

And she had no idea that the investigation into her birth had just uncovered something more troubling than a missing hospital record.

Someone had known enough to open a secret file.

Someone had known enough to remove a document.

And someone had known that the truth needed to remain hidden.

**But they had left one thing behind.**

A secondary sleeve.

Still sealed.

Still waiting.

And on its outer surface, written in the careful handwriting of a long-dead lawyer, were five words:

**For the child's mother only.**
