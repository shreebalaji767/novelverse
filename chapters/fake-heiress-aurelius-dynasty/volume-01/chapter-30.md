# Chapter 30 — Received on Behalf of Another

The photocopy of the ninth-day release register was placed under a document examination lamp.

Edward stood beside the archivist, watching as the faded writing became clearer under the angled light.

The paper had been folded for years. Its creases crossed several handwritten entries, and the carbon transfer had left some letters barely visible.

The archivist adjusted the magnification.

"There's another layer of writing."

"Under the original entry?"

"Yes. Someone added a notation after the document had been completed."

Edward leaned closer.

The words were still incomplete.

**RECEIVED FOR THE—**

The remainder disappeared beneath a darkened section of the paper.

"Can you recover it?"

"Possibly. I'll need to examine the original rather than the photocopy."

"Martha has agreed to provide it."

"Then we should wait."

Edward nodded.

He had learned that waiting for reliable evidence was often more useful than rushing towards an attractive conclusion.

He looked at the document again.

Charles Vale had signed as the receiving guardian for Infant B.

But the notation indicated that he had received the child on behalf of someone else.

Who?

And why had that person's identity been withheld?

He returned to his office with the photocopy.

On his desk were three separate files.

**MARCUS MORELLI.**

**MARTHA VOSS.**

**CHARLES VALE.**

A fourth file had recently been opened.

**DANIEL MERCER.**

The former legal assistant had been connected to the ninth-day arrangements, and someone had reactivated an old telephone account after Martha received the interview request.

The connection was becoming difficult to ignore.

Edward opened Mercer's personnel record.

There was a photograph attached.

A young man in his early thirties, neatly dressed, with a narrow face and dark hair.

The photograph was eighteen years old.

He would be in his early fifties now.

Edward studied it.

"Where are you?" he murmured.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena had a different problem.

Her university application portal had stopped accepting one of her documents.

The system displayed a message stating that the uploaded academic transcript could not be verified.

She had checked the file three times.

The document was correct.

The format was correct.

The file size was below the limit.

And yet the portal refused to accept it.

She sat at the dining table, looking increasingly offended by the computer.

Nicholas walked past.

"What's wrong?"

"The university portal."

"Has it insulted you?"

"Not directly."

"Then perhaps there's still hope."

Elena turned the laptop towards him.

"Look."

Nicholas examined the message.

"Have you tried refreshing?"

"Yes."

"Different browser?"

"Yes."

"Uploading it again?"

"Four times."

Nicholas nodded gravely.

"Then I believe the computer has declared war."

Elena sighed.

"I'll contact the admissions office."

"Good idea."

He leaned over the screen.

"Do you want me to help?"

"Do you know how to solve university application problems?"

"No."

"Then what would you help with?"

"Emotional support."

Elena looked at him.

"That is not particularly useful."

"I could bring food."

"That might be useful."

Nicholas grinned.

He left the room and returned several minutes later with a plate of sandwiches.

Elena accepted one.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

He sat opposite her.

"Have you heard from Singapore?"

"Not yet. This is only the preliminary application."

"Don't spend the entire day fighting with the portal."

"I won't."

"Good."

Elena took a bite.

Nicholas looked at the screen again.

"You know, if the university rejects you because of a file-upload problem, I'll personally complain."

"Please don't."

"I'll write a very stern letter."

"That would make everything worse."

"I'll use formal language."

"That doesn't make it better."

Nicholas laughed.

For a while, the two of them discussed the application.

He asked about accommodation.

She explained the difference between university residences and private student housing.

He suggested that she choose whichever was more convenient.

She pointed out that the costs were not identical.

He said he would rather she prioritised safety and comfort.

She reminded him that she intended to manage her own expenses.

Nicholas listened.

Then he nodded.

"All right. You make the decision. Just don't choose something unsuitable because you're trying to prove you can manage without help."

Elena looked at him.

"I won't."

"Good."

He returned to his sandwich.

There were some things he wanted to say.

That he would miss her if she went abroad.

That the house would feel different without her.

That he did not want the investigation to make her feel she had to leave.

But he said none of them.

Elena deserved to make her decision without having to carry everyone else's feelings.

Isabelle was in Catherine's office at the Valmont Foundation.

A stack of project reports lay on the desk.

She had spent the morning reviewing applications for a student support programme.

The work was not particularly glamorous.

There were forms with missing information, inconsistent income declarations, duplicate applications, and several reports that had been prepared in a hurry.

She was beginning to understand why Catherine often complained about administrative delays.

"This application has two different household income figures," Isabelle said.

Catherine looked at the page.

"Ask the regional office to verify it."

"And this one?"

"Missing supporting documents."

"Should I reject it?"

"No. Request the documents first."

Isabelle made a note.

She looked at the next file.

"This applicant has already received assistance through another programme."

"That doesn't automatically disqualify them. We need to establish whether the programmes overlap."

Isabelle nodded.

She had expected the foundation to involve generous donations, public events, and photographs of smiling beneficiaries.

Instead, she was discovering that most of its work involved making decisions that affected people who might never meet the people making them.

She liked that responsibility.

It was also intimidating.

"Mother?"

Catherine looked up.

"Yes?"

"Do you think I should study business administration or economics?"

Catherine smiled.

"Those are rather different questions."

"I know."

"What interests you more?"

Isabelle considered it.

"Understanding why organisations make certain decisions. How money moves through them. Why some programmes succeed and others fail."

"Then you might enjoy economics."

"But I also want to understand how to manage an organisation."

"Business administration would help with that."

Isabelle sighed.

"That's why I can't decide."

Catherine closed the report.

"You don't need to decide everything today."

"I know."

"And you don't have to choose a subject because you think it will make you a suitable Valmont daughter."

Isabelle looked at her.

Catherine's expression was gentle.

"Choose something you want to learn."

Isabelle lowered her eyes.

"Sometimes I still feel as though I'm trying to catch up."

"With whom?"

"Everyone."

Catherine understood.

Elena had grown up in the house.

She knew the routines, the staff, the family habits, and the unspoken rules.

Isabelle had arrived eighteen years later.

She was learning everything at once.

"You're not behind," Catherine said. "You simply started from a different place."

Isabelle looked at the reports.

"All right."

Catherine reached for another folder.

"Now, shall we see why the regional office has submitted the same invoice three times?"

Isabelle smiled.

"Yes."

The first useful information about Daniel Mercer arrived shortly before noon.

He had lived in four different cities after leaving Vale Private Counsel.

His employment history was inconsistent.

He had worked briefly for two accounting firms, then for a small legal consultancy, and finally as an independent document examiner.

His most recent business registration had been closed six years earlier.

There was no current address.

No active professional profile.

No public telephone number.

But there was one recurring detail.

Every time Mercer changed his residence, he used a different emergency contact.

The first was a woman named Martha Voss.

The second was an accountant named Peter Lang.

The third was a retired physician.

The fourth was a man named Marcus Morelli.

Edward read the last entry twice.

"Morelli?"

"Yes," the investigator replied over the telephone.

"How recent?"

"The emergency contact was listed on a residential medical registration form dated seven years ago."

"Where?"

"A private clinic in the northern district."

Edward stood.

"Does the clinic still have the record?"

"We're checking."

"Was Morelli listed under his real name?"

"Yes."

Edward looked at the file.

For seven years, Marcus Morelli had been connected to Daniel Mercer.

And Daniel Mercer had been connected to Martha Voss.

The three names formed a chain.

A chain that led back to the ninth day.

"Get the clinic records through the appropriate legal process," Edward said.

"Understood."

"And find out whether Mercer is still alive."

"We're working on it."

Edward ended the call.

He opened the document again.

There was something else.

Mercer's emergency contact entry had been made after Morelli's old residence was sold.

That meant the two men had maintained contact long after the original investigation should have ended.

Perhaps Morelli had not disappeared completely.

Perhaps he had simply chosen to remain somewhere nobody thought to look.

At Aurelius Global Headquarters, Adrian was reviewing the surviving records from Vale International Legal Group.

The firm had been contacted formally.

Its current partners had agreed to provide access to historical files, subject to legal restrictions.

The first batch had arrived that morning.

Most of it concerned ordinary legal work.

Corporate contracts.

Property transfers.

Estate planning.

Confidentiality agreements.

Then Adrian found the separation agreement.

Not the original.

A reference to it.

The document index stated:

**CONFIDENTIAL SEPARATION — M. MORELLI**

**APPROVED BY: CHAIRMAN'S OFFICE**

**SUPPORTING FILE: RETAINED BY ORIGINATING CLIENT**

**DUPLICATE: NOT AUTHORISED**

Adrian frowned.

The agreement had been deliberately kept outside the ordinary archive.

He opened the adjacent index entry.

**ORIGINATING CLIENT: NOT IDENTIFIED IN PUBLIC RECORD**

That was unusual.

A confidential separation agreement generally identified the parties involved, even if the details were restricted.

This one did not.

Adrian called the firm's records manager.

"I need clarification on reference M. Morelli."

"Yes, Mr. Aurelius."

"Your index says the supporting file was retained by the originating client."

"That's correct."

"Who was the originating client?"

"We don't have that information."

"Was the agreement signed?"

"The index confirms execution."

"By whom?"

"The surviving record does not specify."

Adrian leaned back.

"Then what exactly does your archive establish?"

"Only that a confidential separation agreement existed and that a supporting file was retained externally."

"Was there a payment?"

"There's a payment reference."

Adrian sat forward.

"What payment?"

"An administrative settlement."

"To Morelli?"

"Yes."

"How much?"

The records manager gave him the amount.

Adrian wrote it down.

It was substantial.

Not enough to suggest a life-changing fortune.

But more than an ordinary severance payment.

"Was it paid by Aurelius Global?"

"No. The payment originated from a private account."

"Whose account?"

"That information is restricted in the surviving record."

Adrian closed his eyes briefly.

Another anonymous source.

Another missing identity.

Another document that seemed designed to preserve the existence of an event while concealing who had arranged it.

"Send me the complete payment reference," he said.

"Certainly."

Adrian ended the call.

He looked at the document index.

Someone had paid Marcus Morelli to leave the Family Office.

Someone had arranged for the supporting file to remain outside the firm's archive.

And someone had made sure the Aurelius family could not easily discover who had initiated the separation.

The original release register arrived at the document examination office the next morning.

Martha had agreed to provide it under a formal chain-of-custody arrangement.

Edward attended personally.

The archivist placed the document inside a protective enclosure.

Unlike the photocopy, the original showed several handwritten corrections.

The archivist examined the first entry.

Then the second.

Finally, he moved to the notation beneath Charles Vale's signature.

"I've recovered most of it."

Edward leaned forward.

"What does it say?"

The archivist pointed to the line.

**RECEIVED FOR THE DESIGNATED FAMILY REPRESENTATIVE.**

Edward read it.

"Which representative?"

"The name was written separately. It appears to have been removed."

"Can you recover it?"

"Not from this section. The paper was scraped."

Edward looked closer.

There were shallow marks beneath the damaged area.

Someone had deliberately removed the name.

The archivist continued.

"There's another notation beside the first infant's release."

Edward moved around the table.

The writing was small.

**RECEIVED BY AUTHORITY OF THE ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE.**

"And the name?"

"Also removed."

Edward stared at the two entries.

Both receiving guardians had acted on behalf of someone else.

Neither had been recorded as the final decision-maker.

The actual authority had been deliberately concealed.

"Can you identify the person who removed the names?"

"No. The damage could have been made at any time after the document was completed."

Edward looked at the signatures.

"Could the names have been removed before the photocopy was made?"

The archivist checked the copy.

"No. The photocopy contains faint impressions of the original writing, although the names are not readable. That suggests the removal happened after the duplicate was prepared."

Edward felt a new question emerge.

If Martha had retained a copy before the names were removed, then the original had been altered later.

Someone had gone back to the register.

Someone had deliberately erased the identities of the people who authorised the releases.

"Was the document ever returned to the hospital archive?"

"We cannot establish that."

"Could someone have taken it?"

"Certainly."

Edward looked at the archivist.

"Who had access?"

"Hospital records staff, authorised legal representatives, and the people listed on the access register."

"Can we identify them?"

"Not yet."

Edward nodded.

"Please prepare a complete examination report."

He stepped away from the table.

The investigation had just established something more important than the identity of Charles Vale.

The two receiving guardians had not been the final authorities.

Both had acted on behalf of designated representatives.

And both representatives had been erased from the surviving document.

That evening, the Valmont and Aurelius families held another joint meeting.

It was smaller than the earlier meetings.

Only Richard, Catherine, Edward, Alexander, Victoria, and Adrian attended.

Sebastian had other business.

Nicholas was spending the evening with Elena and Isabelle.

The document examination report was placed on the table.

Edward explained the findings carefully.

"The original register confirms that Charles Vale received Infant B. But he did so on behalf of a designated family representative. The identity of that representative was removed."

Richard looked at the document.

"And Infant A?"

"Received by an unidentified woman, also acting under authority of another representative."

"Both authorities were removed?"

"Yes."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Then someone deliberately concealed the people who authorised the placements."

"That is the most likely explanation."

Victoria looked at the entries.

"Could the two representatives have been the mothers?"

Edward shook his head.

"There's no evidence of that. In fact, the records indicate that neither mother was informed of the infant assignments."

Catherine's hands tightened.

"Then who had the right to make the decision?"

"That's what we need to establish."

Adrian placed another document on the table.

"We also found a reference to Morelli's separation agreement. It was approved by the Chairman's Office, but the originating client was not identified in the surviving record."

Alexander looked at him.

"Could it have been authorised by me?"

"The approval field says Chairman's Office. It does not contain your verified signature."

Alexander read the document.

"I did not authorise a confidential settlement connected to Morelli's departure."

"Then someone used the office's authority again," Richard said.

"Possibly."

Alexander's voice was controlled.

"Or someone in the office approved it without informing me."

Adrian nodded.

"Either way, the payment came from a private account."

Victoria looked at the report.

"Could Eleanor have arranged it?"

Edward considered the possibility.

"She may have been involved. But we have no proof that she was the originating client."

Catherine spoke quietly.

"What if the person who selected the receiving guardians was trying to protect the children?"

Nobody answered immediately.

It was a possibility.

A troubling one.

If someone had deliberately concealed the children's identities to protect them, then the secrecy might have been motivated by fear rather than malice.

But that did not make the decisions harmless.

Two mothers had been kept from knowing what had happened to their children.

Two families had spent eighteen years without the truth.

And the children had grown up under identities that might not have been theirs by birth.

Alexander finally spoke.

"Whatever the intention, someone had no right to make those decisions without the mothers."

Victoria nodded.

"Agreed."

Richard looked at Edward.

"Where do we go from here?"

Edward closed the report.

"Daniel Mercer."

Daniel Mercer had been found.

He was living in a small town near the northern coast.

His current residence was registered under his own name.

He was fifty-two years old, unmarried, and employed as an independent records consultant.

The investigator had confirmed his identity through public records and a recent professional registration.

He had not been contacted.

Edward reviewed the information.

"How long has he lived there?"

"Nine years."

"Does he have visitors?"

"Occasionally. Mostly a woman in her sixties and a man who appears to be around the same age."

"Can we identify them?"

"The woman may be Martha Voss. The man is not yet identified."

Edward looked at the photograph.

The man was standing beside Mercer outside a small building.

His face was partly obscured.

But his posture was familiar.

Edward enlarged the image.

Then he compared it with the old photograph attached to Morelli's personnel file.

He stopped.

The resemblance was unmistakable.

The man in the recent photograph was older.

His hair was thinner.

His face was heavier.

But it was Marcus Morelli.

Edward sat back.

For eighteen years, the former Director of Family Affairs had remained hidden from the Aurelius family.

He had not left the country.

He had not disappeared into another identity.

He had simply lived somewhere quiet, maintaining contact with the people who had been involved in the ninth-day arrangements.

Edward picked up his telephone.

"Contact the investigator."

"Yes, sir?"

"Confirm that the man in the photograph is Marcus Morelli."

"We're working on that."

"Do not approach him until we have confirmation."

"Understood."

Edward looked at the photograph again.

There was one more detail.

A small object in Morelli's hand.

A folded envelope.

It appeared to have a handwritten label.

The investigator had not noticed it.

Edward enlarged the image.

The words were difficult to read.

But three letters were visible.

**114.**

Edward stared at them.

Box 114.

The restricted archive.

The documents that could only be released to the mother of the children.

Morelli had apparently been carrying something connected to it.

And he was still alive.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was preparing for bed when Julian appeared at her door.

He was holding his notebook.

"Are you busy?"

"Not particularly."

"Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

He sat on the edge of the sofa.

"Have they found her?"

Victoria understood immediately.

"No. Not yet."

"Have they found someone who knows where she is?"

"They've found people who may know what happened."

Julian looked at his notebook.

"That's different."

"Yes."

He opened the page containing his list.

"I changed some of the activities."

Victoria smiled.

"Did you?"

"Yes. I removed the amusement park."

"Why?"

"Lucien said we don't own one."

Victoria laughed softly.

"He's right."

"I know. I thought we could just go to one."

"That sounds more practical."

Julian looked at the page.

"I also added something."

"What?"

He turned the notebook towards her.

**Item 42: Let her choose the first family activity.**

Victoria read it.

Then she looked at him.

"That's a good idea."

"I thought so."

He closed the notebook.

"Do you think she'll like us?"

Victoria sat beside him.

"I hope she will."

"What if she doesn't?"

"Then we will have to give her time."

Julian nodded.

He seemed to be thinking about that.

After a moment, he stood.

"Good night."

"Good night, Julian."

He reached the door, then turned around.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"When we find her, can we tell her about the list?"

Victoria smiled.

"Only if she wants to hear about it."

Julian considered that.

"All right."

He left.

Victoria remained seated.

On the table was a photograph of the nursery, taken before the births.

She looked at it for a long time.

Then she carefully placed it inside the drawer.

There was no point in showing the photograph to a daughter who had not yet been found.

Not yet.

The following morning, Edward received confirmation.

The investigator had obtained a clearer photograph from a recent public event.

The man was Marcus Morelli.

There was no doubt.

Morelli was alive.

And he had been living under his own name.

Edward read the report.

Then he noticed the final paragraph.

**The subject has been observed entering a private legal archive facility on three occasions during the last six months.**

Edward called the investigator.

"Which facility?"

"Vale Historical Records Repository."

Edward looked towards the document cabinet.

The same repository that held the records of Vale Private Counsel.

"Does he have access?"

"Yes. He is listed as an external archival consultant."

Edward slowly put down the report.

Morelli had not merely been hiding.

He had been returning to the place where the missing records were kept.

And he had visited three times in the last six months.

"What documents did he access?"

"We're trying to obtain the access logs."

"Do that."

"There's one more thing."

Edward waited.

"On his most recent visit, he requested access to Box 114."

Edward's expression changed.

The box that had been sealed for eighteen years.

The box that could only be released to the mother of the children.

The box that contained records deliberately kept away from both families.

And Marcus Morelli had tried to open it.

"Was access granted?" Edward asked.

"No."

"Why?"

"The archive refused. His authorisation did not meet the requirements."

Edward looked at the report.

"Did he leave anything?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"A written request."

Edward waited.

The investigator read the final line aloud.

**I am no longer requesting access on behalf of the Family Office. I am requesting access as the person who completed the ninth-day release.**

Edward went very still.

Morelli was not simply returning to retrieve a document.

He was formally claiming responsibility for the decision that had separated the children.

And for the first time, he was no longer hiding behind the authority of the Aurelius Family Office.

He was asking to be recognised as the person who had done it.
