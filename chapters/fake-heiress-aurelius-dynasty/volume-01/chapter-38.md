# **Chapter 38 — The Woman Who Had Been Waiting**

Martha Voss did not leave immediately.

She remained seated, staring at the tenth-day acknowledgment.

Edward had placed the document between them.

Pierce had stopped writing.

Neither investigator wanted to interrupt her.

Finally, Edward spoke.

"Who was the woman?"

Martha shook her head.

"I don't know."

"You said she had been waiting for the child."

"That's what I was told."

"By whom?"

"Marcus Morelli."

Edward leaned forward.

"Morelli told you someone was waiting?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"On the tenth day."

"Before or after the infant returned to the hospital?"

Martha thought for a moment.

"After."

Edward exchanged a glance with Pierce.

"Tell us exactly what happened."

Martha folded her hands.

"I had been working in patient administration. I wasn't a nurse, and I wasn't responsible for the children's medical care. I handled documents, departmental access, and administrative arrangements."

"We know," Edward said.

"On the ninth day, I witnessed the two infants leaving the private nursery. I prepared the duplicate register because Mr. Morelli asked me to keep a copy."

"Did he explain why?"

"He said the original records might be changed."

"Did he say who might change them?"

"No."

Martha looked at the document again.

"On the following morning, he came back."

"Morelli?"

"Yes. He asked me to open a room in the old maternity wing."

"Which room?"

"Room fourteen."

Edward wrote it down.

"Was the room in use?"

"No. It had been reserved for administrative observation."

"Was an infant already there?"

"Yes."

"Did you recognise the child?"

"No."

"Was there an identification band?"

Martha nodded.

"But I wasn't allowed to examine it."

"Who gave you that instruction?"

"Mr. Morelli."

Edward paused.

"Why?"

"He said the child's identity was not to be recorded under the existing nursery register."

Pierce looked up.

"That makes no sense. The child had already been registered."

Martha shook her head.

"Not under the identity that mattered."

Edward felt the significance of the distinction.

"Did you see the identification band?"

"Only briefly."

"What did it say?"

Martha looked toward the window.

"I remember a letter."

"Which letter?"

"A."

Edward's pen stopped.

"You're certain?"

"Yes."

"Did you see the birth time?"

"No."

"Did you see the mother's name?"

"No."

Edward wrote down her answer.

Infant A had been released to Margaret on the ninth day.

The following morning, a child marked A had been placed in Room Fourteen.

But the records did not establish whether it was the same infant.

"Was the woman already in the room?" he asked.

Martha nodded.

"She was sitting beside the cot."

"Did she speak to you?"

"Not at first."

"What did she look like?"

"Young. Perhaps in her thirties. Dark hair. She was wearing ordinary clothes, not a hospital uniform."

"Did she give you her name?"

"No."

"Did you ask?"

"Yes."

"What did she say?"

Martha hesitated.

"She said I didn't need to know."

Edward waited for her to continue.

Martha looked increasingly uncomfortable.

"Did she appear to know the child?" he asked.

"She was holding her."

"That isn't what I asked."

Martha considered the question.

"She seemed familiar with her."

"How?"

"She knew how to hold her. She knew when she was uncomfortable. When the baby began crying, the woman didn't panic."

"Did she behave like the child's mother?"

Martha looked at him.

"I cannot tell you that."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't know."

Edward nodded.

He did not want her to turn an impression into a fact.

"Did Morelli introduce them?"

"No. He arrived a few minutes later."

"What did he say?"

"He told the woman that the room would remain available until further instructions."

"Did she agree?"

"She asked how long."

"What did he say?"

"He said he didn't know."

Martha paused.

"Then she asked whether the child's mother had been informed."

Edward felt his attention sharpen.

"What did Morelli say?"

"He said that the matter was being handled."

"Did she accept that?"

"No."

"What did she say?"

Martha looked down.

"She asked him whether the mother knew the child was alive."

Edward did not move.

"Did Morelli answer?"

"Not immediately."

"And then?"

"He said the mother had been given information."

"What information?"

"I don't know."

"Did the woman believe him?"

Martha gave a small, bitter laugh.

"No."

Edward looked at Pierce.

This was not Margaret's question.

It was another woman asking whether the mother knew her child was alive.

And she had asked it before the tenth-day transfer was completed.

"Did the woman remain in Room Fourteen?" Edward asked.

"For some time."

"How long?"

"About two hours."

"Did she leave with the infant?"

"No."

"Did anyone else collect the infant?"

"Not while I was there."

"Then what happened?"

"Morelli asked me to close the room."

"Where was the baby?"

"Still in the cot."

"And the woman?"

"She had left."

Edward frowned.

"She left the infant alone?"

"Not alone. A nurse was present by then."

"Did the nurse know the circumstances?"

"She had been told that the child was under temporary observation."

"Did you see the woman leave?"

"No."

"Did you know where she went?"

"No."

Martha took a breath.

"Before she left, she gave Morelli something."

"What?"

"A small envelope."

"Did you see what was inside?"

"No."

"Did Morelli open it?"

"Not in front of me."

Edward glanced at the acknowledgment.

"Was the envelope related to this document?"

"I believe so."

"Why?"

"Because he asked me to sign the acknowledgment immediately afterward."

Pierce pointed to the signature.

"Did you understand that you were accepting custody?"

"No. He told me it was an administrative receipt for the room and its contents."

Edward stared at her.

"Room and its contents?"

Martha nodded.

"He said the hospital needed a responsible person listed for the temporary arrangement."

"Did you object?"

"I asked why my name was being used."

"What did he say?"

"He told me that my name would not appear in the child's permanent record."

Edward looked at the wording.

**TEMPORARY CUSTODIAL RECEIPT ONLY.**

The document had been designed to look like a custody acknowledgment.

But Martha insisted she had never taken the child.

It was possible that the legal office had used her administrative responsibility as a substitute for the identity of the actual receiving guardian.

It was also possible that she was withholding something.

Edward did not decide either way.

"Mrs. Voss, did you ever see that child again?"

Martha looked at him.

"Yes."

"When?"

"That afternoon."

"What happened?"

"She was gone."

"Taken by whom?"

"I don't know."

"Did you ask Morelli?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

Martha looked toward the table.

"He said the child had gone where she was supposed to go."

The interview was interrupted by a call from the hospital archive manager.

Pierce answered.

He listened for several minutes before looking at Edward.

"There's another record."

Edward stood.

"What record?"

"The Room Fourteen observation sheet."

Martha went pale.

Pierce ended the call.

"The archive has located a carbon copy. It was filed separately from the maternity movement register."

Edward looked at Martha.

"Did you know it existed?"

She nodded slowly.

"Yes."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because I thought it had been destroyed."

"What does it contain?"

Martha swallowed.

"A name."

They requested the document immediately.

The carbon copy arrived through the archive's secure transfer process shortly after noon.

It contained only a few entries.

**ST. CATHERINE'S HOSPITAL — ROOM 14**

Tenth day after births · Temporary observation record

**10:42 a.m.**

Infant received for temporary observation.

Identification: A — verification pending.

**11:05 a.m.**

Receiving adult present.

Name: Not entered.

**12:18 p.m.**

Additional authorisation received.

**12:46 p.m.**

Infant released.

Receiving representative: **M. VOSS**

**1:02 p.m.**

Room cleared.

Carbon copy. Original record not located.

Edward read the final entries.

"Mrs. Voss, you said you didn't take the child."

"I didn't."

"But the record says you did."

"Then the record is wrong."

"Did you sign it?"

"No."

Pierce examined the carbon copy.

"The handwriting is different from the earlier acknowledgment."

Edward looked at the final line.

"Who entered the release time?"

Martha shook her head.

"I don't know."

"Was the infant physically handed to you?"

"No."

"Did you see who took her?"

Martha hesitated.

Then she nodded.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"Who?"

Martha's face had become very pale.

"I saw the person leave."

"Was it the woman from Room Fourteen?"

"No."

"Was it Morelli?"

"No."

"Then who?"

Martha looked at the two investigators.

"It was Charles Vale."

The name changed everything.

Edward reviewed the sequence.

Morelli had authorised a tenth-day transfer.

Martha had been assigned administrative responsibility for Room Fourteen.

An unidentified woman had visited the infant.

A further authorisation had arrived.

Then the record stated that Martha had received the child.

But Martha claimed Charles Vale had physically taken the infant.

"Did Charles tell you where he was taking her?" Edward asked.

"No."

"Did you ask?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"He said he was returning the child to the person who had originally requested her."

Edward stared at her.

"Which person?"

"He didn't say."

"Did he mention the Aurelius family?"

"No."

"The Valmont family?"

"No."

"Did he say the child was going to a mother?"

Martha hesitated.

"He said she was going home."

Edward wrote the phrase down.

*Going home.*

It was a familiar expression.

It appeared in several old family documents, but never with a clearly identified destination.

"Did Charles seem worried?"

"Yes."

"Angry?"

"No. He seemed tired."

"Did he know the woman had visited the child?"

"Yes."

"Did he meet her?"

"Not while I was there."

Edward paused.

"Did you see the envelope she gave Morelli?"

Martha nodded.

"Charles took it from him."

Edward looked at Pierce.

"You're certain?"

"Yes."

"Did Charles open it?"

"Not in front of me."

Martha looked at the document.

"He told me that whatever happened next, I was never to speak about Room Fourteen."

"Did he threaten you?"

"No."

"Then why did you keep quiet?"

Martha looked down.

"Because I had already signed a document I didn't understand. And because I was afraid I had helped someone take a child."

At the Aurelius residence, Edward delivered the update personally.

Alexander listened without interruption.

Victoria looked increasingly distressed.

"Charles Vale took the child himself?"

"That's what Martha says."

"Which child?"

"The record identifies the infant as A, but the identity has not been verified."

Victoria pressed her fingers against the table.

"Then Margaret may have received Infant A on the ninth day, and another child marked A was placed in Room Fourteen on the tenth."

"Possibly," Edward said. "But we cannot assume the identification band was correct."

Adrian studied the observation sheet.

"Could the child in Room Fourteen have been the same infant Margaret received?"

"Yes. If Margaret's child was returned to the hospital."

"But Margaret says the child remained with her."

"Her memory is incomplete, and we have not yet verified the full chronology."

Damien looked at the record.

"Charles took the infant somewhere."

"Yes."

"Could he have taken her to the receiving guardian?"

"That is what he claimed to Martha."

Lucien leaned forward.

"Then we need to find every person who received a child from Charles Vale."

Edward nodded.

"That's one of our priorities."

Victoria looked at the line marked **RELEASED**.

"Why would he use Martha's name?"

"To conceal the identity of the actual receiving person," Edward replied.

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Then the record was deliberately constructed to hide the destination."

"Yes."

That afternoon, Julian found Victoria in the library.

She was sitting with the photograph of Infant A.

He approached quietly.

"Mother?"

She looked up.

"Yes?"

"Is the investigation going badly?"

Victoria placed the photograph on the table.

"Not exactly."

"Then why do you look sad?"

She considered how to answer.

"We have discovered that someone moved a child from the hospital after the original arrangements failed."

"Was it our sister?"

"We don't know."

"Could it have been?"

"Yes."

Julian sat opposite her.

"Do you think she was scared?"

Victoria looked at the photograph.

"She was a newborn. She wouldn't have understood what was happening."

"But the people carrying her knew."

Victoria nodded.

"Yes."

Julian looked down.

"I don't understand why adults made everything so complicated."

Victoria gave a small, tired smile.

"Neither do I."

He hesitated.

"Do you think she'll want to meet us?"

Victoria reached across the table and held his hand.

"I hope she will."

"And if she doesn't?"

"Then we'll still want to know that she's safe."

Julian nodded.

He glanced at the photograph.

"Can I see it?"

Victoria turned it toward him.

He studied the infant's tiny face.

Then he asked, "Do we know whether this is her?"

"No."

"Okay."

He looked at the photograph a little longer.

"I wish we knew."

"So do I."

At the Valmont estate, Catherine received an unexpected visit from Richard.

He had been reviewing the legal investigation with Edward.

He placed a folder on the table.

"We need to discuss Charles Vale."

Catherine looked up.

"Why?"

"Because he physically received one of the infants on the ninth day, and he personally removed another infant from the hospital on the tenth."

Catherine stared at him.

"Another infant?"

"Possibly the same child. We don't know."

She opened the folder.

The name Charles Vale appeared several times.

She read the observation record.

Then she stopped at the phrase:

**He said she was going home.**

Catherine looked at Richard.

"Was he talking about our daughter?"

"We don't know."

"Did he ever speak to me about the child?"

"Not directly."

Catherine frowned.

She remembered the hospital.

The separate room.

The dark-suited man.

The administrative delays.

But Charles Vale?

She had met him only once, years later, at a legal conference.

Or had she?

She closed her eyes.

There was something familiar about the name.

"Richard."

"What?"

"I think I've seen him before the births."

Richard looked at her.

"Where?"

Catherine searched her memory.

"A hospital corridor."

"When?"

"After the delivery. I remember a man speaking to the administrator."

"Are you certain it was Charles?"

"No."

She shook her head.

"I only remember that he was carrying a folder."

Richard sat down.

"Why didn't you mention this earlier?"

"Because I didn't remember his face."

Catherine looked at the document again.

"Perhaps I still don't."

Edward spent the next two days tracing Charles Vale's movements.

The lawyer had died years earlier.

His private records had been divided between the Vale estate, the legal firm, and a private archive.

Most of the files had already been examined.

But one location had not.

A small records office in the old hospital district.

It had been closed shortly after the maternity wing was renovated.

Its contents had been transferred to a storage company.

Edward obtained permission to inspect the surviving boxes.

The first several contained routine legal correspondence.

The next held hospital invoices.

The final box was marked:

**PERSONAL — C. VALE**

Inside were appointment books, handwritten notes, and a small envelope.

The envelope had no address.

Only a date.

**TENTH DAY.**

Edward opened it carefully.

There was a photograph.

It showed Charles Vale standing outside the hospital.

Beside him was a woman holding a newborn.

The photograph was taken near the rear entrance.

The woman was turned slightly away from the camera.

Her face was visible.

Edward studied it.

He recognised her.

Not personally.

But from another photograph.

He opened his investigation folder and placed the images side by side.

The first showed the woman who had visited Margaret Ellison's home.

The second showed the woman in Room Fourteen.

The third showed the woman outside the hospital with Charles Vale.

They were the same person.

Edward looked at the photograph again.

On the back, Charles had written a short note.

**She has finally received the child she was waiting for.**

Below it was a name.

Not Victoria.

Not Margaret.

Not Martha.

**ELEANOR VALE.**

Edward slowly lowered the photograph.

For eighteen years, Eleanor Vale had been treated as a legal intermediary who had tried to protect two children.

Now the evidence suggested she had personally collected one of them.

But there was another problem.

The woman in the photograph was not holding the child marked A.

Edward turned the photograph over again.

A second handwritten line appeared beneath Eleanor's name.

**INFANT B — FINAL RECEIVING PARTY CONFIRMED.**

He stared at the words.

Eleanor Vale had not collected the child assigned to her.

She had received the other one.

And somewhere in the records, the identity of the child she had been waiting for remained hidden.
