# **Chapter 71 — The Woman Who Used Eleanor's Name**

Edward did not sleep particularly well.

The visitor register remained open on his desk long after midnight.

**Marianne Cross.**

The name appeared beside an appointment at Vale Private Counsel, entered under the identity of Eleanor Vale.

Three days later, the same visitor had returned.

This time, she had been accompanied by Gabriel Vale.

Edward read the entries again.

There were several possible explanations.

Marianne Cross could have been an employee using an authorised alias. She could have been an external consultant whose identity had been entered incorrectly. She could have been someone who deliberately impersonated Eleanor.

And the visitor register itself might have been incomplete.

He had no intention of deciding which explanation was correct before examining the supporting records.

At 7:15 the next morning, he called Jonathan.

"Find Marianne Cross."

"I've already started."

"Where?"

"Her last known address is an office building in the northern business district. The company listed her as an external records consultant."

"Is the company still operating?"

"No. It was dissolved twelve years ago."

"Who owned it?"

"Two shareholders. One was Charles Vale's legal services company."

Edward looked at the visitor register.

"And the other?"

Jonathan paused.

"Gabriel Vale."

Edward leaned back.

That was not an incidental connection.

But it still did not establish what Marianne had done.

"Get the incorporation documents," Edward said. "I want to know what services the company actually provided."

"Understood."

"And check whether Eleanor Vale knew her name was being used."

Eleanor Vale agreed to meet Edward that afternoon.

She was an older woman now, living in a quiet residential neighbourhood. Her home was modest, with carefully arranged furniture and a collection of old photographs on the mantelpiece.

She recognised Edward immediately.

"You have found another document," she said.

"Yes."

"Does it concern the children?"

"It concerns a visitor to Charles Vale's office."

Eleanor looked at him.

"Who?"

"Marianne Cross."

Her expression changed.

"Marianne."

"You know her?"

"I knew of her."

"Did she work for Charles?"

"Not officially."

"Then what was her role?"

Eleanor considered the question.

"She handled correspondence and administrative matters when Charles wanted something kept outside the ordinary office records."

Edward sat forward.

"Was she an employee?"

"No. She was an external consultant."

"What did she consult on?"

"Records management, private placements, and family-office correspondence."

"Did she have medical qualifications?"

"Not that I know of."

"Legal qualifications?"

"She had worked in legal administration. I don't know whether she held any formal qualifications."

Edward placed the visitor-register copy on the table.

"She visited Charles's office under your name."

Eleanor examined the entry.

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then she shook her head.

"I did not make that appointment."

"Did you authorise her to use your name?"

"No."

"Did you know she had done so?"

"Not at the time."

Edward showed her the second entry.

"Three days after the Meridian discharge, she returned with Gabriel Vale."

Eleanor's expression became guarded.

"That would have been around the period when the records were being moved."

"Which records?"

"The hospital records. The receiving instructions. The temporary placement files."

"Did you know where they were being moved?"

"Not always."

"Did Marianne Cross handle those transfers?"

"Sometimes."

Edward waited.

Eleanor looked at the visitor register.

"She was useful to Charles because she could arrange things without involving the main office."

"Such as?"

"Collecting sealed documents. Delivering instructions. Obtaining signatures. Coordinating appointments."

"Could she authorise child transfers?"

"No."

"Could she receive a child?"

"Not under the arrangements I knew about."

"Did she ever claim to be a member of the Aurelius family?"

Eleanor looked up sharply.

"Why would she do that?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

"Isabella Vale remembers a woman calling herself Mrs. Aurelius. She described a silver ring with a dark stone and a scar on the left thumb."

Eleanor became very still.

Then she looked toward the window.

"That sounds like Marianne."

"Are you certain?"

"I remember the ring."

"Did you ever hear her call herself Mrs. Aurelius?"

"No."

"Did she have any connection to the Aurelius family?"

"Not one that I knew of."

Edward studied her.

"Did Charles know she was using that name?"

Eleanor's answer was immediate.

"Yes."

"How do you know?"

"Because I heard him correct someone once. He said that Marianne was not to use the Aurelius name in written correspondence."

Edward leaned forward.

"When was this?"

"During the first month after the births."

"Why did he say that?"

"I don't know. He didn't explain."

"Did you ask?"

"I did."

"What did he tell you?"

Eleanor's expression hardened.

"He told me it was none of my business."

Edward wrote down the answer.

Marianne had not merely been mistaken for a member of the Aurelius family.

She had apparently used the name deliberately.

And Charles knew.

"Why did you allow her to handle the records?" Edward asked.

Eleanor looked at him.

"I didn't allow her to do anything. Charles did."

"Did you object?"

"Several times."

"Why?"

"Because she had no medical authority. She was not a hospital administrator. She had no right to decide which medical records were transferred or withheld."

"Did she make those decisions?"

"Not formally."

"Informally?"

Eleanor did not answer.

Edward waited.

"She would arrive with an instruction from Charles. She would say the matter had already been approved. Sometimes the instruction was genuine. Sometimes it was incomplete."

"Did you challenge her?"

"Yes."

"What happened?"

"She would tell me to contact Charles."

"And when you did?"

"He would tell me the same thing."

Edward understood the pattern.

Responsibility was continually redirected.

Marianne referred to Charles.

Charles referred to the Family Office.

The Family Office denied authorising the action.

Meanwhile, the records continued moving.

"Did you ever see her with a child?" Edward asked.

Eleanor looked down.

"Once."

Edward became attentive.

"Where?"

"At a private medical residence."

"Which one?"

"I don't remember the formal name. It was used for temporary observation and recovery."

"Was the child Infant A?"

"I don't know."

"Was it Child 28-B?"

"I don't know."

"Did you see the child clearly?"

"Yes. It was a baby girl."

"Did Marianne hold her?"

"No. The baby was in a cot."

"Who was present?"

"Marianne, Gabriel, and a nurse."

"Did you hear them discuss the child's identity?"

Eleanor hesitated.

"Marianne asked whether the baby had been entered under the correct reference."

"What did the nurse say?"

"That the reference had not been confirmed."

"And Gabriel?"

"He told her to leave the medical staff alone."

Edward made a note.

"Did Marianne accept that?"

"No."

"What did she do?"

Eleanor's expression became distant.

"She asked whether the baby would be transferred before the end of the day."

Edward looked at her.

"Did it happen?"

"I don't know. I left."

Edward returned to the office with more questions than answers.

Marianne Cross had been involved in administrative work.

She had used Eleanor's name without permission.

She had apparently called herself Mrs. Aurelius.

She had visited a private medical residence with Gabriel Vale.

And she had asked about the identity and transfer status of an unidentified baby.

But none of those facts established which child she had seen.

Jonathan was waiting with the company records.

"Found something?"

"More than I expected."

Edward took the papers.

The company had been registered as Cross Administrative Services.

Its stated purpose was records management and private office coordination.

The two shareholders were Marianne Cross and Gabriel Vale.

Charles Vale's company had provided the initial capital.

The company had operated for less than four years.

Its registered office was a private address rather than a commercial building.

Edward examined the financial statements.

"Where did its income come from?"

"Mostly from Charles's legal services company and two private family-office accounts."

"Which family offices?"

"One was the Aurelius Family Office."

Edward looked up.

"Was Alexander aware?"

"We don't know. The payments were made through a consolidated administrative account."

"Who approved them?"

"The finance department processed them under the external liaison reference."

"EFO-SL/07?"

Jonathan nodded.

Edward examined the payment dates.

Several transactions coincided with the period when the hospital records had been transferred.

One payment had been made three days after Child 28-B's discharge from Meridian.

The recipient was Cross Administrative Services.

The description read:

**Special records coordination and residential closure.**

Edward stared at it.

"How much?"

Jonathan showed him.

The amount was substantial.

"Was there a supporting invoice?"

"Yes."

"What's the service description?"

Jonathan passed him the invoice.

It contained three lines.

- Collection and verification of residential documents.
- Coordination of receiving representative.
- Closure of temporary identity file.

Edward read the final line again.

"Temporary identity file."

"Yes."

"Does it name the child?"

"No."

"Does it give a reference?"

Jonathan pointed to the bottom.

**MCC-RR/092-28B.**

Edward placed the invoice beside the discharge record.

Marianne had been paid for work connected to Child 28-B.

The payment was made after the child left Meridian.

It did not prove she had physically received the child.

But it established that she had been responsible for coordinating the closure of the child's temporary identity file.

"Who approved the invoice?" Edward asked.

Jonathan turned to the last page.

A signature appeared.

Not Alexander's.

Not Victoria's.

It belonged to Gabriel Vale.

Edward looked at him.

"Gabriel approved the payment to his own company?"

"Apparently."

"Was there any conflict-of-interest declaration?"

"No."

Edward closed the folder.

That was no longer a minor administrative irregularity.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander received the payment records before dinner.

Adrian had reviewed the original financial allocation and identified the source account.

"The money came from an authorised administrative budget," he said. "But the individual invoice was not part of the chairman's approval."

Alexander examined the document.

"Who processed it?"

"External accounts."

"Who reviewed it?"

"Gabriel Vale."

"Who approved it?"

"Gabriel."

Alexander looked at the date.

"Then he approved a payment to a company in which he was a shareholder."

"Yes."

"Was the company disclosed to the Family Office?"

"Not in the records we have."

Victoria looked at the invoice.

"Could the company have been created specifically for these arrangements?"

"Possibly," Adrian said. "Its operations were limited, and most of its income came from Charles Vale's company."

Damien examined the payment description.

"Residential closure."

Lucien leaned over.

"That sounds harmless until you see the reference."

Julian sat quietly at the far end of the table.

He had been listening to the adults discuss the documents for several minutes.

"Does closing an identity file mean the child was moved somewhere else?"

Edward answered through the conference screen.

"Not necessarily. It could mean the administrative file was closed while the child remained in the same place."

"Then why pay someone to do it?"

"That is what we are trying to establish."

Julian frowned.

"Wouldn't the hospital know where the child went?"

"Some departments may know. Others may only have received paperwork."

Alexander looked at his youngest son.

"Julian."

"Yes?"

"Let Edward finish the investigation before drawing conclusions."

Julian nodded.

He looked back at the invoice.

"I just don't understand why adults made something as simple as a child's name so difficult."

Nobody had an easy answer.

The next morning, Edward received a call from Dr. Helen Morris at Meridian.

"We found the original residential file," she said.

Edward sat up.

"Is it complete?"

"Not entirely. But it contains a document we had not identified before."

"What document?"

"A receiving confirmation from the private residence."

"Does it identify the child?"

"Only by temporary reference 28-B."

"Who signed it?"

"That is why I called."

Edward waited.

Dr. Morris continued.

"The signature belongs to a woman named Marianne Cross."

Edward was silent.

"Was she listed as the receiving representative?"

"No. Margaret Voss remained the official representative."

"Then what was Marianne signing?"

"An acknowledgement of document collection."

"What documents?"

"The temporary identity file, the medical discharge summary, and the residential review."

Edward looked at the payment invoice.

Marianne had collected the file.

She had been paid to close it.

And she had signed the acknowledgement.

"Was there a destination address?" he asked.

"Yes. The same private residence listed on the discharge record."

"District 4, Building 19?"

"Correct."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

"Can you identify the address?"

"We have an old property register. The building number is incomplete, but the district reference corresponds to a private residential complex."

"Who owned it?"

"We're checking."

"Was the child physically delivered there?"

"The acknowledgement does not establish that."

Edward understood.

It was another record of paperwork moving.

Not necessarily a record of a child moving.

"Send me the document."

"I will."

By the afternoon, the property register had arrived.

District 4, Building 19 referred to a private residence owned by a company called Northbridge Residential Services.

The company had been registered to provide temporary accommodation for individuals under confidential protection arrangements.

Its director was Charles Vale.

Its administrative manager was Marianne Cross.

And its listed medical liaison was Gabriel Vale.

Edward stared at the three names.

The same three people appeared repeatedly.

Charles arranged the financial and residential structures.

Gabriel handled medical and administrative liaison.

Marianne coordinated records, visits, and residential closure.

They had created an organisation capable of receiving people without placing their identities in ordinary institutional records.

But whether they had used it lawfully remained unknown.

Edward requested the original resident register.

The company had been dissolved, but its records were held by a storage provider.

The retrieval would take several days.

For the moment, the available documents established only that Child 28-B had been assigned a place at the Northbridge residence.

They did not establish that the child had arrived.

Nor did they establish that she remained there.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was helping Nicholas sort through a collection of old family photographs.

He had brought several boxes from the storage room, claiming that the library needed reorganising.

Elena suspected he had simply wanted an excuse to avoid a meeting.

She held up a photograph of herself at approximately six years old.

Her hair was tied into two uneven ponytails.

She was standing beside a small cake, looking deeply offended by something outside the frame.

Nicholas laughed.

"You hated that dress."

"I did not."

"You refused to wear it until Mother promised you could change after the photographs."

Elena examined the picture.

"I remember the dress."

"It was expensive."

"That explains why I hated it."

Nicholas shook his head.

"Some things never change."

She placed the photograph on the table.

"Do we have more from that year?"

"Probably."

"I'd like copies."

"Why?"

"For my own records."

Nicholas looked at her.

"You're making an archive?"

"Something like that."

"Of yourself?"

"Of my life."

He considered the answer.

"That's a good idea."

Elena smiled.

"Don't sound so surprised."

"I wasn't."

She returned to the box.

Neither of them knew that, in another building, an investigator was examining an eighteen-year-old residential register that might contain a record of a child whose name had never been made permanent.

At 9:20 that evening, Jonathan called Edward.

"The storage provider located the resident register."

"Can they send it?"

"They have scanned the relevant pages."

"Anything useful?"

Jonathan hesitated.

"Yes. There is an entry for 28-B."

Edward opened the file.

The register contained the names of people who had stayed at the Northbridge residence, along with their arrival dates, departure dates, and responsible representatives.

The entry for 28-B was short.

**Arrival: Day 96.**

**Temporary identity: 28-B.**

**Receiving representative: Margaret Voss.**

**Administrative coordinator: Marianne Cross.**

**Medical liaison: Gabriel Vale.**

Edward examined the next column.

**Departure: Day 103.**

He looked at Jonathan.

"Where?"

Jonathan read the destination.

"Transferred to an external residential facility."

"Which one?"

"Not named."

Edward looked back at the page.

"Who authorised the departure?"

Jonathan turned the scan.

A signature appeared beneath the instruction.

**Margaret Voss.**

Edward's expression tightened.

"Did she sign this?"

"We haven't asked her yet."

"Do that tomorrow."

He continued reading.

There was another note beneath the departure entry.

**Identity file to remain closed. No family name to be entered.**

Then, in a different handwriting:

**Receiving party to be informed that the child is not the Aurelius infant.**

Edward stopped.

He read the sentence again.

The document did not say the child was not an Aurelius.

It said the receiving party was to be informed that the child was not the Aurelius infant.

That was different.

It implied that someone had identified another child as the Aurelius infant.

Or at least claimed to have done so.

Edward enlarged the scan.

At the bottom of the page, beside the instruction, was a handwritten reference.

**A-17.**

He looked through the earlier records.

The reference appeared once before.

On the sixth-day transfer document for Infant A.

The same Infant A taken home by Gabriel Vale.

Edward slowly lowered his hand.

The investigation had spent weeks trying to establish whether Child 28-B, Infant A, IV-204, and the child associated with the proposed name Elena were connected.

Now the Northbridge register suggested that someone had deliberately separated 28-B from an infant identified as A-17.

But the document did not say who the Aurelius infant actually was.

It only recorded what the receiving party was supposed to be told.

Edward called Jonathan again.

"Find out who received Child 28-B on the one hundred and third day."

"I'll try."

"And get Margaret's explanation of the departure signature."

"Understood."

Edward looked once more at the handwritten instruction.

**The child is not the Aurelius infant.**

If that statement had been accurate, it meant someone knew which child was supposed to be the Aurelius infant.

If it had been false, it meant someone had deliberately separated two children by assigning them different identities.

Either way, the person who wrote the instruction had known more than the official records admitted.

And the reference **A-17** led directly back to Gabriel Vale.
