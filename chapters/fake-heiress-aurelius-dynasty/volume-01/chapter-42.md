# **Chapter 42 — The Mother Who Sent the Letter**

The handwriting examiner arrived at the Valmont legal office at nine in the morning.

Edward had requested an urgent comparison of the eleventh-day letter with every surviving maternal document.

The examiner, Dr. Helen Foster, placed three transparent sleeves on the conference table.

"Before I give you my findings," she said, "I want to clarify what these documents can and cannot establish."

Richard sat at the head of the table. Sebastian and Edward were beside him. Jonathan Pierce had joined them remotely.

Dr. Foster opened the first sleeve.

"This is the eleventh-day letter addressed to Marcus Morelli. The phrase 'The Mother' appears at the bottom."

She placed the second sleeve beside it.

"This is the original hospital maternal identification form attributed to Victoria Aurelius."

Then the third.

"And this is the corresponding form attributed to Catherine Valmont."

Edward leaned forward.

"Can you identify the writer?"

"Not conclusively. The maternal forms contain very little handwriting. There are names, dates, and several signatures, but the available samples are limited."

She pointed to the eleventh-day letter.

"However, the phrase 'The Mother' was written by the same person who completed the body of this letter. It was not added by the archive or by someone who handled the document later."

"Can you compare it with either maternal form?"

"I can exclude neither woman."

Richard frowned.

"Then the comparison has established nothing."

"It has established that the letter was not altered after it was written. It also establishes that the sender deliberately chose not to provide a name."

Edward looked at the letter.

"Could the sender have been Eleanor Vale?"

Dr. Foster considered the question.

"Her handwriting samples are available. The letter does not appear consistent with her usual writing. But I would not call that a definitive exclusion without more material."

"Could it have been Morelli?"

"His writing is different."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"So we still don't know who sent it."

"Correct," Jonathan replied.

Dr. Foster packed the sleeves.

"One more observation. The letter was folded before the ink had completely dried."

Edward looked at her.

"Does that matter?"

"It suggests it was written in a hurry. There are slight transfers between the folded surfaces."

"Can you estimate how long before it was folded?"

"Not precisely. But the writer probably did not wait very long."

Edward looked down at the date.

Eleventh day.

Whatever had happened, someone had wanted Morelli to receive that instruction quickly.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was preparing to leave for a foundation meeting when Adrian entered the sitting room.

"Mother, we have an update."

She placed her handbag down.

"Have they identified the sender?"

"No."

"Then what is the update?"

"The letter was written by someone who deliberately concealed their identity. The handwriting cannot be matched conclusively to either you or Catherine."

Victoria looked at him.

"Could it have been Eleanor?"

"Unlikely, but not conclusively excluded."

Victoria sat down.

"Read it to me again."

Adrian took out the copy.

He read the sentence aloud.

*The person who came for Infant A has now been identified, and her involvement makes the original arrangement impossible.*

Victoria remained silent.

"Mother?"

"Whoever wrote that knew the person who came for Infant A."

"Yes."

"And knew that the person's involvement was serious enough to stop the arrangement."

"Yes."

Victoria looked at the window.

"Then perhaps she wasn't trying to retrieve her own child."

Adrian considered that.

"What do you mean?"

"If she had simply discovered that her daughter was alive, she might have demanded that the child be returned. But this letter asks for a replacement guardian. It doesn't ask for the child."

"That could mean the sender didn't know which infant was hers."

"Or that she knew something we don't."

Adrian sat opposite her.

"Do you remember anyone contacting you after the birth?"

Victoria closed her eyes.

She had been asked that question repeatedly.

She remembered the hospital room.

The doctors.

The exhaustion.

The explanations.

The waiting.

She remembered being told that her daughter needed additional observation.

She remembered being assured that the child was alive.

She remembered asking when she could hold her.

But she did not remember receiving a letter.

"No," she said.

"Nothing at all?"

"Not that I can recall."

Adrian nodded.

Victoria looked at him.

"Have you asked Catherine?"

"Edward has."

"And?"

"She remembers nothing either."

Victoria's expression became distant.

"Then perhaps neither of us sent it."

"That is possible."

"But someone wanted us to believe that a mother had intervened."

Adrian did not answer.

Victoria picked up her handbag.

"Continue looking."

Elena's interview preparation was not going well.

She had written three versions of her academic statement.

The first was too formal.

The second sounded as if she were applying for a position at a bank.

The third was, in her opinion, suspiciously similar to the first.

She sat at the dining table with her laptop open.

Nicholas walked in carrying a plate.

"Food."

Elena looked at him.

"What is it?"

"Sandwiches."

"Why?"

"Because you haven't eaten."

"I had breakfast."

"Four hours ago."

"That's not a medical emergency."

"Eat."

She looked at the plate.

"Thank you."

Nicholas sat opposite her.

"How's the interview preparation?"

"Terrible."

"Why?"

"I don't know how to explain my goals without making them sound like a collection of impressive words."

"Then don't use impressive words."

Elena stared at him.

"That's your advice?"

"Yes."

She sighed.

"Everyone is becoming philosophical."

Nicholas took a sandwich.

"Just tell them what you want."

Elena looked at the screen.

"I want to understand how businesses work. Not just how they make money, but how they grow, how they fail, how decisions affect people. I want to learn how to build something that can survive without depending on someone else's name."

Nicholas stopped chewing.

Elena continued.

"I want to be able to make decisions because I understand what I'm doing, not because someone tells me what to do."

Nicholas looked at her.

"That sounds like a good answer."

"It sounds too personal."

"Why?"

"Because it is."

He put the sandwich down.

"Maybe that's the point."

Elena considered that.

Then she typed a new paragraph.

Nicholas watched her for a moment.

"You're going to do fine."

"Don't jinx it."

"I'm not."

"Then stop looking so confident."

He laughed.

Elena finally smiled.

For the next half hour, they worked quietly.

When Nicholas eventually left, she returned to her statement and deleted the formal introduction.

She began again.

This time, she wrote in her own words.

Isabelle's scholarship proposal had reached its first internal review.

Catherine had invited two foundation administrators to examine it, and they had returned with several questions.

The largest concerned eligibility.

One administrator suggested restricting the programme to students with consistently high grades.

Isabelle disagreed.

"That would exclude students who have improved significantly but had a difficult academic history."

The administrator folded her hands.

"We need measurable standards."

"I agree. But measurable doesn't mean identical."

Catherine looked at Isabelle.

"Explain."

Isabelle opened her folder.

"I want to use academic performance as one factor, but not the only one. We can consider improvement, financial circumstances, and the applicant's circumstances. We should also have an independent review panel for unusual cases."

The administrator considered it.

"That will require more work."

"Yes."

"And more time."

"Yes."

"Are you prepared to manage that?"

Isabelle nodded.

"I am."

Catherine watched her daughter.

There was no triumph in Isabelle's expression.

No desire to prove that she deserved the family name.

She simply wanted the programme to work.

The administrator made another note.

"We'll revise the proposal and review it again."

After the meeting, Isabelle remained in the office.

Catherine poured two cups of tea.

"You handled that well."

"She didn't agree with everything."

"She wasn't required to."

Isabelle accepted the cup.

"I thought being part of the foundation would be easier."

"Why?"

"Because everyone would assume I could make decisions."

Catherine smiled faintly.

"Being allowed to make a decision and knowing how to make one are different things."

Isabelle looked at her.

"Did you know what you were doing when you first started?"

"Not always."

"What did you do?"

"I asked questions. I made mistakes. I learned."

Isabelle looked toward the proposal.

"Then I suppose I'll have to do the same."

"Yes."

Catherine reached over and adjusted one page that had slipped out of place.

"And you don't need to do everything immediately."

Isabelle nodded.

Edward's next appointment was with Margaret Vale.

The late Eleanor Vale's niece had been cooperative throughout the investigation, but Edward suspected she knew more than she had disclosed.

She met him in the same small office as before.

"I have something else," she said before he could begin.

Edward sat down.

"What?"

Margaret opened her handbag and removed a small envelope.

"This was among Eleanor's personal correspondence. I found it after our last meeting."

The envelope was old and slightly yellowed.

It had no address.

Only a handwritten date.

**ELEVENTH DAY.**

Edward looked at it.

"Why wasn't this included with the other papers?"

"Because it was inside a separate folder. I didn't know it was connected."

"Have you opened it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Margaret looked at him.

"Because it was marked for someone else."

Edward turned the envelope over.

There was a short inscription on the back.

**For the woman who asked whether the child was alive.**

He looked up.

"Who was that?"

Margaret shook her head.

"I don't know."

Edward carefully opened the envelope.

Inside was a small folded note.

The writing was Eleanor's.

*You asked me whether the child was alive.*

*I told you that she was.*

*You asked whether she was the child you had been promised.*

*I could not answer.*

*You must not come to the hospital again. If you are seen there, the arrangement will collapse.*

*Wait until the person who has agreed to receive her is confirmed.*

*I will not allow another child to be moved without verification.*

There was no signature.

Edward read it twice.

"Do you know who asked Eleanor those questions?"

"No."

"Did she ever mention this woman?"

Margaret thought.

"Not directly."

"Try to remember."

"She received many visitors. Some were business associates. Some were people connected to the foundation. There was one woman who came to the house shortly after the hospital incident."

Edward leaned forward.

"Do you remember her?"

"Not clearly. She was dressed plainly. She didn't look like someone who belonged in Eleanor's usual social circle."

"Did she meet Eleanor?"

"Yes. In the small drawing room."

"Did she stay long?"

"Perhaps twenty minutes."

"Did she have a name?"

Margaret hesitated.

"I remember Eleanor calling her something."

"What?"

Margaret closed her eyes.

"Mrs. Vale, I think."

Edward frowned.

"She called the visitor Mrs. Vale?"

"No. The visitor called Eleanor that. Eleanor called her..."

Margaret opened her eyes.

"I can't remember."

Edward put the note carefully back into its sleeve.

"When did this meeting take place?"

"I don't know exactly. But it was after Eleanor brought Infant B home."

"Before she handed the child to the next guardian?"

Margaret thought again.

"Possibly."

Edward stood.

"Thank you. This is important."

Margaret looked worried.

"Was the child safe?"

Edward paused.

"We're still trying to establish what happened."

"That isn't what I asked."

He met her gaze.

"I know."

Margaret looked down.

"I hope you find her."

Edward left without answering.

That afternoon, Jonathan called him.

"We've found something in the original Family Office ledger."

Edward returned to the legal office immediately.

Jonathan had placed two entries side by side.

The first was the eleventh-day authority suspension.

The second was a payment record.

**PAYMENT AUTHORISED — PRIVATE ACCOUNT.**

**PURPOSE: TEMPORARY RECEIVING ARRANGEMENT.**

**BENEFICIARY: NOT DISCLOSED.**

Edward examined the date.

"Eleventh day?"

"Yes."

"Who authorised it?"

"The approval field contains the initials M.M."

"Morelli."

"Likely."

Edward looked at the amount.

It was substantial.

Not enough to purchase property or fund a permanent household, but far more than a routine hospital expense.

"Who received the money?"

"That's what we're investigating."

"Was it paid directly to the guardian?"

"No. It went through an intermediary."

"Which intermediary?"

Jonathan turned the page.

**VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL.**

Edward stared at the entry.

"Charles."

"Most likely."

"Then Charles was arranging another placement."

"Or funding the existing one."

Edward looked at the payment description.

Temporary receiving arrangement.

The phrase appeared repeatedly in the records.

Temporary.

Provisional.

Pending verification.

Yet the children had remained separated for eighteen years.

"Can we trace the account?"

"Not through the ordinary archive. The bank records were transferred to a successor institution, and the account was closed."

"Then request them."

"That may require a court order."

"Prepare the application."

Jonathan nodded.

Edward picked up the payment record.

Something about the amount bothered him.

It was too large for a simple caretaker payment.

Too small for a permanent legal arrangement.

It suggested a household had been established, not merely a child delivered.

"Find out whether this money was paid in one instalment or several."

Jonathan checked the ledger.

"Three."

Edward looked at him.

"When?"

"Eleventh day, twenty-eighth day, and ninety-second day."

Edward wrote the dates down.

The first payment had been made immediately after the replacement request.

The second came more than two weeks later.

The third came three months after the births.

Someone had continued supporting the arrangement long after the hospital incident.

And someone had expected it to continue.

That evening, Alexander received a separate report from Adrian.

"Morelli's separation agreement contains a payment clause," Adrian said.

Alexander looked up.

"What kind of payment?"

"Monthly compensation for a period of twelve months, followed by a single settlement."

"Was that unusual?"

"Not by itself. But the source account wasn't the ordinary Family Office account."

Alexander read the details.

"Whose account?"

"The payment was authorised through a private account established under the chairman's office."

Alexander looked at Adrian.

"Mine?"

"Yes."

"That is impossible."

"I know."

"Who had access?"

Adrian opened another folder.

"Three people had administrative authority over the account. You, the former finance director, and the Director of Family Affairs."

Alexander looked at the last title.

"Morelli."

"Yes."

"Could he have used it without approval?"

"Possibly. But the transaction records show that the first payment was authorised using your delegated approval code."

Alexander stood.

"That code was restricted."

"Apparently, someone had access."

Victoria entered the room.

"What happened?"

Alexander handed her the report.

She read it slowly.

"Someone used your authority to fund the arrangement."

"Yes."

"Did Morelli know?"

"We don't know."

Victoria placed the report down.

"Then he may not have been the person who selected the guardians."

Alexander looked at her.

"Explain."

"If someone had access to your account and your authority, they could have arranged the payments without Morelli's knowledge."

Adrian nodded.

"That is one possibility."

Victoria looked at the final settlement date.

"Or Morelli knew and accepted responsibility for it."

Alexander turned toward the window.

For the first time, he considered that Morelli's departure might not have been caused by a disagreement over the children.

It might have been caused by the discovery that someone had used the Family Office to carry out an unauthorised arrangement.

"Find the former finance director," he said.

Adrian nodded.

"He died six years ago."

Alexander closed his eyes.

"Then find his records."

At the Valmont Estate, Elena finally finished her interview statement.

She read it once.

Then again.

It was shorter than the previous versions.

It did not mention her family.

It did not mention the DNA test.

It did not mention the uncertainty surrounding her birth.

It explained what she wanted to learn and why.

She saved the document.

Nicholas appeared in the doorway.

"Finished?"

"Yes."

"Good."

He looked at the screen.

"Can I read it?"

Elena hesitated.

Then she turned the laptop toward him.

Nicholas read quietly.

When he finished, he nodded.

"This sounds like you."

"Is that good?"

"Yes."

She closed the laptop.

"Now I have to prepare for the actual interview."

"One problem at a time."

Elena smiled.

For a few minutes, she simply sat there.

Then she looked at Nicholas.

"Do you think things will ever feel normal again?"

Nicholas didn't answer immediately.

He sat down beside her.

"I don't know."

"That's not very reassuring."

"I'd rather tell you the truth."

Elena nodded.

He continued.

"But I think we can make things better. Not by pretending nothing happened. Just by figuring out how to live with what we know."

She looked at the closed laptop.

"And what we don't know?"

"We'll deal with that too."

Elena gave a small smile.

"You're becoming annoyingly sensible."

"I've always been sensible."

She laughed.

Neither of them noticed the message arriving on Edward's phone upstairs.

It was from Jonathan.

The bank had located the intermediary account.

The account holder's name was not Morelli's.

It was not Charles Vale's.

It was not Eleanor Vale's.

It belonged to a woman whose identity had appeared only once before in the investigation.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

And the first payment had been made on the eleventh day.
