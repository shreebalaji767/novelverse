# **Chapter 150 — The Transfer That Was Refused**

The day-162 transfer request had been rejected.

Edward read the rejection notice twice.

**DOCUMENT:** Original Channel Control Register\
**REQUESTED TRANSFER:** Personal Custody\
**CURRENT CUSTODIAN:** V-17 / Exception Control\
**REASON FOR TRANSFER:** Independent preservation review\
**STATUS:** NOT APPROVED\
**REJECTION AUTHORITY:** ORIGINATING OFFICE

There was no name beside the rejection authority.

No explanation.

No indication of who had requested the transfer.

Only a handwritten remark at the bottom:

*Existing custody arrangement remains necessary until the receiving identity process is completed.*

Edward placed the notice beside the day-162 clarification.

The anonymous writer had wanted the register removed from V-17.

Someone else had refused.

And the reason for refusal was the same unfinished receiving identity process that had prevented the original procedure from reaching P-04.

"Can we identify the person who submitted the request?" he asked.

Jonathan shook his head.

"The request itself is missing. We only have the rejection notice and a reference in the transfer index."

"Who maintained the index?"

"V-17."

"Then we'll need the original custody register."

"It's listed as restricted."

Edward looked at him.

"Everything important seems to be."

The original custody register was stored in a private archive maintained by Meridian.

Unlike the ordinary transfer records, it contained the names of people who had physically handled restricted documents.

Access required an authorisation from the current custodian and an independent preservation officer.

Edward submitted a request.

The response arrived the next morning.

**ACCESS APPROVED FOR PRESERVATION REVIEW ONLY.**

It was signed by Meridian's current archive director.

The register was brought into a secure examination room.

Dr Mira Sen attended to document its condition.

Edward opened it to day 162.

The first entry was the transfer request.

**REQUEST RECEIVED:** 09:12\
**DOCUMENT:** Original Channel Control Register\
**REQUESTED DESTINATION:** Personal Custody\
**REQUESTING OFFICE:** Restricted Correspondence Division\
**REQUESTING REPRESENTATIVE:** Not recorded

The next entry documented the refusal.

**DECISION:** Transfer not approved.\
**REASON:** Current custody authority remains active.\
**FOLLOW-UP:** No further action.

Edward turned the page.

A separate handwritten note appeared in the margin.

*The register must not remain under the control of the office whose status is being reviewed.*

He stopped.

"That's the same anonymous writer," he said.

Dr Sen examined the handwriting.

"Consistent with the previous samples."

"Can we determine whether this was written before or after the refusal?"

"The ink crosses the printed ruling but does not overlap the rejection stamp. It appears to have been added after the decision was recorded."

"Could the writer have seen the rejection?"

"Yes."

Edward looked at the note again.

Someone had challenged the custody arrangement directly.

Not because the register was missing.

Because the office holding it was also responsible for maintaining the disputed exception status.

"Was the note entered into the official register?" he asked.

Dr Sen shook her head.

"No. It is a marginal annotation. There is no formal amendment reference."

Edward turned another page.

The next entry was dated day 164.

**DOCUMENT REVIEW REQUEST — ORIGINAL CHANNEL CONTROL.**

The requesting representative field contained a name.

Not a code.

Not initials.

**ELEANOR VALE.**

Edward looked up.

"Eleanor requested the transfer?"

"According to this entry," Jonathan said.

"Why was her request rejected?"

Jonathan turned to the attached decision.

The reason was different from the day-162 refusal.

*Originating representative may review preservation records but may not assume independent personal-channel custody.*

Edward read the sentence.

Eleanor had been allowed to preserve the originating records.

She had not been allowed to control the protected principal's communication channel.

That restriction was consistent with the original separation of responsibilities.

But it left the register in V-17.

Eleanor Vale agreed to meet Edward that afternoon.

She read the day-164 request and did not deny submitting it.

"I wanted the original channel register transferred to an independent custodian," she said.

"Why?"

"Because V-17 was retaining documents that concerned the same process it was administering."

"Did you believe someone had altered the register?"

"I had reason to believe the meaning of the R.V. notation was being expanded."

"Did you know who had entered it?"

"No."

"Did you believe Charles had done it?"

Eleanor paused.

"I believed he had allowed the expanded wording to remain in circulation. That is not the same as knowing who entered the original notation."

Edward nodded.

"Why did you choose Personal Custody as the destination?"

"Because the register concerned personal communication. It should not have remained under the exclusive control of the office managing the proposed receiving arrangement."

"Was Personal Custody an independent office?"

"It was supposed to be."

"Was it formally appointed?"

"No."

Edward looked at her.

"Then why request the transfer there?"

"Because I believed the preservation officer could accept the register temporarily without exercising authority over its contents."

"Was that permitted?"

"It should have been, under the preservation provisions."

"Should have been?"

Eleanor gave a restrained smile.

"The problem with that process was that every office believed another office had to approve something first."

Edward placed the rejection notice before her.

"Who rejected your request?"

"I don't know. The notice came from V-17."

"Did you challenge it?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"I requested the underlying authority."

"Was it supplied?"

"No."

Eleanor looked at the handwritten marginal note.

"I remember this."

"You recognise the handwriting?"

"No. I remember the sentence."

"Did you write it?"

"No."

"Did you know who did?"

"I suspected it was someone who had been reviewing the channel restrictions. But I never established their identity."

Edward waited.

Eleanor continued.

"At the time, I was concerned that the original procedure was being withheld while the same office retained control over the documents that would establish whether withholding it was justified."

"Did you believe P-04 had been contacted?"

"No."

"Did you believe P-04 had refused?"

"No."

"Then what did you believe?"

Eleanor looked directly at him.

"I believed we had failed to communicate properly with a person whose response was being treated as an administrative inconvenience."

The room remained quiet.

"Did you know whether that person was the protected principal?" Edward asked.

"I had no verified identity."

"Did you know whether the day-43 visitor was P-04?"

"No."

"Did you know who sent the day-136, day-149, and day-181 requests?"

"No."

Eleanor folded her hands.

"But I knew the original procedure had not been delivered."

Edward closed his notebook.

That was the most important point.

Eleanor had not been claiming to know who P-04 was.

She had been trying to ensure that the person, whoever they were, could not be treated as having made a decision without receiving the information necessary to make it.

The next question concerned the rejected destination.

Jonathan found the original Personal Custody division's administrative structure.

It had been created to preserve correspondence for individuals whose identities were protected under the AV-18 arrangement.

However, it had never been fully staffed.

Its intended director had withdrawn before appointment.

The temporary administrator was Charles Vale.

The arrangement was supposed to last until an independent custodian was appointed.

No permanent appointment appeared in the records.

Edward read the administrative chart.

"Charles was the temporary administrator of Personal Custody?"

"Yes."

"At the same time he controlled V-17?"

"Yes."

"Then Eleanor was requesting a transfer from one office controlled by Charles to another office temporarily administered by Charles."

Jonathan nodded.

"Although the destination had a separate records function."

Edward stared at the chart.

It was not an independent transfer.

It was a change of filing location.

The documents might have moved, but the authority structure would have remained largely the same.

"Did Eleanor know?"

"She may have known the temporary administrator was Charles. She was requesting independent preservation oversight, not necessarily independent personal authority."

"Then why was the request described as an independent custody transfer?"

"That wording appears in the original request."

Edward considered it.

Perhaps Eleanor had hoped to create a separation that the organisation itself had never properly established.

Perhaps the request had been deliberately routed through a division that could not accept it.

Neither possibility could yet be proved.

At V-17, Arthur Bell was able to locate a separate document relating to the day-162 request.

It was not the original request.

It was a handwritten instruction attached to the refusal.

Arthur had preserved it because it contained a warning about the register's custody.

He placed it on the table.

Edward read the text.

*Do not permit the channel register to be transferred to Personal Custody while the temporary administrator remains the same individual. A change of location must not be represented as an independent transfer of authority.*

The final sentence was underlined.

*The register must remain separate from the sealed personal instruction.*

Edward looked at Arthur.

"Do you know who wrote this?"

"No."

"Was it delivered with the request?"

"I believe it arrived after the refusal."

"Who delivered it?"

Arthur opened the old access ledger.

"Someone left it at reception. The visitor was not entered by name."

"Male or female?"

"Female, according to the receptionist."

"Did she carry anything?"

"A document case."

"Brown leather?"

Arthur looked at Edward.

"Yes."

The familiar detail returned.

The woman with the brown leather case had appeared at V-17 repeatedly.

She had delivered documents.

She had challenged the use of acknowledgements as agreements.

She had warned that consideration was not acceptance.

She had carried material concerning the original procedure.

And now she had delivered an instruction warning against a transfer that would create only the appearance of independent custody.

"Did you see her?" Edward asked.

"Once."

"Can you describe her?"

"Middle-aged, perhaps. Dark hair. She wore ordinary clothes. I couldn't identify her from a photograph."

"Did she give a name?"

"No."

"Did she speak to Charles?"

"Not that day."

Arthur returned the instruction to its sleeve.

"She told the receptionist one thing."

"What?"

"That the register should not be moved merely to make the custody arrangement look independent."

Edward looked at the sentence again.

The woman had understood the distinction.

A new location did not necessarily mean a new authority.

Dr Sen received the handwritten instruction later that evening.

She compared it with the anonymous notes.

The result was cautious.

"The handwriting is consistent with the day-48, day-118, and day-152 instructions," she said. "It is also consistent with the marginal note in the original channel register."

"Can you say it is the same writer?"

"Not conclusively. But the similarities are stronger than in the earlier comparisons."

"Could it be the woman with the brown leather case?"

"Handwriting cannot establish the identity of the person who delivered the document."

Edward accepted the answer.

He had learned not to confuse a delivery record with authorship.

Still, the evidence was beginning to suggest that the anonymous writer and the unidentified woman might have been connected.

Or the woman might simply have been carrying instructions written by someone else.

The distinction remained open.

At the Valmont Estate, Isabelle was sitting with Catherine when Elena called from Singapore.

The video showed Elena's desk, which had become increasingly crowded with notes, diagrams, and test printouts.

Catherine smiled.

"You look busy."

"I am. I think I've finally fixed the reviewer assignment issue."

"Congratulations."

"Thank you. It took longer than expected."

Isabelle leaned toward the screen.

"Does that mean I can test the foundation applications?"

"Almost. I still need to remove identifying details and make sure the old applications don't expose private information."

"Good. We shouldn't use them until you're satisfied."

Elena looked surprised.

"You're not going to argue?"

"Why would I?"

"Because you've been waiting to test it."

"I can wait."

Catherine smiled faintly.

Isabelle glanced at the notes on Elena's desk.

"Your system has a separate status for documents that are received but not verified, doesn't it?"

"Yes."

"Could you also distinguish between a person acknowledging receipt and accepting responsibility?"

Elena paused.

"That's a good idea."

"I've seen foundation applications where a recipient signs for a packet, and the office later treats that as agreement to the conditions."

Elena began typing.

"That should definitely be separate."

Isabelle watched her work.

"You're adding it?"

"I'm writing it down. I need to test the wording."

Catherine looked between them.

"You two are beginning to sound like colleagues."

Isabelle smiled.

"That might be easier than trying to agree about everything else."

Elena looked up.

"Probably."

For a moment, neither said anything.

Then Elena turned her attention back to the screen.

She had work to finish.

And for once, she was pleased that someone had found a problem in her system before it became a problem for someone else.

Later that night, Edward received a new document from Meridian.

It was the original day-162 transfer request.

The archive had located it in a separate correspondence box.

The request was unsigned.

Its destination was Personal Custody.

Its purpose was independent preservation review.

But the final paragraph contained a detail missing from the rejection notice.

*The transfer is requested only for the purpose of preserving the original channel record and preventing further amendment of the R.V. status without documented authority.*

Beneath it was a second instruction.

*The person responsible for personal channel verification must be identified before the register is returned to V-17.*

Edward read it slowly.

The request did not simply seek a different storage location.

It sought identification of the person responsible for verifying P-04's personal channel.

The same position that had remained vacant since the channel was created.

The same position that had been required before substantive communication could occur.

And the same position that someone had attempted to replace with an administrative exception.

Jonathan entered the room.

"There's another record."

Edward looked up.

"What now?"

"The day-162 request included a reference to an earlier appointment proposal."

"Which one?"

"Not AV-18. A separate designation."

Jonathan placed the document on the desk.

**OC-19 / ORIGINAL CHANNEL VERIFIER — PROPOSED APPOINTMENT.**

The proposed appointment date was day 34.

The nominee's identity was sealed.

The status was marked:

**ACCEPTANCE RECEIVED — APPOINTMENT NOT COMPLETED.**

Edward looked at the date.

Day 34.

The same day the original preservation authority had been jointly approved.

Someone had been proposed to verify P-04's personal channel before the first communication attempt.

That person had apparently accepted consideration.

But the appointment had never been completed.

He turned to the final line.

**WITHDRAWAL REFERENCE: DAY 132 — AV-18 / PERSONAL REPRESENTATION.**

Edward frowned.

The same day the personal representation appointment had been withdrawn.

The two roles were not necessarily identical.

But someone had connected their withdrawal records.

He looked at Jonathan.

"Find the sealed nomination schedule."

Jonathan nodded.

"Do you think it's the same person who was proposed as personal representation custodian?"

"I don't know."

Edward returned to the document.

The channel verifier had been proposed.

The appointment had remained incomplete.

The person had apparently accepted consideration.

And then, on day 132, a withdrawal reference had been entered.

For the first time, the investigation had found evidence that the missing verifier might not simply have been an unfilled administrative position.

Someone had been selected.

Someone had been approached.

And someone had withdrawn—or been recorded as having withdrawn—before the principal's identity was independently established.
