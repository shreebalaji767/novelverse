# **Chapter 92 — The Authority That Never Existed**

The envelope remained sealed.

Edward had seen enough documents opened prematurely to understand that an unopened envelope could be more valuable than a hundred pages of conflicting testimony.

It had been preserved for eighteen years.

Its condition was recorded.

Its seal was intact.

And its release condition was unusually specific.

**TO BE OPENED ONLY BY THE PERSON FORMALLY APPOINTED AS RECEIVING IDENTITY CUSTODIAN.**

There was no name beneath the instruction.

There was no appointment certificate.

There was not even a provisional designation.

The person authorised to open the envelope had never been formally identified.

Jonathan read the trustee's latest report.

"Perhaps the appointment was made elsewhere."

"Then there should be a corresponding record."

"Unless it was never filed."

"That would make the appointment difficult to verify."

Jonathan looked at the envelope photograph.

"Could the trustee open it under a court order?"

"Possibly. But the legal team wants to establish whether the release condition can still be satisfied."

"How?"

"By finding the appointment record."

"And if there isn't one?"

Edward closed the report.

"Then we need to understand why someone created a document that could only be opened by a person who was never appointed."

The legal team began with the trust deed.

Its original wording was more detailed than the registration summary.

The receiving identity custodian was not supposed to be a biological parent.

It was a designated administrative authority responsible for confirming that the proposed receiving arrangement corresponded to the correct child.

The custodian's duties included:

- verifying the receiving institution's records;
- confirming that the child identified in the schedule was the child actually transferred;
- obtaining acknowledgement from the receiving family;
- ensuring that the originating medical record and receiving identity record referred to the same infant;
- authorising the final release of the identity documents.

The role was therefore not a ceremonial one.

It was the final verification point.

Without it, no proposed placement could become a confirmed identity arrangement.

Jonathan read the responsibilities.

"So the custodian was supposed to check the receiving side."

"Yes."

"And Hale checked the medical side."

"Correct."

"Then the two authorities were supposed to work together."

Edward nodded.

"Which is why the schedule couldn't be released to either originating family independently."

Jonathan tapped the final paragraph.

"But the appointment itself was made by Charles Vale?"

"Not according to this deed."

Edward turned to the appointment clause.

"The originating records authority nominated the medical representative. The receiving institution nominated its identity custodian. Both nominations had to be accepted by the trustee."

"Was there a nomination from the receiving institution?"

"That's what we're trying to establish."

The trustee supplied a list of organisations that had communicated with R-04 during the first six months.

There were seven.

St Catherine's Hospital.

Meridian Medical Centre.

Northbridge Clinical Review Unit.

Westbridge Children's Facility.

Vale Private Counsel.

Aurelius Family Office.

And a private residential administration service.

None had been formally recorded as the receiving identity custodian.

However, two organisations had submitted documents describing themselves as authorised receiving representatives.

The first was Meridian Medical Centre.

The second was a private residential administration service associated with Charles Vale.

Both submissions had been rejected.

The reasons were different.

Meridian had been rejected because its representative was authorised to manage temporary medical custody, not permanent receiving identity.

The residential service had been rejected because it had no authority to certify biological or originating identity.

Jonathan looked at the two rejection notices.

"Then neither could have been the receiving custodian."

"Correct."

"Who submitted the residential service's nomination?"

"Charles Vale."

"Who submitted Meridian's?"

"Marianne Cross."

Edward looked at the second document.

"Marianne tried to appoint Meridian?"

"She submitted it as a proposed receiving authority. The trustee rejected it because the medical institution could verify custody but could not independently confirm the identity of the family receiving the child."

Jonathan frowned.

"That makes sense."

"It does."

"Then what did Charles expect to happen?"

Edward placed the rejected nominations beside the schedule.

"I don't think he expected the process to be completed in the way the trust deed required."

They found the first appointment attempt in an old correspondence bundle.

It had been submitted on day 22.

The proposed custodian was listed as:

**PRIVATE RECEIVING ADMINISTRATION — V-17**

The document bore Charles Vale's signature.

The stated purpose was to authorise the residential facility to verify the identity of children placed under its care.

The trustee had rejected it within three working days.

The reason was written in a formal review note.

*The proposed authority is not independent of the party arranging the placements. It cannot certify the validity of the receiving arrangement it was instructed to administer.*

Edward read the sentence again.

Charles had attempted to appoint an organisation under his own control.

The trustee had refused.

"Did Charles appeal?" Jonathan asked.

"No formal appeal."

"Did he submit another nomination?"

Edward turned the page.

"Yes."

The second submission had arrived on day 34.

The same day the sealed AV-18 envelope was deposited.

This time, the proposed custodian was not V-17.

It was an individual.

**ADRIAN HOLT.**

Jonathan looked up.

"Holt?"

"Yes."

"Was he appointed?"

"No. The nomination was incomplete."

"What was missing?"

"His written acceptance, proof of independent authority, and confirmation from the receiving institution."

Edward turned the page.

There was another note.

*Nominee previously acted as custodian of originating identity material. Potential conflict of responsibility requires independent review.*

Jonathan leaned back.

"Holt was being asked to verify the receiving side after handling the originating documents."

"Exactly."

"That would have made him responsible for both sides of the same unresolved process."

Edward nodded.

"And the trustee refused again."

Adrian Holt was no longer in the hospital system.

His professional records were old, and his retirement had made his current activities difficult to establish.

But Edward had his address from an earlier interview.

They visited him the following afternoon.

Holt received them in a small office adjoining his house.

He looked irritated when Edward mentioned the R-04 trust.

"I thought we had already discussed this."

"We have."

"Then why are you back?"

"Because you were nominated as the receiving identity custodian."

Holt's irritation disappeared.

For several seconds, he did not speak.

Then he sat down.

"Who told you that?"

"The trustee's records."

Holt rubbed his forehead.

"I was never appointed."

"We know."

"Then there is nothing to discuss."

"There is a document addressed to the person holding that authority. It has remained sealed for eighteen years."

Holt looked at Edward.

"That is not my responsibility."

"Charles Vale nominated you."

"Charles nominated several people for several things."

"Why did you accept the nomination?"

"I didn't."

"Did you know it had been submitted?"

"Yes."

"Did you object?"

Holt looked away.

"I told Charles I wasn't qualified to verify the receiving identity."

"Why?"

"Because I had already handled the original records. I knew the custody chain was compromised. I couldn't independently certify documents that had passed through my own hands."

Edward studied him.

"That sounds like a reasonable objection."

"It was."

"Then why did Charles nominate you?"

"Because he wanted someone familiar with the files."

"Or someone who would sign them."

Holt's expression hardened.

"Do not put words in my mouth."

Edward remained calm.

"I'm asking you to explain why a man who knew the process was compromised continued to seek authority over it."

Holt did not answer.

Jonathan opened his notebook.

"Did you ever see the AV-18 envelope?"

Holt's eyes moved toward him.

"No."

"Did Charles?"

"I don't know."

"Did he tell you what it contained?"

"No."

"Did he tell you who was supposed to receive it?"

Holt paused.

"He said it was intended for the person who could confirm the final placement."

"Who was that?"

"He didn't say."

Edward leaned forward.

"Did you ask?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

Holt looked down.

"He said the person would be appointed when the family arrangements were complete."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

The same circular condition again.

The family arrangements had to be complete before the custodian could be appointed.

The custodian had to be appointed before the arrangements could be verified.

And the final identity schedule could not be released until the verification was complete.

"Did you tell him that the process was circular?" Edward asked.

"Yes."

"What was his response?"

Holt's voice became quieter.

"He said the children could not wait for the paperwork."

On the drive back, Jonathan stared through the window.

"That phrase keeps appearing."

"Which one?"

"The children couldn't wait."

Edward nodded.

"It sounds reasonable when someone says it."

"And sometimes it is."

"Yes. Temporary medical care cannot wait for an administrative investigation."

"But permanent identity assignments can."

Edward looked at the road ahead.

"Exactly."

There was a difference between placing an infant somewhere safe and declaring that the infant belonged to a particular family.

The first might require urgency.

The second required evidence.

Someone had repeatedly treated the two as though they were the same decision.

At the Valmont estate, Isabelle was reviewing a university prospectus.

The dining room table was covered with brochures, printed course descriptions, and a notebook.

Catherine entered carrying two cups of tea.

"Still comparing programmes?"

"Yes."

"You have already narrowed it down to three."

"I know. But the course structures are different."

Catherine sat opposite her.

"What matters most?"

Isabelle considered the question.

"Economics, finance, and practical business experience. I don't want to spend three years studying only theory."

"That is sensible."

"I also want to work at the foundation eventually."

Catherine smiled faintly.

"You don't have to decide that now."

"I know. But I want to understand how it operates before I make any assumptions."

"Then you should start with the public reports."

"I've already downloaded them."

Catherine looked at the papers.

"Of course you have."

Isabelle turned a page.

"I want to understand the accounts, the projects, and how the foundation decides which applications to support."

"That is a good place to begin."

"Will you let me sit in on a meeting?"

"Not a confidential one. But you can attend a general programme review when the next one is scheduled."

Isabelle looked pleased.

"Really?"

"Yes. You'll observe first. You won't be given authority simply because you're my daughter."

"I wouldn't want that."

Catherine watched her for a moment.

There was determination in Isabelle's expression, but also uncertainty.

She had spent so much of her life imagining the family she might one day find that she had not fully considered what would happen after she found them.

Now she was learning that being welcomed did not automatically tell her what to do next.

Catherine reached for the teapot.

"You can build your own place here, Isabelle."

Isabelle looked down at her notebook.

"I know."

And for once, she believed it.

At the Aurelius estate, Julian had added a new page to his list.

The original list contained forty-eight items.

He had not crossed off many of them.

Some required his sister to arrive.

Others required her agreement.

A few were increasingly embarrassing.

He had recently removed the suggestion that she should be given a private helicopter tour because Adrian had explained that an introduction to aviation was not the same thing as a surprise flight.

Julian had considered this unnecessarily restrictive.

Now he was writing a new item.

**49. Find out what she wants to do during her first week.**

He paused.

Then added beneath it:

*Do not make the schedule without asking.*

Damien entered the room.

"What are you writing?"

"Planning."

"Again?"

Julian covered the page.

"Private family business."

Damien looked at him.

"Is it about your sister?"

"No."

Damien waited.

Julian sighed.

"Yes."

"Then don't plan her entire life before she arrives."

"I am not."

Damien glanced at the page.

"You're writing a list of things to do with someone you haven't met."

"That is different."

"How?"

"Because I am preparing."

Damien considered that.

"Preparation is useful. Expectations are dangerous."

Julian looked offended.

"Why does everyone keep saying things like that?"

"Because you keep planning things for a person who hasn't had a chance to tell you what she wants."

Julian stared at his list.

Then he added a small note beneath item forty-nine.

*Ask first.*

Damien left without commenting.

Julian looked at the page for another minute.

He was beginning to understand that waiting for someone was not the same as knowing them.

Edward returned to the trustee's office the following morning.

Helen Ward had arranged for him to inspect the original appointment register.

This time, he was permitted to view the unredacted entries under legal supervision.

The register was handwritten.

Each proposed custodian had a separate entry.

The first was V-17.

The second was Adrian Holt.

The third was a name that had not appeared in any of the earlier copies.

**M. CROSS — NOT ACCEPTED.**

Edward looked up.

"Marianne Cross was nominated?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"Day 41."

"By whom?"

"The nomination came from the R-04 administrative office."

"Who signed it?"

Helen turned the register toward him.

There was no personal signature.

Only a stamp.

**RECEIVING AUTHORITY — TEMPORARY NOMINATION.**

Edward read the entry again.

"Why was it rejected?"

Helen pointed to the note.

*Nominee has acted as notification intermediary and does not possess independent receiving verification authority.*

Jonathan frowned.

"So Marianne was nominated after Holt."

"Yes."

"And rejected because she was the person responsible for delivering notifications."

"Correct."

Edward looked at the following entry.

There was a fourth nomination.

It had been submitted on day 47.

The name was partially obscured by an old adhesive label.

Only the final letters remained visible.

**...BROOKS.**

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Helena Brooks?"

Helen examined the page.

"That is possible."

"Was she appointed?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Her nomination was withdrawn before the review was completed."

"By whom?"

Helen turned to the next sheet.

The withdrawal instruction was brief.

*Candidate no longer available. Maintain sealed status.*

There was no explanation.

No signature.

Only the reference:

**EFO-SL/07.**

Edward slowly closed the register.

Helena Brooks had been the Family Office director who confirmed the authenticity of Alexander Aurelius's general authority.

She had also confirmed that the financial instruction did not authorise specific child placements.

Now her name appeared in a list of proposed receiving identity custodians.

But she had never been appointed.

And someone had withdrawn her nomination.

Edward called Helena that afternoon.

She listened carefully as he explained the discovery.

"Was I nominated as receiving identity custodian?" she asked.

"According to the R-04 register."

There was a long silence.

"I was never told that."

"Did you receive any appointment request?"

"No."

"Did anyone ask you to verify the receiving identity schedule?"

"No."

"Did you know the role existed?"

"I knew the trust required a receiving custodian. I did not know my name had been submitted."

"Could someone have nominated you without your knowledge?"

"Administratively, yes. But that would not make me the custodian."

"Do you know why your nomination was withdrawn?"

"No."

Edward turned a page in his notebook.

"Did Charles Vale ever discuss the receiving identity schedule with you?"

"Not directly."

"Did he discuss the Family Office authority?"

"Yes."

"What did he want?"

"He wanted a broad financial and administrative instruction to cover the costs of preserving records and arranging temporary care."

"Did you approve it?"

"I verified the general expenditure authority. I did not approve any individual placement."

"Did he ask you to certify that the Aurelius family had received a child?"

"No."

"Did he ever claim that they had?"

Helena paused.

"He once told me that the family arrangements were being handled."

"Which family arrangements?"

"He didn't specify."

"Did you ask?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"He said the receiving family would be notified when the records were complete."

Edward looked at the appointment register.

"Did you believe him?"

"I believed there was a process."

"Did you ever see evidence that the process had been completed?"

"No."

The call ended without a definitive answer.

Edward remained at his desk, looking at the names.

V-17.

Adrian Holt.

Marianne Cross.

Helena Brooks.

Four proposed custodians.

Four failures.

None had received the authority.

None had been able to complete the receiving identity verification.

Yet the children had been transferred, records had been separated, and families had been kept uninformed.

The process had not merely stalled.

Someone had continued acting while knowing that the final authority did not exist.

Edward returned to the original register.

The fourth nomination had been withdrawn on day 47.

The AV-18 envelope had been deposited on day 34.

The two events were connected by a period of thirteen days.

He examined the withdrawal reference again.

**EFO-SL/07.**

The same external liaison code that appeared on the original hospital records, the temporary custody instructions, and several disputed transfers.

He called Jonathan.

"I want the complete EFO-SL/07 correspondence history."

"All of it?"

"Every communication between the Family Office, Vale Private Counsel, and R-04 from day 1 onward."

"That will take time."

"Then start with the period between days 34 and 47."

"Because of Helena's nomination?"

"Because someone nominated four different people for an authority that was never formally established."

Jonathan paused.

"And withdrew Helena."

"Yes."

"Do you think she was close to being appointed?"

"I think someone considered her suitable enough to nominate."

"Then changed their mind."

Edward looked at the sealed envelope photograph on his screen.

"Or discovered that she would not sign what they wanted."

That evening, the trustee sent one final document.

It was a supplementary note attached to the fourth nomination.

The note had been omitted from the original register because it was filed under the administrative correspondence rather than the appointment record.

Its date was day 47.

The text was short.

*The proposed custodian must not be drawn from either originating family's administration. An independent receiving authority is required.*

Edward read it twice.

Someone had finally recognised the conflict.

The person who wrote the note had understood that neither the Valmont administration nor the Aurelius Family Office could independently verify the receiving identity.

But the note contained another sentence.

*The identity of the receiving authority is to be established through the originating facility's independent record, not through the placement schedule.*

Edward sat very still.

The originating facility.

Not the Valmonts.

Not the Aureliuses.

Not Charles Vale.

Not even the private trustee.

The answer was supposed to come from the medical institution that had recorded the birth.

He opened the original St Catherine's correspondence file.

There was a reference on the back of the day-nine transfer register that he had not examined closely enough before.

**ORIGINATING FACILITY REPRESENTATIVE — PENDING.**

Beside it were two initials.

**M.V.**

Edward stared at them.

Margaret Voss had held the original Category 17 register.

She had monitored the protected identity process.

She had transferred Annex C Part Two to Holt.

And she had refused to certify entries without original records.

But had she been only a custodian?

Or had someone intended her to become the independent authority who would decide which family could receive the children?

Edward reached for his phone.

"Jonathan."

"Yes?"

"We need to speak to Margaret Voss again."

"About the Category 17 register?"

"Not this time."

Edward looked at the two initials.

"This time, I want to know why her name was written beside the originating facility's authority."

He paused.

"Because if Margaret was meant to verify the receiving identity, then the person who kept telling us she was only a records custodian may have been hiding something important."

And for the first time, Edward wondered whether the person who had refused to sign the final documents had been the only one who understood why they should never have been signed in the first place.
