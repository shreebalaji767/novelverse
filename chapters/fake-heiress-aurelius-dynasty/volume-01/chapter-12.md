# **Chapter 12 — The Two Birth Certificates**

The first person to suggest a DNA test was not a doctor, a lawyer or an investigator.

It was Julian Aurelius.

He made the suggestion at breakfast.

“Why don't we just test them?”

Everyone at the table looked at him.

Julian continued eating his toast.

“What?”

Lucien slowly put down his coffee.

“Sometimes, little brother, you say something remarkably sensible.”

“I always say sensible things.”

“Let's not exaggerate.”

Adrian ignored them.

“Father has already discussed independent testing with the Valmont family.”

Julian looked up.

“Then why haven't they done it?”

“Because there are procedures to follow.”

“What procedures?”

“Consent, sample collection, laboratory verification, chain of custody.”

Julian frowned.

“That's a lot of words for a DNA test.”

Damien nearly smiled.

Victoria, however, was not amused.

“Julian.”

He looked at her.

“Yes?”

“This is not a school project. We cannot simply ask someone to take a test and expect everything to be resolved immediately.”

“I know.”

His voice became quieter.

“I just want to know where she is.”

The table fell silent.

Alexander looked at his youngest son.

“We all do.”

Julian nodded and returned to his breakfast.

For once, Lucien did not make another joke.

At the Valmont Estate, the subject of DNA testing was being discussed with considerably less humour.

Richard had arranged a meeting with the family solicitor, Mr. Laurent, and an independent medical representative.

The meeting was held in the study.

Catherine sat beside Richard.

Sebastian and Edward were present.

Nicholas had been asked to attend but was currently standing near the window, looking unusually impatient.

Elena and Isabelle were not included.

“Before we proceed,” Mr. Laurent said, “I want to clarify the legal position. The existing DNA test established that Elena is not biologically related to Richard or Catherine. It did not establish her biological identity.”

Edward nodded.

“Correct.”

“The newly discovered records suggest that two newborns may have been registered under incorrect identities. However, the records themselves do not establish which child is which.”

Richard leaned forward.

“Can an independent test establish that?”

“Yes. If we obtain appropriate reference samples from the relevant biological parents and samples from both girls, the laboratory should be able to determine the relationships.”

“Should?”

Mr. Laurent remained calm.

“Provided the samples are suitable and the laboratory can establish a reliable chain of custody. We should not promise a result before the testing is completed.”

Sebastian looked at the medical representative.

“How long?”

“Once all samples are received, the laboratory expects approximately five to seven working days for the initial report. A more extensive review may take longer.”

Nicholas turned from the window.

“Five to seven days?”

The representative nodded.

“Approximately.”

Nicholas looked displeased.

“Fine.”

Richard glanced at him.

“Sit down.”

Nicholas sat.

Mr. Laurent continued.

“There is also the matter of consent. Both girls are adults. They must understand the purpose of the testing and agree to participate.”

Catherine lowered her eyes.

“I will speak with them.”

Richard looked at her.

“We should speak with them together.”

“Yes.”

Edward placed the original record photographs on the table.

“The archive evidence should also be provided to the laboratory. It may help establish the historical context, although the DNA results will be independent of the document interpretation.”

Mr. Laurent nodded.

“Agreed.”

Sebastian looked at the two identification records.

“If the results confirm the suspected exchange, what happens next?”

Richard answered.

“Then we discuss the consequences.”

Nicholas looked at him.

“What consequences?”

Richard turned toward his second son.

“Legal identity, family records, inheritance provisions, and the arrangements that affect both girls.”

Nicholas frowned.

“And what about everything else?”

Richard looked at him.

“What do you mean?”

“Everything that isn't paperwork.”

Nobody spoke.

Nicholas leaned forward.

“Isabelle is our sister. Elena is our sister. A DNA test won't change the fact that we grew up together.”

Sebastian's expression tightened.

“Nicholas, nobody is disputing that.”

“Then why does every conversation sound like we're discussing how to rearrange the family?”

“Because there are legal matters we have to handle.”

“And there are people we have to think about.”

Edward looked at Nicholas.

“I agree.”

Nicholas glanced at him.

“Good. At least one person in this room has a functioning heart.”

“Don't push it.”

Catherine gave a small, tired smile.

Richard remained silent.

He understood Nicholas's concern.

He simply did not know how to promise that everything would remain unchanged.

Elena was in the university library when Catherine called.

“Hello, Mother.”

“Are you free this afternoon?”

“I have one lecture left.”

“Would you come home afterward? I would like to talk with you and Isabelle.”

Elena paused.

“Is something wrong?”

“No, darling. I just want us to talk.”

“Okay.”

“Don't rush. Take your time.”

Elena ended the call.

Clara, sitting opposite her, looked up from her notes.

“Family meeting?”

“Apparently.”

“Good news or bad news?”

“I don't know.”

Clara closed her notebook.

“Do you want emergency biscuits?”

Elena laughed.

“Not yet.”

“Keep me informed.”

Elena packed her books.

As she walked out of the library, she thought about the meeting.

Catherine had sounded gentle, but there was something unusual in her voice.

Perhaps the hospital had finally found the original records.

Perhaps the investigation had reached another dead end.

Perhaps they were going to tell the girls about the Aurelius family.

Elena did not know which possibility she preferred.

At three-thirty, Elena and Isabelle were seated in the family sitting room.

Catherine sat opposite them.

Richard stood near the fireplace.

Nicholas had initially intended to leave, but Catherine asked him to remain.

Edward joined the meeting a few minutes later.

Sebastian was absent, having gone to a business appointment.

Catherine folded her hands.

“Thank you for coming.”

Elena looked at her.

“What's happened?”

Richard took a breath.

“The investigation has uncovered two original hospital identification records from the night you were born.”

Isabelle straightened.

“Original records?”

“Yes,” Edward said. “They appear to be connected to the two birth entries we found earlier.”

Elena looked between them.

“Does that mean the hospital knows what happened?”

“Not completely,” Edward replied. “But the documents provide important information.”

Richard placed two copies on the table.

Elena looked down.

The first record contained a name she did not recognise.

**Mother: V. Aurelius**

The second contained a name she knew very well.

**Mother: C. Valmont**

She looked up.

“Why is the Aurelius name on one of them?”

Richard answered carefully.

“The Aurelius family had a daughter born at the same hospital on the same night.”

Isabelle looked at the records.

“Then there were two babies?”

“Yes,” Edward said.

“Two girls?”

“Yes.”

Isabelle slowly looked toward Elena.

Elena was still reading.

“Are you saying one of us was born into the Aurelius family?”

Richard nodded.

“That is one possibility.”

The room became very quiet.

Elena looked at the first record again.

The date matched.

The hospital matched.

The time was 2:17 a.m.

The second record listed 5:46 a.m.

“Which one of us was born first?” she asked.

Edward answered.

“We don't know yet.”

“But the records say…”

“The records may have been altered. That's why we're arranging independent testing.”

Isabelle looked at Catherine.

“Are you saying we were switched?”

Catherine reached for her hand.

“We believe that may have happened.”

Isabelle stared at her.

For a moment, she looked as though she wanted to ask several questions at once.

Then she turned toward Elena.

“Does that mean you might be their daughter?”

Elena did not answer.

She was looking at the name again.

Aurelius.

She had read about the family in university reports.

She knew about the companies.

She knew about the international investments.

She knew the family was considerably wealthier and more influential than the Valmonts.

But she had never imagined the name could appear on her own birth record.

“Who are they?” she asked.

Richard answered.

“Alexander and Victoria Aurelius.”

“Do they know?”

“Yes.”

Elena looked up sharply.

“They know about me?”

“They know there is a possibility that one of the girls may be their biological daughter.”

“Have they asked to meet us?”

“Not yet. We have agreed to wait for the test results.”

Elena nodded slowly.

“What happens if the test says I'm theirs?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Catherine's eyes filled with tears.

Richard stepped forward.

“Then we will have to discuss what happens next.”

Elena looked at him.

“Will I have to leave?”

The question was so direct that nobody seemed prepared for it.

Nicholas immediately spoke.

“No.”

Richard looked toward him.

Nicholas continued.

“Whatever the result says, nobody is asking you to leave.”

Elena looked at him.

“I didn't say I wanted to.”

“I know.”

He leaned forward.

“I'm just making sure you don't think that.”

Catherine reached across the table and held Elena's hand.

“You are not being asked to go anywhere.”

Elena looked at her mother.

“Promise?”

Catherine squeezed her hand.

“Yes.”

Isabelle watched them quietly.

She had been waiting eighteen years to find her biological family.

She had imagined what it would feel like to finally belong.

But she had never imagined that another girl might have to lose a home in order for her to gain one.

And now she was beginning to understand that the two things might be connected.

That evening, the Aurelius family received the Valmonts' proposal for independent testing.

Alexander read the document carefully.

“Five to seven working days,” Adrian said.

“That's reasonable.”

Victoria sat beside him.

“Will both girls agree?”

“The Valmont family believes they will.”

“Have they been told about the possibility?”

“Yes.”

Victoria looked down.

“What did they say?”

Alexander hesitated.

“They have agreed to proceed.”

“Both?”

“Yes.”

Victoria nodded.

She did not ask for more details.

She wanted to know everything about the girls.

Their ages.

Their studies.

Their interests.

Their personalities.

But she also knew that they were not evidence samples.

They were two young women whose lives had already been disrupted.

She would not treat them as objects in an investigation.

“Do we know anything about Elena?” she asked.

Adrian opened a folder.

“Very little. She's eighteen, enrolled at an international university, and was raised in the Valmont household.”

“Does she have brothers?”

“Three Valmont sons.”

Victoria smiled faintly.

“Then she's used to brothers.”

Lucien, who had been listening from the sofa, looked up.

“That's unfortunate.”

Victoria gave him a look.

“Why?”

“Because she's going to have five more.”

Julian immediately sat forward.

“Six, if you count Damien's security team.”

Damien looked at him.

“Don't count my staff.”

Julian grinned.

“I'll count them if I want.”

Adrian shook his head.

“Let's not overwhelm her before we've even met her.”

Victoria looked at him.

“That's exactly what I was thinking.”

She glanced at the photograph of the baby.

“I want her to have time.”

Alexander took her hand.

“She will.”

The next morning, Elena woke earlier than usual.

She had slept poorly.

The previous day's conversation kept returning to her thoughts.

Two children.

Two mothers.

Two families.

One of them might be hers.

She sat at her desk and opened her laptop.

This time, she searched for the Aurelius family.

The first results were predictable.

**Alexander Aurelius — Chairman of Aurelius Global**

**Victoria Aurelius — Chairwoman of Aurelius Foundation**

**Adrian Aurelius — Chief Executive Officer**

**Lucien Aurelius — President, Aurelius Ventures**

**Damien Aurelius — Director, Security and Risk Management**

**Julian Aurelius — Student**

There were photographs from business conferences, charity events and public announcements.

She recognised Alexander from financial news.

He looked serious in every photograph.

Victoria appeared in several photographs from foundation events.

She had a calm, elegant expression.

Elena scrolled further.

There was an article from eighteen years earlier.

It mentioned the disappearance of the Aurelius family's newborn daughter.

The headline was old.

The photograph showed Alexander and Victoria leaving a hospital.

Victoria was holding a blanket.

The article stated that the family had reported their daughter missing shortly after birth.

The search had continued for years.

Elena stared at the photograph.

She had not known that the Aurelius family had lost a child.

She had not known that they had been searching for eighteen years.

And she had not known that she might be connected to them.

She closed the article.

For a moment, she sat quietly.

Then she opened her university timetable.

There was no reason to skip her classes.

Whatever the test revealed, she still had an assignment due on Friday.

She packed her bag.

At the Aurelius Estate, Julian was preparing a list.

He had titled it:

**Things My Sister Might Like**

Lucien looked over his shoulder.

“Why are there twenty-seven items?”

“Because I have many ideas.”

“Why is a gaming console on the list?”

“She might like games.”

“Why is a bookstore?”

“She might like reading.”

“Why is a private cinema?”

“She might like movies.”

Lucien stared at him.

“You've listed an entire entertainment complex.”

Julian frowned.

“It's not my fault we have one.”

Damien entered the room.

“What are you doing?”

“Preparing.”

“For what?”

“My sister.”

Damien looked at the list.

“Have you considered asking what she actually likes?”

Julian paused.

Then he crossed out three items.

“That's a good point.”

Lucien laughed.

“Progress.”

Julian looked at him.

“Don't make fun of me.”

“I'm not.”

“You are.”

“Only a little.”

Adrian entered.

“Julian, Father wants you in the study.”

Julian gathered his list.

“Why?”

“Because the Valmont family has agreed to testing.”

Julian stood immediately.

“Does that mean we'll find out?”

“It means we'll have a chance to find out.”

Julian nodded.

He folded the list carefully and put it in his pocket.

The sample collection was arranged for the following afternoon.

An independent medical team arrived at the Valmont Estate with the necessary equipment.

The procedure was straightforward.

Each participant would provide a sample under supervision.

The samples would be sealed, labelled and transported directly to the laboratory.

The Aurelius family would provide reference samples separately.

The Valmont family would do the same.

The girls would be informed of the results together, unless they requested otherwise.

Elena and Isabelle were seated in the small medical room.

A female medical officer explained the process.

“This is a standard cheek-swab collection. It is painless and takes only a few seconds. Each sample will be labelled with a coded identification number.”

Elena nodded.

Isabelle looked nervous.

“Will we get the results at the same time?”

“Yes. The laboratory will process both samples as part of the same case.”

The medical officer turned to Elena.

“Are you comfortable proceeding?”

Elena glanced at Isabelle.

Then she nodded.

“Yes.”

Isabelle followed.

“Yes.”

The samples were collected.

Each was placed in a separate sealed container.

The medical officer recorded the identification numbers.

Edward watched the chain-of-custody procedure carefully.

When everything was complete, the samples were placed inside a secure transport case.

The courier signed the collection form.

And then they were gone.

Elena looked at the empty collection table.

“That was it?”

The medical officer smiled.

“That was it.”

Isabelle let out a breath.

“I thought it would take longer.”

“Most of the work happens in the laboratory,” Edward said.

Elena looked at him.

“How long now?”

“Five to seven working days.”

She nodded.

Five to seven days.

It sounded short.

It also sounded impossibly long.

That evening, the Valmont family gathered for dinner.

The conversation was quieter than usual.

Nicholas tried to restore the atmosphere.

“So,” he announced, “I have decided that everyone should stop discussing DNA for at least twenty-four hours.”

Edward looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because I am tired of hearing the phrase ‘chain of custody’.”

Sebastian raised an eyebrow.

“Then stop asking questions about the investigation.”

“I don't ask questions. I make observations.”

“You make noise.”

“That's also an observation.”

Elena laughed.

Isabelle smiled.

Catherine looked relieved to see them both smiling.

Richard watched the table.

He had spent most of the day thinking about the results.

He had also thought about what might happen afterward.

He knew Alexander Aurelius would want to meet his biological daughter.

He knew Victoria would want to bring her home.

He knew the Aurelius brothers would be eager to meet their sister.

And he knew Elena might not know how to respond.

But there was another question.

What would Isabelle want?

She had only recently arrived.

She had spent eighteen years imagining the Valmont family.

Now the possibility existed that the family she had imagined might not be hers in the way she expected.

Richard looked at her.

“Isabelle.”

She turned.

“Yes?”

“How are you feeling?”

She thought for a moment.

“I'm nervous.”

“About the test?”

“Yes. And what happens afterward.”

Catherine reached for her hand.

“Whatever the result, we'll talk through it together.”

Isabelle nodded.

Then she looked toward Elena.

“What about you?”

Elena considered the question.

“I'm nervous too.”

Nicholas leaned forward.

“That's understandable.”

Elena smiled faintly.

“I don't know what I'm supposed to feel.”

“You don't have to feel anything in particular,” Edward said.

“Thank you.”

He nodded.

“Just wait for the results.”

Nicholas raised his glass.

“To waiting.”

Sebastian looked at him.

“That's a terrible toast.”

“It's an accurate one.”

Elena laughed.

For a few minutes, the table felt like it used to.

But underneath the familiar conversation, something had changed.

Everyone knew that a laboratory somewhere was examining the identities of two girls.

And soon, the answers would arrive.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria received confirmation that the reference samples had been delivered.

She read the message twice.

Then she called Alexander.

“They've received everything.”

“Good.”

“When will we know?”

“Five to seven working days.”

Victoria looked at the calendar.

Seven days.

She had waited eighteen years.

She could wait seven more.

But that did not mean she wanted to.

She walked to the old nursery and opened the door.

The room was quiet.

The crib stood beneath the window.

The small wardrobe remained untouched.

She placed the confirmation message on the table.

Then she looked at the photograph of her newborn daughter.

“I don't know where you are,” she whispered.

“But we're getting closer.”

Three days later, the laboratory contacted Edward.

The initial analysis was complete.

However, the laboratory had identified an issue with one of the historical reference records.

Edward read the message carefully.

It did not say the samples were invalid.

It did not say the results were inconclusive.

It simply stated that the laboratory had found a discrepancy between the supplied hospital identification information and the original registration details.

A discrepancy that required additional verification before the final report could be issued.

Edward immediately called the laboratory.

“What kind of discrepancy?”

The specialist on the other end of the line explained.

“The biological relationship testing is progressing normally. However, the historical registration number associated with one of the reference samples does not match the number in the hospital's revised record.”

Edward looked at the document on his desk.

“Which registration number?”

The specialist gave it to him.

Edward recognised it.

It was the number from the original identification card marked:

**Mother: V. Aurelius.**

He looked at the photograph again.

Then he asked the question that had been bothering him since the meeting.

“Does this discrepancy affect the DNA result?”

“No,” the specialist replied. “The DNA analysis is separate. But we cannot finalise the report until the historical identity documentation is verified.”

Edward thanked the specialist and ended the call.

He sat back.

The DNA test was not yet complete.

But the original records were proving something important.

The hospital's revised documents did not match the originals.

And if someone had changed the registration numbers, the question was no longer simply whether two children had been switched.

It was why someone had gone to such lengths to make the switch difficult to discover.

That evening, Edward walked into Richard's study.

Richard was reading a financial report.

“What is it?”

“The laboratory has contacted me.”

Richard put the report down.

“Have the results arrived?”

“Not yet. There's a discrepancy in the historical registration numbers. They're verifying the original identification details.”

Richard frowned.

“Does that affect the test?”

“No. The biological analysis is progressing. But the laboratory wants to confirm the original documentation before issuing the final report.”

Richard stood.

“Which record?”

“The Aurelius registration.”

Richard looked toward the window.

“So the original record and the hospital's revised record do not match.”

“Correct.”

“Then someone changed more than the transfer paperwork.”

Edward nodded.

Richard was silent for several seconds.

“Do not tell the girls until we know whether the discrepancy affects their results.”

“Agreed.”

Edward turned to leave.

“Edward.”

He stopped.

Richard looked at him.

“Whatever happens when the results arrive, remember that Elena and Isabelle are not responsible for what someone did eighteen years ago.”

Edward nodded.

“I know.”

He left the study.

Richard remained standing.

On his desk lay two copies of the original birth records.

Two mothers.

Two newborn girls.

Two different registration numbers.

And somewhere between those records, an eighteen-year-old secret was beginning to unravel.

**The test would establish who the girls were biologically.**

But it would not explain who had changed their identities.

And that was still the most important question of all.
