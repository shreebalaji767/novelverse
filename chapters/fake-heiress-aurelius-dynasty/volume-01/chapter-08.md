# **Chapter 8 — The Signature on the File**

The first thing Richard Valmont did on Monday morning was cancel his nine o’clock meeting.

The second was instruct his secretary to clear the rest of his schedule.

The third was call his son.

“Edward. My office. Eight-thirty.”

Edward, who had been awake since six and had already read the same page of the hospital investigation four times, glanced at his phone.

“Good morning to you too, Father.”

“This is not a social call.”

“I gathered that from the time.”

“Bring everything you have.”

The call ended.

Edward stared at the screen for a moment.

Then he looked at the document lying open on his desk.

**AURELIUS FAMILY OFFICE**

**Authorised Representative: Alexander Aurelius**

The name had been printed in a neat, formal typeface. Beneath it sat a reference number, an archive classification and a date.

Eighteen years ago.

He had expected a connection to the Aurelius family. Margaret's testimony and the photograph had made that much difficult to ignore.

But he had not expected a document that appeared to carry Alexander Aurelius's personal authorisation.

Alexander Aurelius was not an obscure businessman whose name could be casually placed on a file. He was the chairman of one of the most influential business dynasties in the world.

His family owned banks, technology companies, hospitals, energy infrastructure and shipping networks across several continents.

And for eighteen years, according to every public account Edward could find, Alexander Aurelius had been searching for his missing daughter.

If the file was genuine, then someone had been acting under his authority on the very night she disappeared.

The question was whether Alexander knew what that authority was being used for.

Edward closed the document.

He would find out.

Richard's office overlooked the eastern gardens of the Valmont Estate.

By the time Edward arrived, Sebastian was already seated on the sofa. Richard stood near the window, holding a cup of untouched coffee.

A second folder lay on the desk.

“You're late,” Sebastian said.

“It's eight-twenty-seven.”

“Exactly.”

Edward put his folder down.

“Should I apologise to the clock?”

“Both of you,” Richard interrupted.

Sebastian leaned back.

Edward sat opposite him.

Richard turned from the window.

“Show me.”

Edward opened the folder and placed the archived index on the desk.

Richard read it once.

Then again.

His expression did not change, but his fingers tightened around the paper.

“Is this original?”

“No. It's a certified archival copy. Jonathan Pierce provided it after reviewing the old index.”

“And Pierce is certain?”

“He was a junior partner at the firm when the file was created. He remembers the classification, but he didn't handle the matter personally. Charles Vale was the senior partner responsible.”

“Charles Vale is dead,” Sebastian said.

“Yes.”

Richard looked at Edward.

“Then what exactly are we looking at?”

“An archival record indicating that the Aurelius Family Office authorised a confidential identity-protection matter. Alexander Aurelius is listed as the authorised representative.”

“Identity protection,” Sebastian repeated. “Not a hospital transfer.”

“Correct.”

“Not a newborn exchange.”

“Correct.”

Richard placed the paper down.

“Then we do not assume more than the document says.”

Edward nodded.

“I haven't.”

Sebastian studied the page.

“Could someone have used Alexander Aurelius's name without his knowledge?”

“Possibly.”

“Could it be a forged authorisation?”

“Possibly.”

“Could the file have been created for an entirely unrelated matter?”

“That is also possible.”

Sebastian frowned.

“That's a lot of possibilities.”

“It's an eighteen-year-old file with missing original records,” Edward replied. “I'd be more concerned if there were only one.”

Richard walked back to his desk.

“What did Pierce say?”

“He said the file was authorised by the Aurelius Family Office, but he couldn't confirm the scope of the authorisation. He has agreed to retrieve the remaining archive material.”

“When?”

“Today, if the archive department permits access.”

Richard picked up his phone.

“Arrange a meeting with him. I want the original chain of custody documented. Every transfer, every signature, every amendment.”

Edward hesitated.

“Father, there is something else.”

Richard looked up.

“The hospital records were altered after the original report was filed. Margaret's photograph shows a man speaking with the hospital administrator. She remembers the instruction referring to the girl who was supposed to go home with the Aurelius family.”

Silence settled over the office.

Sebastian's gaze sharpened.

“And now we have Alexander Aurelius's name on an identity-protection file.”

“Yes.”

“Those two things may be connected.”

“They may,” Edward said. “But we don't know how.”

Richard leaned back in his chair.

“Do not contact the Aurelius family yet.”

Sebastian looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because we have a document, not proof. If we approach a family of that influence with an incomplete accusation, we risk losing access to the investigation before it has properly begun.”

Edward understood the reasoning.

He did not particularly like it.

“Then what do you want me to do?”

“Find out who created the file, who requested it, who handled it, and what happened to the original.”

Richard tapped the paper.

“Especially this signature.”

At the Aurelius Estate, breakfast was rarely quiet.

It was not necessarily loud, either.

It was simply difficult to predict.

On Monday morning, Lucien was attempting to persuade Julian that eating fruit before pancakes counted as a balanced breakfast.

Julian was not convinced.

“Fruit doesn't cancel pancakes,” he said.

“It does if you eat enough of it.”

“That sounds like something you invented.”

“I have a business degree. I know things.”

Adrian did not look up from his tablet.

“Neither of those statements supports your argument.”

Lucien pointed his fork at him.

“You're only saying that because you don't want Julian to eat pancakes.”

“I don't care what Julian eats.”

“You care about everything.”

“I care about people making unnecessary noise before eight in the morning.”

Julian grinned.

“That's why you're everyone's favourite.”

Damien quietly moved the butter dish away from Lucien's elbow before it could fall.

Victoria entered the dining room a moment later.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning, Mother,” Adrian said.

Julian immediately pushed his chair back.

“Mother, can I have pancakes?”

“You already have pancakes.”

“Can I have more?”

“Ask the kitchen.”

Lucien looked offended.

“You're undermining my nutritional research.”

Victoria smiled faintly.

“Your research can continue without involving my breakfast table.”

She took her seat beside Alexander.

The chairman was already reading a report. He closed it when she sat down.

“Did you sleep?” Victoria asked quietly.

“Enough.”

“That isn't an answer.”

“It is the answer I have.”

She studied his face.

For the past eighteen years, she had learned to recognise the difference between Alexander's ordinary silence and the kind that meant he was carrying something he did not intend to share.

This was the latter.

“Another report?” she asked.

“An update from the search team.”

Victoria's expression changed.

“Anything?”

Alexander shook his head.

“No confirmed lead.”

Her gaze dropped to her hands.

Julian, who had been listening despite pretending not to, lowered his fork.

“Did they find something?”

Alexander looked at him.

“Not yet.”

“Will they?”

“We haven't stopped looking.”

Julian nodded, but his disappointment remained visible.

He had been born six years after his sister disappeared. He had never known her, never heard her voice, never seen her do anything beyond the photographs his mother kept.

Yet to him, she was not a distant mystery.

She was his sister.

And he had spent most of his childhood imagining what it would be like when she finally came home.

“Do you think she'll like pancakes?” he asked.

Lucien paused.

“That's your first question?”

“What's wrong with pancakes?”

“Nothing. I simply thought you'd ask whether she likes games, books, or expensive presents.”

“Those can come later.”

Damien looked at him.

“You have already prepared a list of games.”

Julian turned red.

“That was private.”

“You left it on the family tablet,” Adrian said.

Lucien burst out laughing.

Alexander watched his sons for a moment.

Then his eyes moved to Victoria.

He knew she had kept their daughter's room unchanged. He knew she still checked the search reports herself, even when the results were disappointing.

He also knew that the family had begun treating the missing girl as a permanent absence.

He refused to do that.

“Adrian,” he said.

Adrian looked up.

“Yes, Father?”

“Have the legal department review the old family-office authorisations from the year she disappeared.”

Adrian's expression became serious.

“Which department?”

“Corporate legal archives. Private family office records. Start with anything classified under identity protection or family security.”

“Is this connected to the hospital?”

Alexander paused.

“I don't know yet.”

Adrian nodded.

“I'll look into it.”

Victoria glanced at her husband.

“Why now?”

“An old record has surfaced.”

“Does it concern her?”

“I don't know.”

Victoria's fingers tightened around her napkin.

“Alexander.”

He reached across the table and covered her hand.

“I won't allow anyone to interfere with the search.”

She looked at him.

“I know.”

But neither of them said what they were both thinking.

What if the answer had been waiting in their own records all along?

Elena was having a much more ordinary Monday.

At least, she was trying to.

Her university lecture began at ten, and she had arrived early enough to secure her usual seat near the window.

Clara appeared a few minutes later carrying two notebooks, a coffee and a paper bag.

Elena looked at the bag.

“What's that?”

“Breakfast.”

“You already had breakfast.”

“That was breakfast number one.”

Clara sat down and placed the bag between them.

“Breakfast number two is for emergencies.”

“What emergency?”

“Monday.”

Elena laughed.

Clara took out a small pastry and handed it over.

“You look tired.”

“I was reading.”

“Until when?”

“Not very late.”

“Elena.”

“Midnight.”

“That's late.”

“It's not that late.”

“It's late when you have a lecture at ten.”

Elena took a bite.

Clara watched her carefully.

“How are things at home?”

“Fine.”

“That's your automatic answer.”

“Because things are fine.”

“Really?”

Elena considered the question.

The previous few days had been strange. Isabelle's arrival had changed the atmosphere of the mansion, even though nobody had openly demanded that Elena behave differently.

There were more conversations about legal matters. Richard had been making phone calls. Edward was frequently absent. Catherine was trying very hard to ensure that both girls felt included.

And the house itself seemed to be rearranging around a new centre of gravity.

But Isabelle had not been unkind.

If anything, she had been trying just as hard as Elena to avoid making things uncomfortable.

“We're getting along,” Elena said. “Isabelle asked me about the university yesterday. She might come to visit.”

“That's good.”

“Yes.”

Clara waited.

Elena sighed.

“You really don't miss anything, do you?”

“I'm observant. It's one of my many qualities.”

“You also talk too much.”

“Another quality.”

Elena smiled.

Clara leaned closer.

“Have you thought about what you want to do if the investigation takes a long time?”

“Continue studying.”

“And after that?”

“Graduate.”

“And after that?”

“Find a job?”

Clara stared at her.

“That's your grand life plan?”

“I haven't decided everything yet.”

“Good. You shouldn't have to.”

Elena looked toward the window.

“I think I want to do something useful. Something that belongs to me.”

“Like what?”

“I don't know yet. Maybe something related to business. Maybe education. I like the idea of building something rather than simply being handed a position.”

Clara nodded.

“That sounds like you.”

Elena folded the pastry bag.

“Anyway, I have enough to think about without planning the next twenty years.”

“Fair.”

The lecturer entered, and their conversation ended.

For the next hour, Elena concentrated on the lesson.

She took notes, answered a question and corrected a mistake in her calculations.

For a little while, the world was pleasantly simple.

Numbers behaved like numbers.

The lecture followed its schedule.

Nobody asked her to explain who her parents were.

It was a relief.

At eleven-fifteen, Jonathan Pierce received a visitor at the archive office.

Edward arrived with Daniel, the family investigator, and a written request for the complete record.

Pierce was waiting in a small conference room.

He was an older man with silver hair, narrow glasses and the habit of reading a document twice before answering a question.

“I assume you've spoken with your father,” he said.

“I have.”

“And he wants the full file.”

“He does.”

Pierce placed a thin folder on the table.

“This is the index and the available correspondence register. The original file is held in off-site storage.”

Edward opened it.

“Why wasn't this included in the first copy?”

“Because the archive department required a separate retrieval authorisation.”

“Who controls that authorisation?”

“The current legal custodian of the former Vale practice records.”

“Vale International Legal Group?”

“Yes. Their records department has to approve access.”

Edward turned a page.

There were several entries.

One was dated two days before the photograph Margaret had supplied.

Another was dated the morning after.

A third had been added six weeks later.

“What do these codes mean?”

Pierce adjusted his glasses.

“The first indicates file creation. The second is a document transfer. The third is a classification review.”

“Who signed the transfer?”

Pierce examined the page.

“Charles Vale.”

“And the classification review?”

“Also Charles Vale.”

Edward pointed to the first entry.

“File creation?”

Pierce went quiet.

“That's the part I wanted to check.”

“Why?”

“Because the entry lists an authorisation number, but not the name of the person who physically submitted the request.”

“Yet Alexander Aurelius is listed as the authorised representative.”

“Yes.”

“Then who created the file?”

“I don't know.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Mr. Pierce, you worked at the firm. Surely you remember the procedure.”

“I remember the procedure. I also remember that the family office did not always submit documents directly. Requests could be delivered through designated representatives.”

“Who?”

“Senior family-office staff. External counsel. Occasionally, a private security liaison.”

“Can you identify the person who submitted this request?”

Pierce shook his head.

“Not from this index.”

Edward turned another page.

A short notation had been typed near the bottom.

**Supporting authority retained separately.**

He tapped the line.

“What does that mean?”

“It means the original authorisation was not kept inside the main file.”

“Where was it kept?”

Pierce looked uncomfortable.

“That is what we need to establish.”

Edward leaned back.

“So we have a file authorised by Alexander Aurelius, created by an unknown person, with the supporting authority stored separately.”

“That's correct.”

“And the main file was transferred, reviewed and partially archived by Charles Vale.”

“Yes.”

Edward stared at the notation.

“Was this normal?”

“Not entirely.”

“Why?”

“Most identity-protection matters contained the authority document in the same archive package. Separating it was unusual, although not prohibited.”

“Who could approve that?”

“The senior partner.”

“Charles Vale.”

“Yes.”

Edward closed the folder.

“Then I need the original file.”

“I have submitted the request.”

“How long?”

Pierce gave a small, apologetic smile.

“With this classification, I cannot promise a date.”

Edward's expression hardened.

“Eighteen years is already a long time.”

“I understand.”

“Then please don't let the archive process turn another week into another eighteen years.”

Pierce looked at him for a moment.

“I'll do what I can.”

By the time Elena returned home, the mansion had settled into its afternoon quiet.

She found Isabelle in the library.

The other girl was sitting at the large table with a laptop open and a notebook beside it.

Then Elena pointed at the laptop.

Elena leaned forward.

Isabelle took out her notebook.

“You know a lot about this.”

“I've had to.”

For the next twenty minutes, they compared programmes, application requirements and tuition arrangements.

It was not a dramatic reconciliation.

It was not a declaration that everything would be perfect.

It was simply two eighteen-year-old girls sitting in a library, trying to figure out their futures.

For the moment, that was enough.

At four-thirty, Edward returned to the Valmont Estate.

He found Richard in the study with Sebastian.

The meeting with Jonathan Pierce had produced more questions than answers.

Edward placed the new archive register on the desk.

“The authorisation document was kept separately from the main file.”

Richard looked up.

“Who approved the separation?”

“According to the index, Charles Vale.”

“Can Pierce retrieve it?”

“He has requested access to the original archive package.”

Sebastian examined the register.

“Who submitted the original request?”

“Unknown.”

“Could it have been someone from the Aurelius Family Office?”

“Yes.”

“Could it have been someone pretending to represent them?”

“Yes.”

Sebastian sighed.

“Again with the possibilities.”

Edward ignored him.

“And the photograph was taken at five-twenty-one in the morning.”

“Yes.”

“That is what the dates suggest.”

“Which means the identity-protection matter was not created as a reaction to whatever happened in the maternity ward.”

“Then someone anticipated a situation.”

Richard walked to the window.

Especially with Isabelle and Elena living under the same roof.

But as dinner continued, she noticed that Richard seemed unusually distracted.

Sebastian was quieter than usual.

Edward checked his phone twice.

And Catherine kept glancing at her husband.

Elena did not know what had happened during the day.

She did not know about the separate authorisation document.

She did not know that a powerful family she had never met had become the centre of an investigation concerning her birth.

She only knew that something was happening behind closed doors.

And she was beginning to wonder how much longer everyone would keep telling her that everything was fine.

At the Aurelius Global Headquarters, Adrian received a message from the legal archives shortly before nine.

The subject line was brief.

**Historical Family Office Records — Retrieval Confirmation**

He opened the attachment.

The archive department had located several records from the year his younger sister disappeared.

Most were routine.

Some concerned security arrangements.

Others related to the search operation that had begun immediately after the family reported her missing.

One entry stood out.

It was dated eighteen years earlier.

The classification read:

**IDENTITY PROTECTION — PRIVATE FAMILY MATTER**

The authorisation number matched the number on the document Edward Valmont had seen.

Adrian sat back.

He opened the record details.

The file had been created under Alexander Aurelius's authority.

But the person who had physically submitted the request was listed only as:

**Special Liaison — External Office**

There was no name.

No department.

No contact information.

Adrian frowned.

He checked the document history.

The file had been transferred to external legal counsel two days before his sister disappeared.

Six weeks later, it had been reviewed.

And after that, it had been sealed.

He opened the next page.

There was a note.

**Supporting authority retained separately. Access restricted to originating representative and senior counsel.**

Adrian stared at the words.

The file was not merely confidential.

Someone had deliberately restricted access to the document that explained why it had been created.

He called his father.

Alexander answered on the second ring.

“Adrian?”

“I found an old family-office record.”

“Which one?”

“An identity-protection file from the year she disappeared.”

Silence.

Then Alexander asked, “Who authorised it?”

“Your name is listed.”

Alexander did not answer immediately.

Adrian continued.

“The file was transferred to Charles Vale's office. The supporting authority was kept separately. The person who submitted the request is listed as an external special liaison, but there's no name.”

“Send it to me.”

“I will.”

“Do not contact the former legal office until I have reviewed the record.”

“Understood.”

Alexander's voice became quieter.

“Adrian.”

“Yes?”

“Find out who that liaison was.”

“I will.”

The call ended.

Adrian looked at the document again.

His father had not sounded surprised that the file existed.

He had sounded disturbed by the possibility of what it contained.

And that was something Adrian could not ignore.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was preparing to sleep.

She had finished her reading, arranged her notes for the following morning and placed her university card beside her bag.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Nicholas appeared.

**Nicholas:** Are you awake?

**Elena:** No. I'm replying in my sleep.

Outside, the gardens were quiet.

Across the estate, Isabelle's bedroom light was still on.

In the study, Richard was reading the hospital investigation.

And hundreds of kilometres away, in a building Elena had never visited, a document bearing the name of Alexander Aurelius was being prepared for restricted review.

For eighteen years, the Aurelius family had searched for a missing daughter.

For eighteen years, someone had kept an old file sealed.

And now, for the first time, the two stories were beginning to approach each other.

But the most important page was still missing.

The page that named the person who had requested the file.

The page that might explain why a family searching desperately for a lost child had authorised a secret identity-protection matter two days before she disappeared.

And somewhere in the surviving records, that page still existed.

**It had not been destroyed.**

**It had simply been taken out of the file.**
