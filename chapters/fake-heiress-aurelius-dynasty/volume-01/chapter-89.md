# **Chapter 89 — The Two Families in the Wrong Columns**

Edward read the instruction again.

**O.R.-A must not be treated as the receiving mother.**

**O.R.-B must not be treated as the originating mother.**

The wording was unusually specific.

It did not say that the identities were incorrect.

It said they must not be treated as something they were not.

Jonathan pulled another chair toward the desk.

"Could this mean O.R.-A was a biological mother, while O.R.-B was the intended guardian?"

"Not necessarily."

"Then what?"

"We need to establish what the original classification meant before assigning either role."

Edward opened the Annex C index.

The two references had been created on the same date, but their descriptions differed.

O.R.-A had been confirmed by the originating facility.

O.R.-B was awaiting independent confirmation.

Neither entry contained a name.

"Let's go back to the original office," Edward said.

"Vale Private Counsel?"

"Yes. I want the designation instructions, not another summary prepared by someone who handled them later."

Jonathan closed his laptop.

"And if the office no longer exists?"

"Then we find whoever inherited its records."

Vale Private Counsel had ceased operating as an independent firm several years earlier.

Its legal records had been transferred to a successor organisation, which had retained the original archive under a preservation agreement.

The archive manager, Ms. Rachel Morton, received Edward and Jonathan in a conference room.

She listened to their explanation, then asked for the reference numbers.

"Annex C," she said. "That was a restricted family custody matter."

"Can you retrieve the original designation schedule?"

"I can request it. But the material is subject to a preservation restriction."

"What restriction?"

"Access is limited to the originating and receiving representatives, unless a court or competent authority orders otherwise."

Edward placed a copy of the old instruction on the table.

"We are trying to identify those representatives."

Morton examined the document.

"Then you are asking for access to the very information that the restriction protects."

"Yes."

"That may be difficult."

"Can you at least confirm whether O.R.-A and O.R.-B refer to people or institutions?"

She checked the archive index.

"People."

"Two individuals?"

"That is how they are recorded."

"Can you confirm their names?"

"No. The names are held in a separate sealed index."

Edward leaned forward.

"Does the index still exist?"

Morton looked at her screen.

After a moment, she nodded.

"Yes."

Jonathan exhaled.

"Where?"

"Archive Unit Four. It was transferred from the original office with the Annex C records."

"Is it part of the same restricted file?"

"No. It is a separate index."

"Can we inspect it?"

"Not without authority."

Edward looked at the document.

"Then I would like a written confirmation that the sealed index exists and contains the identities assigned to O.R.-A and O.R.-B."

Morton considered this.

"I can provide a preservation confirmation."

"Please do."

She entered the request.

Before sending it, she looked up.

"Mr. Valmont, may I ask something?"

"Yes."

"Are you investigating the children who were transferred from St Catherine's?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

"Why?"

Morton turned her monitor slightly.

"The archive contains another reference associated with the same custody arrangement."

She pointed to a line near the bottom.

**Related matter: EFO-SL/07 — External Family Office Liaison.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The same external liaison code had appeared in the hospital records, the Aurelius archive, and the restricted transfer documents.

"Is there anything else?" Edward asked.

Morton scrolled.

A second line appeared.

**Separate originating authority: A-17/ORIGIN.**

Edward's attention sharpened.

"A-17 was the protected identity route," Jonathan said.

"Yes," Edward replied. "But this says originating authority."

Morton looked between them.

"I cannot explain the classification. I can only tell you that the reference is still active in the preservation index."

"Active?"

"Not operationally. It means the file has not been formally closed."

Edward sat back.

After eighteen years, the administrative matter had never been closed.

The preservation confirmation arrived the next morning.

It contained three useful details.

First, the sealed index still existed.

Second, it had never been opened by the successor organisation.

Third, the file remained under a dual-authority restriction.

The document ended with a note.

**No final recipient designation was recorded.**

Jonathan read it.

"So nobody ever completed the schedule."

"Apparently."

"Then why did they keep the file sealed?"

"Because the absence of a final designation didn't cancel the original restriction."

Jonathan looked frustrated.

"Someone could have corrected this years ago."

"Only if they knew the file existed and had authority to do so."

"Charles knew."

"Yes."

"Adrian Holt knew."

"Yes."

"Dr Hale knew."

"At least part of it."

Jonathan shook his head.

"And nobody completed the process."

Edward looked at the preservation confirmation.

"Perhaps completing it would have exposed something they wanted to keep unresolved."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander received a call from the legal department.

The archive successor organisation had confirmed the existence of the sealed index.

He listened carefully.

"Can the family request access?"

The lawyer explained that Victoria could request access to records associated with her maternity admission, but the sealed index also contained another originating identity.

The request might therefore require judicial review.

Alexander looked toward Victoria, who was sitting beside the window.

"Prepare the application."

The lawyer continued.

"There is one additional issue. The original classification does not identify the Aurelius family as the receiving family."

Alexander's expression changed.

"What does it identify us as?"

"An originating family representative, subject to confirmation."

Victoria stood.

"Subject to confirmation?"

Alexander repeated the lawyer's words.

"Yes."

Victoria looked at him.

"But I gave birth to a daughter."

"That is what the maternity record establishes."

"Then why would our family identity be subject to confirmation?"

The lawyer answered carefully.

"Your maternity admission and the identity of the child later assigned to a receiving record are separate matters. The investigation has not yet established that the two records refer to the same infant."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

She knew this already.

She had heard it repeatedly.

But hearing that the Aurelius family itself had been placed under a provisional designation made the uncertainty feel different.

It was not simply that they did not know where their daughter had gone.

Someone had designed a system in which even the family searching for her could not be treated as confirmed until another person completed a process that had never been completed.

Alexander ended the call.

Victoria returned to her chair.

"Who had the authority to confirm us?"

"We don't know yet."

"Then find out."

Meanwhile, Elena received an email from the Singapore International Academic Programme.

She opened it while eating breakfast.

The subject line was brief.

**Final Selection Result**

She stopped chewing.

For a moment, she simply stared at the screen.

Then she opened it.

The message began with a formal greeting and thanked her for the time she had spent preparing her application.

She read the next paragraph.

Then the next.

Her application had been successful.

She had been offered a place in the programme.

Elena read the offer again, paying particular attention to the conditions.

The programme covered tuition and academic accommodation.

Travel expenses and personal costs would remain her responsibility.

She checked the dates.

Then she checked the deadline for accepting.

A small smile appeared.

She had done it.

Not because someone had arranged a place for her.

Not because of her family name.

Because she had submitted a project, answered difficult questions, and convinced the selection panel that her work deserved consideration.

Nicholas entered the dining room.

"Why are you smiling at your breakfast?"

Elena looked up.

"I got in."

He stopped.

"Got into what?"

"Singapore."

For a second, he did not understand.

Then his face brightened.

"You got the place?"

She nodded.

Nicholas pulled out a chair.

"That's excellent!"

"Thank you."

"Does Mother know?"

"Not yet."

"Does Father know?"

"No."

"Do you want me to tell them?"

Elena looked at him.

"Why is everyone so eager to tell my news before I do?"

"Because your news is good."

She laughed.

Nicholas leaned forward.

"When do you leave?"

"I have to confirm the offer first. Then there are documents, accommodation arrangements, and a few other things."

"How long?"

"The programme is one semester."

He nodded slowly.

"That's not too long."

Elena looked at him.

"Are you going to miss me?"

"Obviously."

"Then I should reconsider."

"No. You should go."

His answer came so quickly that she smiled.

Nicholas picked up a piece of toast.

"Besides, someone has to represent the Valmont family internationally."

Elena raised an eyebrow.

"I thought I was going there as myself."

Nicholas paused.

Then he grinned.

"Fair enough. Someone has to represent Elena internationally."

She returned to her breakfast, still smiling.

For the first time in months, the future felt like something she could plan without waiting for someone else's decision.

At the Valmont residence, Catherine was delighted when Elena finally shared the news.

Sebastian asked practical questions about the programme's schedule, costs, and academic arrangements.

Richard wanted to review the offer letter before Elena accepted.

Nicholas announced that he had known about the acceptance for several minutes and had therefore been the first member of the family to celebrate.

Edward, who had joined them by video call, congratulated her.

"Your project must have impressed them."

"I think they liked the fact that it could work without expensive systems."

"That is a useful idea."

"It still needs improvement."

"Everything does."

Elena smiled.

"I want to work on it while I'm there."

Sebastian nodded.

"Then make sure you have a clear plan before you leave."

Richard looked at her.

"You understand that you will have our support if you need it."

"I know."

"And you don't have to prove anything by refusing help."

Elena looked down at the offer letter.

"I know that too."

She meant it.

She did not want independence to become another form of stubbornness.

But she also wanted the opportunity to discover what she could do without relying on the family's resources.

Catherine reached across the table.

"We're proud of you."

Elena smiled.

"Thank you."

For a little while, the conversation remained entirely about Singapore.

Edward returned to the archive investigation that afternoon.

The preservation confirmation had included a reference to the original designation officer.

The name had been withheld from the ordinary index, but the archive manager had provided an employment record.

The designation officer had been a senior administrator at Vale Private Counsel.

**Eleanor Vale.**

Edward read the name.

He had spoken with Eleanor before.

She had repeatedly insisted that maternal identity and receiving identity must remain separate.

She had explained that she had not personally witnessed every transfer and could not identify every infant.

But she had never mentioned being responsible for the final designation process.

Edward called her.

She agreed to meet the following morning.

Eleanor arrived carrying a small handbag and a thick folder.

She looked older than she had during their previous meeting, but her manner remained composed.

Edward showed her the two originating references.

"O.R.-A and O.R.-B."

She read them.

"Where did you get these?"

"From the original designation index."

Eleanor's expression changed.

"You found it?"

"We found confirmation that it exists."

"That is not the same thing."

"No. But we are getting closer."

She placed the documents down.

"What do you want to know?"

"Who were O.R.-A and O.R.-B?"

Eleanor was silent for several seconds.

Then she answered.

"They were the two maternal representatives."

"Victoria Aurelius and Isabella Vale?"

"Yes."

"Which one was O.R.-A?"

Eleanor looked at the document.

"Victoria."

"And O.R.-B?"

"Isabella."

Edward wrote down the answer.

"Why was Victoria's identity confirmed while Isabella's was awaiting independent confirmation?"

"Because Victoria's originating medical admission had been verified directly by St Catherine's. Isabella's admission had been recorded separately, and the associated clinical material was incomplete."

"But Isabella's maternity record confirms a live female infant."

"Yes."

"Then what remained unconfirmed?"

"The relationship between her maternity record and the child later appearing in the receiving schedule."

Edward looked up.

"Was there any reason to doubt that Isabella had given birth?"

"No."

"Then why was her identity awaiting confirmation?"

"Because the process was not asking whether she had given birth. It was asking which receiving record, if any, belonged to the child she had delivered."

Edward understood.

The distinction had been buried beneath years of confusing terminology.

"Was O.R.-A or O.R.-B ever assigned a final receiving identity?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Eleanor looked at him.

"Because the receiving schedule was never completed."

"Who was responsible?"

"Charles Vale submitted the proposed schedule. Dr Hale was responsible for medical verification. The receiving identity custodian was supposed to confirm the recipient. Marianne Cross was responsible for notification."

"Who was the receiving identity custodian?"

"I don't know."

"Did you ever ask?"

"I did."

"What happened?"

"Charles told me that the designation would be supplied after the receiving families had been informed."

Edward frowned.

"But the families were supposed to be informed only after the designation was complete."

"Yes."

"So the process depended on itself."

Eleanor nodded.

"It was a circular arrangement."

"Was that intentional?"

"I cannot say."

Edward placed the anonymous warning on the table.

Eleanor read it.

Her face became noticeably pale.

"Where did you get this?"

"Someone left it at the hospital."

She examined the handwriting.

Then she looked up.

"I know this writing."

Edward leaned forward.

"Whose is it?"

Eleanor did not answer immediately.

She opened her folder and removed a folded sheet.

It was an old handwritten instruction.

The paper had yellowed, and the ink had faded, but the writing was clear.

Edward placed the anonymous warning beside it.

The handwriting was nearly identical.

He looked at Eleanor.

"Who wrote this?"

She turned the old sheet over.

A name had been written on the reverse.

**MARIANNE CROSS.**

Edward stared at it.

Marianne had been involved in the administrative notifications.

She had delivered documents, handled temporary receiving acknowledgements, and received payment for work that was never fully completed.

But this was the first direct indication that she had written the original instruction warning against mixing originating and receiving identities.

"Why didn't you tell us?" he asked.

Eleanor folded her hands.

"Because I didn't know she had written the warning you found."

"Did you know she had access to the recipient schedule?"

"Yes."

"Did you know she was responsible for completing the notifications?"

"Yes."

"Then why did you never identify her as the person who could explain the final designation?"

Eleanor looked down.

"Because Marianne was not the person who made the final designation."

Edward waited.

"She was the person who was supposed to deliver it."

"To whom?"

Eleanor raised her eyes.

"That is the question I was never able to answer."

She pushed the old instruction toward him.

At the bottom, beneath Marianne's signature, was a final handwritten line.

**The receiving family must not be notified until the originating representative confirms the child.**

Edward read it.

Then he looked at the date.

It had been written on the seventeenth day after the births.

The same day Victoria received the warning about Isabella's identification band.

The same day Eleanor submitted her dispute notice.

And the same day the notification process was effectively stopped.

Edward turned the paper over once more.

There was a second notation on the reverse.

It was not a name.

It was an address code.

**R-04.**

Eleanor watched him.

"Do you know what that means?" he asked.

She shook her head.

"No. But I remember seeing it on Marianne's personal correspondence."

"Where?"

"In the records office."

Edward closed the folder.

For months, they had been searching for the missing receiving family.

Now they had discovered that the person responsible for notifying that family might never have been told who they were.

And the woman who had written the warning had just become the most important witness in the investigation.
