# Chapter 14 — The Instruction That Changed Everything

Edward read the letter for the fourth time.

The words had not changed.

Neither had the date.

Four days after the births.

Four days after two families had taken their newborn daughters home, believing that everything had gone according to plan.

And someone had written an instruction that could explain why one mother had spent eighteen years searching for a child who might have been deliberately hidden from her.

Edward picked up his phone.

"Jonathan, I need you to preserve the original archive."

"Already arranged."

"Who else has seen this letter?"

"Only me, you, and the archive custodian."

"Good. Don't circulate it yet."

There was a pause.

"Edward, I should tell you something else."

"What?"

"The scan wasn't included in the original archive index. It was discovered inside a folder that had been misclassified as routine correspondence."

"Who placed it there?"

"We don't know."

"Can you determine when it was added?"

"Not from the scan. The physical folder may tell us more."

Edward looked at the handwritten addition.

*If the mother asks, tell her the child was never found.*

"Jonathan, that final sentence appears to have been added separately."

"I noticed."

"Can you have the handwriting examined?"

"Yes. But we will need the original document."

"Then find it."

Edward ended the call.

For several minutes, he remained alone with the letter.

He had spent days investigating missing documents, altered registration numbers and the identity of an unknown man in a hospital corridor.

Now he had something different.

An instruction.

Someone had not merely concealed information.

Someone had apparently planned what the hospital should say if a mother came looking for her daughter.

Edward opened his notebook and wrote:

**Eleanor Vale — four days after birth.**

Then, beneath it:

**Who was she protecting?**

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was having a much more ordinary problem.

Her laptop had stopped responding.

She stared at the screen.

Clicked once.

Waited.

Clicked again.

Nothing.

"Please don't do this."

She pressed the power button.

The screen went black.

Elena closed her eyes.

"Wonderful."

A knock came at the door.

"Are you talking to your computer?"

Nicholas entered without waiting for an answer.

Elena turned around.

"How did you know?"

"Because I heard you threaten it from the corridor."

"I didn't threaten it."

"You said, and I quote, 'I hope you understand what you've done.'"

"That was a reasonable statement."

Nicholas looked at the laptop.

"What's wrong?"

"It froze."

"Have you tried turning it off and on again?"

"I just did."

"Then I have exhausted my technical knowledge."

Elena stared at him.

"Why are you here?"

"To invite you downstairs."

"For what?"

"Tea."

"That's not an invitation. That's a beverage announcement."

Nicholas shrugged.

"Would you like to attend the beverage announcement?"

Elena looked at her laptop again.

"I need to finish my assignment."

"Edward said the meeting about the hospital records is postponed until tomorrow. There's nothing you can do about it today."

"That doesn't mean I have nothing else to do."

"Exactly. So you should do something else."

She narrowed her eyes.

"Are you trying to distract me?"

"Yes."

"At least you're honest."

Nicholas picked up the laptop.

"Come on. I'll ask someone to look at this."

"I can fix it myself."

"Of course. And if you can't, we can hold a funeral."

"Give that back."

He handed it to her immediately.

"Fine. Bring it downstairs."

Elena shook her head, but she followed him.

Isabelle was already in the sitting room when they arrived.

She had a book open on her lap and a cup of tea on the table.

"Is your laptop broken?" she asked.

"Temporarily uncooperative," Elena replied.

Nicholas dropped into an armchair.

"She refuses to accept that technology has limits."

"Unlike you," Elena said, sitting beside Isabelle. "You accepted your limits years ago."

Nicholas put a hand over his chest.

"That was unnecessarily personal."

Isabelle laughed.

It was a small sound, but Elena noticed something different about it.

A few days earlier, Isabelle had seemed careful whenever she joined a conversation. She would wait for someone to speak to her, measure her words, and watch the others as though trying to understand the rules of a family she had only recently entered.

Now she occasionally interrupted Nicholas.

She had also started making jokes at his expense.

It was progress.

"Have you decided about the university event?" Isabelle asked.

Elena shook her head.

"Not yet."

"You should come."

"Are you going?"

"I think so. There's a presentation about international business programmes. It might be useful."

Nicholas looked between them.

"Excellent. Two university students discussing university events. I feel intellectually unnecessary."

"You are," Elena said.

"See? This is why I need appreciation."

Isabelle closed her book.

"Do you know what you want to study after this year?"

Elena considered the question.

"Something related to economics or business management, perhaps. I'm still comparing courses."

"Why those?"

"I like understanding how organisations work. How money moves, how decisions are made, and why some businesses succeed while others fail."

Nicholas raised an eyebrow.

"You're planning to become a businessperson?"

"I'm planning to learn something useful."

"That's a very dangerous attitude in this house."

Elena ignored him.

"What about you?" she asked Isabelle.

"I haven't decided completely either. I've considered finance, but I don't want to choose something just because people expect it."

Elena nodded.

"Then don't."

Isabelle looked at her.

"That's easy to say."

"I know. But it should still be your decision."

For a moment, Isabelle seemed to be considering something.

Then she smiled.

"Perhaps we can compare university programmes together."

"Sure."

Nicholas looked pleased.

"Wonderful. A study group."

"No," Elena said.

"Why not?"

"Because you'll distract us."

"I can be serious."

Both girls looked at him.

Nicholas sighed.

"Fine. I'll supervise."

"That sounds worse," Isabelle said.

Edward arrived at the Valmont Estate just before dinner.

He asked Richard to meet him in the study.

Sebastian was there already. Catherine joined them a few minutes later.

Edward placed a printed copy of the newly discovered letter on the desk.

Richard read it silently.

His expression changed as he reached the handwritten addition.

Catherine took the document from him.

"Who wrote this?"

"Eleanor Vale," Edward replied. "At least, the main body appears to be hers. We haven't verified the handwriting yet."

Sebastian leaned forward.

"She was married to Charles Vale?"

"Yes. She was also a senior partner at the legal practice before retiring."

Richard looked at the date.

"Four days after the birth."

"Correct."

"What does she mean by the original arrangement?"

"We don't know."

Sebastian pointed to the first paragraph.

"She specifically mentions the child recorded under the Aurelius name. That suggests the arrangement concerned the Aurelius child, not both girls."

"Possibly," Edward said.

Catherine looked unsettled.

"And the instruction to keep both records separate?"

"That suggests she knew the hospital records were being handled outside normal procedures."

Richard set down the letter.

"Could Eleanor Vale have been responsible for the switch?"

"She may have been involved in the concealment. That is not the same as proving she arranged the original transfer."

Sebastian nodded slowly.

"We need to distinguish what we know from what we suspect."

Edward looked at him.

"Exactly."

Richard turned toward his son.

"What about the external liaison?"

"Still unidentified. The archive reference EFO-SL/07 was temporary authority. Adrian Aurelius is checking their family office records."

Catherine looked at the letter again.

"Did Alexander Aurelius know?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

"We have no proof that he did."

"But his name is on the original authorisation."

"His name is listed as the authorised representative. That does not establish that he personally submitted the instruction, saw the hospital correspondence, or knew what happened afterward."

Richard's expression grew more severe.

"Then we need to find out who used his authority."

"Yes."

Catherine folded her hands.

"And what happens to the girls while we investigate?"

Richard looked at her.

"We continue as we have been."

"That isn't an answer."

"It's the only answer I have at the moment."

Catherine looked down.

She had been trying to keep the household stable. She had welcomed Isabelle, reassured Elena, and told herself that the two girls could both have a place in the family.

But the more documents they uncovered, the more she feared that the truth would force a decision no one was ready to make.

Edward noticed her expression.

"Nothing in this letter changes the girls' immediate circumstances."

Catherine looked up.

"Are you certain?"

"I'm certain that we shouldn't make decisions before we know what happened."

Richard nodded.

"Agreed."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander was reading a report from Adrian.

The investigation into the old archive access had uncovered a second complication.

The manual retrieval entry had been created under an employee account belonging to someone who had left the family office several months before the access date.

The account should have been disabled.

It had not been.

"Was the account used for anything else?" Alexander asked.

Adrian stood across the desk.

"Three other archive requests. All related to the same period."

"Which files?"

"Two involved private medical institutions. The third was a legal correspondence file."

"Can we retrieve them?"

"One is available. The other two were transferred to an external archive before the acquisition."

Alexander looked at the report.

"Who approved the transfers?"

"That's still being checked."

"Then check it."

Adrian nodded.

"There is another matter."

Alexander raised his eyes.

"The hospital records-management company has confirmed that a portion of its old backup data survived. The archive is currently held by Aurelius Technologies."

"Can it be accessed?"

"Yes, but the data is incomplete. Several files are encrypted, and the index is damaged."

"Who has access?"

"Only a small technical team. I've instructed them not to alter anything."

Alexander stood and walked toward the window.

For eighteen years, he had imagined the moment when he might finally receive news about his daughter.

He had imagined a telephone call, a letter, a photograph, perhaps a face that resembled Victoria's.

He had never imagined that the first meaningful clue would be an instruction telling a hospital to conceal the child's existence.

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"If the records show that someone used my authority without my knowledge, I want the original evidence preserved."

"Understood."

"And if I did authorise something I no longer remember, I want to know exactly what it was."

Adrian studied his father.

"Do you believe you might have?"

Alexander remained silent for a moment.

"I signed thousands of documents during that period. Some were routine. Some were urgent. Some were presented through people I trusted."

"That doesn't mean you authorised this."

"No. But it means I cannot dismiss the possibility simply because I don't remember."

Adrian nodded.

It was not an answer he had expected.

Alexander returned to his desk.

"Has Victoria been told about the letter?"

"Not yet. I thought you should decide how much to share."

Alexander considered that.

"Tell her what we know. Don't give her theories."

"All right."

Victoria was in the old nursery when Adrian found her.

The room had been preserved almost exactly as it had been eighteen years earlier.

The pale curtains had been replaced twice. The furniture had been maintained. The small wooden rocking horse remained beside the bookshelf.

On a shelf stood a framed photograph of a newborn baby.

Victoria was holding it.

"Your father wants to speak with you," Adrian said.

She looked up.

"Has something happened?"

"Edward Valmont's investigation uncovered another letter."

Victoria stood.

"From the hospital?"

"From Eleanor Vale. It was written four days after the girls were born."

Victoria's fingers tightened around the frame.

"What does it say?"

Adrian told her.

He did not soften the wording.

When he reached the final handwritten sentence, Victoria turned away.

For a long time, she said nothing.

Then she asked, very quietly:

"Did she know?"

"We don't know."

"Did she know where my daughter was?"

"We don't know that either."

Victoria looked at the photograph.

"Someone told the hospital to say my child had never been found."

Adrian did not answer.

"Why?"

"We're trying to establish that."

Victoria carefully placed the photograph back on the shelf.

"Then I want to know who gave that instruction."

"We will find out."

She looked at him.

"And Adrian?"

"Yes?"

"If we find her, I don't want anyone deciding what she should feel about us."

Adrian nodded.

"She'll have her own choices."

Victoria looked once more at the little nursery.

"I have waited eighteen years," she whispered. "I can wait a little longer for her to decide whether she wants to come home."

The next morning, Elena was late.

Not significantly late.

Only seven minutes.

But seven minutes was enough for Nicholas to send three messages.

**Nicholas:** Where are you?

**Nicholas:** Breakfast is getting cold.

**Nicholas:** I have made a serious decision.

Elena looked at her phone while walking down the stairs.

**Elena:** What decision?

**Nicholas:** I am considering eating your breakfast.

She typed a reply.

**Elena:** Touch it and lose a hand.

**Nicholas:** Such aggression before 9 a.m.

When she entered the breakfast room, Nicholas was sitting beside her usual chair.

Her plate was untouched.

She looked at him suspiciously.

"Why haven't you eaten it?"

"Because I'm a generous person."

"You're a thief."

"A generous thief."

Isabelle was laughing quietly.

Catherine smiled as Elena sat down.

For a little while, the conversation stayed ordinary.

Richard discussed a business meeting.

Sebastian complained about a delayed report.

Edward appeared distracted.

Elena noticed.

"Did something happen?"

Edward looked up.

"Why?"

"You've been staring at the same page for five minutes."

Nicholas glanced at him.

"He's probably discovered that paperwork is boring."

Edward ignored him.

"There's been another development in the hospital investigation."

Elena put down her spoon.

"What kind?"

"We found a letter written by someone connected to the old legal office."

"Does it explain what happened?"

"Not yet."

Isabelle looked at him.

"Does it mention either of us?"

Edward hesitated.

"It mentions the child recorded under the Aurelius name."

Elena felt her stomach tighten.

"What does it say?"

Edward considered how much to tell them.

"It indicates that someone instructed the hospital to keep the original records separate and not inform the families."

Catherine's expression changed.

Elena looked from Edward to Richard.

"Why would anyone do that?"

"We don't know," Richard said.

"Was it the same person who changed the records?"

"We haven't established that."

Elena lowered her eyes.

She had hoped each new discovery would bring them closer to an explanation.

Instead, every answer seemed to open another question.

Nicholas noticed her expression.

He nudged the small dish of fruit toward her.

"Eat."

Elena looked at him.

"I'm not hungry."

"Then eat one."

"Why?"

"Because I said so."

"That's not a convincing argument."

"It's the best one I have."

She picked up a strawberry.

Nicholas looked satisfied.

Isabelle watched them quietly.

Then she reached for the fruit dish too.

"Give me one."

Nicholas looked offended.

"Why does everyone keep taking my food?"

Elena smiled faintly.

"Because you're sitting closest to it."

"That's not a valid reason."

"It is in this family," Sebastian said.

For a moment, they laughed.

But Edward's thoughts remained with the letter.

And Elena, though she did not know its exact contents, could not stop thinking about one sentence.

Someone had apparently wanted the hospital to keep her existence a secret.

She wondered whether that person had been protecting her.

Or hiding her.

That afternoon, a technician at Aurelius Technologies contacted Adrian.

The old Meridian archive had yielded something unexpected.

A backup directory had been recovered from a damaged storage unit.

Most of the files were unreadable.

One, however, had survived.

It was a scanned document with no visible title.

The technician opened it and checked the metadata.

The file had been created eighteen years earlier.

Its last modification date was four days after the birth of the two girls.

Adrian requested a secure copy.

When it arrived, he opened the document.

The first page contained a list of hospital record references.

The second page contained a handwritten note.

The handwriting was unfamiliar.

At the bottom was a name.

**Eleanor Vale.**

Adrian read the note once.

Then again.

It contained only two lines.

*The Aurelius child must remain outside the family registry until the matter is resolved.*

*Do not allow Alexander to receive the original report.*

Adrian slowly leaned back.

For the first time since the investigation had begun, he felt genuinely uneasy.

The letter to the hospital had instructed staff not to inform the families.

This document went further.

It specifically instructed someone to prevent Alexander Aurelius from receiving the original report.

Adrian printed the document and carried it to his father's office.

Alexander was speaking with a director when he entered.

Adrian waited until the meeting ended.

Then he placed the paper on the desk.

"We recovered another document from the Meridian archive."

Alexander read it.

His eyes stopped at the second line.

He looked up.

"Who wrote this?"

"Eleanor Vale."

"Are you certain?"

"The metadata is consistent with the archive, but the original document still needs examination."

Alexander read the two lines again.

"She wanted me kept away from the report."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Adrian had no answer.

Alexander looked toward the window.

For eighteen years, he had believed that his daughter had disappeared because someone had taken her.

Now he was beginning to suspect that someone had made sure he would never find her.

He returned his attention to the document.

"Find out who received this."

"We're tracing the archive transfer."

"And Adrian?"

"Yes?"

Alexander's voice became very quiet.

"Do not assume that everyone who helped us search was telling us the truth."

Adrian nodded.

He understood.

The investigation had changed.

They were no longer simply looking for a missing child.

They were looking for the people who had made sure her parents could not find her.

And somewhere in the surviving records was a name that neither family had yet considered.

**The person who had received the original report.**
