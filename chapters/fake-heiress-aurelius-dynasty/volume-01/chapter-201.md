# **Chapter 201 — The Candidate Who Never Received a Decision**

The candidate's file arrived at 8:41.

Edward had requested it three days earlier.

It was not the complete file.

It was a restricted extract containing the candidate's original proposal, conditional response, appointment status and subsequent correspondence.

The identity remained sealed.

Edward opened the first page.

**Reference:** RA-36/11.

**Proposed role:** Independent receiving-route participant.

**Proposal date:** Day 36.

**Candidate response:** Conditional willingness.

**Appointment status:** Incomplete.

**Final decision:** Not recorded.

He moved to the next page.

The candidate had been informed that the proposed appointment could not proceed until the competent receiving authority was independently confirmed.

The notification was dated day 40.

There was no evidence of delivery.

Edward read the delivery field again.

**Dispatch prepared.**

**Transmission status:** Unconfirmed.

**Acknowledgement:** Not received.

The candidate might never have been told that the appointment had failed to proceed.

Sophie pulled a chair beside him.

“Could the candidate have believed the proposal was still active?”

“Possibly.”

“Could they have believed they had been appointed?”

“Not from the response they signed. But they might have received a different representation later.”

Edward opened the correspondence history.

There were four entries.

- Day 36: Conditional response received.
- Day 38: Appointment certificate prepared.
- Day 40: Clarification requested.
- Day 41: Candidate notification prepared.

Nothing followed.

No withdrawal.

No refusal.

No confirmation.

No final decision.

The candidate's file simply stopped.

Edward looked at the last entry.

“Someone prepared a notification but never established that it reached the candidate.”

“Could that be why the participant submitted RR-47/02?”

“Not necessarily. We know the report's author was a different person. We don't know whether they knew the candidate had received anything.”

He closed the file.

The candidate's position was now another unresolved matter.

But it was no longer an abstract administrative possibility.

There was a person who had agreed to help under specific conditions, whose proposed appointment had never been completed, and who might never have received a final explanation.

Edward requested the dispatch record.

At 9:30, the Aurelius family office received a separate update.

Adrian read it first.

The investigation team had confirmed that the latest procedural findings did not identify P-04, the protected participant or the candidate under RA-36/11.

There was no new biological evidence.

No new maternity record.

No verified link to Elena.

Adrian placed the report on the table.

“Still no identity.”

Alexander looked at the final page.

“Then we continue.”

Victoria sat opposite them.

“Has Edward asked for anything?”

“Not from us,” Adrian said. “His latest request concerns the receiving-route candidate's notification.”

Lucien leaned against the window.

“Another document that might or might not have reached somebody.”

Damien looked at him.

“That is not a reason to dismiss it.”

“I wasn't dismissing it.”

Julian had been quiet.

He was holding the list he had made months earlier.

Fifty things he wanted to do with his missing sister.

The list had become worn at the edges.

He turned to item fifty.

**Ask her what she wants to do, instead of deciding everything myself.**

“Do we know anything about the girl yet?” he asked.

Victoria looked at him.

“We know that several records remain unexplained.”

“That's not what I meant.”

“I know.”

“Do we know whether she's somewhere safe?”

Alexander's expression changed slightly.

“We do not know who she is, Julian. We cannot honestly answer that question.”

Julian lowered his eyes.

“I know.”

Victoria reached across the table and rested her hand over his.

“We will not turn uncertainty into a promise.”

He nodded.

Adrian closed the report.

The search would continue.

But none of them would be allowed to treat a document reference as a daughter, a sister or a name.

Not again.

At 11:15, Elena received a message from Isabelle.

**Isabelle:** The foundation team has reviewed the pilot requirements.

**Elena:** And?

**Isabelle:** They want to discuss whether the system could eventually be used for external applications.

**Elena:** Eventually?

**Isabelle:** They specifically said they are not asking you to promise anything today.

Elena smiled.

**Elena:** That's reassuring.

A second message arrived.

**Isabelle:** They also want to know whether you would consider a formal demonstration after the pilot.

**Elena:** If the results are good.

**Isabelle:** I told them the same thing.

Elena looked at the messages for a moment.

The two of them had not become inseparable.

They still disagreed.

They still occasionally misunderstood one another.

But they had developed a working relationship that did not depend on pretending their history had been easy.

Elena typed again.

**Elena:** How is your economics application going?

**Isabelle:** I submitted it.

**Elena:** Congratulations.

**Isabelle:** Don't congratulate me yet. I have an interview.

**Elena:** Then prepare.

**Isabelle:** That is exactly what I am doing.

Elena laughed quietly.

She put the phone down and returned to her pilot dashboard.

Twelve applications were now in the system.

Each had its own history.

Each had a visible status.

And no application could become approved merely because someone had entered the word into a field.

At 13:40, Edward received the dispatch record.

It was short.

**Reference:** RA-36/11-NOTICE.

**Prepared:** Day 40.

**Destination:** Candidate's registered correspondence address.

**Method:** Restricted courier.

**Collection:** Not recorded.

**Delivery:** Not confirmed.

**Return:** No return record located.

Edward looked at Sophie.

“So it wasn't sent?”

“Not necessarily. The courier record could be incomplete.”

“Can we identify the courier?”

“The dispatch office says the collection register has been preserved.”

“Request it.”

The collection register arrived twenty minutes later.

The notification had been collected on day 41.

The courier's identifier was present.

But the delivery field was blank.

There was no signature.

No recipient acknowledgement.

No returned envelope.

Edward examined the courier identifier.

**NC-41.**

Sophie opened the courier registry.

“That's an external service code.”

“Which company?”

“Northgate Document Transit.”

Edward recognised the name.

Northgate had appeared in an earlier correspondence chain concerning restricted archive transfers.

It had also handled several sealed records for Vale Private Counsel.

But there was no evidence that those transfers involved the same courier or the same person.

Edward requested the delivery trace.

At 15:05, Clara called Elena.

“You've been staring at the same screen for ten minutes.”

“I am thinking.”

“You're not moving.”

“Thinking doesn't always involve moving.”

“That's what people say when they're stuck.”

Elena turned her laptop toward Clara.

“I've been trying to decide whether the pilot needs a separate status for incomplete external delivery.”

Clara read the screen.

“You already have 'dispatch prepared' and 'delivery confirmed.'”

“Yes, but there's a gap between them.”

“Then show the gap.”

Elena considered it.

She added a status:

**In transit — external confirmation pending.**

Then another:

**Delivery attempted — outcome unconfirmed.**

Clara nodded.

“That's clearer.”

“And if the item is never delivered?”

“Then it remains unresolved.”

Elena looked at her.

“You're getting annoyingly good at this.”

“I'm learning from the best.”

Elena rolled her eyes.

But she saved the change.

At 16:32, Northgate Document Transit responded.

The company had located the dispatch reference.

The item had been assigned to a courier on day 41.

It had left the central collection office.

There was no completed delivery scan.

There was, however, a handwritten exception note.

**Recipient requested that the document be held pending confirmation of the issuing authority.**

Edward read it twice.

“Who wrote this?”

“The courier, apparently.”

“Did the recipient request the hold?”

“The note says so.”

“Does it identify the recipient?”

“No.”

“Does it identify who confirmed the issuing authority?”

“No.”

Edward opened the remaining details.

The courier had attempted delivery at 14:18.

The person at the address had not accepted the envelope.

Instead, they had asked for confirmation that the notification had been issued by the Programme Authority Office.

The courier had contacted the dispatch office.

No confirmation was received.

The item had been retained.

Two days later, the courier's assignment had been closed.

The envelope was no longer listed as in transit.

But it had not been returned to the issuing office.

Edward sat back.

The candidate might have refused the notification.

Or someone at the registered address might have refused it on their behalf.

Or the recipient might have been willing to accept it only after verifying the authority behind it.

There was no evidence that the candidate had personally made the request.

He read the handwritten note again.

**Pending confirmation of the issuing authority.**

The wording was familiar.

It was the same issue that had prevented the original appointment from being completed.

At 17:20, Edward received another message.

This one came from RPCA.

It concerned the former participant who had submitted RR-47/02.

The office had authorised disclosure of a final procedural fact.

**The protected participant's continuing communication arrangement remained active after the day-47 report.**

**No record has been located showing that the participant received the candidate's unconfirmed notification.**

Edward read the message.

The report's author had not necessarily known that the candidate's notification had failed.

The report had been submitted to preserve the accuracy of the appointment record.

It had not been a personal intervention on behalf of the candidate.

Edward added the information to the chronology.

Then he opened the candidate's file again.

The notification had been collected.

A delivery attempt had occurred.

Someone had asked for verification.

The issuing office had not responded.

And the document had disappeared from the ordinary delivery process.

The candidate's identity was still sealed.

But the unanswered question had become more specific.

Who had refused to accept a notification that merely explained an incomplete appointment?

At 18:06, a final attachment arrived.

Northgate had located the original courier's incident note.

It was a single page, handwritten and signed.

The courier's name was visible.

**Nora Whitcomb.**

Edward recognised the name from an earlier investigation record.

Nora had previously handled a restricted archive transfer.

She was an independent courier, not a Programme Authority employee.

Edward read her note.

*The person at the registered address did not identify themselves as the proposed candidate.*

*They requested confirmation from the issuing office before accepting the envelope.*

*I explained that I was authorised only to deliver the item.*

*The person declined receipt.*

*No further discussion took place.*

At the bottom was a final sentence.

*They asked me to tell the sender that the candidate had not been contacted through a verified channel.*

Edward stopped.

Sophie leaned closer.

“That's important.”

“Yes.”

“Did Nora identify the person?”

“No.”

“Did she know whether the candidate lived there?”

“No.”

Edward looked at the final sentence again.

The person at the address had not simply refused the document.

They had challenged the channel.

They had said the candidate had not been contacted through a verified route.

Edward opened a fresh request to Northgate.

He asked whether Nora Whitcomb was available for a formal interview.

Then he opened the chronology and added one final entry.

**Day 41 — Candidate notification collected by courier. Delivery refused by unidentified person at registered address, pending verification of issuing authority. Courier recorded that the candidate had not been contacted through a verified channel.**

He saved the file.

The candidate had not received a decision.

The participant who reported the discrepancy had not been the candidate.

And someone at the candidate's registered address had apparently known that the notification was not being delivered through a verified channel.

Edward closed the investigation folder.

The next interview would begin with one question:

**Who was the person who refused the envelope?**
