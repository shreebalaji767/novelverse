# **Chapter 200 — The Request That Required Two Authorities**

The response arrived at 8:19.

Edward had expected a refusal.

Instead, Restricted Personal Communication Administration had supplied a partial copy of PC-41/02's originating record.

The first page contained the administrative details.

**Reference:** PC-41/02.

**Created:** Day 29.

**Purpose:** Continuity of protected personal correspondence during receiving-route withdrawal.

**Authority basis:** PI-07.

**Requesting authority:** Sealed.

**Second authority confirmation:** Recorded separately.

**Administrative execution:** Confirmed.

Edward read the last two fields.

“Two authorities,” Sophie said.

“Yes.”

“Both required?”

“Apparently.”

He turned to the next page.

The first authority had authorised the creation of a restricted communication arrangement.

The second had confirmed that the arrangement could continue even if the participant's receiving-route involvement ended.

Neither authority was identified.

The document also contained a restriction:

**The communication arrangement must not be interpreted as a continuation of receiving-route participation.**

Edward placed it beside the day-41 closure notice.

The wording was almost identical.

Someone had anticipated the possibility that the two roles would be confused.

“Does the record identify who requested the arrangement?” Sophie asked.

“No. It identifies the authority under which it was requested.”

“Can we obtain the request itself?”

“That's what we'll ask for.”

He opened a new correspondence request.

This time he asked for three things:

1. The original request that initiated PC-41/02.
2. The separate confirmation required under PI-07.
3. The record showing which office received each authority.

He did not request the sealed identities.

He wanted to understand the process.

The request was sent at 8:31.

At 9:10, Edward reviewed the day-29 chronology.

PI-07 had been created earlier.

Its purpose was to preserve a personal instruction requiring joint authority for specified decisions.

It had not been created by Programme Administration.

It had not been approved by Vale Private Counsel.

Its custody had later been transferred to Dr Samuel Hale under preservation arrangements.

The two-authority requirement had remained intact.

But PC-41/02 had been created under it.

Edward opened the communication arrangement's permission schedule.

The participant could receive procedural correspondence, ask questions and request corrections to records concerning their role.

They could not nominate a receiving representative.

They could not confirm an identity.

They could not accept placement responsibility.

They could not waive the protected principal's personal acknowledgement.

It was a narrow arrangement.

Yet someone had considered it important enough to preserve after the participant's receiving-route involvement ended.

Edward wrote a question in his notebook.

*What information was the participant entitled to receive that they could not act upon?*

It was possible the arrangement existed simply to protect someone who had been involved in the process.

But the wording suggested something more deliberate.

The participant was allowed to receive correspondence concerning personal communication conditions.

Not the child's identity.

Not the receiving family's identity.

Not the full placement record.

Only the procedural information necessary to understand their own position.

Edward underlined the distinction.

At 10:05, Elena received an email from the academic programme.

The pilot approval had been confirmed.

She read it twice.

Then she called Clara.

“It's approved.”

Clara grinned.

“Really?”

“Supervised pilot. Limited number of applications. The programme team will monitor the results.”

“That counts.”

“It counts as permission to test it.”

“Which is what you wanted.”

Elena looked at the email again.

There was a list of conditions.

The system had to retain original submissions.

Any correction had to create a new version.

Reviewer permissions had to remain separate from final decision authority.

Applicants had to be able to see the difference between receipt, verification and acceptance.

And the programme team would review every access-log entry during the pilot.

Elena smiled.

“They've given me more work.”

Clara laughed.

“You're happy.”

“I am.”

“Then stop pretending the extra work is a punishment.”

Elena leaned back.

For a moment, she allowed herself to enjoy the achievement.

She had not received the approval because of her surname.

She had not received it because someone had decided to help her.

She had built something, tested it, corrected its weaknesses and explained why the distinctions mattered.

That was enough.

At 11:22, RPCA replied.

The original request for PC-41/02 could not be released in full.

However, a redacted extract was authorised.

Edward opened it.

**Requesting authority:** First authority under PI-07.

**Purpose:** Preserve a restricted personal communication channel for a participant whose receiving-route role might conclude before the underlying personal communication conditions were resolved.

**Second authority confirmation:** Required and received.

**Scope:** Procedural correspondence only.

**Duration:** Until completion of the personal communication conditions or further instruction under PI-07.

Edward stopped.

The communication arrangement had been created because the participant's receiving-route role might end before the personal communication conditions were resolved.

That was not an ordinary administrative contingency.

It suggested the two processes were expected to move at different speeds.

The receiving-route process could end.

The personal communication process could remain unresolved.

Sophie read the extract.

“So the participant wasn't supposed to remain involved in the receiving route.”

“Correct.”

“But they weren't supposed to lose their communication rights either.”

“Correct.”

Edward examined the second authority confirmation.

It contained one additional sentence.

**The participant's procedural correspondence rights are not dependent upon acceptance of any receiving appointment.**

He looked at the date.

Day 29.

Seven days before the conditional receiving-route proposal.

Eleven days before the discrepancy report.

The arrangement had been created before the candidate response existed.

The participant's continuing communication rights were not a reaction to the later dispute.

They had been planned in advance.

At 12:40, Edward received the release conditions for PI-07.

Dr Eleanor Vale had approved a limited review.

The original instruction would remain sealed.

But its authority schedule could be examined.

Edward and Sophie attended the controlled records room that afternoon.

Eleanor was already there.

Two separate folders lay on the table.

“PI-07,” she said, indicating the first, “and the authority schedule associated with PC-41/02.”

“Are they held together?”

“No. They are cross-referenced, but they have separate custody records.”

Edward sat down.

“Why?”

“Because the communication arrangement is an administrative consequence of the instruction, not the instruction itself.”

“Can you explain the two-authority requirement?”

“I can explain what the records show.”

Eleanor opened the authority schedule.

The first authority had been recorded on day 28.

The second was recorded on day 29.

Both were valid.

Both were required.

Neither was described as a substitute for the other.

Edward examined the role descriptions.

The first authority had approved the establishment of the personal communication arrangement.

The second had approved the continuation of that arrangement if the participant's receiving-route involvement ended.

There were two separate signatures.

Both were covered.

The credential validation had been completed.

The individual identities remained sealed.

Edward looked at Eleanor.

“Were the two authorities issued by the same person?”

“I cannot certify that.”

“Do the credentials indicate the same office?”

“No. They were issued under separate authority classifications.”

“Could one person hold both classifications?”

“Possibly. The record does not establish whether that happened.”

Edward nodded.

He had learned not to turn a possibility into a conclusion.

“Was the arrangement created at the participant's request?”

“The request extract does not say.”

“Was it created for the participant's protection?”

“It was created to preserve the participant's procedural communication rights.”

“That's not quite the same thing.”

“No,” Eleanor said. “It isn't.”

Edward turned to the final page.

There was a handwritten instruction.

**No receiving-route status shall be inferred from continued personal correspondence.**

He recognised the wording.

It was another version of the same distinction.

The instruction had been written before the day-36 proposal.

Before the candidate response.

Before the day-47 report.

Someone had anticipated that continued correspondence might later be mistaken for continuing authority.

Edward looked at Eleanor.

“Was this restriction included in the original PI-07 instruction?”

“Yes.”

“Was it added to PC-41/02?”

“Yes.”

“Was it repeated in the day-41 closure notice?”

“Yes.”

“Why repeat it so many times?”

Eleanor closed the folder.

“Because the distinction had to survive the administrative process.”

Edward considered that.

The problem might not have been a single incorrect entry.

It might have been the repeated failure to preserve the same distinction across different offices.

At 14:15, Sophie received the access history for PC-41/02.

It contained a short list.

The arrangement had been accessed by RPCA on day 29.

It had been reviewed by the Programme Authority Office on day 41.

It had been referenced by Restricted Governance Review on day 47.

It had been included in the day-49 preservation schedule.

It had not been accessed by Vale Private Counsel.

Edward examined the last entry.

“Charles's office never opened it?”

“Not according to this access history.”

“Did they receive a reference to it?”

“Yes. The day-49 preservation notice listed PC-41/02 as a protected cross-reference.”

“Could they have known its contents?”

“Not from this record.”

Edward looked at the separate custody record.

Vale Private Counsel had held RA-36/11 and its incomplete appointment certificate.

RPCA had held PC-41/02.

The Programme Authority Office had held PI-07's original authority schedule.

Three offices.

Three distinct records.

No evidence that any one of them held the entire process.

“Request the distribution record for the day-29 instruction,” Edward said.

Sophie nodded.

“Who received the authority notice, not the sealed instruction itself?”

“Exactly.”

At 15:50, Edward received a response from the Programme Authority Office.

The distribution record had been located.

It listed four recipients.

**Restricted Personal Communication Administration.**

**Programme Authority Office.**

**Dr Samuel Hale — Preservation Liaison.**

**Restricted Governance Review.**

Vale Private Counsel was not listed.

Programme Administration was not listed.

Edward read the names again.

Dr Hale had received a preservation reference, not the full instruction.

Governance Review had received a restricted authority notice.

RPCA had administered the communication arrangement.

The Programme Authority Office had retained the originating authority schedule.

It was a deliberately limited distribution.

The next line caught Edward's attention.

**Recipient acknowledgement:** All four offices confirmed receipt.

But below that was a qualification.

**Receipt does not establish access to sealed personal instruction content.**

Edward leaned back.

Even the distribution record distinguished between receiving a reference and receiving the underlying information.

The same principle appeared everywhere.

Receipt was not review.

Review was not approval.

A conditional response was not acceptance.

A correspondence route was not a nomination.

A preserved communication channel was not continuing receiving authority.

The documents were repetitive because the boundaries had repeatedly been tested.

At 17:12, a new message arrived from Restricted Governance Review.

It concerned the day-47 report.

The office had authorised disclosure of one additional field from the original submission.

Edward opened it.

**Relationship to RA-36/11:** Submitter was not the proposed candidate.

He read it again.

Sophie looked at him.

“That's definitive.”

“Yes.”

The person who submitted RR-47/02 was not the candidate named in the conditional receiving-route proposal.

That eliminated one possible interpretation.

It did not identify the submitter.

It did not establish the submitter's relationship to the candidate.

But it confirmed that the report had come from someone else.

Edward continued reading.

**Relationship to PC-41/02:** Submitter was the protected participant under the continuing personal communication arrangement.

He stopped.

The report's author was the participant whose communication arrangement had been created under PI-07.

The author was not the candidate.

The author had left the receiving route on day 41.

And six days later, they had submitted a report identifying the improper representation of a candidate's conditional response.

Edward looked at the screen.

“Now we know the report came from the participant.”

“But not who they were.”

“No.”

“Could they have been connected to the candidate?”

“We don't know.”

“Could they have been connected to P-04?”

“The authority record doesn't say.”

Edward closed the message.

The distinction was important.

The person who had raised the discrepancy was now identified by role, not by name.

They had been a protected participant.

They had been involved in the receiving-route process.

They had withdrawn before the report was submitted.

They retained a separate communication arrangement under PI-07.

And they had not been the proposed candidate.

At 18:03, Edward opened the original request for PC-41/02 one last time.

The redacted extract contained a final sentence he had not noticed earlier.

**The participant's continued correspondence must not be used to infer knowledge of, agreement with or participation in any subsequent receiving arrangement.**

Edward read it slowly.

The instruction had anticipated not only confusion about authority.

It had anticipated confusion about knowledge and consent.

A person could remain in communication without knowing what happened next.

They could receive procedural correspondence without being told the identity of a receiving family.

They could preserve their rights without agreeing to a placement.

He closed the document.

The report's author had been protected because their personal communication arrangement continued.

But that arrangement had never given them the right to decide the receiving identity.

And their report had not been an attempt to claim that right.

It had been an attempt to prevent someone else from treating an incomplete process as though that right had already been exercised.

Edward opened his chronology and added the confirmed relationship.

**RR-47/02 submitter = protected participant under PC-41/02.**

**Submitter ≠ proposed candidate under RA-36/11.**

He left the identity field blank.

Then he added a new question.

**What happened to the candidate after the conditional proposal was withdrawn from active consideration?**

Because the candidate's response had been received.

The appointment had never been completed.

The report's author had left the receiving route.

But the original proposal still existed.
