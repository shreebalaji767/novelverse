# **Chapter 53 — The Person Who Came in Her Place**

Edward placed the transfer request beside the visitor register.

The two documents had been recovered from different sources, written by different people, and separated by eleven years of administrative handling.

Yet both contained the same unusual restriction.

The child must not be allowed to believe that the visitor was her mother.

Edward read the sentence again.

"Why would anyone need to specify that?"

Jonathan examined the page.

"Perhaps the visitor looked like her mother."

"That's possible."

"Or perhaps the child had been told someone was coming to collect her."

Edward shook his head.

"Then why not simply tell her the visitor had been unable to meet her?"

"Because someone didn't want her to know the visitor had come at all."

Edward looked at him.

"Exactly."

He opened the original transfer request.

The signature at the bottom was still difficult to read.

**A. V.**

The document had been signed on the day Anna was transferred from St. Agnes.

The receiving party's name was missing.

The destination was blank.

And the authorisation had been issued under a confidential administrative reference.

Edward picked up his phone.

"Get me the original routing record. I want to know where this document went before it reached St. Agnes."

The records department at St. Agnes had retained a partial dispatch ledger.

It was not a complete record.

Several pages had been removed, and the remaining entries contained only reference numbers and brief descriptions.

However, one entry matched Anna's transfer request.

**CASE A.M. — SPECIAL TRANSFER AUTHORISATION.**

**ORIGIN: VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL.**

**ROUTING: PRIVATE FAMILY OFFICE.**

**DELIVERY CONFIRMATION: RECEIVED.**

Edward frowned.

"Vale Private Counsel sent the instruction?"

Jonathan checked the document again.

"The signature is A.V., but the routing origin is Charles Vale's office."

"That means the person signing it may not have been the person who issued it."

"Or Charles may have been acting as an intermediary."

Edward looked at the dispatch date.

It was the same day the child disappeared from St. Agnes.

"Find the delivery confirmation."

The clerk searched the archive.

After several minutes, she located a scanned receipt.

The recipient's name had been partly obscured by a fold.

Only the final letters remained visible.

**...RELLI.**

Edward went still.

"Marcus Morelli?"

"Possibly," Jonathan said.

Edward examined the signature.

It was not a close match to Morelli's known handwriting.

But the name was not necessarily the signature.

A delivery receipt could have been signed by an assistant.

"Ask Morelli whether he received anything from Vale Private Counsel on that date."

Jonathan made a note.

Edward looked at the remaining information.

There was another line.

**PERSONAL DELIVERY — NO OFFICE REGISTRATION.**

Whoever had received the document had not entered it into the ordinary Family Office records.

And that suggested the instruction had been deliberately kept outside the formal system.

Marcus Morelli arrived at the Valmont legal office that afternoon.

He looked tired.

Edward placed the delivery receipt on the table.

"Did you receive this?"

Morelli examined it.

"Yes."

Jonathan leaned forward.

"You recognise it?"

"I recognise the delivery format."

"Did you sign for it?"

"No. One of the private office couriers brought it to my residence."

Edward studied him.

"Why was it sent there?"

"Because I was no longer authorised to receive confidential Family Office correspondence."

"Yet someone still sent it to you."

"Yes."

"Who?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward placed the transfer request beside the receipt.

"Did you authorise Anna's transfer from St. Agnes?"

Morelli looked at the document.

"No."

"Do you recognise the initials A.V.?"

He remained silent for a moment.

Then he said, "I have seen them before."

"Where?"

"In the original correspondence."

"Whose initials are they?"

"I cannot say."

Edward's expression hardened.

"You've been withholding information for weeks."

"I have also told you repeatedly that I will not identify someone without evidence."

"Then tell me what you can prove."

Morelli looked down at the transfer request.

"The person who signed this was not the person who physically collected the child."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

"How do you know?"

"Because I received a separate instruction."

"From Charles?"

"Yes."

"What did it say?"

Morelli opened his leather folder and removed a folded copy.

It was dated the same day as the transfer.

*The child is to be released only after the receiving party has been informed of the restrictions.*

*The receiving party must not be told the child's original hospital designation.*

*The receiving party must not be informed of the identity of the person who arranged the visit.*

*The child must not be told that the visitor was her mother.*

Edward read the final line.

"Who was the receiving party?"

Morelli shook his head.

"I was not given the name."

"Did you meet them?"

"No."

"Did you meet the visitor?"

"No."

"Then what exactly did you do?"

"I confirmed that the transfer had been authorised by the person holding the relevant authority."

Edward placed the document down.

"Who held that authority?"

Morelli looked at him.

"At that time, the records said Charles Vale."

"Did he actually have it?"

Morelli did not answer.

Edward waited.

Finally, Morelli said, "That is what I have spent eighteen years trying to establish."

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian had found another document.

It was an old internal correspondence record from the year Anna had been admitted to St. Agnes.

The message had been sent from a private office account.

Its subject was:

**REQUEST FOR CONFIRMATION — ST. AGNES VISIT.**

The message contained only two sentences.

*The visit was completed as arranged.*

*The child was not informed of the visitor's identity.*

Alexander read it.

"Who sent this?"

"The sender field is missing from the archived copy."

"Who received it?"

"Charles Vale."

Victoria took the paper.

"Was the visit actually completed?"

Adrian shook his head.

"The St. Agnes register says access was refused."

Victoria looked up.

"Then this message is false."

"Yes."

Alexander returned to the desk.

"Could it have referred to a different visit?"

"Possibly, but the reference number matches."

Victoria looked at the date.

"Someone told Charles the visit had been completed, even though the visitor never saw the child."

Adrian nodded.

"That's what the records suggest."

Alexander examined the correspondence.

"Who had access to this account?"

"Three people: Charles Vale, Margaret Voss, and one Family Office administrator."

"Who was the administrator?"

Adrian opened the personnel record.

"Daniel Mercer."

Alexander recognised the name.

"The legal assistant?"

"Yes. He later worked with Northbridge Family Services."

Victoria looked at the documents.

"Then he may know who arranged the visit."

Adrian nodded.

"I'll contact him."

Alexander stopped him.

"Not yet."

Adrian looked back.

"Why?"

"Because if he was involved in the false confirmation, he may already know we're investigating."

Alexander closed the folder.

"First, obtain the original account access log. I want to know who sent the message, not who had permission to send it."

Elena's interview began at ten the following morning.

She arrived early.

Her documents were organised, her laptop was fully charged, and she had checked the university's interview instructions three times.

Nicholas had offered to drive her.

Sebastian had offered to accompany her.

Isabelle had offered to prepare breakfast.

Elena had declined all three offers.

She wanted to arrive independently.

Catherine had simply wished her luck.

At 9:15, Elena received a message from Clara.

**Don't forget to breathe.**

Elena smiled and replied.

**I know how to breathe.**

A second message arrived.

**You forget when you're nervous.**

Elena put her phone away.

The interview lasted thirty-two minutes.

The panel asked about her academic interests, her approach to teamwork, and her understanding of business management.

One interviewer asked why she wanted to study in another country rather than remain in the familiar environment of her own family.

Elena considered the question.

"Because familiarity can make it easy to accept things without examining them. I want to experience different systems, meet people with different perspectives, and learn how organisations function outside the environment I already know."

The interviewer nodded.

"Would you be comfortable working in a team where other students have considerably more experience than you?"

"Yes. I would also expect to learn from them."

"And if you disagree with their approach?"

"I would explain my reasoning, listen to theirs, and try to understand which solution best serves the objective. I don't think disagreement has to become personal."

The panel moved to the next question.

By the time the interview ended, Elena felt more relieved than nervous.

She had not tried to sound extraordinary.

She had simply answered honestly.

Outside the building, she called Nicholas.

"Finished?"

"Yes."

"How was it?"

"Good, I think."

"Only think?"

"I don't know the result yet."

Nicholas laughed.

"Fair."

Elena looked at the university building behind her.

"I actually enjoyed it."

"Then it was worth doing."

She smiled.

"Yes. It was."

That evening, Isabelle found Elena in the dining room.

"How was the interview?"

"Better than I expected."

"Did they ask difficult questions?"

"A few."

"Did you answer them?"

"I think so."

Isabelle pulled out a chair.

"Then you deserve a celebration."

Elena raised an eyebrow.

"What kind?"

"Something that doesn't involve an entire family gathering."

"Good."

"Perhaps dessert."

"Now you're speaking my language."

They ordered two portions from the kitchen.

While they waited, Isabelle examined the notebook Elena had left on the table.

"You wrote all these answers yourself?"

"Yes."

"They're very organised."

"I rewrote them several times."

Isabelle smiled.

"I could never prepare like that."

"You could."

"I would probably spend half the time worrying about whether my handwriting looked professional."

Elena laughed.

"That's why you need to use a computer."

"Very helpful."

The two girls continued talking until the dessert arrived.

Their conversation moved from university applications to scholarship policies, then to an argument about whether a person could survive an entire week eating only instant noodles.

It was not an important conversation.

That was precisely why Elena enjoyed it.

For once, nobody was discussing hospital records, family identities, or inheritance arrangements.

They were simply two eighteen-year-old girls deciding what to eat.

The next morning, Edward received a message from Adrian.

**We found the original account access log.**

Edward called him immediately.

"Who sent the confirmation?"

"Margaret Voss."

Edward sat forward.

"Are you certain?"

"The account records are intact. The message was sent from her assigned terminal."

"Was it sent under Alexander's authority?"

"No. It was sent under her personal administrative credentials."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Then she knowingly reported that the visit had been completed."

Adrian continued.

"There's more. The message was sent at 4:06 p.m."

"What time was the visitor refused?"

"3:42 p.m."

Edward looked at the two timestamps.

The visitor had been turned away.

Twenty-four minutes later, Margaret Voss had reported the visit as completed.

"Did she explain the discrepancy?"

"No explanation in the records."

"Was there a reply?"

"Yes. Charles Vale responded at 4:19 p.m."

"What did he say?"

Adrian read from the message.

*Acknowledged. The child must remain unaware of the attempted contact.*

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

"Send me the complete correspondence."

"Already done."

The call ended.

Edward opened the attachment.

The exchange was short.

But the final message contained an additional instruction.

*Please confirm that the visitor has been redirected to the agreed receiving party.*

Edward read it twice.

Then he called Jonathan.

"Find the visitor."

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian had already reached the same conclusion.

He placed the correspondence before Alexander and Victoria.

"The visitor was not simply turned away," he explained. "Margaret Voss was instructed to redirect them."

"To where?" Alexander asked.

"The records don't say."

Victoria examined the message.

"Agreed receiving party."

Adrian nodded.

"Someone was waiting elsewhere."

Alexander stood.

"Then the visit to St. Agnes may have been a diversion."

Victoria looked at him.

"Or the visitor was supposed to meet the child somewhere else."

"Possibly."

She returned to the message.

"Whoever arranged this knew the visitor would be refused."

Alexander nodded.

"And they had another destination prepared."

Victoria looked towards the old nursery wing.

A child had once been brought into that house.

A child whose bedroom had remained untouched for eighteen years.

But there was still no proof that the child at St. Agnes was theirs.

She would not allow hope to become another reason for making mistakes.

"Find the receiving party," she said.

Edward spent the rest of the afternoon examining the old correspondence.

The phrase appeared three times.

**AGREED RECEIVING PARTY.**

It was not used in the original hospital documents.

It had first appeared in the tenth-day records, then in the Northbridge payment correspondence, and finally in the St. Agnes visitor instructions.

The same terminology had been carried across different institutions for eleven years.

Edward requested every document containing the phrase.

The archive returned a file he had not seen before.

It was a short agreement between Vale Private Counsel and an unnamed receiving party.

The agreement had been signed on the day Anna was transferred from St. Agnes.

Its purpose was described as:

**Temporary care following failed identity verification.**

The receiving party had agreed to provide accommodation, education, and medical care.

In return, Vale Private Counsel would arrange payments and ensure that the child's original identity remained undisclosed.

The agreement contained a handwritten condition.

**The child may be told that her mother is alive only after the receiving party has confirmed the mother's location.**

Edward stopped reading.

That was the first time any document had explicitly stated that the child could be told her mother was alive.

He turned the page.

The receiving party's signature was present.

But the name had been removed.

Only the final part remained.

**...RELLI.**

Edward stared at it.

He called Jonathan.

"Bring Morelli back."

Marcus Morelli arrived an hour later.

Edward placed the agreement before him.

Morelli read it carefully.

His expression changed when he reached the final signature.

"Where did you find this?"

"In the St. Agnes archive."

Morelli looked at the signature again.

"That's not mine."

Edward watched him.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"Then whose signature is it?"

Morelli remained silent.

Edward pushed the document closer.

"The name ends in Relli. Someone signed as the receiving party. Someone agreed to keep the child until her mother's location could be confirmed."

Morelli looked at the document for a long time.

Then he said something Edward had not expected.

"That agreement was never supposed to exist."

Edward's expression sharpened.

"Why?"

"Because the receiving party had already been selected before the child arrived at St. Agnes."

"Who selected them?"

Morelli shook his head.

"I don't know."

"Then what do you know?"

Morelli pointed to the final paragraph.

"The receiving party was not authorised to disclose the child's identity. But they were authorised to tell her that someone had come looking for her."

Edward looked at the agreement.

"Someone came looking for her, and the receiving party was supposed to tell her?"

"Yes."

"But the centre was instructed not to tell her."

Morelli nodded.

"Which means the two instructions contradicted each other."

Edward looked at him.

"Who issued the second instruction?"

Morelli's face became grim.

"Charles Vale."

Edward placed the agreement beside the visitor register.

"Then why did he later tell the receiving party that the mother had to be located before the child could be told the truth?"

Morelli looked down.

"Because the person who came to St. Agnes was not the person the child was supposed to meet."

Edward waited.

Morelli slowly closed the folder.

"The visitor was sent there to collect information, not to collect the child."

"Information about what?"

"Whether Anna still remembered the name she had been given at birth."

Edward went still.

"Why would that matter?"

Morelli looked directly at him.

"Because someone had realised that the child might remember something the adults had failed to erase."

Edward leaned forward.

"What did she remember?"

Morelli hesitated.

Then he answered.

"Her mother's name."

The room became silent.

"Which name?" Edward asked.

Morelli looked at the old agreement.

"I was never told."

Edward glanced at the visitor register.

The surname Aurelius.

The child named Anna.

The visitor who had been turned away.

And now a new possibility: someone had not gone to St. Agnes to reunite with the child.

They had gone to discover what she remembered.

Morelli stood.

"There is one more thing you should know."

Edward looked up.

"The person who signed this agreement was not the final guardian."

"Who was?"

Morelli picked up his folder.

"The final guardian was someone who had already been involved in the original hospital arrangement."

He paused at the door.

"And that person had been told never to let the child meet anyone from the Aurelius family."

Edward stared at him.

"Who?"

Morelli's answer was quiet.

"Margaret Voss."
