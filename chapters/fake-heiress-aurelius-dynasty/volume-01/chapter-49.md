# **Chapter 49 — The Person Who Paid for Silence**

The departure record was only half a page.

That was what made it disturbing.

Margaret Voss had left her registered address on the twenty-eighth day after the births. Her departure had been recorded by the local property office, but no forwarding address was provided.

Beneath her name was the second entry.

**CHILD — NAME NOT ENTERED.**

The child's departure had been recorded separately.

No birth certificate number.

No hospital registration number.

No guardian's name.

Only the words:

**TRANSFERRED PRIVATELY.**

And a handwritten instruction:

**This child must never appear in either family's records.**

Edward placed the document on his desk.

Jonathan read it twice.

"Could this be the missing child?"

"Possibly."

"Which one?"

"We don't know."

"Could it be one of the two infants?"

"It could. But we have no evidence connecting this departure to either Infant A or Infant B."

Jonathan pointed to the date.

"Twenty-eighth day."

"Yes."

"That's the same day Margaret Voss left the hospital."

"And the same day her Aurelius Family Office secondment ended."

Edward opened the financial file.

"Someone arranged her departure, paid for temporary accommodation, and ensured that a child was not registered under either family's name."

"Do we know who paid?"

Edward turned the page.

The payment reference was visible.

**VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL — CONFIDENTIAL SETTLEMENT ACCOUNT.**

Jonathan sighed.

"Charles Vale again."

"His office was involved. That doesn't establish that he personally made the decision."

"You're defending a man who is no longer alive."

"I'm defending the investigation from assumptions."

Edward picked up the telephone.

"Get me the settlement account's complete transaction history."

At 9:00 a.m., Elena was standing in front of her bedroom mirror, practising her interview introduction.

"My name is Elena Valmont. I am interested in business management because—"

She stopped.

The sentence sounded rehearsed.

She tried again.

"My name is Elena Valmont. I want to understand how businesses operate, how decisions affect people, and how organisations can become more effective."

Better.

She wrote it down.

Then she frowned.

The name on the application was still Elena Valmont.

She had considered changing it after the DNA investigation began, but she had not decided what she would use instead.

Her legal documents still carried the Valmont surname.

Her university records did too.

Until the investigation established something definite, changing her name would create more problems than it solved.

A knock came at the door.

"Come in."

Nicholas entered with a small paper bag.

Elena looked at it.

"What is that?"

"Breakfast."

"I already ate."

"Then it's emergency breakfast."

She opened the bag.

Inside were two small pastries.

Elena looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because you have an interview preparation session today."

"That doesn't explain the pastries."

Nicholas shrugged.

"Encouragement."

She smiled.

"Thank you."

He sat on the edge of the chair.

"Have you decided what you'll say if they ask about your family?"

Elena's expression changed.

"I don't know."

"You don't have to explain everything."

"I know."

"You can simply say you want to study abroad because of your academic interests."

Elena nodded.

"I don't want the interview to become a discussion about my personal life."

"Then don't let it."

She looked at him.

"That's easy to say."

"I know."

Nicholas stood.

"Anyway, I won't distract you."

"You're already doing that."

He laughed and left.

Elena returned to her notes.

She had spent eighteen years being introduced as the youngest Valmont daughter.

Now even a simple introduction seemed to require thought.

She crossed out the sentence.

Then she wrote another.

"My name is Elena. I am interested in understanding how organisations work and how I can contribute to building something useful."

She read it again.

For now, that was enough.

The financial records arrived shortly before noon.

Edward opened the transaction history.

The confidential settlement account had been active for several months.

It contained payments to temporary accommodation providers, medical suppliers, private transport companies, and several individuals.

One payment stood out.

A substantial transfer had been made on the twenty-eighth day after the births.

The recipient was a company called **Northbridge Family Services**.

The company had been registered six weeks earlier.

Its declared purpose was temporary family accommodation and child welfare assistance.

Its directors were listed as two legal nominees.

Neither had any known connection to the Valmont or Aurelius families.

Edward opened the company registration.

The registered office was a small legal address in another city.

The company's bank account had received money from Vale Private Counsel, then transferred most of it to a private account.

The account holder's name was hidden behind a trust arrangement.

Jonathan leaned over the desk.

"Can we identify the beneficiary?"

"Not yet."

"Who established the trust?"

Edward looked at the registration date.

"Charles Vale."

Jonathan sighed.

"Of course."

Edward examined the supporting documents.

There were two payment instructions.

The first read:

**For temporary care and accommodation of the child.**

The second:

**For continued silence regarding the identity of the receiving party.**

Jonathan stopped.

"Silence?"

Edward nodded.

"That's what it says."

"Who was being paid?"

"That's what we need to find out."

He turned the page.

The payment was divided into three instalments.

The first had been paid on the twenty-eighth day.

The second on the ninety-second day.

The third exactly one year later.

All three were marked as confidential settlement expenses.

There was no description of the services provided.

Only the same reference number.

**NORTHBRIDGE — CASE 07.**

Edward looked at the number.

"Find the original company directors."

Jonathan made a note.

"And the trust beneficiary?"

"Especially the beneficiary."

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian was reviewing a different set of financial documents.

He had discovered that the confidential settlement account was not the only source of money used during the children's first year.

A second account had been established through an international private bank.

It was registered under the name of a family trust.

The trust had no public beneficiaries.

However, its initial funding had come from a private account associated with the Aurelius Family Office.

Adrian entered his father's study.

"Father, we found another payment source."

Alexander looked up.

"Explain."

"The Northbridge Family Services account received money through Vale Private Counsel. But there was another account established separately."

"Who funded it?"

"An Aurelius private account."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Which one?"

"The account was opened under a legacy trust structure. The original beneficiary information is sealed."

"Did I authorise it?"

"Your name appears on the funding instruction."

Alexander stood.

"Show me."

Adrian placed the document on the desk.

Alexander examined the signature.

It looked like his.

But he did not recognise the transaction.

"I didn't sign this."

Adrian nodded.

"That's what I suspected."

"Who had access?"

"Three people could initiate transfers through this account. You, the former finance director, and the Director of Family Affairs."

"Morelli."

"Yes."

Alexander looked at the date.

The account had been funded eleven days after the births.

The same day the Margaret Ellison account was opened.

"Can we trace the money?"

"Some of it."

"Where did it go?"

Adrian placed another document beside the first.

"Medical expenses, temporary accommodation, and a payment to Northbridge Family Services."

Alexander looked at the company name.

"Find out who received the money."

Adrian nodded.

Alexander remained silent for a moment.

Then he said, "Do not assume that every payment made through my office was authorised by me."

"I understand."

"I want the original instructions, not a reconstructed summary."

Adrian collected the documents.

As he reached the door, Alexander spoke again.

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"If someone used my authority to separate a mother from her child, I want to know exactly how they did it."

Adrian nodded.

"So do I."

The Northbridge company had been dissolved seventeen years earlier.

Its registered directors were difficult to locate.

One had died.

The other was still alive.

Her name was Helen Cartwright.

She lived in a quiet residential neighbourhood and had worked as a legal secretary before retiring.

Edward and Jonathan visited her that afternoon.

Helen was surprised by their questions.

"Northbridge Family Services?" she repeated.

"Yes," Edward said.

"I barely remember that company."

"You were listed as a director."

"Nominal director. I never managed anything."

"Who asked you to serve?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward placed a copy of the company registration on the table.

"Did you know why the company was established?"

Helen studied the document.

"Not really. Mr Vale said it was for a private family matter."

"What kind?"

"Temporary accommodation."

"For whom?"

She shook her head.

"He didn't tell me."

"Did you meet anyone who received services through the company?"

Helen hesitated.

"One woman."

Edward leaned forward.

"Can you describe her?"

"She came to the office twice. She was young, perhaps in her thirties. She carried a baby once."

"Do you remember her name?"

"Victoria."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Victoria what?"

"I don't remember."

"Was she using the name Victoria A.?"

Helen looked surprised.

"Yes. I think so."

"Was she alone?"

"The first time, yes. The second time, a man accompanied her."

"Can you describe him?"

"Older. Well dressed. He did most of the talking."

"Did you recognise him?"

"No."

Edward placed a photograph of Charles Vale on the table.

Helen examined it.

"That might be him."

"You're not certain?"

"It's been many years."

"Did the woman seem comfortable with him?"

Helen considered the question.

"No."

"Why?"

"She barely spoke. When he asked her to sign something, she read it several times."

"What did she sign?"

"I don't know. I wasn't in the room."

Helen looked down at the registration document.

"She asked me something afterward."

"What?"

"Whether the company could guarantee that nobody would know where she lived."

Edward and Jonathan exchanged a glance.

"What did you tell her?"

"I said we could keep the address confidential."

"Did she ask anything else?"

Helen nodded slowly.

"She asked whether the baby's mother knew where the child was."

Edward stopped writing.

"What did you say?"

"I told her that wasn't something I could answer."

Helen looked troubled.

"She seemed upset."

"Did she say why?"

"She said she had been told the mother knew. But she didn't believe it."

Edward closed his notebook.

The woman using the name Victoria A. had been frightened.

She had been carrying a baby.

And she had doubted that the child's mother knew where the baby was.

It was another piece of evidence suggesting that someone had lied to the receiving woman.

At the Valmont Estate, Isabelle was presenting the scholarship programme's revised structure to Catherine.

Richard and Sebastian had joined the meeting.

Elena sat near the window, reviewing a printed copy.

Isabelle finished explaining the independent assessment process.

Catherine nodded.

"This is considerably more detailed than the original proposal."

"I wanted to make sure the selection process could be explained and defended."

Sebastian examined the budget.

"The review panel will require additional funding."

"I've included that."

"How much?"

Isabelle showed him the figures.

He read them carefully.

"Reasonable."

Isabelle looked surprised.

"That's all?"

"What did you expect?"

"At least three objections."

"Don't become accustomed to approval."

Nicholas, who had been sitting quietly, laughed.

Elena turned a page.

"There's one more thing."

Isabelle looked at her.

"What?"

"The programme should publish anonymised annual results."

"Why?"

"So students know what happened to the previous applicants. How many were accepted, how many completed their training, and how many needed additional support."

Catherine smiled.

"That's a good suggestion."

Isabelle wrote it down.

Sebastian closed the budget.

"You've both done useful work."

Nicholas looked at him.

"Was that a compliment?"

"Don't make me repeat it."

The meeting continued for another twenty minutes.

Afterward, Isabelle gathered her papers.

Elena helped her arrange them.

"You're doing well," Elena said.

Isabelle looked up.

"Thank you."

"You seem more comfortable with the foundation work."

"I am."

She paused.

"I used to think that being part of this family would automatically give me a purpose."

Elena listened.

"But it doesn't. I still have to decide what I want to contribute."

Elena smiled.

"That's probably better."

"Why?"

"Because then it's actually yours."

Isabelle considered that.

Then she nodded.

"Yes. I think so."

Edward returned to his office at 7:30 p.m.

Jonathan had received another document from the bank.

The Northbridge trust's beneficiary had finally been identified.

It was not a person.

It was a separate account established for the expenses of a minor child.

The account had been opened on the twenty-eighth day after the births.

The beneficiary designation read:

**CHILD A — IDENTITY UNCONFIRMED.**

Edward frowned.

"Child A?"

Jonathan nodded.

"That's what the bank has."

"Does the account contain a birth date?"

"No."

"Hospital number?"

"No."

"Guardian?"

"None listed."

Edward opened the transaction summary.

The account had received regular payments for the first year.

After that, the payments became irregular.

At the end of the second year, they stopped.

There was one final withdrawal.

A large sum had been transferred to a private account.

The account holder was listed as:

**M. VOSS.**

Edward stared at the screen.

"Margaret Voss."

"Or someone using her identity."

"Can we trace the receiving account?"

"The bank account was closed shortly afterward."

"Where did the money go?"

Jonathan placed another page on the desk.

"A property purchase."

Edward looked at the address.

It was a residential property in a town several hundred kilometres away.

The property had been purchased through a private trust.

Its legal owner was listed as:

**THE CHILD — IDENTITY PENDING.**

Edward read the details.

The purchase had taken place nearly two years after the births.

The property had remained in the trust for fourteen years.

Then it had been sold.

The proceeds were transferred to an account whose beneficiary had not yet been identified.

"Who lived there?" Edward asked.

Jonathan shook his head.

"We don't know."

"Was it occupied?"

"Utility records suggest someone lived there for at least eleven years."

Edward looked at the final page.

The electricity account had been registered under a name.

**ANNA M.**

He went still.

Anna.

The name Margaret Ellison had given to the baby she received.

The name Daniel Mercer remembered from the woman who used Victoria A.

The same name that had appeared in the old rental records.

But there was still no proof that all those references concerned the same child.

Jonathan watched Edward.

"Do you think we found her?"

Edward looked at the property records.

"I think we found a place where someone wanted a child to live without being connected to either family."

"And the child was called Anna."

"Apparently."

He picked up the phone.

"Get the complete occupancy records. I want every registered visitor, utility account, and property inspection."

Jonathan nodded.

Edward looked once more at the beneficiary designation.

**THE CHILD — IDENTITY PENDING.**

For eighteen years, the records had avoided naming the child.

Someone had paid to keep her identity concealed.

Someone had provided accommodation, medical care, and money.

Someone had ensured that she remained outside both families' official records.

But the question was no longer simply who had paid for silence.

It was what they had been trying to keep silent.

At 11:12 p.m., an email arrived from the property archive.

Edward opened the attachment.

It was a scanned inspection report from the residential property.

The inspection had taken place eleven years after the births.

The property was described as clean, maintained, and occupied by one adult and one child.

The adult's name had been withheld.

The child's name was recorded.

**ANNA.**

Under the section titled *Additional Observations*, the inspector had written:

*The child asked whether her mother would be visiting. The occupant requested that this question not be included in future reports.*

Edward read the sentence twice.

Then he opened the final attachment.

It was a photograph taken during the inspection.

A small girl stood near a window, holding a book.

Her face was partially turned away from the camera.

On the reverse of the photograph was a handwritten note.

**She has started asking questions. We cannot keep telling her that nobody is looking for her.**

There was no signature.

Only a date.

And beneath it, a final instruction:

**Find out whether the mother is still alive before telling the child the truth.**
