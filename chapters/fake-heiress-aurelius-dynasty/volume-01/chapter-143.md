# **Chapter 143 — The Original That Was Never Returned**

The first thing Edward discovered about PA-132 was that it had never been registered as a document.

It had been registered as a custody obligation.

That distinction appeared in the archive's index, buried beneath a heading that had not been used in any of the other records.

**PERSONAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT — ORIGINAL CUSTODY**

Reference: PA-132\
Originating matter: AV-18\
Associated procedure: PR-06\
Custody status: Active\
Document status: Not verified\
Receiving custodian: Pending

Edward read the entry again.

“Not verified,” Jonathan said. “That means they didn't establish whether the original acknowledgement was actually inside the package.”

“Then why register it as an original acknowledgement?”

“Because the accompanying paperwork identified it that way.”

Edward turned the page.

There were three separate custody movements.

Day 132, 16:40 — received at V-17.

Day 132, 18:15 — transferred to restricted personal custody.

Day 133, 09:20 — custody confirmed at Meridian.

The final entry had been made by hand.

**Physical package received. Contents not examined.**

Edward looked up.

“Who transferred it?”

“The courier field is blank.”

“Who accepted it at Meridian?”

“Not a person. The receipt carries the institutional seal.”

“Then someone arranged for a package to be transferred without identifying either the person who sent it or the person who delivered it.”

Jonathan nodded.

“And the contents were never examined.”

Edward looked at the final line.

“Find the original receipt.”

Meridian's records director, Mr. Collins, was less pleased to see them than he had been during their previous visits.

He examined the reference before opening the restricted custody register.

“PA-132 is not part of the ordinary medical archive,” he said. “It was placed under a separate personal-document preservation arrangement.”

“Who established that arrangement?”

“The instruction came from V-17.”

“Charles Vale?”

“The record does not identify him as the issuing authority.”

Edward placed the day-132 receipt on the table.

“Your institution accepted this package without examining its contents.”

“We accepted custody of a sealed package.”

“Who delivered it?”

“The courier identity was not recorded in the register.”

“Was that normal?”

“No.”

“Then why accept it?”

Collins adjusted his glasses.

“Because the accompanying instruction expressly limited our responsibility to physical preservation. We were not asked to verify the contents, determine ownership or release the material.”

“Who could authorise access?”

Collins turned to the next page.

“An independently verified personal representation authority, together with the originating representative.”

Edward frowned.

“Was such an authority ever appointed?”

“Not according to the records available to us.”

“Then who could retrieve the package?”

“No one.”

Jonathan looked at him.

“Even the person who deposited it?”

Collins hesitated.

“The depositor did not retain an automatic right of retrieval. The custody instruction was explicit.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Can we see it?”

Collins removed a thin envelope from the register box.

The original instruction was still attached to the receipt.

It read:

**PA-132 — PERSONAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT**

1. The original document shall remain sealed.
2. No copy shall be substituted for the original.
3. The document shall not be attached to the representative's conditional response.
4. Custody shall not be transferred solely on the basis of a proposed personal representation appointment.
5. Release requires independent identification of the principal and verification of the receiving authority.

At the bottom was a handwritten addition.

*The original must remain separate even if the representative's authority is withdrawn.*

Edward read the final sentence twice.

“This was written before or after the withdrawal?”

Collins checked the ink notation.

“The instruction was received on day 132, but the handwritten addition appears to have been made earlier. We cannot establish the exact date.”

“Can you identify the writer?”

“No.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The instruction had anticipated the exact situation that occurred on day 132.

Someone had wanted to ensure that the original acknowledgement could not be transferred merely because the proposed representative's appointment changed.

But why?

And what was so important about keeping the original separate from Gabriel Vale's conditional response?

Dr Mira Sen examined the instruction later that afternoon.

She compared it with the anonymous restrictions from days 48, 118 and 133.

“The handwriting is consistent with the same writer,” she said.

Edward watched her point to the repeated letter formations.

“Can you now attribute it?”

“No. The consistency is stronger, but the available comparison material is still limited.”

She turned to the sentence about the representative's authority.

“This is significant.”

“Because it anticipates withdrawal?”

“Because it separates two independent records. The principal's acknowledgement and the representative's conditional response.”

Edward nodded.

“We already know Gabriel's response was never equivalent to the principal's consent.”

“Yes. But this instruction suggests someone was concerned that the two documents might eventually be merged.”

“Which is what the day-47 summary effectively allowed.”

“Not necessarily merged physically,” Dr Sen clarified. “But merged administratively. One could be used to fill the gap left by the other.”

Edward looked at the paper.

“Could the original acknowledgement contain a refusal?”

“It could.”

“Consent?”

“Possibly.”

“Or a request for more information?”

“Yes.”

“Then why preserve it without examining it?”

Dr Sen looked at him.

“Because its contents may be less important than establishing who was entitled to read them.”

Edward frowned.

“Explain.”

“If a document contains a personal decision, reading it without establishing the identity of the person who made it creates another problem. Even if the document is genuine, you cannot safely attribute its contents to the principal until you know whose document it is.”

Edward glanced at the custody register.

“So the document cannot be used to identify the principal, but the principal must be identified before the document can be examined.”

“That is what the restriction appears to require.”

A silence settled between them.

The process had become circular again.

But this time the circle was different.

The original acknowledgement might contain the very information needed to understand P-04's position.

Yet the investigation could not examine it without first establishing who P-04 was.

Edward gathered the copies.

“Then we need to find out where the original came from.”

Dr Sen nodded.

“And whether it was ever signed.”

The Aurelius family received Edward's report that evening.

Victoria listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she looked at the custody instruction.

“The original acknowledgement was kept separate from Gabriel's response.”

“Yes.”

“Was Gabriel aware of it?”

“He knew a personal acknowledgement was required. He did not know whether the original had been completed or delivered.”

Alexander studied the document.

“Could this have been an acknowledgement from the person who was being considered as the receiving representative?”

“Not according to the section headings,” Edward said. “The original is classified as the principal's personal acknowledgement.”

“Then the principal may have written something.”

“Possibly. We have no proof that the document was ever signed.”

Victoria looked at the blank space where a signature should have been.

“Could it have been returned blank?”

“It could have been.”

“Or never completed.”

“Yes.”

She looked away.

For a moment, the room was silent.

Then Adrian spoke.

“If the document was never signed, the person responsible for the communication process should have recorded that.”

“Unless they never received it,” Damien said.

Edward nodded.

“That's another possibility. The custody record establishes that a package was received. It doesn't establish who created the document or whether it was ever sent to the principal.”

Alexander closed the folder.

“Then we will not treat it as evidence of consent, refusal or even contact.”

Victoria nodded.

“Agreed.”

Lucien, who had been unusually quiet, looked at the day-132 instruction.

“Someone went to considerable trouble to make sure this paper couldn't be used incorrectly.”

“Yes,” Edward replied.

“Could that person have been trying to protect the principal?”

“Possibly.”

“Then perhaps they were the only person who understood what was happening.”

Edward did not answer immediately.

“That is one possibility. Another is that they understood the weaknesses in the process and knew how to use them.”

Damien looked at the handwriting.

“Either way, we need to identify them.”

Alexander turned to Edward.

“Continue with the original custody trail. Do not open anything through an unofficial route.”

Edward nodded.

The family had waited eighteen years.

They would not allow impatience to create another false conclusion.

The following morning, Jonathan found an additional entry in Meridian's financial preservation ledger.

PA-132 had not been stored in the same archive section as the other personal records.

It had been placed in a separate compartment under an account called:

**ORIGINAL PERSONAL DOCUMENTS — NO CONTENTS VERIFICATION**

The preservation costs had been paid by a small administrative trust.

The name of the trust was unfamiliar.

**CROSS PERSONAL RECORDS TRUST.**

Edward stared at the screen.

“Cross?”

“It's connected to Cross Administrative Services,” Jonathan said. “But it was established separately.”

“Who created it?”

“Marianne Cross is listed as the administrative contact. The original settlor is recorded as a protected party.”

“Beneficiary?”

“None.”

Edward looked at the entry.

“Does Marianne know about the package?”

“She might. The trust records say she received an administrative notice on day 133.”

“Did she acknowledge it?”

“Yes. But her acknowledgement is restricted to preservation costs and custody administration.”

Edward leaned back.

“Arrange a meeting.”

Marianne Cross arrived at Meridian shortly after lunch.

She examined the trust reference and seemed unsurprised.

“I wondered when you would find this,” she said.

Edward studied her expression.

“You knew about PA-132?”

“I knew a personal document package had been placed under separate custody. I did not know its contents.”

“Who asked you to establish the preservation arrangement?”

“I didn't establish it.”

“Your trust paid for it.”

“My office administered the account. That is not the same thing.”

“Who was the settlor?”

Marianne folded her hands.

“A protected party.”

“P-04?”

“I cannot confirm that.”

“Do you know who deposited the package?”

“No.”

“Did you see it?”

“No.”

“Then why did you acknowledge the preservation notice?”

“Because the trust was required to maintain the storage account. I was responsible for ensuring that the preservation fees were paid and that the package remained sealed.”

Edward placed the day-132 instruction before her.

“Did you write this?”

Marianne examined the handwriting.

“No.”

“Do you recognise it?”

She remained silent for several seconds.

“I have seen similar wording.”

“Where?”

“In correspondence concerning AV-18.”

“From whom?”

“I cannot identify the writer.”

“Was it Eleanor Vale?”

“I don't know.”

“Charles Vale?”

“No.”

“You sound certain.”

“I worked with both of them. This does not look like either person's handwriting.”

Edward waited.

Marianne returned the paper.

“There is something I can tell you about the trust.”

“What?”

“It was not established to hold a child's identity records.”

“Then what was its purpose?”

“To preserve personal communications that could not safely be placed in the ordinary administrative file.”

“Whose communications?”

“Those belonging to the protected principal.”

Edward looked at her.

“P-04.”

Marianne did not answer.

“Was the trust intended to preserve the principal's original response?”

“It was intended to preserve original personal correspondence, whether or not a response had been completed.”

“Why would that need a separate trust?”

“Because the administrative offices were treating personal communication as part of the placement process.”

“And you believed that was wrong?”

Marianne's expression changed.

“I believed that a person should not lose the right to make a decision simply because other people had failed to complete their paperwork.”

Edward studied her.

It was the clearest statement of her position he had heard.

“Did the principal ever communicate directly with you?”

“No.”

“Did you ever meet them?”

“No.”

“Did you know their identity?”

“I knew the reference P-04. I did not know the identity behind it.”

“Then who did?”

Marianne looked down at the trust record.

“Only the people who were authorised to verify it.”

“Who were they?”

“The originating representative and the independent identity authority.”

“There was no independent identity authority.”

“Exactly.”

Edward waited.

Marianne continued.

“That was why the trust was created. The personal correspondence could not be placed in the ordinary file while the identity remained unresolved. But it could not simply be destroyed or returned to an unverified person either.”

Edward glanced at Jonathan.

The trust had been created to preserve the very thing that the administrative process could not properly handle.

But there was still one question.

“Why was the trust notified on day 133?”

Marianne's expression tightened.

“Because the package was transferred to Meridian.”

“Did the notice include the sender's name?”

“No.”

“Did it include the intended recipient?”

“No.”

“Then what did it say?”

Marianne reached into her folder and removed a copy of the notice.

Edward read it.

**CROSS PERSONAL RECORDS TRUST**

**PRESERVATION NOTICE**

Reference: PA-132\
Document category: Personal acknowledgement\
Custody location: Meridian Records and Preservation Services\
Purpose: Preserve original personal communication pending identity verification\
Release condition: Independently confirmed principal and receiving authority

At the bottom was one final instruction.

*No copy is to be supplied to the originating office while the original remains under restricted custody.*

Edward looked up.

“Why not?”

Marianne shook her head.

“That was not my instruction.”

“Who wrote it?”

“I don't know.”

He returned to the notice.

Someone had not merely wanted the original acknowledgement preserved.

They had wanted to prevent the originating office from receiving a copy.

The implication was troubling.

The originating office was supposed to represent the interests of the family whose maternity record had been verified.

Yet the personal acknowledgement was being withheld even from that office.

Unless the restriction was intended to protect the principal from the originating office itself.

Edward looked at Marianne.

“Did you ever challenge that condition?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Day 134.”

“Where is the objection?”

“I sent it to the preservation administrator. I have a copy.”

She placed a second document on the table.

It was brief.

*Preservation does not justify indefinite non-disclosure. The principal must retain the ability to communicate directly with the authorised originating representative once identity verification is complete.*

Edward read the signature.

**M. CROSS.**

This one was genuine.

He looked up.

“You objected to the restriction.”

“I objected to the possibility that preservation would become permanent withholding.”

“Did anyone respond?”

“No.”

Edward examined the bottom of the page.

A routing reference had been stamped beneath the signature.

**RETURNED TO PERSONAL CUSTODY — DAY 135.**

“Who received it?”

Marianne shook her head.

“The same unidentified office.”

Edward slowly placed the objection beside the preservation notice.

The records were beginning to form a pattern.

Someone had preserved the principal's original communication.

Someone had prevented the originating office from receiving a copy.

Someone had repeatedly blocked the release of the original procedure.

And someone had withdrawn the proposed representative's authority immediately before an attempt to collect the protected documents.

None of those actions established who P-04 was.

But they suggested that the principal's personal correspondence had become the centre of a struggle over who was permitted to speak, who was permitted to receive information, and who could make decisions.

That evening, Edward reviewed the day-134 objection again.

One line had been underlined.

*The principal must retain the ability to communicate directly with the authorised originating representative once identity verification is complete.*

He called Jonathan.

“Check whether anyone ever completed the originating verification after day 134.”

“Across all records?”

“Yes. St Catherine's, Meridian, Northbridge, Vale Private Counsel, the Family Office. Everything.”

“Understood.”

Edward paused.

“And look for any direct correspondence from P-04 after day 134.”

“Even if the identity is masked?”

“Especially then.”

He ended the call.

A few minutes later, a new message arrived.

Jonathan had found a reference in the Meridian correspondence index.

**PA-132 / FOLLOW-UP COMMUNICATION**

Date: Day 136.

Sender: Protected personal correspondence.

Recipient: Restricted.

Status: Received.

Edward sat upright.

There was no description of the contents.

No signature.

No delivery record.

Only a short administrative note.

**Communication received after preservation notice. Do not attach to original acknowledgement until principal identity independently confirmed.**

He read it twice.

A second communication had arrived.

Two days after the original package was preserved.

And someone had again instructed that it must remain separate.

Edward opened the attached scan.

The final line had been typed beneath the status entry.

*The sender has requested confirmation that the original procedure will be delivered before any further response is required.*

Edward stared at the sentence.

For the first time, there was evidence that someone associated with P-04 had asked about the original communication procedure.

They had not necessarily been the principal.

They might have been a representative or an intermediary.

But someone had known the procedure was missing.

And they had asked for it to be delivered.

The response recorded in the system was brief.

**REQUEST NOT COMPLETED — PR-06 REMAINS ACTIVE.**

Edward looked at the date.

Day 136.

The original procedure had been prepared on day 33.

It was now more than one hundred days later.

And the person waiting for it had still not received it.
