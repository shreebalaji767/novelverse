# **Chapter 3 — The Place at the Table**

The first thing Elena noticed the following morning was that the mansion had become unusually quiet.

Not completely quiet, of course.

The Valmont mansion was never truly silent. There were always footsteps in the corridors, the distant hum of the kitchen, gardeners working outside, and Mr. Harris coordinating the household staff with his usual efficiency.

But something was different.

Perhaps it was because everyone seemed to be walking more carefully.

Or perhaps it was because Elena had begun noticing things she had never paid attention to before.

She stood in front of her bedroom mirror, fastening the final button of her cream cardigan.

Her university timetable lay open on the desk. Two lectures, a seminar, and an assignment deadline.

Ordinary things.

She was grateful for ordinary things.

A knock sounded at the door.

"Come in."

Nicholas appeared, holding a paper bag.

"Good morning, abandoned princess."

Elena turned around.

"That's a terrible greeting."

"Good morning, recently demoted princess?"

"Worse."

"Good morning, Elena, who is still legally entitled to steal my breakfast?"

"Acceptable."

Nicholas grinned and raised the paper bag.

"I brought you something."

Elena eyed it suspiciously.

"What did you break?"

"Why does everyone assume I've done something wrong?"

"Experience."

"Unbelievable. I come here with food, and this is how I'm treated."

He placed the bag on her desk.

Inside were two warm croissants and a small container of strawberry jam.

Elena smiled.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

Nicholas leaned against the desk, watching her put the jam into her bag.

"Are you going to university today?"

"Yes. I have a seminar this morning."

"Do you want me to drive you?"

"I can take the car."

"That's what I meant."

"You have work."

"I can rearrange it."

"Nicholas."

"Elena."

She gave him a look.

He sighed dramatically.

"Fine. But message me when you arrive."

"I always do."

"And when you leave."

"I usually do."

"And if anything happens—"

"I'll call you."

"Good."

He nodded as if they had just completed an important business negotiation.

Then his expression softened.

"How did you sleep?"

"Fine."

"Really?"

Elena paused.

"Mostly."

Nicholas didn't press her.

Instead, he picked up one of the croissants and took a bite.

"Then let's make today normal."

Elena looked at him.

"Can we?"

"We can try."

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Nicholas glanced at the clock.

"Come on. Breakfast. Before Sebastian decides we're all late for a board meeting."

"Does he actually do that?"

"He doesn't have to. His face does it for him."

Elena laughed.

It was a small laugh, but Nicholas seemed satisfied.

He held the door open for her.

"After you, Your Highness."

"Stop calling me that."

"Never."

The breakfast room was already occupied.

Richard sat at the head of the table, reading the financial section of a newspaper on his tablet. Catherine was speaking with Mr. Harris about the household schedule.

Sebastian was reviewing documents.

Edward was drinking coffee while scrolling through something on his phone.

And Isabelle was sitting beside Catherine.

She wore a pale blue blouse and had her hair neatly tied back. In front of her was a small plate of fruit and toast.

When Elena entered, Isabelle looked up.

"Good morning."

"Good morning."

Nicholas pulled out Elena's usual chair.

She sat down.

Isabelle glanced briefly at the seating arrangement.

It was a small thing.

The table had always had an established order. Everyone had their own place, even if nobody had officially declared it.

Elena's chair was beside Nicholas.

Isabelle's was beside Catherine.

For now.

"Did you sleep well?" Catherine asked Isabelle.

"Yes, thank you."

"Was the room comfortable?"

"Very much."

"Please tell me if you need anything. You don't have to feel uncomfortable asking."

Isabelle nodded.

"I will."

Elena reached for the fruit bowl.

"Would you like some strawberries?"

Isabelle looked at her.

"Thank you."

Elena passed the bowl.

"There's also mango. The kitchen brought some this morning."

"Thank you."

Nicholas glanced between them.

"Look at that. We're already operating like a perfectly functional household."

Edward lowered his coffee cup.

"We have been operating like a household for years."

"Don't ruin the moment."

"I wasn't aware there was a moment."

"That's because you lack imagination."

Sebastian turned a page.

"Both of you, eat."

Nicholas pointed at him.

"See? Board meeting."

Elena smiled into her glass.

The conversation continued, but there was a noticeable difference from the previous morning.

Isabelle was included in the questions.

Catherine asked about her journey, her previous school, and whether she had received the university information packet.

Richard asked about her academic interests.

Sebastian asked whether she intended to continue her current studies.

Everyone was trying.

Elena could see that.

She also noticed that Isabelle was trying just as hard to answer appropriately.

There were no raised voices. No arguments. No obvious hostility.

Yet Elena felt like someone had quietly moved the furniture in a familiar room.

Everything was still there.

It simply didn't fit the way it used to.

"Elena?"

She looked up.

Richard was watching her.

"Yes?"

"Your university schedule?"

"I have a seminar at ten. I'll be back around four."

"Good."

He returned his attention to the tablet.

Elena waited for something else.

Nothing came.

She lowered her gaze to her plate.

Perhaps that was how things would be now.

Everyone would continue with their lives, making room for Isabelle.

And Elena would have to learn where she fitted.

At 9:40 a.m., Elena left for university.

Nicholas had insisted on walking her to the entrance.

"You have your phone?"

"Yes."

"Wallet?"

"Yes."

"Student identification?"

"Yes."

"Assignment?"

Elena stopped.

Then she looked at him.

Nicholas grinned.

"Forgot something?"

"No."

She opened her bag, took out a folder and waved it at him.

"Here."

"Excellent."

"You sound like a parent."

"Someone has to supervise you."

"I'm eighteen."

"An extremely unreliable eighteen."

She shook her head.

The driver opened the car door.

Before getting inside, Elena turned back.

"Don't forget your meeting."

"I won't."

"And don't skip lunch."

Nicholas blinked.

"You're reminding me?"

"Yes."

"That's usually my job."

"Today it's mine."

He smiled.

"All right."

The car pulled away.

Nicholas remained at the entrance until it disappeared beyond the gates.

Only then did he return inside.

At the university, Elena found her usual seat near the window.

Her friend, Clara, was already there.

Clara was a scholarship student who had known Elena since their first semester. Unlike many of the students who came from influential families, Clara had little interest in the social hierarchy surrounding the university.

She was interested in grades, internships, and getting enough sleep.

In that order.

"You look tired," Clara said.

"Good morning to you too."

"Good morning. You look tired."

"Thank you."

Clara studied her face.

"Did something happen?"

Elena placed her notebook on the desk.

"Something complicated happened at home."

"Complicated how?"

Elena hesitated.

She hadn't told anyone at university yet.

Not because she was ashamed.

She simply didn't know how to explain it.

"My parents found out there was a mistake in the hospital records from when I was born."

Clara waited.

"And?"

"And apparently, I wasn't born to them."

Clara's expression changed.

"What?"

Elena looked down at her notebook.

"There's another girl. Isabelle. She's their biological daughter."

"Wait. Are you saying you were switched at birth?"

"That's what the initial investigation suggests."

Clara stared at her.

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then she leaned closer.

"Are you okay?"

Elena thought about it.

"I don't know yet."

"That's fair."

Clara didn't immediately ask for more details.

She didn't ask whether Elena was rich or poor now, whether she would lose her university place, or whether the other girl was prettier, smarter, or more popular.

She simply sat beside her.

After a moment, Clara pushed a packet of biscuits across the desk.

"Emergency biscuits."

Elena looked at it.

"Why?"

"Because I don't know what else to offer."

Elena laughed quietly.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome. They're not particularly good, but they exist."

"That's enough."

The lecturer entered before they could continue.

Elena opened her notebook.

For the next hour, she concentrated on the lecture.

It helped.

Numbers, theories, diagrams and academic discussions did not care about her family circumstances.

For a little while, she could simply be a university student.

Back at the Valmont mansion, the atmosphere was considerably less ordinary.

Richard had called a private meeting in his study.

Present were Catherine, Sebastian, Edward, and the family's legal adviser, Mr. Laurent.

Nicholas had been invited but was currently dealing with an urgent matter at the company.

Isabelle was not present.

The subject was the family's legal and financial arrangements.

Mr. Laurent opened a folder.

"Before we proceed, I want to clarify that the DNA results establish biological relationships. They do not automatically resolve every legal question regarding guardianship, inheritance, or family assets."

Richard nodded.

"Continue."

"Miss Isabelle is your biological daughter. Miss Elena, however, has been raised by you for eighteen years. Depending on the jurisdiction and the specific documents involved, her legal position may be different from her biological position."

Catherine folded her hands.

"She is still our daughter."

Mr. Laurent glanced at her.

"Emotionally, certainly. Legally, we need to examine the existing documentation."

Sebastian leaned back.

"What exactly needs to be changed?"

"That depends on what the family intends to do."

Richard's expression remained composed.

"Isabelle must be formally acknowledged as our biological daughter. Her identity records will need to be corrected where necessary."

"And Elena?" Edward asked.

Mr. Laurent turned another page.

"We must review her current legal identity and determine whether any records require correction."

Catherine frowned.

"Why should Elena's records change? She has lived under that name her entire life."

"Because the original birth registration may have been inaccurate."

"That doesn't mean we should erase everything she has."

"I did not suggest that, Madam."

Richard raised a hand.

"Enough. We will not make decisions before the investigation is complete."

Sebastian nodded.

"Agreed."

Edward looked at the folder.

"There's another issue."

Richard turned to him.

"What?"

"The hospital's investigation."

Edward placed a printed report on the desk.

"The initial records suggest that the registration was altered after the births were recorded. The hospital has not established who made the changes."

Mr. Laurent examined the report.

"Do they suspect deliberate interference?"

"They haven't made a formal accusation."

"But you do?"

Edward was quiet.

"I think we should find out why the original transfer record is missing."

Richard's eyes narrowed.

"Have they located it?"

"No."

"Then continue the investigation."

Catherine looked between them.

"Could this have been a mistake?"

"It could have," Edward said.

"Then why would someone alter the records?"

Nobody answered immediately.

Sebastian finally spoke.

"Because someone wanted the mistake to remain undiscovered."

The room fell silent.

Richard closed the folder.

"We will establish the facts first. Until then, no speculation leaves this room."

Mr. Laurent nodded.

"Understood."

At lunchtime, Isabelle sat in the smaller dining room with Catherine.

She had initially expected to eat alone.

Instead, Catherine had personally asked her to join her.

"Is the food all right?" Catherine asked.

"Yes. Everything is lovely."

"You don't have to be so formal with me."

Isabelle smiled faintly.

"I'm still getting used to everything."

"I understand."

Catherine watched her for a moment.

Then she reached across the table and adjusted the edge of Isabelle's napkin.

It was an instinctive gesture.

A mother's gesture.

Isabelle went still.

Catherine noticed.

"Sorry. I didn't mean to make you uncomfortable."

"No."

Isabelle looked down.

"It's just..."

She struggled to find the right words.

"For a long time, I imagined what it would be like to meet you."

Catherine's expression softened.

"And now that it's happened?"

"It's different from what I imagined."

"Different in a good way?"

Isabelle considered the question.

"Different in every way."

Catherine nodded slowly.

"That's understandable."

Isabelle hesitated.

"May I ask something?"

"Of course."

"Does Elena usually sit with everyone at breakfast?"

Catherine looked surprised.

"Yes. That's her usual place."

"She seems very close to them."

"She has always been close to her brothers."

Isabelle lowered her eyes.

"I wasn't trying to take her place."

Catherine reached across the table again, this time allowing Isabelle to choose whether to take her hand.

"I know."

"But she must feel strange."

"She does."

"Does she dislike me?"

"No."

"How can you be sure?"

"Because I know Elena."

Catherine paused.

"She has been trying to make you comfortable since the moment you arrived."

Isabelle remembered Elena offering her a drink, asking about her journey, and speaking to her in the library.

She had expected resentment.

Perhaps even anger.

Instead, Elena had treated her like someone who had unexpectedly arrived in the middle of a difficult situation.

It made Isabelle feel guilty.

"I don't want to hurt her," she said.

Catherine's voice was quiet.

"Then don't make her feel that she has to disappear for you to belong here."

Isabelle looked up.

Catherine squeezed her hand.

"There is room for both of you."

At 3:52 p.m., Elena returned to the mansion.

She found Mr. Harris waiting near the entrance.

"Welcome back, Miss Elena."

"Thank you, Mr. Harris."

"Would you like anything prepared for you?"

"Just some tea, please."

"Certainly."

Elena removed her shoes and looked toward the staircase.

"Is everyone home?"

"Mr. Valmont is in his study. Mrs. Valmont is with Miss Isabelle. Mr. Sebastian and Mr. Edward are attending to business matters."

"And Nicholas?"

"Mr. Nicholas has not returned yet."

Elena nodded.

She was about to climb the stairs when Mr. Harris spoke again.

"Miss Elena?"

She turned.

"Yes?"

He seemed to hesitate.

"Your room has been rearranged slightly."

Elena blinked.

"My room?"

"Only some of the furniture. Mrs. Valmont requested that the family albums be moved, and the staff have been asked to prepare additional storage space."

"Oh."

Mr. Harris looked uncomfortable.

"I thought it best to inform you."

"That's all right. Thank you."

She continued upstairs.

The door to her bedroom was open.

At first glance, everything looked familiar.

The curtains were still cream. Her desk remained beside the window. Her bookshelves were untouched.

But the large cabinet that had stood beside the wardrobe was gone.

The family albums were gone too.

In their place stood a smaller cabinet.

Elena entered and placed her bag on the bed.

She walked over to the bookshelf.

The photograph from last year's family holiday was still there.

Richard and Catherine stood in the centre.

Sebastian was looking away from the camera.

Edward was pretending not to smile.

Nicholas had one arm around Elena's shoulders, while she was laughing at something outside the frame.

She picked it up.

For a moment, she considered putting it inside a drawer.

Then she placed it back exactly where it had been.

A knock sounded.

"Come in."

Catherine entered.

"Elena."

"Hi, Mom."

Catherine glanced around.

"Did Mr. Harris tell you about the cabinet?"

"Yes."

"I'm sorry we moved the albums without discussing it with you. We needed to make space for Isabelle's belongings, and I thought we could reorganise things."

"It's okay."

Catherine approached her.

"Is it?"

Elena smiled.

"Really. It's only furniture."

Catherine looked at the photograph on the shelf.

"I didn't want you to feel that your things were being taken away."

Elena lowered her eyes.

"I know."

Catherine reached out and touched her hair.

"We'll make sure you have everything you need."

Elena nodded.

"Okay."

There was a pause.

Then Elena asked, "Is Isabelle settling in?"

"She's trying."

"That's good."

"She asked about you earlier."

Elena looked up.

"Really?"

"She wanted to know whether you were comfortable."

Elena smiled faintly.

"That's kind of her."

Catherine studied her face.

"You don't have to pretend you're not affected by everything."

Elena looked at the photograph again.

"I'm not pretending."

"Elena..."

"I'm just trying not to make things harder."

Catherine's expression changed.

She stepped closer and embraced her.

For a moment, Elena stood still.

Then she wrapped her arms around her mother.

"I love you," Catherine whispered.

"I love you too."

Catherine held on a little longer than usual.

Neither of them mentioned the missing cabinet.

That evening, Nicholas arrived home just before dinner.

He found Elena sitting in the library with a book open on her lap.

"You're hiding."

"I'm reading."

"That's a suspiciously convenient excuse."

She looked up.

"Hello to you too."

Nicholas dropped onto the sofa opposite her.

"How was university?"

"Fine."

"Did you eat?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"Food."

"Elena."

She laughed.

"Sandwich. Fruit. Biscuits."

"Good."

He looked at the book in her hands.

"What are you reading?"

"A book about financial markets."

Nicholas stared at her.

"That's your idea of relaxing?"

"It's interesting."

"You're eighteen."

"And?"

"You should be reading something entertaining."

"This is entertaining."

"Your definition of entertainment concerns me."

She closed the book.

"How was work?"

"Exhausting."

"Did you eat lunch?"

Nicholas paused.

Elena narrowed her eyes.

"Did you?"

"I had a meeting."

"That isn't food."

"I had coffee."

"Coffee isn't food either."

Nicholas leaned back.

"You're becoming very difficult."

"Learned from the best."

He laughed.

Then his gaze drifted toward the hallway.

"Did you see your room?"

"Yes."

"Did they move anything?"

"Just the cabinet and the albums."

Nicholas frowned.

"Why didn't anyone ask you first?"

"It's not a big deal."

"It is if you didn't want it moved."

Elena looked at him.

"Nicholas, Isabelle needs space too."

"That doesn't mean you should lose yours."

"I haven't lost anything."

He looked unconvinced.

Elena picked up her book again.

"Please don't start a fight over a cabinet."

"I'm not starting a fight."

"Your face is."

He rubbed his forehead.

"Apparently my face has become a separate family member."

Elena smiled.

Nicholas watched her for a moment.

Then he spoke more quietly.

"Do you think things will go back to normal?"

Elena considered the question.

"I don't know."

"Neither do I."

He looked down at his hands.

"But I don't want you to think you have to leave."

Elena's fingers tightened slightly around the book.

"I haven't thought about leaving."

"Good."

Nicholas leaned forward.

"Because I would miss having someone to annoy."

She looked at him.

"That's the only reason?"

"Obviously."

"How touching."

"Don't get emotional. It will ruin my reputation."

Elena laughed again.

This time, it came more easily.

Dinner was held in the main dining room.

Everyone was present.

Richard sat at the head of the table. Catherine sat to his right, with Isabelle beside her.

Sebastian and Edward sat opposite.

Nicholas took his usual seat.

Elena sat beside him.

For a few minutes, the conversation was about ordinary matters.

A university event.

A company meeting.

A charity programme Catherine was organising.

Then Richard put down his fork.

"There is something we need to discuss."

The table quietened.

"The hospital investigation is continuing. We have not yet established how the records were altered or who was responsible."

Isabelle looked at him.

"Does that mean there might have been someone involved?"

"It is one possibility," Edward replied.

"Or it may still be an administrative error," Sebastian added.

Richard nodded.

"We will not jump to conclusions."

Catherine looked at Isabelle.

"Regardless of the investigation, we need to make sure you have everything arranged properly."

Isabelle nodded.

"Thank you."

Richard continued.

"There will also be changes to certain family arrangements. We need to discuss education, legal documentation and other matters."

Elena quietly cut a piece of food.

She knew what was coming.

Richard looked at her.

"Elena, this does not mean your education will be interrupted. Your university expenses and existing arrangements will continue."

She looked up.

"Okay. Thank you."

"And we will review the household arrangements as well."

Catherine added quickly, "There is no need for anyone to worry about anything tonight."

Nicholas glanced at Elena.

Sebastian remained silent.

Isabelle looked uncomfortable.

"I don't want anyone to feel that they have to change everything because of me."

Richard regarded her.

"You are our daughter. We have responsibilities toward you."

"I know. But Elena has lived here for eighteen years."

"And she will continue to be treated with respect," Catherine said.

Elena looked at Isabelle.

"It's okay. You don't have to apologise."

Isabelle met her eyes.

"I wasn't apologising."

Elena gave a small smile.

"I know."

The conversation moved on, but the tension remained.

Nobody said anything openly cruel.

Nobody raised their voice.

Yet Elena found herself thinking about the words *family arrangements*.

She wondered what those arrangements would eventually look like.

Later that night, Edward returned to his study.

He had received an encrypted message from the hospital's external investigator.

The message contained only a few lines.

**The secondary registration has been verified.**

**The original transfer record remains missing.**

**We have identified an inconsistency in the identity verification process.**

Edward read the message twice.

Then he opened the attached document.

A scanned copy of an old hospital form appeared on his screen.

The document listed two newborn girls.

One entry had been corrected.

The other had been partially obscured.

Edward enlarged the image.

A handwritten note was visible near the bottom.

It had been written in a different ink from the rest of the document.

He read it carefully.

*Do not release the original copy.*

Edward sat very still.

A moment later, he called the investigator.

The call connected.

"Tell me where you found this."

There was a brief silence on the other end.

"That's the problem, Mr. Valmont."

"What do you mean?"

"The document was not in the hospital's official archive."

Edward's expression hardened.

"Then where was it?"

"It was stored in a separate records facility. Under a different registration number."

Edward looked at the screen again.

"Who had access?"

"We're still checking."

"Do that."

He ended the call.

For several seconds, he stared at the document.

The family had spent years believing Elena's birth records were ordinary.

Now even the hospital's records seemed to have a hidden history.

And somewhere, someone had gone to considerable trouble to make sure the truth stayed buried.

In another part of the mansion, Elena was preparing for bed.

She had finished her assignment, arranged her books for the next day, and placed her phone on the bedside table.

A message appeared.

**Nicholas:** Still awake?

**Elena:** Yes.

**Nicholas:** Good. I have an important question.

**Elena:** What?

**Nicholas:** Are you planning to steal my chocolate tomorrow?

Elena smiled.

**Elena:** Depends. Are you planning to hide it?

**Nicholas:** Obviously.

**Elena:** Then obviously.

**Nicholas:** Criminal behaviour.

**Elena:** Good night.

**Nicholas:** Good night, little sister.

Elena looked at the final message for a long moment.

Then she put her phone down.

Outside, the gardens were dark and quiet.

The Valmont mansion still looked exactly as it had yesterday.

The same windows.

The same lights.

The same familiar corridors.

But Elena was beginning to understand that a home could change without anyone moving its walls.

Sometimes it changed through small decisions.

A cabinet moved.

A photograph relocated.

A new name added to the family arrangements.

A chair that might eventually be occupied by someone else.

She turned off the bedside lamp.

Tomorrow, she would go to university again.

She would attend her lectures, finish her assignment, and probably argue with Nicholas about his chocolate.

For now, that was enough.

She closed her eyes.

And somewhere beyond the familiar gates of the Valmont estate, the first pieces of a much older mystery were beginning to surface.
