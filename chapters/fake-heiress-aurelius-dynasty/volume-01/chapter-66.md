# **Chapter 66 — The Name That Was Used Without Permission**

Edward did not call Catherine immediately.

He read the handwritten instruction several times.

**THE CHILD MUST NOT BE DELIVERED TO CATHERINE VALMONT.**

**THE NAME WAS USED WITHOUT HER KNOWLEDGE.**

The placement request had been submitted under Catherine's name, but the same file explicitly prohibited the residential home from delivering the child to her.

It was an extraordinary contradiction.

Someone had used Catherine's identity to arrange a placement while simultaneously ensuring that she would never receive the child.

Jonathan examined the scanned document.

"At least this confirms Catherine wasn't the receiving guardian."

"It confirms she wasn't authorised to collect the child."

"Isn't that the same thing?"

"No."

Edward pointed to the first sentence.

"The person who submitted the request may have intended someone else to collect the infant. Or the name may have been used to satisfy an administrative requirement."

Jonathan looked at the second sentence.

"And someone wanted to make certain Catherine didn't discover it."

Edward nodded.

"Which means we need the original application."

The residential home had been called Greenfield Children's House.

It had closed twelve years earlier, and its records had been transferred to a regional child welfare archive.

Edward and Jonathan visited the archive the following morning.

The administrator, Mr. Howard, examined their documents.

"Child 28-B," he repeated.

"Yes," Edward said.

"That is an unusual reference."

"Why?"

"Most children admitted here were assigned a standard registration number. This one was handled under a confidential arrangement."

"Who authorised that?"

Howard checked the index.

"Northbridge Family Services."

Edward placed a copy of the placement request on the desk.

"Was the child physically admitted?"

"According to the register, yes."

"Who brought her?"

"The record doesn't specify."

"Who collected her?"

Howard searched another database.

"There is a transfer record."

Edward leaned forward.

"To whom?"

Howard read the entry.

"Receiving representative: M. Voss."

Jonathan looked at Edward.

"Margaret again."

"Was the transfer completed?"

Howard continued reading.

"There's a signature."

"Whose?"

"Margaret Voss."

Edward looked at the date.

It was three months after the child's admission.

"Can we see the original?"

Howard nodded.

"I'll request the physical file."

The original file arrived in a sealed archival folder.

It was thinner than Edward expected.

The first page contained the admission details.

**CHILD 28-B**

**SEX: FEMALE**

**ADMISSION AGE: 28 DAYS**

**NAME AT ADMISSION: UNREGISTERED**

**TEMPORARY NAME: ANNA**

The next page contained the placement request.

Catherine Valmont's name appeared as the proposed family contact.

But the signature was not hers.

Edward recognised the handwriting from the earlier ninth-day amendment.

It belonged to Isabella Vale.

Jonathan looked at him.

"Isabella submitted the request?"

"Apparently."

"Why would she use Catherine's name?"

Edward continued reading.

The form included a handwritten explanation:

*The proposed family contact is not to be informed of the child's admission or identity. The name is supplied only for administrative reference.*

Beneath it was another note.

*The child is not to be released to the named family contact under any circumstances.*

Edward turned the page.

There was a separate statement signed by Isabella.

*I request temporary protection for the infant until the original maternal identity can be verified. I do not claim legal or biological guardianship. I do not authorise permanent placement.*

Edward read the statement again.

"She didn't ask to take the child."

Jonathan looked at the document.

"She arranged for the child to be protected."

"Under Catherine's name."

"Why?"

Edward continued examining the file.

The next page contained an explanation.

*The named family contact has no knowledge of this arrangement. Her identity is being used to prevent the receiving party from being traced through the original request.*

Jonathan frowned.

"That's not an explanation. That's another layer of concealment."

Edward agreed.

He looked at the final page.

A handwritten instruction had been added after admission.

**THE CHILD MUST NOT REMAIN AT GREENFIELD AFTER THE THREE-MONTH REVIEW.**

**TRANSFER TO THE PERSON IDENTIFIED IN THE SEALED SCHEDULE.**

The schedule was missing.

At the Valmont Estate, Catherine was reviewing the foundation's latest scholarship applications when Edward's call came.

"Edward?"

"I need to ask you about something from eighteen years ago."

She put down her pen.

"What happened?"

"Do you remember any contact with Greenfield Children's House?"

"No."

"Did you ever arrange temporary accommodation for a newborn child?"

Catherine's expression changed.

"No. Why?"

"Did you authorise Isabella Vale to use your name on a child welfare application?"

Catherine stood.

"Absolutely not."

Edward waited.

"Is this about the hospital records?"

"Yes."

"Tell me what you've found."

Edward explained the placement request, the use of her name, and the instruction preventing the child from being delivered to her.

Catherine listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she said, "I never knew about this."

"I understand."

"Why would Isabella use my name?"

"We don't know yet."

"Was the child one of the babies from St Catherine's?"

"We cannot establish that."

Catherine sat down slowly.

"Another child?"

"Possibly."

She looked at the papers on her desk.

For eighteen years, she had believed that the mystery concerned two infants and two families.

Now an additional maternity record had appeared, followed by a separate residential placement.

"Edward, I need you to tell me something."

"Yes?"

"Did Isabella ever believe that the child was hers?"

"She believed that the infant in the photograph might be her daughter. But the comparison report established that the photograph and her maternity bracelet belonged to different records."

Catherine closed her eyes briefly.

"Then she may have been trying to protect a child she couldn't identify."

"That is one possibility."

"Did she ever say why she used my name?"

"Not yet."

Catherine looked towards the doorway.

Elena and Isabelle were walking through the corridor.

She watched them for a moment.

Then she turned back to the telephone.

"Please don't involve the girls in this until we know what happened."

"I won't."

"And Edward?"

"Yes?"

"If my name was used to hide a child, I want the truth. But I don't want you to assume I was involved simply because my name appears on a document."

"I won't."

Catherine ended the call.

She remained seated for several minutes.

Then she returned to her scholarship applications.

The work was ordinary.

The questions were practical.

And for the moment, she needed something ordinary.

Isabella agreed to meet Edward again.

This time, she brought another folder.

"I've been thinking about what you showed me," she said.

Edward invited her to sit.

"Do you remember submitting a placement request to Greenfield Children's House?"

Isabella looked at him.

"Greenfield?"

"Yes."

She opened her folder.

"I remember the name."

"Why did you use Catherine Valmont's identity?"

Isabella looked surprised.

"I didn't."

Edward placed the scanned application before her.

She examined the signature.

Then her expression changed.

"That's my handwriting."

"Yes."

"But I never submitted this."

"Did you sign any similar document?"

Isabella read the statement.

"I signed something almost identical."

"When?"

"After Eleanor showed me the photograph."

"What did you ask for?"

"Temporary protection for a child whose identity was uncertain."

"Which child?"

"I wasn't told."

Edward leaned forward.

"Where did you submit the request?"

"At Vale Private Counsel."

"Who received it?"

"Charles."

"Did you name Catherine Valmont?"

"No."

"Did you provide any family contact?"

"No."

Isabella looked at the form again.

"This is not the document I signed."

Edward pointed to the handwritten statement.

"Most of the wording is identical."

"Not the final section."

She traced the last paragraph with her finger.

"I specifically wrote that the child should remain under independent medical supervision. I did not ask for a residential placement."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Can you identify the difference?"

Jonathan compared the two versions.

"The original statement mentions medical supervision. This one says temporary accommodation."

Isabella nodded.

"That's right."

"Did you keep a copy?"

"No. Charles told me the original would be retained by the legal office."

Edward placed another document beside it.

The placement request bearing Catherine's name.

Isabella looked at the signature.

"Who wrote this?"

"We're trying to establish that."

She studied the form.

Then she noticed something.

"This isn't my signature."

Edward looked up.

"You just said the handwriting was yours."

"The handwriting in the statement is mine. But the signature at the bottom isn't."

Edward compared the two.

The signature appeared to have been copied from Isabella's earlier correspondence.

"Someone copied your signature too."

Isabella looked at the document.

"Then someone used my request to arrange something I never authorised."

"Yes."

She slowly closed the folder.

"Who was the child?"

"We don't know."

Isabella looked at him.

"Was she alive?"

"The residential home records confirm that a female infant was admitted."

"How old?"

"Twenty-eight days."

Isabella's eyes filled, but she did not cry.

"Then someone had my daughter for twenty-eight days, or someone had another child and used my name to move her."

Edward did not offer an answer.

He could not.

The transfer record was examined more closely that afternoon.

Margaret Voss had signed for Child 28-B three months after admission.

But the receiving address was not her home.

It was a medical facility.

**MERIDIAN CHILDREN'S MEDICAL CENTRE — PRIVATE OBSERVATION UNIT.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Back to Meridian."

Jonathan nodded.

The same facility had received the infant associated with Isabella's bracelet on the sixth day.

Now a child registered as Anna had been transferred there months later.

The records were beginning to overlap again.

But the dates and identification numbers remained separate.

Edward called the former Meridian administrator.

He requested the observation unit records and the original transfer receipt.

The response arrived late in the evening.

There were two entries.

The first concerned the infant admitted on the sixth day under reference IV-204.

The second concerned Child 28-B, transferred from Greenfield on the ninety-second day.

They were separate patients.

The first infant had been discharged on the twenty-eighth day.

The second had been admitted on the ninety-second day.

Both records contained the same instruction:

**MATERNAL IDENTITY NOT TO BE DISCLOSED.**

Edward examined the discharge details for the first infant.

There was a receiving signature.

**E. VALE.**

He examined the admission details for the second.

The receiving signature was different.

**M. VOSS.**

Two children.

Two separate transfers.

Two different receiving representatives.

And both had been connected to the same network of concealed maternal identities.

At 11:42 p.m., Jonathan called Edward.

"I found the original Greenfield schedule."

"Where?"

"In the Northbridge financial archive."

"What's on it?"

"Three names."

Edward opened the file.

The first entry read:

**CHILD 28-B — TEMPORARY RECEIVING REPRESENTATIVE: MARGARET VOSS.**

The second entry read:

**CHILD IV-204 — MEDICAL CUSTODIAN: ELEANOR VALE.**

Edward stared at the screen.

"That explains the two separate transfers."

"Not entirely," Jonathan replied.

"What else?"

"The third entry."

Edward scrolled down.

It was a separate child.

**CHILD 28-C — FEMALE — TEMPORARY RECEIVING REPRESENTATIVE: ISABELLA VALE.**

Edward stopped.

"Isabella?"

"Yes."

"Was she authorised to receive a child?"

"The schedule says temporary representative only."

Edward examined the date.

It was the same day Child 28-B had entered Greenfield.

The same day Isabella's name had been used on the placement request.

And beneath the entry was a handwritten instruction.

*The representative must not be told whether the child is hers.*

Edward slowly lowered his hand.

Isabella had been given responsibility for a child.

But she had not been told whether that child was her own daughter.

The arrangement had not simply concealed a child's identity from a family.

It had deliberately separated a mother from the knowledge of whether the child placed in her care was biologically hers.

Edward looked at the three entries again.

Child 28-B.

Child IV-204.

Child 28-C.

Three separate references.

Three different arrangements.

And one final question remained.

Who had decided that Isabella Vale should receive a child without being told whether she was receiving her own?

He looked at the signature beneath the schedule.

It was not Charles Vale's.

It was not Eleanor's.

It was not Margaret's.

The signature belonged to someone whose name had appeared only once before in the entire investigation.

**ALEXANDER AURELIUS.**
