# **Chapter 74 — The Evidence Kept by the Aurelius Family**

Adrian Holt had retired eleven years earlier.

That was the first thing Edward discovered.

The second was that nobody at the Aurelius Family Office seemed willing to discuss his retirement.

"He was our legal and administrative director during the period you're investigating," the current records secretary explained. "He left the organisation after completing his service."

"Did he retain any family-office documents?"

"Not officially."

"Did he transfer all his files before leaving?"

"According to the handover record, yes."

Edward placed the Westbridge receipt on the desk.

"Then why does this document say he personally collected an original infant identification band and retained it under sealed family-office evidence custody?"

The secretary examined the page.

Her expression changed.

"I haven't seen this before."

"Can you check the evidence register?"

"I'll need authorisation."

"From whom?"

"The current family-office director."

Edward nodded.

"Please obtain it."

The secretary picked up the telephone.

Edward waited.

He had learned not to interpret hesitation as guilt. The Aurelius organisation was enormous. Even a senior administrator could be unaware of documents kept by a previous generation of executives.

But the receipt was difficult to dismiss.

Adrian Holt had personally signed for the original band.

The document identified its purpose as independent identity verification.

And the band had never been returned to Westbridge.

The family-office director, Helena Brooks, arrived twenty minutes later.

She was in her early fifties, composed and efficient. She read the receipt twice before looking at Edward.

"This is an authentic Holt signature."

"Can you verify the collection?"

"I can verify the signature against his personnel records. The collection itself requires a separate archive search."

"Was this part of the Aurelius missing-child investigation?"

Helena looked at him.

"I cannot answer that yet."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't know which investigation this document belonged to."

Edward frowned.

"There was more than one?"

Helena folded her hands.

"During the first year after the children disappeared, the family office received information from several hospitals, private medical organisations, and external legal representatives. Some concerned possible sightings. Others concerned unidentified children. We opened separate case files."

"How many?"

"I don't know without checking."

"Was A-17 one of them?"

Helena looked down at the receipt.

"That reference is familiar."

Edward leaned forward.

"From where?"

"An old evidence index."

"Can you retrieve it?"

"Yes, if it still exists."

She made a note.

"However, I need to warn you that some of those files contain confidential family information."

"I understand."

"And the family will need to authorise access."

Edward knew what that meant.

Alexander Aurelius would have to be involved.

Alexander received the request that evening.

He was in his office when Helena delivered the receipt.

Victoria sat nearby, reviewing documents from the Aurelius Foundation. Adrian had joined them after finishing a meeting at the headquarters.

Alexander read the receipt without speaking.

Then he looked at Helena.

"Adrian Holt collected this?"

"According to the document, yes."

"Did he report it to me?"

"I haven't found a report."

"Did he report it to Victoria?"

Helena shook her head.

Victoria put down her pen.

"I don't remember receiving anything about an identification band."

Adrian stepped closer.

"Was this during my father's investigation?"

"It appears so," Alexander replied.

He passed the receipt to his eldest son.

Adrian read it carefully.

"Day 116," he said. "That was approximately three months after the children disappeared."

"Yes."

"Why would Holt have collected evidence from Westbridge without reporting it?"

Alexander looked at Helena.

"Find the evidence register. Find his handover documents. Find every reference connected to A-17."

Helena nodded.

Alexander added, "And do not contact the former employees yet."

"Understood."

When she left, Victoria looked at her husband.

"Do you think he found something?"

"I don't know."

"Could it have been our daughter's band?"

Alexander remained silent.

Adrian placed the receipt on the desk.

"We cannot assume that."

Victoria nodded.

"I know."

Her voice was quiet, but she did not look away from the paper.

For eighteen years, she had lived with the possibility that a document, a witness, or a hospital record might finally bring them closer to their missing daughter.

Every new clue brought hope.

Every contradiction threatened to take it away.

Alexander reached for her hand.

"We will find out what it is before we decide what it means."

Victoria nodded again.

"Yes."

The archive search took two days.

On the third morning, Helena returned with a thin folder.

It was labelled:

**A-17 — Protected Evidence Custody.**

Alexander opened it.

The first page was an index.

It listed four items.

1. Original infant identification band, collected from Westbridge on day 116.
2. Photographic copy of Meridian transfer documentation.
3. Preliminary comparison request.
4. External correspondence concerning disputed maternal identification.

The second page contained an inventory of the original band.

**Temporary reference at receiving facility: M-204.**

**Previous identification reference: IV-204 — unverified.**

**Material: Plastic hospital identification band.**

**Condition: Intact, minor surface damage.**

**Inscription: Partially legible.**

Victoria leaned forward.

"IV-204?"

Adrian looked at Alexander.

"That is Isabella Vale's maternity identification reference."

"Yes," Alexander replied.

Helena continued.

"The band was collected because Westbridge had recorded a discrepancy between the band and the accompanying transfer documentation."

"Was it examined?" Edward asked.

He had been invited to the meeting after Alexander authorised his access.

Helena turned the page.

"Yes. The preliminary report is here."

Edward read it.

The examination had been conducted by an independent document and identification specialist.

The report confirmed that the band had originally been issued by the private maternity clinic where Isabella Vale delivered her daughter.

The printed reference was IV-204.

The band had not been manufactured or altered at Westbridge.

It was genuine.

Edward felt a new piece of the puzzle settle into place.

"So M-204 was the infant associated with Isabella's original maternity record."

"Not quite," Helena said.

He looked up.

"The report confirms that the band originated from Isabella's maternity clinic. It does not establish that the band remained with the same infant throughout every transfer."

"Was there evidence of substitution?"

"Yes."

She pointed to the next paragraph.

**The band appears to have been removed and reattached or transferred at an unknown point before arrival at Westbridge. The physical condition of the fastening does not permit a reliable conclusion as to when this occurred.**

Edward read it again.

The band was genuine.

But its presence on the child at Westbridge did not prove that the child had been wearing it continuously since birth.

Someone might have moved it.

Someone might have attached it to another infant.

Or the band might have remained with Isabella's daughter until the transfer.

The examination could not distinguish between those possibilities.

"Was DNA testing performed?" Edward asked.

"No."

"Why?"

Helena turned to the final page.

"The band had no biological material suitable for reliable testing. The examination was limited to the physical item and its manufacturing details."

Victoria looked disappointed.

"But it still proves the band came from Isabella's clinic."

"Yes," Edward replied. "It proves the band came from there. It doesn't prove who wore it at every stage."

Alexander closed the report.

"Was there a recommendation?"

Helena nodded.

"The examiner recommended comparing the original maternity register, the infant discharge record, and the receiving documentation from Meridian."

Edward looked at the index.

"Where are those documents?"

"That is the problem."

Helena placed another sheet on the desk.

"The original band was collected, but the supporting comparison materials were never added to the A-17 file."

"Who was responsible?"

"Adrian Holt."

The room fell silent.

Adrian Holt lived in a small house outside the city.

He was seventy-one, retired, and no longer involved in the Aurelius organisation.

When Alexander's office contacted him, he agreed to meet them.

He arrived at the Aurelius estate the following afternoon.

Victoria recognised him immediately.

"You were there when we first began searching," she said.

Holt inclined his head.

"Yes, Mrs. Aurelius."

"Why didn't you tell us about the band?"

Holt looked at the receipt in Alexander's hand.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he removed his glasses.

"I should have."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"That is not an explanation."

"No."

"Then give me one."

Holt sat down.

"The band was collected as part of an independent identity review. I believed it might establish which infant had been transferred from Isabella Vale's maternity clinic."

"Why was that information not reported?"

"Because the review was incomplete."

"That does not explain why the evidence was kept."

Holt looked at Alexander.

"I was instructed to preserve it."

"By whom?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward immediately looked up.

"Charles instructed you to collect evidence from Westbridge?"

"Not directly. He requested that the item be transferred to the family office for independent examination."

"Why would you accept an instruction from Charles Vale?"

"Because the request came through the external administrative channel authorised for the original emergency arrangements."

"Those arrangements were never authorised by Alexander or Victoria."

Holt lowered his eyes.

"I understand that now."

Alexander's voice remained controlled.

"You knew then that Victoria had not authorised the placement."

"I knew there was a dispute about the authorisation."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

Holt took a slow breath.

"At the time, I believed we were dealing with a complicated but legitimate child-protection arrangement. I did not know that the documents had been manipulated."

Edward placed the preliminary report before him.

"Did you ever tell Charles that the band was genuine?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"He asked whether it proved that M-204 was Isabella's daughter."

"And your answer?"

"I told him it did not."

"Did he accept that?"

Holt hesitated.

"No."

"What did he say?"

"He said the band was enough to establish the child's originating identity."

Edward leaned forward.

"But it wasn't."

"No."

"Did you explain that to him?"

"Several times."

"Did you explain it to Alexander?"

Holt looked at the chairman.

"No."

Alexander's expression did not change.

"Why?"

"Because I believed the review would be completed within days. I intended to submit the evidence with the final report."

"Where is the final report?"

Holt looked uncomfortable.

"There wasn't one."

"Why?"

"The comparison materials never arrived."

"Did you request them?"

"Yes."

"From whom?"

"Meridian, Westbridge, and Vale Private Counsel."

"Did you receive any response?"

"From Meridian, yes. They said the original maternity identification sheet had been transferred to their medical records department."

Edward immediately asked, "Did you obtain it?"

"No. The request required the mother's written consent or a court order."

"Was Isabella contacted?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Holt looked down.

"Charles said the matter should not be brought to her attention until the family identities had been reconciled."

Edward felt his patience thinning.

"Charles Vale was not Isabella's guardian."

"I know."

"Nor was he authorised to decide whether she could access her own maternity records."

"I know that now."

Victoria's expression had become cold.

"Did you ever consider telling us?"

Holt turned toward her.

"Yes."

"But you didn't."

"No."

"Why?"

He struggled to answer.

"Because I believed that if I released incomplete information, it might cause another mistake. I had already seen records in which one child's identity was being attributed to another."

Edward watched him.

That was plausible.

It was also a convenient explanation.

"Did you ever inspect the band yourself?" he asked.

"Yes."

"Was there anything unusual?"

Holt nodded.

"The inside of the band had a handwritten mark."

"What mark?"

"Two letters."

Edward waited.

"V.A."

Victoria went very still.

Alexander looked at him sharply.

"What did you just say?"

"V.A. The initials were written on the inside."

"Why weren't they included in the report?"

"They were."

Holt removed a folded paper from his jacket.

It was a copy of the examiner's handwritten observations.

The report described the letters as faint, partially obscured, and possibly added after the band had been manufactured.

**V.A.**

Edward stared at them.

Victoria Aurelius.

But initials were not proof.

They could have been written by anyone.

Alexander took the paper.

"Did you investigate them?"

"I asked the examiner to determine whether they had been added before or after the band was fastened."

"And?"

"He could not establish that."

"Did you compare them with Victoria's handwriting?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Holt looked at Victoria.

"I did not want to suggest that the band belonged to your daughter without evidence."

Victoria's fingers tightened around the edge of the table.

"But you also didn't tell me that it might."

Holt lowered his head.

"I am sorry."

After Holt left, the family remained in the study.

Edward placed the report beside the original inventory.

"The band is definitely from Isabella's maternity clinic," he said. "It may have belonged to her daughter. But we cannot prove that it stayed with the same infant."

Alexander nodded.

"And the initials?"

"Not reliable enough to identify the child."

Victoria looked at the paper.

"V.A."

Adrian stood near the window.

"Someone may have written those initials after the band was removed."

"Or before," Edward replied.

Lucien, who had joined the meeting halfway through, leaned against the bookcase.

"Could they be the initials of the person who handled the band?"

"Possibly."

"Or a reference to Victoria Aurelius?"

"Possibly."

Julian sat quietly in an armchair.

He had been unusually silent throughout the discussion.

Finally, he asked, "Does this mean the child with the band might not be our sister?"

Victoria looked at him.

"It means we don't know yet."

Julian lowered his eyes.

"I thought we were getting closer."

"We are," Alexander said.

"But every time we find something, it turns out to mean something different."

Alexander considered his youngest son.

"That is because we are investigating what happened, not choosing the answer we want."

Julian nodded slowly.

Then he looked at Edward.

"Can you find out who wrote the initials?"

Edward gave him a small, reassuring nod.

"We'll try."

That evening, Edward returned to his office.

He had requested the original Westbridge evidence envelope, but Helena had informed him that it was no longer stored with the A-17 file.

Adrian Holt had retained it separately.

When he left the Aurelius organisation, the evidence had been transferred to a private archival storage company.

Holt had provided the storage receipt.

Edward read it.

The band was still listed as being held in a sealed container.

But the receipt contained another detail.

**Additional contents: One original hospital maternity identification sheet.**

Edward sat upright.

He read the line again.

The original maternity identification sheet.

Not a photocopy.

Not a summary.

The document that could potentially connect IV-204 to Isabella's original maternity admission.

He immediately called Helena.

"Did Holt tell you about an additional document?"

"No."

"His storage receipt lists an original maternity identification sheet."

There was a pause.

"Are you certain?"

"I'm looking at it."

"Then we need to retrieve the container."

"Arrange it."

"I'll contact the storage company."

Edward ended the call.

For several minutes, he stared at the receipt.

The band had been preserved.

The original maternity sheet had been preserved.

But the two documents had been kept outside the official Aurelius evidence archive.

Someone had deliberately separated them from the rest of the investigation.

Why?

He opened his notebook and wrote:

**A-17 — Original band IV-204.**

**Original maternity identification sheet — location confirmed.**

Then he added:

**Determine whether the sheet identifies the mother, the infant, or both.**

His telephone rang.

It was Jonathan.

"I found something else," Jonathan said.

"What?"

"Westbridge's old correspondence register has a reference to the day 116 collection."

"Go on."

"The woman who brought the original band to Adrian Holt wasn't an unknown representative."

Edward sat forward.

"Who was she?"

"Her name was Eleanor Vale."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

Eleanor had been involved in the original transfer records. She had served as medical-record custodian for IV-204. She had also handled correspondence concerning the unidentified infant.

But the register contained another detail.

Jonathan continued.

"She didn't bring the band alone. The visitor register lists someone accompanying her."

"Who?"

There was a pause.

"Isabella Vale."

Edward stared at the wall.

Isabella had visited Meridian on day 93, worried that her daughter might have been given to the Aurelius family.

Now, according to Westbridge's visitor register, she had accompanied Eleanor when the original band was collected.

"Was she told what the band was?"

"There's no record of that."

"Did she sign anything?"

"No. But there's a handwritten note beside her name."

"What does it say?"

Jonathan read it slowly.

**Mother permitted to view identification material. No infant presented.**

Edward looked down at his notebook.

Isabella had been allowed to see the band.

Not her daughter.

Not a photograph.

Not a medical record.

Only the identification band.

And someone had decided that this was enough to bring her to Westbridge.

"Jonathan," Edward said quietly, "find out exactly what Isabella was shown that day."

"Do you think she recognised the band?"

"I think we need to ask her."

He ended the call.

The original band might finally lead them back to Isabella's missing daughter.

But the more Edward examined the records, the more he suspected that someone had been carefully controlling what each person was allowed to see.

Margaret had been given an administrative explanation.

Marianne had been given an instruction.

Gabriel had been given a medical boundary.

Adrian Holt had been given an evidence item without the supporting documents.

And Isabella had been shown a band without being allowed to see the child who might have worn it.

Eighteen years earlier, someone had separated the children from their identities.

Now Edward had to discover who had separated the evidence from the truth.
