# **Chapter 95 — The Signature That Was Never Meant to Decide**

Edward did not show the document to Richard immediately.

He spent the first hour examining its physical properties.

The paper was consistent with the type used by Vale Private Counsel during that period. The signature appeared genuine. The date matched the surviving correspondence. Even the handwritten restriction beneath the approval appeared consistent with Richard's known writing.

But authenticity was not the same as authority.

A genuine signature could be placed beneath a misleading document.

A valid instruction could be copied into an invalid schedule.

And a person could approve one thing without knowing that someone else intended to use that approval for another.

Edward had learned not to confuse those possibilities.

He requested the original document from the trustee and arranged for an independent document examiner to inspect it.

Then he called his father.

"I need to speak with you about something from the old hospital investigation."

Richard's response was immediate.

"Come to my office."

Richard was reviewing a quarterly financial report when Edward arrived.

He closed it and looked at his son.

"What's happened?"

Edward placed the document on the desk.

Richard read the heading first.

Then the date.

Finally, his eyes reached the signature.

He did not react immediately.

But Edward noticed his hand tighten around the edge of the paper.

"Where did you find this?"

"In the R-04 correspondence archive."

Richard read the document again.

"Day fifty-six."

"Yes."

"Who gave it to you?"

"The trustee."

Richard looked up.

"Did you verify the signature?"

"An independent examiner is reviewing it."

Richard pushed the document away.

"I signed something like this."

Edward remained still.

"You remember it?"

"I remember approving an administrative continuity instruction. I do not remember approving a family allocation."

"Did you sign this exact page?"

"I don't know."

"Did you see Schedule 4?"

Richard leaned back.

"No."

"Did you know it contained proposed receiving identities?"

"No."

Edward watched him carefully.

"Then what did you approve?"

Richard rubbed his forehead.

"A request to preserve the existing temporary care arrangements without treating them as permanent placements."

"Who submitted it?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Did he bring it to you personally?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"Somewhere around that period. I cannot remember the exact day."

"What did he say?"

Richard considered the question.

"He said the hospitals were struggling to maintain consistent records because the children were being moved between temporary medical and residential facilities. He wanted permission to preserve the administrative references while the medical records were reconciled."

"Did he mention the Aurelius Family Office?"

"No."

"Did he mention Victoria Aurelius?"

"No."

"Did he mention a proposed permanent receiving family?"

"No."

Edward looked at the document.

"Then why is your signature on a revision to the family allocation?"

Richard's expression hardened.

"That is precisely what I want to know."

Edward placed a second sheet beside the first.

It was the original request submitted by Charles.

The wording was different.

The heading read:

**REQUEST FOR CONTINUITY OF TEMPORARY CARE RECORDS.**

The document explained that several children were being managed under provisional references and that changes in temporary custody needed to be recorded consistently.

It requested permission to maintain those references until the originating medical records could be independently reconciled.

There was no mention of permanent family allocation.

There was no mention of biological parentage.

There was no request to assign a child to the Valmont or Aurelius family.

Richard read it.

"This is what I remember."

Edward placed the two documents side by side.

"Your signature appears on the amendment, not the original request."

Richard examined the signature again.

"Then someone attached my approval to a different document."

"Possibly."

"Did Charles prepare this?"

"The revision was circulated through Vale Private Counsel."

Richard looked increasingly angry.

"Then ask him."

"Charles is dead."

The room fell silent.

Richard looked away.

For a moment, Edward wondered whether his father had forgotten that fact.

Then Richard spoke.

"Yes. I know."

Richard stood and walked to the window.

"I was told that the children were in temporary care. I was told that the hospitals needed consistent administrative records. I was not told that anyone intended to assign them permanently."

"Did you ask for proof that the children were safe?"

"Yes."

"What were you shown?"

"Summaries. Not the complete medical files."

"Did you see any infant identification cards?"

"No."

"Did you meet any receiving guardians?"

"No."

"Did you know that the recipient schedule was being prepared?"

Richard shook his head.

"I knew there was an administrative schedule. I did not know it contained proposed family identities."

Edward let the silence continue.

Richard returned to his desk.

"Do you think I authorised the placement of a child?"

"I don't know."

Richard looked directly at him.

"Then find out."

"I intend to."

The document examiner's preliminary report arrived two days later.

The signature was genuine.

The handwriting in the restriction beneath it was also consistent with Richard's.

However, the document had been assembled from two separate sheets.

The upper section containing the title and allocation language had been printed on a different type of paper from the lower section carrying Richard's approval.

The examiner could not establish exactly when the two sections had been joined.

But the physical examination showed that the page had been reconstructed.

Edward studied the report.

"Can they determine whether Richard signed the original request or this amended version?"

"Not from the available material," the examiner replied.

"What about the staple marks?"

"The paper shows evidence of an earlier attachment. It is possible that the approval was originally attached to another document."

"Can you identify that document?"

"Not yet."

Edward thanked the examiner and ended the call.

He now had an important distinction.

Richard's signature was genuine.

But the page carrying it was not necessarily the page he had signed.

Jonathan arrived shortly afterward with another discovery.

"The original request has a reference number."

Edward looked up.

"Which one?"

"RV-56/CONTINUITY."

Edward turned toward him.

"R.V."

"Yes."

The same initials that had appeared repeatedly in the restricted records.

But now they had a different context.

"Could R.V. stand for Receiving Verification?"

"Possibly. But the request is titled continuity, not receiving verification."

Jonathan placed the document on the desk.

"There is also a routing stamp."

**RECEIVING VERIFICATION — ADMINISTRATIVE REVIEW ONLY.**

Edward examined it.

The request had been routed through a department responsible for receiving verification.

But the approval had been given by Richard Valmont.

"Why would a temporary care continuity request go through receiving verification?" Jonathan asked.

"Because someone wanted it connected to the recipient schedule."

"Even though Richard was only approving temporary record preservation."

"Exactly."

Edward looked at the stamp.

"Find out where this department operated."

The answer came from the old Vale Private Counsel organisation chart.

Receiving Verification had not been a separate company.

It had been an internal administrative function established to coordinate documents between originating hospitals and proposed receiving institutions.

It had no independent authority to determine biological identity.

Its responsibilities were limited to checking whether the relevant parties had submitted the required acknowledgements.

In practice, however, it had become the point through which several different organisations communicated.

Its director had been listed as:

**CHARLES VALE — TEMPORARY ADMINISTRATIVE SUPERVISOR.**

Jonathan read the entry.

"So R.V. wasn't a person."

"No."

"It was a department."

"Yes."

"And Charles supervised it."

Edward looked at the organisation chart.

That explained the repeated references.

The receiving authority had never been a properly appointed individual because the administrative function had been treated as though it could substitute for one.

A department could receive documents.

It could track acknowledgements.

It could record that a family had been notified.

But it could not independently certify that a particular infant belonged to that family.

Someone had allowed the administrative function to grow beyond its actual authority.

The next step was to find the department's original staff.

Most had left the firm years earlier.

Some had retired.

Others had moved into unrelated work.

One former employee agreed to speak.

Her name was Patricia Moore.

She had been a junior records coordinator at Vale Private Counsel.

Edward met her in a quiet café.

Patricia arrived with a handbag and a small notebook.

"I don't remember everything," she warned.

"I understand."

"I was junior staff. I moved documents, recorded references, and followed instructions."

"That is exactly what we need to understand."

She looked at the photograph of the organisation chart.

"Receiving Verification."

"Do you remember it?"

"Of course."

"What did it do?"

"Everything that involved checking whether paperwork had reached the next office."

"Did it verify identities?"

Patricia gave him a puzzled look.

"No. We didn't have medical authority."

"Did you ever use the term receiving identity?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because that was the category used in the schedule."

"Who decided which families appeared under that category?"

"Mr Vale."

Edward leaned forward.

"Charles?"

"Yes."

"Did he prepare the schedule himself?"

"Sometimes. Sometimes his assistant prepared the drafts."

"Did he ask you to enter family names?"

Patricia hesitated.

"I entered names into documents that were already approved for circulation."

"Who approved them?"

"Mr Vale."

"Was there a separate receiving identity custodian?"

She shook her head.

"Not in our department."

"Did you know the role existed?"

"I knew it was supposed to exist."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

"Supposed to?"

Patricia nodded.

"We had a checklist. It had a box for receiving custodian approval. We were told to leave it blank until the appointment was confirmed."

"Was it ever confirmed?"

"Not while I worked there."

"When did you leave?"

"About four months after the first schedule was prepared."

Edward asked another question.

"Did you ever see a document bearing Richard Valmont's signature?"

Patricia thought for a moment.

"Yes."

"On what?"

"A continuity request."

"Did it contain proposed family identities?"

"No. It was a temporary care document."

"Did you attach it to another document?"

Patricia's expression changed.

"I might have."

Edward waited.

"Why?"

"Mr Vale asked me to combine the approval page with the revised schedule."

"Did he explain why?"

"He said the original request and the schedule were part of the same administrative matter."

"Did you check whether the approval covered the schedule?"

Patricia looked uncomfortable.

"No. That wasn't my responsibility."

"Did you notice that the title was different?"

"I remember the heading changed."

"From what to what?"

"Continuity of temporary care records. It became something about family allocation."

Edward kept his voice even.

"Did you understand that the meaning had changed?"

"Not at the time."

"Did you raise the issue?"

"No."

Patricia looked down at her hands.

"I was young. I was following instructions from the director."

Edward did not accuse her.

He knew that a junior employee could be part of an administrative chain without understanding the consequences of every document she handled.

But her memory had established something important.

The page had been physically assembled inside Receiving Verification.

And Charles Vale had ordered it.

"Do you remember who received the revised schedule?" Edward asked.

Patricia thought.

"It went to several places."

"Which ones?"

"Meridian. The Family Office. The residential administration service."

"Did it go to the Valmont family?"

"No. Not directly."

"Did it go to the Aurelius Family Office?"

"Yes."

"Was it returned?"

Patricia nodded.

"I remember that."

"Who handled the return?"

"I did."

"Did you record the reason?"

"I wrote down that the Family Office had not accepted the receiving allocation."

Edward placed the day-54 return acknowledgement before her.

"Is this it?"

Patricia examined the document.

"Yes. I remember the wording."

"Did Charles receive the returned schedule?"

"Yes."

"What did he do?"

Patricia hesitated.

"He asked me to prepare another copy."

"With the same family allocation?"

"I don't know."

"Did you make it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

She looked at him.

"Because he told me to leave the allocation section blank until the receiving verification was complete."

Edward paused.

That was unexpected.

"Did he say that after the Aurelius rejection?"

"Yes."

"Then why did the revised schedule bearing Richard's signature continue to circulate?"

Patricia shook her head.

"I don't know."

Edward returned to the office with more questions than answers.

The sequence now appeared to be:

- Richard approved a request to preserve temporary care records.
- The request was routed through Receiving Verification.
- Charles Vale's department attached the approval to a revised family allocation document.
- The Aurelius Family Office rejected the proposed receiving role.
- Charles received the returned schedule.
- A later instruction from Charles told staff to leave the allocation section blank until verification was complete.

But the last point complicated the theory that Charles had simply wanted to force a permanent allocation.

He had apparently instructed his own staff not to complete the allocation section.

Why?

Was he trying to correct the process?

Was he protecting himself?

Or had someone else been using the schedule without his knowledge?

Edward looked at the dates.

The revised schedule had been prepared on day 56.

The instruction to leave the allocation section blank came later.

The date was day 61.

The same day Dr Hale returned Part One of Annex C to Adrian Holt.

There was a gap of five days.

Five days in which the schedule might have been used.

At the Aurelius residence, Helena Brooks was reviewing the old Family Office correspondence when Alexander entered.

"I found the day-54 acknowledgement," she said.

"I have seen it."

"Then you know we rejected the receiving allocation."

"Yes."

Helena placed another document beside it.

"This is the internal instruction I issued afterward."

Alexander read it.

It was dated day 57.

The instruction stated that the Aurelius Family Office was not to acknowledge any proposed child placement without independent medical verification and a formally appointed receiving identity custodian.

Alexander looked up.

"Why was this necessary?"

"Because documents continued arriving under the Family Office reference."

"After we had rejected the schedule?"

"Yes."

"Did anyone claim we had accepted a child?"

"Not in a formal letter."

"Then what happened?"

Helena opened a second folder.

"On day 60, a representative from Vale Private Counsel requested confirmation that the Family Office would retain the originating records for future reconciliation."

"Did we agree?"

"We agreed to preserve the records we already held. We did not agree to accept any child or receiving identity."

Alexander read the correspondence.

The distinction was explicit.

The Family Office had agreed to preserve records.

It had not accepted custody.

It had not accepted a proposed placement.

It had not confirmed biological identity.

Victoria entered while they were reading.

"What is it?"

Helena explained.

Victoria studied the documents.

"So the Family Office was being used as a records custodian."

"Yes," Alexander replied.

"Not as the receiving family."

"Correct."

Victoria looked at the day-60 letter.

"Then someone knew the distinction."

Helena nodded.

"Several people knew."

Victoria's expression became distant.

"And yet our daughter was still missing."

At the Valmont estate, Richard received Edward's preliminary findings.

He read the summary without interruption.

When he reached the section describing the reconstructed signature page, he stopped.

"Charles attached my approval to the allocation schedule."

"That is what the evidence suggests."

"Without my knowledge."

"Patricia Moore remembers him ordering the document to be combined."

Richard placed the report down.

"Did she say why?"

"No."

Richard stood.

"I want the original request."

Edward had brought it.

He handed it over.

Richard examined the wording.

"This is what I approved."

"Yes."

"And this is what appeared in the revised schedule."

Edward placed the second document beside it.

Richard read both.

The anger in his face was controlled but unmistakable.

"I never approved a permanent family allocation."

"I believe you."

Richard looked at him.

"Do you?"

"Your original request was limited to temporary record preservation. The signature page was attached to a different document later."

Richard returned to his chair.

"Then Charles used my approval to support something I never authorised."

"Possibly."

"Not possibly, Edward. Look at the wording."

Edward did not argue.

He understood why his father was angry.

But he also knew that the investigation had not yet established who had used the revised schedule or whether any child had been permanently assigned through it.

"There's another question," Edward said.

Richard looked up.

"Did you ever meet anyone from R-04?"

"No."

"Did you know it existed?"

"Not by that name."

"Did Charles mention Receiving Verification?"

"Only as an administrative department."

"Did he tell you that it was responsible for the identity schedule?"

"No."

Edward placed the organisation chart on the desk.

Richard studied it.

"Charles was supervising it?"

"Yes."

Richard's expression darkened.

"Then he knew exactly what authority it did and did not have."

That evening, Edward received a message from Dr Samuel Hale.

The retired physician had reviewed the new documents and wanted to speak again.

Edward called him.

"Doctor?"

"I have been thinking about your questions."

"Have you remembered something?"

"Perhaps."

"What is it?"

Hale paused.

"After the day-56 schedule was prepared, Charles asked me to acknowledge that the temporary care arrangements were consistent with the available medical records."

"Did you?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the schedule contained a family allocation section."

"Did you object?"

"Yes."

"What did Charles say?"

"He told me that the allocation was only administrative."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

The same explanation again.

"Did you believe him?"

"I believed that was how he intended to use it."

"Did you know he had attached Richard's approval?"

"No."

"Would you have accepted the schedule if you had known?"

"Absolutely not."

Hale's voice was firm.

"Mr Valmont's approval was for preservation of temporary care records. It did not establish the identity of any child."

Edward looked at the documents.

"Doctor, did you sign anything on day 56?"

"No."

"Did anyone sign the medical verification section?"

"No."

"Then the schedule was incomplete."

"Yes."

"Did you tell Charles?"

"I did."

"What happened afterward?"

Hale hesitated.

"Charles asked me to leave the schedule with him."

"Did you?"

"I returned the medical pages. I did not keep a copy of the family allocation."

Edward sat forward.

"Why?"

"Because it was not a medical record."

"Do you remember who collected it?"

Hale was silent for several seconds.

Then he answered.

"Adrian Holt."

Edward's expression changed.

"Holt collected Schedule 4?"

"He collected a sealed administrative package. I assumed it contained the recipient schedule."

"When?"

"Day 61."

Edward looked at the timeline.

The same day Part One of Annex C was returned to Holt.

"Did you see the package being sealed?"

"No. It was already sealed when Holt arrived."

"Who sealed it?"

"I don't know."

"Did Holt sign a receipt?"

"Yes."

"Do you still have it?"

"I may."

Hale promised to search his archived correspondence.

Edward ended the call and immediately contacted Jonathan.

"Day 61. We need Holt's receipt."

"Another transfer?"

"Possibly the transfer of Schedule 4."

Jonathan opened the chronology.

"That would mean Holt collected Part One of Annex C and the recipient schedule on the same day."

"Yes."

"From different places?"

"Possibly."

Jonathan looked at the documents.

"Then the two records may have been brought together."

Edward nodded.

The investigation had finally reached a point where two previously separate chains might intersect.

Part One contained originating medical information.

Schedule 4 contained temporary care arrangements and proposed family allocations.

If someone had combined them, they might have been able to create the appearance of a complete identity file.

Even though the medical verification was still missing.

The next morning, Hale sent a scanned receipt.

It was brief.

**DAY 61 — SEALED ADMINISTRATIVE PACKAGE**

**COLLECTED BY: ADRIAN HOLT**

**CONTENTS: SCHEDULE 4 / CURRENT CARE ARRANGEMENTS**

**RESTRICTION: NOT FOR FINAL IDENTITY DETERMINATION**

The final line had been added in handwriting.

**Medical reconciliation remains incomplete.**

Edward read it twice.

Holt had collected the schedule.

But the restriction was explicit.

It could not be used as a final identity determination.

Edward called Jonathan.

"Find out where Holt took it."

"His records say Unit 6."

"Which means the same secure archive that held Annex C."

Jonathan paused.

"Then Schedule 4 may have been stored with Part One and Part Two."

"Yes."

"That could explain why the records became so difficult to trace."

Edward looked at the receipt.

"Or why someone wanted them kept together."

The secure archive's old inventory register was finally obtained.

It listed three items received on day 61.

1. Annex C — Part One.
2. Annex C — Part Two.
3. Schedule 4 — Current Care Arrangements.

The first two had been stored together under the Annex C reference.

The third had been placed in a separate compartment.

Its release condition was different.

**ACCESS ONLY WITH ORIGINATING RECORDS AUTHORITY AND RECEIVING VERIFICATION AUTHORITY.**

Edward stared at the entry.

The original recipient schedule had been stored with the identity file.

But it had been kept separate.

The restriction had not been removed.

Someone had preserved the distinction between medical records and administrative arrangements.

And someone had also ensured that no one could access the schedule without the two authorities that had never been appointed.

Jonathan pointed to the last line.

"Look at the inventory notation."

Edward read it.

**COMPARTMENT SEAL REVIEWED DAY 118.**

"Who reviewed it?" he asked.

Jonathan checked the next page.

There was a signature.

Not Adrian Holt's.

Not Dr Hale's.

Not Margaret Voss's.

It belonged to a woman named Eleanor Vale.

Edward looked up.

"Eleanor."

The same Eleanor who had repeatedly insisted that maternal identity and receiving identity must remain separate.

The same Eleanor who had handled the IV-204 records.

The same Eleanor whose initials appeared on several instructions warning against attaching maternal references to temporary receiving identities.

She had inspected the compartment on day 118.

But the inventory did not say whether she opened it.

It only recorded that the seal had been reviewed.

Edward turned the page.

A handwritten note appeared beneath the entry.

**Schedule 4 must remain separate until the receiving authority is correctly appointed. No family notification permitted on the basis of this schedule.**

The instruction was signed.

**E.V.**

Edward read it again.

For the first time, he had a document showing that Eleanor Vale had personally intervened after the schedule had been placed in secure storage.

She had not simply preserved the records.

She had prevented the proposed family allocation from being used as proof.

But why?

And what had she discovered between day 61 and day 118 that made her so determined to keep the schedule sealed?

Edward closed the file.

They had spent months following the people who had moved the children.

Now they needed to understand the person who had repeatedly stopped the records from being turned into identities.

Because Eleanor Vale might have been the only person who understood what Schedule 4 could do if it ever reached the wrong hands.
