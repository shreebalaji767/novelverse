# **Chapter 5 — The Woman Who Remembered**

At 7:15 on Monday morning, Nicholas Valmont discovered that his younger sister had committed an unforgivable crime.

She had eaten the last strawberry.

His strawberry.

The strawberry he had deliberately left in the refrigerator because he intended to eat it after breakfast.

He stood in the kitchen doorway, staring at the empty fruit container.

"Who did this?"

Elena, sitting at the counter with a glass of milk, slowly looked up.

"Good morning."

"Where is my strawberry?"

She glanced at the container.

"Oh."

Nicholas narrowed his eyes.

"You ate it."

"It was in the refrigerator."

"That doesn't answer my question."

"I thought it was for everyone."

"It was for me."

"Then you should have labelled it."

Nicholas stared at her.

"You're telling me I need to label individual strawberries in my own home?"

Elena nodded thoughtfully.

"Perhaps."

"Unbelievable."

Mr. Harris, who had been speaking with a member of the kitchen staff, quietly turned away.

His shoulders were shaking.

Nicholas noticed.

"Mr. Harris, I expect better from you."

"Of course, sir."

"You're supposed to maintain order in this household."

"Certainly, sir."

"Then why is my breakfast being stolen?"

Mr. Harris remained perfectly composed.

"I shall investigate the matter."

Elena covered her mouth.

Nicholas pointed at her.

"Don't laugh. This is serious."

"All right."

"You don't sound sorry."

"I'm not."

He placed a hand over his heart.

"I have raised you better than this."

Elena blinked.

"You are three years older than me."

"Exactly. I've spent three years educating you."

"Your education has clearly failed."

Nicholas looked toward the ceiling.

"Father! Your daughter has become disrespectful!"

From the dining room, Richard's voice answered.

"Then stop arguing over fruit and come to breakfast."

Nicholas sighed.

"Nobody understands my suffering."

The breakfast table was unusually full that morning.

Richard had an early meeting at Valmont Holdings. Sebastian was reviewing the agenda for a corporate acquisition. Edward had arrived home late the previous night and looked as though he had barely slept.

Catherine was speaking with Isabelle about university arrangements.

Elena sat beside Nicholas, who was still mourning his lost strawberry.

"Are you going to eat that?" he asked, looking at her plate.

Elena moved her toast slightly farther away.

"Yes."

"I was only asking."

"You were looking at it."

"I have eyes."

"Then use them on your own plate."

Edward gave a quiet laugh.

Sebastian glanced up.

"Can we finish breakfast without another argument?"

Nicholas looked offended.

"Why am I always blamed?"

"Because you're usually responsible," Edward replied.

Isabelle smiled.

Elena noticed.

It was a small smile, but it seemed more natural than the ones Isabelle had offered during her first evening at the mansion.

Perhaps the household was becoming less unfamiliar to her.

"Isabelle," Catherine said, "the university admissions office has sent the information we requested. You can review the available courses this afternoon."

"Thank you."

"There's no need to decide immediately."

"I understand."

Richard put down his tablet.

"Your previous academic records will be reviewed. We will arrange for the university to assess which credits can be transferred."

Isabelle nodded.

Elena took a sip of water.

She had already completed her own registration for the coming semester.

For a brief moment, she wondered whether she and Isabelle would eventually attend the same university.

The thought was strange.

Not unpleasant.

Just strange.

After breakfast, Richard left for the office.

Sebastian followed shortly afterward.

Edward remained behind.

He had been waiting for a telephone call.

At 9:06 a.m., it arrived.

"Mr. Valmont," Daniel said, "we have located Margaret Ellis."

Edward stood beside his study window.

"Where?"

"She lives in a retirement apartment on the eastern side of the city. She confirmed that she worked at the hospital eighteen years ago."

"Did she agree to speak with us?"

"Not exactly."

Edward frowned.

"What did she say?"

"She said she remembers very little about that night."

"Did she sound uncertain?"

"She sounded nervous."

Edward looked at the old document on his desk.

"Arrange a meeting."

"She asked whether the hospital would be present."

"Tell her we only want to ask questions. No accusations."

"Understood."

Edward ended the call.

He looked once more at the duty roster.

Margaret Ellis.

Senior maternity nurse.

Temporary transfer to medical records.

Six days after the births.

There was something about that sequence he did not like.

At 10:30 a.m., Margaret Ellis opened her apartment door.

A man in a dark suit stood outside.

"Mrs. Ellis?"

"Yes."

"My name is Daniel. I'm representing the Valmont family."

Margaret's expression changed.

"Valmont?"

"Yes, ma'am. We'd like to speak with you about your work at St. Catherine's Hospital."

She held the door handle tightly.

"I already told someone that I don't remember anything."

Daniel remained polite.

"We understand. Mr. Edward Valmont would simply appreciate the opportunity to ask a few questions."

"I have nothing to tell him."

"Then perhaps we can establish that together."

Margaret looked past him toward the corridor.

"Is he coming here?"

"He is waiting in the car. If you prefer, we can arrange another location."

She hesitated.

Then she opened the door a little wider.

"Give me a few minutes."

"Of course."

She closed the door.

Inside, Margaret stood in the hallway, staring at the cabinet.

The envelope was still in the bottom drawer.

She could pretend it didn't exist.

She could tell them she remembered nothing.

She had done so for eighteen years.

But the old fear had returned.

And this time, she was not sure she could continue carrying it alone.

Edward waited in the car.

He had expected the former nurse to refuse.

He had also expected her to be more cooperative once she understood that the family was investigating the circumstances of the two births.

What he had not expected was her reaction to hearing the Valmont name.

She had looked frightened.

Not surprised.

Frightened.

Daniel returned to the car after several minutes.

"She's willing to meet."

Edward opened the door.

"Good."

"She asked if the meeting could take place somewhere private."

"Then we'll respect that."

They entered the apartment building together.

Margaret was waiting in her sitting room.

She was a small elderly woman with neatly arranged silver hair. A pair of reading glasses rested on the table beside her.

"Mrs. Ellis," Edward greeted her.

"Mr. Valmont."

He sat opposite her.

"Thank you for agreeing to meet."

"I don't know how much help I can be."

"That's all right. I only want to understand what happened at the hospital on the night two girls were born."

Margaret folded her hands.

"I worked in the maternity ward."

"I know."

"It was a long time ago."

"Yes."

"I've forgotten many things."

Edward nodded.

"Then perhaps we can begin with what you remember."

Margaret looked at him.

For a few seconds, she said nothing.

Then she asked, "How is the girl?"

Edward frowned slightly.

"Which girl?"

"The one who stayed with your family."

He felt a sudden tension in his shoulders.

"Elena?"

Margaret lowered her eyes.

"Is that her name?"

"Yes."

She nodded slowly.

"Is she well?"

Edward studied her.

"She's well. Why do you ask?"

Margaret looked toward the window.

"Because I remember her."

The meeting lasted almost an hour.

Margaret did not offer a complete explanation.

She spoke about the hospital's busy night shift, the unusually high number of deliveries, and a temporary shortage of staff.

She remembered two newborn girls.

She remembered that one of the families had been waiting anxiously for an update.

She remembered a senior administrator entering the maternity ward.

But she did not remember seeing either infant being transferred to the wrong cot.

Edward listened carefully.

"Did you notice anything unusual about the identification bands?"

Margaret hesitated.

"I don't remember."

"Did anyone ask you to change a record?"

"No."

"Did anyone ask you to move a document?"

She looked at him.

Then she looked away.

"I don't remember."

Edward did not push immediately.

Instead, he opened his folder and placed a copy of the old hospital record on the table.

"Have you seen this before?"

Margaret looked at it.

Her face became pale.

Edward noticed.

"Mrs. Ellis?"

She slowly adjusted her glasses.

"That isn't the original."

Edward leaned forward.

"What do you mean?"

"The handwriting."

"Whose handwriting is it?"

Margaret shook her head.

"I can't say."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't know."

Edward waited.

Margaret stood up abruptly.

"I need some water."

She walked to the kitchen.

Edward remained seated.

Daniel leaned toward him.

"She's hiding something."

"I know."

A minute later, Margaret returned carrying a glass.

She sat down again.

"Mr. Valmont, I don't want to be involved in this."

"I understand."

"Then please leave it alone."

Edward studied her.

"Two families have spent eighteen years believing they know what happened to their children. One of those children may have been separated from her biological family because someone altered a hospital record."

Margaret's hands trembled slightly.

"That wasn't supposed to happen."

Edward went still.

"What wasn't supposed to happen?"

Margaret looked at him.

The words seemed to have escaped before she could stop them.

She pressed her lips together.

Then she stood again.

"I can't."

Edward rose slowly.

"Mrs. Ellis—"

"Please leave."

He did not argue.

But before he reached the door, he turned back.

"If you remember anything, contact me. Whatever happened that night, we need to know the truth."

Margaret did not answer.

Back at the Valmont estate, Elena was in the garden with Isabelle.

They had been walking along the stone path near the fountain.

Isabelle was holding a small notebook.

"What are you writing?" Elena asked.

"Things I need to arrange."

"Like what?"

"University documents. Medical records. My old school certificates. There are quite a few things."

Elena nodded.

"That sounds exhausting."

"It is."

"Do you want help?"

Isabelle looked at her.

"Would you?"

"Of course."

"Wouldn't it be strange?"

Elena smiled.

"Most things are strange lately."

Isabelle laughed softly.

They continued walking.

After a while, Isabelle asked, "What do you want to study after this semester?"

Elena told her about her interests.

She had not yet made a final decision about her future specialisation, but she was considering finance and economics.

Isabelle listened with interest.

"That suits you."

"Why?"

"You seem organised."

Elena looked at her.

"Have you seen my desk?"

"No."

"Then don't make assumptions."

Isabelle laughed again.

They reached a bench beneath a large tree.

Elena sat down.

Isabelle remained standing for a moment before joining her.

"Can I ask you another question?"

"Sure."

"Have you ever wanted to leave this house?"

Elena considered it.

"Sometimes."

"Why?"

"Not because I dislike it. I just want to know what it's like to live independently."

"Would you move out?"

"Maybe after university. Or during it, if I found a suitable place."

Isabelle looked toward the mansion.

"I've never had a place like this."

Elena followed her gaze.

"I suppose neither of us knows what to do with it now."

Isabelle looked down at her notebook.

"I don't want you to lose your home."

Elena was quiet for a moment.

Then she said, "I don't want you to feel like you don't have one."

Isabelle looked at her.

For the first time, neither girl seemed to know what to say.

That afternoon, Catherine received a call from the family's legal adviser.

She was in her office reviewing documents when her phone rang.

"Hello, Mr. Laurent."

"Good afternoon, Madam."

"Have you finished reviewing the trust documents?"

"Yes. I would like to discuss them with you and Mr. Valmont."

"Is there a problem?"

"There are several matters that require clarification."

Catherine put down her pen.

"Such as?"

"The inheritance provisions, the family beneficiary list, and the question of whether any changes should be made while the hospital investigation remains open."

Catherine frowned.

"Richard already told you that nothing will change until we know the facts."

"Yes, Madam. I wanted to confirm that this remains the family's position."

"It does."

"Very well."

He paused.

"There is one more matter."

"What is it?"

"The hospital has requested additional information regarding Elena's original birth registration."

Catherine's expression changed.

"What information?"

"They want to compare the original records with the documents currently held by the family."

"Why?"

"Because they believe the discrepancy may have occurred before the final registration was issued."

Catherine was silent.

Then she asked, "Does that mean Elena's identity is uncertain?"

"Her biological parentage is uncertain, Madam. Her legal identity is a separate matter."

Catherine closed her eyes briefly.

"Please arrange the meeting."

"Certainly."

When the call ended, she remained seated.

She looked at the family photograph on her desk.

Elena stood in the centre, laughing.

Catherine touched the edge of the frame.

She had not expected motherhood to become a question of documents.

At 6:40 p.m., Edward returned home.

He found Elena in the sitting room.

She was helping Nicholas search for a missing company presentation.

"You're looking in the wrong folder," she said.

Nicholas leaned over her shoulder.

"How do you know?"

"Because the presentation is called Annual Strategy Review, not Annual Review Strategy."

"That's an unreasonable distinction."

"Search properly."

He typed again.

A moment later, the file appeared.

Nicholas stared at the screen.

"Excellent."

"You're welcome."

He turned toward her.

"You're hired."

"I already have a job."

"What job?"

"Being your sister."

Nicholas grinned.

"Unfortunately, that position pays terribly."

"Then I'll resign."

"Denied."

Edward entered.

"You're both still arguing?"

Nicholas looked up.

"She has threatened to resign."

"Understandable."

Elena laughed.

Edward smiled briefly.

But his expression changed when he looked at her.

"Elena, can I speak with you for a moment?"

She looked up.

"Of course."

Nicholas glanced between them.

"Everything all right?"

"Yes," Edward replied.

He led Elena toward the quieter end of the room.

"How are you settling in?"

Elena blinked.

"Here?"

"Yes."

"Fine."

Edward studied her face.

"Really?"

She nodded.

"Why?"

He hesitated.

"I wanted to make sure you're comfortable."

Elena smiled.

"You sound like Mom."

"That's unfortunate."

She laughed.

Edward placed his hands in his pockets.

"Elena, whatever happens with the hospital investigation, I don't want you to think you have to handle it alone."

Her smile faded slightly.

"Did something happen?"

"Not yet."

"That sounds worrying."

"It isn't meant to."

Elena looked at him carefully.

"Edward, is there something you're not telling me?"

He considered lying.

Not because he wanted to deceive her, but because he did not yet have anything reliable to tell her.

"We're trying to understand the old records," he said.

"Did you find something?"

"Possibly. But nothing conclusive."

Elena nodded.

"All right."

He watched her for a moment.

Then she asked, "Do you think someone deliberately switched us?"

Edward was silent.

"I don't know."

"Do you think we'll find out?"

"Yes."

She looked down.

"I hope so."

Edward gently touched her shoulder.

"We will."

At 8:12 p.m., Margaret Ellis opened the cabinet again.

She had waited until the evening before retrieving the envelope.

It was still there.

She took it out and placed it on the table.

The paper had become fragile.

She carefully opened the flap.

Inside was a folded sheet of paper and a small photograph.

The photograph showed a hospital corridor.

Nothing particularly unusual.

But in the background stood a man wearing a dark suit.

Margaret looked at the photograph for a long time.

She remembered him.

He had arrived at the maternity ward shortly before dawn.

He had not been a doctor.

He had not been a nurse.

And yet, everyone had obeyed him.

She unfolded the paper.

There were only two handwritten sentences.

*The original records must remain separate.*

*The child must not be returned until further instructions.*

Margaret's breathing became shallow.

She folded the paper again.

For eighteen years, she had believed that the instruction referred to an administrative procedure.

Now she was no longer certain.

She looked at the telephone.

Then she looked at the photograph.

Finally, she picked up her phone and searched for the number Edward Valmont had left with her.

Her finger hovered over the call button.

She hesitated.

Then she pressed it.

Edward was in his study when his phone rang.

The number was unfamiliar.

He answered.

"Edward Valmont."

A trembling voice came through.

"Mr. Valmont?"

"Yes."

"It's Margaret Ellis."

Edward immediately sat upright.

"Mrs. Ellis. Is everything all right?"

"No."

He waited.

"I remembered something."

Edward glanced toward the clock.

"What did you remember?"

Margaret took a breath.

"I have something that belonged to the hospital records. Something I kept after that night."

"What is it?"

"A photograph. And a written instruction."

Edward stood.

"Can you bring them to us?"

"No."

"Then I'll come to you."

She was silent.

"Mrs. Ellis?"

"There's something else."

"What?"

"The man in the photograph."

Edward's grip tightened around the phone.

"Who is he?"

"I don't know his name."

"Do you remember anything about him?"

Margaret swallowed.

"He was not hospital staff."

Edward waited.

"He came to the maternity ward shortly before dawn. He spoke to the administrator. After that, the records were separated."

"Separated how?"

Margaret's voice dropped.

"One set was kept in the normal archive."

"And the other?"

"It was taken away."

Edward felt a chill run through him.

"Taken where?"

"I don't know."

"Mrs. Ellis, what did the instruction say?"

There was a long silence.

Then Margaret answered.

"It said the child must not be returned until further instructions."

Edward stared at the wall.

For eighteen years, everyone had believed the two girls had been accidentally exchanged.

But what if the exchange had not been accidental?

What if someone had taken one of the newborns deliberately?

And what if the truth had been hidden not to protect the hospital, but to protect someone much more powerful?

Edward spoke carefully.

"Mrs. Ellis, don't tell anyone else about this. I'll come tomorrow morning, and we'll make arrangements to preserve the evidence."

"All right."

He ended the call.

Then he immediately contacted Daniel.

"Cancel my morning schedule."

"Sir?"

"We have a witness and possible evidence connected to the original hospital records."

Daniel became alert.

"Understood."

"And Daniel?"

"Yes?"

"Find out who had access to that maternity ward that night. Everyone. Hospital staff, administrators, visitors, security personnel."

"Yes, sir."

Edward ended the call.

He looked at the photograph of Elena on his desk.

For the first time, he wondered whether the family had been asking the wrong question.

They had spent days trying to discover how two babies had been switched.

Perhaps they should have been asking something else.

**Who had wanted one of those babies to disappear?**

*End of Chapter 5.*
