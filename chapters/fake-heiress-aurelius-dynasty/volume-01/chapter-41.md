# **Chapter 41 — The Guardian Who Was Replaced**

The restricted correspondence tray was emptied every morning.

Most of its contents were ordinary archival requests: missing signatures, incomplete references, duplicate invoices, and documents that had been incorrectly classified decades earlier.

The envelope marked **ELEVENTH DAY — RETURN INSTRUCTION** was different.

It had been waiting in the tray for less than twenty minutes when Jonathan Pierce received a call.

"Mr. Pierce?"

"Yes."

"This is the Vale archive. We've located an additional correspondence entry connected to the Morelli file."

Jonathan stopped writing.

"What kind of correspondence?"

"An eleventh-day instruction. It appears to concern Infant B."

He glanced at the report beside him.

"Send me the complete entry. Not just the index."

"We're preparing the request."

"Please don't move the original document."

There was a pause.

"Understood."

Jonathan ended the call and immediately contacted Edward.

Edward arrived at the archive shortly after eleven.

The document was already waiting in a controlled examination room.

It was a narrow sheet of paper, folded twice, with a faded reference number written along its upper edge.

Jonathan stood beside the table.

"Before you read it, there's something you should understand."

Edward put on the supplied gloves.

"What?"

"The index describes this as a return instruction. But the document itself doesn't use that term."

Edward unfolded the paper.

The writing was brief.

*The original receiving representative is no longer authorised to receive Infant B.*

*The replacement must be verified through the originating authority before any further movement.*

*No disclosure of the child's previous placement is permitted.*

At the bottom was a signature.

**M. MORELLI**

Edward read the document again.

"Morelli wrote this?"

"That's what the signature suggests."

"Then why was it filed as a return instruction?"

"Because of the index entry."

Edward looked at the paper.

"Someone misclassified it."

"Possibly."

He pointed to the second paragraph.

"Who was the replacement?"

"Not named here."

"Who was the original receiving representative?"

"Not named either."

Edward looked up.

"Then what exactly does this document tell us?"

Jonathan turned another page.

"It tells us that, on the eleventh day, someone had decided the person originally expected to receive Infant B could no longer do so."

Edward looked back at the signature.

"And Morelli agreed?"

"We don't know whether he agreed. His signature could indicate that he issued the instruction, acknowledged receiving it, or witnessed it."

Edward exhaled.

"Another document that tells us something happened without telling us who did it."

Jonathan gave a tired smile.

"That seems to be the theme of this investigation."

Edward wasn't amused.

He studied the paper more closely.

There was a small handwritten reference beneath Morelli's signature.

**R-11 / C.V.**

"Charles Vale?"

"That's our working assumption."

"Then we need to ask him."

Jonathan nodded.

"There's a problem."

"What?"

"Charles Vale died nine years ago."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

Of course he had.

Another witness who could no longer be questioned.

"Then we find whoever inherited his records."

The first call to Morelli went unanswered.

The second was rejected.

The third was answered.

"What is it?" Morelli asked.

Edward did not waste time.

"We found an eleventh-day instruction."

Silence.

"It bears your signature."

Morelli did not respond immediately.

"Where did you find it?"

"In the Vale archive."

Another pause.

"Read it to me."

Edward read the three sentences.

When he finished, Morelli sighed.

"That wasn't a return instruction."

"We know."

"Then why are you calling it that?"

"The archive index describes it that way."

"Archives are full of people who assign titles to documents they don't understand."

Edward glanced at Jonathan.

"Who was the original receiving representative for Infant B?"

Morelli's answer was immediate.

"Someone who had been selected before the children left the nursery."

"By whom?"

"Not by me."

"Was it Eleanor Vale?"

"No."

"Was it Charles Vale?"

"No."

"Was it someone from the Aurelius family?"

Morelli went silent.

Edward waited.

Finally, Morelli said, "I won't give you a name over the telephone."

"Then meet us."

"I already gave you the information I was prepared to give."

"Not all of it."

"Because you still haven't understood what happened."

Edward's jaw tightened.

"Then explain it."

"I cannot explain the final arrangement until you understand the original one."

"You're asking us to solve this backwards."

"Yes."

Morelli ended the call.

Edward lowered his phone.

Jonathan folded his arms.

"He's still protecting someone."

"Or himself."

"Possibly both."

Edward looked at the document again.

*The original receiving representative is no longer authorised to receive Infant B.*

The question was no longer simply where Infant B had gone.

It was why someone had been removed from the arrangement after Eleanor had already received the child.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander read the new report in silence.

Adrian stood opposite him.

Damien had joined them, carrying a separate folder containing the hospital's surviving administrative records.

Victoria sat beside the window.

"Morelli signed the eleventh-day instruction?" Alexander asked.

"Apparently," Adrian replied.

"Did he explain why?"

"He refused to identify the receiving representative."

Alexander put the paper down.

"Then we need to establish whether he was acting under an existing authority or issuing a new instruction."

Damien opened his folder.

"There is something else."

He placed a photocopy on the table.

It was a short entry from the old Family Office correspondence ledger.

**TENTH DAY — AUTHORITY SUSPENDED.**

**ELEVENTH DAY — REPLACEMENT REQUEST RECEIVED.**

**ORIGINATING PARTY NOT VERIFIED.**

Victoria looked at the final line.

"Not verified?"

"Correct."

"Then why was the request processed?"

"It wasn't. The record says the replacement request was held."

Alexander looked at Damien.

"Who held it?"

"Marcus Morelli."

The room fell quiet.

Victoria looked from the document to her husband.

"So he stopped the replacement?"

"That's what the ledger suggests," Adrian said.

"But Infant B was already moved after the tenth day."

"Yes."

"Then who was trying to replace the receiving representative?"

No one answered.

Alexander stood.

"Get the original ledger. I want the surrounding entries, not an isolated extract."

Adrian nodded.

Victoria picked up the photocopy again.

"Alexander."

He turned.

"If Morelli stopped the replacement, why did he leave the Family Office the same day?"

Alexander looked at the date.

The eleventh day.

His expression changed.

"Because he may have been dismissed."

"Or because he resigned."

"His separation agreement was confidential."

Victoria looked at him.

"Then perhaps it wasn't a resignation."

Alexander said nothing.

For eighteen years, the Family Office had treated Morelli's departure as a private personnel matter.

Now it was beginning to look like the final consequence of something that had happened to their daughter.

Elena was having a much less dramatic problem.

Her university interview invitation had arrived.

She read it three times.

Then she checked the date.

Then she checked the time zone.

Then she opened a calendar.

Finally, she called Clara.

Clara answered with a cheerful, "If this is about the emergency biscuits, I haven't forgotten."

"It's not about biscuits."

"That's disappointing."

"I received an interview invitation."

There was a brief silence.

Then Clara shouted.

"YOU DID?"

Elena pulled the phone away from her ear.

"Yes."

"That's wonderful!"

"It's only an interview."

"An interview is better than a rejection."

"Thank you for that inspiring observation."

"You're welcome."

Elena smiled.

Clara continued, "When is it?"

"Next week."

"Have you prepared?"

"I haven't even decided what to wear."

"That is not preparation."

"I know."

"Have you reviewed your application?"

"Yes."

"Your academic statement?"

"Yes."

"Your reasons for choosing the programme?"

Elena paused.

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

"I have a reason. I just need to explain it without sounding like I'm trying to escape my family."

Clara's tone softened.

"Then don't mention escaping."

Elena looked at the interview invitation.

"That's easy to say."

"Tell them why you want to study there. Tell them what you want to learn. Tell them what you intend to do afterward. Your family situation isn't the centre of your life."

Elena was quiet.

Then she smiled.

"You're right."

"Of course."

"Don't become unbearable."

"Too late."

After the call, Elena opened a new document.

She typed a heading.

**Why I Chose This Programme**

For several minutes, she stared at the blank page.

Then she began writing.

Isabelle was in the foundation office when Catherine arrived.

The scholarship proposal had changed considerably since their first discussion.

The original plan had been a relatively simple university scholarship.

Now it included academic grants, vocational training support, examination fees, and a small fund for students who needed assistance obtaining missing educational documents.

Catherine reviewed the updated pages.

"You've made substantial changes."

"I spoke with Elena."

Catherine looked up.

"What did she suggest?"

"That we shouldn't limit the programme to university students. Some people need technical qualifications or professional training. She also pointed out that requiring every document could exclude students who have difficulty obtaining them."

Catherine read the revised eligibility section.

"Those are sensible suggestions."

"I thought so."

Catherine closed the folder.

"How do you feel about the work?"

Isabelle considered the question.

"I like it."

"More than you expected?"

"Yes."

She looked toward the board.

"I thought being here would feel strange. Everyone knows who I am. Everyone knows why I came back."

Catherine listened.

"But when I'm working on this, I don't have to think about that all the time. I can just do the work."

Catherine smiled.

"Then perhaps we should give you more of it."

Isabelle laughed.

"That sounds suspiciously like more responsibility."

"It is."

"You're not even pretending otherwise."

"No."

Isabelle shook her head, smiling.

Then she became thoughtful.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think Elena will get the scholarship interview?"

Catherine looked surprised.

"Her university interview?"

"Yes."

"I hope so."

Isabelle looked down at the folder.

"She's been working hard."

"I know."

"She deserves the opportunity."

Catherine reached across the desk and touched her hand.

"So do you."

Isabelle looked at her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Isabelle nodded.

"Thank you."

At the Vale archive, Jonathan had found the original correspondence envelope.

It was stored in a separate collection, not with the hospital records.

The outside was marked:

**FOR M. MORELLI — PERSONAL DELIVERY ONLY.**

The date was the eleventh day.

The envelope had been opened carefully, then resealed with an archival strip many years later.

Inside was a single sheet.

Jonathan read it first.

Then he called Edward.

"Come here."

Edward arrived twenty minutes later.

Jonathan placed the letter on the table.

"Read the final paragraph."

Edward looked down.

The letter began with a reference to the tenth-day placement.

It stated that the child had been delivered to a receiving party whose identity had not been fully verified.

The sender requested that the original receiving representative be replaced.

The reason was given in one sentence.

*The person who came for Infant A has now been identified, and her involvement makes the original arrangement impossible.*

Edward stopped reading.

"Her?"

"Yes."

"Do we know who the sender was?"

Jonathan pointed to the bottom of the page.

There was no full name.

Only a handwritten designation.

**THE MOTHER.**

Edward looked at him.

"Which mother?"

"That's the question."

The letter continued.

*Do not return Infant B to the person who came for Infant A. The two children must remain separate until their identities can be independently established.*

Edward read it again.

The wording was almost identical to Eleanor's later instruction to Margaret.

"Someone sent Eleanor the same warning," he said.

"Or Eleanor copied the warning into her own instructions."

"Was Eleanor the intended recipient?"

"The envelope was addressed to Morelli. But the letter refers to Eleanor's placement."

Edward looked at the date.

"Whoever wrote this knew about the tenth-day transfer."

"Yes."

"And knew that the person who came for Infant A was involved."

"Yes."

Edward sat down.

"Then this wasn't a random change of guardian."

Jonathan nodded.

"It was a response to something someone had discovered."

Edward looked at the final line.

**THE MOTHER.**

"Can we compare this handwriting with the maternal identification records?"

"We can, if we recover the original pages."

"Then Box 114-B becomes even more important."

Jonathan nodded.

"But there's another problem."

Edward looked up.

"The letter doesn't say which mother."

Edward leaned back.

There were two women.

Victoria Aurelius.

Catherine Valmont.

One had been told her daughter was under medical observation.

The other had been told there was an administrative delay.

Neither had been told where the children were.

And now someone had written to Morelli claiming to be a mother.

Which one?

Later that evening, Edward sat alone in his study.

He had spread the documents across his desk.

The ninth-day release.

The tenth-day acknowledgment.

Eleanor's letter to Margaret.

The eleventh-day instruction.

The correspondence ledger.

He arranged them by date.

The sequence was becoming clearer.

1. **Ninth day**

   Two infants left the private nursery under provisional arrangements. Their actual receiving guardians were not the people named in the original instructions.
2. **Tenth day**

   Infant B was received by Eleanor Vale. Infant A was brought to Room Fourteen, where Martha Voss signed an administrative receipt. Charles Vale later physically removed the infant.
3. **Eleventh day**

   A mother sent an instruction to Morelli. The original receiving representative for Infant B was no longer authorised. A replacement was requested, but the request was held.
4. **Following weeks**

   Eleanor amended her instructions to Margaret, warning her not to give Infant B to the person who had come for Infant A.

Edward stared at the final document.

The mother had known.

At least one of the mothers had discovered something.

But had she discovered that her child was alive?

Had she discovered that another woman had attempted to collect the wrong infant?

Or had someone simply used the words *the mother* to make the instruction appear more authoritative?

He picked up the phone and called Jonathan.

"There's one more thing I want examined."

"What?"

"The phrase 'the mother' on the eleventh-day letter."

"You think it could be a false designation?"

"I think we need to establish whether it was written by the same person who signed the original maternal identification records."

"That may take time."

"Then take it."

He ended the call.

Outside, the estate was quiet.

Downstairs, Elena was probably preparing for her university interview. Isabelle was likely still working on the scholarship proposal. Nicholas would be looking for another excuse to buy something unnecessary.

Their lives continued.

And somewhere in an archive, an old letter had finally revealed something the families had been missing.

One of the mothers had not remained unaware.

One of them had sent an instruction on the eleventh day.

But the document contained no name.

Only two words.

**THE MOTHER.**

And for the first time, Edward wondered whether the person who had written it had actually been one of the two women they were searching for.
