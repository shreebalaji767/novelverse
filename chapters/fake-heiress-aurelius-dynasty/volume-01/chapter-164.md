# **Chapter 164 — The Fifth Name on the Invitation**

The meeting invitation was not in the migration archive.

It was in the correspondence system of Vale Private Counsel.

Jonathan found it shortly after eleven.

The subject line read:

**RESTRICTED MIGRATION REVIEW — DAY 142**

The invitation had been sent to four named employees and one external adviser.

The four employees were familiar.

Helen Sloane.

Daniel Mercer.

Martin Keane.

Sophie Grant.

The fifth recipient was listed as:

**C. VALE — EXTERNAL ADVISER.**

Edward examined the distribution record.

"Charles was invited."

"Yes."

"Was he present?"

"The attendance sheet suggests someone using those initials attended."

"Can we confirm it was him?"

"Not yet."

Edward opened the invitation attachment.

There was an agenda.

1. Transfer of restricted records.
2. Unresolved appointment statuses.
3. Continuity arrangements during migration.
4. Preservation of original personal-channel procedures.
5. Responsibility for outstanding verification.

Edward looked at the final item.

"That was the meeting where the R-17 status was discussed."

"Most likely."

"Find the minutes."

Jonathan shook his head.

"The archive contains no approved minutes."

"Draft notes?"

"One document."

He placed it on the screen.

It was a single-page handwritten summary, scanned from an original sheet.

The heading read:

**DAY 142 — RESTRICTED MIGRATION DISCUSSION**

Beneath it were five numbered points.

The first three concerned ordinary document transfer.

The fourth stated:

*Unresolved personal appointments must remain unresolved until the original procedure has been delivered and acceptance verified.*

The fifth was different.

*Temporary custody must not be converted into personal authority by migration mapping.*

Edward leaned forward.

"Who wrote this?"

"Unknown."

He examined the handwriting.

The letter forms were consistent with the anonymous instructions found in the earlier correspondence.

Dr Reed later confirmed that the writing was highly consistent with those notes, although the limited sample prevented a definitive identification.

Edward looked at the attendance sheet again.

The anonymous writer had been present.

And they had explicitly warned against the conversion that later occurred.

The meeting notes contained a second page.

It had been filed separately under technical implementation.

This page described the proposed continuity mapping.

*Where the current custodian remains responsible for physical records, the receiving system shall preserve the custody reference.*

That was all.

There was no provision allowing the custodian to represent the protected principal.

No mention of personal communication.

No replacement authority.

Edward placed the page beside the final day-150 configuration.

"The original meeting did not approve the expanded category."

Jonathan nodded.

"The expanded wording appeared later."

"Who requested it?"

"The day-150 configuration request lists Family Allocation Review."

"Not an individual?"

"No."

Edward returned to the meeting notes.

A sentence had been crossed out beneath point five.

*No temporary custody assignment shall be used as evidence of personal appointment.*

The sentence remained readable.

Someone had crossed it out with a single line.

In the margin, another hand had written:

*Retain in original record. Not approved for migration summary.*

Edward stared at the note.

"Two different people."

"Possibly."

"One wrote the warning. Another crossed it out."

"Yes."

"Can we identify the second handwriting?"

Dr Reed was asked to examine the scan.

His preliminary assessment found that the crossed-out sentence and the margin instruction were likely written by the same person.

That writing did not match the anonymous writer.

It also did not match the known handwriting samples from Charles Vale, Daniel Mercer, Martin Keane or Helen Sloane.

The writer was still unidentified.

Edward requested the original meeting file from Vale Private Counsel.

The firm initially responded that the meeting had been classified as an internal migration discussion and that no separate approval record existed.

Edward sent a second request.

He included the attendance sheet, the handwritten notes and the day-150 configuration change.

This time, the firm agreed to produce the remaining material.

The archive delivered three items.

A draft meeting summary.

A technical action list.

And an envelope marked:

**PERSONAL CHANNEL — DO NOT INCLUDE IN GENERAL MIGRATION PACK.**

Edward opened the envelope carefully.

Inside was a one-page note dated day 142.

It read:

*The protected principal's original communication route remains unresolved. No substitute acknowledgement may be accepted until the original procedure has been delivered through a verified channel.*

The note was unsigned.

At the bottom appeared the reference:

**PR-06 / OC-19 / R-17**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"All three."

"Yes."

"Whoever wrote this understood that they were separate but connected."

Jonathan examined the note.

"It doesn't say they are the same person or authority."

"No. It says their procedures intersect."

Edward placed it beside the meeting notes.

The anonymous writer had not been trying to appoint R-17.

They had been trying to prevent the appointment process from moving forward without the original procedure.

The draft meeting summary contained a different account.

It described the meeting as having agreed that all unresolved appointments would be transferred into a continuity category.

It did not mention the warning about temporary custody.

It did not mention the requirement to deliver PR-06.

It did not mention the distinction between OC-19 verification and R-17 personal representation.

The summary ended with:

*Existing administrative arrangements shall remain active until the receiving office completes the migration review.*

Edward read the sentence.

"Who prepared this?"

The document properties identified Daniel Mercer as the author.

"Did he approve it?"

"No signature. It was saved as a draft."

"Was it distributed?"

Jonathan checked the correspondence system.

A version had been sent to four offices.

But it was not identical to the draft.

The distributed copy contained an additional sentence.

*The current document custodian may be retained as responsible continuity authority.*

Edward looked at the revision history.

The sentence had been inserted after the original draft was saved.

The editor field was blank.

"Was the additional sentence in the version Charles received?"

Jonathan checked the distribution record.

"Yes."

"Was it in the version Helen received?"

"No."

"Martin?"

"No."

"Daniel?"

"His archived copy contains the original draft."

Edward leaned back.

"Then the expanded wording was distributed selectively."

Charles Vale agreed to a formal interview.

He arrived shortly after two.

Edward placed the day-142 attendance sheet before him.

"Did you attend this meeting?"

Charles examined it.

"Yes."

"Under what authority?"

"I was acting as temporary document custodian and external liaison for the administrative records."

"Did you participate in the discussion about R-17?"

"I participated in the discussion about unresolved personal appointments."

"Did you agree that temporary custody could become personal representation authority?"

"No."

Edward placed the revised meeting summary before him.

"Did you receive this version?"

Charles read it.

"I received a summary."

"Did you approve it?"

"No."

"Did you know the wording had been expanded?"

"I noticed that the summary did not match the discussion."

"Did you object?"

"I asked for clarification."

"Where is that request?"

Charles looked at the document.

"I believe it was sent to the review desk."

"Do you have a copy?"

"Not with me."

Edward made a note.

"Did you know the day-152 ledger would list you as responsible custodian?"

"No."

"Did you authorise that entry?"

"No."

"Did you act as personal representative for the protected principal?"

Charles looked directly at Edward.

"No."

"Did you ever claim that the principal had personally accepted or refused anything?"

"No."

Edward paused.

"Then why did you allow the continuity arrangement to remain active after Northbridge rejected it?"

Charles folded his hands.

"Because I was instructed to preserve the existing records until the authority question was resolved."

"Who instructed you?"

"The administrative review desk."

"An individual?"

"Not one that I can identify."

"Did you believe the instruction was valid?"

"I believed I was being asked to preserve the records, not to make a personal decision."

Edward studied him.

"Yet the system used your name to maintain a personal representation authority."

"I did not create that authority."

"Did you challenge it?"

Charles was silent for a moment.

"I asked that the wording be corrected."

"When?"

"After the day-152 entry."

"Where is the request?"

"I will provide the copy."

Edward nodded.

"Please do."

The request arrived that evening.

It was dated day 153.

Charles had written:

*I acknowledge temporary custody responsibility for restricted documents. I do not accept appointment as personal representative, independent channel verifier or receiving authority. The current register should not describe document custody as personal representation authority.*

Edward read it twice.

The request was genuine.

It had been received by Family Allocation Review on day 153.

Its status was:

**RECEIVED — INFORMATIONAL ONLY.**

No correction had followed.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Charles objected to the same entry he is accused of benefiting from."

"Yes."

"That doesn't clear him of every action."

"No."

"But it changes the picture."

Jonathan nodded.

"Someone may have used his name without his approval."

"Or his objection may have been made after he had already allowed the arrangement to operate."

"That's also possible."

Edward closed the file.

The evidence was becoming more complicated, not less.

And that was useful.

A convincing investigation did not require every person to fit neatly into the role of victim or culprit.

The next morning, Dr Samuel Reed examined the day-142 meeting notes and the revised summary.

He placed them side by side.

"The anonymous note writer and the person who amended the summary are different individuals."

"How confident are you?" Edward asked.

"Moderately confident from the available samples. I would not make a definitive attribution."

"Can you compare the amendment with the day-150 configuration notes?"

Reed did.

He examined the handwriting on the revised meeting summary, the crossed-out sentence and the margin instruction.

"The summary amendment and the margin instruction may have been written by the same person."

"And the configuration notes?"

"Not enough material for a reliable conclusion."

Edward looked at the page.

The person who had crossed out the warning might have been the person who added the authority wording.

But there was no proof.

Reed continued.

"There is another point."

"What?"

"The anonymous writer's note was not simply a warning. It contains a specific instruction about how the original communication route must be handled."

Edward looked at the sentence again.

*No substitute acknowledgement may be accepted until the original procedure has been delivered through a verified channel.*

Reed tapped the final phrase.

"That is not generic administrative language. It appears to refer to a specific requirement in PR-06."

"Meaning?"

"Whoever wrote it had access to the original procedure or a detailed explanation of its requirements."

Edward considered that.

The anonymous writer knew the procedure.

They knew the distinction between the principal and the representative.

They knew that an acknowledgement could not substitute for personal consent.

And they had attended a meeting where the migration plan was discussed.

"Can you identify them?"

"No."

"Can you narrow the possibilities?"

"Only by establishing who had access to the original procedure before day 142."

Edward nodded.

"Then that's our next task."

At the Aurelius estate, Victoria received a brief update from the independent review team.

The report confirmed that the day-142 migration meeting had discussed unresolved personal appointments and the original communication procedure.

It did not identify the anonymous writer.

It did not identify the protected principal.

It did not establish a family connection.

Victoria read the report twice.

Alexander stood beside the window.

"Anything new?"

"Someone objected to the conversion of temporary custody into personal authority before the migration took place."

"Who?"

"Unknown."

Adrian looked up from his tablet.

"That means the warning existed before the false status was entered."

"Yes."

Damien examined the report.

"Was it ignored?"

"It was omitted from the distributed summary."

Lucien leaned back.

"That's not the same as someone never seeing it."

"No," Victoria said. "It is worse in a different way. Someone saw it and decided it would not be included."

Julian, who had been quietly reading, looked up.

"Could the person who wrote the warning be trying to protect the missing person?"

Victoria considered the question.

"Possibly."

"Then why don't they just tell us who they are?"

"Perhaps they cannot."

"Or perhaps they don't trust anyone."

Alexander turned from the window.

"We cannot build an identity from assumptions. We continue with the evidence."

Julian lowered his eyes to his notebook.

He did not like waiting.

But he had learned that asking what someone wanted was different from deciding what they needed.

Edward returned to the archive that afternoon.

The original PR-06 procedure had a distribution record.

It had been prepared on day 33.

The control copy had been collected by Charles on day 44.

But a preliminary review copy had circulated earlier.

The list contained five recipients.

Dr Samuel Hale.

Eleanor Vale.

Helen Ward.

Thomas Greer.

And an unnamed recipient under the reference:

**ORIGINAL CHANNEL REVIEW — RESTRICTED ADVISER.**

Edward examined the date.

Day 35.

The recipient had received a review copy of the procedure two days after it was prepared.

The delivery record contained a signature.

But the name field was sealed.

Jonathan brought up the access restriction.

"The identity is held in a separate personnel envelope."

"Can we open it?"

"Only with independent authority."

"Who can authorise that?"

"The originating records authority and the independent preservation officer jointly."

Edward checked the current authority list.

The originating records authority was still active.

The independent preservation officer had not been appointed.

He closed the access request.

"Then we don't force it."

Jonathan looked at the signature.

"Should we request an appointment?"

"Yes. But the request must not assume the sealed recipient is R-17 or the anonymous writer."

"Understood."

Edward looked once more at the signature.

It was clear enough to establish that someone had received the procedure before the day-142 meeting.

It was not clear enough to identify them.

The investigation had finally found a person who might explain how the anonymous writer knew the original rules.

But the name remained sealed.

That evening, Edward received a final message from Eleanor.

It contained a scanned copy of an objection she had submitted on day 142.

The subject line was:

**MIGRATION OF UNRESOLVED PERSONAL AUTHORITY**

The objection was short.

*The proposed migration must not convert the absence of a verified appointment into a temporary appointment by default.*

*The document custodian's role must remain limited to preservation.*

*The original communication procedure must be delivered before any person is asked to accept personal representation authority.*

At the bottom was a sentence that Edward had not seen before.

*If the warning is removed from the migration summary, the originating record must remain unchanged and independently accessible.*

Edward read it again.

The warning had been removed from the summary.

But the original record had survived.

Someone had anticipated that possibility.

He called Jonathan.

"Find out who requested that the original meeting notes be preserved separately from the migration summary."

Jonathan checked the archive index.

"There's a preservation request."

"From whom?"

"The request was filed under an anonymous restricted code."

"Which code?"

Jonathan opened the document.

**R.V.**

Edward stared at the screen.

R.V.

The same initials that had appeared on the day-152 preservation exception.

The same reference that had been described as an administrative preservation route, not a receiving authority.

He read the preservation request.

It was dated day 143.

One day after the meeting.

The instruction was simple.

*Retain the original meeting notes, including all crossed-out text and handwritten amendments. No summary shall replace the original record.*

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"R.V. may have preserved the evidence of the disagreement."

"Does that mean R.V. is the anonymous writer?"

"No."

"Could it be?"

"Possibly. But we don't have enough to say that."

Edward opened the original meeting notes again.

The crossed-out warning remained visible.

The anonymous writer had wanted it preserved.

Someone else had wanted it omitted.

And the R.V. preservation request had ensured that both versions survived.

Edward leaned back.

For months, the investigation had treated R.V. as a small administrative exception.

Now it appeared to have been used to protect the original record of a disagreement that had taken place before the false appointment was created.

He looked at the date.

Day 143.

The day before R-17 was recorded as having declined appointment.

Someone had preserved the warning just in time.

**And whoever had done it knew the false status was coming.**
