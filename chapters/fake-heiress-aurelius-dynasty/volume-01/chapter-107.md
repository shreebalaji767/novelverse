# **Chapter 107 — The Instruction Written in Two Different Hands**

Patricia Moore arrived at Edward's office at ten fifteen.

This time, she did not bring her solicitor.

She had asked to review the documents before answering further questions, and her solicitor had agreed to attend by telephone if necessary.

Edward placed the original OR-28 Status Continuity Request on the table.

Patricia looked at it.

“Where did you find this?”

“Northbridge's technical migration archive.”

“I thought that document had been destroyed.”

“Why?”

“Because it was a working copy.”

Edward turned to the first page.

“Did you prepare it?”

“Yes.”

“Did you write the wording?”

Patricia examined the document again.

“I prepared the request.”

“That wasn't my question.”

She looked up.

“I wrote the initial wording.”

Edward pointed to the second section.

**Administrative review closed. Existing Schedule 4 procedure to continue.**

“Did you write this?”

“Yes.”

“Did you understand what it meant?”

“I understood it as an administrative closure.”

“Of the independent review?”

“Of the administrative task associated with OR-28.”

“Was that distinction written anywhere in your request?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because I believed the review had been suspended and the records were being handled under Schedule 4.”

Edward placed the R-04 register beside it.

“This register says the independent review was not completed and no receiving identity custodian had been appointed.”

“I know.”

“This letter says no permanent receiving identity could be entered until the review and receiving authority requirements were satisfied.”

“I know that too.”

“Then why did you prepare a request stating that no further independent review was required?”

Patricia looked down.

“I was told the independent review had been suspended.”

“By whom?”

“Charles Vale.”

“Did he tell you it was permanently cancelled?”

“No.”

“Did he tell you that the receiving identity had been confirmed?”

“No.”

“Then why did you use that wording?”

Patricia was quiet for several seconds.

“Because I thought it referred to the administrative review task, not the independent identity review.”

Edward did not respond immediately.

She continued.

“The two processes had separate references. One was the independent review. The other was the administrative task that kept the documents moving through the system. I believed the administrative task could be closed while the identity review remained unresolved.”

“Did you verify that interpretation?”

“No.”

“Did you ask anyone?”

“I asked Charles.”

“What did he say?”

“He said the review was no longer required for administrative continuity.”

Edward placed the handwritten note beside the document.

“Then why did he write this?”

Patricia read the note.

**Do not issue as identity determination. Temporary administration only.**

“I don't know.”

“Did he add it before or after you submitted the request?”

“I believe afterward.”

“Did you see him write it?”

“No. The document came back with the note.”

“Did you object to the wording?”

“I thought the note clarified the limitation.”

“Did it?”

Patricia looked at the page.

“No.”

Edward moved to the second part of the document.

The approval section had been completed through the existing PM-06 supervisor record.

“Did you personally approve this request?”

“I approved the administrative submission.”

“Did you approve the closure wording?”

“I approved the request as a whole.”

“Then why did you say yesterday that you could not remember approving the exact wording?”

“Because I didn't remember this document.”

“Yet you prepared it.”

“I had prepared hundreds of documents during that period.”

Edward looked at her.

“Do you understand why that explanation is difficult to accept?”

“Yes.”

“Did you know the approval would be reproduced by the migration system?”

“I knew the system would retain the existing approval event.”

“Did you understand that it would make the new status appear to have your approval?”

“I believed the approval applied to the administrative request.”

“Did it apply to the statement that no further independent review was required?”

Patricia hesitated.

“I didn't consider that distinction carefully enough.”

Edward let the admission settle.

It was not a complete explanation.

But it was more than she had given previously.

“Did Charles tell you to use your existing approval?”

“He said the document should be processed through the continuity procedure.”

“Did he specifically tell you to attach PM-06?”

“He told me to use the existing supervisor approval rather than create a new identity determination.”

Edward looked at the handwritten note.

The wording was almost identical.

“Did you know Martin Keane would process the request?”

“No.”

“Did you know he would use the temporary override?”

“No.”

“Did you give him permission to use your credentials?”

“No.”

“Did you know he had retained your credentials?”

“No.”

Edward closed the folder.

“Then we have two separate questions. You prepared and approved the request. Martin processed it using your existing approval. Charles added a restriction that contradicted part of the wording.”

Patricia nodded.

“Yes.”

“Who decided the independent review was no longer required?”

“I don't know.”

“You prepared the sentence.”

“I prepared it based on what I had been told.”

“Who told you?”

“Charles.”

“And he added a note saying it must not be treated as an identity determination.”

“Yes.”

“Did you ask him to clarify the contradiction?”

“No.”

“Why?”

Patricia looked at the table.

“Because I thought the note was the clarification.”

Jonathan placed another document in front of her.

It was the original version of the status request, recovered from a temporary working folder.

Patricia read it.

Her eyes widened slightly.

“This isn't the version I submitted.”

Edward examined her expression.

“What is different?”

She pointed to the second section.

“The original wording.”

The document said:

**Administrative task to remain active for temporary care and preservation purposes. Independent identity review remains outstanding.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

That was not the wording in the final request.

Patricia continued.

“I wrote this.”

“Then what happened?”

“I submitted it for review.”

“Who returned it?”

“Charles's office.”

“Did they explain the changes?”

“No. I received the revised version with a note that the status needed to be closed for continuity.”

“Did you accept the revision?”

“I assumed it had been approved by the legal office.”

“Did you compare the two versions?”

“I must have.”

“Did you notice that the independent review reference had been removed?”

Patricia looked at the original.

“Yes.”

“Then why did you process the revised version?”

She took a breath.

“Because I believed the independent review had been suspended separately, and the administrative closure did not change that.”

Edward pointed to the sentence.

**No further independent review required for administrative continuity.**

“That says the review is not required.”

“It says for administrative continuity.”

“It does not say the review remains outstanding.”

“No.”

“Why didn't you restore the original wording?”

Patricia looked at him.

“I should have.”

For the first time, she sounded less defensive.

She looked tired.

“I should have asked for a formal clarification. I should not have approved the revised version without one.”

Edward nodded slowly.

“Who changed your original document?”

“I don't know.”

“Was it Charles?”

“I received it from his office.”

“Did he tell you he had changed it?”

“No.”

“Did he sign the revision?”

“No. He added the handwritten restriction.”

“Was there anyone else who could have revised it?”

“Several people in the family allocation department had access to the working folder.”

“Names?”

Patricia thought for a moment.

“Daniel Mercer was the department supervisor. There was also a senior legal assistant, but I don't remember whether she was assigned to the file.”

“Did Daniel have authority to alter the wording?”

“He could review and return documents. He could not approve a permanent family allocation.”

“Could he change an administrative status request?”

“Yes.”

Edward made a note.

Another person had entered the process.

The original working document was sent to Dr Mira Sen for examination.

She confirmed that the first version and the revised version had been produced from the same original file, but the wording had been changed between two saved copies.

The first version contained the sentence:

**Independent identity review remains outstanding.**

The second version replaced it with:

**No further independent review required for administrative continuity.**

The revision history showed that the change had been made on day fifty-six.

The user field identified a departmental account.

**FA-ADMIN / REVIEW DESK**

Not Patricia's personal account.

Not Charles's.

Not Martin's.

The account was shared by the family allocation review desk.

Dr Sen could not identify the individual who had made the change.

But she found something else.

The handwritten restriction had been added after the revised document was printed.

Charles's initials appeared beneath it.

The sequence was now clearer.

1. Patricia prepared a document stating that the independent review remained outstanding.
2. Someone at the family allocation review desk changed the wording.
3. The revised document was returned to Patricia's department.
4. Charles added a handwritten restriction that it must not be treated as an identity determination.
5. Patricia approved the administrative submission.
6. Martin used the temporary override to reproduce her existing approval.
7. The resulting status appeared to close OR-28 and remove the requirement for further independent review.

No single step explained the entire alteration.

But together, they had produced a misleading administrative record.

Edward met Daniel Mercer that afternoon.

Daniel was older than when Edward had first interviewed him. He now worked in compliance consultancy and had retained several old employment documents.

When Edward showed him the two versions, Daniel recognised the department account.

“FA-ADMIN was used by the review desk,” he said.

“Did you have access?”

“Yes.”

“Could you change a document?”

“Yes.”

“Did you change this one?”

Daniel studied the pages.

“I don't remember.”

“Did you know the independent review remained outstanding?”

“Yes.”

“Then why was the sentence removed?”

Daniel looked at the revised version.

“I remember the discussion.”

“What discussion?”

“Charles wanted the administrative records closed. He believed the temporary arrangements were already functioning and the independent review was delaying the process.”

“Did he say the family allocations were confirmed?”

“No.”

“Did he say the children had been correctly identified?”

“No.”

“Then why close the review?”

Daniel paused.

“He said the review could be reopened later if the original records became available.”

Edward frowned.

“Was that instruction written?”

“I don't know.”

“Who changed the wording?”

Daniel looked at the document again.

“I did.”

Edward remained still.

“You changed it?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I thought we were closing the administrative task, not cancelling the independent review.”

“Did you write the sentence saying no further independent review was required?”

Daniel nodded.

“I wrote it.”

“Did you understand that it could be interpreted as permanently cancelling the review?”

“I didn't think of it that way.”

“Did you consult Dr Shaw?”

“No.”

“Did you consult Eleanor Vale?”

“No.”

“Did you consult Marianne Cross?”

“No.”

“Did you check the R-04 register?”

“No.”

Edward placed the original version beside the revised one.

“Then you removed a clear warning and replaced it with wording that suggested the review was no longer necessary.”

Daniel looked uncomfortable.

“Yes.”

“Did you know the receiving identity custodian had not been appointed?”

“Yes.”

“Did you know no family notification was authorised?”

“I knew the notification process was incomplete.”

“Then why did you allow the revised wording to proceed?”

Daniel took a long breath.

“Because Charles said the matter needed to be closed administratively. He believed the care arrangements should not remain stuck because of an unfinished review.”

Edward watched him.

“Did you believe the children should remain in temporary care?”

“I believed the children should have stable arrangements.”

“That is not the same answer.”

“No.”

“Did you know the proposed families had not accepted responsibility?”

“I knew the receiving authority field was incomplete.”

“Did you think that mattered?”

Daniel looked down.

“Yes.”

Daniel's statement changed the investigation.

The wording had not been accidentally generated by the migration system.

It had been deliberately revised.

Daniel had changed it.

Patricia had approved the revised submission.

Martin had processed it through the temporary override.

Charles had added a restriction that should have prevented the wording from being used as an identity determination.

And the R-04 register had continued to record the independent review as incomplete.

Edward now had a clearer sequence.

But the most important question remained unanswered.

Why had Charles wanted the administrative task closed so urgently?

The records showed no permanent receiving authority.

The proposed family allocation was incomplete.

The independent review had not occurred.

Yet the administrative process continued.

Edward asked Daniel directly.

“Did Charles ever explain why he was so concerned about closing the records?”

Daniel hesitated.

“He said the arrangements were creating financial and legal exposure.”

“What kind?”

“Temporary accommodation costs. Medical responsibility. Insurance. Custody records. Several institutions were asking who was responsible for the children.”

“Did he say the families had accepted responsibility?”

“He said the arrangements were already understood.”

“By whom?”

“He didn't say.”

“Did he mention the Aurelius Family Office?”

“Yes.”

“What did he say?”

Daniel looked uncomfortable.

“He said the Aurelius office had been informed of the proposed arrangements.”

“Was that true?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you see proof?”

“No.”

Edward placed Helena Brooks's preservation acknowledgement before him.

“This document says the Aurelius Family Office accepted preservation material only and did not accept receiving identity or placement responsibility.”

Daniel read it.

“I hadn't seen this.”

“Did Charles show you a different document?”

“He showed me a summary.”

“What did it say?”

“That the Aurelius office had been notified.”

“Did it say they had accepted?”

“I don't remember.”

Edward closed the folder.

The investigation was no longer only about who altered the records.

It was about why an incomplete arrangement had been pushed forward despite repeated warnings.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena had begun making a list of things she wanted to finish before leaving.

She had completed her academic paperwork.

She had confirmed accommodation.

She had checked her travel documents.

Only a few things remained.

One was a visit to the bookstore.

She wanted to buy a notebook for the programme, preferably one with thick pages and a simple cover.

Isabelle offered to accompany her.

They spent nearly an hour looking through shelves.

Elena finally selected a dark green notebook.

Isabelle picked up a similar one.

“Are you getting this?”

“Maybe.”

“Do you need one?”

“I don't know yet.”

Elena smiled.

“You can always use it for your foundation notes.”

Isabelle examined the cover.

“That's true.”

They continued browsing.

At the checkout, Isabelle looked at Elena's notebook.

“Do you write everything down?”

“Not everything.”

“What do you write?”

“Things I don't want to forget.”

Isabelle looked at the notebook in her hand.

“Does that include important conversations?”

“Sometimes.”

“Even arguments?”

“Especially arguments.”

Isabelle laughed.

“That's dangerous.”

“Only if I remember them accurately.”

They walked out together.

For a few minutes, neither spoke.

Then Isabelle said, “I used to think you had everything.”

Elena glanced at her.

“What do you mean?”

“The house. The family. Everyone's attention. The things I had spent years imagining.”

Elena looked ahead.

“I used to think I had everything too.”

Isabelle slowed slightly.

Elena continued.

“But I don't think either of us understood what the other person was missing.”

Isabelle looked down at the notebook.

“No,” she said quietly. “We didn't.”

They did not resolve everything that afternoon.

They did not need to.

For now, they had managed to speak honestly without turning the conversation into an accusation.

That evening, Edward received the final part of Dr Sen's report.

The revised OR-28 document contained a faint impression beneath the printed text.

It appeared to have been produced by a sheet placed above it when the original document was signed.

The impression revealed a short handwritten instruction.

**Proceed with closure. Receiving identity to be completed separately.**

Edward read it carefully.

The wording seemed to support Daniel's explanation.

The administrative task could be closed while identity remained unresolved.

But the next line changed everything.

**Do not retain the original independent review requirement in the final status record.**

Edward went still.

He read it again.

The instruction had not simply asked for administrative closure.

It had specifically asked for the removal of the outstanding review requirement from the final status record.

He called Jonathan.

“Come here.”

Jonathan arrived and examined the report.

“That's different.”

“Yes.”

“Who wrote it?”

“The impression is incomplete. Dr Sen cannot identify the handwriting yet.”

“Could it be Daniel?”

“Possibly.”

“Charles?”

“Possibly.”

“Patricia?”

“Possibly.”

Edward placed the original and revised documents side by side.

The first version said the independent review remained outstanding.

The second removed that statement.

The handwritten note said the document must not be treated as an identity determination.

And the faint instruction said the original review requirement must not appear in the final status record.

Three different intentions had been recorded on the same document.

Edward looked at the final line.

There was a reference beneath it.

**R-04 / RECIPIENT STATUS — DO NOT DUPLICATE**

He looked up.

“Someone was concerned about the R-04 register.”

Jonathan nodded.

“Because it still showed the review as incomplete.”

“Exactly.”

Edward returned to the R-04 distribution register.

Marianne had preserved the original status.

Someone had altered the Northbridge administrative record.

But the R-04 copy had not been changed.

Which meant the same administrative process had produced two conflicting records.

One said the review remained incomplete.

The other said it was no longer required.

Edward closed the file.

They had finally found evidence that the contradiction was not an accident.

Someone had wanted the official administrative record to stop showing that the independent review was still outstanding.

And someone had taken care to ensure that the original R-04 register was not duplicated into the final record.

The question was no longer merely who had changed the wording.

It was who had wanted the two offices to preserve different versions of the truth.
