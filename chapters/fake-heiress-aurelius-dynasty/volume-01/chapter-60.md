# **Chapter 60 — The Photograph That Named the Wrong Woman**

The photograph was small enough to fit inside an envelope.

Its edges had yellowed.

The image showed a young woman standing outside a building, holding a folder against her chest. She appeared to be in her late twenties. Her hair was tied behind her head, and her expression was serious.

There was nothing particularly unusual about the photograph.

Except for the name written on its back.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

Gabriel Vale held it beneath the reading lamp.

He had not looked at it in years.

For eighteen years, he had kept the photograph without understanding why Charles Vale had given it to him.

Now, after speaking to Edward, he wondered whether the answer had been obvious.

Perhaps the photograph had never been intended to identify a mother.

Perhaps it had been intended to identify a receiving guardian.

But why had Charles written *She must not know*?

And who was the person Margaret Ellison was not supposed to know?

Edward received Gabriel's call shortly after nine.

"I found something."

"What?"

"A photograph."

"Of whom?"

"Margaret Ellison."

Edward immediately reached for his notebook.

"Is it the same Margaret Ellison who received Infant A?"

"I assume so."

"Don't assume. Can you send me a copy?"

"I'll arrange that."

"Was there anything written on it?"

Gabriel hesitated.

"She must not know."

Edward stopped writing.

"Who wrote that?"

"Charles."

"Are you certain?"

"I recognise his handwriting."

"Did he explain what it meant?"

"No."

Edward leaned back.

"Mr. Vale, I need you to understand something. Margaret Ellison has already spoken to us. She has told us that she cared for a baby named Anna. She believed the child was placed with her temporarily."

Gabriel was silent.

"Did she know the baby was Infant A?"

"No. She was never told."

"Did she know the child's biological mother?"

"No."

"Then perhaps the instruction meant she wasn't supposed to know the child's identity."

"Perhaps."

"But why would Charles give you her photograph?"

Gabriel had no answer.

Edward ended the call with a request for the original photograph to be preserved.

Then he called Jonathan.

"We need to revisit Margaret Ellison."

Margaret Ellison received them in her small apartment.

She was considerably older than the woman in the photograph, but Edward recognised her immediately.

He placed the photograph on the table.

Margaret stared at it.

For a long moment, she did not touch it.

"Where did you get this?"

"Gabriel Vale kept it."

Her expression changed.

"Gabriel?"

"You know him?"

"I knew of him."

"Did you meet him eighteen years ago?"

Margaret looked at the photograph again.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"When?"

"At the hospital."

"Why?"

"He was bringing a baby."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

"Was that the child you later called Anna?"

Margaret nodded slowly.

"Yes."

"Did you know him personally?"

"No. Charles introduced us."

"What did he tell you?"

Margaret folded her hands.

"He told me that the baby needed temporary care. He said the hospital had made an error and that the child's identity had to be protected until the records were corrected."

"Did he tell you the baby was Infant A?"

"No."

"Did he tell you the child belonged to the Aurelius family?"

"No."

"Did he mention the Valmont family?"

"No."

Edward studied her.

"Did he tell you who the child's mother was?"

Margaret shook her head.

"He said the mother had been informed that the child was receiving medical care."

"Did you believe him?"

"I wanted to."

"That's not the same thing."

"No," Margaret admitted. "It isn't."

Margaret remembered the first time she had held the child.

She had been living alone in a modest house and had applied for temporary guardianship several months earlier.

She had never expected to receive a newborn so quickly.

When Charles arrived, she was nervous.

Gabriel had been carrying the baby.

He explained the feeding schedule, the medical instructions, and the importance of keeping the infant away from unnecessary visitors.

Margaret asked whether she would be allowed to keep the child permanently.

Gabriel told her he could not answer that.

Charles had then handed her a folder.

Inside were instructions concerning the child's temporary care.

One page had been marked in red.

**DO NOT DISCLOSE THE RECEIVING PARTY'S IDENTITY TO THE BIOLOGICAL FAMILY.**

Margaret had asked why.

Charles replied that it was for the child's safety.

She had accepted the explanation.

Not because she was convinced.

Because the baby was crying, and she had no experience caring for a newborn.

At that moment, she had been more concerned about preparing a bottle than understanding the complicated legal arrangement.

Edward returned to the present.

"Do you remember anything unusual about the day Gabriel brought the child?"

Margaret thought.

"He seemed uncomfortable."

"How?"

"He kept looking at the door."

"Was he expecting someone?"

"I don't know."

"Did he say anything about Charles?"

"He told me Charles had instructed him to deliver the baby directly."

"Did he say why?"

"No."

"Did you see anyone else?"

Margaret looked down.

"Yes."

Edward waited.

"A woman."

"Who?"

"I don't know her name."

"What did she look like?"

"Young. Perhaps thirty. She was standing near the hospital entrance."

"Did she approach you?"

"No. She was watching Gabriel."

"Did Gabriel recognise her?"

"I think so."

"Did he speak to her?"

"Not while I was there."

Edward placed the photograph beside her.

"Was this the woman?"

Margaret examined it.

Then she shook her head.

"No."

Edward frowned.

"You're certain?"

"Yes. This is me."

He looked at her.

"What?"

Margaret pointed to the photograph.

"This photograph was taken before the baby was born. Charles had asked me to provide a copy of my identification photograph for the guardianship application."

Edward looked down.

The woman in the photograph was indeed Margaret Ellison.

He had misunderstood the evidence.

The photograph did not show an unknown woman.

It showed the receiving guardian herself.

"Then why did Charles give Gabriel your photograph?" Jonathan asked.

Margaret considered the question.

"Perhaps he wanted Gabriel to know whom he was delivering the baby to."

"Then what did 'She must not know' mean?"

Margaret looked at the back of the photograph.

"I don't know."

Edward examined the handwriting.

It was definitely Charles Vale's.

But the instruction could refer to several things.

Margaret must not know the child's identity.

Margaret must not know the mother's identity.

Or Margaret must not know something about the person who had arranged the placement.

Edward turned the photograph over.

There was another detail.

A small handwritten number beneath the name.

**17.**

He showed it to Jonathan.

"Does that mean anything?"

Jonathan examined the image.

"Could be an identification number."

Margaret shook her head.

"It wasn't my application number."

Edward looked at her.

"Do you remember what your application number was?"

"Yes. It was 114."

Edward became still.

Box 114.

The restricted archive.

The box that had been reserved for the mother of the children.

He placed the photograph beside the earlier documents.

The number 17 appeared nowhere in the visible records.

But it was too specific to ignore.

At the Valmont Estate, Isabelle was preparing to attend a meeting with Catherine.

The scholarship programme had reached its final review stage.

Elena was helping her organise the evaluation sheets.

"How many applications?" Elena asked.

"Forty-three."

"That's a lot."

"And we can only support twelve in the first year."

Elena examined the criteria.

"Have you decided how to handle applicants who meet the academic requirements but don't qualify financially?"

"They won't be rejected automatically. The programme has separate categories."

"Good."

Isabelle smiled.

"You really do enjoy organising things."

"I enjoy making things understandable."

"That's probably why your notes are always longer than mine."

Elena looked offended.

"My notes are organised."

"They're organised into seven different colours."

"That makes them easier to understand."

Catherine entered.

"Are you two arguing?"

"No," Isabelle replied.

"Yes," Elena said at the same time.

Catherine laughed.

She looked at the evaluation sheets.

"This is excellent work."

Isabelle sat a little straighter.

"Thank you."

"I've spoken to the foundation's trustees. They're impressed with the independent review process."

Isabelle looked pleased.

"But they want you to present the final structure yourself."

"Me?"

"Yes."

Isabelle glanced at Elena.

Elena smiled.

"You can do it."

Isabelle looked back at Catherine.

"I'll prepare."

Catherine nodded.

"Good. And remember, this programme should become something that continues working even when you're no longer supervising it."

Isabelle considered that.

"Then we need a permanent review committee."

"Exactly."

Elena quietly returned to her notes.

She liked seeing Isabelle become involved in something that was genuinely hers.

It made their relationship feel less like an arrangement created by a family crisis and more like an ordinary sisterhood developing through shared work.

The Aurelius investigation had reached a different kind of obstacle.

Adrian discovered that the number 17 appeared in an old Family Office document.

It was a reference to a confidential identity category.

**CATEGORY 17 — TEMPORARY RECEIVING PARTY, NO FAMILY AFFILIATION.**

Alexander read the entry.

"How many such categories existed?"

"Twenty-four."

"Who created them?"

"The former Family Affairs division."

"Under whose authority?"

Adrian looked at the document.

"Charles Vale's legal office submitted the original classification system."

Alexander's expression darkened.

"Why was an external legal office defining our family identity categories?"

"Because the Family Office had outsourced part of its confidential records management."

"Who approved that?"

"The approval bears your authority code."

Alexander stood.

"I never approved it."

Victoria looked at Adrian.

"Could the code have been copied?"

"Yes."

"Then we need to know who had access."

Adrian placed another document on the table.

"The category was used for Infant A."

Alexander looked at it.

"So the photograph's number 17 was not arbitrary."

"No."

Damien examined the record.

"Does Category 17 identify the receiving guardian?"

"Not directly. It identifies the type of placement."

Lucien leaned back.

"Then the person who received Infant A was officially treated as someone with no family affiliation."

Victoria looked at the document.

"Someone outside both families."

Adrian nodded.

Edward requested the original Category 17 register.

The archive initially returned only a summary.

He insisted on the underlying records.

After several hours, an old classification ledger was located.

It contained twenty-four categories.

Most were routine.

Temporary foster placements.

Medical observation.

Confidential legal custody.

Emergency accommodation.

But Category 17 was different.

Its description read:

**PRIVATE RECEIVING GUARDIAN — IDENTITY KNOWN TO ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE ONLY.**

Below it was a list of approved case references.

The first entry was:

**EFO-SL/07 — INFANT A.**

The second was dated four days later.

**EFO-SL/07 — INFANT B.**

Edward looked at the page.

Both children had been placed under the same confidential category.

That meant the arrangement was not created for only one infant.

It had been designed to accommodate both.

He read the next line.

**RECEIVING PARTY INFORMATION TO BE RETAINED OUTSIDE THE FAMILY OFFICE.**

Jonathan leaned closer.

"Where?"

Edward pointed to the final column.

**ORIGINAL REGISTER: VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL — ARCHIVE 17.**

Edward immediately contacted the archive.

The response was discouraging.

Archive 17 had been transferred to an external storage company seventeen years earlier.

The storage company had later been acquired.

Its records were now held by a document-management firm.

Edward requested the transfer inventory.

It arrived the next morning.

The inventory contained several boxes.

Most were ordinary legal files.

One was described as:

**CATEGORY 17 — RECEIVING PARTY IDENTITIES.**

But its status was marked:

Missing from physical inventory

The last recorded movement had occurred eighteen years earlier.

It had been signed out by Gabriel Vale.

Edward returned to Gabriel's care facility.

This time, Gabriel was expecting him.

"You found the category register," the old man said.

"Yes."

"And the missing archive?"

"Your signature is on the transfer record."

Gabriel nodded.

"I took it."

"Where is it?"

"I don't have it."

Edward studied him.

"Who did you give it to?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"Margaret Voss."

"Why?"

"Because she was responsible for monitoring the placements."

"Did she receive it?"

"Yes."

"Where did she keep it?"

"I don't know."

Edward placed the transfer record on the table.

"Why did you remove it from the archive?"

Gabriel took a long breath.

"Because Charles Vale wanted it destroyed."

"Why?"

"Because the register contained the names of the people who received the children."

Edward leaned forward.

"And you wanted to preserve those names?"

"I wanted someone to be able to find the children."

"Then why didn't you give it to the families?"

Gabriel looked away.

"Because the register also contained the names of people who had been promised that the children would never be taken from them."

Edward remained silent.

Gabriel continued.

"I didn't know which promise was legitimate. I didn't know which person had been told the truth. I didn't know whether returning either child would put her in danger."

"Did you know where the register went after Margaret received it?"

"No."

"Did you ask her?"

"Yes."

"What did she say?"

Gabriel's expression became distant.

"She told me that the original register had to remain separate from the children's records."

"Why?"

"Because she believed one of the names would reveal who had arranged the entire transfer."

Edward looked at him.

"Whose name?"

Gabriel shook his head.

"She never told me."

That evening, Margaret Voss received a call from Edward.

He asked whether she still possessed the Category 17 register.

She did not answer immediately.

Then she said:

"Yes."

Edward sat upright.

"Where?"

"Not here."

"Where is it?"

"Somewhere safe."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because it contains more than the names of the receiving guardians."

"What else?"

"Instructions from the person who arranged the placements."

Edward paused.

"Do you know who that person was?"

Margaret's voice became quieter.

"I know the name written in the register."

"Then tell me."

"No."

"Mrs. Voss—"

"I said I know the name. I didn't say I know whether it is genuine."

Edward waited.

Margaret continued.

"That register was prepared using the authority of the Aurelius Family Office. But the person who signed the original instruction was not Alexander Aurelius."

"Who was it?"

She hesitated.

Then she answered.

**"Victoria Aurelius."**

Edward went completely still.

"That's impossible. Victoria has denied authorising any child transfers."

"I know."

"Then the signature must be forged."

"Perhaps."

"Or someone used her name."

"Perhaps."

Edward gripped the telephone.

"Do you have proof?"

Margaret was silent for several seconds.

Then she said:

"I have the original signature."

And before Edward could ask another question, she added:

"But I also have a document proving that Victoria Aurelius was not the person who signed it."

The call ended.

Edward remained seated.

For eighteen years, the Aurelius family had believed someone had used Alexander's authority to conceal their missing daughter.

Now a new document suggested that someone had also used Victoria's name.

And Margaret Voss had evidence that the signature itself was not genuine.
