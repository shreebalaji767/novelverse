# **Chapter 112 — The Instruction Kept Outside the Register**

The sealed personal-instruction archive had not appeared in any of the earlier inventories.

It was not part of the ordinary legal correspondence collection.

It was not included in the Family Office preservation records.

And it was not held with the R-04 trust documents.

It belonged to Vale Private Counsel.

More precisely, it belonged to the office of Charles Vale.

Edward read the archive description.

**PERSONAL INSTRUCTIONS — PRINCIPAL COUNSEL**

**ACCESS: PRINCIPAL COUNSEL OR SPECIFICALLY AUTHORISED SUCCESSOR**

**DUPLICATION: PROHIBITED**

**TRANSFER: REQUIRES INDIVIDUAL INVENTORY**

The final line had been added in a different typeface.

**PRIVATE INSTRUCTIONS ARE NOT TO BE INCORPORATED INTO GENERAL CLIENT CORRESPONDENCE WITHOUT EXPRESS AUTHORISATION.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Who controlled the archive after Charles left?”

“The successor firm inherited the physical collection. But the access authority remained restricted.”

“Who is the authorised successor?”

“According to the current register, nobody.”

Edward looked again at the description.

“Then how did the archive survive the closure of Vale Private Counsel?”

“The successor firm retained it as a protected legal collection. They have not opened it.”

“Did Charles leave an instruction concerning its future access?”

“Apparently.”

Jonathan placed another document on the desk.

It was a transfer certificate.

The archive had been sealed on day 174, several months after the original infant-placement records had been moved to Unit Six.

The certificate listed four boxes.

Only three were physically present.

The fourth had been transferred separately under a reference:

**PI-07 / PERSONAL INSTRUCTION 07.**

Edward stared at the missing item.

“Where was it sent?”

“The certificate says private custody.”

“Whose custody?”

“No name.”

“Then we have a problem.”

Jonathan nodded.

“The successor firm has no record of receiving it.”

Edward closed the certificate.

“Find the courier record.”

The courier record was recovered from an external storage company.

It showed that Box Four had been collected from Vale Private Counsel on day 175.

The collection receipt contained a delivery reference but no personal name.

**DESTINATION: PRIVATE RESIDENTIAL CUSTODY — V-17.**

Edward read it twice.

V-17.

The same private residential property where Annex C Part Two and Schedule 4 had been stored.

The same property connected to Charles Vale.

“Was the box included in the V-17 property inventory?”

Jonathan searched the records.

“No.”

“Then how do we know it arrived?”

“There is a separate acknowledgement.”

“Signed by whom?”

Jonathan opened the image.

The signature was partly obscured by a fold.

But the printed name beneath it was clear.

**ARTHUR BELL.**

Edward recognised the name.

Arthur had been the former caretaker at V-17.

He had previously confirmed that a sealed administrative package had been kept in the restricted west corridor.

“Contact him.”

Arthur Bell agreed to meet them at a small office near the former estate grounds.

He was an elderly man now, with a careful manner and an excellent memory for details that seemed insignificant to other people.

When Edward showed him the collection receipt, Arthur examined it.

“I remember this box.”

“What was inside?”

“I wasn't told.”

“Who delivered it?”

“A courier.”

“Did you sign for it?”

“Yes. I was instructed to acknowledge delivery, not open it.”

“Who gave you that instruction?”

“Mr Vale.”

“Charles?”

Arthur nodded.

“Did he explain why it was being kept at V-17?”

“He said it was personal legal correspondence and should not be included in the ordinary property inventory.”

“Was it stored in the west corridor?”

“No. It was kept in a separate locked cabinet in the administrative room.”

“Who had access?”

“Mr Vale. His solicitor. And one other person.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Who?”

Arthur hesitated.

“I don't remember the name.”

“Man or woman?”

“A woman.”

“Did she visit regularly?”

“Only twice.”

“Did she carry anything?”

Arthur looked at the photograph of the brown leather case that Edward had shown him previously.

“Yes.”

“The brown leather case?”

Arthur nodded slowly.

“She carried a case like that.”

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

“Did she open the box?”

“No.”

“Did she collect it?”

“No. She brought another document.”

“What document?”

“A sealed envelope.”

Arthur thought for a moment.

“It had a number written on it. I remember because Mr Vale told me not to place it with the other papers.”

“What number?”

“Seven.”

Edward remained still.

“Private Instruction 07?”

Arthur looked uncertain.

“That may have been it.”

“Did the woman speak to Charles?”

“Yes.”

“Do you remember what they discussed?”

“Not much. But I remember one sentence.”

“What sentence?”

“Arthur's eyes moved toward the window.”

“She said, ‘If you keep the instruction separate, you must also keep the consequences separate.’”

Edward wrote it down.

“Did Charles answer?”

Arthur nodded.

“He said, ‘The instruction is not the decision.’”

Edward looked up.

“Anything else?”

Arthur shook his head.

“No.”

The exchange was not enough to establish what the document contained.

But it suggested that the woman with the brown leather case had known about Private Instruction 07.

And she had understood that it was separate from the decisions being made elsewhere.

“Do you know who she was?”

“No.”

“Did Charles ever identify her?”

“No. He referred to her as the records representative.”

Edward closed his notebook.

“Thank you, Mr Bell.”

As they stood to leave, Arthur spoke again.

“There was something else.”

Edward turned.

“After the woman left, Mr Vale told me that the box must remain sealed even if someone asked for it.”

“Who might ask?”

Arthur shook his head.

“He didn't say.”

“Did anyone ask?”

“Yes.”

Edward stopped.

“When?”

“Several weeks later.”

“Who?”

“A man from the Family Office.”

“Name?”

“I don't remember. But he had a document from Mr Vale's office.”

“Did he collect the box?”

“No. Mr Vale's instruction was still in force.”

“Did the man leave anything?”

Arthur nodded.

“A written request. I put it in the cabinet with the box.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Was that request included in the V-17 inventory?”

“No.”

Arthur shook his head.

“The cabinet was cleared years later. I gave everything to the solicitor who arranged the property closure.”

Edward made a note.

“Do you remember the solicitor's name?”

Arthur did.

And that name would become their next lead.

At the Aurelius residence, Alexander received an update from Helena Brooks.

“The old EFO-SL/07 access records are being reconciled,” she said. “We have identified several instances where the shared liaison account was used to process requests without an attached originating authority approval.”

“Gabriel's day-37 entry?”

“Yes.”

“Has he been interviewed?”

“Edward has arranged that.”

Alexander looked at the report.

“Does it establish that Gabriel knowingly withheld the reports?”

“No. It establishes that he manually changed the status to disclosure deferred.”

“Then we must not treat the action as proof of intent.”

Helena nodded.

“There is another issue. The personal instruction archive at Vale Private Counsel appears to have been transferred to V-17.”

Alexander looked up.

“Was it part of the original investigation?”

“No. It was discovered through the day-37 cross-reference.”

“Why wasn't it included in the earlier property inventory?”

“Because it was classified as private legal correspondence.”

Alexander was silent for a moment.

“Was Charles entitled to keep documents relating to the children outside the formal records?”

“Not if those documents were part of the identity or placement process.”

“Do we know whether they were?”

“Not yet.”

Alexander closed the report.

“Then we need the original document.”

He looked at Helena.

“And we need to know who else had access.”

The following morning, Edward contacted the solicitor who had handled the final V-17 property closure.

The solicitor was retired but agreed to review his archived records.

His name was Thomas Greer.

He arrived with two boxes of old property documentation.

“I remember the clearance,” he said. “It was unusual.”

“How?”

“Most of the documents at V-17 were transferred to the family archive. But a small number were excluded because they were described as restricted legal material.”

“Were they destroyed?”

“No.”

“Where were they transferred?”

“One box went to the successor firm. Another was collected by a private records representative.”

“Do you know the representative?”

Greer opened a file.

“The receipt says the representative acted under authority from Charles Vale.”

Edward placed the day-175 collection record in front of him.

“Was this the same transfer?”

Greer examined it.

“Yes.”

“Did you see the contents?”

“No.”

“Did you see the request to collect it?”

Greer opened another folder.

“I believe this is the relevant document.”

It was a one-page instruction.

The heading read:

**PRIVATE INSTRUCTION 07 — ACCESS CONTROL**

The text stated:

**The sealed instruction shall remain outside the general identity file until the receiving authority has been independently appointed.**

**No person acting solely under originating, medical, legal, or administrative authority may open it.**

**Access requires the receiving authority and the originating representative to be present together.**

Edward read the second paragraph.

“Who wrote this?”

Greer shook his head.

“It was prepared by Charles's office. I don't know who drafted it.”

“Was it signed?”

“Yes.”

Edward examined the signature.

It was Charles Vale's.

But there was another line beneath it.

**Acknowledged for preservation: Eleanor Vale.**

The document had been signed by both.

Edward looked at the date.

Day 34.

The same day as the original OR-28 review request.

“Was this instruction sent to R-04?”

Greer checked the attachment list.

“No. It was sent to V-17 for restricted custody.”

“Why?”

“The document itself says it was not to be incorporated into the general identity file.”

Edward read it again.

The instruction did not authorise anyone to decide which child belonged to which family.

It did something else.

It prevented the sealed instruction from being opened by one side alone.

The receiving authority and originating representative had to be present together.

But the receiving authority had never been appointed.

Which meant the instruction could remain sealed indefinitely.

Edward looked at Greer.

“Was this the only copy?”

“I believe so.”

“Who received the original?”

“The V-17 custodian.”

“Arthur Bell?”

“Yes.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Then the box may still be there.”

Jonathan shook his head.

“The property was cleared years ago.”

“Where was the cabinet transferred?”

Greer checked his notes.

“The contents were moved to a private records facility.”

“Which one?”

Greer turned to the final page.

A location code appeared.

**NORTHBRIDGE ARCHIVAL SERVICES — PRIVATE HOLDING 7.**

Edward slowly set the paper down.

Northbridge.

The same name had appeared in the infant placement records, the original neonatal comparison, and the later financial instruction.

It might have been a coincidence.

It might have been a separate business using the same name.

He would not assume they were connected.

But the facility's private holding reference was unmistakable.

“Jonathan, contact Northbridge Archival Services.”

Elena's departure was now only a few days away.

Her final documents had been confirmed.

Her accommodation had been allocated.

The programme office had sent a reminder about orientation.

She had also completed a first draft of the project proposal she intended to develop during her stay.

She was sitting with Isabelle in the library when her phone vibrated.

Isabelle glanced at the screen.

“Clara?”

Elena smiled.

“Yes.”

“She's probably checking whether you've packed.”

“She has already asked three times.”

Isabelle returned to the book in front of her.

After a moment, she asked, “Are you nervous?”

Elena looked up.

“A little.”

“About the programme?”

“About everything being unfamiliar.”

Isabelle nodded.

“I think I'd be nervous too.”

Elena closed her notebook.

“You're applying to universities soon.”

“Yes.”

“That's unfamiliar too.”

“Not in the same way.”

Elena considered her.

“Maybe not. But you won't know what it's like until you go.”

Isabelle smiled faintly.

“Are you telling me to stop worrying?”

“I'm telling you to prepare properly and then stop worrying about things you can't control.”

“That sounds suspiciously like advice.”

“It is.”

Isabelle laughed.

The silence that followed was comfortable, if still cautious.

Then Isabelle looked at Elena's project notes.

“Can I ask you something?”

“Of course.”

“Why did you choose grant management?”

Elena considered the question.

“Because I noticed how much time small organisations spend managing applications manually. They have limited staff, inconsistent records, and sometimes no reliable way to track where the money goes.”

“You want to build software for them?”

“Eventually. First, I want to understand the actual problems. I don't want to build something that looks impressive but doesn't help anyone.”

Isabelle nodded.

“That makes sense.”

Elena closed the notebook.

“What about you?”

“Economics, probably. I want to understand how organisations make decisions, especially foundations and public programmes.”

“Then perhaps you can review my budget model.”

Isabelle raised an eyebrow.

“You'd let me?”

“Why not?”

For a moment, Isabelle looked surprised.

Then she smiled.

“I'd like that.”

Neither of them made a grand promise.

Neither pretended that everything between them had been repaired.

But they had found something they could discuss without returning to the past.

For now, that was enough.

The response from Northbridge Archival Services arrived late that afternoon.

The facility confirmed that Private Holding 7 had existed.

However, the storage contract had been closed years earlier.

Its records indicated that a sealed box had been transferred to another location under a private preservation instruction.

The new destination was listed as:

**MERIDIAN RECORDS AND PRESERVATION SERVICES.**

Edward stared at the name.

Meridian.

The facility that had held the IV-204 records.

The facility that had issued the replacement M-204 band.

The facility where several of the earliest infant transfer records had been recovered.

The same organisation might have held the private instruction box for years.

Jonathan read the response.

“Do you think it's the same Meridian?”

“We verify before assuming.”

Jonathan checked the corporate details.

“It is.”

Edward looked up.

“Same legal entity?”

“Yes. The archival service was operated under the same holding company. Its private storage division handled the transfer.”

“Then we request the custody records.”

Jonathan began preparing the request.

Edward returned to the instruction.

The receiving authority and originating representative had to be present together.

No one had ever been appointed as receiving authority.

But someone had preserved the document anyway.

Why?

Because it might contain information that could not safely be disclosed to only one side.

Or because someone had wanted to prevent a unilateral decision.

Edward looked again at the date.

Day 34.

The same day Charles and Eleanor had signed the access restriction.

The same day the OR-28 review was initiated.

And the same day the private instruction was sealed.

There was another line at the bottom of the document.

It had not been included in Greer's summary.

Edward enlarged the scan.

**The sealed instruction concerns the proposed recipient, not the identity of the originating child.**

He read it slowly.

The document was not necessarily about identifying the biological child.

It concerned the proposed recipient.

That meant the person who opened it would learn something about the intended receiving arrangement.

But it still would not prove that the child had been correctly identified.

Edward closed the file.

“Jonathan, request the original box from Meridian.”

“Under whose authority?”

“Preservation and custody review. No opening until we have verified the original seal and the access conditions.”

Jonathan nodded.

“And if the box is intact?”

Edward looked at the final paragraph.

“Then we find out who the instruction was meant to protect.”

That evening, the Aurelius family received a short update.

The private instruction had been found.

It remained sealed.

It concerned a proposed recipient rather than a biological identity.

And access required the presence of both an originating representative and a receiving authority.

Victoria read the report twice.

“So we still cannot open it.”

“Not yet,” Alexander said.

“Because no receiving authority was ever appointed.”

“Yes.”

Victoria looked at the document's final line.

“Then whoever wrote this expected the process to reach a point where both sides could meet.”

“Apparently.”

“And it never did.”

Alexander did not answer.

Victoria looked at him.

“Could this have been intended to protect the child?”

“It may have been.”

“Or the family receiving the child?”

“Possibly.”

She lowered her eyes.

For eighteen years, they had been searching for their daughter.

Now they were discovering that someone had built a process around a child whose identity had never been properly established.

They still did not know whether that child was Elena.

They still did not know whether the proposed recipient was the Aurelius family.

But the records were beginning to reveal that someone had anticipated a dangerous mistake.

And someone else had allowed the process to continue despite the warning.

Victoria folded the report.

“I want the original preserved.”

“It will be.”

“And I want the investigation to continue without forcing an answer.”

Alexander nodded.

“That is the only way we can be certain.”

At Meridian Records and Preservation Services, a preservation supervisor located the old transfer box the following morning.

The original label was still attached.

**PRIVATE INSTRUCTION 07**

**DO NOT OPEN WITHOUT JOINT AUTHORITY**

The box was stored in a separate cabinet.

Its seal was intact.

But the supervisor found something unexpected in the custody file.

A second receipt had been attached to the transfer record.

It was dated day 61.

The same day Annex C Part One, Part Two, and Schedule 4 had entered Unit Six.

The receipt stated:

**Supplementary document deposited separately.**

**Do not attach to existing identity file.**

The signature belonged to Eleanor Vale.

Beneath it was a handwritten note.

**The receiving authority remains unappointed. If the proposed recipient is not verified, this instruction must remain unopened.**

Edward read the scan.

Then he noticed a second line written beneath Eleanor's note.

It was in a different hand.

**The proposed recipient has already been notified.**

There was no signature.

No date.

And no supporting receipt.

Edward stared at the statement.

Someone had claimed that the proposed recipient had already been notified.

But the R-04 records showed no valid notification.

Susan Bell had refused the allocation.

The receiving authority had never been appointed.

And Eleanor had insisted that the instruction remain sealed.

Edward slowly set the scan down.

The mystery was no longer simply who had been told.

It was whether someone had been using the claim of notification to justify opening a document that no one had the authority to open.

And if that was true, the unsigned statement might be the most dangerous line in the entire file.
