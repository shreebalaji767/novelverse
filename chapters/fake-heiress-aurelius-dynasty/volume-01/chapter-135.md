# **Chapter 135 — The Follow-Up That Was Never Allowed**

The reference PO-04/CONTROL-44 did not appear in the ordinary Personal Instructions Division index.

Jonathan found it in a separate correspondence register maintained by Vale Private Counsel.

The entry was brief.

**Day 44 — Personal Representation Control**

**Subject:** Follow-up delivery request.

**Previous action:** Delivery incomplete.

**Visitor position:** Willing to receive correspondence following verification.

**New action:** Contact suspended.

**Reason:** Proposed receiving arrangement unresolved.

**Authorising office:** Originating Office / Personal Representation.

There was no individual name.

No signature.

No appointment reference.

Edward read the entry again.

“The visitor asked for another attempt, and someone suspended it because the receiving arrangement was unresolved.”

Jonathan nodded.

“That's what the record says.”

“Was the person informed?”

“No notification appears.”

“Was the courier informed?”

Jonathan checked the delivery service register.

“Nora received a cancellation notice on day 45.”

“Who sent it?”

“Personal Instructions Division.”

“Did she receive an explanation?”

“Only that the assignment had been withdrawn.”

Edward looked at the date.

Day 45.

The same day Eleanor had written that P-04 had neither accepted nor refused the proposed arrangement.

Someone had ignored that distinction and stopped the next attempt.

“Find the original cancellation notice,” Edward said.

Nora Whitcomb remembered receiving it.

“It was a short message,” she told Edward. “The assignment had been cancelled. I was told to return any remaining delivery materials.”

“Did you ask why?”

“Yes.”

“What were you told?”

“That the protected contact was no longer available.”

“Did you believe that?”

“I had no reason to question the administrative notice.”

“Did you know the visitor had requested another appointment?”

“No.”

“Would you have accepted another assignment?”

“Of course. I had not completed the first one.”

Edward showed her the day-44 follow-up record.

Nora read it carefully.

“I never saw this.”

“Does the wording surprise you?”

“Yes. I thought the assignment had been cancelled because the destination was unavailable. This says the visitor was willing to receive the correspondence.”

“Did you keep any notes from the cancellation?”

Nora opened her folder.

“I kept the original assignment and the return receipt. I also kept the cancellation message because the courier company required a reason for closing an incomplete delivery.”

She handed it to Edward.

The message contained two sentences.

*Assignment PO-04 is withdrawn.*

*No further delivery action is required.*

There was no mention of the visitor's request.

Edward placed it beside the day-44 register.

“Someone removed the reason from the courier's copy.”

Nora frowned.

“Why would they do that?”

Edward did not answer.

He was beginning to understand that the investigation was no longer about a single missing document.

It was about the sequence of decisions that had prevented the document from reaching its intended recipient.

The original cancellation record had been processed through the Personal Instructions Division.

Its audit trail identified a department account rather than an individual.

But the approval field contained a reference to a paper instruction.

**PI-07 / CONTROL AMENDMENT — DAY 44.**

Edward requested the original.

The archive initially returned only a scanned cover page.

It showed that a control amendment had been prepared on day 44 and distributed to three offices.

- Personal Instructions Division.
- Vale Private Counsel.
- Cross Administrative Services.

The main instruction was missing.

Jonathan examined the distribution list.

“Why was this sent to Marianne?”

“She coordinated the correspondence.”

“But the instruction concerned whether P-04 could be contacted.”

“Exactly.”

Edward looked at the distribution stamp.

There was another recipient.

**Eleanor Vale — Personal Review.**

“Did Eleanor receive a copy?”

“Apparently.”

“Then she may know what was changed.”

Eleanor remembered the amendment.

She retrieved her own copy from a locked correspondence cabinet.

It was a two-page instruction.

The first page contained the revised procedure.

**Personal Representation Control — Amendment 04**

*Pending completion of the proposed receiving arrangement, all further contact with the protected principal shall be suspended unless separately authorised by the originating office.*

The second page contained a list of permitted administrative actions.

- Preserve original correspondence.
- Maintain existing temporary care arrangements.
- Retain representative's conditional response.
- Do not issue further contact instructions.
- Do not treat the absence of a personal response as consent or refusal.

Edward read the final bullet twice.

“Who wrote this?”

Eleanor looked at the signature field.

“Charles authorised the distribution.”

“Did he author the wording?”

“I cannot say. The drafting record is missing.”

“Did you object?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because the instruction made further contact dependent on the resolution of the receiving arrangement. But the receiving arrangement could not be resolved without the principal's response.”

Edward placed the day-44 follow-up request beside it.

“The visitor had already asked for another attempt.”

“I know.”

“Did you know that when you received this amendment?”

Eleanor nodded.

“I had seen the delivery report. I knew the first attempt had failed because the verification key was not provided.”

“Did you know the visitor had requested another appointment?”

“Yes.”

“Then why didn't you challenge the suspension immediately?”

“I did.”

She removed another letter.

It was dated day 45.

*The proposed arrangement cannot be resolved by withholding the information required for the principal's response. The delivery failure must not be treated as a refusal. A further attempt should be arranged once the verification reference is available.*

Edward examined the letter.

“Did you send it to Charles?”

“Yes. I also sent a copy to the Personal Instructions Division.”

“Did they respond?”

“No.”

“Did you have authority to override the amendment?”

“No.”

“Could you release the verification key yourself?”

“No. I had checked its preparation, not its release authority.”

Eleanor looked at him.

“I could identify the problem. I could preserve evidence of it. But I could not appoint myself as the person authorised to resolve it.”

Edward understood.

Eleanor had been able to challenge the process, but not to replace the missing authority.

“What happened after your letter?”

Eleanor opened a final folder.

“Something else was recorded.”

The document was a day-47 administrative summary.

It stated:

**Personal Representation Status: Administrative Continuity Maintained.**

**Principal Response: Not Required for Temporary Care Continuity.**

**Further Contact: Suspended.**

**Receiving Authority: Pending Appointment.**

Edward examined the wording.

“Did you approve this?”

“No.”

“Did you receive it?”

“Yes.”

“Did you challenge it?”

“I sent a correction.”

She showed him the correction.

*Temporary care continuity does not establish personal consent. The principal's response remains outstanding. Further contact has not been completed.*

The correction had been received.

But the summary had not been amended.

Edward looked at the dates.

“Who authorised the day-47 status?”

“Charles's office.”

“Did you ever see a separate decision?”

“No.”

“Was there a meeting?”

“Not that I attended.”

“Was P-04 represented?”

“Not to my knowledge.”

Edward placed the documents together.

The administrative summary had not explicitly claimed that P-04 agreed.

But it had allowed the temporary arrangement to continue while the personal response remained uncollected.

The practical result was the same.

The people arranging the process could continue acting.

The person whose response was required remained unheard.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria read Eleanor's correction.

She was quiet for a long time.

“Did anyone tell the principal that contact had been suspended?” she asked.

“We have no evidence of that,” Edward replied.

“Did anyone tell the person why the first delivery failed?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell them that another attempt had been requested?”

“No.”

Victoria looked at Alexander.

“Then the person might have believed the matter had simply disappeared.”

Alexander's expression was grave.

“Or they might have tried to contact someone themselves.”

Edward nodded.

“That's another possibility.”

“Could there be a record of that?”

“We haven't found one.”

Victoria returned to the day-43 note.

*Please tell whoever sent you that I cannot answer a question I have not been allowed to read.*

She placed it carefully on the table.

“If that person was P-04, they were asking for the information.”

“Yes.”

“And the next day, someone stopped the follow-up.”

“Yes.”

Victoria looked towards the garden.

“I hope they weren't left believing that their answer didn't matter.”

In Singapore, Elena was preparing for a review session with her project supervisor.

She had added the unsuccessful verification procedure and tested it with several sample cases.

One example involved a grant applicant whose identity documents had expired.

The system did not mark the applicant as ineligible.

Instead, it recorded the verification attempt, requested updated documents, and kept the application open for a defined period.

Daniel examined the result.

“So an incomplete check doesn't automatically close the application.”

“Correct.”

“What happens if the person doesn't respond?”

“Then the authorised reviewer can decide what happens next, based on the actual policy. The system doesn't invent a refusal.”

Professor Lin joined them.

“That's an important principle. Administrative systems should record decisions, not manufacture them.”

Elena saved the final changes.

She felt a quiet satisfaction.

The project had begun as a way to make a complicated process easier to manage.

Now it was teaching her how much responsibility could be hidden inside an ordinary status field.

That afternoon, Jonathan found a second copy of the day-44 control amendment.

It had been retained in the Northbridge Continuity Trust records.

The copy differed from Eleanor's version.

The first page was identical.

The second page, however, contained an additional paragraph.

*Where personal contact remains suspended, the communication representative may provide any necessary response for administrative continuity, subject to existing authority limitations.*

Edward read it carefully.

“That's not in Eleanor's copy.”

“No.”

“Was it in the original distribution?”

Jonathan checked the page reference.

“The additional paragraph appears only in the copy held by Northbridge.”

“Who sent it?”

“The Family Allocation Review office.”

“Who authorised the change?”

Jonathan enlarged the bottom margin.

There was a handwritten reference.

**PR-06 / REPRESENTATIVE RESPONSE SUFFICIENT FOR CONTINUITY.**

Edward's expression hardened.

“Gabriel's response.”

Jonathan nodded.

“Someone used his conditional response to support the continuation of temporary arrangements.”

“But the paragraph says subject to existing authority limitations.”

“Yes.”

“Which means it doesn't grant him any additional authority.”

Jonathan examined the date.

The Northbridge copy had been received on day 48.

Two days after the day-47 administrative summary.

Edward leaned forward.

“Find out who added the paragraph.”

Daniel Mercer was the first person they contacted.

He remembered the Northbridge distribution.

“I processed a copy of the control amendment,” he said.

“Did you add the paragraph?”

“No.”

“Did you approve it?”

“I don't remember approving any additional wording.”

“Who prepared the copy?”

“The Family Allocation Review office received the amendment and sent an administrative extract to Northbridge.”

“Why?”

“To confirm that temporary care arrangements could continue while the receiving authority remained unappointed.”

“Did Northbridge request that confirmation?”

“Yes. Dr Hale wanted written clarification before processing further temporary-care payments.”

“Did the clarification mention Gabriel's response?”

Daniel considered the document.

“I don't remember seeing that paragraph.”

Edward placed Eleanor's original copy beside the Northbridge version.

“It's not in the original.”

Daniel studied the additional wording.

“Then someone added it to the distribution extract.”

“Who?”

“I can't identify the person from this copy.”

“Could you retrieve the outgoing document history?”

Daniel nodded.

“I can request it.”

Edward closed the folder.

“Do that.”

The outgoing document history arrived the following morning.

It showed that the Northbridge extract had been assembled from two separate files.

The first was Eleanor's original day-44 amendment.

The second was a PR-06 communication summary.

The summary contained Gabriel's conditional response.

A third document had been used as a cover instruction.

**NORTHBRIDGE CONTINUITY — TEMPORARY CARE AUTHORISATION**

Its author was listed as the Family Allocation Review office.

But the attachment history revealed something else.

The paragraph about representative response had been copied from a separate document titled:

**PR-06 / TEMPORARY COMMUNICATION EXCEPTION.**

The document had been created on day 47.

No appointment reference was attached.

No principal acknowledgement was recorded.

And its approval field contained only one name.

**Charles Vale.**

Edward read the document slowly.

*Where the protected principal cannot be contacted, the authorised communication representative's conditional response may be retained as the current administrative position for temporary continuity purposes.*

Below it was a qualification.

*This does not constitute permanent consent, receiving identity confirmation, or appointment of a receiving authority.*

The wording was careful.

It did not claim that P-04 had agreed.

It did not grant Gabriel permanent authority.

But it allowed his conditional response to become the only recorded position used to maintain the arrangement.

Jonathan looked at Edward.

“So the system could continue without the principal.”

“For temporary purposes, yes.”

“And no one had to ask again?”

Edward looked at the date.

The exception had been created one day after the failed delivery.

One day after the visitor had requested another attempt.

One day after Eleanor had challenged the suspension.

He placed the document beside the day-43 delivery note.

The person at C-19 had asked to read the letter.

The verification key had been withheld.

The second attempt had been suspended.

And then an administrative exception had been created using someone else's conditional response.

Edward looked at the final line.

**Review of exception required upon appointment of receiving identity custodian.**

No review had ever taken place.

He closed the file.

The exception had been described as temporary.

But no one had established when it would end.

And without the principal's own response, the arrangement could continue indefinitely while everyone involved claimed that no permanent decision had been made.
