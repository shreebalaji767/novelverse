# Chapter 162 — The Question That Was Never Answered

Edward read the handwritten sentence for the fourth time.

*This notice does not answer the question I asked.*

It was written beneath the delivery acknowledgement for the day-155 notice sent through the R-17B contact route.

There was no signature.

No name.

No confirmation that the sender was the person originally assigned R-17.

But the handwriting was familiar.

Jonathan placed three scanned documents beside it.

"The day-137 request, the day-141 response and the day-155 acknowledgement."

Edward examined them.

The first document contained the refusal to accept any appointment before receiving the original procedure.

The second was the proposed neutral review arrangement.

The third was the handwritten response to the notice about Charles Vale.

Edward compared the writing.

"The same person?"

"Dr Samuel Reed's preliminary comparison says the day-155 sentence is consistent with the day-137 request. He cannot establish authorship conclusively from the scans."

"Then we have a possible connection, not an identification."

"Correct."

Edward returned to the day-155 notice.

"What exactly did they send?"

Jonathan opened the attachment.

It was a two-page administrative statement.

**PERSONAL REPRESENTATION CONTINUITY — STATUS NOTIFICATION**

The statement informed the recipient that R-17 had been recorded as having declined appointment and that Charles Vale had been retained as responsible custodian under the administrative continuity arrangement.

It did not mention that the original procedure had never been delivered.

It did not mention the unanswered request for independent verification.

It did not mention Eleanor's objections.

And it did not explain why Charles's temporary document custody had become a personal representation status.

Edward looked at the bottom of the page.

"Who authorised this notice?"

"The dispatch record references the day-152 ledger entry."

"Which was never validly approved."

"Yes."

Edward closed the document.

"Then we need the question that came before it."

The original R-17 correspondence file was held separately from the Personal Custody register.

That separation had been intentional.

The correspondence file contained the questions and responses exchanged with the unidentified external contact.

The custody register contained the access credentials and preservation records.

Neither was supposed to substitute for the other.

Jonathan requested the original correspondence index.

The archive returned a list of six entries.

| Day | Record                                        |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 137 | Appointment refusal and request for procedure |
| 141 | Neutral review proposal                       |
| 146 | Request for original authority                |
| 149 | Follow-up concerning identity verification    |
| 152 | Request for clarification                     |
| 155 | Administrative continuity notice and response |

Edward stared at the list.

"There are six entries, but we have only seen two responses."

"Three," Jonathan said. "The day-141 proposal was received."

"Not answered?"

"No."

"What about days 146, 149 and 152?"

"The summaries say the correspondence was retained under restricted custody."

Edward looked up.

"Retained is not answered."

"No."

"Find the originals."

The day-146 request was brief.

*Please provide the complete original procedure governing the proposed appointment, including the limits of the personal representative's authority and the conditions under which the protected principal's personal acknowledgement may be recorded.*

The second paragraph was more important.

*Please confirm whether temporary preservation access has been used to create, amend or maintain any personal representation status.*

Edward read it slowly.

The person behind R-17 had asked about the exact conversion he was now investigating.

They had wanted to know whether their temporary access had been turned into something more.

The office's response was marked:

**RECEIVED — NO SUBSTANTIVE RESPONSE REQUIRED.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Who decided no response was required?"

"The status field was updated through V-17."

"An individual?"

"No individual is recorded."

Edward placed the request aside.

"Next."

The day-149 letter asked for confirmation that the original procedure had been made available to the proposed replacement nominees.

It specifically named neither Harriet Cole nor Margaret Voss nor Anna Mercer-Smith.

Instead, it asked whether any replacement candidate had received the same information and privacy protections requested by R-17.

The response field was blank.

The day-152 request was different.

It contained only one question.

*Has any person been granted authority to communicate, accept, refuse or make a personal decision on behalf of the protected principal without a completed appointment and verified authority?*

Edward stopped reading.

That was not a request for a general explanation.

It was a direct challenge to the day-152 ledger entry.

And the day-155 notice had not answered it.

"Who had access to these letters?" Edward asked.

Jonathan brought up the custody log.

"V-17 received all six. The correspondence was placed under restricted external personal-custody handling."

"Who could read them?"

"Charles Vale, Peter Ashford, the PC-04 shared account and R-17."

"Anyone else?"

"Marianne Cross handled the dispatch records."

"Did she read the correspondence?"

"Her access was limited to envelopes, routing information and delivery confirmations. There is no record showing she opened the sealed letters."

Edward considered the possibilities.

Charles had received the day-155 notice through his office.

Peter had maintained the physical custody records.

The shared account had handled status changes.

R-17 had access to the restricted correspondence route.

But none of that established who had written the responses.

"Can we tell whether the original questions were delivered to the person responsible for answering them?"

Jonathan checked.

"The system says delivered to V-17."

"That is the office, not the responsible person."

"Yes."

Edward stood.

"Then the first task is to establish whether anyone actually considered them."

Peter Ashford agreed to meet them at V-17.

He looked tired.

When Edward showed him the day-146 request, Peter recognised it.

"I remember the envelope."

"Did you open it?"

"No. It was sealed for restricted correspondence."

"Who received it?"

"The V-17 desk."

"Who signed?"

"An internal receipt code."

"Which code?"

"PC-04."

Edward waited.

Peter added, "I know that doesn't identify a person."

"It doesn't."

"At the time, we were told the correspondence was to remain sealed unless the relevant authority requested a review."

"Who gave that instruction?"

Peter looked down at the file.

"The instruction was already attached to the custody schedule."

"Signed by whom?"

"No signature."

Edward placed the day-155 notice beside it.

"Did you know this was sent to the R-17B contact?"

Peter examined the delivery record.

"I knew a notice had been dispatched. I did not know its contents."

"Did you know Charles had been listed as responsible custodian?"

"I saw the status."

"Did you believe he had been formally appointed?"

Peter hesitated.

"I believed the system was reflecting an existing continuity arrangement."

"Did you check the appointment ledger?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I was responsible for document custody, not appointment approval."

Edward nodded.

Peter had made an assumption.

But the assumption was not proof of an appointment.

"Did anyone ask you to confirm that Charles was authorised to act for the principal?"

"No."

"Did you provide that confirmation?"

"No."

Edward closed the folder.

"Then your custody records cannot be used to establish his authority."

Peter nodded.

"I understand."

Marianne Cross was more direct.

She arrived with a dispatch ledger and a small envelope containing the original day-155 receipt.

"I did not send the notice on my own authority," she said.

"Who instructed you?"

"The dispatch instruction came through Vale Private Counsel."

"From Charles?"

"I cannot confirm that. The request was received through an administrative mailbox."

"Did the instruction say the recipient had been appointed?"

"It said the status had been updated."

"Did you ask whether the update was authorised?"

"No."

"Why?"

Marianne looked uncomfortable.

"Because my role was to deliver correspondence according to the approved dispatch category."

Edward showed her the handwritten response.

"Did you receive this?"

"Yes."

"Did you recognise the handwriting?"

"No."

"Did you forward it to Charles?"

"It was returned to the restricted correspondence desk."

"Did he see it?"

"I don't know."

"Was it entered into the response register?"

Marianne opened her ledger.

"It was recorded as a delivery acknowledgement."

Edward looked at her.

"Not as a substantive objection?"

"No."

"Who classified it?"

"The classification came from the receiving office."

"Which receiving office?"

"V-17."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The message had been received.

It had been recorded.

It had not been treated as a response to the content of the notice.

The same pattern was repeating itself.

A question became an administrative receipt.

An objection became a delivery acknowledgement.

A refusal to accept an undefined role became a declined appointment.

The words were preserved.

Their meaning was not.

That evening, Edward met Eleanor Vale.

He showed her the day-152 question.

*Has any person been granted authority to communicate, accept, refuse or make a personal decision on behalf of the protected principal without a completed appointment and verified authority?*

Eleanor read it carefully.

"I remember this request."

"You knew it had been sent?"

"Yes."

"Did you answer?"

"No. It was not addressed to me."

"Did you ask for it to be reviewed?"

"I asked that the original channel office examine the status."

"Was that request accepted?"

"Not formally."

Edward placed the day-155 notice beside it.

"They received a notice saying Charles was responsible custodian."

"That is not an answer."

"Exactly."

Eleanor looked at him.

"The person behind R-17 was asking whether their temporary access had been converted into authority. The office responded by telling them that the conversion had occurred."

"Without explaining the basis."

"Or providing the procedure."

Edward watched her expression.

"Do you know who R-17 is?"

"No."

"Do you know whether the person is the same individual proposed as OC-19?"

"No."

"Could they be?"

"It is possible, but there is no evidence establishing that."

Edward nodded.

"Then we keep the identities separate."

Eleanor looked again at the six-entry correspondence index.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"The person behind R-17 did not simply ask to be appointed."

Edward waited.

"They repeatedly asked for the original procedure before deciding whether to accept any role. That means they were trying to preserve the right to make an informed decision."

"Yes."

"And the office repeatedly treated that request as a delay in appointment."

Edward looked at the records.

The distinction was becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

The next morning, Jonathan brought Edward a document from the Original Channel Review archive.

It was a day-156 internal assessment of the R-17 correspondence.

The assessment had been prepared after the day-155 handwritten response.

It contained three findings.

**Finding One:** R-17 had not completed an appointment.

**Finding Two:** R-17 had not been provided with the original personal representation procedure.

**Finding Three:** The day-155 response did not constitute acceptance or refusal of the proposed role.

Edward read the third finding twice.

"Who wrote this?"

"The Original Channel Review Office."

"Who signed it?"

"Dr Samuel Reed."

Edward looked up.

"Reed reviewed the response?"

"Yes. He was asked to assess whether the handwritten statement could be treated as a decision."

"What did he conclude?"

"That it could not."

Edward read the assessment again.

A fourth paragraph had been added.

*The statement appears to challenge the adequacy of the notice rather than the underlying appointment proposal. The sender's position remains undetermined.*

Edward placed it on the table.

"That's the first accurate description we've found."

Jonathan pointed to the next page.

"But there is a complication."

The assessment had been received by Family Allocation Review on day 157.

Its status was:

**RECEIVED — INFORMATIONAL ONLY.**

No correction had been made to the appointment ledger.

No review of Charles's authority had been initiated.

No notice had been issued to the protected principal.

The assessment was accurate.

But it had changed nothing.

Edward went to the archive himself.

He wanted to see the document that had been sent to Family Allocation Review.

The original assessment had four pages.

The copy in the Family Allocation Review file had five.

The additional page was titled:

**ADMINISTRATIVE INTERPRETATION — CONTINUITY STATUS.**

It stated that the day-155 response did not constitute a formal refusal and therefore did not affect the existing continuity arrangement.

Edward read the final sentence.

*The responsible custodian shall continue to maintain the current administrative position pending further appointment.*

He looked at Jonathan.

"They used the finding that R-17 had not refused to justify keeping Charles in place."

"Yes."

"But Reed's assessment also said R-17 had not accepted."

"Yes."

"And that no appointment had been completed."

"Yes."

Edward closed the file.

"The office selected the one part that supported continuity and ignored the parts that challenged it."

He examined the added page.

It carried no individual signature.

Only a processing reference.

**FA-ADMIN / REVIEW DESK.**

The same shared account.

The same untraceable operator field.

At 17:40, an envelope arrived at Edward's office.

It had been delivered through the independent records service.

There was no sender's name.

Inside was a single page.

It was not a new appointment request.

It was a copy of the original day-137 R-17 objection, with one sentence underlined.

*My identity must not be disclosed or treated as verified until I receive the original procedure and authority governing the proposed role.*

Beneath it, the sender had written:

*The office continues to treat my request for information as a failure to cooperate. I have not withdrawn the request. I have not accepted the appointment. I have not authorised anyone to act in my place.*

Edward read it silently.

Jonathan stood beside the desk.

"Is it from R-17?"

"We cannot establish that."

"But the handwriting?"

"Consistent with the earlier correspondence. Not conclusive."

Edward turned the page over.

There was another sentence on the back.

*Please identify the person who changed the appointment status after the original objection was received.*

Edward looked at the date stamp.

The letter had been received by the independent records service that morning.

It had been forwarded to him without a sender identity.

He placed it beside the day-156 assessment.

For the first time, the question was no longer only whether R-17 had been properly appointed.

Someone wanted to know who had changed the record after being told that no appointment had been accepted.

Edward opened the day-144 audit trail again.

The false declined status had been entered at 10:18.

The day-145 replacement search had begun the following morning.

The day-150 category mapping had converted temporary custody into continuity authority.

The day-152 ledger had listed Charles as responsible custodian.

And the day-157 assessment had been filed without correcting any of it.

Edward wrote a new heading on the investigation board.

**WHO CHANGED R-17'S STATUS?**

Below it, he added three possibilities.

- The person who entered the day-144 summary.
- The person who approved the day-150 category mapping.
- The person who authorised the day-152 ledger update.

They might be the same person.

They might not.

He drew a line beneath the three entries.

"Jonathan, I want the original operator records for all three events."

Jonathan nodded.

"And the access logs for the shared account?"

"Everything."

Edward looked once more at the unsigned letter.

The sender had not asked to be identified.

They had asked for the procedure, the authority and an accurate record.

The system had answered with an appointment that had no date.

Now someone was asking who had created it.
