# **Chapter 65 — The Child Who Had Already Been Moved**

Edward did not sleep well.

The sentence from the comparison report remained in his mind.

*The child identified by the bracelet has already been moved.*

*The child identified by the photograph has not yet been located.*

It was the first document that explicitly distinguished the two children.

Until then, the investigation had treated the bracelet and photograph as potentially connected evidence belonging to one infant.

Now that assumption had collapsed.

The bracelet belonged to Isabella Vale's maternity clinic.

The photograph came from St Catherine's.

They had been placed together, but they did not belong together.

And Margaret Voss had known.

Edward arrived at the office before eight.

Jonathan was already there.

He had arranged the documents across the conference table, separating them into three groups.

"Good morning," Edward said.

Jonathan pointed towards the first group.

"Isabella's original maternity records."

The second group contained St Catherine's documents.

"And these are the private nursery records."

Edward looked at the third group.

"What are those?"

"Records concerning Margaret Voss's receipt of the comparison report."

Edward sat down.

"Let's start with the report."

Jonathan opened the scanned document.

The comparison had been performed on the twenty-fourth day after the births.

It examined the following:

- Isabella Vale's maternity bracelet, IV-204.
- The photograph labelled Infant A.
- The original St Catherine's private nursery register.
- The available maternal identification records.

The conclusion was brief.

**THE IDENTIFICATION NUMBER IV-204 DOES NOT CORRESPOND TO THE INFANT SHOWN IN THE PHOTOGRAPH.**

A second conclusion followed.

**THE PHOTOGRAPH CANNOT BE USED TO ESTABLISH MATERNAL IDENTITY.**

And finally:

**THE INFANT ASSOCIATED WITH IV-204 HAS ALREADY BEEN TRANSFERRED FROM THE ORIGINAL MATERNITY FACILITY. CURRENT LOCATION UNCONFIRMED.**

Edward read the last sentence.

"Transferred from Isabella's clinic."

"That's how I understand it," Jonathan replied.

"Not necessarily to St Catherine's."

"Correct."

Edward looked at the second group of records.

"Then why was Infant A involved?"

"Because someone attached the photograph to Isabella's maternity file."

"Who?"

Jonathan turned to the document's distribution section.

"The report doesn't say."

Edward leaned back.

"Margaret received the result."

"On the twenty-eighth day."

"Four days after the comparison."

"Yes."

"Did anyone else receive it?"

Jonathan showed him the recipient list.

There were four names.

Margaret Voss.

Charles Vale.

Eleanor Vale.

And Marcus Morelli.

Edward examined the final column.

Only Margaret's entry had a handwritten notation.

**ORIGINAL MATERIALS RETURNED — RECEIPT CONFIRMED.**

He pointed to it.

"She collected the bracelet."

"Yes."

"And the photograph?"

"Apparently."

"Then she had both items."

Jonathan nodded.

"At least for a time."

Edward looked at the photograph again.

"Find out who received the bracelet after her."

The first person Edward contacted was Margaret Voss.

She answered on the third attempt.

"I was expecting your call," she said.

"Then you know why I'm calling."

"Yes."

"You received the final comparison report on the twenty-eighth day."

"I did."

"You knew Isabella's bracelet and the photograph belonged to different children."

"Yes."

Edward waited.

Margaret did not elaborate.

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because the report did not establish where either child was."

"It established that the bracelet belonged to a different infant."

"It established that the identification materials were different. That is not the same as identifying the children."

"Who received the bracelet?"

"I don't remember."

"Margaret."

"I remember the report. I remember the photograph. I remember being told to keep the original identification materials separate."

"Who gave you that instruction?"

"Eleanor."

"Did Eleanor know which child Isabella had given birth to?"

"No."

"Did Charles?"

"I don't know."

"Did Morelli?"

"I don't know."

Edward closed his notebook.

"Then tell me something you do know."

Margaret was silent.

Finally, she said, "The child associated with Isabella's bracelet was not the child Gabriel Vale took home."

Edward sat upright.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"How?"

"Because Gabriel's discharge record was connected to Infant A. The bracelet belonged to a different maternity record."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The distinction was significant.

Gabriel had taken Infant A on the sixth day.

Isabella's bracelet belonged to another child.

That meant the child transferred from Isabella's clinic had followed a separate path.

"Where did that child go?" Edward asked.

"I don't know."

"Was the child taken to another family?"

"I don't know."

"Was the child ever recorded at St Catherine's?"

Margaret hesitated.

"There was another reference."

"What reference?"

"An external receiving record. It wasn't part of the main nursery register."

"Where is it?"

"I don't have it."

"Who does?"

"I believe Eleanor kept a copy."

Edward looked at the documents.

"Where?"

Margaret's answer came quietly.

"In the same collection she left behind."

The Vale estate had been sold several years earlier.

Its remaining documents were held by the estate administrator.

Edward contacted Andrew Bell and requested access to Eleanor's personal correspondence and the remaining sealed boxes.

Bell agreed.

By the afternoon, a new box had arrived at the legal office.

It contained a collection of personal papers, old medical correspondence, and several envelopes.

One was labelled:

**IV-204 — RECEIVING RECORD.**

Edward opened it carefully.

Inside was a single page.

It was not a hospital discharge form.

It was a receiving acknowledgment from an external medical facility.

The top section identified the infant.

**INFANT: FEMALE**

**SOURCE RECORD: IV-204**

**RECEIVING FACILITY: MERIDIAN CHILDREN'S MEDICAL CENTRE**

**TRANSFER DATE: SIXTH DAY AFTER BIRTH**

Edward read the details twice.

"Meridian."

Jonathan searched the old records.

"That facility was part of the Meridian Medical Network. It closed years ago."

"Was it connected to Aurelius Medical?"

"Not at the time. Aurelius acquired a separate records management company that later absorbed some of Meridian's administrative assets."

Edward examined the signature.

**RECEIVED BY: M. VOSS**

Jonathan looked up.

"Margaret?"

"Possibly."

The handwriting was similar.

But the signature was incomplete.

There was no full first name.

The acknowledgment included another line:

**RECEIVING PARTY: TEMPORARY MEDICAL CUSTODIAN.**

No guardian was named.

No family was listed.

At the bottom was an instruction.

*The infant must not be transferred to a private guardian until the maternal identity review is completed.*

Edward looked at the date.

Sixth day.

The same day Gabriel Vale had taken Infant A.

But this was a different child.

A different record.

A different facility.

For the first time, the investigation had evidence of two separate infant movements on the same day.

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian received the Meridian document.

He brought it directly to Alexander.

"Meridian Children's Medical Centre."

Alexander examined the page.

"Was this facility connected to our family?"

"No direct connection. But it received administrative support from a private legal network that Charles Vale used."

"Did Aurelius Medical ever provide services there?"

"Not according to the records we've found."

Alexander looked at the transfer date.

"Sixth day."

"Yes."

"Gabriel took Infant A on the sixth day."

"Yes."

Alexander looked at the receiving acknowledgment.

"But this is another child."

"That's what the document suggests."

Victoria entered.

"What are you looking at?"

Alexander turned the page towards her.

She read it.

"Another infant?"

"Possibly Isabella's daughter," Adrian explained. "The source identification matches her maternity bracelet."

Victoria looked at the name of the receiving facility.

"Was the child medically unwell?"

"The record doesn't say."

"Then why was she transferred?"

"We don't know."

Victoria studied the document.

"Who was responsible for her?"

"The receiving custodian's name is incomplete."

Victoria looked at Alexander.

"How many children were moved?"

Alexander did not answer immediately.

He had no reliable number.

They had been searching for one missing daughter.

Now they had records suggesting that several newborns had been moved between facilities, with their maternal identities separated and their receiving guardians concealed.

The investigation was no longer a simple question of which child had gone to which family.

It was a question of how many children had been affected by the same arrangement.

Elena spent the afternoon in the university application portal.

Her provisional admission had advanced to the next stage.

She needed to submit a certified academic transcript, a financial declaration, and a signed statement confirming her intended course of study.

She printed the checklist.

Nicholas appeared at the library entrance.

"Still working?"

"Almost finished."

"That's what you said yesterday."

"Yesterday I was almost finished with a different section."

He walked over and examined the papers.

"Do you need help?"

Elena looked at him.

"Do you know how to certify a transcript?"

"No."

"Then no."

Nicholas pulled out a chair.

"That's harsh."

"You asked."

He grinned.

"Fair."

After a moment, he became more serious.

"Are you nervous?"

Elena looked at the application.

"A little."

"About going abroad?"

"About making a decision that affects the next few years."

Nicholas nodded.

"That's normal."

"I don't want to go just because everyone thinks it would be impressive."

"Then don't."

Elena looked at him.

"I also don't want to stay because I'm afraid of leaving."

Nicholas smiled.

"That sounds more like you."

She folded the checklist.

"I want to try."

"Then try."

He stood.

"And if you need anything, you know where to find me."

Elena pointed at him.

"Don't start asking every five minutes."

Nicholas raised both hands.

"I wasn't going to."

She gave him a doubtful look.

He laughed and left.

Elena returned to her application.

For once, the uncertainty ahead of her did not feel like something imposed by other people.

It was a decision she was making herself.

Edward and Jonathan visited the former Meridian Children's Medical Centre the next day.

The building had been converted into a general outpatient facility.

Its old records department had been relocated to a regional archive.

The administrator, Mrs. Helen Porter, reviewed the investigative order.

"You're looking for records from eighteen years ago?"

"Yes," Edward replied.

"That is a long time."

"We understand."

She examined the reference number.

"IV-204."

"Correct."

Mrs. Porter searched the digital catalogue.

After several minutes, she stopped.

"There's an archived patient file."

Edward leaned forward.

"Can we see it?"

"Not immediately. The record is restricted."

"By whom?"

"The original custodian."

"Who?"

Mrs. Porter checked the system.

"Margaret Voss."

Jonathan looked at Edward.

"Again."

Mrs. Porter continued reading.

"There's also a later amendment."

"What does it say?"

She turned the monitor slightly.

**RECORD TRANSFERRED TO PRIVATE CUSTODY — TWENTY-EIGHTH DAY.**

**RECEIVING REPRESENTATIVE: E. VALE.**

Edward looked at the screen.

"Eleanor Vale?"

Mrs. Porter checked the details.

"Yes."

"Did she take the original medical file?"

"According to this entry, she received the complete record."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

Eleanor had collected the file on the same day Margaret received the final comparison report.

The timing was not accidental.

"Is there a receipt?"

Mrs. Porter opened another section.

A scanned document appeared.

It contained Eleanor's signature.

Beneath it was a handwritten statement.

*I accept custody of the medical record only. No authority to remove or transfer the infant is granted by this acknowledgment.*

Edward read the sentence twice.

"She took the file, not the child."

"That's what the document says," Jonathan replied.

Edward looked at the administrator.

"Do you have a record of the child's subsequent medical care?"

Mrs. Porter searched again.

"No."

"Any discharge record?"

"No."

"Any receiving guardian?"

"No."

"Then what happened to the child?"

Mrs. Porter looked uncomfortable.

"I can only tell you what the archive contains."

Edward nodded.

"Then please preserve the entire record. We will return with the necessary authorisation."

That evening, Margaret Voss called Edward.

"I heard you went to Meridian."

Edward did not conceal his surprise.

"How?"

"The archive administrator contacted the former records supervisor."

"Did you know Eleanor collected the medical file?"

"Yes."

"Why did she take it?"

"She said the record needed to be protected."

"From whom?"

"Charles."

"Why?"

Margaret paused.

"Because he wanted the original maternal identification removed."

Edward looked at the receiving acknowledgment.

"Was Isabella's child still at Meridian when Eleanor collected the file?"

"I don't know."

"Margaret, the record says Eleanor accepted custody of the medical file only."

"That was intentional."

"Explain."

"She wanted to preserve the child's medical history without giving anyone the impression that she had authority over the child."

"Did she know where the child was?"

Margaret did not answer immediately.

Then she said, "She knew where the child had been taken."

"Where?"

"That is not the same as knowing where the child was afterward."

Edward rubbed his forehead.

"Did she ever see the infant?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"On the sixth day."

Edward went still.

"At Meridian?"

"Yes."

"Was the child healthy?"

"Yes."

"Did Eleanor hold her?"

"I believe so."

"Why didn't she take the child?"

"Because she wasn't authorised to."

Edward looked at the receiving acknowledgment.

The infant had been transferred under temporary medical custody.

Eleanor had collected the medical file.

But the child had remained somewhere outside the records.

"Who was authorised to receive her?"

Margaret's answer was almost inaudible.

"The person who had arranged the transfer."

"Who was that?"

"I don't know."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

"Did Eleanor know?"

"Yes."

"Then why didn't she tell you?"

"Because she didn't trust the person."

Edward's voice hardened.

"Margaret, we're eighteen years into this investigation. Another child may have disappeared. We need a name."

"I understand."

"Then give me one."

Margaret remained silent for several seconds.

Finally, she spoke.

"Find the twenty-eighth-day payment."

"What payment?"

"The money paid to the person who agreed to keep the child from Meridian."

Edward opened his notebook.

"Who received it?"

"I never saw the name."

"Then what do you know?"

Margaret's answer came slowly.

"The payment wasn't made to a family."

Edward frowned.

"Who was it made to?"

"A school."

Edward ended the call and immediately searched the financial records.

There was a payment on the twenty-eighth day.

It had been made through Northbridge Family Services.

The recipient was a small educational institution that had since closed.

The payment description read:

**CHILD ACCOMMODATION AND EARLY CARE — PRIVATE ARRANGEMENT.**

The amount was considerably larger than the ordinary medical expenses.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Find the institution."

Jonathan began searching.

Within an hour, they had an address.

It had been a residential children's home.

Not a hospital.

Not a private nursery.

A place where children could be accommodated temporarily while their guardianship arrangements were reviewed.

The records showed that a female infant had been admitted on the twenty-eighth day.

Her name had been left blank.

Her identifying number was not IV-204.

Instead, the entry contained a new reference.

**CHILD 28-B.**

Edward read the admission note.

*Receiving party has requested that the child's maternal identity remain confidential.*

A second note had been added three months later.

*The child is to be referred to by the name Anna.*

Edward stared at the screen.

Another Anna.

Another infant whose identity had been hidden.

He looked at the photograph of the little girl from the rental property.

Then at the St Agnes admission record.

Then at the new residential home entry.

Three different records.

Three references to a child named Anna.

But there was no proof they belonged to the same person.

Jonathan spoke quietly.

"Could this be the child Isabella has been searching for?"

Edward looked at the admission record.

"Possibly."

"Could she be the child at St Agnes?"

"Possibly."

"Could she be Elena or Isabelle?"

Edward shook his head.

"We cannot make that assumption."

He read the final line of the record.

**TRANSFER TO LONG-TERM RECEIVING PARTY — PENDING APPROVAL.**

Beneath it was the name of the person who had requested the placement.

For the first time, the record contained a complete name.

**RECEIVING PARTY: CATHERINE VALMONT.**

Edward stopped breathing for a moment.

He read it again.

Then he called Jonathan's attention to the screen.

Jonathan stared at the name.

"Catherine?"

Edward nodded.

"Yes."

Neither man spoke.

The Catherine Valmont in the record was the woman who had raised Elena and was now trying to build a relationship with Isabelle.

But the entry was eighteen years old.

It did not prove she had received the infant.

It did not even prove she had personally submitted the request.

It only established that someone had used her name to request a long-term placement for Child 28-B.

Edward looked at the date.

The request had been made on the twenty-eighth day after the births.

The same day Eleanor collected Isabella's medical file.

The same day Margaret received the comparison report.

And the same day another child entered a residential home under the name Anna.

Edward picked up his telephone.

He had to speak to Catherine Valmont.

But before he could press the call button, another notification appeared on the screen.

A second document had been attached to the placement request.

It was a handwritten instruction.

**THE CHILD MUST NOT BE DELIVERED TO CATHERINE VALMONT.**

And beneath it:

**THE NAME WAS USED WITHOUT HER KNOWLEDGE.**
