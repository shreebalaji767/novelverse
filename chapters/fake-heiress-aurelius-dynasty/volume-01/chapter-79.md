# **Chapter 79 — The Man Who Kept the Original Card**

Gabriel Vale had not destroyed the original identification card.

That was the first conclusion Edward reached after examining the records Marianne Cross had promised to provide.

The second was considerably more troubling.

Gabriel had never formally returned it to St Catherine's Hospital.

There was no receipt.

No release confirmation.

No destruction certificate.

No record of its transfer to an authorised laboratory.

The card had simply disappeared from the documented chain of custody.

And for eighteen years, nobody had demanded an explanation.

Edward was no longer willing to accept that omission as an administrative accident.

Jonathan arrived at the office shortly before nine with a folder.

"I found something."

Edward looked up from his computer.

"Gabriel's correspondence?"

"Part of it."

Jonathan placed the folder on the desk.

"Cross Administrative Services maintained a separate document archive. Most of it was transferred to a records-management company after the business was dissolved."

"Did you obtain the archive?"

"Not yet. But I found the transfer inventory."

Edward opened the folder.

The first page listed the company's administrative records.

Invoices.

Staff correspondence.

Client agreements.

Medical coordination documents.

Residential placement files.

Then he reached the section Jonathan had marked.

**Protected identity correspondence — EFO-SL/07.**

There were seventeen folders listed beneath it.

The tenth folder contained an entry dated the tenth day after the births.

**Original St Catherine's identification card.**

**Received from Marianne Cross.**

**Temporary custody: Gabriel Vale.**

Edward looked up.

"That's it."

"There's more."

Jonathan turned the page.

**Condition: Sealed.**

**Storage location: Private records cabinet, Room 4.**

**Release restriction: No external transfer without originating clinical confirmation.**

Edward read the last line twice.

"Gabriel didn't lose the card."

"No."

"He kept it."

"Apparently."

Edward closed the inventory.

"Where is Room 4?"

"The company's old office was demolished years ago. The building was sold, and the records were moved before demolition."

"To which company?"

Jonathan gave him a name.

**Meridian Records and Preservation Services.**

Edward leaned back.

"Let's find out whether they still have the original files."

Meridian Records and Preservation Services occupied an unremarkable building in the industrial district.

Its exterior was plain, its reception area quiet, and its security procedures unusually strict.

The company specialised in the storage of legal, medical and corporate archives.

It had acquired several private administrative collections over the years, including the dissolved Cross Administrative Services archive.

The records director, Mr. Arvind Mehta, reviewed Edward's authorisation before agreeing to assist.

"We received the collection approximately eleven years ago," he explained. "It was transferred in sealed containers, with an inventory prepared by the previous custodian."

"Did you inspect the contents?"

"Only for storage classification. We don't examine the substantive contents of client records unless authorised."

"Do you still hold the EFO-SL/07 collection?"

Mehta checked the database.

"Yes."

"Is the original St Catherine's identification card listed?"

He searched.

A moment later, he stopped.

"There's an item under that reference."

Edward leaned forward.

"Where?"

"Container 17. Restricted medical correspondence."

"Can we examine it?"

"Not without a further authorisation."

Edward placed the relevant legal documents on the desk.

Mehta reviewed them carefully.

"This authorises examination of the records connected to the identity investigation. However, the item is marked as containing third-party medical information."

"We'll respect the restrictions."

Mehta considered it.

"Then I can arrange supervised access."

Container 17 arrived on a trolley.

It was smaller than Edward expected.

The outer label carried the Cross Administrative Services reference and a date from eleven years earlier.

Mehta checked the seal.

"Intact."

The container was opened under supervision.

Inside were several folders and a smaller metal document case.

Edward found the inventory.

**Folder 10 — Protected identity correspondence.**

**Item 10-C — Original St Catherine's identification card.**

**Status: Sealed custody.**

**Last recorded custodian: Gabriel Vale.**

He examined the metal case.

It was locked.

"Do you have a key?"

Mehta checked the acquisition inventory.

"There's a key reference."

He returned a few minutes later with a small envelope.

The key fitted.

Inside the case was a transparent protective sleeve.

And inside the sleeve was a sealed paper envelope.

The label read:

**ST CATHERINE'S HOSPITAL**

**INFANT A — ORIGINAL IDENTIFICATION CARD**

**STC-A**

**Received from Northbridge Clinical Review Unit.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Received from Northbridge?"

"That's what it says."

Edward examined the seal.

Unlike the card preserved at the Aurelius archive, this envelope had a second label attached to its reverse.

**Comparison material — not to be opened without originating clinician.**

There was a handwritten date.

The eleventh day.

Edward checked the custody inventory.

Gabriel had taken the card on the tenth day.

The following day, it had been recorded as received from Northbridge.

That suggested Gabriel had returned to the review unit after the initial refusal.

But why had the unit returned the card to him?

And why had nobody recorded its subsequent destination?

Edward photographed the envelope.

"Can we examine the document inside?"

Mehta checked the authorisation again.

"Yes, under supervised conditions. But the seal should be preserved and the opening documented."

Edward nodded.

The archivist photographed the envelope, recorded the seal number and carefully opened it.

A small identification card lay inside.

Its printed details matched the hospital's neonatal register.

**Infant A.**

**Female.**

**2:17 a.m.**

**Temporary reference: STC-A.**

But there was something else.

A faint line had been written across the lower edge.

It was not the IV-204 cross-reference.

It was a handwritten instruction.

**Comparison permitted only against original maternal and neonatal materials.**

The initials beneath it were:

**P.L.**

Peter Lang.

Jonathan examined the card.

"Is this the missing original?"

Edward checked the printed details, the paper stock and the hospital's identification format.

"It appears to be the original card."

"Then why did Northbridge refuse it?"

Edward looked at the reverse.

There was a small stamp.

**RECEIVED — NORTHBRIDGE CLINICAL REVIEW UNIT.**

**DAY 11.**

The card had reached Northbridge after all.

The comparison office had received it.

But the status notice from the twenty-first day had claimed the original neonatal identification card was unavailable.

Edward read the stamp again.

"Someone reported it missing after it had already been received."

Jonathan's expression changed.

"Could the card have been returned?"

"Not according to this record."

Edward turned the sleeve over.

There was another document beneath it.

A short receipt.

**Original identification card retained pending further instruction.**

**Receiving officer: Gabriel Vale.**

The date was the twelfth day.

Edward stared at it.

Gabriel had collected the card again.

He had not simply held it while Northbridge decided what to do.

He had received it back.

And then he had kept it.

The next document was a letter from Dr. Peter Lang.

It was dated the thirteenth day.

The wording was precise.

*The original identification card has been received and examined for administrative consistency. The comparison cannot proceed because the corresponding original maternal material has not been made available.*

*The receiving representative has requested that the card remain in protected custody until the originating records are reconciled.*

*No conclusion concerning infant identity has been reached.*

Edward read it twice.

Jonathan pointed to the first paragraph.

"He's saying the card arrived, but the other material didn't."

"Yes."

"Yet the original maternity band was still at Meridian."

"Correct."

"Then the comparison couldn't proceed."

"Not without it."

Edward placed the letter beside the seventeenth-day correspondence.

The timeline was beginning to make sense.

The original neonatal card had been delivered to Northbridge.

The original maternity band had remained in separate custody.

The comparison office had not received both original materials together.

Then the band was sent to the Aurelius Family Office in a separate administrative process.

And the comparison was suspended.

Edward looked at the final paragraph.

"Who was the receiving representative?"

The answer appeared in the accompanying receipt.

**Gabriel Vale.**

Edward requested a meeting with Gabriel that afternoon.

The request was accepted.

This time, Gabriel did not insist on a private office.

He agreed to meet at the headquarters of his current legal practice, with his own solicitor present.

When Edward entered the conference room, Gabriel was already seated.

He looked older than he had in the photographs from the original investigation, but his posture remained straight.

His expression was calm.

"Mr. Valmont."

"Mr. Vale."

They sat opposite each other.

Edward placed the photographed envelope on the table.

Gabriel glanced at it.

For the first time, his expression changed.

Only slightly.

But Edward noticed.

"You recognise it," he said.

Gabriel folded his hands.

"I recognise the reference."

"STC-A."

"Yes."

"Did you receive the original identification card from Marianne Cross?"

Gabriel looked at the photograph.

"Yes."

"Did you deliver it to Northbridge Clinical Review Unit?"

"Yes."

"Was it accepted?"

"It was received."

"Then why did you collect it again?"

Gabriel took a breath.

"Because the comparison could not proceed."

"That doesn't answer the question."

"The receiving office requested that the material be held separately until the corresponding original records were available."

"By whom?"

"Dr. Lang."

Edward placed the letter on the table.

"Dr. Lang's letter says the receiving representative requested protected custody."

Gabriel examined it.

"Then the wording is accurate."

"Who was the receiving representative?"

"I was."

"Why?"

"Because I was responsible for medical coordination under the existing administrative arrangement."

"You weren't a medical practitioner."

"No."

"You couldn't determine infant identity."

"I never claimed that I could."

Edward leaned forward.

"Then why were you repeatedly placed between the hospital, the clinic, the comparison office and the residential facilities?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"Because the parties involved did not trust one another."

"Who didn't trust whom?"

"Charles Vale did not trust the hospital's records department. The hospital did not trust the private legal office. The private maternity clinic did not trust the external review unit. And the Aurelius Family Office wanted its own records preserved."

Edward listened.

Gabriel continued.

"I was asked to coordinate documents and ensure they were not lost between institutions."

"Yet the documents were repeatedly withheld."

"Because the identities were unresolved."

"Or because somebody wanted them unresolved."

Gabriel's expression hardened.

"That is an accusation."

"It is a question."

"Then I cannot answer it."

Edward placed the twelfth-day receipt before him.

"Did you retain the original card?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

"In protected custody."

"At Cross Administrative Services?"

"Initially."

"Then?"

Gabriel paused.

"It was transferred to a separate secure archive."

"Which one?"

"I would need to check the old records."

"Did you personally sign the transfer?"

"I don't recall."

Edward showed him the current archive inventory.

"This is your custody record."

Gabriel read it.

His face became unreadable.

"That appears to be my signature."

"Do you dispute it?"

"No."

"Then you knew the card remained in your possession."

Gabriel looked up.

"I knew it had not been destroyed."

"That isn't the same thing."

"No."

Edward waited.

Finally, Gabriel said, "I was instructed not to release it without the original maternity materials."

"By Charles?"

"Yes."

"Did you agree?"

"I believed it was necessary to prevent a false identification."

Edward took out the seventeenth-day letter.

"Then explain this."

Gabriel read the letter addressed to Victoria.

"I didn't write it."

"Marianne says she did."

"Then you should ask her."

"She says she wrote it because she wanted Victoria to know the original maternity identification material existed."

Gabriel nodded.

"That was sensible."

"Was it?"

"Yes. The Aurelius family had a legitimate interest in the identity dispute."

"Yet you retained the original neonatal card."

"Because the comparison had not been authorised to proceed."

"Who prevented it?"

Gabriel looked at the table.

"Charles."

Edward watched him carefully.

"Did Charles know the card had reached Northbridge?"

"Yes."

"Did he know you collected it again?"

"Yes."

"Did Alexander Aurelius know?"

"No."

"Did Victoria?"

"I don't know."

"Did Isabella?"

Gabriel did not answer.

Edward placed another document on the table.

It was the original clinic consent form.

Isabella's signature was visible.

"She consented to the comparison on the sixteenth day."

Gabriel looked at it.

"Yes."

"You knew?"

"Yes."

"Then why was the comparison not completed?"

Gabriel's jaw tightened.

"Because consent from one mother did not resolve the identity of the other infant."

"That is not a reason to prevent a comparison."

"It is a reason not to issue a conclusion."

"Nobody asked you to issue a conclusion. They asked for the records to be examined."

Gabriel remained silent.

Edward continued.

"Did you ever see the original IV-204 band?"

"Yes."

"Was it compared with the St Catherine's card?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the original neonatal material and the maternity identification material were never brought together under a properly authorised examination."

Edward leaned back.

For a moment, the room was silent.

Then he asked, "Where is the original card now?"

Gabriel looked at him.

"I don't know."

Edward's eyes narrowed.

"You were the last recorded custodian."

"I transferred it."

"To whom?"

"I cannot remember."

"To which archive?"

Gabriel looked towards his solicitor.

The solicitor intervened.

"My client will need time to review the historical records before answering that question."

Edward nodded slowly.

"Of course."

He collected the documents.

But before leaving, he placed one final paper on the table.

It was a copy of the Northbridge status notice from the twenty-first day.

**Original neonatal identification card unavailable.**

Gabriel read it.

Then he looked up.

"That statement was not written by me."

"I didn't say it was."

"Someone knew the card had been received."

"Yes."

"Then someone else must have changed its status."

Edward stood.

"That's what we're trying to establish."

Outside, Jonathan waited beside the car.

"What happened?"

"Gabriel admits he retained the card."

"Did he tell you where it went?"

"No."

"Did he deny that he knew the comparison was suspended?"

"No."

Jonathan opened the passenger door.

"So we have the card, the receipt and his admission."

Edward shook his head.

"We have a card that appears to be the original, a record showing Gabriel received it, and his admission that he retained it."

"What's missing?"

"Proof of where it went afterwards."

Jonathan sighed.

"Of course."

Edward looked back at the building.

"He's hiding something."

"Do you think he knows which infant it was?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because if he knew, he would have answered differently."

Jonathan considered that.

"Or he's a very good liar."

"That is possible too."

Edward got into the car.

"But there's another problem."

"What?"

"Gabriel says Charles knew the card had reached Northbridge and knew it had been returned to him."

Jonathan nodded.

"So Charles was aware the original material was available."

"Yes."

"Then why did he continue telling everyone that the comparison couldn't proceed because the records were incomplete?"

Edward looked through the windscreen.

"Because the card alone wasn't enough."

He paused.

"Or because he didn't want the other material to reach it."

At the Valmont Estate, Richard was reviewing a financial report when Edward entered his study.

Catherine was seated near the window.

Isabelle had joined them after finishing a meeting at the family foundation.

Edward placed the new documents on the table.

"I found the original St Catherine's identification card."

Richard immediately looked up.

"Where?"

"In the Cross Administrative Services archive."

Catherine leaned forward.

"Was it the same card that appeared in the Aurelius archive?"

"No. The Aurelius archive contained an administrative identification card. This was the original neonatal comparison card."

Edward explained the custody trail.

St Catherine's.

Northbridge.

Gabriel Vale.

Cross Administrative Services.

Then an undocumented transfer.

Isabelle listened without speaking.

When Edward finished, she asked, "Did they ever compare it with my band?"

"No."

"Not even once?"

"Not according to the available records."

She looked down.

"Then what was the point of keeping it?"

"That is one of the questions we're trying to answer."

Richard studied the documents.

"Gabriel says Charles knew the card had been returned?"

"Yes."

"Did Charles tell us the comparison had failed?"

"Yes."

Richard's expression hardened.

"Then he knew more than he disclosed."

"Possibly."

"Edward."

"I am not going to turn a possibility into a conclusion."

Richard fell silent.

Isabelle picked up the clinic consent form.

Her signature looked smaller than she remembered.

She had signed it eighteen years earlier, while believing that the hospital might finally help her locate her daughter.

She had waited.

Then she had been told the comparison could not proceed.

And now she knew the original material had been sitting in a private administrative archive.

"Can I ask something?" she said.

Edward nodded.

"Did anyone ever tell me that the original card had reached Northbridge?"

"No."

"Did anyone tell me Gabriel had taken it away?"

"No."

Isabelle placed the form down.

"Then they weren't merely protecting the evidence."

Edward looked at her.

"They were deciding who was allowed to know it existed."

Nobody contradicted her.

That evening, Elena received an unexpected call from the academic programme coordinator.

The interview had been scheduled.

It would take place the following week.

She wrote down the date, thanked the coordinator and ended the call.

Then she opened her application folder.

There was still a considerable amount of work to do.

She needed to obtain a recommendation from one of her teachers, prepare a short presentation about her academic interests and submit a revised financial declaration.

She began with the presentation.

Her first idea was a project examining how small businesses could use simple digital tools to improve financial record-keeping.

It was practical.

It interested her.

And she understood the problem well enough to explain it without pretending to be an expert.

She spent an hour arranging her slides.

Then she stopped.

The title looked too ambitious.

She changed it from *Digital Transformation of Small Enterprises* to *Simple Digital Record-Keeping for Small Businesses*.

It was less impressive.

It was also more honest.

Elena smiled at the screen.

That was better.

A knock came at the door.

Sebastian entered.

"Are you preparing for the interview?"

"Yes."

He glanced at the slides.

"What is this about?"

"Using simple digital tools to help small businesses maintain financial records."

"Interesting."

"I hope so."

Sebastian read the next slide.

"You've included an example of a family business."

"Yes. It's easier to explain when the example is familiar."

He looked at her.

"Are you planning to discuss Valmont Holdings?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I don't want the presentation to become about the company."

Sebastian nodded.

He understood.

"You want the idea to stand on its own."

"Exactly."

He looked at the screen again.

"Then make sure you include the limitations."

Elena turned towards him.

"What limitations?"

"Digital tools don't automatically solve poor management. Bad data remains bad data, even when it's stored in an application."

She smiled.

"That's actually useful."

"You're welcome."

Sebastian was about to leave when she spoke.

"Do you think I should mention that I want to build something of my own?"

He considered the question.

"Yes."

"Even if I don't know exactly what it is yet?"

"Especially then."

Elena looked back at her presentation.

For once, she did not feel obliged to have her entire future planned.

She only needed to explain what she wanted to learn next.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander received a call from Edward.

"Gabriel has admitted retaining the original St Catherine's identification card," Edward reported.

Alexander stood near his study window.

"Where is it now?"

"Unknown."

"Did he identify the person to whom he transferred it?"

"No. He claims he needs to review his old records."

Alexander was silent.

"Do you believe him?"

"I believe he knows more than he has told us. I cannot yet establish whether he knows the child's identity."

"Was the comparison ever performed?"

"Not with the original materials together."

Alexander looked towards the garden.

"Then we still have no answer."

"No."

"Continue."

Edward hesitated.

"There is one more thing."

"What?"

"Gabriel confirmed Charles knew the card had reached Northbridge and that it was returned to his custody. Charles continued telling the others that the comparison was incomplete because the records were missing."

Alexander's expression darkened.

"That was deliberate."

"It appears so."

"Find out why."

"I will."

After the call ended, Alexander remained at the window.

Victoria entered quietly.

"Edward?"

"Gabriel retained the original card."

"Did he say where it went?"

"No."

Victoria sat down.

"At least we know it existed."

Alexander looked at her.

"Yes."

She glanced towards the family photographs on the wall.

"Do you think the card would have answered the question?"

"I don't know."

"Neither do I."

Victoria folded her hands.

"But I would like to know whether someone had the opportunity to find out."

Alexander nodded.

That was the question now.

Not whether the card was enough to establish the identity of their daughter.

Whether someone had deliberately prevented the evidence from reaching the people who could have examined it.

Two days later, a package arrived at Edward's office.

It came from Gabriel Vale's solicitor.

The outer envelope contained a letter requesting additional time to review the historical records.

Beneath it was a photocopy of a document.

Edward recognised the format immediately.

It was a transfer receipt from Cross Administrative Services.

The date was the fifteenth day after the births.

**Original St Catherine's identification card.**

**Transferred from Gabriel Vale.**

**Receiving representative: A. Holt.**

Edward stared at the name.

Adrian Holt.

The man who had kept the Aurelius evidence archive.

But the date was significant.

Holt had previously said he received the St Catherine's card on the seventeenth day.

This receipt showed that he had received it two days earlier.

Edward turned the page.

A second line had been added beneath the signature.

**Card not to be opened until originating maternity materials are received.**

The initials were unfamiliar.

But the receiving signature was clear.

Adrian Holt.

Edward called Jonathan.

"Come to my office."

"What happened?"

"We have a new transfer receipt."

"From Gabriel?"

"Yes."

"Where did the card go?"

Edward looked at the document.

"Adrian Holt."

There was a pause.

"But Holt told us he received the card on the seventeenth day."

"He did."

"Then why does this say the fifteenth?"

Edward placed the receipt beside the earlier archive inventory.

"Because someone has given us two different dates for the same transfer."

Jonathan arrived a few minutes later.

He examined the document.

"Could one be the date of dispatch and the other the date of receipt?"

"Possibly."

"Then there may be no contradiction."

Edward nodded.

"But look at the second line."

Jonathan read it.

**Card not to be opened until originating maternity materials are received.**

He looked up.

"That sounds like a preservation instruction."

"Yes."

Edward turned the receipt over.

On the reverse was a handwritten note.

It contained only one sentence.

**The original card must remain separate from the material held by Victoria Aurelius.**

Jonathan stared at it.

Edward felt the room grow very quiet.

Someone had known Victoria was holding separate maternity evidence.

Someone had specifically instructed Adrian Holt not to combine the two items.

And that instruction had been written before the seventeenth-day letter reached Victoria's archive.

Edward read the sentence once more.

Then he noticed the final detail.

The note was not signed by Gabriel.

It was not signed by Charles.

It was not signed by Adrian Holt.

It carried the initials:

**E.V.**

Eleanor Vale.

And for the first time, Edward wondered whether the woman who had repeatedly insisted that the records remain separate had been trying to prevent a false comparison—

or had been keeping two pieces of evidence apart for a reason she had never disclosed.
