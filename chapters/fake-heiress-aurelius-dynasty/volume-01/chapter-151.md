# **Chapter 151 — The Person Who Accepted Without Being Appointed**

The nomination schedule arrived at Meridian in a sealed archival container.

It had been stored separately from the ordinary OC-19 register, under a restriction requiring independent preservation review before access.

Edward examined the exterior.

**OC-19 — ORIGINAL CHANNEL VERIFIER**

**NOMINATION RECORD — DAY 34**

**IDENTITY SCHEDULE — SEALED**

The container had two security seals.

Both were intact.

Dr Mira Sen photographed them before the preservation officer opened the outer case.

Inside was a thin folder containing four documents.

The first was a nomination request.

The second was a proposed role description.

The third was a conditional acceptance.

The fourth was a withdrawal notice.

Edward began with the nomination request.

The proposed verifier was expected to:

- Confirm that correspondence received through the original channel came from the protected principal.
- Distinguish personal communication from communication delivered by an intermediary.
- Preserve the principal's right to receive the original procedure.
- Ensure that representative acknowledgement was not treated as personal acknowledgement.
- Record whether the principal had received sufficient information to make an independent decision.

The role did not include authority to determine biological identity, approve placement, or appoint a receiving family.

It was a narrow responsibility.

But it was central to the entire process.

Edward turned to the conditional acceptance.

The nominee had written:

*I am willing to be considered for the proposed verification responsibility, subject to receipt of the governing procedure, confirmation of the limits of the role, and independent confirmation that the appointment will not compromise the principal's privacy.*

The signature field was obscured by a sealed identity flap.

The date was day 36.

"That isn't an appointment," Jonathan said.

"No. It's an agreement to be considered."

Edward examined the document.

The wording was precise.

The nominee had not accepted authority.

They had accepted the possibility of taking responsibility after receiving the necessary information.

"Was the governing procedure ever delivered?" Edward asked.

Jonathan checked the inventory.

"No delivery record."

Edward turned to the withdrawal notice.

It was dated day 132.

*The proposed nomination is withdrawn. No authority shall be exercised under the proposed designation. Any existing document-preservation obligation shall continue only under separate custody instructions.*

There was no signature.

The identity flap was still sealed.

The reference beneath it was familiar.

**AV-18 / PERSONAL REPRESENTATION.**

Edward looked up.

"Why is a channel-verifier nomination being withdrawn under a personal-representation reference?"

Jonathan examined the document.

"Because the two roles may have been administratively combined."

"Or someone wanted them treated as the same."

Neither possibility was yet established.

Edward returned to the conditional acceptance.

The nominee had agreed to consider a role.

They had not accepted an appointment.

They had not received the procedure.

And the withdrawal had been entered nearly three months later.

The question was no longer merely who had been nominated.

It was who had connected two responsibilities that were supposed to remain separate.

Helen Ward examined the nomination record later that afternoon.

She recognised the role description.

"OC-19 verification was meant to be independent from the receiving family process," she explained.

"Why?"

"Because the person verifying the original channel needed to confirm that a communication came from the protected principal. They were not supposed to decide where a child belonged or who should receive one."

"Could the receiving authority also perform this role?"

"Not under the original procedure."

"Why not?"

"Because the receiving authority would have an interest in the outcome. The channel verifier needed to establish the authenticity of the principal's communication without deciding what that communication should mean for placement."

Edward showed her the conditional acceptance.

"Did this person ever receive the governing procedure?"

Helen read the delivery field.

"No."

"Could they have been appointed without it?"

"Not properly."

"Could they have been authorised to verify the principal's identity?"

"Not on the basis of this document."

"Then why was the nomination withdrawn under AV-18?"

Helen considered the wording.

"AV-18 governed personal representation. It may have been used as a general reference for any role involving the principal's communication."

"Would that have been correct?"

"No. The roles were different."

Edward placed the two records beside each other.

The first described an independent verifier.

The second concerned a personal representation custodian.

Both had been withdrawn on day 132.

Both had incomplete appointment records.

And both had been connected to the same unresolved personal communication process.

"Who could approve the verifier appointment?" he asked.

"The originating representative and the independent preservation authority, subject to the principal's privacy requirements."

"Was Charles Vale involved?"

"He could submit a nomination. He could not complete the appointment alone."

"Could Eleanor?"

"She could confirm the originating records. She could not independently verify the principal's personal channel."

"Who could?"

Helen shook her head.

"That was the purpose of the proposed appointment."

Edward arranged a meeting with Eleanor Vale.

She studied the nomination request for several minutes.

"I remember this role," she said.

"Did you nominate the person?"

"No. I received a copy of the proposed role description."

"Did you know who was selected?"

"Not from the version I received."

"Did you know someone had accepted consideration?"

"I knew a conditional response had been received. I was not given the identity."

"Did you approve the appointment?"

"No."

Edward placed the day-132 withdrawal notice before her.

"Did you authorise this?"

Eleanor shook her head.

"Not as a channel-verifier withdrawal."

"Did you know the nomination had been withdrawn?"

"I was informed that the personal representation designation had been withdrawn. I was not told that the OC-19 verifier nomination had been included."

"Did you object?"

"I questioned the wording."

"Do you have a record?"

Eleanor opened a folder.

She produced a day-133 correspondence note.

*The withdrawal of a proposed personal representative must not be recorded as withdrawal of the independent channel-verifier nomination unless the nominee has separately withdrawn or the original appointing authority has issued a distinct instruction.*

Edward read it carefully.

"Who received this?"

"V-17. Personal Custody."

"Was it answered?"

"No."

"Did you know whether the nominee had withdrawn?"

"No."

Eleanor placed the note back in the folder.

"The distinction was important. A person could decline to represent the principal but still be willing to verify the channel. The roles were not interchangeable."

"Did you believe the nominee was still willing?"

"I had no evidence of a further withdrawal."

Edward looked at the day-132 notice.

The document had used one reference to withdraw two different responsibilities.

Yet Eleanor had specifically warned against doing that.

"Did Charles know about your objection?"

"Yes. I sent him a copy."

"Did he respond?"

"He said the administrative records would be reconciled."

"Were they?"

Eleanor looked at him.

"You already know the answer."

The next morning, Dr Sen completed her examination of the withdrawal notice.

The main text was written by an unidentified person.

The reference AV-18 had been added separately.

The date was printed by the records system.

The signature field was blank.

"Can you compare the handwriting with the anonymous instructions?" Edward asked.

"Yes."

She displayed the results.

"The main text is consistent with the anonymous writer. The AV-18 reference is not."

"Could the reference have been added later?"

"Yes. The ink and pressure marks indicate a separate entry."

"Does that mean the anonymous writer did not intend to withdraw the verifier nomination?"

"That is possible. The writer may have drafted a general withdrawal instruction, and someone else added the reference."

"Could the reference have been added before the document was issued?"

"Certainly. The physical evidence cannot establish the sequence with enough precision."

Edward examined the report.

The document had not been signed.

The reference had been added separately.

The nomination identity remained sealed.

And the withdrawal had been recorded as though it applied to the entire personal representation process.

"Could the identity flap be opened under the current order?" he asked.

Dr Sen checked the restriction.

"Not yet. The access order permits examination of the nomination process, not disclosure of the nominee's identity."

"Who can authorise that?"

"The original channel custodian, or a court-appointed preservation officer."

"Has either been appointed?"

"No."

Edward gave a short, humourless laugh.

"Of course."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander reviewed the latest report.

Victoria sat opposite him.

"The nominee accepted consideration but never received the governing procedure," she said.

"That means the appointment was never completed."

"And then the withdrawal was recorded under the personal representation reference."

Alexander nodded.

"Which may have ended the nomination without the nominee ever receiving the procedure."

Victoria looked troubled.

"That would mean someone could have been willing to help verify the principal's identity, but was never given the information necessary to accept the responsibility."

"Possibly."

"Could the person have been the woman with the brown leather case?"

"We don't know."

"Could it have been Anna?"

"There's no evidence of that."

Victoria closed the report.

"I wish we had one document that simply told us what happened."

Alexander looked at her.

"We have documents. What we don't have is a reliable chain connecting them."

He was right.

They had learned that the missing principal had been protected by multiple layers of administrative separation.

But those layers had not produced certainty.

They had produced gaps.

And people had repeatedly filled those gaps with assumptions.

Victoria looked again at the nomination record.

"Whoever this person was, I hope they were not made responsible for a decision they never agreed to make."

Edward returned to the Valmont Estate and found Jonathan waiting.

"I found something in the day-34 nomination routing."

"What?"

"The conditional acceptance wasn't delivered directly to the appointing office."

"Where did it go?"

"V-17."

Edward frowned.

"Why?"

"The nomination was submitted through the original channel office, but the response was routed to Personal Custody for preservation."

"Was the nominee informed?"

"There is no delivery acknowledgement."

"Was the response forwarded to Eleanor?"

"No."

"To Charles?"

"Yes. The routing register records receipt at V-17."

Edward examined the entry.

The acceptance had reached Charles's office.

But the nominee had never received the governing procedure.

And the people responsible for completing the appointment had not been informed that the conditional acceptance had arrived.

"Did Charles act on it?" Edward asked.

"Not directly. The file contains a note from his office."

Jonathan showed him the note.

*Conditional acceptance received. Appointment to remain pending until receiving identity process completed.*

Edward read it.

"That is not necessarily wrong."

"No. But it was entered on day 39."

"Three days after the acceptance."

"Yes."

"Was the nominee informed?"

"No."

"Was the procedure delivered?"

"No."

Edward placed the note beside the day-132 withdrawal.

The nominee's response had been received and retained.

The appointment had remained pending.

Then, months later, the entire designation had been withdrawn under a different reference.

The nominee might never have known that their conditional acceptance had been preserved.

A second document appeared in the same routing file.

It was dated day 41.

The title was:

**OC-19 — PROCEDURE DELIVERY REQUIREMENT**

The text stated:

*The proposed verifier must receive the original procedure before accepting appointment. The appointment shall remain incomplete until the nominee acknowledges the scope and limitations of the role.*

A handwritten addition appeared beneath it.

*No substitute appointment may be inferred from the nominee's conditional acceptance.*

Edward recognised the handwriting.

It was consistent with the anonymous writer.

He read the final line.

*The identity of the nominee must not be disclosed through the receiving family allocation process.*

Jonathan looked at him.

"That could explain why the name remained sealed."

"Yes."

"But it also means someone knew the nomination existed."

"Someone knew enough to protect the nominee's identity."

Edward placed the document on the table.

The anonymous writer had insisted that the verifier's identity remain separate from the receiving family allocation process.

They had also insisted that the nominee receive the original procedure before accepting the role.

And they had warned against treating conditional acceptance as appointment.

Those instructions were consistent.

The day-132 withdrawal, however, had ignored the distinction between the verifier and the personal representative.

Someone had collapsed two roles into one administrative event.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Find out who received the day-41 procedure-delivery requirement."

Jonathan checked the routing list.

There were three recipients.

Meridian's original channel office.

Vale Private Counsel.

And V-17.

The acknowledgement fields were complete for the first two.

The third was blank.

Edward stared at the blank field.

"Charles's office received the instruction, but did not acknowledge it."

"That's what the register says."

"Was there any follow-up?"

"Yes. Day 44."

Jonathan placed the final document before him.

It was a short notice from the original channel office.

*The governing procedure has not been delivered to the proposed verifier. Appointment remains incomplete. Please confirm whether delivery will be arranged.*

A handwritten response had been added beneath it.

*No further action required. Existing administrative continuity shall be maintained.*

No signature.

No authority reference.

No explanation.

Edward looked at the wording.

The same phrase had appeared in the day-157 migration.

Administrative continuity.

It was being used before the migration had even occurred.

"Can we establish who wrote this?"

"Dr Sen is examining it."

Edward looked at the unsigned response.

The proposed verifier had accepted consideration.

The procedure had not been delivered.

The appointment had remained incomplete.

A follow-up request had been sent.

And someone had answered that no further action was required.

The identity of the person who accepted consideration was still hidden.

But Edward now had a more important question.

Who had decided that the appointment could remain incomplete indefinitely while the rest of the process continued as though the principal's communication needs had been addressed?
