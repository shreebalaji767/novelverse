# Chapter 33 — The Agreement That Named No Child

The first custody acknowledgment was found in a records cabinet that had not been opened in eleven years.

The second was not found at all.

Edward stood in the archive room of Vale International Legal Group, looking at two separate folders placed on a long table.

The first folder contained a document dated the ninth day after the births.

**TEMPORARY RECEIVING ACKNOWLEDGMENT**

**Receiving party:** Charles Vale
**Capacity:** Legal representative acting on behalf of a designated guardian
**Subject:** Infant B
**Duration:** Until further written instruction
**Identity disclosure:** Restricted

The document carried Charles Vale's signature.

Below it was another signature, belonging to a witness.

Daniel Mercer.

Edward turned the page.

There was a paragraph in smaller print.

*The receiving party acknowledges that the infant's permanent identity and family placement have not been established. No representation regarding biological relationship, parental identity, or future guardianship is made by this acknowledgment.*

Edward frowned.

This was not a permanent custody agreement.

It was an arrangement for temporary care.

But that did not explain why the child had remained with the receiving family.

He turned to the next page.

A handwritten notation appeared at the bottom.

**Original receiving guardian unavailable. Alternative arrangement authorised by originating representative.**

No name.

No date of authorisation.

Only a reference number.

EFO-SL/07.

Edward placed the document down.

The second folder was much thinner.

It contained an index card.

**INFANT A — RECEIVING ACKNOWLEDGMENT**

**Status:** Original issued.

**Copy:** Not retained.

**Receiving party:** Confidential.

**Acknowledgment returned:** No.

Edward stared at the final line.

No acknowledgment had been returned.

That could mean the receiving guardian had never signed it.

Or that the document had been returned through a different channel.

He called Jonathan Pierce.

"I've found the acknowledgment for Infant B."

"And Infant A?"

"Only an index reference. The original was issued, but no copy was retained."

"Who received it?"

"Confidential."

"Can you identify the issuing office?"

"Vale Private Counsel."

Pierce was silent for a moment.

"Then the two documents were handled differently."

"Yes."

"Do we know why?"

"Not yet."

Edward looked at the temporary acknowledgment again.

"Infant B's receiving party was told the child's identity was not established. The agreement was explicitly temporary."

"And Infant A?"

"We don't know."

"That is a significant difference."

"More than that. The second acknowledgment was never returned."

Pierce exhaled.

"Then the person who received Infant A may never have formally accepted the arrangement."

Edward looked at the index card.

"Or someone made sure we couldn't find out whether they did."

At the Valmont estate, Elena was preparing for her university interview.

She had arranged her notes into three sections.

Academic reasons.

Personal goals.

Practical arrangements.

She had also written a list of questions she wanted to ask the programme coordinator.

The list was shorter than the one she had initially prepared.

She had decided that asking twenty questions would probably make her appear more anxious than interested.

Nicholas entered the sitting room carrying a plate.

"Emergency supplies."

Elena looked at it.

Biscuits.

"Thank you."

"I've been informed that students preparing for interviews require adequate nutrition."

"Who informed you?"

"An extremely reliable source."

"Mother?"

"Possibly."

Elena took a biscuit.

Nicholas sat across from her.

"How's the preparation?"

"All right. I think I've covered the important things."

"Then stop studying."

"I have to review the programme details once more."

"You've reviewed them four times."

"Three."

"Four. I counted."

Elena gave him a doubtful look.

"Why?"

"Because I was curious how many times a person could read the same university brochure."

She laughed.

Nicholas leaned back.

"Are you nervous?"

"A little."

"That's normal."

"What if I don't get selected?"

"Then you apply for something else."

Elena looked at him.

"That's your entire strategy?"

"Yes."

"Very sophisticated."

"I prefer practical solutions."

He glanced at her notes.

"You've worked hard. Whatever happens, you haven't wasted your time."

Elena looked down.

She knew he was trying to reassure her.

But there was something else beneath his words.

He was reminding her that her future belonged to her.

Not to the DNA investigation.

Not to the Valmont family.

Not to the Aurelius family she had never met.

She smiled.

"Thank you."

Nicholas stood.

"Now finish your biscuit and go outside. You've been sitting here all morning."

"I have an interview preparation session."

"With whom?"

"Myself."

"Reschedule it."

Elena laughed again.

Isabelle was in Catherine's office when the foundation's finance director arrived.

He carried a printed budget proposal.

"Miss Valmont, I reviewed your revised figures."

Isabelle straightened.

"And?"

"The pilot programme is feasible, provided the participating schools agree to the reporting requirements."

She looked at Catherine.

"Does that mean we can proceed?"

"It means we can present it to the foundation committee," Catherine said.

The finance director placed the proposal on the desk.

"There's one issue. Your proposed support allowance varies according to the student's circumstances. The current foundation system uses fixed amounts."

"I know," Isabelle replied. "But fixed amounts may not cover the same needs in every household."

"That creates administrative complexity."

"Could we establish three support bands?"

The director considered it.

"Possibly."

Isabelle opened her notebook.

"I prepared a draft."

Catherine watched as she explained the revised system.

She did not interrupt.

Isabelle spoke clearly, acknowledged the limitations, and accepted criticism without becoming defensive.

When she finished, the finance director nodded.

"I think the committee will consider this."

"Thank you."

After he left, Catherine looked at her daughter.

"You handled that well."

Isabelle closed her notebook.

"I was nervous."

"That is not always a bad thing."

"I still have a lot to learn."

"Everyone does."

Isabelle looked at the proposal.

"Do you think I can eventually manage something like this independently?"

Catherine's expression softened.

"Yes."

"Even without the Valmont name?"

"Especially if you learn to do it without relying on the Valmont name."

Isabelle smiled faintly.

It was the kind of answer she had wanted.

Not a promise that everything would be easy.

A belief that she could become capable enough to manage it herself.

The next morning, Edward met Daniel Mercer.

The former Vale legal assistant had agreed to meet at a small café near his home.

He arrived carrying a folder.

He looked older than in the photograph Edward had seen, but his manner remained precise.

"Mr. Valmont."

"Mr. Mercer."

They shook hands.

Mercer placed the folder on the table.

"I was told you found the acknowledgment."

"Yes."

"Which one?"

"Infant B."

Mercer nodded.

"That was the one I prepared."

Edward looked at him.

"You prepared it?"

"I typed the document. Charles Vale dictated most of the wording."

"Did you witness the signing?"

"Yes."

"Was the receiving guardian present?"

"No."

"Then who signed?"

"Charles."

Edward frowned.

"But the document identifies him as a legal representative acting on behalf of a designated guardian."

"That's correct."

"Who was the guardian?"

Mercer opened his folder.

"I don't know."

Edward waited.

"Mr. Mercer, you prepared the document."

"I did."

"Surely you knew who it was intended for."

"I knew the name."

"Then tell me."

Mercer looked at him.

"I was instructed not to retain it."

"Why?"

"Because the name was not to appear in the hospital's records or the legal office's general correspondence."

"Was it written in the original?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

Mercer opened a page in his folder.

It was a photocopy of an old typing instruction.

The text was faded, but one sentence remained readable.

*Insert designated guardian's name on original only. Do not reproduce in office copy.*

Edward leaned forward.

"Was that instruction given by Charles Vale?"

"Yes."

"Did he explain why?"

"No."

"Did you question him?"

"I was a junior legal assistant. Mr. Vale did not encourage unnecessary questions."

"Yet you knew the document concerned two newborn children."

Mercer looked down.

"I knew it concerned a child whose identity had not been established. I did not know there were two until later."

"When?"

"After the ninth-day release."

"Who told you?"

"Marcus."

Edward studied him.

"What did he say?"

Mercer took a breath.

"He told me that the original plan had failed, and that two children had been placed with separate guardians. He also said that neither guardian had been given the complete circumstances."

"Did he say why?"

"He said it was intended to prevent the children from being taken again."

Edward's expression sharpened.

"Taken again?"

Mercer nodded.

"That was the word he used."

"Who had tried to take one of them before?"

"I don't know."

"Did Marcus identify the child?"

"No."

"Did he identify the person?"

"No."

Edward sat back.

"Then why did you keep this document?"

Mercer looked at the folder.

"Because I was uncomfortable with what I had been asked to do."

"Why?"

"Because I was asked to prepare a legal acknowledgment for a person who would never see it."

Edward looked at him.

"What do you mean?"

Mercer opened the folder to another page.

It was a handwritten note from Charles Vale.

**The receiving guardian is not to be contacted directly. All communication will pass through the originating representative.**

Edward read it twice.

"Who was the originating representative?"

Mercer shook his head.

"I was never told."

That afternoon, Edward returned to the Vale Historical Records Repository.

Jonathan Pierce was waiting for him.

"I've been thinking about the missing acknowledgment," Pierce said.

"Anything useful?"

"Perhaps."

He placed a document on the table.

It was a copy of the repository's old intake procedure.

"Box 114 was created to hold documents that could not be released through the normal family office channels. The original maternal pages were placed in 114-B. But the receiving acknowledgments were indexed separately."

Edward studied the page.

"Where?"

"Box 114-C."

Edward looked up.

"There was a 114-C?"

"It was never listed in the public archive catalogue."

"Why?"

"Because it was classified as a restricted legal instrument rather than a family record."

"What's the access condition?"

Pierce pointed to the final paragraph.

**Access permitted only upon written authority of the originating representative or by order of a competent court.**

Edward read it carefully.

"Who is the originating representative?"

"Unknown."

"Then we're stuck."

"Not necessarily."

Pierce opened another file.

"The repository received a request for Box 114-C eleven years ago."

"From whom?"

He turned the page.

**Martha Voss.**

Edward frowned.

"Martha?"

"Yes."

"What did she want?"

"She requested access to the ninth-day receiving acknowledgments."

"Was she granted access?"

"No. Her application was rejected."

"Did she explain why she wanted them?"

Pierce pointed to the handwritten note.

**I need to verify whether the receiving parties were informed of the temporary nature of the arrangement.**

Edward stared at it.

"She knew."

"She suspected."

"Why didn't she tell us?"

"Perhaps she didn't know the documents existed at the time she first spoke with you."

Edward looked at the date.

The request had been made six years after the births.

That meant Martha had continued investigating long after leaving the hospital.

He called her immediately.

Martha Voss answered on the fourth ring.

"Mr. Valmont?"

"Martha, I need to ask you about a request you made to the Vale repository eleven years ago."

There was a pause.

"Which request?"

"Box 114-C. The receiving acknowledgments."

Another pause.

Then she sighed.

"So you've found that."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because I never saw the documents."

"You suspected they existed."

"I knew there had to be something."

"Why?"

Martha's voice became quieter.

"Because the hospital staff were never given copies of the final acknowledgments. We were told the legal office would retain them. But the nursery release procedure required confirmation that the receiving parties had understood the terms."

Edward sat down.

"Did they?"

"I don't know."

"Then why did you request the records?"

"Because I remembered something from the ninth day."

"What?"

Martha took a breath.

"One of the receiving parties asked whether the child would be returned."

Edward went still.

"Who?"

"I didn't see the person's face clearly."

"Was it the man or the woman?"

"The woman."

Edward glanced at Pierce.

"What did she say exactly?"

"She asked how long she would have to keep the child."

"And what did someone tell her?"

"That it would depend on the verification."

"Did she agree?"

"She asked another question."

"What question?"

Martha was silent for several seconds.

Then she said:

"She asked whether the child's mother knew where she was."

Edward felt his grip tighten around the phone.

"What was the answer?"

"Someone told her that the mother had been informed."

"Was that true?"

"I don't know."

"Who told her?"

"I think it was Charles Vale."

Edward looked at Pierce.

Martha continued.

"But there was something else."

"What?"

"The woman didn't sound surprised that she was being asked to take a child."

Edward frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"She sounded as though she had been waiting for one."

That evening, the Aurelius family received Edward's report.

Alexander read it in silence.

Victoria sat beside him.

Adrian stood near the fireplace, while Lucien and Damien listened from the opposite sofa.

Julian had been told he could remain, provided he did not interrupt.

He had not interrupted.

When Alexander finished reading, he placed the report on the table.

"One of the receiving guardians asked whether the child would be returned."

Victoria looked at him.

"And was she told that the mother had been informed?"

"Apparently."

"Which mother?"

"We don't know."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

"Then someone may have told a woman that the child's mother knew where she was, even though neither of us had been told the truth."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"That is possible."

Lucien leaned forward.

"Could the woman have been a relative?"

"Possibly," Adrian said.

"Someone from the Valmont side?"

Richard's name was not mentioned, but the implication was clear.

"Or someone connected to the Aurelius family," Damien replied.

Alexander shook his head.

"We cannot assume either."

Julian looked at the report.

"Did the woman want the baby?"

Everyone turned toward him.

"What do you mean?" Victoria asked.

"She asked how long she had to keep the child. But Martha said she sounded as though she had been waiting for one."

He looked at the photograph of the two infants.

"Maybe she wanted a child."

The room became quiet.

It was a simple observation.

But it opened another possibility.

The receiving guardian might not have been selected solely because she was trusted.

She might have been selected because she had a reason to accept a child.

A reason that had nothing to do with the Aurelius or Valmont families.

Alexander looked at Adrian.

"Find out whether any woman connected to the original arrangements had requested guardianship or adoption through a private legal office eighteen years ago."

"That could involve a considerable number of people."

"Then narrow it down using the date and the legal office."

Adrian nodded.

Victoria looked toward Julian.

"That's a thoughtful question."

Julian shrugged.

"I just wondered."

He looked at the photograph again.

For the first time, he imagined that his sister might have grown up in a family that had been waiting for a child of their own.

Perhaps she had been loved.

Perhaps she had been wanted.

Perhaps the people who raised her had never known that another family was searching for her.

He hoped that was true.

He also hoped that if they ever found her, she would not feel that she had to choose between the people who raised her and the people who had been looking for her.

At the Valmont estate, Elena was sitting in the garden with Isabelle.

A light breeze moved through the trees.

Isabelle had brought her revised scholarship proposal.

Elena was reading it.

"Your new budget is much clearer."

"Do you think so?"

"Yes. You've separated the administrative costs from the student allowances."

"That was Catherine's suggestion."

"It's a good suggestion."

Isabelle looked at the page.

"I've also added a review process."

"Who will review the applications?"

"A committee from the participating schools and two foundation representatives."

"Will you be on it?"

Isabelle shook her head.

"I don't think I should be."

"Why?"

"Because I don't want students to feel that they're being judged by the chairman's daughter."

Elena nodded.

"That's sensible."

Isabelle glanced at her.

"What about your interview?"

"Tomorrow."

"Are you prepared?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

Elena smiled.

"I've decided that if they ask me why I want to go, I'll tell them the truth."

"Which is?"

"I want to study something different, become more independent, and see how people live and work somewhere else."

Isabelle nodded.

"That's a good answer."

They sat quietly for a moment.

Then Isabelle asked, "Do you ever think about what happened before we were brought home?"

Elena looked toward the garden.

"Sometimes."

"Do you think we were together?"

"I don't know."

"Do you think someone deliberately separated us?"

Elena considered the question.

"Maybe."

Isabelle looked down.

"Would you want to know?"

"Yes."

"Even if the answer is unpleasant?"

Elena turned toward her.

"I think we deserve to know what happened. But I don't think knowing everything will automatically make our lives better."

Isabelle nodded slowly.

"That's what I'm afraid of."

Elena closed the folder.

"Whatever happened, it wasn't our decision."

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Elena stood.

"Come on."

"Where?"

"Inside. I want to show you something."

"What?"

"A recipe."

Isabelle looked confused.

"A recipe?"

"Yes. I've been trying to make the lemon cake from the old family cookbook. Nicholas says the last one was too dry."

"Was it?"

"Very."

Isabelle laughed.

"Then perhaps we should begin with something easier."

Elena smiled.

"That sounds like an insult."

"It's practical advice."

They walked toward the house together.

At 11:47 that night, Edward received a message from an unknown number.

There was no introduction.

No explanation.

Only a photograph.

He opened it.

The image showed a document with a faded hospital seal.

At the top was the heading:

**RECEIVING GUARDIAN — INFANT A**

Below it was a handwritten name.

The first letters were clear.

**MARGARET...**

Edward stared at the screen.

The name could have belonged to several people.

But beneath it was another line.

**Relationship to child: NONE.**

He enlarged the image.

A handwritten note appeared in the margin.

*The receiving party has requested that the child's identity remain undisclosed until the mother makes contact.*

Edward read it again.

Then another message arrived.

**You have been looking for the wrong mother.**

Edward immediately called the number.

It was switched off.

He looked at the photograph again.

The receiving guardian had no stated family relationship to the child.

And she had apparently been waiting for the child's mother to make contact.

But the most troubling detail was at the bottom of the document.

The receiving guardian's signature had been witnessed by someone whose name Edward recognised.

**Marcus Morelli.**

And beside his signature, in handwriting that did not belong to him, were four words:

**She must not be told.**
