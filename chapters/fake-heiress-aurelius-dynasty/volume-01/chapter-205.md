# **Chapter 205 — The Authority Behind the Continuity**

The original authority record was released at 8:48.

Not the complete record.

Only a certified extract, approved by Dr Eleanor Vale, with the identity fields and personal instruction sealed.

Edward read the cover sheet first.

**Reference:** PAO/29/02\
**Date:** Day 29\
**Issuing office:** Programme Authority Office\
**Subject:** Protected correspondence continuity\
**Purpose:** Preservation of a restricted personal communication arrangement during possible termination of receiving-route participation.

The extract contained three sections.

The first established the need for continuity.

The second defined the permitted communication functions.

The third specified what the arrangement could not do.

Edward turned to the third section.

**The continuing correspondence arrangement shall not:**

- Establish or confirm the identity of a receiving candidate.
- Create a receiving appointment.
- Authorise placement or allocation.
- Replace the protected principal's personal acknowledgement.
- Convert an administrative contact location into a verified candidate address.
- Permit the transfer of protected contact information for an unrelated administrative purpose.

Edward stopped.

The final restriction was unusually specific.

He read it again.

“That's not a general warning,” Sophie said.

“No.”

“It names the exact kind of transfer that happened in RA-36/11.”

“Yes.”

Edward checked the issue date.

Day 29.

Seven days before the address was entered into the candidate's file.

The authority had anticipated the risk.

Or, at the very least, had explicitly prohibited it.

At 9:30, Dr Eleanor Vale joined the review.

She had brought the original preservation certificate.

“I authorised the extract,” she said. “The underlying identity schedule remains sealed.”

“Can you confirm whether PAO/29/02 was validly issued?”

“Yes.”

“Was it issued under the same authority as PI-07?”

“No. PI-07 was the personal instruction. PAO/29/02 was the administrative authority to implement a limited continuity arrangement under that instruction.”

Edward looked at the distinction.

“So one record established the personal requirement, and the other authorised the administrative process.”

“Correct.”

“Were the two authorities issued by the same person?”

“The identities remain sealed. I cannot certify common authorship.”

“Were they required to be separate?”

“The schedule required two distinct confirmations. Their functions were separate, and neither could substitute for the other.”

Edward returned to the extract.

“Did PAO/29/02 authorise the use of CR-29/08 as a candidate address?”

“No.”

“Did it authorise Programme Administration to use the location for a notification concerning RA-36/11?”

“No.”

“Could the office have believed that continuity permission covered the transfer?”

“It could have misunderstood the permission. That would not change its scope.”

Edward nodded.

“Was the restriction communicated to Programme Administration?”

“Yes. The distribution register confirms receipt.”

“Who received it?”

“Programme Authority Office, Restricted Personal Communication Administration, the preservation liaison, and the designated administrative continuity office.”

“Was Vale Private Counsel included?”

“No.”

“Was the general Programme Administration Review Desk included?”

“No.”

“Then how did the restriction reach the people processing RA-36/11?”

Eleanor opened the distribution record.

“Through a separate implementation notice.”

She placed it on the table.

**PAO/29/04 — Administrative Continuity Notice.**

The notice had been distributed to Programme Administration on day 30.

It stated that the protected correspondence location could remain active for its original purpose, but could not be reused as a receiving-candidate address.

Edward looked at Sophie.

The restriction had been sent to the office that later changed the task.

At 10:15, Sophie examined the acknowledgement record.

Programme Administration had received PAO/29/04 on day 30.

The acknowledgement was entered by a shared administrative account.

**PA-REVIEW-06.**

No individual recipient was named.

Edward checked the day-36 task.

The same account had modified the instruction that allowed CR-29/08 to be copied into RA-36/11.

The account had acknowledged the restriction.

Six days later, the account had processed a task that conflicted with it.

That did not establish who had acted.

It did establish that the office had received the rule.

“Can we say the revision knowingly violated the restriction?” Sophie asked.

“Not yet.”

“Why not?”

“Because the account is shared. We don't know which person read the notice, who changed the task, or whether the person changing it had access to the complete restriction.”

“But the notice was acknowledged.”

“By the office. Not by an identified individual.”

Edward added a note.

**Office-level receipt established. Individual awareness not established.**

He wanted the distinction preserved.

An administrative system could have received a warning without every person using the system understanding it.

That did not excuse the transfer.

But it mattered when determining responsibility.

At 11:40, Eleanor produced a second extract.

It concerned the original purpose of the correspondence arrangement.

**PAO/29/02 — Schedule B**

The arrangement had been created for a participant whose involvement in a proposed receiving route might end before an underlying personal communication matter had been resolved.

The participant would retain the ability to:

- Receive procedural correspondence.
- Ask questions about the continuing communication arrangement.
- Request corrections to administrative descriptions.
- Preserve records of original communications.
- Receive confirmation that correspondence had been received by the responsible office.

The arrangement did not require the participant to remain involved in the receiving route.

It did not depend on the participant accepting an appointment.

It did not establish that the participant had agreed to any placement.

Edward studied the wording.

“Was this the person who submitted RR-47/02?”

“Yes.”

“Can you confirm that?”

“The protected participant associated with the continuing correspondence arrangement was the submitter.”

“Was that person the candidate under RA-36/11?”

“No.”

“Was that person the individual who refused the envelope?”

“We cannot establish that.”

“Was the protected participant the person whose address was entered into the candidate record?”

“The address reference was associated with the protected correspondence arrangement. That does not establish who physically controlled the location or who answered the door.”

Edward nodded.

The distinctions were becoming clearer.

The protected participant had been granted a communication arrangement.

The candidate had been proposed for a separate receiving role.

The address had been copied between the records.

The person at the door remained unidentified.

And none of those facts established a biological relationship, family identity or even personal acquaintance between the people involved.

At 13:05, Edward asked about the original reason for creating the arrangement.

“Why was it necessary to preserve communication after a receiving-route role ended?” he asked.

Eleanor looked at the sealed portion of the file.

“The original authority identified a risk that the administrative end of a person's participation could be mistaken for the end of their personal communication rights.”

“Was that risk hypothetical?”

“I cannot discuss the personal circumstances beyond the authorised extract.”

“Was there a dispute?”

“The extract does not establish one.”

“Was there a safety concern?”

“It does not establish one.”

“Was the participant asking to remain involved?”

“That is not recorded in the extract.”

Edward paused.

“Then what do we know?”

“That the issuing authority considered continued communication necessary, independent of receiving-route participation.”

“Did the participant request it?”

“The record does not establish whether the arrangement originated from the participant's own request or from the originating authority's decision.”

Edward looked down at his notes.

The arrangement could have been protective.

It could have been precautionary.

It could have been intended to preserve a right the participant had not personally requested.

The record did not allow him to choose among those explanations.

He moved to the next question.

“Did the authority name a person responsible for maintaining the arrangement?”

“Yes.”

“Can we see that designation?”

“The office designation is available. The personal identity remains restricted.”

Eleanor turned the page.

**Responsible office:** Restricted Personal Communication Administration.

**Original custodian:** Dr Samuel Hale.

**Implementation liaison:** Restricted Governance Review.

The names of the originating authorities remained sealed.

Edward looked at the office designation.

“Was Programme Administration authorised to administer the arrangement?”

“No.”

“Was Vale Private Counsel?”

“No.”

“Was Marianne Cross?”

“No. She was a correspondence liaison. Her role was to preserve the transmission distinction, not to control the arrangement.”

Edward made another note.

The arrangement had a responsible office.

It had a custodian.

It had a defined purpose.

And it had never been transferred to the office that later copied its address.

At 14:50, the Programme Authority Office sent a new response.

It concerned the day-36 modification.

The office had completed a preliminary review of the task history.

The revised instruction had been submitted through PA-REVIEW-06.

The original task had been returned because the candidate's contact details could not be confirmed.

The revised task had been accepted as an administrative workaround.

However, the review found no record of a competent authority approving the use of CR-29/08 for RA-36/11.

The office also confirmed that the person who submitted the revision had not been identified.

Edward read the conclusion.

**The revised task should not have been treated as a substitute for contact verification.**

Sophie let out a breath.

“At least they've acknowledged it.”

“They've acknowledged the procedural failure.”

“Isn't that enough?”

“It's enough to correct the record. It isn't enough to identify who made the decision.”

Edward opened a new action.

The office was required to preserve all records relating to the shared review account, including workstation access, task comments, revision history and any retained administrative communications.

He added a second requirement.

The office must not describe the candidate as having refused, withdrawn or accepted the proposed role.

The candidate's status remained unresolved.

At 16:10, Elena completed her revised pilot build.

The platform now displayed a warning when a user attempted to transfer information from a restricted record.

**Transfer review**

Original record

Valid source

Protected correspondence reference

Transfer permission

Not established

Destination verification

Pending

The source record remains valid for its original purpose. Transfer is not permitted until separate authority and destination verification are established.

The programme manager read the warning.

“Can the user override it?”

“Only with a recorded approval from an authorised reviewer.”

“What if the reviewer is unavailable?”

“Then the transfer remains pending.”

He looked at her.

“You understand that this will frustrate people.”

Elena smiled.

“I've met people.”

The manager laughed.

“Fair point.”

He approved the revised pilot.

Elena saved the build.

For a moment, she sat quietly.

The platform was not going to solve every problem.

But it could prevent an incomplete record from becoming a false conclusion.

That was worth something.

At 18:25, Edward received a call from Adrian Aurelius.

The conversation was brief.

“Any progress?” Adrian asked.

“Yes. We have established that a protected correspondence address was reused in a separate candidate file without verified permission.”

“Does that identify the protected participant?”

“No.”

“Does it identify the candidate?”

“No.”

“Does it connect either person to Elena?”

“No.”

Adrian was silent for a moment.

“Thank you for being precise.”

Edward looked at the open file.

“We have to be.”

“Has anything changed in the biological investigation?”

“Nothing that would support a conclusion.”

“Then we'll wait.”

Edward appreciated that answer.

The Aurelius family had every reason to want certainty.

But wanting certainty did not make a record conclusive.

Before ending the call, Adrian asked one more question.

“Is Elena doing well?”

Edward glanced at the latest update from Singapore.

“Her academic programme is progressing. Her pilot has been approved for further testing.”

“Good.”

There was no demand to contact her.

No request to send a gift.

No attempt to turn an uncertain investigation into a family claim.

Edward ended the call.

At 19:02, Eleanor sent a final message.

It contained an index reference Edward had not seen before.

**PAO/28/17 — Originating Personal Communication Authority.**

**Date:** Day 28.

**Relationship:** Precedent authority for PAO/29/02.

**Access:** Restricted identity schedule.

Edward opened the reference.

A note appeared beneath it.

**The day-29 continuity arrangement was not an independent decision. It implemented a personal communication requirement established the previous day.**

He looked at the date.

Day 28.

Before the correspondence location.

Before the candidate proposal.

Before the address transfer.

Before the notification that had never been delivered.

The original authority had existed first.

Edward requested the certified extract.

The response came back immediately.

**Custodian review required.**

He closed the request and returned to the chronology.

For months, the investigation had followed administrative records forward.

A candidate proposal.

An incomplete appointment.

A protected correspondence arrangement.

A copied address.

A failed notification.

Now the records pointed backward.

The first decision had not been made by Programme Administration.

It had not been made by the review desk.

It had not been made by the person who entered the address.

It had been made on day 28, under a personal communication authority whose identity remained sealed.

And until that original requirement was understood, the later administrative actions could not be interpreted correctly.
