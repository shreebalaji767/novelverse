# **Chapter 152 — The Instruction That Allowed Everything to Continue**

Edward read the unsigned response again.

*No further action required. Existing administrative continuity shall be maintained.*

It was dated day 44.

The proposed verifier had not received the governing procedure.

The appointment remained incomplete.

The original channel was not independently verified.

Yet the response instructed the offices involved to continue their existing arrangements.

Edward placed it beside the day-41 delivery requirement.

"Whoever wrote this knew the appointment was incomplete," Jonathan said.

"Yes."

"And still decided that nothing needed to change."

"That is what the wording says."

Jonathan turned to the distribution record.

"The response was sent to the original channel office, Vale Private Counsel, and V-17. The original channel office marked it received. Vale Private Counsel marked it received. V-17 did not acknowledge."

"Again."

"Again."

Edward looked at the repeated blank acknowledgement.

The pattern was becoming familiar.

Instructions were sent to V-17. They were received or retained without a proper acknowledgement. Later summaries referred to the instructions as though their contents had been accepted.

He picked up his pen.

"Find the original channel office's follow-up."

The follow-up had been issued on day 47.

It was more direct than the previous notice.

**SUBJECT: OC-19 — APPOINTMENT STATUS**

*The proposed verifier has not received the governing procedure.*

*Conditional acceptance must not be treated as completed appointment.*

*No alternative verifier has been confirmed.*

*Please identify the authority under which personal communication may continue without independent verification.*

The final paragraph contained a warning.

*If no valid authority is supplied, substantive communication must remain suspended.*

Edward read it slowly.

This was not an ordinary reminder.

The original channel office had recognised that the process could not proceed indefinitely without an appointed verifier.

"Who received this?" he asked.

Jonathan checked the distribution list.

"Meridian, Vale Private Counsel, and V-17 again."

"Any response?"

"Two."

He displayed the first.

It came from Meridian's preservation director.

*The original channel register remains preserved. No personal identity determination has been made.*

The second came from Vale Private Counsel.

*The proposed verifier's conditional acceptance remains on file. Administrative continuity shall continue under existing instructions.*

Edward frowned.

"That's the same answer."

"Almost."

"Did they identify the authority?"

"No."

"Did they explain why the procedure wasn't delivered?"

"No."

Edward looked at the third recipient field.

V-17 had not responded.

"Then the office responsible for the instruction never answered the question."

Thomas Greer agreed to meet Edward again.

This time, Edward brought the day-44 response and the day-47 follow-up.

Greer examined both.

"The day-44 response is not a legal approval," he said.

"What is it?"

"An administrative instruction. It directs the offices to maintain existing arrangements, but it does not identify the authority permitting those arrangements."

"Could it have been issued by Charles Vale?"

"It could have come from his office."

"Would he have had authority to issue it?"

"Not to waive the original-channel requirements."

"Could he have maintained temporary preservation?"

"Yes. That was within the limited custody arrangement."

"Could he have allowed substantive communication to continue without an appointed verifier?"

"No."

Edward placed the day-47 response before him.

"Vale Private Counsel says administrative continuity could continue under existing instructions."

Greer read the sentence.

"That wording is too broad."

"Why?"

"Because it does not distinguish preservation from communication. The original arrangement allowed the documents to remain safe. It did not allow the process to proceed as if the principal had been contacted."

"Did you approve this wording?"

"No."

"Did you know it was being used?"

Greer hesitated.

"I knew that the phrase administrative continuity was appearing in summaries. I did not know it was being used to support the original-channel process."

"Did you object?"

"I asked for clarification."

"Was it provided?"

"No."

Edward closed his notebook.

"Then someone used a general preservation instruction to answer a question about personal communication authority."

Greer nodded.

"That appears to be what happened."

At Meridian, Dr Mira Sen had completed her handwriting examination.

The day-44 response had been written in two stages.

The main sentence was consistent with a routine administrative clerk.

The final instruction was different.

*Existing administrative continuity shall be maintained.*

That sentence was written by the same unidentified hand found on the day-48, day-118, and day-152 instructions.

Edward examined the report.

"Can we establish whether the anonymous writer added it before or after the response was approved?"

"Not precisely. But the ink is consistent with a later addition."

"Could the clerk have written the main response and left the final line blank?"

"That is possible."

"Could someone have added the sentence after the response had already been issued?"

"Also possible."

"Then we cannot establish who authorised the addition."

"Correct."

Dr Sen showed him another feature.

"The final line crosses a faint impression from the sheet beneath it. That suggests the document was completed while another page was underneath."

"Can you identify that page?"

"Not yet. The impression is incomplete."

Edward looked at the image.

"Could it be the original procedure?"

"Not enough of the text remains to say."

The next lead came from Daniel Mercer.

He had processed several of the administrative continuity entries and had previously admitted changing the OR-28 status wording.

Edward asked him whether he remembered the day-44 instruction.

Daniel studied the document.

"I remember this phrase."

"Who supplied it?"

"I don't know."

"Did you write it?"

"No."

"Did you enter it into the system?"

"I may have recorded the response after it was received."

"Did you understand it as permission to continue communication without verification?"

Daniel shook his head.

"I understood it as permission to keep the administrative task open while the appointment was pending."

"Was the task actually open?"

"Yes."

"Was the verifier appointed?"

"No."

"Was the principal contacted?"

"Not according to the records."

"Then what did continuity mean?"

Daniel looked at the page.

"Keeping the existing process from being marked as abandoned."

Edward studied him.

"Did anyone tell you that the principal's response was no longer required?"

"Not at that stage."

"When did you first see that wording?"

"Later. In the day-47 status summary."

"Who prepared it?"

"I processed the summary. I didn't write the underlying instruction."

"Did you question the phrase?"

"I thought it referred to administrative preservation."

Edward leaned forward.

"Daniel, you have used that explanation before. But the same wording repeatedly appeared in records concerning personal authority. Did you ever check whether the original instructions permitted that?"

Daniel looked uncomfortable.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because the task was presented as a continuation of an existing arrangement. I was not asked to make a new identity determination."

Edward closed the folder.

The answer explained how the wording could pass through an administrative office.

It did not explain who had created it.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria received an update from Edward.

She read the day-47 notice and the response from Vale Private Counsel.

"Someone kept saying the process could continue," she said.

Alexander looked at the page.

"While the person responsible for verifying the principal's communication remained unappointed."

"Yes."

"Did they ever deliver the procedure?"

"No."

Victoria put the report down.

"Then what exactly was continuing?"

Alexander considered the question.

"Document preservation. Temporary care arrangements. Administrative reporting. Possibly correspondence handling."

"But not a verified personal decision."

"Correct."

Victoria looked toward the family library.

For years, she had imagined the missing child growing up somewhere, perhaps knowing nothing about the family searching for her.

Now she was confronted with another possibility: someone had tried to communicate with a protected individual but had never completed the process required to establish that the communication had reached them.

She did not know whether this person was connected to her own daughter.

She did not know whether the requests from C-19 came from the principal.

But she could understand the injustice of asking someone to make a decision without giving them the original information.

"Do we know who the proposed verifier was?" she asked.

"Not yet."

"Then that should be the next priority."

Alexander nodded.

At the Valmont Estate, Jonathan found the original day-44 routing envelope.

It had been stored separately from the response.

The envelope contained a delivery instruction.

**PERSONAL DELIVERY — V-17 WEST CORRIDOR**

The recipient field was blank.

But the reverse contained a handwritten note.

*Deliver to the individual maintaining the original procedure control copy.*

Edward looked at the wording.

"Who maintained the control copy?"

"Margaret Voss."

"Did she receive it?"

"The delivery record doesn't say."

"Was she at V-17 on day 44?"

Jonathan checked the visitor register.

"No. She was at Meridian."

"Then who maintained the original procedure control copy at V-17?"

Jonathan opened the day-44 custody index.

There was a second name.

**CUSTODY OFFICER: PETER ASHFORD.**

Edward recognised the name.

Ashford had previously admitted using Eleanor's open account during the migration process under instructions from Thomas Greer.

He had also handled the personal representation documents.

"Find him," Edward said.

Peter Ashford arrived at the estate two days later.

He looked at the day-44 delivery instruction and frowned.

"I remember this."

"Did you receive the envelope?"

"Yes."

"Who delivered it?"

"I don't remember."

"Was it the woman with the brown leather case?"

Peter shook his head.

"I can't say."

"Did you open it?"

"No. The instruction said personal delivery to the individual maintaining the control copy. I accepted it for custody."

"Did you deliver it to Margaret Voss?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because she wasn't the custodian at V-17."

Edward placed the instruction before him.

"Who was?"

"At that time, the control copy was held in a separate cabinet under temporary custody."

"Who had access?"

"Charles Vale, the restricted records officer, and myself."

"Did Charles collect the envelope?"

Peter thought for a moment.

"Yes. I believe he did."

"Did he sign?"

"No. He took it from the cabinet."

"Was there a collection record?"

Peter shook his head.

"Not that I remember."

Edward watched him carefully.

"Do you know what was inside?"

"I didn't open it."

"Did Charles tell you?"

"He said it concerned the original procedure and that it was not to be entered into the general correspondence register."

"Did you question that?"

"At the time, I believed it was a protected personal document."

Edward looked at the date.

Day 44.

The same day the anonymous instruction had said administrative continuity should be maintained.

"Was the original procedure in the cabinet?"

"Yes."

"Was it removed?"

Peter hesitated.

"I don't know."

"Did you see it after day 44?"

"No."

Edward leaned forward.

"Then Charles may have collected the control copy of the original procedure on the same day that the instruction to maintain administrative continuity was delivered."

Peter nodded slowly.

"That is possible."

The control-copy inventory was located in Margaret Voss's old records.

It confirmed that the original procedure had been prepared on day 33.

A control copy had been issued to Meridian.

A second copy had been retained by Margaret.

A third copy had been delivered to V-17 for preservation.

The V-17 copy had been collected on day 44.

The collection field was blank.

The inventory contained a later entry:

**DAY 61 — CONTROL COPY RETURNED TO RESTRICTED PRESERVATION.**

The return was signed by Charles Vale.

Edward looked at the sequence.

Charles had collected the control copy on day 44.

He had returned it on day 61.

During those seventeen days, the procedure had remained outside the ordinary preservation system.

The day-47 summary had been prepared during that interval.

And the anonymous instruction preventing release had appeared on day 48.

Jonathan looked at the inventory.

"Do you think Charles held the procedure to keep it from being delivered?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

The evidence established custody.

It did not establish what Charles had done with the document or why he had collected it.

"Find out whether the day-44 envelope contained the control copy or a separate instruction."

Jonathan nodded.

Dr Sen examined the original envelope and its remaining contents.

The envelope contained two sheets.

The first was the day-44 instruction.

The second was a transfer slip.

It read:

**ORIGINAL PROCEDURE CONTROL COPY — TEMPORARY PERSONAL CUSTODY**

**REASON:** Pending review of communication status.

**RELEASE CONDITION:** Independent verification or documented principal acknowledgement.

**COLLECTOR:** C.V.

Edward looked at the slip.

"Charles collected the control copy."

"Yes," Dr Sen replied.

"Was the transfer authorised?"

"The slip contains no approval signature."

"Could the custody have been valid under the existing arrangement?"

"That depends on the scope of the temporary custody authority."

Edward remembered the day-34 instruction.

Charles could preserve documents.

He could not use custody to control the principal's communication.

"Can you determine whether the control copy was opened?"

"No. The envelope was not sealed when it was collected, and the paper itself shows no reliable evidence of whether it was read."

Edward looked at the release condition.

Independent verification or documented principal acknowledgement.

Neither had occurred.

Yet the document had been held for seventeen days.

He placed the transfer slip beside the day-48 anonymous instruction.

The dates were close.

The wording was related.

But the writer remained unknown.

That evening, Jonathan brought one final piece of information.

The day-61 return of the control copy had been accompanied by a short statement from Charles Vale.

*The original procedure is restored to preservation custody. No principal acknowledgement has been received. Independent verification remains outstanding.*

Edward read it.

Charles had acknowledged the central problem.

He had not claimed that the principal had responded.

He had not claimed that the verifier had been appointed.

He had simply returned the document.

"That doesn't absolve him," Jonathan said.

"No."

"But it complicates the idea that he was trying to destroy the procedure."

"Yes."

Edward looked at the statement again.

Perhaps Charles had wanted to review the procedure.

Perhaps he had been trying to manage an increasingly complicated administrative arrangement.

Perhaps he had deliberately delayed communication.

The records did not yet establish which.

Then Jonathan pointed to the bottom of the statement.

There was a final handwritten line.

*Control copy returned. Original release request remains unresolved.*

The handwriting matched the anonymous writer.

Edward looked up.

"Someone added this after Charles returned it."

"Yes."

"And they knew the release request was still unresolved."

Jonathan nodded.

Edward placed the statement in its sleeve.

The original procedure had moved through several hands.

It had been collected, retained, and returned.

The person who wrote the anonymous restrictions had continued to monitor its custody.

And the proposed verifier had still not received it.

Edward turned to the day-36 conditional acceptance.

The nominee had agreed to be considered only after receiving the governing procedure.

The procedure had been available.

Someone had held it.

And someone had decided that the existing arrangement could continue without completing the appointment.

The question was no longer only who had prevented the procedure from being delivered.

It was whether the proposed verifier had ever been given the chance to withdraw their conditional acceptance before their nomination was later recorded as withdrawn.
