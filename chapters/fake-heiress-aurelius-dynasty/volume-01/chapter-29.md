# Chapter 29 — The Register She Kept

Martha Voss did not sleep particularly well that night.

The wooden box remained on her kitchen table.

She had taken it down from the cupboard after receiving the letter from the Valmont legal office. She had expected to look through its contents, perhaps confirm a few dates, and then return everything to its original place.

Instead, she had spent nearly two hours staring at a single document.

The private nursery release register.

It was not an original hospital record.

The original had remained at St. Catherine's Medical Centre, where it had eventually disappeared from the administrative archive. Martha's copy had been made using an old office duplicating machine, shortly before the register was transferred to the hospital's central records department.

She had kept it because someone had instructed her to destroy the copy.

At the time, she had considered that instruction strange.

Now, eighteen years later, she understood that the document might be important.

She opened the wooden box again.

The register was wrapped in brown paper.

She removed it carefully.

The pages were yellowed, and the ink had faded in places, but the important entries remained readable.

Two infants.

Two receiving guardians.

Two signatures.

And one additional handwritten note.

Martha had seen that note many times.

She had never understood it.

**RELEASE AUTHORISATION VALID ONLY AFTER MATERNAL IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMATION.**

Below it, in smaller handwriting:

**MATERNAL CONFIRMATION DOES NOT CONSTITUTE INFANT IDENTITY CONFIRMATION.**

Martha turned the page.

The first infant's entry was partially obscured by a fold.

The second was clearer.

She looked at the signatures.

One was unfamiliar.

The other was not.

Her hands became cold.

She placed the document back inside the paper wrapping.

Then she called the same number again.

This time, nobody answered.

Edward arrived at Valmont Holdings before eight.

He had barely finished reviewing the previous day's information when Sebastian entered his office.

"You look tired."

"Good morning to you too."

"Did Martha agree to the interview?"

"Not yet."

Sebastian sat down.

"Then why are you here so early?"

"Because she contacted someone after receiving our letter."

"Morelli?"

"Possibly. The number hasn't been identified."

Sebastian looked at the documents spread across Edward's desk.

"She has something."

"I think so."

"Why?"

"Because she was willing to speak to someone immediately after receiving our letter, and that person told her not to meet us."

Sebastian studied him.

"That's an assumption."

"Yes. A reasonable one, but still an assumption."

Edward opened the visitor register.

"Martha had administrative access to the private nursery on three occasions. The second was the seventh day. The third was the ninth."

"The same dates connected to Eleanor and Morelli."

"Correct."

"Did she sign the register?"

"We haven't recovered the original visitor sheets."

"Then we need them."

Edward nodded.

He turned another page.

"The ninth-day release document is the most important missing record. If Martha has a copy, it might tell us who received the children."

"Could it establish which child was biologically related to which family?"

"No."

Sebastian looked at him.

"Not even if the receiving guardians are named?"

"No. A receiving guardian's identity tells us who took custody of an infant. It does not establish biological identity."

Edward tapped the photocopy.

"We need to keep those questions separate."

Sebastian nodded.

"Understood."

A knock came at the door.

Nicholas entered carrying a paper bag.

"I brought breakfast."

Edward glanced at the clock.

"You brought breakfast at eight in the morning?"

"Some people enjoy eating."

"Some people enjoy working."

"Those people are usually unpleasant."

Nicholas placed the bag on the desk.

Sebastian looked inside.

"Pastries?"

"Yes."

"Thank you."

Nicholas sat down.

"What's happening?"

Edward gave him a brief summary.

Nicholas listened without interrupting.

When Edward finished, he leaned back.

"So someone told Martha not to meet us."

"We don't know that."

"But you suspect it."

"Yes."

Nicholas picked up a pastry.

"Then perhaps the person is worried about what she kept."

Edward looked at him.

"That's what I think."

Nicholas took a bite.

"Could she be protecting someone?"

"Possibly."

"Could she be protecting herself?"

"Also possible."

Nicholas nodded.

"Then don't treat her like a suspect."

Edward looked at him for a moment.

"I wasn't planning to."

"Good."

Nicholas swallowed.

"People who are frightened don't always tell the truth. Sometimes they simply tell you what they think will keep everyone safe."

Edward glanced at the papers.

That was a reasonable observation.

Martha Voss might have been involved in the original arrangements.

She might have been an innocent administrative employee who had been ordered to preserve a record.

Or she might have known far more than she was willing to admit.

They would not know until they spoke to her.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was reviewing the latest report with Adrian.

There was no new information about the missing daughter.

No confirmed location.

No verified biological identity.

No name.

But the report contained a new detail.

Martha Voss.

Victoria read the name.

"She worked at the hospital?"

"Yes. Patient administration."

"Did she know Eleanor Vale?"

"We don't know."

"Did she know Morelli?"

"She was listed as his emergency contact on a residential lease application several years after he left the Family Office."

Victoria looked up.

"That's a personal connection."

"It suggests one."

"Could she know where he is?"

"Possibly."

Victoria placed the report down.

"Then we should speak with her."

Adrian shook his head slightly.

"Edward is arranging that."

"I know. I only mean that we should be prepared if she has information."

"We are."

Victoria looked towards the window.

The garden was bright with morning sunlight.

She had once imagined that finding her daughter would be the end of the waiting.

Now she understood that it might only be the beginning of another difficult process.

Even if they discovered which child had been recorded under the Aurelius identity, they would still need to establish whether that record was correct.

Even if they established the biological identity, they would still have to find the girl.

And even if they found her, they would have to introduce themselves to someone who had grown up without them.

"Adrian?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think she has a family?"

He understood what she meant.

"A family that raised her?"

Victoria nodded.

"I hope she does."

Adrian sat opposite her.

"So do I."

She looked at him.

"Even if that makes things more difficult?"

"Especially then."

Victoria smiled faintly.

Adrian had always been practical.

Sometimes excessively so.

But he understood something important.

The person they were searching for was not an empty space waiting to be filled.

She was a person.

She would have memories, relationships, habits, ambitions, and a life that had continued for eighteen years without the Aurelius family.

They could not erase that life.

Nor should they want to.

Elena was having difficulty choosing between two university programmes.

The problem was not that either was unsuitable.

The problem was that both were good for different reasons.

One offered a stronger economics curriculum.

The other offered better opportunities for business research and international internships.

She had made a comparison table.

Then she had made another one.

Then she had discovered that the second table contradicted the first.

Clara had been watching the entire process through a video call.

"You're doing it again."

Elena looked up.

"Doing what?"

"Trying to solve a decision by creating more decisions."

"That's not what I'm doing."

"Your spreadsheet has three worksheets."

"Four."

Clara stared at her.

"That's worse."

Elena sighed.

"I just want to choose properly."

"You can choose properly without predicting the next five years of your life."

"That's not very reassuring."

"It's realistic."

Clara held up a packet of biscuits.

"Emergency biscuits."

Elena laughed.

"You actually bought them?"

"I said I would."

"You're ridiculous."

"And yet you keep talking to me."

Elena returned to the table.

Clara's expression became a little more serious.

"Have you told Isabelle you're still considering Singapore?"

"Yes."

"And?"

"She's looking at exchange programmes too."

"Together?"

"Separately."

"Good."

Elena looked at her.

"Why good?"

"Because you both need to make your own choices."

Elena nodded slowly.

Clara continued.

"Listen. I know things at home are complicated. But you don't have to turn every decision into a statement about your place in the family."

Elena stopped typing.

Clara had a habit of saying things casually that took several seconds to settle.

"I don't think I do that."

"You do sometimes."

Elena looked down at the comparison table.

"Maybe."

"You're allowed to want something because you want it. You don't have to prove that you deserve it."

Elena was quiet.

Then she closed the spreadsheet.

"That sounds suspiciously sensible."

"I have occasional moments of brilliance."

"Occasional?"

"Don't push your luck."

They both laughed.

The conversation moved on to classes, assignments, and Clara's complaint about a lecturer who had apparently discovered a new way to make ordinary coursework unnecessarily complicated.

For a while, Elena forgot about the hospital records.

She was simply a student discussing university with a friend.

And she liked that.

Martha finally agreed to meet Edward.

Her reply arrived through the legal office at noon.

She requested a neutral location, no cameras, and no representatives from either family.

Edward agreed.

They chose a private meeting room at a small legal consultation centre.

The location was deliberately ordinary.

No Valmont logos.

No Aurelius security staff inside the room.

No formal conference table surrounded by executives.

Only Edward, Martha, and a lawyer who would remain present to ensure that the conversation was properly documented.

When Martha arrived, she carried a handbag and a thin folder.

She was smaller than Edward expected.

Her grey hair was neatly arranged, and she wore a plain dark coat.

She looked at him for a moment before sitting down.

"Mr. Valmont?"

"Yes. Thank you for coming."

"I haven't decided how much I intend to tell you."

"That's your choice."

Martha glanced at the lawyer.

"I was told this would be a conversation about hospital records."

"It is."

"Not about family disputes?"

"Not unless they relate to the records."

She seemed to consider that.

Then she opened her handbag.

"I worked at St. Catherine's for fourteen years."

"We have your employment record."

"I know."

"Your position was patient administration assistant."

"That's correct."

"Did you work in maternity administration?"

"For part of that time."

"During the period when two newborn girls were placed in the private nursery?"

Martha's fingers stopped moving.

Edward noticed.

She looked at him.

"So you know about the nursery."

"We know that two infants were kept there for approximately eight days."

"And you know they left on the ninth day?"

"Yes."

Martha looked down.

"Then you know more than I expected."

Edward did not respond.

She opened the folder.

Inside was an old hospital photograph.

It showed a group of administrative employees standing outside a corridor.

Martha pointed to herself.

"I was responsible for checking patient identification documents. Not making medical decisions. Not approving discharges. I checked that the paperwork matched the records."

"Did you have access to the private nursery?"

"Administrative access. I was not part of the medical team."

"Why were you there?"

"Because the records were complicated."

"What was complicated about them?"

Martha looked at the photograph.

"Two mothers had given birth within a few hours of each other. Both deliveries were handled by the same hospital. Both infants were transferred to the private nursery."

Edward waited.

Martha continued.

"At first, I thought it was an ordinary administrative issue. Then I noticed that the identification details on the nursery records did not match the discharge instructions."

Edward leaned forward.

"How?"

"The maternal identification numbers were correct. The infant assignment references were not."

"Can you explain?"

Martha shook her head.

"Not fully. I was never given the complete records."

"Who gave you the instructions?"

"Dr. Whitmore's office."

"Did Dr. Whitmore speak to you directly?"

"Once."

"What did he say?"

"He told me not to treat the infant assignment references as final until the maternal records had been independently verified."

Edward thought of the note on the release register.

Maternal confirmation did not constitute infant identity confirmation.

"Did you understand what that meant?"

"Not at the time."

"What happened next?"

Martha folded her hands.

"On the seventh day, I was asked to prepare a duplicate of the private nursery release register."

"By whom?"

"Mr. Hale."

Edward's expression remained neutral.

"Martin Hale?"

"Yes."

"Did he work for the hospital?"

"No. He represented the Aurelius Family Office."

"Did he explain why he needed the register?"

"He said the family office required a copy for its records."

"Did you provide it?"

"I prepared one."

"And the original?"

"Remained at the hospital."

"Did you give him the copy?"

Martha nodded.

"Yes."

Edward paused.

"Why did you keep another copy?"

Martha looked at him.

"Because I was asked to destroy it."

"By Mr. Hale?"

"No."

"Who?"

She hesitated.

Then she answered.

"Mr. Morelli."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Marcus Morelli?"

"Yes."

"Did you know him personally?"

"Not before the hospital."

"How did he approach you?"

"He came to the administration office with Mr. Hale. They were discussing the release records. Mr. Hale wanted the duplicate destroyed after it had been transferred."

"And Morelli?"

"He told me to keep a copy."

"Why?"

Martha looked towards the window.

"He said there might be a dispute later about what had been written in the register."

Edward remained silent.

"At the time, I thought he was being unnecessarily cautious," she continued. "I didn't understand why a private family office would need so many copies of a hospital document."

"Did he explain?"

"No."

"Did you ask?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

Martha looked down at her hands.

"He said the children had not been assigned to the families listed in the original instructions."

Edward felt the words settle.

Not delivered to the originally named representatives.

Not assigned to the families listed.

The same distinction appeared in Morelli's notebook.

"What else did he say?"

"That I should not assume the people receiving the children knew about the original records."

Edward looked at her.

"Did he identify those people?"

"No."

"Did you see them?"

Martha nodded slowly.

"Yes."

Edward did not interrupt.

"On the ninth day, I was asked to verify two receiving guardians. I did not make the decision. I only checked their documents."

"Did you know who they were?"

"I saw their identification."

"Do you remember their names?"

Martha looked at him.

For a moment, Edward thought she might answer.

Then she shook her head.

"I remember one of them."

"Which one?"

"The second."

"Why?"

"Because the person who accompanied her told me that the name on the identification was not the name she would use after leaving the hospital."

Edward's expression changed.

"Someone used an assumed identity?"

"I don't know. That's what I was told."

"Who told you?"

Martha hesitated.

"Mr. Morelli."

Edward sat very still.

"Did the person receiving the child know?"

"I don't know."

"Did the other receiving guardian use a different name?"

"I cannot remember."

"Did you see either infant being discharged?"

"Yes."

"Did you see who carried them?"

Martha nodded.

"One was carried by a woman. The other by a man."

"Did they arrive together?"

"No."

"Did they leave together?"

"No."

"How much time passed between the two departures?"

"Less than an hour."

Edward wrote that down.

Two separate departures.

Two receiving guardians.

At least one person using an identity different from the name on the hospital paperwork.

The matter was becoming more complicated.

"Do you know where the children were taken?"

"No."

"Did Morelli tell you?"

"No."

"Did he tell you why they had to be separated?"

Martha looked towards the door.

Then she lowered her voice.

"He said someone had already tried to take one of them."

Edward stopped writing.

"Who?"

"He didn't say."

"When?"

"Before the ninth day."

"Was the attempt made at the hospital?"

"I don't know."

"Was either child harmed?"

"No. Not as far as I knew."

Edward let the silence remain for a moment.

Then he asked the question he had been waiting to ask.

"Do you still have the duplicate release register?"

Martha looked at the folder on her lap.

"Yes."

"Would you be willing to provide it for examination?"

She did not answer immediately.

"There's something you should know first."

Edward waited.

"The register has two signatures. One belongs to a receiving guardian. The other belongs to the person who approved the final release."

Edward looked at her.

"Do you remember the second signature?"

Martha nodded.

"Yes."

"Who signed it?"

She opened the folder and removed a photocopy.

She placed it on the table.

Edward looked down.

The signature was faded, but the name beneath it was still legible.

**MARCUS MORELLI.**

That was not unexpected.

But the line below it was.

**FINAL RELEASE WITNESS: M. VOSS.**

Edward looked up.

"You signed as a witness?"

"Yes."

"Why didn't you mention that earlier?"

"Because I was not the person who approved the release."

"But you witnessed it."

"Yes."

"Then you can tell us who received the children."

Martha's expression tightened.

"I can tell you what I saw."

Edward waited.

She looked down at the document.

"One of the receiving guardians was a woman I had never met before. The other was a man I recognised."

"Who?"

Martha lifted her eyes.

"Charles Vale."

Edward felt his stomach tighten.

Charles Vale.

The lawyer who had handled the sealed records.

The man whose office had received the original maternal identification pages.

The man whose archive contained Box 114.

The man whose documents repeatedly instructed people not to release information to the Aurelius Family Office.

And apparently, one of the people who had received a newborn infant on the ninth day.

Edward looked at the signature again.

"Are you certain?"

Martha nodded.

"I recognised him."

"Which infant did he receive?"

She looked at the register.

Then she pointed to the second entry.

"Infant B."

Edward wrote it down.

"Do you know where he took the child?"

"No."

"Did he say anything?"

Martha hesitated.

"He said he was receiving the child on behalf of someone else."

Edward looked up.

"On whose behalf?"

Martha's answer was almost a whisper.

"He didn't give me a name."

The interview ended forty minutes later.

Edward did not ask Martha to identify the other receiving guardian immediately.

He could see that she was exhausted, and the information she had provided was already significant.

The legal representative arranged for the photocopy to be examined and for the original document to be transferred under a formal evidence procedure.

Martha stood near the exit.

"Mr. Valmont?"

Edward turned.

"Yes?"

"There's one thing I want you to understand."

He waited.

"I didn't know what was happening to those children. I was not part of any arrangement. I was an administrative employee."

"I understand."

"I only checked the documents I was given."

Edward nodded.

"That's why we're asking you to help us establish what happened."

Martha looked at him for a long moment.

Then she said, "Mr. Morelli was afraid."

Edward's expression changed slightly.

"Of what?"

"Not of the families. Not exactly."

"Then what?"

Martha shook her head.

"I don't know."

She picked up her handbag.

"But he told me something on the day the children left."

"What?"

"He said that if either family ever came looking for the register, it meant the arrangement had failed."

Edward remained still.

Martha continued.

"He said the children should never be forced to answer for what the adults had done."

Then she left.

Edward stood in the corridor for several seconds.

It was the same principle Nicholas had mentioned earlier.

The children should not be made responsible for the decisions of adults.

Yet the adults had spent eighteen years hiding the truth, and now the consequences were beginning to reach the children themselves.

Edward returned to Valmont Holdings just before six.

Richard was in his office.

Sebastian and Nicholas were already there.

Catherine had joined them, having finished a meeting at the foundation.

Edward placed the photocopy on the table.

"I met Martha Voss."

Richard looked up.

"What did she say?"

"She retained a duplicate of the ninth-day release register. She has agreed to provide it for examination."

Sebastian leaned forward.

"What does it contain?"

Edward placed a finger beside the second entry.

"Two receiving guardians. Separate departures. One woman and one man."

"And their identities?"

"Martha remembers the man."

Richard's expression tightened.

"Who?"

"Charles Vale."

Nobody spoke.

Nicholas was the first to react.

"Charles Vale personally received one of the children?"

"Yes."

"Which one?"

"Infant B."

Catherine looked at the document.

"Then he was not merely handling the paperwork."

"No," Edward replied. "He was physically involved in the final release."

Sebastian examined the photocopy.

"Did he say who he was acting for?"

"He told Martha he was receiving the child on behalf of someone else."

"Who?"

"He didn't identify them."

Richard stood.

"Then we need to find out who."

Edward nodded.

"There is another detail."

He turned the page.

"Martha recognised the other receiving guardian as a woman she had never met before. She cannot identify her yet."

"Was the woman alone?"

"No. Martha said she arrived separately."

"And Charles?"

"He arrived separately as well."

Nicholas frowned.

"Two children, two guardians, two separate departures."

"Correct."

Catherine looked at the entries.

"Was Elena's name anywhere on the register?"

"No."

"Isabelle's?"

"No."

Edward paused.

"The register records only infant references. It does not identify either child by the names they were later given."

Richard nodded slowly.

"Then we still don't know which child went where."

"Correct."

He looked at the second entry.

"But we now know that Charles Vale personally received Infant B."

Catherine's eyes remained on the paper.

For eighteen years, she had believed that the hospital's records were incomplete because of administrative mistakes.

Now she was beginning to understand that people had actively participated in the children's separation.

Some of them had known more than others.

Some might have believed they were protecting the infants.

Others might have had different motives entirely.

And at least one person had carried a child out of the hospital while claiming to act on behalf of someone whose name was never recorded.

That evening, Edward sent the new information to the Aurelius family.

Alexander read the report twice.

Victoria was standing beside him.

Adrian examined the photocopy.

Lucien, who had been unusually quiet during the earlier discussions, finally spoke.

"Charles Vale received one of the children himself."

"Yes," Adrian said.

"Then he knows where that child went."

"Unless he was told only part of the arrangement."

Damien looked at the signature.

"Could he have been the person who selected the receiving guardians?"

"We don't know," Adrian replied.

Alexander closed the report.

"Find out whether Charles Vale had any authority to receive an infant on behalf of the Family Office."

"His legal firm had handled family matters," Adrian said. "But we have no record of an authorised guardianship arrangement."

"Then he may have been acting privately."

Victoria looked at the document.

"Or on behalf of someone else."

Alexander nodded.

"Find out who."

Adrian collected the papers.

"I'll contact the surviving partners at Vale International Legal Group."

"Do that."

Victoria remained beside the table.

"Alexander?"

He looked at her.

"Do you think Charles knew which child he was receiving?"

Alexander considered the question.

"He may have known the infant's assigned reference. That doesn't mean he knew the child's biological identity."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

"Another distinction."

"Yes."

She looked at the two entries.

One infant had been received by a woman.

The other by Charles Vale.

But neither record identified the children as Elena or Isabelle.

Neither proved which mother had given birth to which infant.

And neither explained why the children had been separated.

Later that night, Edward received a message from the investigator.

The person connected to the reactivated telephone account had been identified.

The account was registered under the name of an old legal assistant who had worked at Vale Private Counsel.

The name was:

**Daniel Mercer.**

Edward searched the name.

Daniel Mercer had worked under Charles Vale for approximately six years.

His employment ended shortly after Morelli left the Aurelius Family Office.

His personnel file contained one unusual notation.

**Resignation accepted following internal review.**

No explanation.

No disciplinary record.

No further employment reference.

Edward opened the old correspondence index.

There it was again.

A small handwritten entry beside Daniel Mercer's name.

**Ninth-day arrangements — supporting witness.**

Edward stared at the words.

Martha had witnessed the release.

Charles Vale had received one child.

Daniel Mercer had been connected to the arrangements.

And Morelli had completed the final release.

Four people.

Two infants.

One missing receiving guardian.

Edward picked up his telephone.

"Find Daniel Mercer."

"Where should we start?"

"Everywhere he has lived since leaving Vale Private Counsel."

He paused.

"And don't contact him yet."

"Understood."

Edward ended the call.

Then he looked at the final line of the photocopy.

There was a small handwritten notation beneath Charles Vale's signature.

It had been partially covered by the edge of the old carbon paper.

The archivist had not yet been able to decipher it.

But Edward could make out three words.

**RECEIVED FOR THE—**

The remainder was unreadable.

He leaned closer.

Then he noticed something else.

The handwriting was not Charles Vale's.

It belonged to someone who had added the notation after the release had been completed.

Someone had written it later.

Someone had wanted the record to say that Charles Vale had received the child on behalf of another person.

And that person had deliberately left the recipient unnamed.

Edward sat back.

The investigation had found one of the people who physically received a child.

But the most important question remained unanswered.

**Whose child had Charles Vale taken out of the hospital?**
