# **Chapter 55 — The Woman Who Collected the Children's Papers**

The estate inventory was incomplete.

Edward discovered that within an hour of requesting it.

Eleanor Vale's personal belongings had been divided into three categories after her death.

Ordinary possessions had been distributed among relatives.

Financial documents had been transferred to Vale International Legal Group.

And a third category, described simply as *private family correspondence*, had been collected separately.

There was no recipient's name.

No collection receipt.

No inventory of the individual documents.

Only one line in the estate administrator's register:

**COLLECTED IN PERSON — AUTHORISED BY THE DECEASED'S PRIVATE INSTRUCTION.**

Edward read it twice.

"Whoever collected those papers had permission from Eleanor before she died," Jonathan said.

"Possibly."

"Margaret Ellison said the woman came three weeks after Eleanor's death."

"That doesn't mean the instruction was written three weeks earlier."

Edward opened the estate file.

The collection had taken place on the twenty-third day after Eleanor's death.

The private instruction authorising it had been signed six months earlier.

Edward leaned back.

"So Eleanor expected someone to collect the documents."

"Yes."

"And she didn't want the estate administrators handling them."

Jonathan pointed to another line.

"There's a witness signature."

Edward enlarged it.

The name was partially obscured.

**D. MERCER.**

Daniel Mercer.

The former Vale legal assistant had appeared in the ninth-day release records, the Northbridge accounts, and the financial arrangements surrounding Anna's accommodation.

Edward picked up his phone.

"Find him."

Daniel Mercer was living in a small apartment above an accounting office.

He agreed to meet Edward and Jonathan the following afternoon.

When they arrived, he was already waiting.

"I wondered when you'd reach this part of the records," he said.

Edward placed the estate inventory on the table.

"Did you witness the collection of Eleanor Vale's private correspondence?"

"Yes."

"Who collected it?"

"A woman."

"Name?"

"She gave one."

"Which was?"

Mercer looked uncomfortable.

"I can't remember whether it was her real name."

Edward waited.

"She introduced herself as Eleanor's authorised representative. She had a signed instruction and the necessary estate documentation."

"Did you verify her identity?"

"Yes."

"Through what document?"

"A private authorisation letter and a solicitor's certification."

"Who issued the certification?"

"Vale International Legal Group."

Edward studied him.

"So the collection was legally documented."

"As far as I knew."

"Did she take every document in the category?"

"Not every document."

"What did she take?"

"Three boxes. One wooden box and two archive cases."

"What was inside?"

"I didn't examine them."

"Did she tell you?"

"She said they contained records relating to two children."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Did she mention their names?"

"No."

"Did she mention Anna?"

Mercer hesitated.

Then he said, "She mentioned the second child."

Edward leaned forward.

"What exactly did she say?"

Mercer closed his eyes briefly.

"She said, 'I have waited long enough. I want to know whether she is still alive.'"

Edward remained silent.

"Did you ask who she meant?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because Eleanor had specifically instructed me not to question the recipient."

Edward placed the St. Agnes transfer authorisation on the table.

"Do you recognise this signature?"

Mercer examined it.

"Yes."

"Whose?"

"Eleanor's."

"Did she tell you why she had signed it?"

"No."

"Did she explain the children's arrangement?"

Mercer looked at the document.

"She told me that two children had been placed under different identities, and that one of them might have been moved more than once."

"Did she say which one?"

"No."

Edward leaned forward.

"Did she believe one of them had been given to the wrong family?"

Mercer looked up.

"She believed the original arrangements had failed."

"That's not the same thing."

"No," Mercer admitted. "It isn't."

He paused.

"She also told me something else."

"What?"

"That the woman collecting the papers had been promised a child."

Edward stopped.

"Promised by whom?"

"She didn't say."

"Was the woman a biological relative?"

"I don't know."

"Did she have a relationship with either child?"

"She said she had been waiting for one of them."

Edward wrote it down.

"Can you describe her?"

Mercer thought carefully.

"Middle-aged. Dark hair, perhaps in her forties. She wore a plain grey coat. She was carrying a small handbag."

"Anything distinctive?"

"She had a mark on her left hand."

"What kind of mark?"

"A small scar across the base of her thumb."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Did she give you a name?"

Mercer shook his head.

"Not one I can confidently remember."

The next morning, Edward visited the former solicitor who had certified the collection.

The solicitor, Andrew Bell, was retired but still maintained copies of certain historical legal records.

He found the relevant certification.

It identified the recipient as:

**MARGARET VOSS.**

Edward stared at the page.

"Margaret Voss collected Eleanor's papers?"

"That's the name on the certification."

"Was her identity verified?"

"Yes."

"Was she acting in her personal capacity?"

Andrew examined the document.

"No. She was acting under a private authority granted by Eleanor Vale."

"Was the authority limited to collecting documents?"

"Yes."

"Could she access the estate?"

"Only for the collection."

Edward placed another document beside it.

It was Margaret Voss's old personnel photograph.

Andrew examined it.

"That appears to be the same person."

Edward looked at him.

"You're certain?"

"I remember her. She was very particular about the collection process."

"Did she ask about anything besides the documents?"

Andrew thought for a moment.

"She asked whether Eleanor had left any personal letters addressed to the two children."

"Did she receive any?"

"One."

"Which child?"

Andrew shook his head.

"The envelope was not addressed to a child. It was marked 'For the person who comes looking.'"

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Do you have a copy?"

Andrew opened another folder.

"I do."

He removed a photocopy of the envelope.

The handwriting was Eleanor's.

**For the person who comes looking.**

Below it, a sentence had been added.

**Do not give this to the first person who claims to know the child.**

Edward read it twice.

"Was the letter inside?"

Andrew nodded.

"But I was not permitted to read it."

"Who received it?"

"Margaret Voss."

Edward sat back.

Margaret Voss had not merely collected the children's papers.

She had also received a letter specifically intended for whoever came looking for one of them.

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian was examining a different set of records.

He had obtained the bank's original account access logs.

The results were more complicated than expected.

The confidential account had been accessed by four different terminals over several years.

Three belonged to the Family Office.

The fourth belonged to a private legal services company.

Adrian showed Alexander the access history.

"Margaret Voss used the Family Office terminal until her departure. After that, the account was accessed through Vale Private Counsel."

"By Charles?"

"Not necessarily. The terminal was shared."

"Who had access?"

"Charles Vale, Daniel Mercer, and an external administrator."

Alexander looked at the final name.

"Who?"

"The records identify the administrator as M. VOSS."

Alexander looked at him.

"Margaret continued accessing the account after she left?"

"Yes."

"Was she authorised?"

"According to the system, her credentials remained active."

Alexander stood.

"Then she was never completely removed from the arrangement."

Adrian nodded.

"That's correct."

Victoria entered the office.

"Have you found something?"

Adrian showed her the access history.

She examined the dates.

"She continued managing the payments."

"Yes."

"Even after leaving the hospital and the Family Office."

Alexander nodded.

Victoria looked at the final transaction.

"There's a payment here dated three weeks after Eleanor died."

Adrian checked the details.

"It was a transfer to a private account."

"Whose?"

"The beneficiary is recorded as CHILD — IDENTITY PENDING."

Victoria looked at the date again.

"Was that before or after Margaret collected Eleanor's papers?"

"Two days before."

Victoria slowly put the document down.

"She collected the papers and received the payment."

Alexander considered the sequence.

"That may have been part of the same arrangement."

"Or she was finally being paid to complete something."

Neither of them spoke.

Elena was in the garden when Nicholas found her.

She was reading the university's programme handbook.

"Still studying?"

"Reading."

"That looks suspiciously like studying."

Elena closed the booklet.

"Do you need something?"

"No. I wanted to ask whether you've received the final result."

"Not yet."

"You're not worried?"

"Of course I am."

Nicholas sat beside her.

"But?"

"But I can't change the result by checking my email every five minutes."

He smiled.

"That's a sensible approach."

"I know."

He looked at the booklet.

"Have you considered what you'll do if you get accepted?"

"Yes."

"Will you stay in Singapore for the whole programme?"

"If the offer is for the full term, yes."

Nicholas nodded.

"That's a long time."

"Not forever."

"I know."

Elena watched him for a moment.

"Are you worried?"

"A little."

"Why?"

"Because you've always been here."

Elena looked down at the booklet.

"I'll still be here. Just not every day."

Nicholas smiled.

"Then we'll manage."

She leaned back.

"I want to try something different, Nicholas. I don't want to spend my entire life wondering whether I could have done more."

He nodded.

"Then go and do more."

Elena smiled.

"Thank you."

Nicholas stood.

"And when you become a famous businesswoman, remember who supported you."

"I'll put your name on a very small plaque."

"Very small?"

"Extremely small."

He laughed and walked away.

Edward received a call from Andrew Bell at 6:20 p.m.

The solicitor had found something else.

A second copy of Eleanor's letter had been retained in his private archive.

The first page was identical to the envelope Edward had already seen.

But the second page contained additional instructions.

*To Margaret,*

*You know why I cannot give you the child's name.*

*You know what happened when the original arrangements were changed.*

*I have tried to ensure that neither child is moved again without proper verification.*

*But I no longer believe that the families will receive the truth through the usual channels.*

*If the second child is still with you, do not assume that the arrangement was permanent.*

*If she is not, find out who has her before you decide what to tell the first family.*

*The two children must not be confused again.*

Edward stopped reading.

He returned to the beginning.

**If the second child is still with you...**

Margaret Voss had apparently been responsible for one child.

But Eleanor's letter did not say that she was the permanent guardian.

It suggested that the child might already have been moved.

And the reference to the first family implied that the arrangement involved two separate families.

Edward read the final paragraph.

*You asked me whether you should tell the child about her mother.*

*My answer has not changed.*

*Do not tell her that nobody came looking.*

*But do not tell her that the person who came was her mother.*

*We still do not know who that person was.*

Edward put the letter down.

The visitor at St. Agnes might not have been the mother.

The person who collected the papers might not have been the guardian.

And Margaret Voss might have been responsible for a child she had never personally raised.

At the same time, Margaret Voss was being traced through an old financial account.

The account had been opened under her name, but the address had changed several times.

The last registered address belonged to a small residential building near the northern coast.

Edward requested a discreet welfare inquiry.

The response arrived the next morning.

The property was still occupied.

The current resident was a woman in her late sixties.

Her name was not Margaret Voss.

It was **Margaret Vale**.

Edward looked at the report.

The photograph attached to the property record showed an elderly woman with grey hair.

Her left hand rested on the railing.

Across the base of her thumb was a thin, pale scar.

The same scar Daniel Mercer had described.

Edward immediately called Jonathan.

"She's alive."

"Margaret Voss?"

"Yes. She's living under another surname."

"Where?"

Edward gave him the address.

Jonathan was silent for a moment.

"Should we contact her?"

"Yes. But carefully."

"Why carefully?"

Edward looked at Eleanor's letter.

"Because she may be the only person left who knows where the second child went."

The Aurelius family received the news that evening.

Adrian placed the current photograph on the table.

Victoria stared at it.

"She's alive."

"Apparently."

"Does she know where the child is?"

"We don't know."

Alexander examined the photograph.

"Has she been contacted?"

"Not yet."

Victoria looked at the woman's face.

"She looks older than I expected."

"Almost seventy," Adrian said.

Victoria touched the edge of the photograph.

"Then she has spent most of her adult life carrying this secret."

Alexander looked at her.

"Perhaps."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

"I don't want her frightened."

"Neither do I."

"She may have been protecting the child."

"Or she may have been part of the reason the child was hidden."

Victoria nodded.

"We need to hear her account before deciding."

Alexander agreed.

Edward arrived at the coastal residence the following afternoon.

The building was modest.

There was a small garden, a narrow driveway, and a single bell beside the entrance.

Jonathan stood behind him.

Edward pressed the bell.

After a short wait, footsteps approached.

The door opened.

An elderly woman stood in the doorway.

Her hair was grey.

Her expression was guarded.

Her left hand rested against the doorframe.

Edward recognised the scar immediately.

"Mrs. Voss?"

The woman looked at him.

For several seconds, she did not answer.

Then she said:

"Nobody has called me that in seventeen years."

Edward kept his voice calm.

"My name is Edward Valmont. I'm investigating the records concerning two children born eighteen years ago."

The woman's expression changed.

"Which child?"

Edward glanced at Jonathan.

Then he answered.

"Both."

Margaret Voss slowly opened the door.

"Come inside."

The living room was neat, with very little decoration.

A small wooden cabinet stood against one wall.

On top of it was a framed photograph.

Edward noticed it immediately.

It showed two newborn babies lying in separate hospital bassinets.

The photograph was old and slightly faded.

Neither infant's face was clearly visible.

But on the back, in handwriting he recognised, were four words:

**THEY MUST REMAIN SEPARATE.**

Edward looked at Margaret.

"Who took this photograph?"

She followed his gaze.

For the first time, her expression showed something close to fear.

"Eleanor."

"Why did she give it to you?"

Margaret looked at the photograph.

Then she turned towards Edward.

"Because she knew I would eventually have to choose."

"Choose what?"

Margaret sat down.

"Which child to tell the truth first."

Edward remained standing.

"Where are they now?"

Margaret looked at him for a long moment.

Then she gave an answer that changed the direction of the investigation.

"I know where one of them went."

She paused.

"But I don't know where the other one is."

And before Edward could ask another question, she added:

"The child I was responsible for was never supposed to be Elena."
