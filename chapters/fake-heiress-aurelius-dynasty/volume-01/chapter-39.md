# **Chapter 39 — The Child Eleanor Vale Had Been Waiting For**

Edward did not immediately call the Aurelius family.

He placed the photograph beneath the desk lamp and examined it again.

Charles Vale stood near the rear entrance of St. Catherine's Hospital.

Beside him was Eleanor Vale.

She held a newborn infant wrapped in a pale blanket.

Her expression was difficult to interpret. She wasn't smiling, but she wasn't visibly distressed either. She appeared to be looking down at the child.

On the back, Charles had written:

**She has finally received the child she was waiting for.**

Below that:

**INFANT B — FINAL RECEIVING PARTY CONFIRMED.**

Edward turned the photograph over.

The handwriting was unquestionably Charles's.

The photograph raised several questions.

Why had Eleanor received Infant B?

Why had she been waiting for that particular child?

And why had the original ninth-day arrangement placed the infant elsewhere?

He opened the file containing the earlier photograph of Margaret Ellison.

The two infants had been identified as A and B in separate records, but the investigation had never established whether those labels had remained consistent throughout the hospital transfers.

Now Charles's photograph seemed to establish something important.

Eleanor had received Infant B.

But that did not establish whether Infant B was biologically the child of Victoria Aurelius or Catherine Valmont.

Edward wrote the distinction in his notes.

**Administrative identity is not biological identity.**

Then he called Jonathan Pierce.

"I've found Eleanor."

"Where?"

"In a photograph taken outside the hospital on the tenth day."

Pierce was silent.

"She's holding a child."

"Which child?"

"Infant B, according to Charles's handwritten note."

"Are you certain?"

"The photograph and note are together in Charles's personal archive. The notation is consistent with his handwriting."

Pierce took a breath.

"Then we need to establish why Eleanor was the final receiving party."

"Yes."

"And whether the child remained with her."

Edward looked at the photograph.

"That's what I'm trying to find out."

The Aurelius family received the photograph that afternoon.

Victoria examined it first.

She did not immediately recognise the woman.

Then Adrian enlarged the image.

"Eleanor Vale," he said.

Victoria looked up.

"Charles's wife?"

"Yes."

Alexander took the photograph.

"Why would Eleanor receive one of the children?"

Edward explained the handwritten note.

**She has finally received the child she was waiting for.**

Lucien frowned.

"That sounds as though Eleanor had been expecting a child."

"Possibly," Edward said.

"Was she unable to have children?" Julian asked.

Nobody answered immediately.

Adrian looked through the available biographical records.

"Eleanor and Charles had no publicly recorded children."

"Publicly recorded?" Damien repeated.

"Their personal records are incomplete. We shouldn't draw conclusions."

Victoria looked at the photograph again.

"She looks as though she knows the baby."

Edward nodded.

"Margaret Ellison described a woman who visited her approximately a year after the ninth-day release. She left a note saying that someone had come for the child. We haven't established whether that woman was Eleanor, but the descriptions may be connected."

Alexander looked at the photograph.

"Was Eleanor the woman who visited Margaret?"

"Possibly. We need a reliable identification."

"Then find out."

Victoria placed the photograph on the table.

"She was involved from the beginning."

Edward nodded.

"She visited both mothers seven days after the births. She reviewed the restricted correspondence. She signed the final instruction found in Morelli's notebook. And now we have evidence that she received Infant B."

Lucien leaned back.

"She was not merely trying to protect the children from the outside."

"No," Edward said.

"She was making decisions about where they would go."

"That appears increasingly likely."

Victoria looked at Alexander.

"Did you know her?"

Alexander considered the question.

"Not personally. Charles handled most of the legal work. Eleanor occasionally attended meetings with him."

"Did she ever speak to you about the missing child?"

"No."

"Did she know you were searching?"

"She must have known."

Victoria looked down.

"Then why didn't she tell us?"

Alexander had no answer.

At the Valmont estate, Catherine received a copy of the photograph.

She stared at it for a long time.

Richard stood beside her.

"Do you recognise her?"

Catherine shook her head.

"Not clearly."

"Edward believes she was involved in the hospital arrangements."

"Then why was she holding a baby?"

"Because she received one on the tenth day."

Catherine looked at the photograph again.

"Eleanor Vale."

"Yes."

"Was she the woman who visited me?"

"We don't know."

Catherine closed the folder.

"Richard, I remember someone telling me that the baby had been moved to another observation room."

Richard turned toward her.

"When?"

"After the delivery. I don't remember the exact day."

"Who told you?"

"A hospital administrator."

"Did they say why?"

"They said the child needed additional observation."

Catherine rubbed her forehead.

"I was exhausted. I had been through an emergency examination, and they kept telling me that everything was under control."

Richard sat opposite her.

"Did you see the baby?"

"Yes."

"Which baby?"

Catherine looked at him.

"I thought it was ours."

He did not interrupt.

"I remember a nurse bringing a baby into the room. I remember the blanket. I remember asking whether she was healthy."

Catherine's voice became quieter.

"But I don't remember the identification band."

Richard looked at the photograph.

"Could it have been Infant B?"

"I don't know."

She paused.

"That's the worst part. I remember holding a child, but I cannot prove which child I held."

Richard reached for the folder.

"We'll establish what happened."

Catherine looked at him.

"And if we discover that the child I held wasn't ours?"

Richard did not answer immediately.

Then he said, "Then we will have to accept that someone allowed us to believe something that wasn't true."

Catherine looked away.

For the first time, the investigation was no longer simply about finding the person who had altered the records.

It was about questioning memories that had shaped eighteen years of family life.

Edward and Pierce visited the Vale estate two days later.

The property had been sold after Charles and Eleanor died.

Most of their possessions had been distributed among relatives or transferred to storage.

The estate's former caretaker, Mr. Roland, agreed to meet them.

He was in his late seventies.

He remembered Eleanor better than Charles.

"She was a quiet woman," he said. "Very organised. She didn't like people entering her private rooms."

"Did she ever have a child?" Edward asked.

Roland looked at him.

"Not that I knew."

"Did she ever care for a child?"

The old man hesitated.

"Yes."

"When?"

"After she returned from the hospital."

"Which hospital?"

"St. Catherine's."

Edward exchanged a glance with Pierce.

"Do you remember when?"

"The tenth day, I believe."

"What happened?"

"She came home carrying a baby."

"Alone?"

"No. Charles was with her."

"Did she explain who the child was?"

"She said the baby needed somewhere to stay."

"Did she say the child was hers?"

"No."

"Did she say she was adopting the child?"

"No."

Roland looked down at his hands.

"She told me not to ask questions."

Edward waited.

"Did the child remain at the estate?"

"For a while."

"How long?"

"Not very long."

"Days? Weeks?"

"Perhaps a few weeks. I can't be certain."

Edward leaned forward.

"Where did the child go afterward?"

Roland looked toward the window.

"Another woman came."

"Who?"

"I don't know her name."

"Did Eleanor know her?"

"Yes. They spoke privately."

"Did she collect the child?"

Roland nodded.

"She took the baby away."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Was it the same child Eleanor had brought from the hospital?"

"Yes."

"Did Eleanor appear upset?"

Roland thought for a moment.

"She was."

"Did she cry?"

"No. She simply stood at the entrance for a long time after the car left."

"Did she say anything?"

Roland looked at him.

"She said, 'At least this time, she knows where the child is going.'"

Edward wrote the sentence down.

"Did she mention who the woman was?"

"No."

"Did Charles?"

"Charles said the matter was finished."

Roland gave a faint, troubled smile.

"But Eleanor didn't look as though it was finished."

The new information created a second timeline.

Infant B had been taken from the hospital by Eleanor.

The child had stayed at the Vale estate for some period.

Then another woman had collected the baby.

The final destination was unknown.

Edward brought the information to the Aurelius family.

Victoria listened carefully.

"So Eleanor did not keep the child."

"Not permanently, as far as we can establish."

"She handed her to another woman?"

"Yes."

"Who?"

"We don't know."

Adrian looked at the photograph.

"That might be the person who selected the final receiving guardians."

Edward nodded.

"Or someone acting under that person's instruction."

Damien studied the dates.

"How long did Eleanor keep the infant?"

"Roland estimates a few weeks. We need independent confirmation."

Lucien leaned forward.

"Then the ninth-day and tenth-day records weren't the end of the movement. The child changed hands again."

"Correct."

Julian looked confused.

"How many people held the same baby?"

Nobody answered immediately.

Then Edward said, "We don't know."

Julian looked at the photograph.

"That's terrible."

Victoria reached for his hand.

"Yes."

He looked at her.

"Did the baby know any of them?"

"She was too young to remember."

"Then how did anyone know where she belonged?"

Victoria looked at the documents.

"That is exactly what we are trying to discover."

At the Valmont estate, Elena was preparing for her second university interview.

She had decided not to memorise answers.

Instead, she had written down a few ideas and examples.

Isabelle was sitting nearby, reviewing the scholarship proposal.

Nicholas entered carrying a large box.

Elena looked at it.

"What is that?"

"Something for you."

"Why?"

"Because I wanted to give you something."

"That's not an explanation."

Nicholas placed the box on the table.

"Open it."

Elena lifted the lid.

Inside was a new laptop.

She stared at it.

"Nicholas."

"What?"

"This is expensive."

"I know."

"My current laptop works."

"I know."

"Then why did you buy another one?"

"Because the new programme might require better equipment."

Elena closed the box.

"Did you check the specifications?"

Nicholas looked offended.

"Of course."

"Did you check whether it supports the software used by the university?"

He paused.

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

Isabelle covered a smile.

Nicholas sighed.

"Fine. I checked the reviews."

Elena laughed.

"That's not the same thing."

He sat down.

"Then we'll check the specifications together. If it isn't suitable, we'll exchange it."

Elena looked at him.

"You don't have to keep buying me things."

Nicholas's expression softened.

"I know."

"Then why do you?"

He considered the question.

"Because I don't know what else to do sometimes."

Elena became quiet.

Nicholas looked at the closed box.

"When all this started, I thought I could help by making sure you didn't feel left out. But every time I try, I end up buying something."

Elena smiled faintly.

"You're not very good at choosing gifts."

"I am excellent at choosing gifts."

"You bought me a decorative lamp last month."

"It was a very good lamp."

"It doesn't fit on my desk."

Nicholas laughed.

"All right. I need practice."

Elena opened the box again.

"Let's check the specifications first."

Nicholas grinned.

"Deal."

That evening, Edward received a call from Margaret Ellison's care facility.

Margaret had asked to speak to him.

He visited the following morning.

She was sitting in the same room, the photograph of Infant A resting on her lap.

When Edward entered, she looked at him.

"You found something."

"Yes."

"About the baby?"

"Yes."

Margaret studied his face.

"Is she alive?"

"We haven't established that."

Margaret looked down.

"I see."

Edward sat opposite her.

"Mrs. Ellison, I need to ask you about the woman who visited your house."

"The one who left the note?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"We have a photograph of a woman who may be connected to her."

Margaret looked at him.

"Who?"

"Her name was Eleanor Vale."

The old woman went still.

For several seconds, she did not speak.

Then she whispered, "Eleanor."

"You remember her?"

Margaret nodded slowly.

"She came to the house."

Edward leaned forward.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"Did she identify herself?"

"No. But Charles introduced her."

"What did he call her?"

"Mrs. Vale."

Edward waited.

"What did she say?"

Margaret looked at the photograph.

"She asked whether I had kept the baby safe."

"What did you tell her?"

"I said yes."

"Did she ask to see the child?"

"Yes."

"Did you allow her?"

Margaret nodded.

"She held her."

Edward felt his attention sharpen.

"Did she say anything while holding her?"

Margaret's eyes became distant.

"She said she had waited a long time."

"Did she say why?"

"No."

"Did she say the child's mother had been informed?"

Margaret shook her head.

"She asked whether I had told the mother."

Edward paused.

"Had you?"

"No. I didn't know who she was."

"What did Eleanor say?"

Margaret looked at him.

"She said that was good."

Edward's expression changed.

"She said it was good that you hadn't told the mother?"

"Yes."

"Did she explain why?"

Margaret shook her head.

"She told me the mother must not be disturbed until everything was settled."

Edward looked at the photograph.

"Did Eleanor take the child?"

"No."

"Did she ask you to give the child to someone else?"

Margaret hesitated.

"She asked me whether I would be willing to let the baby go if the proper person came."

"What did you say?"

"I said yes."

Margaret looked down.

"I thought someone would eventually come for her mother."

Edward waited.

But Margaret's next words were almost inaudible.

"I didn't know Eleanor was the one who would decide when."

Edward returned to the office with a new question.

Why had Eleanor visited Margaret?

She had already received Infant B.

Margaret had received Infant A.

Eleanor had held both children at different times.

She had asked about their mothers.

She had instructed that the mothers not be informed.

And she had arranged for at least one child to be transferred again.

Perhaps she had been trying to correct the original arrangement.

Perhaps she had been protecting the children from someone.

Or perhaps she had been trying to keep both children under her control until a particular condition was met.

The evidence did not yet establish which.

Pierce entered Edward's office carrying a newly received file.

"We've found something."

Edward looked up.

"What?"

"An old correspondence envelope from Eleanor's estate. It was among the documents we hadn't examined."

"What's inside?"

"A letter addressed to Margaret Ellison."

Edward stood.

"Read it."

Pierce opened the folder.

The letter was dated three weeks after the births.

It contained a short message.

**You have been entrusted with a child whose identity has not been established. You must not assume that the child is the one named in the original hospital instruction.**

Edward looked at Pierce.

"Continue."

Pierce read the next paragraph.

**If the child's mother contacts you, do not confirm that the child is in your care. Refer her to Charles Vale. If Charles is unavailable, contact me directly.**

Edward's expression hardened.

The letter continued.

**The child must remain where she is until the second placement has been verified. No further transfer is to be made without my written instruction.**

At the bottom was Eleanor's signature.

But there was something else.

A handwritten addition had been made in a different ink.

**This instruction applies only to Infant A. Infant B has already been received by me.**

Edward stared at the final sentence.

For the first time, the documents clearly separated the two children.

Eleanor had personally received Infant B.

Margaret had been entrusted with Infant A.

And Eleanor had instructed that Infant A remain with Margaret until a second placement was verified.

Edward looked at Pierce.

"Who sent this letter?"

"The archive has no outgoing register."

"Then how did it reach Margaret?"

"She may have received it directly from Charles."

Edward shook his head.

"Margaret says she never saw this letter."

Pierce looked at him.

"Then someone kept it from her."

Edward examined the handwritten addition.

It appeared to have been added after the original letter was written.

He turned the page.

There was a faint indentation beneath the signature.

A second sentence had been written on the sheet that once rested above it.

The document examiner had reconstructed most of it.

Edward read the recovered words.

**If Infant B is ever returned, do not give her to the person who came for Infant A.**

He stopped.

The instruction suggested that Eleanor had anticipated another attempt to exchange the children.

But the final sentence was worse.

Beneath the recovered text, the examiner had identified a partial name.

Only the first letters remained.

**CATH...**

Edward looked at the letter again.

Catherine.

Was Eleanor warning someone not to give Infant B to Catherine Valmont?

Or was the name part of a different sentence entirely?

He could not tell.

He picked up his phone.

Before contacting either family, he requested a full examination of the paper.

There was one more thing he needed to know.

Whether the instruction had been written before or after the tenth-day transfer.

Because if it had been written afterward, Eleanor might already have known that the child she had received was not the child she had been waiting for.

And if that was true, the photograph outside St. Catherine's Hospital was not evidence of a successful placement.

It was evidence of the beginning of another mistake.
