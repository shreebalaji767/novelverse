# **Chapter 52 — The Visitor Who Was Turned Away**

The visitor register contained only one word.

**AURELIUS.**

No first name.

No address.

No identification number.

Just a surname, a time, and a handwritten instruction confirming that the visitor had been refused access to Anna M.

Edward had spent the previous night examining the document.

By morning, he had reached one conclusion.

Someone from the Aurelius family had known about the child at St. Agnes.

Whether that person had known Anna's true identity was another matter entirely.

He called Jonathan.

"I need the original visitor register."

"Already requested."

"Also obtain the centre's security records, if anything survived."

"Eleven years ago?"

"Even an old contractor's invoice could help. We need to establish who was responsible for visitor approval."

Jonathan paused.

"Do you think the visitor was Victoria?"

"I don't know."

"Alexander?"

"Possibly."

"Then why would the centre refuse an Aurelius family member?"

Edward looked at the handwritten instruction.

"Because someone had instructed them to."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander was reading an investigation summary when Adrian entered.

"We have a development."

Alexander put down his tablet.

"What is it?"

"An Aurelius visitor attempted to see a child named Anna M. at St. Agnes eleven years ago."

Alexander's expression changed.

"An Aurelius visitor?"

"Yes. The visitor register contains only the surname."

"Who was it?"

"We don't know yet."

Alexander stood.

"Show me."

Adrian placed the scanned register on the desk.

Alexander read the entry.

3:20 p.m.

Visitor: AURELIUS.

Access refused.

The child had been transferred, according to the note.

He read it again.

"Was this institution near our old residence?"

"Approximately forty minutes away."

"Did the Family Office know about it?"

"We're checking."

Alexander looked at the date.

Eleven years after the births.

Victoria entered the room.

She noticed the document immediately.

"What's happened?"

Alexander handed it to her.

She read the entry.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she asked, "Was someone looking for our daughter?"

"We don't know."

"But someone came."

"Yes."

Victoria looked at Adrian.

"Can you identify the visitor?"

"We're trying."

She returned the document.

"Please don't assume it was one of us."

Alexander nodded.

"We won't."

Victoria sat down slowly.

"Eleven years ago."

Her voice was barely above a whisper.

"If I had known there was a child at that centre, I would have gone myself."

Alexander looked at her.

"I know."

"Wouldn't I?"

He did not answer immediately.

Then he said, "Yes."

Victoria looked back at the register.

"Then who went?"

The Aurelius Family Office had undergone several administrative reorganisations since the births.

Its old visitor logs were stored in an external archive.

Adrian requested the relevant records.

The first response was disappointing.

The visitor register for that year had been destroyed under the office's standard retention policy.

However, the appointment calendar had survived.

It contained a single entry on the date in question.

**3:20 P.M. — ST. AGNES CHILDREN'S RECOVERY CENTRE.**

The visitor's name had been removed.

But the appointment had been arranged through the office of the Chairman.

Alexander stared at the screen.

"Who was my secretary then?"

Adrian checked the personnel archive.

"Margaret Voss."

Alexander looked up.

"Again?"

"Yes."

"Was she still authorised to arrange appointments under my office?"

"Her formal secondment had ended, but the access records show she retained limited administrative privileges."

Alexander stood.

"Who approved that?"

"The authorisation came from Charles Vale's legal office."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Charles Vale had no authority to retain access to my office."

"I know."

"Then why was he able to do it?"

Adrian closed the file.

"That's what we're investigating."

Alexander looked at the appointment record.

The visitor had been sent to St. Agnes through the Chairman's office.

But the institution had refused access.

Someone had arranged the appointment and someone else had prevented the meeting.

"Find the person who made the appointment."

Adrian nodded.

"And find out who cancelled it."

At St. Agnes, the records clerk had located a second document.

It was an internal communication between the centre's administration and Vale Private Counsel.

The message was short.

*The visitor has arrived.*

*The child is still present.*

*Please confirm whether the existing restriction remains in effect.*

The reply had arrived fourteen minutes later.

*The restriction remains unchanged. The visitor must not be informed of the child's current location.*

Edward read the timestamps.

The visitor had arrived at 3:20 p.m.

The centre had sent its message at 3:26 p.m.

The reply had arrived at 3:40 p.m.

The refusal had been recorded at 3:42 p.m.

The visitor had waited twenty-two minutes.

Then left.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Someone knew the child was there."

"Yes."

"And deliberately refused access."

"Yes."

Edward turned the page.

The authorisation had been signed by a representative of Vale Private Counsel.

The name was partially obscured.

But the signature began with a letter.

**C.**

Jonathan frowned.

"Charles?"

"Possibly."

Edward examined the document under magnification.

The signature looked similar to Charles Vale's known handwriting, but the image quality was poor.

"We'll need an examiner."

He turned to the final page.

There was a handwritten note.

**The visitor must not be allowed to establish contact with the child until the mother has been located.**

Edward stopped.

Jonathan read it.

"Until the mother has been located."

Edward nodded.

"That suggests the visitor was not necessarily the mother."

"Or they were deliberately keeping the mother away from the child."

"Both possibilities remain."

He placed the document in its folder.

The investigation had reached another contradiction.

Someone had brought an Aurelius visitor to the centre.

Someone else had refused access.

And the reason given was that the mother had not yet been located.

Elena's mock interview took place the following afternoon.

Sebastian sat across from her in the library.

Nicholas had volunteered to keep time.

Isabelle had offered to ask questions, but Elena had insisted that only one person interview her at a time.

"Let's begin," Sebastian said.

Elena straightened.

"Good afternoon."

"Why have you applied to this programme?"

"Because I want to develop a stronger understanding of management, organisational structures, and the relationship between business decisions and their practical consequences."

Sebastian nodded.

"What do you hope to gain from studying abroad?"

"Different perspectives. I want to work with people who have different experiences and approaches to problem-solving."

"Why Singapore?"

Elena answered confidently.

Nicholas watched the timer.

"Describe a difficult situation you've faced."

Elena paused.

She could have discussed the recent family circumstances.

But she chose something else.

"During a group project, our team disagreed about how to divide the work. Everyone had different priorities, and the deadline was approaching. I suggested we list the remaining tasks, estimate the time required, and assign responsibilities according to each person's strengths."

Sebastian nodded.

"What did you learn?"

"That being correct isn't always enough. Sometimes the important thing is helping a group reach a workable decision."

Sebastian closed his notebook.

"Good."

Elena exhaled.

"Really?"

"Yes."

Nicholas stopped the timer.

"Fourteen minutes and thirty seconds."

Elena looked at him.

"Was that too long?"

"No. I was checking whether you could speak for fifteen minutes without threatening to leave."

She threw a cushion at him.

Isabelle laughed.

For a moment, the library felt almost ordinary again.

Later that evening, Victoria received a message from Adrian.

He had found an old correspondence file.

It contained an appointment request dated three days before the visit to St. Agnes.

The request had been sent from the Aurelius Chairman's Office.

The recipient was Vale Private Counsel.

The subject line read:

**REQUEST FOR INFORMATION — CHILD'S CURRENT PLACEMENT.**

Victoria read it twice.

"Alexander."

He looked up from his desk.

"Yes?"

"Someone asked Charles Vale where the child was."

Alexander took the document.

The request was brief.

*Please confirm the current location and welfare of the child whose placement remains under review.*

*An authorised family representative may attend in person if access can be arranged.*

The sender's identity was not visible on the copy.

But the routing stamp showed that it had originated from Alexander's office.

Alexander frowned.

"I don't remember sending this."

"Could someone have sent it without your knowledge?"

"Apparently."

Victoria examined the date.

"Three days before the visit."

Alexander called Adrian.

"Was this request authorised by me?"

"No."

"Who sent it?"

"The original routing record lists Margaret Voss."

Alexander looked at Victoria.

She was silent.

"Why would Margaret request information about the child?" she asked.

Alexander returned to the document.

"Perhaps she was trying to correct something."

"Then why was the visitor refused?"

He had no answer.

Edward received the handwriting examiner's preliminary report the following morning.

The signature on the St. Agnes restriction was consistent with Charles Vale's known handwriting.

The report did not establish that he had personally signed it, but the similarities were strong.

There was also a second finding.

The request for information sent from the Aurelius Chairman's Office had been written by a different person.

The handwriting was consistent with Margaret Voss.

Edward placed the two documents side by side.

"Margaret requested information about the child," Jonathan said.

"Yes."

"Charles refused to provide it."

"Not exactly. He allowed someone to visit."

"Then blocked the meeting."

Edward nodded.

"Which means he wanted the visitor to believe access was possible."

Jonathan considered that.

"Why create that impression?"

"Perhaps because refusing the request outright would have raised questions."

Edward opened the appointment record.

There was another small detail.

The visitor's appointment had been scheduled for 3:20 p.m.

But the refusal instruction had been issued before the visitor arrived.

The centre had already been told not to admit them.

"Someone arranged a meeting they knew would never happen," Edward said.

Jonathan looked at him.

"Then the visitor was being deceived too."

"Yes."

Edward returned to the correspondence.

A name appeared in the lower margin of the original request.

It had been omitted from the scanned copy.

He enlarged the document.

The writing was faint, but legible.

**For the attention of Mrs. Victoria Aurelius.**

Edward stopped.

The request had not merely come from Alexander's office.

It had been addressed to Victoria.

He immediately called Adrian.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria read the original request.

She looked at the date.

Then she looked at the signature.

"I didn't receive this."

Alexander stood beside her.

"It was sent to your office?"

"Apparently."

"Who handled your correspondence then?"

"Margaret Voss."

Adrian placed the personnel record on the desk.

"She was assigned to the Family Office for confidential identity matters. Her responsibilities included correspondence concerning the missing child."

Victoria looked at him.

"Did she tell anyone that she had requested information?"

"No."

"Did she arrange the visit?"

"The appointment was created through the Chairman's Office. The visitor's name remains unknown."

Victoria returned to the letter.

"Why would she address this to me if she never intended me to receive it?"

Alexander considered the question.

"Perhaps she wanted someone else to see it."

Victoria looked at him.

"Who?"

He did not answer.

She read the final line.

*An authorised family representative may attend in person if access can be arranged.*

Then she looked at the refusal notice.

**The visitor must not be allowed to establish contact with the child until the mother has been located.**

Victoria placed both documents together.

"Alexander, what if Margaret was trying to bring me to the child?"

He looked at her.

"And Charles stopped it."

"Yes."

"That is possible."

Victoria's eyes remained on the papers.

"But if she was trying to bring me there, why didn't she tell me directly?"

Alexander had no answer.

That afternoon, Edward received a call from the former director of St. Agnes.

Her name was Dr. Helen Crawford.

She had retired shortly after the centre closed.

"I found something in my personal files," she said.

Edward arranged to meet her.

When she arrived, she carried a sealed envelope.

"This was given to me by one of the administrative staff," she explained. "I was told to keep it in case the child ever returned."

Edward looked at the envelope.

"Who gave it to you?"

"Margaret Voss."

Jonathan exchanged a glance with Edward.

"When?"

"On the day of the visitor appointment."

Edward opened the envelope.

Inside was a single handwritten note.

*If the visitor is refused access, please ensure that the child is not moved without a formal review.*

*The child has the right to know that someone came looking for her.*

*Do not tell her that nobody came.*

Edward read the final sentence again.

**Do not tell her that nobody came.**

The note was signed:

**M. Voss.**

Dr. Crawford watched him.

"Did the child ever receive that message?"

Edward looked up.

"I don't know."

"She asked about her mother repeatedly. She wanted to know whether anyone was looking for her."

Dr. Crawford's voice softened.

"I remember her sitting in my office. She was very polite. She always asked before touching anything."

Edward waited.

"One day, she asked me whether people could forget their children."

He lowered his eyes.

"What did you tell her?"

"I told her that I didn't know what had happened in her family, but that she deserved an honest answer."

Dr. Crawford paused.

"She asked me to promise that if someone ever came looking for her, I would tell her."

Edward looked at the note.

"Did you make that promise?"

"Yes."

"Did you keep it?"

Dr. Crawford's expression changed.

"No."

Edward said nothing.

"The visitor was refused. Then the child was transferred before I could speak to her again."

"Where was she transferred?"

"I was never told."

Dr. Crawford opened her handbag and removed another document.

It was a copy of the child's transfer request.

The destination had been left blank.

But a handwritten instruction appeared beneath the approval section.

**Transfer only after confirmation that the receiving party has been informed of the child's request for contact with her mother.**

Edward examined the signature.

It was not Charles Vale's.

It was not Margaret Voss's.

It was a third handwriting.

The signature read:

**A. V.**

Edward looked up.

"Who signed this?"

Dr. Crawford shook her head.

"I don't know."

"Did you recognise the initials?"

"No."

Edward placed the document beside the photograph of Anna.

Aurelius.

Victoria A.

Margaret Voss.

And now A. V.

Too many initials.

Too many people making decisions about one child.

He carefully placed the transfer request inside the folder.

"Doctor, do you remember whether Anna was told that someone had come to see her?"

Dr. Crawford looked down.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because the instruction said not to tell her until the receiving party had been confirmed."

Edward's expression hardened.

"Who gave that instruction?"

Dr. Crawford turned the document over.

On the reverse, in small handwriting, was a final sentence.

**The child must not be allowed to believe that the visitor was her mother.**

Edward stared at it.

The visitor had come from the Aurelius side.

The child had asked whether her mother was alive.

And someone had specifically forbidden the institution from allowing her to believe the visitor was her mother.

But that raised a question Edward had not yet considered.

If the visitor was not her mother, who had sent them?

And why had someone gone to such lengths to ensure that Anna never learned they had come at all?
