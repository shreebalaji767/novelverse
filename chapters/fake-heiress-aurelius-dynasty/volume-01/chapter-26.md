# **Chapter 26 — The Man Who Left the Family Office**

Marcus Morelli had been gone for eighteen years.

His name appeared in the old Aurelius corporate directory, buried between former department heads and retired senior executives.

Director of Family Affairs.

Appointment: twelve years before the births.

Departure: nine days after the births.

Reason for departure: personal circumstances.

There was no public statement.

No announcement of a dismissal.

No record of a dispute.

He had simply left.

Edward stared at the directory entry.

The timing was too precise to ignore.

Nine days after the births, the children were discharged from the private nursery.

Nine days after the births, the originating representative withdrew their authority.

And nine days after the births, Marcus Morelli left the Aurelius Family Office.

Edward opened the old investigation file and placed the documents side by side.

The hospital visitor register.

The private nursery discharge register.

The withdrawal of authority.

The corporate directory.

Four records.

One date.

He picked up his phone.

"Adrian, I found someone."

### **1. The Former Director**

Adrian received the call in his office.

He listened without interrupting.

"Marcus Morelli?"

"Yes. He was Director of Family Affairs when the children were born."

Adrian opened the corporate directory.

"I know the name."

"Did you ever meet him?"

"No. He left before I joined the company."

"His departure was recorded nine days after the births."

Adrian looked at the date.

"That's the same day as the nursery discharge."

"And the withdrawal of the original authority."

Silence followed.

Adrian stood.

"Do we know where he is?"

"Not yet. I'm checking the surviving employment records."

"I'll search the Aurelius archive."

"Don't alert anyone who might have worked with him until we know more."

Adrian understood the implication.

If Morelli had been involved in the original arrangement, someone might still be protecting him.

Or he might have been the person protecting the children.

"Understood."

The call ended.

Adrian immediately requested Morelli's personnel file.

The system returned a restricted-access notification.

**HISTORICAL PERSONNEL RECORD — SEALED**

**RELEASE REQUIRES AUTHORISATION FROM THE CHAIRMAN'S OFFICE.**

Adrian frowned.

He had access to most historical corporate records.

This was different.

He contacted the archive administrator.

"Why is Morelli's file sealed?"

The administrator checked the record.

"According to the index, the restriction was applied shortly after his departure."

"By whom?"

"That field is incomplete."

"Can you recover the original access instruction?"

"I'll have to request the physical archive."

"Do it."

Adrian ended the call.

A moment later, he opened his father's office door.

Alexander was reviewing documents.

"We found the name behind the initial."

Alexander looked up.

"Who?"

"Marcus Morelli."

For the first time that evening, Alexander's expression changed noticeably.

"Marcus?"

"You remember him?"

Alexander slowly put down his pen.

"Of course I remember him."

### **2. A Man Alexander Had Trusted**

Alexander walked towards the cabinet behind his desk.

He opened a drawer and removed an old photograph.

It showed a younger Alexander standing beside several senior members of the family office.

Marcus Morelli stood on his left.

He was in his late thirties in the photograph, neatly dressed, with dark hair and a restrained smile.

Alexander studied it.

"Marcus was responsible for family administration, confidential correspondence and several matters involving the private family office."

Adrian examined the photograph.

"Was he trustworthy?"

Alexander looked at him.

"At the time, I believed so."

"Did he have authority to arrange the private nursery?"

"He had administrative authority to coordinate certain family arrangements. He did not have authority to decide the identity or placement of my child."

"Could he have accessed your signature?"

"Yes."

Adrian looked at him.

"That may explain the authorisation document."

Alexander returned the photograph to the desk.

"Perhaps."

"Do you remember him discussing the hospital?"

Alexander was quiet.

"I remember asking him to help investigate the missing infant."

"After the hospital reported that the child registered under the Aurelius name could not be located?"

"Yes."

"Was that before or after he left?"

"Before."

Adrian opened his notes.

"Do you remember asking him to arrange a private nursery?"

Alexander shook his head.

"No."

"Did you authorise him to communicate with Charles Vale?"

"I authorised the legal office to investigate the hospital's report. I did not authorise any transfer of children."

Adrian nodded.

That distinction mattered.

The original letter requesting an investigation was genuine.

The later authorisation to establish the private nursery was not yet verified.

Marcus Morelli had been in a position to coordinate the arrangement.

But that did not establish whether he had acted independently, followed someone else's instructions or attempted to prevent a worse situation.

Alexander looked at the photograph again.

"Find him."

### **3. A File with No Address**

The Aurelius archive administrator returned Adrian's call the following morning.

"Mr. Aurelius, we've located the personnel file."

"Send it."

"There's a problem."

Adrian closed his eyes briefly.

"Of course there is."

"The file contains an unusual departure arrangement. Mr. Morelli's employment ended under a confidential separation agreement."

"Who authorised it?"

"Chairman's office."

Adrian looked towards the door.

"My father's?"

"According to the surviving record."

"Does the file contain his signature?"

"No. Only a confirmation that approval was received."

"Who submitted the request?"

The administrator hesitated.

"Mr. Morelli himself."

Adrian leaned forward.

"What reason did he give?"

"Personal circumstances."

"That isn't a reason."

"It's the only explanation in the file."

Adrian looked at the personnel record.

"Was he paid a settlement?"

"Yes."

"How much?"

The administrator provided the figure.

Adrian frowned.

It was substantial.

Much larger than the ordinary separation package for a senior administrative officer.

"Was there a non-disclosure agreement?"

"Yes."

"Can we see it?"

"The original is missing."

Adrian slowly lowered the file.

"Missing?"

"The personnel file contains a reference to the agreement, but the agreement itself is not present."

"Is there a recipient record?"

"One."

"Who received it?"

The administrator checked.

Then he answered.

"Charles Vale."

Adrian stared at the screen.

Another connection.

Marcus Morelli had left the family office under a confidential agreement.

Charles Vale had received the original.

And Eleanor Vale had later maintained the sealed archive containing the hospital records.

The names were beginning to form a pattern.

But the pattern still did not reveal who had been protecting whom.

### **4. A Quiet Afternoon at the Valmont Estate**

Elena was sitting in the garden with Isabelle.

A notebook rested on her knees.

Isabelle had brought a university prospectus and a tablet.

They were comparing programme structures.

"Your preferred course has more statistics modules," Isabelle observed.

"Yes. That's one reason I like it."

"Most people I know try to avoid statistics."

"I don't mind it."

Isabelle looked at her.

"You're unusual."

"Thank you?"

"That wasn't necessarily a compliment."

Elena smiled.

They continued comparing the programmes.

After a while, Isabelle put down her tablet.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Sure."

"Do you ever think about what happens if the investigation proves we're not who everyone thinks we are?"

Elena closed her notebook.

"I think about it sometimes."

"What do you imagine?"

Elena considered the question.

"I imagine things changing. Legal documents. Family names. Maybe where people expect us to live."

"And what about everything else?"

"What do you mean?"

"People. Relationships. Memories."

Elena looked towards the garden.

"I don't think those things disappear just because someone finds a different birth record."

Isabelle nodded slowly.

"Do you believe that?"

"I want to."

Isabelle looked down at her hands.

"Sometimes I feel as though I've arrived halfway through someone else's life."

Elena understood.

She had felt something similar since the DNA report.

Her childhood had not changed.

But the meaning of certain memories had become uncertain.

"You're not intruding on my life," Elena said.

Isabelle looked at her.

"You're sure?"

"Yes."

Elena smiled faintly.

"We're both trying to understand something that happened before either of us could even speak."

Isabelle looked at the prospectus.

"And we still have university applications."

"Unfortunately."

"Your application is due Friday."

"Don't remind me."

"Have you finished your personal statement?"

"Almost."

"Then you should work on it."

Elena stared at her.

"You're beginning to sound like Sebastian."

"Is that bad?"

"Very."

They both laughed.

A moment later, Elena's phone vibrated.

It was a message from Clara.

**Clara:** Emergency biscuit delivery tomorrow. Bring your application draft.

**Elena:** You're becoming very involved in this application.

**Clara:** Someone has to make sure you don't write an entire essay about spreadsheets.

Elena laughed.

Isabelle glanced at the screen.

"What?"

"Clara."

"Your friend?"

"Yes. She thinks I might write about spreadsheets."

"Would you?"

"Absolutely not."

Isabelle raised an eyebrow.

Elena hesitated.

"Probably."

### **5. The Photograph in the Archive**

Edward received the photograph of Marcus Morelli shortly after lunch.

The old image had been recovered from the Aurelius personnel archive.

He compared it with the hospital corridor photograph from eighteen years earlier.

The image quality was poor.

The hospital photograph showed a man in a dark suit, speaking with Dr. Whitmore.

The man's face was partially obscured.

But there was a distinctive feature.

A small scar above the left eyebrow.

Edward enlarged the personnel photograph.

Marcus Morelli had the same scar.

He sent both images to the forensic specialist.

The response arrived twenty minutes later.

The specialist could not provide a definitive identification from the available material, but the facial proportions and visible features were consistent.

Edward immediately contacted Adrian.

"The man in the hospital photograph may be Marcus Morelli."

Adrian was silent.

"Are you certain?"

"Not completely. The image is too poor. But the match is plausible."

"Then we need to locate him."

"His personnel file is sealed."

"I know."

"His separation agreement was sent to Charles Vale."

Adrian sighed.

"That makes things more complicated."

Edward glanced at the photograph.

"There's another detail."

"What?"

"The man in the hospital photograph wears a lapel pin."

"Yes."

"We've been treating it as an unknown emblem. The personnel archive contains an old photograph of Morelli wearing the same pin."

Adrian leaned forward.

"Can you identify it?"

"Not yet. But it appears to be a private family office insignia, not the Aurelius corporate emblem."

Adrian was quiet.

"Then Morelli may have represented the family office without using its official identity."

"Exactly."

Edward looked at the old visitor record.

The person had entered the hospital as EFO-SL/07.

No personal name.

No formal office title.

Just a reference.

"Someone wanted him to be difficult to identify," Edward said.

### **6. The Former Employee**

The investigator located an old address connected to Marcus Morelli.

It was a house in a quiet residential district outside the city.

The property had changed ownership twice.

A neighbour remembered an older man living there several years earlier.

"He was very private," the neighbour told the investigator. "Didn't have many visitors."

"Do you remember his name?"

"Marcus, I think."

"Do you know where he moved?"

The neighbour shook his head.

"No. He left suddenly."

"How long ago?"

"About three years."

The investigator showed him a photograph.

The neighbour studied it.

"Yes. That's him."

"Did he have family?"

"I don't know. Sometimes a woman visited him. Older than him, perhaps. She drove a dark blue car."

"Did you know her name?"

"No."

The investigator recorded the information.

A search of property records revealed that Morelli had sold the house through a legal representative.

The sale had been completed three years earlier.

The representative's name was unfamiliar.

But the firm's address was not.

It belonged to Vale International Legal Group.

The investigator immediately contacted Edward.

Edward read the report.

"He's still using the Vale legal network."

"Possibly. The representative may simply have been appointed years earlier."

"Find out who instructed the sale."

The investigator nodded.

Edward looked at the photograph again.

Marcus Morelli had disappeared from the family office eighteen years ago.

He had later left his residence three years ago.

And his property sale had been handled by the same legal network connected to the hospital records.

Edward was beginning to suspect that Morelli had never truly left the story.

He had simply stayed out of sight.

### **7. A Question from Julian**

At the Aurelius Estate, Julian was having difficulty concentrating on his homework.

His mathematics textbook was open.

His notebook was open.

His attention was elsewhere.

Lucien walked past the study and stopped.

"You're staring at the same question."

Julian looked up.

"I'm thinking."

"About mathematics?"

"No."

Lucien entered.

"Your sister?"

Julian nodded.

Lucien pulled out a chair.

"What's troubling you?"

"Everyone keeps finding more documents, but nobody knows where she is."

Lucien leaned back.

"That's true."

"How long does it take to find someone?"

"Sometimes longer than people expect."

"She's been missing for eighteen years."

Lucien looked at him.

"I know."

Julian turned a page in his notebook.

"I've made a list of things I want to do with her."

"I've seen it."

"It's not finished."

"How many things now?"

"Thirty-nine."

Lucien smiled.

"You're going to overwhelm her."

"I can remove some."

"Perhaps ask what she wants first."

Julian frowned.

"What if she doesn't want to do anything with us?"

Lucien's expression softened.

"Then we'll respect that."

Julian looked disappointed.

"But she's our sister."

"That doesn't mean she owes us every minute of her time."

Julian thought about it.

Lucien continued.

"If we find her, she might be frightened. She might be angry. She might not want to come here immediately."

"But Mother wants her home."

"Mother wants her safe. Those aren't always the same thing."

Julian lowered his eyes.

After a moment, he nodded.

"I'll leave the list unfinished."

Lucien smiled.

"That's probably wise."

Julian looked at his mathematics textbook.

"Can you help me with this?"

Lucien examined the problem.

"Of course."

He read it for several seconds.

Then he looked up.

"Have you tried doing the first step?"

Julian stared at him.

"Obviously."

"Then why is the page blank?"

"Because I was thinking."

Lucien sighed.

"Your sister is going to have a very interesting family."

### **8. The Letter That Changed More Than One Life**

That evening, Pierce received a response from the former estate administrator who had handled Marcus Morelli's property sale.

The administrator had located an old instruction letter.

It had been kept in a closed client file.

The letter was dated three years earlier.

It requested that all personal correspondence belonging to Marcus Morelli be transferred to a private address.

The recipient was not Morelli.

It was Eleanor Vale's estate.

Pierce stared at the letter.

Eleanor had died seven years earlier.

The instruction had been written four years before her death.

That meant Morelli had arranged for his personal correspondence to be preserved under Eleanor's estate.

Pierce immediately contacted Margaret Vale.

She was surprised when he explained the discovery.

"Are you saying Marcus Morelli sent my aunt his personal papers?"

"According to the legal instruction, yes."

"Why?"

"We don't know."

"Did he contact her directly?"

"The letter suggests that he did."

Margaret was silent.

Then she said something that made Pierce sit forward.

"My aunt once told me that she was keeping something for a man who had made a terrible mistake."

Pierce gripped his pen.

"Did she name him?"

"No. But she said he had spent years trying to correct it."

"Do you remember anything else?"

Margaret thought for a moment.

"She said he had been told to choose between two children."

Pierce stopped writing.

"Two children?"

"Yes."

"Did she say who gave him that choice?"

"No."

Margaret hesitated.

"She also said he chose neither."

Pierce looked at the letter.

A man who had made a terrible mistake.

A man who had spent years trying to correct it.

A man who had been told to choose between two children.

And a man who had chosen neither.

He thanked Margaret and ended the call.

Then he sent a message to Edward and Adrian.

**Marcus Morelli's personal correspondence may have been preserved by Eleanor Vale. We need access to the estate archive.**

### **9. The Last Page**

Margaret Vale agreed to another meeting the following morning.

She had not realised that the personal papers connected to Morelli might be relevant to the hospital investigation.

She brought a sealed storage box to the legal office.

Inside were several envelopes, a notebook and a small bundle of correspondence.

Pierce examined the labels.

Most of the papers were ordinary.

Financial statements.

Property documents.

Personal letters.

Then he found an envelope with a handwritten label.

**M. MORELLI — PERSONAL INSTRUCTIONS**

The envelope had not been opened.

Margaret looked at Pierce.

"Should I open it?"

"Only if you're comfortable."

She nodded.

Pierce opened the envelope.

Inside was a letter written by Marcus Morelli.

It was dated two years after his departure from the Aurelius Family Office.

The letter was short.

> Eleanor,
>
> I have spent two years trying to understand whether what I did was necessary.
>
> I know what the families believe happened. I know what the hospital recorded. I know what Charles placed in the archive.
>
> But neither family knows what happened after the children left the nursery.
>
> I cannot correct the record until I know which child was meant to be protected.
>
> I will not choose between them again.
>
> M.

Pierce read it twice.

Edward, who had joined the meeting, looked at him.

"Again?"

Pierce nodded.

"That's what it says."

Adrian examined the letter.

"Which child was meant to be protected?"

Edward looked at the date.

"That suggests the original arrangement was not simply about returning both children to their biological families."

Margaret stood quietly beside them.

"Does it mean he was responsible?"

Pierce folded the letter carefully.

"We don't know."

Edward looked at the remaining papers.

"There's more."

At the bottom of the box was a thin notebook.

It contained several pages of handwritten notes.

Most were difficult to read.

One page, however, had been marked with a paper tab.

The heading read:

**NINTH DAY — FINAL RELEASE**

Below it were two entries.

**INFANT A — RECEIVING GUARDIAN CONFIRMED**

**INFANT B — RECEIVING GUARDIAN CONFIRMED**

Then, beneath both entries, a single sentence:

**Neither infant was delivered to the person originally named.**

Edward felt his pulse quicken.

This was the first document to state that the children had been delivered to actual, identified receiving guardians.

Not provisional placements.

Not unnamed representatives.

Real people.

But the names had been torn from the page.

Only two sets of initials remained.

**A.V.**

**C.V.**

Adrian stared at them.

"Those could be the mothers."

Edward shook his head.

"Or the receiving guardians."

Pierce examined the torn edge.

"The page has been deliberately separated from another sheet."

Margaret looked at the notebook.

"Can the missing page be found?"

Pierce turned to the last pocket.

It was empty.

But a small note had been folded inside the binding.

He unfolded it.

The handwriting was Marcus Morelli's.

**If the two receiving guardians are ever identified, do not assume they knew what they were receiving.**

Edward looked up.

The sentence changed everything.

For eighteen years, the investigation had focused on who had arranged the transfer, who had authorised the nursery and who had altered the records.

Now there was another possibility.

The people who received the children might not have known their true identities.

They might have believed they were taking home their own children.

Or they might have been told they were receiving someone else's.

Edward looked at the initials again.

**A.V.**

**C.V.**

One could refer to Aurelius.

The other to Valmont.

But the note warned against assuming that the receiving guardians knew what they were receiving.

Adrian slowly closed the notebook.

"Find the missing page."

Pierce nodded.

Margaret looked troubled.

"Is Marcus Morelli still alive?"

Nobody answered immediately.

Then Edward said, "We believe he is."

Margaret looked at the letter.

"Then perhaps he should be asked what he meant."

Edward stood.

"Yes."

He took one last look at the notebook.

The final page contained a date.

The date the children had left the hospital.

And beneath it, in a different handwriting, was a message:

**The original plan has failed. The children must remain separated until the person who selected their receiving guardians is found.**

There was no signature.

Only a name.

**ELEANOR.**
