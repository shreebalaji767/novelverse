# **Chapter 101 — The Envelope Eleanor Never Opened**

Edward placed the collection receipt in front of Jonathan.

Neither man spoke for several seconds.

The document was ordinary: a date, a name, a reference number, and a destination.

Yet its implications were anything but ordinary.

**Collected by: Eleanor Vale.**

**Recipient requested that the document remain sealed pending review of originating and receiving authority.**

**Transferred to: Unit 6 — Separate Preservation Compartment.**

Jonathan broke the silence.

“She collected the false appointment.”

“She collected a sealed document associated with it.”

“And transferred it to Unit Six.”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't she tell us?”

Edward closed the folder.

“That's what we're going to ask her.”

The archive manager approached them.

“There is one additional record.”

Edward turned.

“The document was not transferred alone. It was accompanied by a separate instruction.”

“Where is it?”

“Stored under the same restricted reference. You will need to examine it here.”

The manager retrieved a smaller envelope.

Its original seal was intact.

On the front, the writing was unmistakable.

**ELEANOR VALE — PERSONAL CUSTODY**

Beneath it:

**NOT TO BE OPENED UNTIL RECEIVING AUTHORITY IS INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED.**

Edward stared at the envelope.

“Is this the same document?”

“No. This is the accompanying instruction.”

“Was it transferred with the false appointment?”

“Yes.”

“Why was it placed under personal custody?”

The archivist consulted the index.

“Because the recipient requested that it be kept separate from the main correspondence file.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Photograph everything. Then arrange a meeting with Eleanor.”

Eleanor Vale received their request the following morning.

She did not ask why they wanted to see her.

She simply replied that she would be available after lunch.

When Edward and Jonathan arrived, she was waiting in the same office where they had examined the day-118 review.

She noticed the folder in Edward's hand.

“You found the C-17 records,” she said.

“Yes.”

“Then you know I collected the document.”

“I know you collected something.”

Eleanor looked at him.

“That's a distinction.”

“Was it the day-131 confirmation identifying Adrian Holt as receiving representative?”

“Yes.”

The answer came without hesitation.

Jonathan opened his notebook.

“Why didn't you tell us?”

“Because you had not asked me whether I collected it.”

Edward gave her a measured look.

“We asked about the day-118 review, the notification, and your preservation instructions.”

“You did. You did not ask about the C-17 correspondence.”

“You knew it was relevant.”

“I knew it was connected. I did not know whether it was relevant to the questions you were pursuing at the time.”

Edward placed the collection receipt on the table.

“You collected a document that appeared to appoint someone who had already refused the position.”

“I collected a document that appeared to establish an authority that had never been granted.”

“And you kept it sealed.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I did not have the authority to determine whether the document was valid.”

Jonathan looked up.

“You didn't need to open it to know it was a false appointment?”

“I knew Adrian Holt had declined the nomination. I had seen the withdrawal record. I also knew the receiving identity custodian position remained vacant.”

“Then why preserve the document?”

“Because a false document is still evidence.”

Edward considered that.

It was a simple answer.

Perhaps too simple.

“What was inside the accompanying instruction?” he asked.

Eleanor's expression changed.

“You found that too?”

“Yes.”

“Did you open it?”

“No. The seal was intact.”

She looked down at the table.

For the first time since they had met, she seemed uncertain.

“Then you should understand something,” she said. “I did not collect those documents because I knew who the children were. I collected them because I knew the records were being used to suggest an identity that had not been established.”

“Who told you about the false appointment?”

“No one.”

“How did you know it existed?”

“The C-17 collection register showed the document's reference and its stated purpose. The transfer description identified it as a receiving-authority confirmation.”

“But you didn't know what it contained?”

“I knew what it claimed to establish. I did not know whether the underlying evidence existed.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Did you speak to Adrian Holt?”

“No.”

“Did you tell Charles Vale you had collected it?”

“No.”

“Did you tell Dr Hale?”

“No.”

“Did you tell Victoria Aurelius?”

“No.”

“Did you tell Isabella Vale?”

“No.”

Eleanor paused.

“I told the archive custodian that the document must remain sealed.”

“Why didn't you report the false appointment?”

“I did.”

“To whom?”

“To the preservation authority responsible for the archive.”

“Was that report retained?”

“I don't know.”

Edward opened the folder and placed a copy of the collection receipt before her.

“Your instruction says the document could not be released until the receiving authority was independently verified. But the authority never existed.”

“Correct.”

“So the restriction could never be satisfied.”

“It could have been satisfied if a valid receiving identity custodian had later been appointed.”

“But nobody was appointed.”

“No.”

“Then the document remained sealed indefinitely.”

“Yes.”

Edward studied her.

“Was that intentional?”

Eleanor met his eyes.

“It was necessary.”

Jonathan placed the day-47 nomination withdrawal beside the day-131 confirmation.

“Adrian Holt refused the appointment on day forty-seven. You knew that?”

“Yes.”

“The confirmation appeared eighty-four days later.”

“Yes.”

“And you collected it the following day.”

“Yes.”

“Did you believe someone had deliberately reused his signature?”

“I believed the document required examination.”

“Did you suspect Charles Vale?”

“I suspected the document had originated from his office.”

“That's not quite the same thing.”

“No.”

Edward moved to the next page.

“Did you know the signature had been copied from the preservation receipt?”

“Not at the time.”

“Then how did you know the document was unreliable?”

“Because the appointment was unsupported. The signature could not create an authority that did not exist.”

Jonathan nodded slowly.

“Even if the signature had been original?”

“Even then.”

Edward leaned back.

Eleanor had returned to the same principle again.

A signature could establish that someone had signed a document.

It could not establish that the document was legally or factually correct.

“What did the accompanying instruction say?” he asked.

Eleanor looked at the sealed-envelope reference.

“I don't know.”

“You never opened it?”

“No.”

“Not once?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because the instruction was addressed to the person who would eventually hold the receiving authority. I was not that person.”

Jonathan frowned.

“But you were authorised to collect it.”

“Collection did not make me the intended recipient.”

Edward looked at her carefully.

“Who was?”

“I don't know.”

“Then why did you take responsibility for it?”

“Because it was at risk of being merged with the other records.”

“Was someone trying to remove it?”

“I cannot prove that.”

“Did someone request it?”

“Yes.”

Edward's expression sharpened.

“Who?”

Eleanor reached into her folder and removed a small piece of paper.

It was a request slip from the archive.

The date was day one hundred and thirty-three.

The requested material was the sealed C-17 instruction.

The requester field had been left blank.

The purpose field contained a single phrase:

**For completion of receiving identity record.**

The request had been refused.

Eleanor had written the refusal herself.

**No appointed receiving identity custodian exists. Material cannot be released.**

Edward read it.

“Was the request made by Charles Vale?”

“The name was not recorded.”

“By Adrian Holt?”

“I don't know.”

“Could it have been Marianne Cross?”

“Possibly.”

“Did you ask the archive?”

“I did. They had no additional information.”

Edward sat back.

The unidentified request had come only one day after Eleanor collected the false appointment.

Someone had wanted the sealed instruction.

And someone had wanted it for the completion of the receiving identity record.

Yet the person who was supposed to complete that record had never been appointed.

“Why didn't you tell us about this request earlier?” Jonathan asked.

“Because I didn't know who made it.”

“But it could be important.”

“It could also be unrelated. I won't present an unidentified request as evidence of a specific person's involvement.”

Edward looked at her.

He was beginning to understand that Eleanor's caution was not merely a habit.

It was the principle around which she had organised her entire response to the investigation.

Unfortunately, it also meant every conversation with her produced another unanswered question.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria received a message from Helena Brooks.

The message contained a summary of the latest archive discovery.

Eleanor Vale had collected the day-131 confirmation.

She had kept it sealed.

She had refused a subsequent request to release the accompanying instruction.

Victoria read the report twice.

Then she walked to Alexander's study.

He was reviewing a meeting schedule when she entered.

“Have you seen this?”

Alexander accepted the report.

He read it silently.

“Eleanor collected the false appointment,” he said.

“Yes.”

“And kept it sealed.”

“Yes.”

“Did she tell Edward why?”

“She says she wanted to prevent it from being merged with the other records.”

Alexander placed the report on his desk.

“Does that make sense?”

“It might.”

“Or she may know something we don't.”

Victoria looked toward the window.

“Both can be true.”

Alexander remained silent.

Victoria continued.

“I don't want us to assume that every person who protected a document was protecting the same thing. Some may have been protecting a child. Some may have been protecting their own decisions. Some may have been trying to protect a family from information they couldn't verify.”

“And some may have been hiding what they did.”

“Yes.”

Alexander stood.

“Do you want to speak to Eleanor?”

“No.”

He looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because she has already been questioned by Edward. We should not approach her simply because we are impatient.”

Victoria returned the report to his desk.

“I want the original instruction preserved. I want to know who requested it. And I want the authority records examined independently.”

Alexander nodded.

“Agreed.”

Victoria glanced at the folder containing Elena's programme information.

The name on the page was still only Elena Valmont.

She had no proof that the young woman was her daughter.

She had no right to turn an unanswered question into a claim.

For now, all she could do was wait for the truth to become something more than a possibility.

Meanwhile, Elena had discovered a problem with her travel documents.

The programme office had requested a revised copy of her emergency contact information because one of the telephone numbers on the submitted form had been entered incorrectly.

It was a small mistake.

But Elena disliked leaving errors in official documents.

She opened the original form and checked every field.

Her passport number.

Her programme reference.

Her accommodation details.

Her emergency contacts.

Then she found the incorrect number.

She corrected it, saved the revised document, and sent it to the programme coordinator.

Nicholas appeared at the doorway.

“Everything all right?”

“Yes. Small correction.”

“Problem?”

“No. Just a wrong digit.”

He came closer.

“You checked the entire form?”

“Of course.”

“Even though only one number was wrong?”

Elena looked at him.

“That's how mistakes hide.”

Nicholas smiled.

“Fair enough.”

She returned to her laptop.

“I'm also preparing a digital copy of every important document.”

“You've already done that.”

“I'm making a separate folder.”

“Why?”

“So I can access the documents even if my laptop has a problem.”

Nicholas considered this.

“You've thought of everything.”

“Not everything.”

“What have you missed?”

“Probably something.”

He laughed.

Elena saved the folder to her external drive.

She was not afraid of travelling.

She was simply aware that preparation made unfamiliar places easier to manage.

And Singapore was going to be unfamiliar.

She wanted to arrive ready to learn, not spend her first week searching for documents she should have organised at home.

The next morning, Edward received a message from the archive manager.

The unidentified request for Eleanor's sealed instruction had a corresponding entry in the physical access register.

The request itself had been unsigned.

But the person who collected the refusal notice had signed for it.

Edward opened the attached scan.

The signature was familiar.

Not because it belonged to Charles Vale.

Not because it belonged to Adrian Holt.

Not because it belonged to Marianne Cross.

It belonged to someone else.

**Margaret Voss.**

Edward stared at the name.

Margaret had refused to certify the original identity records.

She had refused to act as originating facility representative.

She had never been appointed receiving identity custodian.

She had repeatedly warned that the records could not establish which infant belonged to which family.

Yet she had personally collected the refusal notice for a request to open the sealed instruction.

Edward called Jonathan.

“Come to my office.”

“What happened?”

“We found the person who collected the refusal notice.”

“Who?”

“Margaret Voss.”

There was a pause.

“Did she request the instruction?”

“No. The request was unsigned. She collected the refusal notice after it was denied.”

“Why would she do that?”

Edward looked at the date.

Day one hundred and thirty-three.

Two days after Eleanor had collected the false appointment.

One day after the unidentified request to open the instruction.

“I don't know,” he said.

Then he noticed a second line in the access register.

**Additional material deposited by collector: One sealed envelope.**

The envelope's reference was not C-17.

It was:

**MV-18 / ORIGINAL CORRESPONDENCE**

Edward slowly sat down.

Margaret Voss had not merely collected a refusal notice.

She had deposited something of her own.

Something she had kept for eighteen years.

And the archive had stored it beside Eleanor's sealed instruction.

Neither document had been opened.

Neither had been released.

And both had been preserved under restrictions connected to the receiving identity that no one had ever been authorised to confirm.
