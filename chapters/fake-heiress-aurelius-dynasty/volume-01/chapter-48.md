# **Chapter 48 — A Name Missing from Two Families**

Edward read the confidential placement review for the fourth time.

*The applicant has been approved.*

*The child has not been assigned.*

*Do not inform the applicant that the child may already be in her care.*

The final sentence bothered him most.

**If the child asks about her mother, tell her that the answer is not in the hospital records.**

It was not ordinary legal language.

It was written for someone who might eventually have to answer a child's questions.

"Whoever wrote this expected the child to grow up asking," Jonathan said.

"Or expected the applicant to ask on the child's behalf."

"Do you think Margaret Ellison knew?"

Edward closed the folder.

"I think she knew something was wrong. Whether she knew the whole truth is another matter."

Jonathan examined the document's reference number.

"There's no originating authority."

"There's no applicant signature either."

"Could it have been added later?"

"Possibly. We need the original."

Edward picked up his phone.

"Get the registry to preserve the entire box. Nothing else leaves the archive without a record."

He made another call.

This time, it was to Marcus Morelli.

Morelli answered after the third ring.

"I was expecting you."

"Then you know why I'm calling."

"I can guess."

"We found the sixth-day placement review."

There was a pause.

"Where?"

"In Margaret Ellison's guardianship file."

Morelli exhaled.

"That document was never supposed to be in the ordinary registry."

"Why?"

"Because it was not a placement approval."

"Then what was it?"

"A warning."

Edward leaned back.

"Against whom?"

"Against the people who believed they had already arranged the children's futures."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the only one I can give you over the telephone."

Morelli ended the call.

Edward stared at the screen.

Jonathan sighed.

"He's becoming predictable."

"He's becoming careful."

"What's the difference?"

"Predictable people repeat themselves. Careful people decide what not to say."

The meeting took place the following afternoon at a small private reading room in a hotel near the old financial district.

Morelli had selected the location.

There were no family representatives, no security personnel, and no recording equipment.

Edward arrived with Jonathan.

Morelli was already seated.

He looked older than he had during their previous meeting. His hair had become almost entirely grey, and the lines around his eyes were deeper.

He had brought a thin envelope.

Edward sat opposite him.

"You said the sixth-day document was a warning."

"Yes."

"Explain."

Morelli placed the envelope on the table but did not open it.

"Margaret Ellison had been approved as a temporary receiving guardian before the births. That part was genuine. She had been interviewed, assessed, and considered suitable."

"By whom?"

"By the people responsible for the original arrangement."

"Which people?"

Morelli looked at him.

"You're asking me to name people whose involvement has not yet been established."

"You're also asking me to accept your interpretation of documents you have refused to explain."

Morelli nodded.

"Fair enough."

He opened the envelope.

Inside was a copy of a letter dated six days after the births.

The handwriting was familiar.

Edward recognised Eleanor Vale's distinctive capital letters.

*Margaret has agreed to receive the child temporarily. She must not be told which infant is being considered, and she must not be told that the other child may already have been placed.*

Edward looked up.

"Why?"

"Because the arrangement had changed."

"How?"

"One of the receiving arrangements was no longer considered safe."

"Which one?"

Morelli hesitated.

"Infant A."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Margaret was supposed to receive Infant A?"

"She was considered as a possible receiving guardian for Infant A. But she was not authorised to receive that child at the time."

"Yet she received a baby on the ninth day."

"Yes."

"Was that Infant A?"

"That is what the hospital register says."

"Was it correct?"

Morelli looked directly at him.

"I cannot confirm that."

Edward placed the letter on the table.

"Then what did Margaret receive?"

"A child whose identity had not been verified."

"Did she know?"

"She knew the placement was temporary."

"That wasn't my question."

Morelli looked down.

"She was told the child's identity would be established later."

Edward waited.

"She was not told that the child might be one of the two infants involved in the original arrangement."

Jonathan frowned.

"Why would you conceal that?"

"Because the person responsible for selecting the receiving guardians believed that knowledge would put the child at risk."

"From whom?"

Morelli did not answer.

Edward opened his notebook.

"Let's talk about Margaret Voss."

Morelli's expression changed.

"Where did you find her?"

"Hospital records. Aurelius Family Office records. A temporary guardianship application."

"Then you've found the beginning of the problem."

"Was she the person who prepared the original placement documents?"

"No."

"Did she alter them?"

"Not the original documents."

"Did she use Martha Voss's identity?"

Morelli shook his head.

"Margaret Voss and Martha Voss were two different women."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

"Then why was Martha's name used?"

"Because Martha had access to the hospital's administrative system. Margaret had access to the family office's confidential records. Someone wanted the documents to appear as though they had been processed through the hospital rather than through the Family Office."

"Who?"

Morelli looked at the envelope.

"That's what I was trying to discover."

Edward leaned forward.

"You were the Director of Family Affairs."

"Yes."

"You had access to the system."

"Yes."

"And you couldn't identify the person using your own authority?"

Morelli's expression hardened.

"Mr Valmont, I was not the only person with access to that authority."

The room fell silent.

Edward studied him.

"Then give us the access register."

Morelli slowly closed the envelope.

"I don't have it."

"Where is it?"

"With the person who took it."

"And who is that?"

Morelli looked at him for a long moment.

"I don't know."

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian was reading a newly recovered personnel record.

The name at the top was Margaret Voss.

Unlike the hospital record, this one contained a photograph.

A woman in her early thirties looked directly at the camera.

Her hair was tied back. Her expression was serious, almost stern.

Adrian enlarged the photograph.

Damien stood beside him.

"That's definitely not Martha Voss."

"No."

"Could she be Margaret Ellison?"

"We need to compare it with the guardianship records."

Damien placed another photograph on the desk.

It showed an older woman in a care facility.

Margaret Ellison.

Adrian examined both images.

"They don't look alike."

"Not necessarily proof. The photographs are eighteen years apart."

"Still."

Damien opened the personnel record.

Margaret Voss had been assigned to the Family Office on a temporary secondment.

Her responsibilities included confidential identity documentation, family records, and coordination with external legal representatives.

Her immediate supervisor was Marcus Morelli.

Her final employment review contained a short statement.

**Employee released from confidential duties following internal disagreement.**

Adrian read it again.

"An internal disagreement?"

"That's all the file says."

"Who signed the review?"

Damien pointed to the bottom.

The signature was obscured by a damaged section of paper.

Only a surname remained.

**VALE.**

Adrian looked up.

"Charles?"

"Possibly."

"Can we recover the original?"

"The paper archive is being searched."

Adrian stood.

"Find out whether Margaret Voss is still alive."

Damien nodded.

"And Adrian?"

"Yes?"

"There's another issue."

He turned the monitor towards him.

A record of Margaret Voss's final salary payment appeared on the screen.

The payment had not gone to a bank account.

It had been transferred to a legal trust.

The trust's beneficiary was not Margaret Voss.

It was listed as:

**THE CHILD — IDENTITY PENDING.**

Adrian stared at the entry.

"Which child?"

Damien shook his head.

"No name."

"Who established the trust?"

"Charles Vale."

Adrian looked at the screen again.

A trust had been created for a child whose identity was supposedly unknown.

Someone had been preparing money for that child while ensuring that neither family could identify her.

The next morning, Elena was reviewing her interview notes when Isabelle entered the library.

"Are you busy?"

"Not particularly."

Isabelle held a printed document.

"I wanted to show you something."

Elena moved her laptop aside.

"What is it?"

"The foundation's revised selection process."

She placed the document on the table.

Elena read through the first page.

The new process included an independent assessment panel, written reasons for decisions, and an appeal procedure.

It also contained a provision for students whose academic performance had been affected by family or financial circumstances.

"This is much better," Elena said.

"Do you think it's too complicated?"

"No. But you should make the application instructions simpler."

Isabelle pulled out a chair.

"Why?"

"Because the people who need the programme most might not have anyone to help them complete complicated forms."

Isabelle nodded.

"I'll ask the coordinator to review the language."

Elena turned another page.

"How many students will receive support?"

"The first group will be small. Perhaps twenty."

"That's reasonable."

"I wanted to begin with fifty."

"Can the foundation afford fifty?"

"Yes."

"Can the programme properly assess and support fifty students in its first year?"

Isabelle considered the question.

"Probably not."

"Then twenty is better."

Isabelle smiled.

"Sebastian said the same thing."

"He was right."

"Don't tell him that."

Elena laughed.

Isabelle closed the folder.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"Are you worried about going abroad?"

Elena thought about it.

"A little."

"Because of the family situation?"

"Partly. But also because I've never lived away from home."

"Neither have I."

Elena looked at her.

Isabelle continued.

"I spent so much time imagining what it would be like to find my biological family that I never really considered what would happen afterward."

"And now?"

"Now I have to build a life that isn't just about finding them."

Elena nodded.

"I think that's important."

Isabelle looked at her.

"Do you think we can both have separate lives without making things difficult for everyone?"

Elena smiled faintly.

"I hope so."

For a moment, Isabelle seemed relieved.

Then she gathered her documents.

"I'll let you finish your interview preparation."

"Good luck with the foundation meeting."

"Good luck with Singapore."

They exchanged a smile.

It was not a dramatic reconciliation.

It was something quieter.

Two young women beginning to understand that they could care about the same family without having identical futures.

At 2:15 p.m., Edward received a message from Jonathan.

**The hospital archive has located Margaret Voss's original secondment agreement.**

Edward opened the attached scan.

The agreement had been signed eight months before the births.

It confirmed that Margaret Voss had been temporarily assigned to the Aurelius Family Office.

Her duties included confidential maternity identity coordination.

There was also a clause stating that she could not independently approve the transfer of any child.

Any placement required written authorisation from the originating representative and confirmation from the hospital's designated administrator.

Edward read the clause twice.

Then he noticed an attachment.

It was a list of authorised contacts.

Three names appeared.

- Marcus Morelli — Director of Family Affairs.
- Charles Vale — External Legal Representative.
- Dr Samuel Whitmore — Hospital Administrator.

A fourth line had been crossed out.

The original name was still partly visible.

**V. AURELIUS.**

Edward leaned back.

Victoria Aurelius.

He checked the date.

The contact had been removed from the authorised list three days after the births.

There was no explanation.

No replacement.

No signed request.

Just a handwritten note.

**Mother's authority suspended pending verification.**

Edward stared at the words.

"Jonathan."

His colleague entered.

"What?"

Edward showed him the page.

Jonathan read it slowly.

"Victoria's authority was suspended?"

"Apparently."

"By whom?"

"No signature."

"Could Alexander have authorised it?"

"His office code was used on the transfer record. But he denies approving the child placements."

Jonathan looked at the crossed-out name.

"Why would someone suspend the mother's authority?"

Edward did not answer immediately.

Because the obvious explanation was also the most troubling.

Someone had decided that the mother should not be allowed to make decisions about her own child.

And they had done it using an authority that did not belong to them.

That evening, Victoria Aurelius received a call from Edward.

She was in the family library with Alexander when the phone rang.

"Edward?"

"Mrs Aurelius, I'm calling because we've recovered an old administrative record."

"What kind of record?"

"A list of authorised contacts from the Family Office."

Alexander looked towards her.

Edward continued.

"Your name appears on the original list. However, it was crossed out three days after the births."

Victoria went very still.

"Why?"

"We don't know yet."

"Was my authority suspended?"

"That is how the document describes it."

Victoria looked at Alexander.

He had heard enough to understand.

"Who authorised it?" she asked.

"There's no signature."

"Then how was it accepted?"

"We're investigating."

Victoria closed her eyes.

For eighteen years, she had believed she had been waiting for an answer.

Now she was beginning to understand that someone might have spent those same eighteen years preventing her from asking the right questions.

"Edward," she said quietly.

"Yes?"

"Was I ever informed that my authority had been suspended?"

"No record indicates that you were."

She ended the call.

Alexander stood.

"Victoria."

She looked at him.

"They took away my right to make decisions about my daughter."

"We don't know exactly what happened."

"But the document says it."

"Then we'll establish who wrote it."

Victoria placed the phone on the table.

"I was her mother."

Alexander came closer.

"I know."

"I should have been allowed to decide what happened to her."

He did not try to contradict her.

There was nothing useful he could say.

At 9:40 p.m., Jonathan sent Edward the final page from the secondment agreement.

It was a confidential annex.

The annex listed the responsibilities of Margaret Voss and the restrictions placed on her.

Most of the text was ordinary administrative language.

But the final clause was unusual.

**In the event of a disagreement between the originating representative and the receiving guardian, the receiving guardian's identity must remain undisclosed until the originating representative's authority is restored.**

Edward read it slowly.

The receiving guardian's identity.

Not the child's.

The document suggested that someone had anticipated a disagreement between the mother and the person receiving her child.

He looked at the date.

The annex had been prepared before the births.

Before either infant had been transferred.

Before the hospital records had been altered.

Someone had anticipated the possibility that a mother would object to the person selected to receive her child.

Edward turned to the final line.

It contained the name of the person who had requested the annex.

**REQUESTED BY: MARGARET VOSS.**

But the approval field contained another name.

**APPROVED: C. VALE.**

Edward placed the page beside the sixth-day placement review.

The woman who had been assigned to handle confidential maternity records had requested special restrictions before the children were born.

Charles Vale had approved them.

And somewhere between those two decisions, the mothers had lost control of their children's placements.

Edward closed the file.

He now had a new question.

Had Margaret Voss helped create the arrangement?

Or had she been trying to prevent something she already knew was coming?

The answer might depend on finding her.

But when Jonathan finally located her last registered address, the property records revealed something unexpected.

Margaret Voss had not disappeared alone.

Her departure had been registered on the same day as another person's.

**MARGARET VOSS — DEPARTURE CONFIRMED.**

**CHILD — NAME NOT ENTERED.**

The second entry had no birth certificate number.

No hospital registration.

No legal guardian.

Only a handwritten note.

**Transferred privately.**

And beneath it, a final sentence:

**This child must never appear in either family's records.**
