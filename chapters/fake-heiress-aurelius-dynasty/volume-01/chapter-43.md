# **Chapter 43 — The Account in Margaret Ellison's Name**

Edward read the message twice.

Then he called Jonathan.

"Tell me exactly what you've found."

"The intermediary account was registered under Margaret Ellison's name."

"The woman who received Infant A?"

"Yes."

Edward looked at the documents scattered across his desk.

"How certain are we?"

"The bank has recovered an archived account-opening record. The name matches, but the identification number is partially obscured. We're requesting the original verification documents."

"Who opened the account?"

"That's unclear. The signature doesn't match Margaret Ellison's known signature."

Edward went still.

"Someone opened an account in her name?"

"That's one possibility."

"Could she have authorised someone?"

"Possibly. We don't have enough evidence to say."

Edward looked at the three payment dates.

Eleventh day.

Twenty-eighth day.

Ninety-second day.

All three payments had passed through the account.

"Was the money withdrawn?"

"Yes."

"By whom?"

"The account records show cash withdrawals and two transfers to separate accounts. We're tracing those now."

Edward stood.

"Did Margaret know?"

"I haven't asked her yet."

"Don't contact her until we verify the account documents. If someone used her identity, I don't want to alert them through an unnecessary inquiry."

"Understood."

Edward ended the call.

For several seconds, he remained standing.

Margaret Ellison had received a baby on the ninth day.

She had been told the arrangement was temporary.

She had cared for the child, whom she called Anna.

She had believed she was protecting her.

But someone had also used her name to receive money.

Was it payment for caring for the child?

Was it an arrangement she had knowingly accepted?

Or had someone used her identity without her permission?

Edward looked at the photograph of Infant A.

The investigation had reached another person who might have been both a witness and a victim.

He hoped it wasn't the latter.

Margaret Ellison was sitting beside the window when Edward arrived at the care facility.

The television was switched off.

A knitted blanket lay across her knees.

She looked up as he entered.

"You're back."

"Yes, Mrs. Ellison."

"Did you find her?"

Edward pulled a chair closer.

"Not yet."

Margaret looked disappointed but did not appear surprised.

"People keep saying they'll find her."

"I know."

"Then they go away."

Edward sat down.

"I won't promise something I can't guarantee."

She studied him.

"You're a serious young man."

"I've been told."

"Your mother must be proud."

Edward looked down briefly.

He wondered what Margaret would think if she knew how many mothers were involved in the records he was investigating.

"I wanted to ask you about something from the eleventh day."

Margaret frowned.

"The eleventh day?"

"After you received the baby."

She looked toward the window.

"I remember the baby."

"Did anyone contact you about financial support?"

Margaret turned back.

"Financial support?"

"Did someone offer to pay for the child's care?"

She considered the question.

"Charles said there would be money."

"Did he explain where it came from?"

"No."

"Did you receive it?"

"I don't remember."

Edward watched her carefully.

"Did you have a bank account in those days?"

"Of course."

"Do you remember opening another account?"

"No."

"Did anyone ever ask you to sign financial documents?"

Margaret thought for a long time.

Then she shook her head.

"No. I didn't have much money. I remember that. But I had enough for Anna."

"How?"

She looked at him.

"I was working."

"At the time?"

"Before. I had savings."

Edward wrote that down.

"Did Charles Vale ever give you money directly?"

"Once."

"When?"

"A few weeks after the baby came."

"How much?"

Margaret struggled to remember.

"I don't know. He gave me an envelope. I didn't count it."

"What did he say?"

"That the child's expenses would be covered."

"Did you accept it?"

Margaret looked offended.

"What was I supposed to do? Refuse food and medicine for a baby?"

Edward lowered his notebook.

"No. I'm not suggesting you did anything wrong."

She looked at him for a moment.

Then her expression softened.

"I loved that child."

"I believe you."

"I wasn't paid to love her."

Edward did not answer.

Margaret looked toward the garden.

"She used to wake up very early. Before sunrise. I would carry her around the house until she fell asleep again."

Her voice became quieter.

"I used to wonder who her mother was. Whether she had the same habit. Whether she liked mornings."

Edward felt something tighten in his chest.

"Did you ever ask Charles?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"That her mother knew where she was."

Margaret looked down at her hands.

"I believed him."

The bank's archived records arrived that afternoon.

Jonathan called Edward into the examination room.

"We have the account-opening file."

Edward took the first page.

The account had been created under Margaret Ellison's name eleven days after the births.

The address was not Margaret's residence.

It was a rented office belonging to Vale Private Counsel.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Why would her account be registered at a law office?"

"That's what I asked."

He handed Edward another sheet.

"The account was opened through a representative."

"Name?"

Jonathan pointed to the signature.

**D. MERCER.**

Edward recognised it immediately.

Daniel Mercer.

The former Vale legal assistant who had been listed as a supporting witness in the ninth-day arrangements.

"Mercer opened the account."

"Apparently."

"Did Margaret sign anything?"

"There's a signature on the authorisation form, but it doesn't match her known handwriting."

Edward examined it.

The signature was short and unusually neat.

"Could it have been copied?"

"Possibly. The examiner hasn't completed the comparison."

Edward turned to the next page.

The account had a second authorised user.

**CHARLES VALE.**

Edward looked up.

"So Charles controlled the account."

"At least administratively."

"Was Margaret ever given access?"

"The bank has no record of her visiting a branch or using the account."

Edward read the withdrawal history.

Three cash withdrawals.

Two transfers.

The first transfer had gone to a private medical supplier.

The second had gone to a residential address.

He pointed to the first.

"Who received this?"

"An account belonging to a medical supply company."

"Which one?"

Jonathan handed him the supporting record.

Edward read the name.

It was a small medical supplier that had closed more than a decade ago.

The second transfer was more interesting.

The recipient address belonged to a property in the northern part of the city.

A property that had been rented for only four months.

"Who lived there?"

"We're checking."

Edward looked again at the account.

Margaret had been the named beneficiary.

Charles had been the authorised controller.

Daniel had opened it.

And Morelli's payments had funded it.

"Why would Morelli send money through an account controlled by Charles?"

Jonathan shook his head.

"Either he knew who controlled it, or someone arranged the payments without telling him."

Edward closed the file.

"Find Mercer."

Daniel Mercer received Edward's call at 4:20 p.m.

He did not sound surprised.

"I wondered when you would reach the account."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"You knew about it?"

"I knew an account existed."

"Under Margaret Ellison's name?"

"Yes."

"Did she authorise it?"

"No."

Edward paused.

"Then why was her name used?"

"Because the money was intended for the child."

"That doesn't answer the question."

Mercer sighed.

"Charles told me the account was necessary. He said the guardian shouldn't have direct access to the source of the money."

"Why?"

"Because the arrangement was confidential."

"Was Margaret told?"

"Not by me."

"Did she know Charles controlled the account?"

"I doubt it."

Edward wrote something down.

"Who received the money?"

"Expenses were paid on behalf of the child. Medical supplies, clothing, household costs."

"Who received the second transfer?"

Mercer did not answer.

"Mr. Mercer?"

"The address belonged to a woman who was looking after another infant."

Edward's pen stopped.

"Another infant?"

"Yes."

"Which one?"

"I don't know."

"You were involved in the legal arrangements."

"I was an assistant. I prepared documents. I didn't know the children's identities."

"Did the woman live alone?"

"Yes."

"Did she receive the child from Charles?"

"I don't know."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Can you give us her name?"

Mercer hesitated.

"I can give you the name on the rental agreement."

"Do it."

"Her name was..."

He stopped.

"Mr. Mercer?"

"Victoria."

Edward's expression changed.

"Victoria what?"

"I don't remember the surname. The agreement was filed under a shortened name."

"Did it include an initial?"

"Yes."

"What initial?"

"A."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

**Victoria A.**

The name used on the tenth-day receiving acknowledgment.

The woman who had collected an infant under a false or incomplete identity.

Edward returned to the call.

"Was this property rented on the tenth day?"

"No. The lease began on the twelfth."

"Who arranged it?"

"Charles."

"Who paid?"

"That account."

Edward slowly put down his pen.

The money from the account in Margaret Ellison's name had funded a property occupied by a woman called Victoria A.

The same woman who had received an infant under an identity that was not Victoria Aurelius.

"Did you meet her?"

"Once."

"What did she look like?"

"Ordinary. I don't remember much."

"Did she have a child?"

Mercer was silent.

"I saw a baby."

"Was the baby marked Infant A or Infant B?"

"I don't know."

"Did Charles tell you whose child it was?"

"No."

Edward leaned forward.

"Did he tell you why the woman was using the name Victoria?"

Mercer took a breath.

"He said it was the name she had been instructed to use."

"By whom?"

"I don't know."

Edward ended the call only after obtaining the address and the remaining details.

He sat in silence.

The account had connected three people.

Margaret Ellison.

Charles Vale.

And the unnamed woman called Victoria A.

But the money had been registered in Margaret's name.

Which meant the financial records had been designed to make one arrangement look like another.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was reading a report about the old private account when Alexander entered.

"Have you seen this?"

She handed him the paper.

He read it.

"An account under Margaret Ellison's name?"

"Yes."

"Controlled by Charles Vale."

"Yes."

Alexander placed the report on the desk.

"Why was our Family Office paying into an account controlled by Vale?"

"That's what we're trying to find out."

He looked at the dates.

"The first payment was on the eleventh day."

"After the tenth-day transfer."

Alexander nodded.

Victoria sat down.

"Do you remember a woman named Victoria A.?"

He looked at her.

"No."

"She collected an infant using your name."

"I know."

"Was there anyone in the Family Office who could have arranged it?"

Alexander considered the question.

"Several people could have accessed administrative records. But very few could have used my delegated authority."

"Morelli?"

"Possibly."

"Charles?"

"Not directly."

Victoria looked at the account record.

"Someone used your authority, used my name, and arranged for a child to be moved."

Alexander's face hardened.

"Yes."

"And someone paid for it using an account registered under another woman's identity."

He looked at her.

"Victoria, I want you to understand something."

"What?"

"Until we establish who authorised these arrangements, we cannot assume every person involved knew what was happening."

Victoria nodded slowly.

"I know."

She looked toward the garden.

"But someone did know."

Alexander did not disagree.

Elena received a message from the university later that evening.

The interview would take place online.

She read the instructions, checked the required documents, and made a list of questions she might be asked.

Then she closed the laptop.

Nicholas was passing through the living room.

"Everything okay?"

"Yes."

"Interview?"

"Confirmed."

"Good."

He continued walking.

Then he stopped.

"Do you need anything?"

Elena looked at him.

"Actually, yes."

"What?"

"Could you stop asking me whether I need anything every five minutes?"

Nicholas looked wounded.

"I was trying to be helpful."

"I know."

"That's a terrible reward."

She laughed.

Then her expression softened.

"I'm nervous."

Nicholas returned to the sofa.

"That's normal."

"What if I don't answer something properly?"

"Then answer the next question properly."

"What if I don't get selected?"

"Then you apply somewhere else."

"What if—"

"Elena."

She stopped.

He looked at her.

"You don't have to solve every possible problem before it happens."

She stared at him.

Then she slowly smiled.

"That's annoyingly reasonable."

"I know."

He stood again.

"Now I'm going to leave you alone."

"Thank you."

"Unless you need something."

"Nicholas."

He laughed and walked away.

Elena returned to her notes.

For once, she allowed herself to think about the interview without thinking about the hospital, the records, or the mystery surrounding her birth.

It was her opportunity.

Whatever happened elsewhere, she wanted to earn it herself.

That night, Edward received one final message from Jonathan.

The property rented under the name Victoria A. had been located.

It had been occupied for four months.

The landlord remembered the tenant.

He also remembered the baby.

But the tenant had not been the child's permanent guardian.

She had left the property with the infant after receiving a sealed instruction from Charles Vale.

The landlord had kept the rental application.

It contained a single emergency contact.

Edward opened the attachment.

The name was familiar.

Not because it belonged to a member of either family.

But because it had appeared in the investigation months earlier.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

Edward stared at the screen.

The same woman whose name had been used to open the intermediary account was also listed as the emergency contact for the woman called Victoria A.

He immediately called Jonathan.

"Did Mercer confirm this?"

"No. The landlord provided the original rental application."

"Was Margaret Ellison aware of the arrangement?"

"We don't know."

Edward looked at the document again.

The woman called Victoria A. had been connected to Margaret.

But Margaret had received Infant A on the ninth day.

And Eleanor had received Infant B on the tenth.

So why had another woman, using the name Victoria A., rented a property and received financial support through an account registered under Margaret's name?

Edward looked at the final field on the application.

**RELATIONSHIP TO EMERGENCY CONTACT:**

The answer had been written in faded ink.

**SISTER.**

For the first time, the investigation had uncovered a possible connection between the two women who had received the children.

Margaret Ellison might not have been an isolated caretaker.

She might have been part of a much larger arrangement.

And somewhere in the old records, there was now a name that Edward needed to verify.

The woman called Victoria A. had listed Margaret Ellison as her sister.

But Margaret had never mentioned having one.
