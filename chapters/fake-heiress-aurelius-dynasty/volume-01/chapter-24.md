# **Chapter 24 — The Pages That Were Never Returned**

Edward read the preservation note a third time.

**Original maternal identification pages transferred to authorised recipient for confidential verification.**

**Reference: EFO-SL/07.**

**Do not return these pages to the hospital until both mothers have been identified.**

There was something deeply troubling about the wording.

The pages had not simply been lost.

They had been removed deliberately, transferred to someone outside the hospital and withheld for a specific purpose.

Someone had wanted the identities of both mothers verified.

Yet eighteen years later, neither family had received a reliable explanation of what happened to their children.

Edward picked up his phone.

"Adrian, I need you to check something."

"Go ahead."

"The preservation note is signed by Martin Hale. It says he transferred the original maternal identification pages to an authorised recipient under EFO-SL/07."

Adrian was silent.

"Did the note identify the recipient?"

"No. Only the reference number."

"Then we need to locate the transfer receipt."

"That's what I'm thinking."

"Don't confront Hale until we know what document we're looking for."

Edward looked at the signature again.

"I agree."

He ended the call and opened the hospital's archival inventory.

The missing pages had been assigned a temporary storage reference.

But the destination field contained something unexpected.

**VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL — CONFIDENTIAL HOLDING.**

Edward slowly leaned back.

The pages had not gone to an unknown storage facility.

They had gone to the same legal office that maintained Box 114.

And somewhere in that office, the missing pages might still exist.

### **1. A Second Visit to the Archive**

Jonathan Pierce arrived at the Valmont Estate shortly before ten the following morning.

He carried a slim leather folder.

Edward was waiting in the study with Richard and Sebastian.

"Did you find something?" Richard asked.

"Possibly."

Pierce placed the folder on the desk.

"The hospital's preservation note refers to a transfer to Vale Private Counsel. I checked the surviving archive index."

Edward opened the folder.

Inside was a photocopy of an old transfer register.

One entry had been highlighted.

**TRANSFERRED MATERIAL: MATERNAL IDENTIFICATION — TWO ORIGINAL PAGES**

**ORIGIN: MERIDIAN MATERNITY HOSPITAL**

**DESTINATION: VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL — CONFIDENTIAL HOLDING**

**RECEIVING REFERENCE: EFO-SL/07**

**RECEIVED BY: C. VALE**

Sebastian looked up.

"Charles Vale received them personally?"

"According to this register, yes."

Richard examined the document.

"Then why were they not included in Box 114?"

"Because the transfer register lists them separately. They were not formally incorporated into the legal file."

Edward tapped the receiving reference.

"Do we know what happened after Charles received them?"

"Not yet. The legal repository has a secondary inventory, but it isn't part of the ordinary file index."

"Can we access it?"

Pierce hesitated.

"We can request it. However, there is a complication."

Richard's expression tightened.

"What complication?"

"The secondary inventory is connected to the same restricted holding as Box 114."

Sebastian exhaled.

"Of course it is."

Pierce continued.

"The repository's access record says the original pages were transferred into a sealed subfolder. That subfolder was subsequently assigned a separate retention number."

Edward leaned forward.

"Do we have that number?"

Pierce turned the page.

**VALE PRIVATE COUNSEL — HOLDING 114-B.**

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Richard finally asked, "Is 114-B part of Box 114?"

"Yes. But it has its own access restriction."

"What restriction?"

Pierce looked at his notes.

"The same recipient designation."

Edward frowned.

"The mother of the children?"

Pierce nodded.

"Exactly."

Sebastian looked at the two documents side by side.

"So the original identification pages and the final handover documents were deliberately kept together."

"Or deliberately kept separate within the same restricted holding," Pierce corrected.

Richard stood and walked towards the window.

"How many people knew about this?"

"That is what we're trying to establish."

Edward looked at the transfer register.

Charles Vale had received the pages.

Eleanor Vale had later requested that the file remain sealed.

Martin Hale had signed the hospital preservation note.

And EFO-SL/07 appeared on every important document.

The reference was no longer an incidental administrative code.

It was the centre of the entire investigation.

### **2. The Man Who Had Signed the Note**

Martin Hale did not answer his telephone.

He had stopped responding to Jonathan Pierce after their last conversation, and the investigator assigned to locate him reported that he had not visited his usual residence for two days.

Adrian was informed shortly before lunch.

He immediately contacted Damien.

"Find out where Hale is."

"Already checking."

"Don't approach him aggressively. We need his cooperation."

Damien paused.

"Understood."

"Someone has threatened him before. Make sure the team knows that."

"I'll handle it."

Adrian ended the call.

Alexander was standing near the office entrance.

"What happened?"

"Hale signed the preservation note that transferred the original maternal identification pages to Charles Vale."

Alexander's expression changed.

"Does he remember doing it?"

"We haven't asked him yet."

"Then ask him."

Adrian opened the report.

"There's something else. The pages were stored under Holding 114-B, alongside the final handover documents."

Alexander looked at the reference.

"Then the person who controlled EFO-SL/07 had access to both."

"Yes."

"Find that person."

"We're working on it."

Alexander remained quiet for a moment.

Then he asked, "Have we established whether Charles Vale acted independently?"

"No."

"Could he have been following instructions from someone else?"

"Certainly."

Alexander looked towards the windows.

"Then we should not assume he was responsible for everything simply because his name appears on the documents."

Adrian nodded.

It was a reasonable warning.

The investigation had already shown how easily a name could appear on a document without proving who had authorised its contents.

Alexander knew that better than anyone.

His own name had appeared on a transfer authorisation he did not remember signing.

### **3. A Different Kind of Preparation**

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was having a considerably more productive morning.

Her university application was almost complete.

She had selected three programmes, compared their course structures and prepared a preliminary budget.

The final part required a personal statement.

She had been staring at the blank document for nearly twenty minutes.

Nicholas entered the library carrying a plate.

"Emergency supplies."

Elena looked up.

"What emergency?"

"You haven't eaten anything since breakfast."

"I had breakfast."

"That was three hours ago."

"I'm working."

"Which is why I brought food."

He placed the plate beside her laptop.

There were sandwiches and several pieces of fruit.

Elena looked at them.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

Nicholas glanced at the screen.

"Personal statement?"

"Yes."

"What's the problem?"

"I know what I want to say. I just don't know how to write it without sounding as though I'm trying to impress someone."

Nicholas pulled out the chair opposite her.

"Then don't try to impress them."

"That's easy for you to say."

"Write what you actually think."

Elena stared at the document.

"I want to study economics because I want to understand how organisations work. I want to learn how to evaluate projects, manage resources and make decisions."

"That sounds reasonable."

"But it feels too ordinary."

Nicholas frowned.

"Why?"

"Because everyone applying will say something about wanting to make a difference or become a leader."

"Then don't write that."

Elena looked at him.

He shrugged.

"You don't need to sound like everyone else."

She considered his words.

Nicholas picked up a sandwich.

"Besides, you have a genuine reason. You have spent years watching how businesses operate. You ask questions about everything. You once spent an entire family dinner arguing with Sebastian about whether a company should expand into a market where it had no established distribution network."

Elena blinked.

"I remember that dinner."

"I remember it because Sebastian looked like he wanted to resign from being your brother."

"That's not helpful."

"It is. It proves you care about the subject."

Elena smiled.

She returned to the document.

Nicholas watched her for a moment.

"Are you nervous about going abroad?"

"A little."

"About the university?"

"About everything being unfamiliar."

"That's normal."

She looked at him.

"You don't think I should stay?"

Nicholas stopped eating.

"Is that what you want?"

"No. I'm asking what you think."

"I think you should go if you want to go."

Elena lowered her gaze.

"Even if everyone is busy with the investigation?"

Nicholas's expression softened.

"Especially then."

He leaned back.

"Your life doesn't have to stop because the adults are trying to solve an eighteen-year-old mystery."

Elena laughed quietly.

"You're making it sound very simple."

"It isn't simple. But your application is due next Friday, and I refuse to let Edward's paperwork ruin your chances."

"Edward isn't ruining anything."

"Not intentionally."

She shook her head.

For a moment, she felt the familiar warmth of her family.

It was reassuring.

And increasingly difficult to take for granted.

### **4. Isabelle's First Independent Decision**

Isabelle was in Catherine's foundation office.

She had spent the previous afternoon reviewing the foundation's education programmes.

There were scholarship initiatives, school renovation projects and financial assistance programmes for students from low-income families.

Catherine had offered to arrange a meeting with the programme director.

Isabelle had accepted, but with one condition.

She wanted to participate as a student volunteer first.

Not as the Valmont daughter.

Not as the biological heiress.

As someone who wanted to understand the work.

The programme director, Mrs. Bennett, had been surprised by the request.

Now she was reviewing a set of application forms with Isabelle.

"These are the current scholarship applications," she explained. "Most applicants come from families who cannot afford the full cost of university education."

Isabelle examined the forms.

"What happens if a student qualifies academically but their family income is slightly above the limit?"

"We review exceptional circumstances. But we have to be careful. The funds are limited."

"Is there an appeals process?"

"Yes."

"How often are applications rejected because documents are incomplete?"

Mrs. Bennett considered the question.

"More often than we would like."

Isabelle looked through the pages.

"Then perhaps the application instructions could be clearer."

Mrs. Bennett smiled.

"That's one of the things we are trying to improve."

Isabelle began making notes.

She was not trying to prove anything to Catherine.

She simply wanted to be useful.

When Catherine entered the office later, she found Isabelle comparing two versions of the application form.

"How is it going?"

"Good. I think the instructions need simplifying."

Catherine looked at the notes.

"You've been busy."

"There's quite a lot to learn."

Catherine sat beside her.

"Would you like me to arrange a meeting with the foundation's senior team?"

Isabelle hesitated.

"Not yet. I would rather understand the programme first."

Catherine studied her daughter.

"All right."

Isabelle returned to her notes.

After a moment, she spoke again.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think people will assume I'm doing this because I want to inherit the foundation?"

Catherine was quiet.

"Some people may make that assumption."

"Does that bother you?"

"It would bother me if you allowed their assumptions to decide what you do."

Isabelle looked down.

"I don't want to be compared with Elena all the time."

Catherine gently closed the folder.

"Then don't measure your life against hers."

"That's easier said than done."

"I know."

Catherine placed a hand over Isabelle's.

"You have your own abilities. You have your own interests. You don't have to become Elena, and you don't have to become the daughter I imagined eighteen years ago."

Isabelle looked at her.

For the first time that day, she seemed uncertain.

"What if I don't know what kind of daughter you imagined?"

Catherine smiled sadly.

"Then we will get to know each other without trying to force you into that picture."

Isabelle nodded.

She returned to her work.

But the conversation stayed with her.

### **5. The Storage Record**

At 3:15 p.m., Damien located Martin Hale.

He was staying in a small serviced apartment on the outskirts of the city.

Damien went with one investigator and a lawyer representing the Aurelius family.

Hale opened the door after the third knock.

He looked tired.

"Mr. Aurelius's people again."

"We need to speak with you," Damien said.

"I've already answered your questions."

"Then this shouldn't take long."

Hale looked at the lawyer.

"What's this about?"

"The original maternal identification pages," the lawyer replied.

Hale's expression changed.

He stepped back.

"Come in."

The apartment was modest.

A small table stood near the window, covered with unopened mail and several old folders.

Hale offered them chairs.

Damien remained standing.

The lawyer placed a copy of the preservation note on the table.

"Do you recognise this?"

Hale examined it.

"Yes."

"Your signature?"

"Yes."

"Did you transfer the original maternal identification pages to Charles Vale?"

Hale rubbed his forehead.

"I transferred a sealed packet."

"Did you know what it contained?"

"Not at the time."

"Who instructed you?"

"Charles."

"Was he acting on behalf of EFO-SL/07?"

Hale stared at the reference.

"I don't know."

Damien stepped closer.

"Mr. Hale, we have found this reference on several documents. You were involved in the transfer. You must know something."

Hale looked towards the window.

"I knew there was an external liaison. I didn't know the person's identity."

"Did you ever meet them?"

"Once."

The room became still.

The lawyer opened his notebook.

"When?"

"At the hospital. Shortly before the births."

"Was it the same man you described previously?"

Hale nodded slowly.

"Yes. I saw him speaking to the administrator."

"Can you describe him?"

Hale closed his eyes, searching his memory.

"Middle-aged. Tall. Dark hair, though it was beginning to grey. He wore a dark suit. I remember his voice more than his face."

"What did he say?"

Hale hesitated.

"He asked whether the private nursery had been prepared."

Damien's expression hardened.

"And?"

"He asked whether the two maternal identification pages had been separated from the infant records."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

The lawyer leaned forward.

"Did he identify himself?"

Hale shook his head.

"No."

"Did he carry identification?"

"Not that I saw."

"Did he use the EFO-SL/07 reference?"

Hale looked at him.

"Yes."

The lawyer stopped writing.

"What exactly did he say?"

Hale took a breath.

"He said, 'The liaison has already confirmed the authority. You will follow the original arrangement unless a written amendment is received.'"

Damien exchanged a glance with the lawyer.

"Did you ask who had confirmed it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Hale's expression became bitter.

"Because I was a junior administrative officer. I had been told that the Aurelius family office had approved the arrangement. Charles Vale was the senior partner. The hospital administrator was cooperating. I had no reason to believe the paperwork was false."

"But you later signed the preservation note."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Hale looked down at his hands.

"Because by then, I knew something had gone wrong."

"What had gone wrong?"

Hale did not answer immediately.

Finally, he said, "The two children had been released from the private nursery."

Damien frowned.

"We already know that."

"No. You know what the register says. You don't know what happened afterwards."

The room fell silent.

Hale looked at the old note.

"I was told to sign the preservation document because the receiving guardians had not been independently identified. The original maternal pages were transferred to Charles Vale for verification."

"Did you know who received the children?" the lawyer asked.

Hale shook his head.

"No."

"Did you ever see them again?"

"No."

Damien studied him.

"Then why did someone call you and tell you not to reveal the archive?"

Hale's face tightened.

"Because I know who delivered the final instruction."

### **6. The Final Instruction**

Hale stood and walked to the table.

He opened one of the folders.

Inside were several old letters, a photocopy of a hospital register and a small envelope.

He picked up the envelope.

"I kept this because I was told it might be important one day."

The lawyer examined it.

"Who gave it to you?"

"Charles Vale."

"When?"

"Several months after the births."

Damien looked at him.

"Why didn't you provide this earlier?"

"Because I was told not to release it unless the original authority was questioned."

"By whom?"

Hale hesitated.

"Charles."

He handed over the envelope.

The lawyer checked the seal.

It was intact.

"May we open it?"

Hale nodded.

The lawyer carefully broke the seal.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

It contained a brief instruction.

**To Martin Hale,**

**The original maternal identification pages must remain outside the hospital's ordinary archive. Their return is prohibited until the originating representative confirms that both mothers have been independently identified.**

**Do not accept confirmation from the hospital administrator.**

**Do not accept confirmation from the family office.**

**Only the originating representative may authorise the return.**

There was no signature.

Only the reference:

**EFO-SL/07.**

Damien read the final line.

"Only the originating representative."

The lawyer looked at Hale.

"Did Charles tell you who that was?"

Hale shook his head.

"No."

"Did he say why the family office could not be trusted?"

Hale looked at the paper.

"He said someone had been using the family's authority without permission."

Damien's expression darkened.

"Did he identify the person?"

"No."

"Did he say whether the person was connected to the Aurelius family?"

Hale hesitated.

Then he gave a quiet answer.

"He said the person had access to both families."

### **7. The Report**

The documents reached Adrian that evening.

He read them once.

Then again.

Alexander joined him in the study.

"What's happened?"

Adrian handed him the instruction.

Alexander read it slowly.

**Do not accept confirmation from the hospital administrator.**

**Do not accept confirmation from the family office.**

His expression became increasingly grave.

"Who wrote this?"

"We don't know. Charles Vale gave it to Hale."

"And the originating representative?"

"Still unidentified."

Alexander placed the paper on the desk.

"Someone had enough authority to establish the private nursery arrangement, but the instruction later prohibited the hospital and the Aurelius Family Office from confirming the mothers' identities."

Adrian nodded.

"It suggests the originating representative was supposed to remain independent of both institutions."

"Or someone wanted us to believe that."

Adrian looked at him.

"Yes."

Alexander turned towards the window.

The investigation was no longer simply about a missing daughter.

It was about a chain of authority that had been deliberately obscured.

Someone had used the Aurelius name.

Someone had arranged the private nursery.

Someone had controlled the original maternal identification pages.

Someone had prevented the hospital and the family office from verifying the children's identities.

And someone had instructed Charles Vale to keep the truth away from both families.

Alexander spoke quietly.

"Find the originating representative."

"We're trying."

"Then widen the search. Examine every person who had authority to communicate with both the hospital and Vale Private Counsel."

Adrian nodded.

"I'll coordinate with Edward."

Alexander looked at the instruction once more.

"And find out who delivered the final instruction to Hale."

### **8. The Name on the Visitor Register**

At 9:20 p.m., Edward received a message from the hospital archivist.

It contained a scanned copy of an additional visitor register.

The register had been discovered inside a separate administrative folder.

It listed visitors who had entered the maternity department during the week the two infants were in the private nursery.

Edward opened the attachment.

There were several names.

Doctors.

Administrative staff.

A maintenance supervisor.

Two representatives from Vale Private Counsel.

Then he reached the final entry.

**VISITOR: EFO-SL/07**

**DATE: SEVEN DAYS AFTER BIRTHS**

**PURPOSE: MATERNAL IDENTITY VERIFICATION**

**ACCOMPANIED BY: MARTIN HALE**

The visitor's name field was blank.

But the receiving officer had added a handwritten note.

*Visitor requested to see both mothers separately. Meeting conducted without hospital administrator present.*

Edward read it twice.

Both mothers.

Separately.

The visitor had met Victoria Aurelius and Catherine Valmont.

Seven days after the births.

At a time when the children were still in the private nursery.

He enlarged the scanned page.

At the bottom, beneath the visitor's entry, was a small signature.

It was not Martin Hale's.

It was not Charles Vale's.

It was a woman's signature.

Edward stared at it.

The archivist had written a note beside the image:

*Signature identified provisionally as Dr. Eleanor Vale. Verification pending.*

Edward slowly lowered his phone.

Eleanor Vale had not merely reviewed the legal documents after the births.

She had been at the hospital.

She had met both mothers.

And she had done so under the authority of EFO-SL/07.

He called Adrian.

When Adrian answered, Edward spoke carefully.

"We may have found the person who handled the original identity verification."

"Who?"

Edward looked again at the visitor register.

"Eleanor Vale."

A pause.

Then Adrian asked, "Are you certain?"

"No. The signature is being verified."

Edward enlarged the image once more.

"But there's something else."

"What?"

"The register says she met both mothers separately."

He stopped.

The next line was almost hidden beneath the edge of the scanned page.

It was a handwritten instruction, added after the visitor had left.

**Neither mother was to be told which child had been assigned to her.**

Edward felt a chill run through him.

Because it meant that, seven days after the births, someone had already known that the children were not going to the families listed in the original instructions.

And Eleanor Vale had personally made sure neither mother learned the truth.
