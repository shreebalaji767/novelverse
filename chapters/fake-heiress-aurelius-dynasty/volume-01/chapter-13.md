# **Chapter 13 — The Result That Wasn't Ready**

The problem with waiting for important news was that ordinary life refused to wait alongside it.

Elena discovered this at 7:18 on Monday morning, when her alarm rang for the third time and she realised she had spent the previous night worrying about a laboratory report instead of studying for her upcoming university examination.

She stared at the ceiling.

Then she turned over and buried her face in the pillow.

"Five more minutes."

A knock sounded at her bedroom door.

"Five minutes ago, you said that."

Nicholas.

Elena closed her eyes again.

"You're imagining things."

"Then the imaginary person outside your door is going to eat your breakfast."

She sat up immediately.

"Don't you dare."

"Excellent. You're awake."

His footsteps retreated down the corridor.

Elena looked at her alarm clock.

7:19.

She had approximately twenty minutes before she needed to leave.

The laboratory report could wait.

Her breakfast could not.

The breakfast room was unusually crowded.

Richard was already seated at the head of the table, reading through a document on his tablet. Catherine was speaking quietly with Isabelle, who had arrived early and was examining a university timetable.

Sebastian was drinking coffee.

Edward was looking at his phone.

Nicholas, naturally, was eating something that did not belong to him.

Elena stopped beside the table.

"That's mine."

Nicholas glanced down at the pastry in his hand.

"Good morning to you too."

"You're eating my almond croissant."

"I thought it was abandoned."

"It was on my plate."

"Exactly. An abandoned plate."

Elena pulled out her chair.

"You're impossible."

"Yet you continue to live with me."

"Unfortunately."

Isabelle covered a smile with her teacup.

Nicholas pointed at her.

"See? Isabelle understands my suffering."

"I haven't said anything," Isabelle replied.

"Silence is support."

"That's not how it works," Edward said without looking up.

Nicholas sighed.

"Nobody appreciates me."

"Have you tried being less irritating?" Sebastian asked.

"Have you tried being less Sebastian?"

For a moment, the breakfast room felt almost normal.

Elena found herself appreciating that.

There were too many things she could not control at the moment. The hospital records, the old photograph, the Aurelius family, the mysterious letter, and the laboratory report all seemed to exist somewhere beyond her reach.

But Nicholas stealing her breakfast was a problem she understood.

She reached across the table and took the remaining half of his pastry.

Nicholas stared at her.

"That's theft."

"Consider it compensation."

"For what?"

"Emotional damages."

Edward finally looked up.

"Can we establish a formal record of this incident?"

"You're supposed to be investigating the hospital," Nicholas said.

"I can do both."

The laboratory called at 10:40 a.m.

Edward was in his office when his phone rang.

He recognised the number immediately.

"Dr. Mehta."

"Mr. Valmont. Thank you for returning my earlier call."

"Has the verification been completed?"

"Not entirely. We have completed the initial genetic analysis, but the historical identity documentation still requires clarification."

Edward leaned back in his chair.

"Explain precisely what has been established."

There was a brief pause.

"The biological samples are intact. Their identification and chain-of-custody records are consistent with the documentation submitted to us. The discrepancy concerns the historical hospital registration number associated with one of the reference records."

"The original V. Aurelius registration?"

"Yes. The number on the historical identification card does not correspond with the number appearing in the hospital's revised registration database."

"Does that affect the DNA analysis?"

"No. Genetic relationship analysis does not depend on the hospital's registration number. However, we cannot issue a final report that connects the biological findings to the disputed historical birth records until the identity documentation has been properly reconciled."

Edward considered that.

"So you can establish the biological relationships independently."

"Yes. But we need to maintain a clear distinction between biological findings and the historical identities recorded at the hospital."

"How long?"

"We have requested additional documentation. Depending on how quickly it is supplied, the final report may take several more working days."

Edward rubbed his forehead.

"Understood. Send me a written explanation of the discrepancy and the documents you require."

"Certainly."

The call ended.

Edward remained seated.

The laboratory had not found a problem with the samples.

It had found a problem with the paperwork.

That distinction mattered.

Someone had changed the hospital's records. Someone had separated the original transfer documents. Someone had placed a letter inside a sealed legal file and made certain it would reach a particular person only under specific circumstances.

And now, even the number attached to the original birth registration did not match the number in the revised system.

Edward opened his notebook.

He wrote three words.

**Original identity verification.**

Then he added another line beneath them.

**Who changed it?**

At university, Elena was discovering that her classmates had an impressive ability to discuss several unrelated subjects simultaneously.

"You're telling me you haven't decided whether you're attending the networking event?"

Clara sat opposite her in the university cafeteria, holding a sandwich.

Elena looked at her laptop.

"I haven't decided whether I want to attend."

"That's a different sentence."

"Is it?"

"Yes. One means you're busy. The other means you're avoiding something."

Elena closed the document.

"You're becoming annoyingly perceptive."

"I've always been perceptive. You simply weren't paying attention."

Elena smiled.

Clara leaned forward.

"How are things at home?"

"Complicated."

"Still?"

"Still."

"Any new developments?"

"The laboratory is checking some old registration details."

Clara frowned.

"Does that mean something went wrong?"

"Not with the samples, apparently. There's a mismatch between an old hospital number and the revised records."

Clara considered this.

"So the hospital paperwork is suspicious."

"That's one way of describing it."

"That's a very polite way of describing it."

Elena stirred her coffee.

"I don't think anyone knows what happened yet."

"Do you want to know?"

Elena looked at her.

"Of course I do."

"Then why do you look like you wish everyone would stop investigating?"

The question caught her off guard.

She thought about it.

"Because every time they find something, it becomes another thing I have to think about."

Clara nodded.

"Fair."

Elena looked out through the cafeteria windows.

She had spent eighteen years believing that the story of her birth was simple. Her parents had brought her home, her brothers had grown up with her, and she had become part of the family.

Now she was learning that the beginning of her life might have been decided by people she had never met.

She did not know whether she wanted to confront them.

She only knew she wanted the truth.

"I still have an examination next week," she said eventually.

Clara blinked.

"That's your conclusion?"

"Yes."

"Your life is apparently falling apart, and you're worried about an examination?"

"My life is not falling apart."

"Your birth records are being investigated by an international dynasty."

"That doesn't excuse me from submitting my assignment."

Clara stared at her.

Then she laughed.

"You're unbelievable."

"Thank you."

"It wasn't a compliment."

"I'll accept it anyway."

That afternoon, Edward presented the laboratory's written explanation to Richard.

Sebastian was present, along with Catherine and Isabelle.

Elena had been invited as well.

She arrived carrying a notebook and a glass of water, looking as though she had expected a university lecture rather than a family meeting.

Nicholas came in behind her.

"Is this the meeting where someone finally explains everything?"

"No," Edward said.

"Then I have no interest."

"Sit down."

Nicholas sat.

Richard placed the document on the table.

"The laboratory has completed its initial analysis. However, the final report has been delayed because the original hospital registration number does not match the revised record."

Isabelle looked at the paper.

"Does that mean they don't know who we are?"

"Not exactly," Edward replied. "The genetic analysis and the historical records are separate matters. The laboratory can determine biological relationships from the samples. The issue is establishing which original birth registration belongs to which child."

Elena folded her hands.

"Could someone have entered the wrong number?"

"That is possible," Edward said.

"Or deliberately changed it?" Isabelle asked.

"That is also possible."

Catherine looked uncomfortable.

"Can the hospital explain the discrepancy?"

"Not yet. Their current archive contains the revised records. The original documentation is incomplete."

Richard's expression hardened.

"Then we will obtain the original records."

"From where?" Nicholas asked.

Edward looked at him.

"That's what I'm trying to establish."

Nicholas leaned back.

"Excellent. A simple question with a complicated answer."

Sebastian ignored him.

"What documents does the laboratory need?"

Edward listed them.

"The original birth registration ledger, the maternity ward admission record, the infant identification records, and any surviving transfer documentation. They also requested an independent confirmation of the historical registration number."

Richard nodded.

"I'll have our legal team contact the hospital again."

"There's another possibility," Edward added.

Everyone looked at him.

"The private records-management company that acquired the hospital's old backup systems may still possess an earlier database."

"Wasn't that company acquired by Aurelius Technologies?" Sebastian asked.

"Yes."

Silence settled over the room.

Elena glanced between them.

"Does that mean the Aurelius family has the records?"

"Not necessarily," Edward said. "Aurelius Technologies acquired the company, not every document personally. The archive may be held in a separate subsidiary. We need to establish whether the relevant backup survived."

Nicholas frowned.

"Why does everything lead back to Aurelius?"

No one answered immediately.

It was a reasonable question.

It was also one that none of them could answer.

Richard turned toward Elena and Isabelle.

"The investigation may take longer than expected. I want both of you to understand that the delay does not change your place in this household."

Elena looked at him.

He seemed sincere.

She wanted to believe him without reservation.

"All right," she said quietly.

Isabelle nodded too.

Catherine reached for both girls' hands.

"We will deal with this together."

For a moment, neither girl pulled away.

Across the country, at Aurelius Global Headquarters, Adrian Aurelius was reading a report that had arrived from the family archive department.

It contained a list of companies that had handled historical records for the family office.

One name had been highlighted.

**Meridian Records Management.**

The company had originally been contracted to maintain physical and digital archives for several large institutions, including hospitals, private medical centres and legal offices.

Aurelius Technologies had acquired its parent company eleven years earlier.

Adrian read the acquisition summary twice.

Then he called the head of corporate compliance.

"I need the complete historical inventory of Meridian's pre-acquisition archives."

"All of them?"

"Every archive related to medical institutions, legal offices and family identity records."

"That could take some time."

"Then begin."

The compliance officer hesitated.

"Is this connected to the missing-child investigation?"

"Yes."

"Understood."

Adrian ended the call and looked through the glass wall of his office.

The city stretched beneath the headquarters building.

For eighteen years, the Aurelius family had searched for a daughter who had disappeared before they could bring her home.

They had searched hospitals, government registries, adoption records and private institutions.

They had hired investigators in several countries.

They had followed false leads and paid for examinations that produced nothing.

And now, a trail of old documents had appeared.

It was the first promising development in years.

But Adrian was not prepared to celebrate.

Not yet.

He opened the original archive index again.

The reference EFO-SL/07 appeared beneath the authorisation record.

He enlarged the scanned page.

The initials were followed by a small handwritten annotation.

**External liaison — temporary authority.**

Adrian frowned.

Temporary authority should have expired when the file was closed.

Why had the archive retained it?

He requested the access history.

A few minutes later, a response arrived.

The original digital file had been accessed twice after its classification.

One access was recorded during a scheduled archive transfer.

The second had no corresponding employee identification.

It was listed simply as:

**Manual retrieval — authorised override.**

Adrian stared at the screen.

Someone had entered the archive without leaving a proper access record.

And the date was eighteen years old.

At the Aurelius Estate, Julian was having a disagreement with his mother.

"I think she should have a proper welcome."

Victoria looked up from her correspondence.

"Who?"

"My sister."

Victoria's expression softened.

"Julian, we don't know when we'll find her."

"But we will."

"I hope so."

"So why can't we prepare?"

Victoria set down her pen.

"What were you thinking?"

Julian took out his phone.

"I made a list."

She accepted it.

The document contained several headings.

**Things My Sister Might Like**

1. Books.
2. Games.
3. Snacks.
4. A tour of the estate.
5. A room with a balcony.
6. A personal library.
7. A swimming pool schedule.
8. A shopping trip.
9. A proper introduction to everyone.
10. Let her choose everything herself.

Victoria read the last item twice.

"That's thoughtful."

"I know."

"How do you know she likes books?"

"I don't. That's why it's on the list."

Victoria smiled faintly.

"And the shopping trip?"

"Lucien said girls like shopping."

"Your brother is not an authority on what your sister might enjoy."

"I thought so too."

Julian sat opposite her.

"Do you think she'll be nervous?"

Victoria looked down at the list.

She imagined a girl standing at the entrance of the estate, uncertain whether she belonged there.

She imagined her daughter looking at the enormous house and wondering why strangers were suddenly calling her family.

"Perhaps," Victoria said.

"Then I'll make sure she doesn't feel alone."

Victoria reached across the desk and touched his hand.

"That would mean a great deal."

Julian smiled.

"Can I still show her the games room?"

"Only if she wants to see it."

"Of course."

He paused.

"Do you think she'll be older than me?"

Victoria laughed softly.

"No, Julian. She'll be younger."

"Right."

He considered that.

"Then I can teach her things."

"Perhaps she will teach you something too."

Julian looked mildly offended.

"What could she possibly teach me?"

Victoria gave him a knowing look.

"Patience."

He stood.

"I don't need lessons in patience."

"That is exactly why you do."

The following morning, Edward received an unexpected message from Jonathan Pierce.

**I have located another name connected to the old file. It was not included in the original index. Please call me.**

Edward immediately dialled his number.

"Jonathan?"

"I've been reviewing the supplementary archive material," Pierce said. "There was a second set of correspondence attached to the file, but it was stored under a separate classification."

"Who was it addressed to?"

"That is the unusual part."

"Go on."

"It was addressed to the hospital's former administrator, Dr. Samuel Whitmore."

Edward straightened.

"Whitmore died eleven years ago."

"Yes. But the correspondence was not written by him."

"Who wrote it?"

Pierce paused.

"The sender was a woman named Eleanor Vale."

Edward frowned.

"Charles Vale's wife?"

"Yes."

Edward knew the name. Eleanor Vale had been a senior partner in the legal practice before retiring. She had died several years earlier.

"Was she involved in the original case?"

"According to the archive, she was not supposed to be."

"Then why was she writing to the hospital administrator?"

"That is what I would like to know."

Edward looked at the photograph lying beside his notebook.

The man in the corridor.

The circular emblem.

The missing original records.

The sealed letter.

And now a woman from the legal office whose name had never appeared in the official index.

"Do you have the correspondence?" he asked.

"Only a scanned copy. The original envelope is missing."

"Send it to me."

"I will."

The message arrived less than a minute later.

Edward opened the attachment.

It was a single-page letter dated four days after the two girls were born.

The handwriting was neat and deliberate.

The first paragraph contained no greeting.

The second contained a sentence that made Edward stop reading.

**The child who was recorded under the Aurelius name must not be returned through the usual channels.**

He read it again.

Then he continued.

**The original arrangement is no longer safe. Until I receive confirmation from the person who authorised the transfer, both records must remain separate. Do not inform the families.**

At the bottom was a signature.

**Eleanor Vale.**

Edward sat motionless.

The letter did not explain who had made the original arrangement.

It did not explain why the child could not be returned.

It did not explain what had become unsafe.

But it established something important.

Four days after Elena and Isabelle were born, someone had already known that the records were not being handled normally.

And that person had instructed the hospital not to inform either family.

Edward looked at the date again.

Then he noticed a handwritten addition in the lower margin.

A line that appeared to have been added later, in a different ink.

**If the mother asks, tell her the child was never found.**

Edward slowly lowered the page.

For eighteen years, the Aurelius family had been searching for their daughter.

And someone had apparently instructed the hospital to tell her mother that she had never been found.
