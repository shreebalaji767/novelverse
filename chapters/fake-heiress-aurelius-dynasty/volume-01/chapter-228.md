# **Chapter 228 — The Result That Could Not Make a Decision**

The laboratory's first update arrived on a Thursday.

It was not the result.

It was a confirmation that all three biological samples had passed the initial integrity checks.

Elena read the message while sitting at her desk in the university's project room.

**Sample A — Subject:** Identity and collection verified.

**Sample B — Reference participant:** Identity and collection verified.

**Sample C — Reference participant:** Identity and collection verified.

**Analysis status:** Processing.

She forwarded the message to Laura.

Then she returned to her work.

The six pilot applications had entered the preliminary review stage. Two applicants had uploaded incomplete receipts, one had submitted a duplicate supporting document, and another had misunderstood the difference between an application acknowledgement and an approval notice.

Elena spent the next hour helping Mr. Lim correct the workflow.

“Could we automatically mark an application complete once all files are uploaded?” he asked.

“No.”

“Even if every required field is filled?”

“Uploading a file doesn't prove that the file is valid.”

Mr. Lim nodded.

Elena opened the evidence panel.

“We can show that the document was received. We can show whether its format passed the initial check. But authentication and substantive verification are separate steps.”

Dr. Rebecca Ong, who had joined the review meeting, smiled.

“You've said that about twelve times.”

“Because people keep trying to combine them.”

“Then perhaps the system needs a warning.”

“It already has one.”

“Perhaps a larger warning.”

Elena laughed.

For a few minutes, she forgot about the laboratory.

At the Aurelius estate, the wait was less successful.

Julian had asked Adrian three times whether the laboratory had contacted them.

“No,” Adrian replied the third time.

“But they said the initial checks would take three days.”

“They said the initial checks could take three days.”

“That's almost the same thing.”

“It's not.”

Julian looked offended.

Lucien leaned against the doorway.

“Your brother has become very interested in laboratory administration.”

“I'm interested in the result.”

“We all are.”

Julian closed his notebook.

“Do you think she'll be happy?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Victoria looked toward the garden.

“We don't know what she wants.”

“That's not what I asked.”

“I know.”

She sat beside him.

“If the result is positive, she may feel happy, angry, frightened, confused, or all of those things. We cannot choose that for her.”

Julian looked down.

“I want her to be happy.”

“Then we must not make her responsible for making us happy.”

He considered that.

Then he opened his notebook and wrote:

**A positive result is not permission to demand affection.**

Victoria read the sentence.

She did not correct it.

Two days later, the laboratory requested clarification.

The reference samples had been correctly collected, but the examiner wanted confirmation that the analysis should compare the subject against both reference participants jointly and separately.

The authorisation already allowed both.

Laura confirmed the instruction.

Elena received a copy of the clarification request.

She read it carefully.

“Why would they need separate comparisons?” she asked during the call.

“Because the examiner will assess the genetic evidence against each reference participant individually and then evaluate the combined result,” Laura explained.

“Would the result tell me whether both are my biological parents?”

“It can provide strong evidence about biological parentage. The report will explain the statistical strength and limitations.”

“Will it identify anyone else?”

“No. The authorised comparison is limited to the three samples.”

“Good.”

Elena looked at the clock.

“Do you know when it will be ready?”

“The laboratory has not committed to a final date. They have indicated that the report may be available within several working days.”

Elena nodded.

“I'll wait.”

That evening, she received a call from Edward.

“There's been a development,” he said.

Elena straightened.

“What kind?”

“The laboratory has confirmed that the analysis is progressing. Separately, the records examiner has completed a review of the original authority documents.”

“Does that change anything?”

“It clarifies the historical record. The originating family's authority to request preservation and personal communication was valid. It did not establish the identity of the infant or authorise a receiving-family appointment.”

“So the old paperwork still doesn't prove who raised me.”

“Correct.”

“Does it prove that the Aurelius family knew about the hospital record?”

“It proves that they initiated a protected request connected to the historical identity material. It does not establish everything they knew at the time.”

Elena thought for a moment.

“Did they try to contact the child?”

“The F-02 process was intended to establish direct personal communication. The records show that delivery was never confirmed.”

“Why?”

“The proposed channel was not independently verified. The receiving institution declined to accept responsibility without confirmation of the recipient's identity.”

“Then the family might have been waiting for an answer that never came.”

“Yes.”

Elena looked down.

It was difficult to know what to feel.

She had spent years believing that the Valmont family had been the only family she had.

Now she was learning that another family might have been searching for a child they believed they had lost.

But the records still left too many questions.

“Edward?”

“Yes?”

“Please don't tell me what you think the result will be.”

“I won't.”

“And don't let anyone else tell me either.”

“I'll respect that.”

She ended the call.

The next morning, the Valmont Foundation review resumed.

Isabelle had prepared the updated conflict-of-interest procedure.

Elena examined it.

The new version required each reviewer to declare potential conflicts before accessing an application. It also separated the authority to approve exceptions from the authority to initiate payments.

“This is better,” Elena said.

Isabelle looked relieved.

“Anything else?”

“Yes. The exception approval needs a recorded reason and a separate confirmation that the person approving it has the correct authority.”

“I added that.”

“Good.”

Isabelle smiled.

“You sound almost pleased.”

“I am pleased. It's just not a reason to become careless.”

Richard, who had joined the meeting remotely, cleared his throat.

“Elena, we appreciate your work.”

“Thank you.”

“We would like to discuss whether you could continue advising the foundation after this review.”

Elena looked at the draft agreement.

“Not today.”

Richard nodded.

“Of course.”

“I agreed to a limited process review. If you want additional work, send a separate proposal. I'll evaluate it independently.”

“Understood.”

Catherine watched her daughter.

There was a time when Elena would have accepted a request simply because the Valmont family had made it.

Now she was asking for scope, authority, and written terms.

Catherine did not interrupt.

The meeting ended with a list of recommendations and no new commitments.

Elena left the room feeling more comfortable than she had expected.

She had not needed to reject the family to maintain her independence.

She only needed to keep the boundaries clear.

At 4:32 p.m., Laura called.

“The laboratory has completed the analysis.”

Elena stopped walking.

“Is the report final?”

“Yes. The examiner has signed it. I have received the authorised copy.”

“Can you tell me the result?”

“I can, but I would prefer to send you the report first and explain it with you. It is a significant document, and I don't want to reduce it to a single sentence.”

Elena looked for an empty room.

“Send it.”

A minute later, an encrypted document arrived.

The first page contained the laboratory's identification details.

The second described the testing method.

The third listed the verified sample identities.

The fourth contained the findings.

Elena read the conclusion.

Then she read it again.

**The genetic evidence strongly supports the conclusion that the subject is the biological child of Reference Participant B and Reference Participant C.**

Below it was the examiner's statistical assessment.

The combined result was consistent with a parent-child relationship between Elena and both reference participants.

The individual comparisons supported the same conclusion.

The report stated that the probability of the observed genetic findings under the tested parent-child hypothesis was overwhelmingly greater than under the unrelated-person hypothesis, given the assumptions and population data described in the report.

Elena's fingers tightened around the phone.

Reference Participant B.

Reference Participant C.

She scrolled to the authorised identity key.

**Reference Participant B: Alexander Aurelius.**

**Reference Participant C: Victoria Aurelius.**

She stopped.

The room was silent.

The result was not a surname in an old register.

It was not an incomplete hospital band.

It was not a provisional receiving reference.

It was not an administrative summary written years after the original event.

It was a biological comparison conducted under an independent protocol.

Laura's voice came through the phone.

“Elena?”

She did not answer immediately.

“Elena, can you hear me?”

“Yes.”

“Would you like me to explain the report?”

Elena swallowed.

“I'm reading it.”

“I understand.”

She looked at the conclusion again.

“It's positive.”

“Yes.”

“Both of them?”

“Yes. The combined analysis strongly supports biological parentage by both reference participants.”

Elena closed her eyes.

Alexander Aurelius.

Victoria Aurelius.

The people whose names appeared in the report were her biological parents.

The conclusion was finally supported by evidence.

But the result did not explain how she had grown up in another family.

It did not explain who had changed her identity.

It did not explain why the receiving record remained provisional.

And it did not tell her what to do next.

“Who has seen this report?” she asked.

“Only the authorised examiner, myself, and the restricted records officer responsible for the disclosure.”

“Have the Aurelius family seen it?”

“No.”

“Are they waiting?”

“Yes. They have been informed that the report is complete, but they have not received the result.”

Elena looked at the document.

“Don't send it to them yet.”

“I won't. The disclosure instruction requires your decision first.”

“Can I have a day?”

“Of course.”

“And can you send me the laboratory's full explanation of the statistical assessment?”

“Yes.”

“I want another independent specialist to review the report.”

“That can be arranged.”

Elena nodded.

“Thank you.”

The call ended.

She remained seated.

For the first time, she knew.

She was the biological daughter of Alexander and Victoria Aurelius.

But the discovery did not suddenly make her childhood disappear.

She was still the girl who had been raised at the Valmont estate.

Still the person who had been disowned after a DNA test.

Still the person who had moved into a modest apartment and learned to manage her own expenses.

Still the student who had come to Singapore to build a future independent of either family's wealth.

The report changed one part of her history.

It did not replace her entire life.

At the Aurelius estate, Alexander received a short message from Laura.

**The final report has been delivered to the subject. No result has been disclosed to the family. The subject has requested time and an independent technical review.**

Victoria read it over his shoulder.

“She knows?”

“Yes.”

“Does she want to meet us?”

“The message doesn't say.”

Victoria sat down.

For a moment, her composure faltered.

“After all these years, we're waiting for her to decide whether she wants to see us.”

Alexander placed the phone on the table.

“Yes.”

“It's difficult.”

“I know.”

Adrian stood beside the window.

“We should prepare for any answer.”

Lucien nodded.

“She may want to meet us. She may not.”

Damien added, “And if she does, it may not happen immediately.”

Julian was unusually quiet.

He opened his notebook.

He had reached the final page again.

Under the previous lines, he wrote:

**If she is our sister, we still have to earn the chance to know her.**

He closed it.

Elena returned to her apartment that evening.

She made tea.

She sat at the kitchen table.

The laboratory report remained open on her laptop.

She read the conclusion once more.

Then she opened a blank document.

At the top, she typed:

**What the result establishes.**

- Alexander Aurelius is my biological father.
- Victoria Aurelius is my biological mother.
- The result was obtained through an independent comparison.
- The tested samples passed identity and collection verification.
- The result does not establish how I came to be raised by the Valmont family.
- The result does not establish who arranged or authorised the receiving placement.
- The result does not create an obligation to meet, live with, or accept anyone.

She paused.

Then she opened a second document.

**Questions that remain.**

- Who arranged the original transfer?
- Why was the receiving identity left provisional?
- Who knew the identity had not been verified?
- Why did later summaries describe it as confirmed?
- Who was responsible for ensuring that the original family communication reached its intended recipient?
- Why had no one corrected the record for so many years?

She saved both documents.

The first described what she now knew.

The second described what she still needed to understand.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Laura appeared.

**The Aurelius family has requested permission to send you a personal message. They have not been given your contact details. You may accept, decline, or request that any message be delivered through me.**

Elena stared at the screen.

She had expected the question.

She had not expected it to feel so difficult.

She typed a reply.

**I will accept one written message through you. I am not agreeing to a meeting or direct contact at this stage.**

She sent it.

Then she turned off her laptop.

The result had answered the question of biological parentage.

The next question was whether the people who had lost her could meet the person she had become.

And Elena was not ready to answer that yet.
