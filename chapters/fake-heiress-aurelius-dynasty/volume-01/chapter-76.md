# **Chapter 76 — The Comparison That Never Reached Its Destination**

Edward had begun to dislike the word *comparison*.

It appeared everywhere.

In hospital registers. In transfer instructions. In correspondence between private legal offices. In documents that seemed to promise an answer but never actually provided one.

And now, after weeks of examining records from five different institutions, he had reached a particularly troubling possibility.

The comparison between St Catherine's Infant A and Isabella Vale's newborn daughter might never have been completed at all.

Or worse, someone might have completed it and hidden the result.

Edward sat at his desk, staring at the original transfer instruction recovered from Meridian Children's Medical Centre.

**Originating record: IV-204.**

**Cross-reference: STC-A.**

**Instruction: Do not treat cross-reference as proof of infant identity.**

Gabriel Vale's signature appeared beneath it.

Edward read the last line again.

"Why create a cross-reference if nobody intended to verify it?"

Jonathan, sitting across from him, turned a page in his notebook.

"Because they needed the records connected for administrative purposes."

"That explains the connection. It doesn't explain why the comparison was repeatedly postponed."

"Perhaps they didn't have the necessary documents."

"Perhaps."

Edward closed the folder.

"But someone requested the comparison. Someone prepared the transfer. Someone kept the original identification band. And someone made certain that Isabella was never told where her daughter went."

Jonathan leaned back.

"You're suggesting those were separate decisions."

"I'm suggesting we shouldn't assume they were made by the same person."

That distinction had become increasingly important.

Charles Vale had arranged transfers and controlled the legal paperwork. Gabriel had approved medical coordination and administrative payments. Eleanor had handled certain records but repeatedly refused to certify identities. Margaret Voss had maintained the beneficiary schedule without completing its verification.

And Adrian Holt had taken possession of the original identification band without informing the Aurelius family.

Every person had held a different piece of the same complicated arrangement.

None had yet explained the entire picture.

Edward opened a fresh notebook.

"Let's find the comparison request."

The St Catherine's archive was not located inside the hospital anymore.

The hospital had undergone two major administrative reorganisations since the births. Its maternity department had been renovated, several departments had been relocated, and older paper records had been transferred to a central archival facility.

The archive manager, Mr. Daniel Mercer, received Edward and Jonathan in a small consultation room.

He was a careful man in his late fifties, with reading glasses resting low on his nose.

"I understand you're investigating historical neonatal records," he said.

"Yes. Specifically, records from eighteen years ago involving Infant A, recorded at 2:17 a.m."

Mercer opened his laptop.

"Do you have a formal authorisation?"

Edward placed the legal documents on the table.

"We have authority to examine the records connected to the ongoing identity investigation. We are not requesting unrestricted access to unrelated patient information."

Mercer reviewed the papers before nodding.

"Very well. I should warn you that some of the original neonatal files were incomplete even before the archive transfer."

"That's precisely why we're here."

The first box arrived forty minutes later.

It contained the maternity admission register, neonatal observation sheets, temporary identification records and transfer documentation.

Edward began with the neonatal register.

The entry was familiar.

**Infant A.**

**Female.**

**2:17 a.m.**

**Temporary identification: STC-A.**

He compared it with the transfer document from Meridian.

The numbers matched.

But the handwriting did not.

"Different departments?" Jonathan asked.

"Probably. The neonatal entry was written by a hospital nurse. The cross-reference was added by someone in administration."

Edward examined the next page.

A transfer note had been attached to the original register.

**Receiving institution: External medical facility.**

**Transfer date: Sixth day after birth.**

**Transport representative: Gabriel Vale.**

**Receiving confirmation: Pending.**

There was another line, written in blue ink.

**Identity comparison requested before permanent registration.**

Edward looked up.

"Here."

Jonathan moved closer.

"Is that the original request?"

"No. It's an entry in the transfer register. But it establishes that the hospital knew a comparison was required."

Edward turned the page.

The next entry concerned the same infant.

**Comparison materials dispatched.**

The date was also the sixth day.

There was no receiving institution listed.

Instead, a reference appeared in the destination column.

**EFO-SL/07.**

Edward went still.

Jonathan noticed immediately.

"Same reference."

"Yes."

The reference that had appeared in the Aurelius Family Office archive, the Vale Private Counsel correspondence, and the Cross Administrative Services payment records.

Edward copied the entry.

Then he examined the following page.

The comparison request had been amended.

The original wording was still partially visible beneath a correction strip.

The amended entry read:

**Comparison deferred pending receipt of originating maternity materials.**

The date was the ninth day.

The signature was missing.

Edward studied the paper carefully.

"Can you tell whether this correction was made at the hospital?"

Mercer adjusted his glasses.

"The handwriting appears to be from our records department. The correction strip is unusual, though."

"Unusual how?"

"Corrections were normally initialled. This one isn't."

"Could the original writing beneath it be recovered?"

"Not by looking at it."

"Then we'll need a document examiner."

Mercer nodded.

"That would be appropriate."

Edward photographed the page.

For the first time that morning, the investigation seemed to be moving in a clear direction.

Then he found the next entry.

It was dated the fourteenth day.

**Comparison materials returned.**

There was no accompanying report.

No laboratory name.

No result.

Only a short notation:

**Not suitable for identity determination.**

Edward read it twice.

Jonathan looked over his shoulder.

"Does that mean the samples were inadequate?"

"Possibly."

"Or that the wrong samples were sent."

Edward didn't answer.

He was already looking at the next line.

**Original request retained by originating representative.**

No representative was named.

But the reference beside the entry was unmistakable.

**EFO-SL/07.**

By noon, they had established that St Catherine's had at least three separate records concerning the comparison.

The sixth-day request.

The ninth-day deferral.

The fourteenth-day return of materials.

Yet no final report existed in the hospital archive.

Edward requested the laboratory dispatch register.

Mercer returned with another file.

"This register is incomplete," he warned. "It records outgoing material and acknowledgements, but not necessarily the contents of every sealed package."

Edward opened it.

The sixth-day dispatch was listed under a temporary reference.

**SC-CMP/06.**

The destination was not a hospital or laboratory.

It was a private medical assessment office.

**Northbridge Clinical Review Unit.**

Jonathan frowned.

"Northbridge again."

Edward continued reading.

The dispatch had been authorised by a medical administrator named Dr. Peter Lang.

The receiving acknowledgement carried a signature.

**G. Vale.**

Gabriel.

"Was Gabriel authorised to receive medical comparison materials?" Jonathan asked.

Mercer checked the accompanying authority sheet.

"According to this document, he was authorised as an external administrative representative."

"Not as a doctor?"

"No."

Edward leaned back.

"So he could receive the package, but he couldn't certify a medical identity comparison."

"That would be correct."

Edward looked at the dispatch record again.

"Then why was it sent to him?"

Mercer had no answer.

The following entry was even more interesting.

**Ninth-day cancellation request received.**

The cancellation had come from Northbridge Clinical Review Unit.

It stated:

**Comparison not to proceed until all originating identity materials are reconciled.**

The authorising signature belonged to Dr. Peter Lang.

But underneath it, another name appeared.

**Charles Vale — Legal Representative.**

Jonathan exhaled slowly.

"Charles requested the cancellation."

"Or endorsed it," Edward replied.

"Is there a difference?"

"A considerable one. We need the original request, not an interpretation of a register."

He turned to Mercer.

"Do you have any correspondence attached to the cancellation?"

Mercer searched the archive index.

After several minutes, he found a reference.

**SC-CMP/06-CORR-2.**

The corresponding envelope had been stored separately.

Inside were two letters.

The first was dated the eighth day after the births.

It had been sent by Charles Vale to Dr. Peter Lang.

Edward read it silently.

Then he passed it to Jonathan.

The letter did not explicitly demand that the comparison be cancelled.

Instead, it stated that the identity records were undergoing administrative reconciliation and that premature comparison could create confusion between temporary receiving references.

It requested a short delay.

A handwritten sentence appeared at the bottom.

**No receiving family should be informed until the originating records are settled.**

Jonathan looked up.

"That's the same instruction we've seen before."

"Yes."

Edward examined the second letter.

It was dated the tenth day.

This one came from Northbridge Clinical Review Unit.

It confirmed that the comparison would remain suspended.

But it contained something the first letter did not.

**The requested reference materials were not all received in their original form.**

Edward's expression changed.

"Jonathan."

Jonathan read the sentence.

"Not all received?"

"That means someone had sent something."

"Or someone had sent copies instead of originals."

Edward examined the attached inventory.

There were three listed items.

1. St Catherine's Infant A temporary identification record.
2. Meridian IV-204 maternity identification sheet.
3. Original neonatal identification material — pending.

The third item had never been received.

The first two had been delivered.

But the original neonatal identification material was missing.

Edward stared at the inventory.

For eighteen years, the investigation had revolved around documents that connected two infants.

Now they had found a record showing that the one piece of evidence needed to test that connection had never arrived.

They returned to the office shortly after three.

Edward placed the documents in chronological order.

"Let's reconstruct what we know."

Jonathan took out his notebook.

"Sixth day. St Catherine's sends comparison materials to Northbridge Clinical Review Unit. Gabriel receives the package."

"Correct."

"Ninth day. Charles requests a delay, and Dr. Lang suspends the comparison."

"Correct."

"Tenth day. Northbridge records that the original neonatal identification material is missing."

"Correct."

"Fourteenth day. The materials are returned as unsuitable for identity determination."

Edward nodded.

Jonathan tapped the table.

"That means the comparison wasn't completed."

"Not the way it was originally intended."

"Could a preliminary comparison have been made?"

"Possibly. But there is no report confirming it."

Jonathan studied the papers.

"Then why did someone keep saying the child was not the Aurelius infant?"

Edward's gaze shifted to the separate instruction recovered from Northbridge.

**Receiving party to be informed that the child is not the Aurelius infant.**

"Because someone wanted that statement accepted without the comparison."

"Charles?"

"Perhaps."

"Gabriel?"

"Perhaps."

"Marianne?"

"She admitted she delivered the message. That doesn't mean she created it."

Jonathan nodded.

Edward picked up the original dispatch register.

"There is another problem."

"What?"

"The missing material."

He pointed to the third item.

**Original neonatal identification material — pending.**

"St Catherine's had the infant's original identification record. Meridian had Isabella's original band. Someone needed to reconcile them."

Jonathan considered it.

"Could the original neonatal identification material be the original band from St Catherine's?"

"That's what we need to establish."

"And if it was?"

"Then we need to know who held it after the sixth day."

Edward reached for his telephone.

There was only one person who might have known.

Adrian Holt.

Adrian Holt received Edward at a private office in the western district.

He had retired from the Aurelius Family Office eleven years earlier and now lived quietly, managing a small charitable trust and a collection of historical documents.

He was a thin man with neatly combed silver hair and a habit of listening without interrupting.

Edward placed the dispatch inventory before him.

Holt read it.

His expression remained neutral.

But his fingers stopped moving.

"You have found the comparison request," he said.

"Yes."

"And the cancellation."

"Yes."

Holt closed the folder.

"I wondered when someone would find it."

Edward watched him carefully.

"You knew the comparison had been suspended?"

"I knew it had not been completed."

"Did you know why?"

"At the time, I was told that the originating identity materials had not been reconciled."

"By whom?"

"Charles Vale."

"Did you accept that explanation?"

Holt paused.

"I accepted that the matter was complicated. I did not accept that the explanation was sufficient."

"Then why didn't you investigate?"

Holt looked at him.

"Because I was not the investigator. I was responsible for preserving certain records."

"That is not an answer."

"No," Holt agreed. "It isn't."

He opened a drawer and removed a thin, grey folder.

"I kept this separately."

Edward did not reach for it immediately.

"What is it?"

"An inventory of materials received by the Family Office under reference EFO-SL/07."

He placed it on the table.

"The original band you recovered was not the only item I received."

Edward opened the folder.

The first page listed the M-204/OR-1 envelope.

The second listed copies of Meridian's transfer records.

The third contained a receipt for the maternity identification sheet.

Then Edward found another entry.

**St Catherine's neonatal identification card — sealed envelope.**

Date received: the seventeenth day after birth.

Receiving officer: Adrian Holt.

Edward looked up.

"You had the original identification card?"

"Yes."

"Where is it?"

"In the family archive."

"Why wasn't it included in the earlier evidence release?"

Holt's expression became more serious.

"Because it was never part of the file I was instructed to release."

"Who instructed you to keep it?"

"Charles Vale requested that the materials remain sealed until a formal identity review could be conducted."

"Did Alexander Aurelius authorise that?"

"No."

"Did Victoria?"

"No."

Edward leaned forward.

"Then whose authority did you rely on?"

Holt was silent for several seconds.

Finally, he said, "My own."

Jonathan shifted in his chair.

Holt continued.

"I believed preserving the evidence was safer than allowing it to circulate through several private offices. I was concerned that someone might alter it or attach it to the wrong infant record."

"Yet you didn't inform the parents of the missing child."

"I believed I was protecting the evidence."

"You were also preventing them from knowing it existed."

Holt lowered his eyes.

"Yes."

Edward looked down at the inventory.

"Was the card ever examined?"

"Not by a laboratory."

"Then how was it identified as belonging to St Catherine's Infant A?"

"The envelope label and the hospital reference."

"Did you verify the contents?"

"I checked the number."

"Which number?"

"STC-A."

Edward felt a familiar tension settle behind his eyes.

"Did you open the envelope?"

"No."

"Then you cannot confirm that the card inside was the original identification card."

Holt's face tightened.

"I suppose not."

Edward closed the folder.

"Where is it now?"

"At the Aurelius Family Office archive. In the restricted evidence cabinet."

"Can we examine it?"

"I can arrange access."

"Today?"

Holt considered the request.

"Tomorrow morning."

Edward nodded.

"Then we'll be there."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria Aurelius stood in the doorway of the family library.

Alexander was reading a report when she entered.

"Edward has found something."

Alexander looked up.

"What?"

"An original St Catherine's identification card. Adrian Holt kept it in the archive."

Alexander slowly put down the report.

"Why was I never told?"

"Edward asked him the same question."

Victoria approached the desk.

"Apparently, Holt believed the materials needed to remain protected until the identity review was complete."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"An identity review that was never completed."

"Yes."

For a moment, neither spoke.

The old photograph on Victoria's desk showed five boys standing in the garden, years before the disappearance of their younger sister.

She had never removed it.

She had also never allowed herself to imagine what her daughter might look like now.

Not because she had forgotten.

Because imagination could become another kind of suffering when there was nothing to confirm it.

Alexander stood.

"Did Edward say whether the card could establish the child's identity?"

"No. He hasn't examined it."

"Good."

Victoria looked at him.

"Good?"

"We have spent eighteen years allowing incomplete records to dictate what we believe. I won't make another assumption because a document looks promising."

Victoria nodded slowly.

Then she picked up the report.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"The card was received on the seventeenth day. The comparison materials were returned on the fourteenth."

Alexander frowned.

"Three days later."

"Yes."

"Someone had the original material while the comparison was being suspended."

Victoria looked at him.

"And someone decided it should be kept."

Elena was unaware of any of this.

At the Valmont Estate, she was sitting at the dining table with three university application brochures spread around her.

Nicholas wandered in carrying a glass of water.

He stopped behind her chair.

"You're still looking at those?"

Elena didn't look up.

"Yes."

"You've been looking at them for two days."

"I've been comparing them for two days."

"That's worse."

She turned a page.

Nicholas leaned over her shoulder.

"Singapore International Academic Programme?"

"Yes."

"That's the one you mentioned last week?"

"Yes."

"How long is the programme?"

"Six months initially, with the possibility of extending it depending on the academic placement."

Nicholas pulled out the chair beside her.

"And you've already checked the accommodation?"

"Twice."

"Transport?"

"Yes."

"University facilities?"

"Yes."

"Food?"

Elena finally looked at him.

"Do you think I'm going to travel to another country without checking whether I can eat there?"

Nicholas considered that.

"Actually, I was going to ask whether you had checked the cost of living."

"I have."

"And the insurance?"

"Yes."

"And—"

"Would you like me to prepare a complete travel risk assessment for you?"

Nicholas grinned.

"That would be useful."

Elena threw a folded brochure at him.

He caught it.

For a few seconds, the dining room felt almost normal.

Then Nicholas noticed the separate sheet beside her notebook.

It contained a list of projected expenses.

Tuition.

Accommodation.

Food.

Transport.

Books.

Emergency savings.

He looked at the final column.

"You've calculated everything yourself?"

"Of course."

"You don't need to do that."

"I know."

"Then why?"

Elena placed her pen down.

"Because I want to know what I'm agreeing to before I agree to it."

Nicholas studied her.

She wasn't trying to prove anything.

She wasn't making a dramatic declaration of independence.

She simply wanted to understand the consequences of her own decisions.

It was a quality he had always admired in her.

He glanced at the application.

"Have you told Mother?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"I wanted to finish checking the programme first."

Nicholas nodded.

"I'll tell her you're considering it."

"No."

He raised an eyebrow.

"No?"

"I'll tell her myself."

He smiled faintly.

"All right."

Elena returned to her calculations.

Nicholas remained seated.

After a moment, he asked quietly, "Do you really want to go?"

She looked at the brochure.

"Yes."

"Even if things here are still complicated?"

Elena was silent for a few seconds.

"Perhaps especially because things are complicated."

Nicholas didn't know what to say.

She continued writing.

"I don't want every decision I make to depend on what happened eighteen years ago."

He looked at her.

Elena's expression remained calm, but her fingers had tightened around the pen.

"I still want to study. I still want to work. I still want to build something that belongs to me."

Nicholas nodded.

"Then you should."

She glanced at him.

"You're not going to give me a speech about staying?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because you'd throw another brochure at me."

Elena laughed.

And for a little while, the future seemed to belong to her again.

The following morning, Edward and Jonathan arrived at the Aurelius Family Office archive.

Helena Brooks had arranged access.

Adrian Holt was already waiting.

The restricted evidence cabinet stood inside a small climate-controlled room. Its contents were recorded in a digital register, and every item required two authorised signatures before removal.

Holt signed first.

Helena signed second.

Then the archivist brought out a sealed envelope.

The label read:

**ST CATHERINE'S HOSPITAL**

**NEONATAL IDENTIFICATION CARD**

**STC-A**

**EFO-SL/07**

Edward examined the seal.

It appeared intact.

He checked the receipt from the seventeenth day.

The seal number matched.

"Open it," he said.

The archivist photographed the envelope from every side before cutting the seal.

Inside was a small laminated identification card.

It carried the hospital's original printed details.

**Infant A**

**Female**

**2:17 a.m.**

**Temporary reference: STC-A**

But beneath the printed details was a handwritten notation.

Edward leaned closer.

It was not a name.

It was a second number.

**IV-204.**

Jonathan stopped breathing for a moment.

Holt stared at the card.

Edward examined the handwriting.

"Is this notation on the original register?"

"No," Helena replied. "I've never seen it."

Edward turned the card over.

A second handwritten line appeared on the reverse.

**Cross-reference entered for comparison only. No identity determination authorised.**

The initials beneath it were unfamiliar.

**P.L.**

Jonathan looked at Edward.

"Peter Lang."

Edward nodded.

The same medical administrator who had cancelled the comparison.

He carefully photographed both sides of the card.

Then he examined the lower edge.

Something was wrong.

The laminated surface showed a faint rectangular impression beneath the printed label, as though another identification strip had once been attached and later removed.

"Can you see that?" he asked.

The archivist brought over a magnifying lens.

"Yes."

"Could it have been an original label?"

"Possibly."

"Can you determine whether it was removed before or after lamination?"

The archivist studied it.

"Not without a more detailed examination."

Edward returned the card to its protective sleeve.

The discovery had created a new question, not answered the old one.

The number IV-204 had been deliberately written on the St Catherine's identification card.

But the notation itself explicitly stated that the cross-reference was for comparison only.

It did not prove that Infant A and Isabella's newborn daughter were the same child.

It proved that someone had considered the possibility.

And someone had made sure the original card was preserved.

Edward turned to Adrian Holt.

"Who gave you this envelope?"

Holt looked at the receipt.

"I don't remember the individual who delivered it."

"Was it Charles Vale?"

"No."

"Gabriel Vale?"

Holt hesitated.

"I believe it was a woman."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

Edward waited.

Holt looked again at the receipt.

Then his eyes moved to the final line.

The receiving officer had written a note beneath the envelope description.

**Delivered personally. Sender requested that the card remain sealed until the comparison could be independently verified.**

There was no sender's name.

Only an additional reference.

**M.C.**

Edward slowly straightened.

"Marianne Cross?"

Holt looked at him.

"That name is familiar."

"She was employed by Cross Administrative Services."

"Yes. I remember her."

"Did she deliver this envelope?"

Holt read the notation again.

"I cannot be certain."

Edward looked at the card.

The comparison had been suspended.

The original material had been missing.

Then, three days after the comparison materials were returned, an unidentified woman had delivered a sealed identification card to Adrian Holt.

The card carried the very cross-reference that the investigation had spent weeks trying to understand.

And it had remained unopened in the Aurelius archive for eighteen years.

Edward placed the receipt beside the card.

"Find Marianne Cross," he said to Jonathan.

Jonathan nodded.

But before he could move, Helena Brooks spoke.

"There's something else you should see."

She opened the archive's digital register and searched for the reference M.C.

A second entry appeared.

It was dated four days after the first envelope had been received.

**Additional evidence received.**

**Origin: Private maternity clinic.**

**Description: Original maternal identification material.**

**Reference: IV-204.**

Edward stared at the screen.

The original band was already in Adrian Holt's possession.

Now the archive showed that another item had arrived four days later.

He looked at Holt.

"Did you receive a second envelope?"

Holt's face had gone pale.

"I don't remember one."

Helena opened the attached receipt.

The receiving officer's signature was not Adrian Holt's.

It belonged to someone else.

**Victoria Aurelius.**

Edward looked at the date.

Then at Victoria's signature.

The missing comparison materials had not simply been held by an external representative.

At least one additional piece of evidence had been delivered directly to the Aurelius Family Office.

And Victoria had personally signed for it.
