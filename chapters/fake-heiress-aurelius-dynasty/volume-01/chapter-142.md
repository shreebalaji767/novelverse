# **Chapter 142 — The Appointment That Ended Before the Request**

The withdrawal had been recorded on day 132.

The collection request had arrived on day 133.

Edward wrote both dates on the whiteboard.

He drew a line beneath them.

“One day,” Jonathan said.

“One day is enough to change the meaning of the entire request.”

They were in the records room at the Valmont residence. The table was covered with copies of AV-18 correspondence, PR-06 documents and the recently recovered V-17 custody records.

Edward circled the reference.

**AV-18 / PRINCIPAL REPRESENTATION**

“We need the complete appointment history,” he said. “Not the summary. Every nomination, temporary designation, withdrawal and acknowledgement.”

Jonathan opened his laptop.

“The AV-18 file is held across three archives. The original correspondence is at Meridian, the administrative index is at Vale Private Counsel's successor archive, and the custody record is at V-17.”

“Then we compare all three.”

“There's another issue.”

Edward looked at him.

“The day-132 withdrawal was entered in the administrative index, but the person whose appointment was withdrawn may not have received notification.”

“Is there a delivery record?”

“Not yet.”

Edward looked again at the dates.

“If they weren't notified, they might still have believed they were authorised.”

“Which would explain the day-133 request.”

“Or someone else could have submitted it using the old appointment.”

Jonathan nodded.

“Either way, we need to know who was nominated.”

Edward closed the folder.

“And who withdrew them.”

The successor archive of Vale Private Counsel had retained a separate AV-18 appointment register.

Rachel Morton, the archive manager, brought it into the consultation room with a set of access restrictions.

“This register contains proposed personal representation arrangements,” she explained. “It does not establish that any appointment became effective.”

Edward turned to the relevant section.

The first entry was dated day 34.

**Principal: P-04**

**Proposed personal representation custodian: Pending**

**Temporary document custodian: Charles Vale**

**Communication representative: Gabriel Vale**

**Independent verification: Outstanding**

The next entries were more complicated.

Day 39: a proposed representative had been considered.

Day 43: the nomination was placed on hold.

Day 47: the administrative summary recorded the representative as available for continuity.

Day 92: the arrangement remained under temporary review.

Day 106: a request for independent review was received.

Day 118: the arrangement was continued for administrative purposes.

Then came day 132.

**PERSONAL REPRESENTATION DESIGNATION — WITHDRAWN.**

Edward looked at the entry.

“Where is the name?”

Rachel turned the page.

“It is in the restricted nomination schedule.”

“Can we access it?”

“Not through this register. The nomination schedule was filed separately because the proposed representative had not consented to disclosure of their personal details.”

“Who authorised the withdrawal?”

Rachel examined the entry.

“The authorisation reference is AV-18/PR-132.”

“Who signed it?”

“No signature appears in the index.”

“Was it submitted by Charles Vale?”

“The routing field says V-17 / Personal Custody. It does not identify an individual.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Then how was the withdrawal accepted?”

Rachel looked uncomfortable.

“It was accepted for administrative recording only.”

“Was the appointment actually withdrawn?”

“The register says withdrawn.”

“That wasn't my question.”

She studied the document again.

“The register does not contain the underlying authority. I cannot tell you whether the withdrawal was legally effective.”

Jonathan wrote that down.

Edward turned to the next page.

A second entry appeared beneath the withdrawal.

**Collection authority associated with proposed representative: Suspended.**

The date was day 132.

There was no indication that the suspension had been communicated to the proposed representative.

Edward pointed to it.

“Was the collection authority separate from the representation appointment?”

“Yes. The proposed representative could not collect protected material merely because they were being considered for personal representation.”

“So even before the withdrawal, the person needed a separate collection authorisation.”

“Correct.”

“And after the withdrawal?”

“The collection authority would also have to be suspended or withdrawn.”

“Yet the day-133 request was submitted.”

Rachel nodded.

“The request appears to have relied on the previous designation.”

Edward sat back.

Someone had attempted to use an old appointment after its status had changed.

But the register still did not reveal whether the person submitting the request was the proposed representative, someone acting on their behalf, or someone attempting to use their name.

“Can we see the nomination schedule?”

Rachel hesitated.

“The schedule is restricted under the original privacy instruction.”

“What authority would permit access?”

“An order from the relevant records authority, or a joint instruction from the originating representative and the independently appointed receiving identity authority.”

Edward almost smiled.

The same circular requirement again.

“And no receiving identity authority was ever appointed.”

“Not according to the records available here.”

“Then we need another route.”

Rachel closed the register.

“There may be one.”

She removed a smaller folder from the archive box.

“This is the correspondence index. It contains references to the nomination, not the identity itself.”

Edward opened it.

The first item was a day-39 request for a proposed personal representation custodian.

The second was a day-43 communication to the proposed representative.

The third was a day-132 withdrawal notice.

The fourth was the day-133 collection request.

Edward stopped.

“There's no acknowledgement for the day-43 communication.”

“No.”

“Was it delivered?”

“The dispatch field says sent through protected correspondence.”

“Recipient?”

“Restricted.”

“Delivery confirmation?”

“Not retained.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The proposed representative might have received the original appointment request.

They might have declined.

They might have accepted conditionally.

Or they might never have known they had been considered.

The records did not answer any of those questions.

Then Jonathan noticed something.

“Edward.”

He pointed to the fourth entry.

The day-133 collection request had a cross-reference not visible in the main register.

**PR-06 / CONTROL COPY — PRIOR CUSTODY CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.**

Edward read it carefully.

“Prior custody confirmation?”

Rachel nodded.

“That would have been necessary if the proposed representative had already received or held a related document.”

“Did the proposed representative ever receive anything?”

“The correspondence index doesn't say.”

Edward closed the folder.

“Then find the custody confirmation.”

At Meridian, Dr Mira Sen had completed the handwriting comparison.

She presented her findings in a small examination room.

“The day-48 instruction and the day-118 instruction are consistent with the same writer,” she said. “The day-133 restriction is also consistent with that hand.”

Edward placed the three documents beside one another.

“Can you attribute them?”

“Not conclusively.”

“Could the writer be the person who submitted the collection request?”

“No. The request itself is typed. The restriction was added afterwards. There is no handwriting on the original request that would allow a direct comparison.”

She placed another document on the table.

“This is the day-132 withdrawal notice.”

Edward looked at it.

The final sentence had been handwritten.

*The proposed representative's authority must not be treated as continuing beyond this date.*

He read it twice.

“Same writer?”

Dr Sen compared the letters.

“The formation is similar. The spacing is similar. But the paper is different, and the writing pressure is lighter.”

“Your conclusion?”

“Consistent with the same writer. Not sufficient for individual attribution.”

Edward looked at the date.

Day 132.

One day before the collection request.

“Was this instruction part of the withdrawal?”

“It appears to have been attached to the withdrawal notice before it entered the archive.”

“Does it identify who requested the withdrawal?”

“No.”

Dr Sen turned the page.

“There is another detail.”

A small box at the bottom contained a routing instruction.

**Notification to proposed representative: Hold pending identity verification.**

Edward frowned.

“Why would the withdrawal be held from the person whose appointment was being withdrawn?”

“Possibly because the person had not been independently identified.”

“But the appointment had been recorded.”

“An administrative record can contain a proposed name without establishing that the person was correctly identified.”

Edward looked at the page.

The same distinction had appeared throughout the investigation.

A name could be written.

A role could be assigned.

A document could be received.

None of those actions proved that the correct person had been identified or authorised.

“Could the proposed representative have been known to the person who wrote this?”

“Possibly.”

“Could the writer have been protecting them?”

Dr Sen considered the question.

“Or protecting the principal from an unverified representative. The wording does not tell us which.”

Edward took a photograph of the page.

“Thank you.”

As he reached the door, Dr Sen spoke again.

“One more thing. The day-132 notice has an impression from another sheet.”

Edward turned.

“What does it say?”

She placed the page beneath the examination light.

Three words gradually became visible.

**RETURN ORIGINAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT.**

Edward stepped closer.

“What acknowledgement?”

“I can't tell yet.”

“Could it be the principal's personal acknowledgement?”

“It could be. It could also be the representative's acknowledgement of the proposed appointment.”

Edward stared at the impression.

If the original acknowledgement had been returned on day 132, then someone might have known the proposed representative had never completed the required process.

And if that acknowledgement belonged to P-04, the question became even more serious.

Had the principal ever been given a chance to respond?

Or had the original response been withheld before it could reach the person it concerned?

At the Valmont estate, Isabelle was reviewing a draft foundation report when Elena called.

The video connected after two attempts.

“You look tired,” Isabelle said.

“I am tired.”

“University?”

“Prototype testing.”

Isabelle smiled.

“Your computer is still refusing to cooperate?”

“It has become more reasonable. Unfortunately, that means I have to find more complicated problems.”

“What happened?”

Elena explained the reviewer-assignment issue and how she had changed the system to keep unassigned applications separate from rejected ones.

Isabelle listened carefully.

“That sounds useful for the foundation.”

“It might be, eventually. But the system needs more testing before anyone uses it for real applications.”

“I wasn't asking you to launch it.”

“I know.”

“I was asking whether I could review the prototype when you're ready.”

Elena leaned back.

“You really want to?”

“Yes. Our current process has a problem with incomplete applications. Sometimes the applicant doesn't know whether a document is missing, under review, or simply waiting for someone to check it.”

“That is exactly the kind of problem I'm trying to address.”

“Then perhaps we can test it with old, anonymised applications.”

Elena considered the suggestion.

“That could work. But I'd want the testing criteria agreed beforehand.”

“Of course.”

“And no one should change the results just because they dislike them.”

Isabelle laughed softly.

“You sound like a very strict software developer.”

“I am trying to become one.”

“Fair enough.”

There was a brief silence.

Then Isabelle asked, “How is Singapore?”

“Busy. Good, though.”

“Are you settling in?”

“Mostly. Clara has been helping me explore the campus. I still get lost in some buildings.”

“That's normal.”

Elena glanced at the time.

“I should finish my testing report.”

“Then I won't keep you.”

“Send me the foundation documents when you're ready. I'll look at the workflow, not the final decisions.”

“I will.”

They ended the call.

Elena returned to her laptop.

She had not expected Isabelle to ask for help.

A few months earlier, the idea of working together would have seemed impossible.

Now it was becoming something they could discuss without pretending the past had never happened.

It wasn't a perfect relationship.

But it was no longer standing still.

Edward returned to the Valmont records room after dinner.

Jonathan had located the custody confirmation.

It was not in the main archive.

It had been filed under a separate reference:

**AV-18 / PERSONAL REPRESENTATION — ACKNOWLEDGEMENT RETURN**

The document was dated day 132.

Edward opened the scanned copy.

It contained two sections.

**SECTION A — REPRESENTATIVE ACKNOWLEDGEMENT**

The proposed representative was required to confirm that they understood the limits of the role, including the fact that they could not make personal decisions on behalf of P-04 without separate authority.

The signature field was blank.

**SECTION B — PRINCIPAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT**

The principal was required to confirm that the original procedure and relevant information had been received.

That signature field was also blank.

At the bottom, a handwritten statement appeared.

*Neither acknowledgement is to be inferred from the existence of a proposed appointment.*

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“That's the same principle again.”

“Yes.”

“Who wrote it?”

“Dr Sen believes the handwriting is consistent with the day-48 and day-118 restrictions.”

Edward looked back at the page.

“Then the person who kept blocking the collection may also have been responsible for preserving the acknowledgement requirements.”

Jonathan nodded.

“But there's a problem.”

“What?”

“The custody confirmation has a receipt number. It was physically returned to V-17 on day 132.”

“By whom?”

“The return field is blank.”

“Was it delivered with the withdrawal notice?”

“No. The two documents were received separately.”

Edward examined the bottom corner.

A faint administrative stamp appeared beneath the handwriting.

**RECEIVED — PERSONAL CUSTODY**

**DAY 132 — 16:40**

There was no recipient's signature.

He looked at the other papers.

The withdrawal notice had been entered earlier that day.

The acknowledgement form had arrived later.

Then, the following day, someone had attempted to collect the control copy and sealed instruction.

Edward slowly arranged the documents in chronological order.

Day 132.

- The proposed representation designation was withdrawn.
- The collection authority was suspended.
- The acknowledgement form was returned with both sections blank.
- The original procedure remained unreleased.

Day 133.

- An unidentified person requested the control copy.
- The same request sought the sealed personal instruction.
- The collector's identity was not verified.
- The request was refused.

He looked at Jonathan.

“Someone knew the proposed representative was no longer authorised.”

“Or someone knew the appointment had never been completed.”

“And still submitted the collection request.”

“Yes.”

Edward turned to the final page.

A short line had been added beneath the receipt stamp.

It was not a signature.

It was a routing instruction.

**Forward to the originating representative. Do not return to the proposed collector.**

Edward read it again.

“Who was the originating representative on day 132?”

Jonathan checked the authority register.

“Victoria Aurelius and Isabella Vale were listed as originating representatives for their respective maternity records. But this instruction doesn't specify which one.”

“Was it sent to either of them?”

“The dispatch record is incomplete.”

Edward looked at the two names.

For the first time, the investigation had produced a document that might have been intended to reach one of the two women whose maternity records remained unresolved.

But it did not say which woman.

And there was no proof either had received it.

He picked up the page.

At the bottom, beneath the routing instruction, was one final reference.

**ORIGINAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT — HELD UNDER SEPARATE CUSTODY.**

The reference number was unfamiliar.

**PA-132/ORIGINAL.**

Edward stared at it.

The acknowledgement had not been destroyed.

It had not been filed with the withdrawal.

And it had not been returned to the proposed representative.

It had been placed somewhere else.

Somewhere under separate custody.

“Find PA-132,” he told Jonathan.

Jonathan was already searching.

But the first result did not identify a person or a location.

It identified a storage category.

**PERSONAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT — ORIGINAL DOCUMENTS ONLY.**

Beneath it was a status line.

**ACCESS RESTRICTED — ORIGINAL PRINCIPAL NOT YET IDENTIFIED.**

Edward slowly closed the file.

The proposed representative's appointment had ended before the collection request.

The principal's acknowledgement had been returned separately.

And the original procedure still had not been delivered.

The next question was no longer simply who had tried to collect the documents.

It was what the original acknowledgement contained—and why someone had kept it apart from every other record.
