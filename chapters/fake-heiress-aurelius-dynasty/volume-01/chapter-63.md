# **Chapter 63 — The Bracelet That Belonged to Another Child**

The first thing Edward did the following morning was request a meeting with the former legal representative who had sent him Isabella's letter.

The second thing he did was ask for the original photograph.

He received neither immediately.

Instead, he received a telephone call.

"Mr. Valmont?"

"Yes."

"This is Andrew Bell. I believe you requested information concerning Isabella Vale's personal archive."

"I did. I need the original photograph mentioned in her correspondence with Eleanor."

There was a pause.

"That may be difficult."

"Why?"

"Because the photograph is not in the archive anymore."

Edward straightened.

"Where is it?"

"I don't know."

"Who removed it?"

"Isabella herself, apparently. She collected several personal belongings from the Vale office shortly before leaving."

"When?"

"About seventeen years ago."

Edward looked at Jonathan, who had just entered the office.

"Do you have a record of what she collected?"

"Yes. A receipt."

"Send it to me."

"I can send you a copy. But there is something else."

"What?"

"Isabella left a written instruction. She specifically requested that her maternity records not be destroyed, transferred, or altered without her personal approval."

Edward waited.

"Mr. Bell, did she ever return to collect them?"

"No."

"Did she ask anyone to send them to her?"

"Once. Approximately eleven years ago."

"Was the request fulfilled?"

"No. Charles Vale's office rejected it."

Edward ended the call after thanking him.

Jonathan had already taken a seat.

"Another blocked request?"

"Yes."

"At least we're beginning to understand the pattern."

Edward placed Isabella's letter on the desk.

"Not yet. We still don't know whether the child in her photograph was Infant A."

Jonathan glanced at the letter.

"And if it wasn't?"

"Then someone deliberately encouraged a mother to believe that a stranger's child might be hers."

Neither man spoke for a moment.

It was an unpleasant possibility.

But it was also consistent with almost everything they had discovered.

The receipt arrived before noon.

It listed five items collected by Isabella Vale:

1. Personal correspondence.
2. Medical admission summary.
3. Original maternity bracelet.
4. One infant photograph.
5. Unidentified hospital document.

The fifth item had been described in handwritten remarks.

**ORIGINAL IDENTIFICATION RECORD — NOT TO BE COPIED.**

Edward read it twice.

"That's interesting."

Jonathan leaned over.

"Why?"

"Because the bracelet and photograph may have been deliberately kept together, but the original identification record was treated separately."

"Could it identify the infant?"

"That's what we need to find out."

Edward called Andrew Bell again.

This time, he asked about the unidentified hospital document.

Bell searched his records.

"I have a note here."

"What does it say?"

"Isabella requested that the document be kept in a sealed envelope. She said it was the only remaining record connecting her maternity admission to the infant photograph."

"Was it sealed?"

"Yes."

"Where is it now?"

"According to the receipt, it was collected with the other items."

"By Isabella?"

"Yes."

Edward ended the call.

Jonathan looked at him.

"Then we need to find her."

"Yes."

"And if she refuses to cooperate?"

Edward gathered the documents.

"Then we ask her what she is willing to tell us. We don't have the right to demand that she relive eighteen years of uncertainty simply because we need another piece of evidence."

Jonathan nodded.

"Understood."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was having breakfast when Adrian entered with a printed report.

"Edward has found another maternity record."

Victoria put down her cup.

"Whose?"

"Isabella Vale's."

"Isabella?"

"She gave birth to a girl eighteen years ago."

Victoria remained still.

"Where?"

"At a separate private clinic. Her delivery was recorded at 2:31 a.m."

Alexander, seated at the other end of the table, looked up.

"That's close to the time on our original record."

"Yes," Adrian said. "But it isn't the same time."

Damien entered, carrying his tablet.

"There's more."

He placed it beside Adrian's report.

"Isabella was later told that her daughter had died. There is no medical death certificate, no neonatal death record, and no post-mortem report."

Victoria stared at the documents.

"Who told her?"

"An external legal representative. We haven't identified the person yet."

Alexander read the summary.

"Was the child transferred?"

"The medical records say temporary transfer. The receiving party isn't named."

Victoria looked towards Adrian.

"Could this be connected to our daughter?"

"Possibly. But we have no proof."

"Could Isabella's child have been one of the infants at St Catherine's?"

"Possibly."

Victoria closed her eyes briefly.

Two maternity facilities.

Two different recorded delivery times.

Two children whose identities had been obscured.

And a woman who had spent eighteen years believing that her daughter might still be alive.

"Find her," Victoria said.

Adrian nodded.

"But carefully."

Victoria looked at him.

"She has already lost enough time."

Elena was in the library when Isabelle found her.

A laptop was open on the table, surrounded by application documents and handwritten notes.

Isabelle glanced at the screen.

"Still working on Singapore?"

"Trying to."

"Trying?"

Elena leaned back.

"The financial declaration has three sections that seem to ask the same question in slightly different ways."

Isabelle pulled out a chair.

"Let me see."

Elena turned the laptop towards her.

Isabelle read the form.

"These aren't exactly the same."

"They look the same."

"One asks about the applicant's personal resources. Another asks about the household contribution. The third asks about external sponsorship."

Elena stared at her.

"How did you notice that so quickly?"

"I've been reading scholarship applications for two weeks."

"That's becoming a dangerous skill."

Isabelle smiled.

"Would you prefer that I deliberately misunderstand them?"

"Yes. It would make me feel better."

Isabelle laughed.

They worked through the remaining questions together.

After a while, Isabelle noticed that Elena had written a separate note beside the final section.

**DO NOT DECLARE FAMILY SUPPORT AS PERSONAL INCOME.**

"You're being very careful."

"I don't want my application rejected because I misunderstood something."

"Fair."

Elena saved the document.

Then she looked at Isabelle.

"Have you decided what you want to study?"

"Economics, probably. But I want to work with the foundation first."

"Wouldn't studying economics help?"

"It would. But I don't want to choose a degree just because everyone says it suits me."

Elena nodded.

"That's reasonable."

Isabelle glanced at her.

"You're not going to tell me that I should decide quickly?"

"No. I think people make enough decisions for us already."

For a moment, Isabelle looked thoughtful.

Then she smiled.

"You're becoming quite good at saying things like that."

"Thank you. I practise in front of the mirror."

"Of course you do."

Elena closed her laptop.

They left the library together.

Neither girl knew that, elsewhere in the city, an investigation was beginning to connect another missing child to the same eighteen-year-old mystery.

Edward found Isabella's current address through an old professional registration.

It was not a family residence.

It was a small apartment in a quiet district, registered under her own name.

He sent a letter requesting a meeting.

He did not mention Infant A.

He did not mention the Aurelius family.

He simply stated that he was investigating historical maternity records involving Vale Private Counsel and wanted to discuss documents relating to her admission eighteen years earlier.

He expected no immediate response.

Instead, the reply arrived that evening.

It contained only one sentence.

*I will meet you, but I will not allow another person to tell me which child I am supposed to have lost.*

Edward read it twice.

Then he replied.

*That is not what I am asking you to do.*

Isabella chose the meeting place herself.

It was a small private room in a public library, away from the legal offices and the old hospital buildings.

Edward arrived ten minutes early.

Jonathan came with him but remained outside the room until Isabella agreed to his presence.

At precisely three o'clock, the door opened.

Isabella Vale entered carrying a brown leather folder.

She was in her early fifties, with neatly arranged dark hair and a composed expression.

Her eyes moved briefly across the room before settling on Edward.

"Mr. Valmont?"

"Yes. Thank you for agreeing to meet."

"I haven't agreed to tell you everything."

"I understand."

She placed the folder on the table but kept one hand on it.

"Before we begin, I want to make something clear."

Edward waited.

"I know about the Aurelius investigation."

He was surprised.

"How?"

"Because someone contacted me several weeks ago."

"Who?"

"A man who introduced himself as a representative of the Aurelius family."

"Did you meet him?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because he asked whether I could identify a missing Aurelius daughter."

Edward remained silent.

Isabella continued.

"I have spent eighteen years trying to find my own daughter. I am not interested in helping another wealthy family locate a child while everyone continues pretending that my situation is unrelated."

Edward nodded slowly.

"That's fair."

She seemed surprised by his response.

"You're not going to tell me that the two matters are separate?"

"I don't know whether they are."

"At least you're honest."

Edward opened his notebook.

"I found your maternity records."

Isabella's hand tightened around the folder.

"Where?"

"Vale Private Counsel's archive."

"And?"

"They confirm that you gave birth to a girl at 2:31 a.m."

Isabella looked down.

"Yes."

"The infant was transferred under a temporary medical arrangement."

"I know."

"Do you know where she was taken?"

"No."

"Do you know why you were told she had died?"

"No."

Edward paused.

"Did you ever receive a death certificate?"

"No."

"A hospital report?"

"No."

"Did anyone show you medical evidence?"

"No."

Her voice remained controlled, but the words became shorter.

"They gave me a letter. It said there had been complications. It said the child had not survived."

"Do you still have it?"

"Yes."

"Would you be willing to let us examine it?"

Isabella looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because the person who issued it may have had no medical authority to do so."

She slowly opened the folder.

Inside were several documents.

One was a faded hospital bracelet.

Another was a small photograph.

Edward recognised the photograph immediately.

It showed the newborn infant wrapped in a white blanket.

On the back was the handwritten notation:

**INFANT A — 2:17 A.M.**

Isabella placed it on the table.

"This is the photograph Eleanor gave me."

Edward studied it.

"She told you this might be your daughter?"

"Yes."

"Did she explain why?"

"She said there had been a mistake involving two newborn children. She said the records were unreliable and that she couldn't confirm which child belonged to which mother."

"Did she tell you the infant's original birth time?"

"She said the photograph had been taken from a hospital file. She didn't know whether the notation was genuine."

Edward looked at the picture again.

"Did she give you any other photograph?"

"No."

"Any identification record?"

Isabella hesitated.

Then she opened the folder further and removed a sealed envelope.

"This is the document I mentioned in my letter."

Edward looked at the envelope.

It had never been opened.

The seal was intact.

On the front, someone had written:

**MATERNAL IDENTIFICATION — ISABELLA VALE**

Edward did not touch it.

"May I examine it?"

"Not yet."

She held the envelope between her hands.

"I want to know something first."

"Ask."

"Have you found the child in this photograph?"

Edward looked at her.

"No."

"Do you know whether she is alive?"

"No."

"Do you know whether she was raised by another family?"

"We have evidence of several temporary placements, but we cannot establish that this photograph shows the child who was placed with any particular guardian."

Isabella stared at him.

"So you have eighteen years of records, and you still don't know."

"That's correct."

She looked away.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Then Isabella pushed the envelope towards him.

"Examine it."

Edward accepted it carefully.

The envelope was opened under controlled conditions later that afternoon.

Inside was a single original document.

It was a maternity identification sheet from Isabella's clinic.

The infant's details were recorded clearly.

**MOTHER: ISABELLA VALE**

**DELIVERY TIME: 2:31 A.M.**

**INFANT: FEMALE**

**IDENTIFICATION NUMBER: IV-204**

Below it was a section for the receiving medical facility.

The destination had been left blank.

But there was an additional handwritten reference.

**CROSS-REFERENCE: ST. CATHERINE'S — INFANT A.**

Edward read the notation again.

Jonathan, who had joined them after Isabella's permission, leaned closer.

"That's the same hospital."

"Yes."

Edward examined the bottom of the page.

There was a signature.

**C. VALE.**

Jonathan looked at him.

"Charles?"

Edward nodded.

The document had been signed on the day Isabella gave birth.

But the transfer date was different.

It had been entered two days later.

And beside the transfer authorisation was another reference:

**EFO-SL/07.**

Edward looked at Isabella.

"Did you know this document existed?"

"No."

"Did you authorise the transfer?"

"I signed a temporary medical consent. I never authorised my child to be given to another family."

Edward carefully returned the document to its protective sleeve.

"Then this may be one of the missing links between your maternity admission and the two infants at St Catherine's."

Isabella's face had gone pale.

"Are you saying my daughter was taken to St Catherine's?"

"I'm saying the document records a cross-reference to Infant A. It does not yet prove that the child in the photograph was yours."

Isabella looked at the picture.

"Then what does it prove?"

Edward answered honestly.

"That someone connected your maternity record to a child recorded at another hospital."

That evening, Edward brought the new evidence to the Aurelius Estate.

The family gathered in the private conference room.

Alexander examined the document.

Victoria read the maternity identification sheet.

Adrian checked the reference numbers.

Damien compared the dates with the old hospital movements.

Lucien, unusually quiet, studied the photograph.

Finally, Victoria spoke.

"Isabella's child may have been transferred to St Catherine's."

Edward nodded.

"Yes."

"Then there may have been three newborns involved."

"At least three separate maternity records," Edward corrected. "We cannot yet establish how many children were actually moved between the facilities."

Alexander placed the document down.

"Who authorised the cross-reference?"

"Charles Vale."

"And the external reference?"

"EFO-SL/07."

Adrian looked at the date.

"Two days after the birth."

Damien pointed to the transfer section.

"Someone connected Isabella's child to Infant A. But the photograph was later labelled with Infant A's 2:17 a.m. birth time."

Victoria looked at the two records.

"Isabella's daughter was born at 2:31."

"Yes," Edward said.

"Fourteen minutes apart."

"Yes."

Victoria looked towards Alexander.

"If the records were deliberately altered, those fourteen minutes may be the only distinction left between them."

Alexander looked at the photograph.

"Or the notation may never have been genuine."

Nobody disputed him.

The photograph could not identify the child.

The maternity record could not establish the final destination.

And the signature of Charles Vale connected both documents without explaining why.

Late that night, Edward returned to his office.

He had one final task.

He requested the original admission and transfer register from Isabella's maternity clinic.

The register arrived as a scanned historical record.

He searched for the entry corresponding to 2:31 a.m.

The delivery was recorded.

The infant was recorded.

The temporary medical transfer was recorded.

But there was something beneath the entry that had not appeared in the earlier copy.

A correction.

It was dated three days after the birth.

The original destination had been crossed out.

A new destination had been written in its place.

**ST. CATHERINE'S PRIVATE NURSERY — INFANT A.**

Beneath it was a handwritten instruction.

*Do not merge the maternal identity with the receiving infant record. The two records must remain separate until the originating representative confirms the child.*

Edward stared at the screen.

Someone had deliberately connected Isabella's daughter to Infant A.

But the instruction also warned that the two identities must not be merged.

He enlarged the final line.

There was a second signature.

Not Charles Vale's.

Not Gabriel Vale's.

Not Margaret Voss's.

It belonged to a woman whose name Edward had never seen before.

**ELEANOR VALE.**

And beside her signature was a handwritten date.

The twelfth day after the births.

Edward leaned back.

Eleanor had not merely discovered the confusion between the children.

She had personally authorised the separation of Isabella's maternal identity from Infant A's receiving record.

Which meant that, twelve days after the births, Eleanor knew something the families did not.

She knew that the child Isabella had given birth to and the child recorded as Infant A could not yet be treated as the same person.

And she had made certain that the records would never be combined.
