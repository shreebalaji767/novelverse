# **Chapter 82 — The Custodian of Two Names**

Margaret Voss had been given a document that was never supposed to be circulated.

That was the first thing Edward wrote in his notebook.

The second was more troubling.

**Annex C contained the maternal and receiving identities of two mothers.**

Victoria Aurelius.

Isabella Vale.

He drew a line beneath both names.

The investigation had spent weeks examining separate records, individual transfers and administrative references. Now there was evidence that someone had deliberately created a file to connect the two mothers with the receiving identities assigned to their children.

Yet the file itself was missing.

Edward called Jonathan.

"Where is Margaret Voss now?"

"She lives outside the city. Her solicitor says she's willing to cooperate, but she won't release any private records without a formal request."

"Send the request."

"Already done."

"Ask her whether she ever received Annex C."

"I'll do that."

Edward paused.

"And Jonathan?"

"Yes?"

"Don't tell her what the register says."

"Why?"

"Because I want to hear what she remembers before she sees the evidence."

Margaret Voss arrived two days later.

She carried a leather handbag and a thin folder. Her hair was neatly arranged, and she walked with the careful movements of someone who had learned to conserve her energy.

Edward greeted her and offered a seat.

"I understand you've found another record," she said.

"We have."

"Does it concern the children?"

"Yes."

Margaret placed her folder on the table.

"Then I hope you've finally found something that identifies them."

Edward did not answer immediately.

He opened the recovered annex register.

"Do you recognise this reference?"

He showed her the entry.

**EFO-SL/07 — Annex C.**

Margaret read it.

Her fingers tightened around the edge of her handbag.

"Where did you find this?"

"In Charles Vale's legal archive."

She looked at the page again.

"Annex C."

"Do you remember receiving it?"

Margaret took a moment.

"I remember a sealed file with that reference."

"When?"

"During the first month after the births."

"Did it contain the maternal and receiving identities of Victoria Aurelius and Isabella Vale?"

Margaret looked up.

"Who told you that?"

"The annex register."

She lowered her eyes.

"Then the register survived."

Edward leaned forward.

"You know what was in the file."

"I know what I was told it contained."

"Who gave it to you?"

"Charles."

"Personally?"

"Yes."

"Did you sign a receipt?"

"I believe so."

"Do you still have it?"

Margaret opened her folder and removed several papers.

She sorted through them slowly.

At the bottom was a photocopy of a receipt.

The heading read:

**EFO-SL/07 — ANNEX C**

Below it:

**Receiving custodian: Margaret Voss.**

**Purpose: Restricted identity preservation.**

**Access permitted only upon completion of originating verification.**

The receipt carried Charles Vale's signature.

Margaret's signature appeared beneath it.

Edward examined the date.

The twenty-second day after the births.

"Was the file sealed?"

"Yes."

"Did you open it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I was instructed not to."

"Who instructed you?"

"Charles."

"Did you know what the file contained?"

Margaret hesitated.

"Not initially."

"And later?"

"I was told it contained two maternal references and two receiving identity records. I was not given permission to examine the complete contents."

Edward watched her carefully.

"Did you ever see the children's names?"

"No."

"Did you know whether the two receiving records referred to the same infant or different infants?"

"No."

"Did you know which child was intended to be placed with which family?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No."

Edward placed the receipt on the table.

"Then why were you made the custodian?"

Margaret looked at him.

"Because I was responsible for monitoring the restricted records."

"That doesn't explain why Charles entrusted you with the only file containing both mothers' identities."

"I didn't ask him to."

"Did he explain?"

"He said the information could not be released until the originating medical institutions had completed their verification."

Edward glanced at Jonathan.

That wording was familiar.

It was almost identical to the restrictions on the St Catherine's card.

"Did anyone ever request access?"

Margaret thought for a moment.

"Yes."

"Who?"

"Victoria Aurelius's representative."

"When?"

"Somewhere around the twenty-eighth day."

"Was access granted?"

"No."

"Why?"

"The required originating verification had not been completed."

"Who refused it?"

"Charles."

Edward leaned back.

"Did Victoria herself request the file?"

"I don't know. I only received the request through an intermediary."

"Who was the intermediary?"

Margaret looked uncomfortable.

"Marianne Cross."

Edward placed the original correspondence on the table.

"Marianne Cross requested access to Annex C on Victoria Aurelius's behalf?"

"That's what the request stated."

"Did she have authority to act for Victoria?"

"I never saw a formal authorisation."

"Then why did you accept the request?"

"I didn't accept it. I forwarded it to Charles."

"And he refused."

"Yes."

"Did he explain why?"

Margaret looked down.

"He said Victoria had already received the information she was entitled to receive."

Edward glanced at the note Victoria had recently discovered.

The empty pouch.

The letter warning that the IV-204 band should not be treated as proof.

The separate instruction concerning the receiving identity.

"Did you believe Victoria had received the complete information?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the file was still sealed."

Edward waited.

Margaret looked at him.

"I knew she had received correspondence. I knew she had received some identification material. But I did not know what else had been withheld."

"Did you tell her?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"I wasn't authorised to contact her."

"Did you consider doing so?"

Margaret's expression tightened.

"Yes."

"What stopped you?"

"Charles told me that contacting either family could compromise the children's placements."

Edward studied her.

"Did you believe him?"

"I believed there were children whose identities had not been properly established. I did not want to create another problem."

"Did you know where the children were?"

"No."

"Did Charles?"

Margaret did not answer.

Edward repeated the question.

"Did Charles know where they were?"

"I believe he knew more than he told me."

"Why?"

"Because he received the transfer reports."

"Which transfer reports?"

"All the reports routed through EFO-SL/07."

Edward wrote that down.

"Did you ever see a report identifying a child as Elena?"

Margaret looked up.

"Elena?"

"Yes."

She shook her head.

"No."

"Did you see the name Isabella?"

"No."

"Did you see the name Anna?"

Margaret hesitated.

"I saw that name in a separate residential file. It wasn't part of Annex C."

Edward did not press her.

He had already learned that the same name could appear in several unrelated records without establishing that they concerned the same child.

"Do you still possess Annex C?"

Margaret looked at him for a long moment.

"I don't know."

Edward's expression remained neutral.

"You don't know?"

"I never opened it. I transferred it."

"To whom?"

"To the person listed as the next custodian."

"Who was that?"

Margaret took a breath.

"Adrian Holt."

The name appeared again.

Edward had already interviewed Holt about the M-204 band and the St Catherine's card.

Now he had another reason to return.

"Do you have a transfer receipt?"

Margaret opened her folder again.

She removed a second document.

It was dated the thirty-fourth day.

**Transfer of restricted identity materials.**

**From: Margaret Voss.**

**To: Adrian Holt.**

**Contents: EFO-SL/07 — Annex C, sealed.**

Edward read the final line.

**No opening permitted without written instruction from the originating medical authority.**

There was another signature.

Adrian Holt's.

But below it was a handwritten note.

**Two parts remain separate.**

Edward looked up.

"Did you write this?"

"No."

"Do you know who did?"

Margaret studied the handwriting.

"I think it was Holt."

"Why?"

"He used to write notes like that."

"Did he explain why the two parts had to remain separate?"

"No."

Edward turned the document over.

The reverse side contained another entry.

**Part One — originating medical records.**

**Part Two — receiving identity records.**

The two sections had been listed separately.

Neither was described as containing a complete identity determination.

Edward looked at Margaret.

"Did you ever see Part One?"

"No."

"Did you ever see Part Two?"

"No."

"Then you never possessed the complete Annex C file."

Margaret frowned.

"I possessed the sealed file."

"But it contained two parts?"

"That is what the receipt says."

Edward examined the transfer document.

"Was the file sealed when you handed it to Holt?"

"Yes."

"Was it sealed when you received it from Charles?"

"Yes."

"Did you notice any difference in the seals?"

"No."

"Did anyone tell you whether both parts were inside?"

Margaret hesitated.

"No."

Edward closed the folder.

He had expected the missing file to be a single document.

Instead, Annex C appeared to be a divided archive.

One part contained originating medical records.

The other contained receiving identity records.

The purpose of keeping them apart was not yet clear.

But it was becoming difficult to believe that the separation had been accidental.

Holt agreed to meet Edward the following morning.

He looked considerably less comfortable than he had during their previous interview.

Edward placed Margaret's transfer receipt on the table.

"Do you remember receiving this?"

Holt examined it.

"Yes."

"Was Annex C inside?"

"Yes."

"Both parts?"

Holt looked at the document.

"I believe so."

"You believe so?"

"I received a sealed package. I did not open it."

"Why?"

"Because the transfer instruction prohibited it."

Edward placed another document beside the receipt.

It was the note discovered in Victoria's archival pouch.

**Do not attach this material to the infant identification card.**

Holt read it.

"Where did you find this?"

"In Victoria Aurelius's archive."

He examined the reference.

"Annex C."

"Yes."

Holt placed the paper down.

"That belongs to the restricted identity file."

"What did the file contain?"

"I never examined it."

"Did you know it contained two separate parts?"

"Yes."

"Why were they separated?"

Holt was silent.

Edward waited.

Finally, he said, "Because the originating records and receiving records were not supposed to be treated as interchangeable."

"That sounds like the same explanation Eleanor gave."

"She was correct."

"Then why was the file transferred to you?"

"Because I was responsible for preserving the original material."

"Did you ever open it?"

"No."

"Did you ever transfer it?"

Holt nodded.

"Yes."

"To whom?"

He looked uncomfortable.

"That is where my records become incomplete."

Edward placed his notebook on the table.

"Explain."

"I transferred the package to the secure archive. I don't remember the exact date."

"Who received it?"

"An archive representative."

"Name?"

"I don't recall."

Edward looked at him.

"Mr. Holt, you have already admitted that you retained an original identification band for years without completing the comparison. You also received the St Catherine's card. Now you say you received a sealed file containing the maternal and receiving identities of two children, and you cannot remember where you sent it."

Holt lowered his eyes.

"I understand how that sounds."

"Then help me understand it."

Holt remained silent for several seconds.

Finally, he opened his own folder.

"I kept a separate index of restricted items."

He removed a thin sheet.

"Not the contents. Only the custody references."

Edward took it.

The entry for Annex C was there.

**EFO-SL/07-C.**

**Received: Day 34.**

**Transferred: Day 41.**

**Destination: Secure Archive — Unit 6.**

Edward looked up.

"Where is Unit 6?"

"It was a storage facility used by the Family Office."

"Is it still operating?"

"No. The facility was closed several years later."

"Where were the records transferred?"

"To the central archive."

"Which archive?"

Holt gave him the address.

Edward wrote it down.

Then he noticed another line.

**Access authority: E.V.**

He looked at Holt.

"Eleanor Vale?"

Holt nodded.

"She was listed as the external authority for the file."

"Did she request the transfer?"

"I don't remember."

"Did she ever ask you to open Annex C?"

"No."

"Did she ask you to keep it sealed?"

"Yes."

"When?"

Holt looked at the index.

"On the thirty-eighth day."

Edward read the note.

**Custodian to retain sealed package. No further transfer without originating authority.**

The signature was not Eleanor's.

It was Charles Vale's.

Edward slowly placed the sheet down.

"Then why does the index list Eleanor as the access authority?"

Holt shook his head.

"I cannot explain that."

At the Valmont estate, Isabelle was sitting with Catherine in the small sitting room overlooking the gardens.

She had been reviewing the documents Catherine's solicitor had collected over the previous weeks.

There were no definitive answers.

There were only fragments.

A hospital admission.

A bracelet.

An identification band.

A photograph that had not proved anything.

And now another sealed file that had supposedly contained the connection between two mothers and two children.

Isabelle placed the papers aside.

"Mother?"

Catherine looked up.

"Yes?"

"Do you think the hospital records were deliberately changed?"

Catherine considered the question.

"I think someone interfered with them."

"Do you think they did it because they wanted to hide something?"

"Possibly."

Isabelle looked out of the window.

"Everyone keeps saying that the records are incomplete. But what if the people who had the records knew exactly what happened?"

Catherine did not answer immediately.

"Then we need to find out who knew."

Isabelle turned towards her.

"Did you ever know about Annex C?"

Catherine looked surprised.

"No. What is that?"

Isabelle explained what Edward had told them.

Catherine listened carefully.

When she finished, Catherine shook her head.

"No one ever mentioned such a file to me."

"Did you ever receive a sealed document from Charles?"

"Several."

"About the children?"

"Some were administrative reports. Others concerned the hospital's legal arrangements."

"Did you read them?"

"Yes."

"Did any contain two maternal identities?"

Catherine thought for a moment.

"No."

Isabelle looked down at her hands.

"Then perhaps the information was never meant for us."

Catherine reached across the table, but stopped before touching her daughter's hand.

"Isabelle, I know how difficult this has been."

"I don't want you to think I'm questioning whether you wanted me."

Catherine's expression changed.

"I don't."

"I just want to know what happened to my child."

"I know."

Isabelle looked at her.

"And if the records show that someone deliberately withheld information from you, I don't want you to defend them just because they were helping the family."

Catherine was silent.

Then she nodded.

"I won't."

Isabelle looked away again.

For the first time, she was beginning to understand that finding the truth might require her to question not only the people who had deceived her, but also the people who had believed they were protecting her.

That evening, Edward received a call from the central archive.

The records officer had located the Unit 6 inventory.

Annex C had been transferred there.

The package was still listed as sealed.

Edward requested a formal inspection.

The officer hesitated.

"There is a problem."

"What problem?"

"The inventory lists two separate sealed packets."

"Part One and Part Two?"

"Yes."

"Are both present?"

A pause.

"Part Two is present."

Edward sat forward.

"And Part One?"

"The inventory says it was removed."

"By whom?"

The officer read the entry.

"Authorised release to originating medical authority."

"Name?"

There was another pause.

"Dr. Samuel Hale."

Edward stared at the desk.

Hale.

The Westbridge medical officer who had received both infants under different references.

The man who had documented the replacement identification bands.

The man who had confirmed that 28-B and M-204 were separate infants.

The man who had said he could not establish where either child had gone after leaving his facility.

Edward asked for the date.

The officer read it.

"Day 43."

"Was there a receipt?"

"Yes."

"Who signed it?"

The officer hesitated.

"Dr. Hale."

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

Part One had gone to the originating medical authority.

Part Two remained sealed in the archive.

The two halves of Annex C had been separated again.

But this time, there was a named recipient.

"Send me the receipt," Edward said.

"I will."

He ended the call.

On his desk, the two parts of the investigation seemed to be moving in opposite directions.

One contained the original maternal records.

The other contained the receiving identities.

And the person who had collected the maternal section was the same doctor who had handled the infants at Westbridge.

Edward looked at the clock.

It was late.

He could wait until morning.

But then another message arrived from Jonathan.

It contained a photograph of the Unit 6 release receipt.

Edward opened it.

The first line confirmed the release to Dr. Samuel Hale.

The second line identified the material.

**Annex C — Part One.**

The third line was handwritten.

**To be returned only after maternal identity reconciliation.**

Edward read it twice.

Then he noticed the signature beneath the instruction.

It was not Dr. Hale's.

It was not Charles Vale's.

It was not Eleanor Vale's.

It belonged to someone whose name had appeared only once in the investigation.

**Victoria Aurelius.**
