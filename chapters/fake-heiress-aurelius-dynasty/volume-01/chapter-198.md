# **Chapter 198 — The Report That Had No Author**

The first response from Vale Private Counsel arrived at 8:06.

It was not the report Edward had requested.

It was a custody confirmation.

**Reference:** RR-47/02.

**Original record located:** Yes.

**Current custodian:** Restricted Archive, Vale Private Counsel.

**Originating office:** Restricted Governance Review.

**Author identification:** Not held by current custodian.

**Commissioning authority:** Not Vale Private Counsel.

**Disclosure:** Pending originating-office confirmation.

Edward read the last two lines again.

“They're making a point of separating custody from authorship,” Sophie said.

“They have reason to.”

“Because the report concerns records they hold?”

“Possibly. Or because someone has already accused them of controlling more than they did.”

Edward opened the previous correspondence.

Vale Private Counsel had acknowledged the request without refusing it. They had also volunteered that the report had not been commissioned by their office.

That was not a denial of involvement in the underlying events.

It was a clarification of one specific responsibility.

Edward added it to the chronology.

At 8:22, he sent a request to Restricted Governance Review.

He asked for the originating authority, authorisation history, circulation list and the complete version history of RR-47/02.

He did not request the protected identities referenced in the report.

He wanted to know who had raised the discrepancy, who had received it and what action had followed.

At 9:15, Sophie placed another file on his desk.

“There's a related record.”

Edward looked up.

“From where?”

“The PAO/49/01 preservation schedule. It lists a receiving-route appointment instrument that wasn't included in the original disclosure package.”

“Which instrument?”

“RR-47/02-A.”

Edward opened the file.

It was a short index entry.

**RR-47/02-A — Original Receiving-Route Appointment Instrument**

**Record date:** Day 36.

**Proposed appointment:** Receiving-route representative, identity restricted.

**Execution status:** Conditional.

**Receiving authority:** Not confirmed.

**Original appointment certificate:** Not completed.

**Review status:** Superseded by subsequent review.

Edward stopped.

“Superseded?”

“That's what the index says.”

“By what?”

“RR-47/02.”

“That is a discrepancy report, not an appointment instrument.”

“I know.”

Edward opened the related-document field.

There was a reference to an earlier document.

**RA-36/11 — Conditional Receiving-Route Proposal.**

He searched the index.

The result appeared.

RA-36/11 had been created on day 36.

It contained a proposed representative arrangement.

The candidate's name was sealed.

The document had been marked:

**Conditional — pending independent confirmation of receiving authority.**

It had never been completed.

Edward read the next field.

**Candidate response:** Received.

**Acceptance:** Not established.

**Appointment:** Not established.

He looked at Sophie.

“Who received the response?”

“Restricted Governance Review.”

“Not Programme Administration?”

“No.”

“Not Vale Private Counsel?”

“No. They were listed as the records custodian for the original instrument, but the response went to Governance Review.”

Edward leaned back.

The distinction was becoming clearer.

A candidate could respond to a proposal without being appointed.

A response could be received without constituting acceptance.

An appointment could be proposed without the receiving authority being confirmed.

The report had been created because something in that process had not been represented correctly.

But what?

At 10:40, Restricted Governance Review replied.

The response was signed by Helen Sloane.

She confirmed that RR-47/02 had originated from a restricted internal review and that the report was not created by Vale Private Counsel.

She also confirmed that the author had not been identified in the released record.

However, the report's circulation history could be disclosed.

Edward opened the attachment.

The first entry was dated day 47, 09:12.

**Recipient:** Programme Authority Office.

**Purpose:** Urgent preservation consideration.

The second entry was dated 09:26.

**Recipient:** Governance Secretariat.

**Purpose:** Independent review of receiving-route authority.

The third entry was dated 09:41.

**Recipient:** Restricted Archive, Vale Private Counsel.

**Purpose:** Preservation of original appointment instrument.

The fourth entry was dated 10:03.

**Recipient:** Programme Administration.

**Purpose:** Suspension of administrative representation pending authority reconciliation.

Edward read the fourth entry twice.

“Suspension of administrative representation,” Sophie said.

“Not suspension of the proposed candidate.”

“Correct.”

The wording mattered.

The report had not necessarily rejected a person.

It had questioned the way the proposed receiving arrangement was being represented.

Edward opened the attachment's final page.

There was a handwritten note.

**Do not convert a candidate response into an appointment. Preserve the original proposal and the uncompleted certificate separately.**

No signature.

No name.

Only a date.

Day 47.

Edward placed the note beside the day-36 proposal.

The proposal had remained conditional.

The candidate response had been received.

The appointment certificate had never been completed.

Yet some later administrative records had apparently treated the receiving route as though it had an established representative.

That was the discrepancy.

It still did not identify who had made the incorrect representation.

But it narrowed the problem.

At 11:30, Edward called Dr Eleanor Vale.

“I've received the RR-47/02 circulation history.”

“Then you have the reason for preservation.”

“I have a likely reason. I don't yet have the underlying report.”

“The distinction is fair.”

“Was the discrepancy known to your office before day 47?”

Eleanor was silent for a moment.

“Not through the records I hold.”

“Did you know a receiving-route proposal existed on day 36?”

“I knew a conditional proposal had been created. I did not know its contents.”

“Did you know a response had been received?”

“I knew a response had been registered. I did not know whether it represented acceptance.”

“Did you believe the proposed representative had been appointed?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because the appointment certificate was incomplete.”

Edward glanced at the index.

“Was that information communicated to Programme Administration?”

“It should have been.”

“Was it?”

“I cannot certify that without the original dispatch record.”

“Do you know who was responsible for communicating it?”

“Not from the documents I hold.”

Edward paused.

“Dr Vale, did you request the preservation of the original appointment instrument?”

“No.”

“Did you request the report?”

“No.”

“Did you know it existed before the reconciliation?”

“I knew a discrepancy had been identified. I did not know the report reference.”

“Who informed you?”

“The Programme Authority Office.”

“When?”

“After the preservation arrangement had been completed.”

“Did they identify the person who raised the discrepancy?”

“No.”

Edward looked down at his notes.

“Then the originating author remains unidentified.”

“Yes.”

Eleanor's voice was calm.

“But the absence of an identified author does not mean the report had no authority to trigger preservation. The office acted on the report's content, not on a personal attribution.”

Edward wrote that down.

It was an important distinction.

A report could be validly received and acted upon even if its author was restricted.

But the restriction could not be used to invent an author.

At noon, a separate response arrived from the Programme Administration office.

It concerned the day-46 alternative candidate note.

The office confirmed that the note had been entered after the day-47 preservation report was created.

Edward frowned.

He checked the chronology.

The day-46 note was earlier.

He read the response again.

Then he saw the wording.

The note had been entered into the register on day 46, but the classification had been modified on day 47.

The office was referring to the modified version.

Edward called Sophie.

“Pull the original entry and the later classification side by side.”

She did.

The original day-46 entry read:

**Alternative candidate may be considered following clarification of competent nomination authority.**

The source field read:

**Pending confirmation.**

The modified version, entered on day 47, read:

**Alternative candidate consideration initiated through C-19 correspondence route.**

The source field read:

**C-19.**

Edward stared at the two versions.

The wording had changed.

Not merely the label.

The original entry had said that a candidate might be considered after the nomination authority was clarified.

The later version suggested that the process had already been initiated through C-19.

It did not name a candidate.

It did not complete an appointment.

But it changed the apparent origin of the process.

“Who made the modification?” Sophie asked.

“The shared account is PA-REVIEW-06.”

“Patricia Moore's supervisory account.”

“Associated with it. Not proof she used it.”

Edward opened the access log.

The modification time was 10:42 on day 47.

The RR-47/02 report had been circulated to Programme Administration at 10:03.

Thirty-nine minutes earlier.

Edward looked at Sophie.

“The report was received before the classification changed.”

“Could the person have been trying to correct the record?”

“Possibly.”

“Or make it look as though C-19 had initiated the process.”

“Also possible.”

He opened the modification reason.

The field contained a single sentence.

**Administrative source alignment.**

No explanation.

No author.

Edward copied the wording into his notes.

The report had identified a problem with the representation of the receiving route.

Then, less than forty minutes after Programme Administration received it, someone modified the record in a way that made the C-19 route appear more authoritative.

The timing was significant.

It was not proof of intent.

At 14:20, Patricia Moore arrived for her interview.

She looked tired but composed.

Edward began with her role.

“Were you responsible for supervisory review of Programme Administration tasks on day 47?”

“I was one of the supervisors.”

“Could you access PA-REVIEW-06?”

“Yes.”

“Was it a personal account?”

“No. It was a shared supervisory credential.”

“Did you use it on day 47?”

“I don't remember.”

Edward placed the access log before her.

“This shows the credential was used at 10:42 to modify the classification of the day-46 alternative candidate note.”

Patricia examined the record.

“I can see that.”

“Do you remember making this change?”

“No.”

“Did you instruct someone else to make it?”

“No.”

“Did you approve the wording?”

“No.”

Edward placed the two versions beside each other.

“Can you explain why the source field changed from 'Pending confirmation' to 'C-19'?”

Patricia read both.

Her expression tightened.

“That is not a correction.”

“What is it?”

“It changes the meaning.”

“How?”

“The first entry says the nomination authority had not been confirmed. The second says the correspondence route initiated the candidate process.”

“Would C-19 have had that authority?”

“No.”

“Did you know that on day 47?”

“Yes. The restrictions were already in the procedure.”

“Then why would someone enter it?”

“I don't know.”

Edward waited.

Patricia continued.

“The correspondence route could carry a question. It could not nominate a candidate. That distinction was in the task guidance.”

“Was the guidance available to everyone using the shared account?”

“It should have been.”

“Was there a reason to change the source field?”

“Not that I can see.”

Edward turned to the access log.

“Could several people have used the credential?”

“Yes.”

“Who?”

“I would need the duty roster and terminal allocation.”

“Can you provide them?”

“Yes.”

“Would they identify the individual?”

“Not necessarily. The credential was shared, but the terminal was assigned to a workstation. There may be a local access record.”

Edward noted it.

“Did you receive RR-47/02 on day 47?”

Patricia looked at the report reference.

“I remember receiving a discrepancy notice.”

“Did you read it?”

“I read the administrative summary.”

“What did it say?”

“That the receiving-route records were being preserved because a candidate response might have been represented as an appointment.”

Edward's pen stopped.

“Was that the wording?”

“Not exactly. That was the substance.”

“Did you understand it?”

“Yes.”

“Did you agree?”

“I agreed that the original records should be preserved.”

“Did you modify the day-46 note after receiving the report?”

“No.”

“Did you instruct anyone to modify it?”

“No.”

“Did you investigate the changed classification?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

Patricia looked at the table.

“Because I was told the source field had been aligned with the correspondence record.”

“Who told you?”

“I don't remember.”

“Was that written?”

“I believe it was in a task comment.”

“Can we see it?”

“I don't know whether the comment was retained.”

Edward closed his folder.

“Thank you. We'll request the full comment history.”

Patricia looked at the two versions again.

“Mr Valmont?”

“Yes?”

“I want to make one thing clear.”

“Go ahead.”

“The fact that my supervisory credential was used does not mean I authorised that change. I would not have approved it in that form.”

Edward nodded.

“Your statement will be recorded as such. The access record will be examined separately.”

Patricia stood.

At the door, she paused.

“One more thing.”

Edward waited.

“On day 47, the instruction was not to close the receiving route. It was to preserve it.”

“Why?”

“Because the appointment status was uncertain. If we closed the route, we might lose the distinction between a proposed representative and an appointed one.”

She left.

Edward remained seated.

That was the first direct explanation of why the original records had been preserved rather than simply corrected.

The office had wanted to keep the incomplete proposal intact.

Someone else had later changed its classification.

At 16:05, Sophie found the task comment.

It had been attached to the day-47 modification.

The comment was short.

**Source alignment required for continuity. C-19 remains the active contact route pending confirmation of successor authority.**

There was no name.

The comment had been entered through the same shared credential.

Edward read it twice.

“Active contact route,” Sophie said.

“Not active appointment.”

“Correct.”

The wording was carefully chosen.

It could mean that C-19 remained available to receive and forward correspondence while a successor authority was being confirmed.

It did not necessarily mean that C-19 had been appointed to anything.

But the source field had been changed from pending confirmation to C-19.

The comment explained the administrative reason.

It did not explain why the field had been altered in a way that could be mistaken for nomination.

Edward added a request for the original task comment metadata.

Then he returned to RR-47/02.

The report had been created because a candidate response might have been treated as an appointment.

The preservation authority had been issued to prevent that confusion from destroying the original record.

And yet the administrative classification had been changed after the report was received.

The discrepancy had not disappeared.

It had acquired another layer.

At 18:12, a final message arrived from Restricted Governance Review.

It was an additional disclosure attached to the RR-47/02 circulation file.

The document was a one-page intake record.

**Report received:** Day 47, 08:38.

**Delivery channel:** Restricted Governance Submission Portal.

**Submission category:** Receiving-route authority discrepancy.

**Originating credential:** Restricted external review credential.

**Identity disclosure:** Not authorised.

**Supporting records:** RA-36/11, candidate response receipt, incomplete appointment certificate.

**Requested action:** Preserve original instruments and suspend any administrative representation of completed appointment pending competent authority review.

Edward read the final line.

The report had been received at 08:38.

The preservation authority had been issued later that day.

The day-46 classification had been modified at 10:42.

He checked the original report creation record.

The report itself had been created at 08:11.

Twenty-seven minutes before submission.

It had not been generated by the Programme Authority Office.

It had not been commissioned by Vale Private Counsel.

It had been submitted through a restricted external review credential.

Edward looked at the credential field.

**External Credential Group: E-3.**

No personal name.

No office.

No organisation.

He opened the credential classification.

The final line was marked:

**Credential category: Former receiving-route participant.**

Edward stopped.

The report had been submitted by someone who had previously participated in the receiving-route process.

Someone who had access to the original proposal, the candidate response receipt and the incomplete appointment certificate.

Someone who had recognised that a response was being represented as an appointment.

He looked at Sophie.

“Can we identify the credential holder?”

“Not from this record.”

“Can we request the credential history?”

“Yes. But the originating office may restrict the identity.”

“Request it anyway.”

He returned to the report.

There was one more field.

**Reason for restricted identity:** Protection of continuing personal communication arrangements.

Edward read it slowly.

The person who had reported the discrepancy was not merely an administrative observer.

Their identity had been protected because of an ongoing personal communication arrangement.

That did not make them P-04.

It did not make them Gabriel Vale.

It did not establish that they were a family member, a receiving representative or the original candidate.

But it meant the person had a connection to the protected communication process that the report itself had not explained.

Edward added one final line to the chronology.

**RR-47/02 — submitted by a former receiving-route participant using restricted external credential E-3. Identity withheld under continuing personal communication protection.**

He saved the file.

The next question was no longer simply who had noticed the discrepancy.

It was why that person had needed protection after reporting it.
