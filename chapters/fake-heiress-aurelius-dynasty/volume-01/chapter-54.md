# **Chapter 54 — The Woman Who Was Never Meant to Return**

For several seconds, Edward said nothing.

Margaret Voss.

The name had appeared in the investigation so many times that it had almost become ordinary.

She had worked in the hospital's patient administration department.

She had been seconded to the Aurelius Family Office.

She had handled confidential correspondence, controlled access to identity records, and signed instructions concerning the two newborn children.

She had left her position eighteen days after their births.

Yet eleven years later, she had still been involved in Anna's case.

And now Morelli was claiming that she had been the final guardian.

Edward stood.

"You're telling me Margaret Voss personally raised Anna?"

"No."

"Then what do you mean by final guardian?"

"She was the person responsible for the arrangement. That does not mean she lived with the child."

"Who did?"

"I don't know."

Edward stared at him.

"You've had eighteen years to investigate this."

"And eighteen years to discover that every person involved was instructed to keep a different part of the truth."

"Did Margaret Voss know Anna's identity?"

"She knew the hospital designation."

"Did she know the biological identity?"

Morelli hesitated.

"Not necessarily."

"Did she know who Anna's mother was?"

"I cannot confirm that."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

Another distinction.

Another piece of information that could easily be mistaken for proof.

"Why would Margaret be made responsible for a child she didn't raise?"

Morelli answered quietly.

"Because someone had to ensure that the arrangement continued."

The following morning, Edward and Jonathan met with Adrian at the Aurelius Global headquarters.

The meeting had been arranged after Morelli's statement.

Alexander joined remotely, while Victoria remained at the estate.

Adrian placed Margaret Voss's personnel records on the screen.

"Her official employment ended on the eighteenth day after the births," he explained. "However, the confidential appointment records show that her administrative authority continued."

Edward pointed to the dates.

"She was still signing documents eleven years later."

"Correct."

Alexander leaned towards the camera.

"Who authorised the continuation?"

"Charles Vale's office."

"Under what authority?"

Adrian displayed another document.

"An agreement between the Aurelius Family Office and Vale Private Counsel. The agreement concerned confidential identity protection."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"I never signed this."

"We know," Adrian replied.

The document carried an authority code associated with Alexander's office.

But the signature field was blank.

Edward examined the page.

"Was the agreement ever reviewed?"

"Once, approximately six months after the births."

"By whom?"

"Margaret Voss."

"Was it approved?"

"No. Her review contains a handwritten objection."

Adrian enlarged the note.

*The authority granted under this arrangement exceeds the original instruction. The originating family has not been informed of the continuing restrictions.*

Edward read it twice.

"So she objected to the arrangement."

"Yes."

"Then why did she continue working under it?"

Alexander answered.

"Because someone had retained control of her access and correspondence."

Edward looked at him.

"Could she have been threatened?"

"Possibly. But we need evidence before drawing that conclusion."

Adrian opened the next file.

It contained a record of Margaret's final salary settlement.

The payment had been made through Vale Private Counsel.

There was also a second payment.

**CONFIDENTIAL FAMILY SUPPORT — BENEFICIARY UNDISCLOSED.**

The amount was considerably larger than her annual salary.

Edward examined the date.

"She received this after leaving the Family Office?"

"Yes."

"Did she receive any further payments?"

"Three more. Over the following fourteen months."

Alexander looked at the figures.

"Charles paid her."

"Through an intermediary account," Adrian said.

"Find the beneficiary."

Victoria was waiting in the family library when Alexander returned from his office.

He placed the documents on the table.

"Margaret Voss was still involved eleven years after the births."

Victoria looked up.

"Morelli confirmed it?"

"Yes."

"Was she looking after the child?"

"Not physically. She appears to have been responsible for the arrangement."

Victoria studied the personnel file.

"She knew about the child."

"She knew about at least one child."

"She knew about both."

Alexander nodded.

Victoria turned to the final payment record.

"Why was she paid after leaving?"

"We don't know."

"Could she have been protecting them?"

"Possibly."

Victoria looked at him.

"Or could she have been paid to keep quiet?"

"That is also possible."

She closed the file.

"Then we must find her before deciding which."

Alexander sat opposite her.

"I agree."

Victoria looked towards the garden.

"All these years, I thought the people who knew what happened had simply disappeared."

She paused.

"But some of them were still receiving money."

Edward's investigation led him to a former residential address in the northern district.

The property had been sold fourteen years earlier.

The purchaser was a private trust.

Its administrator was listed as Northbridge Family Services.

The same organisation that had funded Anna's earlier accommodation.

A neighbour remembered a woman living there for approximately three years.

"She wasn't particularly social," the neighbour explained. "She kept to herself."

"Did she have children?"

"Not that I saw."

"Did she receive visitors?"

"Sometimes. Usually one man."

"Can you describe him?"

"Older than her. Tall. He wore glasses."

Edward showed him a photograph of Charles Vale.

The neighbour studied it.

"That might be him."

"Did you see anyone else?"

The man thought for a moment.

"Once, a young woman came. She carried a folder and stayed for less than half an hour."

"Do you remember her appearance?"

"Dark hair. Perhaps in her twenties."

"Did she arrive alone?"

"Yes."

"Did she leave alone?"

The neighbour frowned.

"No."

Edward leaned forward.

"Who left with her?"

"A little girl."

"How old?"

"Six or seven, perhaps."

"Did the girl seem upset?"

"No. She was holding the woman's hand."

"Did you hear their names?"

The neighbour shook his head.

Then he added something.

"The woman who lived there watched them leave from the upstairs window."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Did she appear upset?"

"Very."

At the Aurelius Estate, Julian was preparing a list.

He had recently added three new items.

The list now contained forty-six activities.

He had written them carefully in a notebook, dividing them into categories.

Things to do indoors.

Things to do outside.

Things to do with all five brothers.

Things that would not require Elena to participate if she didn't want to.

Damien found him sitting on the floor.

"What are you doing?"

"Planning."

"Again?"

Julian looked offended.

"It's important."

Damien glanced at the notebook.

"Forty-six things?"

"Yes."

"Have you considered asking her what she actually wants to do?"

Julian frowned.

"Of course."

"Then why have you planned everything?"

"Because she might not know what she wants yet."

Damien considered that.

"Fair point."

Julian returned to his list.

After a moment, he added another line.

**Ask her what she wants to add.**

Damien read it.

"That's better."

Julian looked up.

"Do you think she'll like the list?"

"I think she'll find it excessive."

Julian's expression fell.

"Why?"

"Because it is."

"You're supposed to be supportive."

Damien sat beside him.

"I am. I'm also trying to prevent you from organising her entire life before meeting her."

Julian looked at the notebook.

Then he closed it.

"Fine."

Damien stood.

"Good."

A moment later, Julian reopened it.

He added another line.

**Do not show her all forty-seven items at once.**

Damien shook his head and left.

Elena received her university interview confirmation at 4:12 p.m.

She read the email twice before showing it to Isabelle.

"Does this mean anything?"

Isabelle examined the message.

"It means your interview was successful."

"But the final selection isn't complete."

"That's still good news."

Elena smiled.

"I know."

Catherine entered the room.

"Have you heard something?"

Elena showed her the email.

Catherine read it and smiled.

"Congratulations."

"Thank you."

Richard joined them a few minutes later.

He examined the message.

"Well done."

Sebastian offered a brief nod of approval.

Nicholas immediately suggested ordering dinner from Elena's favourite restaurant.

Elena laughed.

"One interview confirmation doesn't require a family celebration."

"Why not?"

"Because I'll have to celebrate again if I get accepted."

Nicholas looked thoughtful.

"Then we'll celebrate twice."

Isabelle smiled.

"That seems reasonable."

Elena put her phone away.

For a moment, the family was united around something uncomplicated.

A university application.

A possible future.

An achievement that belonged to Elena alone.

Edward received the handwriting examiner's report the following afternoon.

The signature on the St. Agnes transfer authorisation had been examined against Margaret Voss's known handwriting.

The conclusion was significant.

The signature **A. V.** was not written by Margaret Voss.

It was not written by Charles Vale.

And it did not match Marcus Morelli.

However, the examiner had found a possible match with another document.

An old financial instruction from the Vale Private Counsel archive.

The signature belonged to a woman whose name had appeared only once in the entire investigation.

**ELEANOR VALE.**

Edward read the report again.

Eleanor had signed the transfer authorisation.

She had been involved in the original hospital arrangement.

She had personally received Infant B on the tenth day.

She had written instructions concerning both children.

And now her signature appeared on the document that had authorised Anna's transfer from St. Agnes.

Jonathan looked at the report.

"She was still making decisions eleven years later."

"Apparently."

"Why?"

Edward placed the report beside the old letter from Eleanor.

"Because she never stopped believing the arrangement was unfinished."

"Could she have been trying to protect Anna?"

"Possibly."

"Or was she moving the child to prevent her from being found?"

Edward looked at the documents.

"Those two things are not necessarily mutually exclusive."

That evening, Edward visited Margaret Ellison.

She was sitting beside the window when he entered.

Morelli was already there.

Margaret looked at the folder in Edward's hand.

"Have you found something?"

"Yes."

He showed her the photograph of Anna.

Margaret studied it.

"Is that the girl from the old property?"

"We believe so."

She looked at Morelli.

"Was she one of the babies?"

Morelli did not answer.

Edward sat down.

"We still don't know."

Margaret looked back at the photograph.

"She looks about eleven."

"That is when the photograph was taken."

"Did she know who she was?"

"She knew that something had been hidden from her."

Margaret lowered her eyes.

"Poor child."

Edward placed another document on the table.

It was the St. Agnes transfer authorisation.

"Do you recognise this signature?"

Margaret examined it.

Her expression changed.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"Who?"

She touched the paper gently.

"Eleanor."

"You're certain?"

Margaret nodded.

"She used to write her capital A like that. She always left the lower stroke unfinished."

Edward looked at Morelli.

"Did you know?"

Morelli nodded.

"I suspected it."

"Why didn't you tell us?"

"Because suspicion is not proof."

Margaret continued examining the document.

Then she noticed the date.

"She signed this eleven years later?"

"Yes."

Margaret looked at Edward.

"Then she must have known the child was still alive."

Edward nodded.

"That is what we're trying to establish."

Margaret was quiet for a moment.

Then she asked, "Did Eleanor ever tell you why she became involved?"

Edward shook his head.

Margaret looked towards the window.

"She once told me that she had made a promise to two mothers."

"What promise?"

"That neither child would be lost."

Edward waited.

"Did she keep it?"

Margaret's expression became troubled.

"I don't know."

She looked back at the photograph.

"But I remember something else."

"What?"

"After Eleanor died, someone came to collect a small wooden box from her study."

"Who?"

"A woman."

"Do you remember her name?"

Margaret shook her head.

"She said she had come for the children's papers."

Edward leaned forward.

"What papers?"

"Birth records. Letters. Photographs. Anything Eleanor had kept."

"Did she identify herself?"

Margaret thought carefully.

"She said she was the person who had promised to take care of the second child."

Edward stopped.

"The second child?"

Margaret nodded.

"Those were her exact words."

"Did she say which child she meant?"

"No."

"Did she give you her name?"

Margaret's eyes narrowed as she tried to remember.

"She said something about being the one who had been waiting."

Edward looked at Morelli.

Morelli had gone very still.

Margaret continued.

"She also said Eleanor had finally done what she was supposed to do."

Edward took out his notebook.

"Do you remember anything else?"

Margaret looked towards the ceiling.

Then she whispered:

"She said the child had been returned to the wrong family once already."

The room fell silent.

Edward looked at the photograph of Anna.

Then at the transfer authorisation.

Eleanor Vale had signed the document that moved the child from St. Agnes.

A woman had collected the children's papers after Eleanor's death.

And that woman had described herself as the person who had been waiting for the second child.

Edward closed his notebook.

"Do you remember when she came?"

Margaret nodded slowly.

"Yes."

"How long after Eleanor died?"

"About three weeks."

"Do you remember the date?"

Margaret looked at Morelli.

Then she gave Edward an answer.

"She came on the day Eleanor's study was emptied."

Edward wrote it down.

He had assumed Eleanor's papers had been dispersed through the estate after her death.

But someone had specifically collected the children's records.

Someone who knew there were two children.

Someone who believed one of them had been returned to the wrong family.

And someone who had waited until Eleanor could no longer explain what she had done.

**The next step was no longer to find Eleanor's missing papers.**

**It was to find the woman who had taken them.**
