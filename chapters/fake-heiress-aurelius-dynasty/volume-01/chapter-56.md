# **Chapter 56 — The Child Who Was Never Supposed to Be Elena**

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Edward remained beside the wooden cabinet.

Jonathan had stopped taking notes.

Margaret Voss sat with her hands folded in her lap, looking at the photograph of the two newborns.

Finally, Edward asked the question both men were thinking.

"What do you mean?"

Margaret looked up.

"I mean exactly what I said. The child I was responsible for was never supposed to be Elena."

"Then who was she supposed to be?"

"I don't know."

Edward frowned.

"You managed confidential identity records. You had access to the hospital arrangements, the Family Office, and the payments made through Northbridge. Surely you knew which child you were assigned."

"I knew which child the paperwork described."

"Which was?"

"Infant A."

Edward took out his notebook.

"And the child you were responsible for?"

"An infant who was delivered to a temporary receiving guardian. I was instructed to monitor the placement and ensure that the child remained safe."

"Was that Margaret Ellison?"

Margaret's expression changed.

"She was one of the receiving guardians."

"One of them?"

"Yes."

"How many were there?"

"That depends on what you mean by receiving guardian."

Edward pulled a chair closer.

"I mean the people who physically took responsibility for the children."

Margaret considered the question.

"At different times, there were at least four."

Jonathan looked up.

"Four?"

"Margaret Ellison. Eleanor Vale. A woman whose name I was never given. And another person who collected a child after the arrangements had already changed."

Edward wrote the names down.

"Did you personally meet all four?"

"I met three."

"Which one did you never meet?"

"The last person."

"Then how do you know that person existed?"

"Because I was instructed to prepare the transfer."

Edward paused.

"Which child?"

Margaret looked at the photograph again.

"I cannot tell you that from memory."

Edward's patience was beginning to wear thin.

"Mrs. Voss, we have spent weeks recovering records that were deliberately concealed. We have found children moved between temporary guardians, false identities, payments made through private accounts, and an eighteen-year-old girl whose origins remain uncertain. We need facts."

Margaret did not appear offended.

Instead, she nodded.

"Then you should understand why I won't guess."

She reached for the photograph.

"I have already seen what happens when people decide they know which child belongs to whom."

Margaret went to the cabinet and unlocked its lower drawer.

Inside were several envelopes, a small notebook, and a document folder secured with a faded ribbon.

She removed the folder.

"I kept copies of three things," she said. "The original monitoring instructions, the receiving-party records, and the correspondence Eleanor sent me."

Edward looked at the folder.

"Why did you keep them?"

"Because I was told to destroy them."

"By whom?"

"Charles Vale."

"Did you refuse?"

"Not immediately."

She opened the folder.

"I destroyed some copies. I retained these."

"Why?"

"Because the instructions changed too often. Every time I received a new document, someone wanted me to treat the previous one as though it had never existed."

She removed a page.

It was a monitoring schedule.

The first entry was dated the ninth day after the births.

**INFANT A — TEMPORARY RECEIVING ARRANGEMENT.**

**INFANT B — TEMPORARY RECEIVING ARRANGEMENT.**

Below that were separate instructions.

- Receiving guardian identities to remain confidential.
- Biological identity not to be disclosed.
- No permanent registration without originating authority.
- Both placements subject to independent review.

Edward recognised the wording.

"These are the original restrictions."

"Yes."

"Who issued them?"

"Charles Vale delivered the documents. The authority reference was EFO-SL/07."

"Did you know who controlled that reference?"

Margaret looked at him.

"At the beginning, I thought Marcus Morelli did."

"And later?"

"I discovered that several people could issue instructions under the same reference."

Edward's expression hardened.

"Who?"

"Charles Vale. Morelli. Me."

She paused.

"And someone above all three of us."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Do you know who?"

Margaret shook her head.

"I was never shown the original authorisation."

The next document was a handwritten instruction.

Edward recognised the signature immediately.

Eleanor Vale.

*The two infants must not be treated as interchangeable.*

*Their original identities remain unresolved.*

*Any receiving party who believes they have been assigned a particular child must be informed that the assignment is provisional.*

Below it was another sentence.

*The child must not be told that her mother has abandoned her.*

Edward looked up.

"Was that instruction followed?"

Margaret's face became distant.

"Not consistently."

"Who failed to follow it?"

"Several people."

"Did you?"

She did not answer immediately.

Then she said, "Yes."

The admission was quiet.

Edward waited.

"I believed I was protecting the children," Margaret continued. "I told myself that withholding information was safer than allowing the wrong people to reach them."

"Which information did you withhold?"

"That someone had been looking for them."

Edward thought of the St. Agnes visitor register.

"The Aurelius visitor?"

Margaret looked at him sharply.

"So you found that record."

"We did."

"Then you know someone tried to see the child."

"Who was the visitor?"

"I don't know."

"You arranged the appointment."

"I arranged the request."

"That's not the same thing."

"No," she agreed. "It isn't."

"Who requested it?"

Margaret looked down.

"Victoria Aurelius."

Edward became still.

"Victoria?"

"That was the name written on the original request."

"Did Victoria Aurelius personally contact you?"

"No."

"Then how do you know it came from her?"

"Because it was accompanied by a letter bearing her signature."

Edward placed both hands on the table.

"Where is that letter?"

Margaret closed the folder.

"I don't have it."

"Who does?"

"Charles Vale took it."

Edward stared at her.

"Why?"

"Because the letter contained a question that the arrangement was never supposed to answer."

"What question?"

Margaret looked directly at him.

"Which child was hers?"

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was reviewing the old hospital correspondence.

She had asked Adrian to obtain every surviving document bearing her name.

Most were ordinary.

Medical bills.

Insurance forms.

Correspondence with the hospital.

A handful of letters concerning the missing infant.

But there was one document she had never seen.

It was a photocopy of an appointment request.

The handwriting was familiar.

Her own.

Victoria stared at the date.

It had been written eighteen years earlier, less than three weeks after the births.

"I don't remember sending this."

Alexander stood beside her.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

He read the request.

It asked the hospital to arrange a private meeting with the child recorded under the Aurelius maternal identity.

The final paragraph was brief.

*I do not wish to disturb the child's current arrangements. I only want confirmation that she is alive and receiving appropriate care.*

Victoria covered her mouth.

"Alexander..."

He placed a hand on the back of her chair.

"That isn't proof that you sent it."

"I know."

"But the handwriting appears consistent."

Victoria looked at the signature.

"I would never have forgotten writing this."

"Perhaps you didn't."

"What does that mean?"

Alexander turned the page.

There was a small administrative notation beneath the signature.

**REQUEST RECEIVED THROUGH AUTHORISED FAMILY REPRESENTATIVE.**

"Someone may have submitted it on your behalf," he said.

Victoria read the line.

"Then why wasn't I told?"

Alexander did not answer.

He was beginning to suspect that the concealment had been much more extensive than either family had understood.

Back at Margaret's house, Edward had reached the most important question.

"Did you know where Elena was?"

Margaret studied him.

"I knew where a child named Elena Valmont was."

"That's not what I asked."

"I understand."

"Did you know she might be the missing Aurelius daughter?"

Margaret remained silent.

Edward leaned forward.

"Did you?"

"Not when she was born."

"And later?"

"Later, I suspected that the records had been altered."

"Why didn't you investigate?"

"I did."

"What did you find?"

Margaret opened the folder again.

She removed a small sheet of paper.

It contained a handwritten list of names.

**ELENA VALMONT.**

**ISABELLE VALMONT.**

**ANNA M.**

Beside each name was a reference number.

Edward recognised two of them from the hospital documents.

The third was unfamiliar.

"What is this?"

"A comparison list."

"Who prepared it?"

"I did."

"When?"

"After Anna was admitted to St. Agnes."

Edward looked at her.

"You were comparing Elena, Isabelle, and Anna?"

"I was trying to establish whether the three names referred to the same child, or whether one of the children had been recorded under more than one identity."

"And?"

"I couldn't establish it."

"Why not?"

"Because the birth references were incomplete. The hospital had removed the original maternal identification pages. The receiving records were provisional. The later guardianship documents used different descriptions."

Edward examined the list.

There was another handwritten note at the bottom.

**DO NOT ASSUME THAT THE CHILD CURRENTLY KNOWN AS ELENA IS THE CHILD ORIGINALLY RECORDED AS INFANT A.**

Edward looked up.

"Who wrote this?"

"I did."

"Why?"

"Because somebody had already made that assumption."

"Who?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward felt a familiar tension settle in his shoulders.

"What did he believe?"

Margaret closed the folder.

"He believed that Elena Valmont was the child who had been assigned to the Aurelius family."

Jonathan looked up sharply.

"Then he believed Isabelle was the Valmont child?"

"Yes."

Edward shook his head.

"But the placement documents were provisional."

"I told him that."

"And he ignored you?"

"He said the family records had already been corrected."

"By whom?"

Margaret's expression hardened.

"That is the question I have been trying to answer for eighteen years."

The Aurelius family gathered in the library that evening.

Adrian had received Edward's preliminary findings.

He explained them carefully.

"Margaret Voss confirms that multiple people handled the children after their hospital discharge. She also says the identity assignments were never finalised."

Alexander listened without interruption.

Lucien leaned forward.

"Then how did the Valmont family end up raising one of the children?"

"We don't know," Adrian replied.

Damien looked at the old hospital records.

"Someone must have completed the placement."

"Yes," Edward said through the speakerphone. "But the person who completed it may not have known the child's biological identity."

Victoria looked distressed.

"Then Catherine may have been raising a child whose identity she was never told."

"That is possible."

"And I may have been searching for a child who was somewhere entirely different."

"That is also possible."

Julian, who had been unusually quiet, looked at the photograph of the two newborns.

"Does Margaret know where they are?"

"She knows where one of the children went at a particular point," Edward answered. "She doesn't know where both are now."

Julian lowered his eyes.

"So we still don't know which one is our sister."

Nobody corrected him.

Victoria reached for the photograph.

"Not yet."

Alexander looked around the room.

"We will not make assumptions. Not about Elena. Not about Isabelle. Not about Anna."

Adrian nodded.

"And we will not approach any of them until we have reliable evidence."

Julian looked at his list of forty-seven activities.

He had brought it to the library but had not opened it.

For once, he seemed uncertain what to add.

Then he quietly wrote something on the last page.

**48. Find out what she wants before making plans for her.**

He closed the notebook.

That night, Edward returned to Margaret's house for one final question.

"Why did you say the child you were responsible for was never supposed to be Elena?"

Margaret looked tired.

"Because the child assigned to my monitoring file had a different reference number."

"Which one?"

She hesitated.

Then she removed a small card from the folder.

It was an old hospital identification reference.

The name field had been left blank.

The reference number was visible.

**INFANT B — 2:17 A.M.**

Edward stared at it.

"That's the Aurelius birth time."

"Yes."

"And you were responsible for Infant B?"

"On paper."

"Who was responsible for Infant A?"

Margaret looked towards the window.

"That was supposed to be someone else."

"Who?"

She did not answer.

Edward placed the card on the table.

"Mrs. Voss, if you know who was responsible for Infant A, tell me."

Margaret turned back towards him.

"I don't know who received that responsibility after the original arrangement collapsed."

"Then who did?"

She reached into the folder once more.

This time she removed a sealed envelope.

It was old, yellowed at the edges, and addressed in Eleanor Vale's handwriting.

**TO THE PERSON WHO WAS GIVEN INFANT A.**

Edward looked at the envelope.

"Why didn't you give us this earlier?"

"Because I was instructed to give it only when both families were prepared to accept that the child might not be the one they expected."

"Who instructed you?"

Margaret's expression became unreadable.

"Eleanor."

Edward examined the seal.

It had never been opened.

"Do you know what's inside?"

"No."

"Then why keep it?"

Margaret looked at the envelope.

"Because Eleanor told me that one day someone would ask where Infant A went."

She pushed it towards him.

"And when that happened, I was to give them this."

Edward carefully picked it up.

On the back, beneath the seal, was one final handwritten sentence.

**THE PERSON WHO RECEIVED INFANT A WAS NOT A MEMBER OF EITHER FAMILY.**

Edward looked at Margaret.

"Who was it?"

Margaret shook her head.

"I never knew."

Then, after a pause, she added:

"But Eleanor did."

And for the first time since the investigation began, Edward held a document that might reveal not merely where one of the children had gone—

but who had deliberately taken her outside both families.
