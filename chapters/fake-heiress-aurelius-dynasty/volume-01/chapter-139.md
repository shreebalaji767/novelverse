# **Chapter 139 — The Instruction Written Before Anyone Had Answered**

The index entry was dated day thirty-three.

Edward read it again before Jonathan opened the accompanying archive history.

**CONTINUITY OF REPRESENTATIVE AUTHORITY — PRIOR TO PRINCIPAL CONTACT**

The file had been created one day before the duration clarification entered the authority archive.

It had been transferred to V-17 on day sixty-one, alongside other documents connected to Private Instruction 07.

The custody restriction was identical.

*Access requires the originating representative and receiving authority to be present together.*

No receiving authority had ever been appointed.

The instruction had therefore remained sealed.

“Can we establish whether the document was physically placed inside the Private Instruction 07 box?” Edward asked.

Jonathan examined the inventory.

“Not from this entry. It was transferred to V-17 under the same custody reference, but the inventory lists it as a separate sealed item.”

“Then it may not be part of the original instruction.”

“Correct.”

“Find the transfer receipt.”

Jonathan opened the preservation record.

The document had been collected from Charles Vale's private office by Arthur Bell on day sixty-one.

The receipt listed three items:

1. Private Instruction 07 — original sealed box.
2. AV-18 — personal representation correspondence.
3. PR-06 — prior-to-contact instruction, sealed separately.

All three had been delivered to V-17.

But the third item had an additional note.

*Do not attach to the general identity file. Retain until receiving authority is appointed.*

Edward stared at the wording.

“Who wrote that?”

“The handwriting is not attributed.”

“Could it be Eleanor's?”

“We've asked Dr Sen to compare it.”

Edward closed the file.

“Then we need to find out who prepared the instruction and why.”

Arthur Bell remembered the third item.

He was more certain about it than he had been about several of the earlier documents.

“It was smaller than the other packages,” he said. “A narrow envelope, sealed with a dark blue strip.”

“Was it delivered by Charles?”

“No. It was already in the office when I collected the other documents.”

“Who gave it to you?”

“A records assistant.”

“Name?”

“I don't remember.”

Arthur considered the question.

“But I remember what he said.”

Edward waited.

“He told me the envelope had to remain separate because it concerned the communication representative, not the child allocation.”

“Did he identify the representative?”

“No.”

“Did he say the envelope was intended for Gabriel Vale?”

Arthur shook his head.

“He said it was to remain available for the person authorised to receive it.”

Edward looked at Jonathan.

That was another distinction.

The instruction concerned the communication representative, but it was not necessarily addressed to Gabriel personally.

“Did Charles ever ask you to open it?”

“No.”

“Did he ever ask you to deliver it?”

“No. He said it was to remain in custody until the joint authority condition was satisfied.”

Arthur paused.

“Actually, there was one occasion when he asked whether the envelope had been moved.”

“When?”

“Several months after the transfer.”

“Did you tell him it was still sealed?”

“Yes.”

“What did he say?”

Arthur looked toward the window.

“He said, 'Then the arrangement remains as intended.'”

Edward wrote down the sentence.

“Did he explain what arrangement?”

“No.”

Dr Mira Sen's preliminary comparison arrived that afternoon.

The handwriting on the V-17 custody note was not Eleanor's.

It was also not Charles's.

The examiner found similarities with several administrative documents prepared by the Personal Instructions Division, but the sample was too short for reliable attribution.

The blue sealing strip was original to the archive.

The envelope showed no evidence of opening or resealing.

The instruction remained intact.

Edward read the report and called Jonathan.

“We need a legal route to inspect it.”

“The joint-authority condition prevents ordinary access.”

“Could the preservation custodian release a copy of the outer record without opening the envelope?”

“Yes. We already have the outer inventory.”

“Could the court authorise examination for the limited purpose of determining whether the instruction creates a continuing risk to the protected principal?”

“Possibly. But the application would need to distinguish preservation access from substantive authority.”

“Then prepare it.”

Jonathan nodded.

“And the receiving authority?”

“Still unappointed.”

“Would the application need to identify one?”

“No. We are not asking to appoint a receiving authority. We are asking whether an instruction concerning the principal's communication rights can be examined without treating the person examining it as the receiving authority.”

Jonathan gave a small, humourless smile.

“That distinction is going to take several pages.”

“Write them.”

The legal application was submitted through the independent preservation review process.

It did not request access to the proposed recipient schedule, the sealed identity records, or the original Private Instruction 07.

It asked only for limited examination of the separate PR-06 envelope.

The application argued that the instruction might contain information relevant to the continuing restriction on personal communication.

It also stated that no investigator should be treated as an appointed receiving authority merely because they were examining a document.

The reviewing solicitor accepted the distinction.

Three days later, a conditional examination order was issued.

The envelope could be opened in the presence of the preservation custodian and an independent legal observer.

Its contents would be photographed and returned to sealed custody.

No identity information could be disclosed unless separately authorised.

Edward read the conditions twice.

“Good.”

Jonathan nodded.

“The review is scheduled for tomorrow.”

The envelope was opened in a secure records room.

Arthur Bell attended as preservation custodian.

A solicitor from the independent review office observed.

Dr Sen photographed the seal before it was cut.

The envelope contained four pages.

The first was a cover instruction.

The second was a communication procedure.

The third was a draft acknowledgement.

The fourth was a blank continuation sheet.

Edward began with the cover.

**PR-06 — PERSONAL REPRESENTATIVE CONTINUITY**

**Date: Day 33**

**Prepared under authority of the Originating Office**

The first paragraph read:

*The purpose of this instruction is to preserve a communication channel for the protected principal while originating identity and receiving arrangements remain subject to independent verification.*

Edward continued.

*The designated communication representative may receive procedural information, request clarification, preserve correspondence, and return a response expressly limited to the scope of the representative's authority.*

That was consistent with Gabriel's original certificate.

But the next paragraph was different.

*Where direct communication with the protected principal has not been completed, the representative may remain available for administrative continuity, provided that no personal consent, refusal, placement acceptance, or identity determination is inferred from the representative's participation.*

Edward paused.

The wording was careful.

It did not say the representative could make decisions for P-04.

It did not say the principal had agreed.

It simply allowed the representative to remain available.

He turned the page.

The communication procedure contained four stages:

1. The principal must be provided with the original information through a verified channel.
2. The principal must be given an opportunity to respond personally or through an independently verified representative.
3. Any representative response must be recorded separately from the principal's own decision.
4. If communication fails, the failure must remain unresolved until a valid further attempt or authorised review occurs.

Edward read the fourth stage again.

*The absence of a completed communication must not be converted into a refusal, consent, or administrative acceptance.*

It was exactly the distinction Eleanor had repeatedly defended.

Then he reached the third page.

The draft acknowledgement contained two separate sections.

**SECTION A — REPRESENTATIVE'S PROCEDURAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT**

*I acknowledge receipt of information for the limited purpose of preserving the principal's communication rights.*

**SECTION B — PRINCIPAL'S PERSONAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT**

*I confirm that I have personally received the original information and understand the choices available to me.*

The second section had a separate signature line.

It was blank.

Edward examined the document.

“Was this draft ever issued?”

The solicitor checked the inventory.

“No dispatch record is attached.”

“Was it sent to Gabriel?”

“No evidence of that.”

“Was it sent to P-04?”

“No.”

Edward looked at the blank signature line.

The distinction had been designed into the original procedure.

A representative could acknowledge receipt of information.

But the principal's own acknowledgement remained separate.

The two could not be substituted for each other.

He turned to the final page.

It contained no text.

Only a handwritten reference at the bottom.

**AV-18 / CONTACT COMPLETION — PENDING**

Edward looked up.

“Who wrote this?”

Dr Sen photographed the note.

“We will need to compare it.”

The solicitor examined the document.

“There's something else.”

She pointed to the lower margin of the second page.

A sentence had been added in smaller handwriting.

*The communication representative must not be asked to supply the principal's decision where the principal has not received the original information.*

Edward read it twice.

It was not part of the printed procedure.

The handwriting was familiar.

Dr Sen examined it under magnification.

“Preliminary comparison suggests Eleanor Vale.”

Edward looked at the solicitor.

“Can we confirm the date?”

“The document is dated day thirty-three. The handwritten addition may have been made later.”

“Can you establish when?”

“Not precisely. The ink is consistent with other additions made during the same general period, but we cannot assign a day.”

Edward looked at the note again.

Eleanor had apparently reviewed the instruction before it was sealed.

And she had reinforced the distinction between a representative's procedural role and the principal's personal decision.

“Was there any evidence the instruction was rejected?”

The solicitor checked the file.

“No formal rejection.”

“Was it approved?”

“Not in the documents before us.”

“Then what happened to it?”

Arthur Bell answered.

“It was sealed and transferred to V-17.”

Edward looked at him.

“Without being issued?”

Arthur nodded.

“That's correct.”

The examination revealed something else.

A small routing slip had been folded inside the cover.

It was not listed separately in the original inventory.

The slip was dated day thirty-four.

Its recipient field read:

**COMMUNICATION REPRESENTATIVE — PENDING VERIFICATION**

The dispatch field was blank.

The delivery field was blank.

The return field contained one handwritten instruction.

*Retain until receiving authority is appointed.*

Edward looked at the solicitor.

“Why would a communication instruction depend on the appointment of a receiving authority?”

“That is not necessarily a valid dependency,” she replied. “The two roles are separate.”

“Could it have been an administrative restriction?”

“Yes. But the document itself does not explain why.”

Edward studied the routing slip.

The instruction had been prepared to preserve P-04's communication rights.

Yet it had never been delivered.

It had been sealed because the receiving authority remained unappointed.

That meant the same unresolved appointment was being used to restrict two separate processes.

The receiving authority had been required to open Private Instruction 07.

Now it appeared to have been required before the communication procedure could be delivered.

“Photograph everything,” Edward said.

The solicitor nodded.

The original documents were placed back into the envelope.

A new seal was applied.

The preservation register recorded the examination and its limited scope.

No receiving authority was appointed.

No identity was disclosed.

The envelope returned to V-17.

Victoria listened to Edward's report that evening.

“So the original procedure protected the principal's right to respond,” she said.

“Yes.”

“But the procedure was never delivered.”

“Correct.”

“And the later exception allowed the representative's conditional response to remain active.”

“Yes.”

Victoria looked at the photographs.

“Then someone created a system in which the representative's response could continue to be used while the principal's own opportunity to respond remained incomplete.”

Edward nodded.

Alexander stood beside the table.

“Was the original instruction written by Charles?”

“The cover is signed under the originating office authority. The procedure itself is not individually signed.”

“Could Charles have intended to protect the principal?”

“Yes.”

“Could he have intended to preserve administrative continuity?”

“Yes.”

Alexander's expression remained controlled.

“But neither possibility explains why the instruction was never delivered.”

“No.”

Victoria turned to the routing slip.

“Why was the receiving authority required before Gabriel could receive it?”

“That is what we need to establish.”

She looked at Edward.

“Could this have been an error?”

“It could have been. But it appears in several separate records. We cannot dismiss it as a single clerical mistake.”

Victoria lowered her eyes.

“Then someone repeatedly made the same distinction disappear.”

Edward did not answer.

He had not yet proved that anyone deliberately suppressed the procedure.

But the records were beginning to show a pattern that could no longer be explained by one mistaken entry.

The next morning, Edward met Gabriel again.

He showed him the original day-33 procedure.

Gabriel read it carefully.

When he reached the draft acknowledgement, he stopped.

“I never saw this.”

“Are you certain?”

“Yes.”

“Was this the acknowledgement you expected to receive?”

“No. I was given the conditional response form.”

“Did you know there was supposed to be a separate acknowledgement for P-04?”

Gabriel shook his head.

“I knew the principal's personal acknowledgement was outstanding. I did not know a separate form had been prepared.”

Edward placed the routing slip beside it.

“Would you have accepted the communication procedure if it had been delivered?”

“Of course.”

“Would you have signed Section A?”

“Yes, if it accurately described my authority.”

“And Section B?”

Gabriel looked at the separate signature line.

“That would have been for the principal.”

“Not you?”

“Absolutely not.”

Edward studied him.

“Did anyone ever ask you to sign on behalf of P-04?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell you that your conditional response could replace the personal acknowledgement?”

Gabriel thought for a moment.

“Not in those words.”

“What words were used?”

“Charles said my response could preserve the current administrative position while the principal's communication remained incomplete.”

“Did he show you this instruction?”

“No.”

Gabriel returned the photographs.

“I would have objected if I had seen it.”

Edward watched him.

“Why?”

“Because it says the communication must remain unresolved until the principal receives the original information or an authorised review takes place.”

“And that never happened.”

“No.”

Gabriel looked at the documents again.

“I think I understand something now.”

Edward waited.

“I believed my role was to keep a channel open. I did not realise that my response had become the thing keeping the administrative arrangement open.”

Edward said nothing.

Gabriel continued.

“If I had known that, I would have asked for the status to be suspended until the principal had been contacted.”

“You did ask for your authority to be reviewed.”

“Yes.”

“And the request was not acted upon.”

Gabriel lowered his eyes.

“No.”

The final discovery came from Jonathan.

He had compared the day-33 instruction with the day-47 exception.

The original procedure required a completed communication attempt before any representative response could be treated as part of the principal's administrative position.

The exception removed that requirement for temporary continuity.

But the day-47 document did not mention the original instruction.

It cited only the communication representative's conditional response and the inability to contact P-04.

Jonathan placed both documents side by side.

“Look at the dates.”

Edward examined them.

Day thirty-three.

Day forty-seven.

“Fourteen days.”

“Yes.”

“During those fourteen days, the original procedure was prepared but never delivered.”

“Correct.”

“And the verification key was withheld on day forty-two.”

“Yes.”

“Delivery attempt failed on day forty-three.”

“Yes.”

Edward turned to the day-47 exception.

“Who approved it?”

“Charles.”

“Who received the original procedure?”

“No one.”

“Who knew it existed?”

“Eleanor, at least. Possibly Charles. The instruction was prepared under his office.”

Edward looked at the blank delivery fields.

The original procedure had required the principal to receive information before any response could be treated as meaningful.

The later exception had allowed the representative's conditional response to remain active without that completed communication.

And the document designed to protect the principal's personal acknowledgement had never left the archive.

Jonathan opened another record.

“There is one more entry.”

It was dated day forty-eight.

The day after the exception.

The entry read:

**PR-06 — ORIGINAL COMMUNICATION PROCEDURE**

**Custody status: Retained.**

**Distribution status: Not issued.**

**Reason: Receiving authority not appointed.**

Edward stared at the screen.

“Who entered that?”

“Shared Personal Instructions account.”

“Was there an approval?”

“No.”

Edward looked at the wording.

The original procedure had not been rejected.

It had not been withdrawn.

It had simply been retained.

And the reason given was the same unresolved appointment that had prevented access to Private Instruction 07.

He opened his notebook.

At the top of a fresh page, he wrote:

**Day 33 — Procedure prepared to protect P-04's personal communication rights.**

**Day 34 — Procedure retained pending receiving authority.**

**Day 42 — Verification key withheld.**

**Day 43 — Delivery attempt failed.**

**Day 47 — Temporary communication exception authorised.**

**Day 48 — Original procedure still not issued.**

He underlined the final entry.

Then Jonathan opened the audit history.

A second instruction had been added to the day-48 record.

It was short.

**Do not issue the original procedure while PR-06 remains active.**

There was no signature.

No authorisation reference.

No explanation.

Edward read it once.

Then again.

The original procedure had been prepared to preserve P-04's right to make a personal decision.

The exception had been authorised to keep temporary arrangements moving.

Now the exception itself had become the reason the original procedure could not be delivered.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Find out who added that instruction.”

Jonathan nodded.

“And find out whether anyone ever tried to cancel PR-06.”

He closed the record.

Because if the temporary exception had become a reason to withhold the very procedure it was supposed to protect, then the next question was no longer simply who had created it.

It was who had benefited from keeping it active.
