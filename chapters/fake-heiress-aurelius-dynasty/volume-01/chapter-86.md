# **Chapter 86 — The Item That Was Never Inventoried**

Edward printed the clearance certificate.

He placed it beside the original V-17 custody register, then added the property maintenance inventory and the security access report.

Four documents.

Four different departments.

One recurring problem.

Every document acknowledged that restricted material had existed. None established where it had ultimately gone.

Jonathan stood behind his chair, reading the final notation again.

**One item retained outside the property inventory. Custody transferred separately.**

"Why would anyone remove an item from a restricted room and deliberately exclude it from the inventory?"

"Because the person transferring it did not want it recorded as part of the property's contents."

"That sounds like concealment."

"It may be. It may also have been a separate custody arrangement."

Jonathan pulled out a chair.

"You're defending them again."

"I'm distinguishing what we know from what we suspect."

Edward wrote the reference number on a separate sheet.

**RC-18/FINAL.**

"Adrian Holt signed the clearance certificate," he continued. "He received both parts of Annex C. He transferred them to V-17. He then certified that the room had been cleared, except for one item transferred separately."

"So we ask him what the item was."

"We ask him for the original clearance file, not his recollection."

Jonathan nodded.

"And if he says he doesn't remember?"

"Then we find the person who countersigned it."

Adrian Holt received them the following afternoon.

He had grown older since their previous meeting, but his manner remained precise. He did not seem surprised when Edward placed the clearance certificate on his desk.

"I wondered when you would find this."

Edward studied him.

"You knew about RC-18/FINAL?"

"I knew there was a separate transfer reference."

"What was transferred?"

Holt removed his glasses.

"I cannot tell you from memory."

"Then perhaps the original file will help."

Holt looked at the reference number.

"That is an old administrative code."

"Was it used for Annex C?"

"Not directly."

"Was it used for the sealed package in V-17?"

"I don't know."

Edward leaned forward.

"You signed the certificate."

"I signed the clearance certificate. That does not mean I personally packed every item or supervised every transfer."

"Who did?"

"The external custodian."

"Name?"

Holt paused.

"Dr Samuel Hale."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

"Hale was responsible for the medical records component."

"He was one of the authorised representatives."

"Was he the person who removed the final item?"

"I did not witness the removal."

"Then why does the certificate carry your signature?"

"Because I was responsible for confirming the property's custody status. The certificate stated that the room had been cleared according to the instructions provided."

Edward placed the security report beside it.

"The room was not cleared completely. One item remained outside the inventory."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because it was not being transferred as property."

"What was it being transferred as?"

Holt looked at the paper for a long moment.

"Evidence."

The word settled between them.

Jonathan spoke first.

"Evidence of what?"

"That is not something I can answer without the original transfer schedule."

"Where is it?"

"I don't know."

Edward did not move.

"You signed a certificate referring to an evidence transfer, and you cannot identify the evidence?"

"I can identify the administrative reference. I cannot identify the contents."

"Did you ever see the item?"

Holt hesitated.

"Yes."

"What was it?"

"A document envelope."

"How thick?"

"Not particularly. A few sheets, perhaps."

"Was it marked Annex C?"

"No."

"Was it marked with the names of Victoria Aurelius or Isabella Vale?"

"I don't remember."

"Who handed it to Dr Hale?"

Holt looked up.

"I did not say Dr Hale collected it."

"You said he was the external custodian."

"I said he was one of the authorised representatives."

Edward closed his notebook.

"Mr Holt, we have been trying to establish the fate of two newborn children for months. You have repeatedly described your involvement as administrative. Yet you received a restricted identity file, moved it to a private residence, signed a clearance certificate, and allowed a separate evidence transfer to disappear from the inventory."

Holt's expression tightened.

"I did not decide what happened to those children."

"Perhaps not. But you helped decide which records would remain available to the people searching for them."

Holt said nothing.

After a moment, he opened a drawer and removed a small folder.

"I retained a copy of the transfer reference."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"Why?"

"Because I did not agree with the way the final clearance was handled."

He placed the folder on the desk.

Inside was a single carbon copy.

It contained the same reference number, the same date, and the same notation.

But beneath the line about the separate transfer was an additional entry.

**Receiving custodian: S. Bell.**

Edward read it twice.

"Who is S. Bell?"

"I don't know."

"Was this person employed at V-17?"

"No."

"At the Family Office?"

"Not to my knowledge."

"Then where?"

Holt shook his head.

"The name was supplied by the external custodian."

"Dr Hale?"

"That is what the record indicates."

Edward took a photograph of the page.

"Did you ever contact S. Bell?"

"No."

"Did you verify that the item was delivered?"

"The receipt was signed."

"By S. Bell?"

"Yes."

"Then the transfer was completed."

"Administratively, yes."

Edward looked up.

"That is not the same as verifying the contents arrived."

Holt did not disagree.

The first search for S. Bell produced hundreds of results.

The second, restricted to medical and residential administration records from the relevant period, produced eleven.

The third produced one person whose employment history matched the dates.

**Susan Bell.**

Edward recognised the name immediately.

Jonathan did too.

"Meridian?"

"Yes."

Susan Bell had been the residential review officer who refused to make Child 28-B's proposed name permanent because the necessary identity documents were missing.

She had also been involved in the earlier temporary placement process.

Edward opened the old interview transcript.

Susan had described her role carefully.

She had received administrative records, reviewed proposed placements, and refused to certify identities that could not be supported by original documentation.

She had not claimed to know the biological identity of any child.

"Could this be the same Susan Bell?" Jonathan asked.

"Possibly."

"Then why would she receive an item from V-17?"

"That is what we need to ask."

Susan Bell was living in a retirement community.

When Edward showed her the transfer reference, she adjusted her reading glasses.

"RC-18/FINAL," she said.

"Does it mean anything to you?"

"No."

He showed her the carbon copy.

She examined the signature.

Then she looked up.

"That is my name."

"Did you sign this receipt?"

"I don't remember."

"Would you have received a document envelope from Dr Samuel Hale?"

Susan looked at the paper again.

"I received many sealed envelopes during those years. Most were clinical or administrative records."

"Would you have accepted one without opening it?"

"Of course. If it was properly sealed and the custody instructions were clear."

"Do you remember an envelope from V-17?"

"No."

"An envelope relating to Annex C?"

She became still.

"Annex C?"

Edward nodded.

Susan placed the paper carefully on the table.

"I was never given a complete Annex C file."

"Did you receive any part of it?"

"I don't know."

"Did you receive an envelope from Adrian Holt?"

She shook her head.

"Not that I can remember."

"Then how did your signature end up here?"

Susan studied the carbon copy for a long time.

Finally, she said, "I would like to see the original."

Edward handed it to her.

She turned it sideways, examining the lower corner.

Then she pointed to a small mark beside the signature.

"This is not my usual receiving stamp."

"What does that mean?"

"It means this may not have been signed at my office."

"Could you have signed it elsewhere?"

"Possibly. I sometimes acknowledged sealed materials at the hospital or at an external records facility."

"Would you have signed without checking the package?"

"I would have checked the seal and the reference number. I would not have opened a package if the custody instructions prohibited it."

"Do you recognise the handwriting?"

Susan shook her head.

Then she noticed something Edward had overlooked.

The signature was followed by a short notation.

**Received for onward delivery.**

She read it aloud.

"Onward delivery."

Jonathan leaned forward.

"To whom?"

Susan examined the rest of the page.

There was no recipient.

No destination.

No delivery date.

Only a small handwritten addition beside the reference number.

**R.V. — pending.**

Susan looked at Edward.

"I may have signed for an envelope that I was never authorised to deliver."

Back at the office, Jonathan placed the carbon copy under the document scanner.

"That is quite a development."

Edward remained standing.

"Susan Bell may have received the item, but she cannot remember it. Her signature could be genuine, yet it does not prove the contents."

"She might have received an empty envelope."

"Or a sealed document."

"Or something entirely unrelated."

"Yes."

Jonathan frowned.

"Then why did the transfer certificate connect it to V-17?"

"Because somebody wanted a record that the item had left the property."

Edward enlarged the image.

The notation **R.V. — pending** appeared beside Susan's signature.

"Receiving Verification," Jonathan said.

"Most likely."

"Meaning the item was not supposed to reach its final recipient until verification was complete."

Edward nodded.

"And we already know that verification was never completed."

He opened the old correspondence from Marianne Cross.

Her administrative invoices showed payments for notification work. The final payment had been released before the two required approvals were obtained.

The record did not establish that she had notified either family.

It only established that she had been paid.

Edward compared the dates.

The separate transfer had occurred after the original custody arrangement was supposedly concluded.

That raised another possibility.

Perhaps the envelope had not contained the Annex C file itself.

Perhaps it had contained a document needed to identify the receiving family.

Or perhaps it had contained a notification instruction that was never acted upon.

There was no way to know yet.

At the Valmont residence, Isabelle was working through a university prospectus when Catherine entered.

"You have been reading the same page for ten minutes."

Isabelle looked up.

"I'm comparing the economics programmes."

"That is not what I asked."

Isabelle closed the prospectus.

"I was thinking about the investigation."

Catherine sat opposite her.

"Edward hasn't told us anything new?"

"Not today."

"Then perhaps you should take a break from it."

Isabelle looked at her mother.

"How am I supposed to take a break when every new record tells me that someone knew something about my child and decided not to tell me?"

Catherine's expression softened.

"I know."

"No, Mother. You know that I'm angry. You know that I want answers. But I still don't know whether my daughter was the child in the photograph, the child with the bracelet, or someone else entirely."

Catherine reached across the table, then stopped before touching her hand.

"I won't pretend to know what that feels like."

Isabelle looked down.

"I don't want to spend the rest of my life waiting for someone to tell me who my daughter was."

"Then don't."

Isabelle looked up.

Catherine continued.

"You can study. You can build your own career. You can decide what kind of life you want. Searching for your daughter matters, but it does not have to become the only thing you are allowed to do."

Isabelle was quiet.

After a while, she reopened the prospectus.

"Do you think I could work at the foundation while studying?"

"Yes."

"Without being given a position just because I'm your daughter?"

Catherine smiled faintly.

"I think you would be rather difficult to convince otherwise."

For the first time that afternoon, Isabelle smiled too.

That night, Edward received a message from Susan Bell.

It contained a photograph of an old personal diary.

She had spent several hours searching her belongings after their meeting.

One entry had caught her attention.

The handwriting was hers.

The date matched the RC-18/FINAL transfer.

The entry was short.

**Received sealed envelope from Dr Hale's office. Forwarding instructions incomplete. Retained pending confirmation of receiving authority. Mr. Vale requested immediate delivery. Declined.**

Edward read it again.

Susan had not delivered the envelope.

She had retained it.

He immediately called her.

"Mrs. Bell, do you still have the envelope?"

A pause.

"I don't know."

"Please check."

"I have several old boxes of professional records. I will need to look."

"Was it stored with your personal papers?"

"No. I kept restricted documents separately."

"Where?"

Susan hesitated.

Then she answered.

"In a storage cabinet at my former office. The cabinet was transferred to the hospital records department when I retired."

Edward sat down.

"Does the hospital still have it?"

"I don't know."

"Can you contact them?"

"I can try."

He looked at the diary photograph.

"Mrs. Bell, there is something else. Did you ever open the envelope?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because the instructions were incomplete."

"Did you know what was inside?"

"No."

"Then how did you know it was important?"

Susan's answer came quietly.

"Because the outside of the envelope carried a warning."

Edward waited.

She continued.

**DO NOT SEPARATE FROM ANNEX C.**

For several seconds, Edward did not speak.

The envelope had been removed from V-17.

Susan had received it.

She had refused to deliver it because the receiving authority was incomplete.

And she had kept it with the expectation that someone would eventually return with proper instructions.

"Mrs. Bell," he said carefully, "did the envelope have any other markings?"

"Yes."

"What?"

She looked at the diary again.

"I wrote them down because I thought they might matter."

Edward heard paper moving at the other end.

Then Susan read the words.

**PART TWO — ORIGINAL SEAL INTACT.**

Edward closed his eyes briefly.

Part Two.

The receiving identity records.

The very document that had remained sealed while Part One was released, examined, and returned.

He opened his eyes.

"Mrs. Bell, please don't move or open anything until we can arrange a proper review."

"I understand."

"And please don't contact Mr. Vale."

There was a pause.

"Mr. Edward?"

"Yes?"

"I think someone already did."

Edward straightened.

"What do you mean?"

"The storage department called me this afternoon. They said an individual had requested access to my old cabinet."

"Who?"

"They didn't give me a name."

"Did they say why?"

Susan's voice became quieter.

"They said the person claimed to be collecting a family document."

Edward looked at the photograph of the diary.

For eighteen years, Part Two had been kept away from the families whose identities it was supposed to establish.

Now someone was trying to collect it.

And they had not asked for the file by its reference number.

They had asked for it as a family document.
