# **Chapter 195 — The Review That Had Already Ended**

The outcome document arrived at 8:17.

Edward did not open it immediately.

He read the accompanying release notice first.

**Reference:** AR-164/09.

**Review completion date:** Day 190.

**Outcome status:** Completed.

**Disclosure status:** Restricted.

**Release condition:** Confirmation of originating authority and preservation-custodian concurrence.

**Substantive decision-maker:** Not identified in the administrative summary.

Edward placed the notice beside the original request.

Three days had passed since the review was completed.

The document existed.

Its status was final.

But the office responsible for releasing it had not identified the person who made the decision.

Sophie entered carrying two coffees.

“Still restricted?”

“Still restricted.”

“Did they give a reason?”

“They say the outcome contains a determination about the originating authority.”

“Which is exactly what we're trying to establish.”

“Yes.”

Sophie placed one coffee beside him.

“Then we should ask for the decision record, not just the outcome.”

Edward nodded.

“Ask for the decision record, the authorisation chain, and the release conditions. We don't need the protected principal's personal information.”

Sophie began typing.

Edward opened the chronology again.

Day 164: reconciliation initiated.

Day 190: reconciliation completed.

Day 193: outcome remained restricted.

The process had taken twenty-six days.

It had concluded.

Yet nobody had told them what it concluded.

He looked at the release notice once more.

The field for substantive decision-maker was not marked *restricted*.

It was marked *not identified*.

That distinction mattered.

At 9:05, Elena's platform demonstration began.

The programme coordinator introduced her as the student developer responsible for the grant-management prototype.

Elena stood beside the screen.

She had prepared a short presentation, but she had also brought a working demonstration.

“I want to show you what happens when a decision process reaches a point where the administrative record is complete but the authority behind the decision is not.”

She opened a sample application.

The application had been received.

The documents had been checked.

The eligibility review had been completed.

A reviewer had submitted a recommendation.

But the final decision authority had expired.

The screen displayed:

**Application GR-204**

Application status

Review completed

Reviewer assignment

**Closed**

Successor appointment

Pending

Decision authority

Expired

Final decision

Blocked — authority not verified

All administrative review tasks may be complete without creating authority to issue a final decision.

A programme officer raised her hand.

“Wouldn't that delay the applicant unnecessarily?”

“It might,” Elena replied. “But showing a decision as final when the decision-maker lacks authority creates a different problem.”

“Could the system automatically assign another officer?”

“It can suggest an eligible person. It cannot appoint one.”

The officer looked at the screen.

“Even if the department head has already approved the replacement?”

“Then the department head's appointment instrument should be recorded. The system can verify that the required fields are present. It shouldn't invent the approval.”

A few people nodded.

Elena moved to the audit history.

“Every status change has an operator, timestamp, previous value, new value and supporting authority reference. If someone changes a decision from pending to approved, the system records the authority used for that change.”

Another officer asked, “What if the authority document is confidential?”

“The system can store a restricted reference and access permissions. The public-facing application only needs to display the status. It doesn't need to expose the confidential document.”

The coordinator smiled.

“That is a useful distinction.”

Elena returned to her presentation.

She did not mention OI-07.

She did not mention P-04.

But the problem she was trying to solve had become very clear to her.

A system should not turn an incomplete authority chain into a complete decision simply because everyone wanted the file closed.

At 10:42, Edward received a reply from the Programme Authority Office.

The office agreed to provide the decision record in a limited form.

It would not release the underlying personal authority.

It would not identify the protected principal.

It would, however, disclose the decision's administrative effect.

Edward opened the attachment.

**AR-164/09 — Review Outcome Extract**

**Review completed:** Day 190.

**Purpose:** Reconciliation of originating authority and restricted instruction custody.

**Finding 1:** OI-07 was issued under a valid originating authority.

**Finding 2:** The administrative instruction did not confer appointment authority upon the Governance Secretariat, Programme Administration, Vale Private Counsel or C-19.

**Finding 3:** The subsequent OC-19 review remained incomplete because no independent verifier had been appointed.

**Finding 4:** The day-46 alternative candidate entry did not constitute a nomination or appointment.

**Finding 5:** No evidence in the reviewed material established that P-04 personally received the PR-06 communication.

**Finding 6:** No evidence established that the representative procedural acknowledgement could substitute for the principal's personal acknowledgement.

Edward read the findings slowly.

Sophie leaned closer.

“They've confirmed all the distinctions.”

“Yes.”

“And the original instruction was valid.”

“Yes.”

“But nobody had authority to use it as an appointment.”

“Correct.”

Edward moved to the final section.

**Administrative direction:**

1. Preserve the OI-07 original and its supporting authorisation.
2. Maintain OC-19 as unfilled until a competent originating authority submits a valid nomination.
3. Maintain PR-06 as active until the required personal communication and independent verification conditions are satisfied.
4. Do not treat the day-46 C-19 classification as evidence of nomination, consent or authority.
5. Continue administrative closure only within existing individual appointments.

Edward stopped.

“That's the decision.”

Sophie nodded.

“It confirms the review did not appoint anyone.”

“It also confirms the C-19 entry was not a nomination.”

“And PR-06 remains active.”

Edward looked at the date.

The review had been completed on day 190.

Three days earlier.

But the status of the underlying communication procedure had not changed.

No personal acknowledgement had been recorded.

No independent verifier had been appointed.

No closure authorisation had been issued.

The file had reached a conclusion about what the records meant.

It had not resolved the original communication.

“Who made this decision?” Sophie asked.

Edward scrolled down.

The signature field was absent.

Instead, the final line read:

**Decision released under the authority of the Programme Authority Office.**

No individual name.

No signature.

No personal approval reference.

“Again,” Sophie said quietly.

“Again.”

At 11:30, Edward called the Programme Authority Office.

The administrative secretary answered.

“Thank you for releasing the outcome extract,” Edward said. “I need the individual decision record.”

“The extract reflects the approved outcome.”

“Who approved it?”

“The Programme Authority Office.”

“Which officer?”

“The office acts under the Chair's authority.”

“Was the Chair the decision-maker?”

“I cannot confirm that from the release record.”

“Was another officer delegated authority?”

“That information is contained in the restricted approval schedule.”

“Can that schedule be released?”

“Not without a separate disclosure review.”

Edward took a breath.

“Then please explain how the outcome was approved.”

“The review findings were submitted to the originating office. The office confirmed the administrative direction.”

“Was that confirmation written?”

“Yes.”

“By whom?”

“The approval schedule identifies the authorising officer.”

“Then we need the schedule.”

“I can submit the request.”

“Please do.”

Edward ended the call.

Sophie looked at him.

“Do you think the Chair made the decision?”

“I don't know.”

“She holds the register, the sealed authorisation, and the preservation package.”

“Those are custody responsibilities. They don't automatically make her the decision-maker.”

“Could she have been?”

“Possibly. But we need the actual authority.”

He wrote another request.

This time, he included a specific question.

**Please identify whether the day-190 administrative direction was approved by the Programme Authority Chair personally, by a delegated officer, or through a collective office decision.**

He sent it.

At 13:15, Edward received an unexpected call from Peter Langford.

“I understand you've received the AR-164/09 extract.”

“Yes.”

“I wanted to clarify the administrative consequences.”

Edward waited.

“The outcome confirms that routine closure activity may continue within existing individual appointments.”

“It also confirms that the control function remains unfilled.”

“Yes.”

“Was a successor appointed after the day-190 review?”

“No.”

“Was one proposed?”

“Not through my office.”

“Did you request a suspension of the control function?”

“No.”

Edward looked at the chronology.

“Then the position remains as it was on day 165.”

“Correct.”

“Why?”

Peter was silent.

“Mr Langford?”

“The Programme Authority Office determined that the underlying authority reconciliation needed to be completed before any further restricted appointment could be considered.”

“That doesn't explain why you did not request a formal suspension.”

“I believed the existing arrangement was sufficient for ordinary closure work.”

“Your own day-165 response said that the shared desk could not replace the vacant control function.”

“I did not say it could.”

“But you allowed ordinary closure activity to continue.”

“Within existing authority.”

Edward looked at the day-190 outcome.

“Did you receive the review findings before they were released?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Day 191.”

“Were you asked to approve them?”

“No.”

“Were you asked to nominate a successor?”

“No.”

“Were you asked to suspend the control function?”

“No.”

Edward paused.

“So the review concluded that no one had authority to fill the vacancy without a valid originating nomination. But your office was not asked to provide one.”

“That is correct.”

“Why not?”

“I cannot answer for the Programme Authority Office.”

“Did you ask them?”

“I received the outcome extract.”

“That's not what I asked.”

Peter exhaled.

“No. I did not ask them to identify the individual decision-maker.”

Edward made a note.

“Why?”

“Because the review was not directed at my appointment responsibilities.”

“It concerns a control function that became vacant under your supervision.”

“I understand that.”

“Then why didn't you ask?”

Peter's answer came after a long pause.

“Because I believed the review had already settled the matter.”

Edward looked at Sophie.

She had stopped typing.

“What did you believe it had settled?” Edward asked.

“That the original authority remained valid, that the C-19 entry did not create a nomination, and that the independent verification function must remain unfilled until a competent authority acted.”

“That is what it settled.”

“Yes.”

“But it did not settle who would act.”

“No.”

“Or when.”

“No.”

Peter's voice sounded tired.

“I understand the distinction now.”

Edward ended the call without further comment.

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was reviewing a foundation report when Alexander entered.

“Adrian sent the latest investigation update,” he said.

Victoria closed the report.

“What have they established?”

“An originating instruction existed. It was validly issued. It did not appoint anyone.”

“And the communication procedure?”

“Still active. The protected principal's personal acknowledgement remains outstanding.”

Victoria looked down at the folder.

“Does that mean the person never received the information?”

“No. It means the record does not establish receipt.”

She nodded.

The distinction was difficult, but she understood it.

For years, she had wanted a simple answer.

A name.

A date.

A document that could finally connect the missing child to the family.

Instead, every new record seemed to establish what could not be concluded.

Alexander sat opposite her.

“Adrian is keeping the identity investigation separate from the procedural review.”

“Good.”

“I've told him not to treat the existence of P-04 as evidence that P-04 is our daughter.”

Victoria looked at him.

“Thank you.”

He hesitated.

“The review may eventually identify who initiated the communication procedure.”

“Will it identify the child?”

“Not necessarily.”

Victoria closed the folder.

“Then we must be prepared for both possibilities.”

Alexander nodded.

He knew what she meant.

They might find the person who had initiated the communication procedure without finding their daughter.

They might find a valid communication without learning whether it had reached its intended recipient.

They might discover that someone had acted with good intentions but without the authority to make the decision.

The family could not turn any of those possibilities into certainty.

At 16:40, Edward received the approval schedule.

It was three pages long.

The first page identified the approving authority.

**Approving office:** Programme Authority Office.

**Authorising officer:** Office of the Chair.

**Approval method:** Written confirmation.

**Individual signature:** Held separately.

The second page listed the scope of the approval.

The third page contained a reference to the underlying written confirmation.

**Approval reference:** PAO/190/CONF-2.

Edward checked the document index.

The confirmation had been created on day 190 at 16:32.

The outcome extract had been released the following morning.

He requested the original confirmation.

The response was immediate.

**Document available for restricted review.**

Edward opened the first page.

It contained only two sentences.

*The findings of AR-164/09 are accepted for administrative effect within the stated scope.*

*No further substantive authority is created, extended or transferred by this confirmation.*

There was no signature visible.

Only a reference to a sealed approval certificate.

Edward looked at the footer.

**Authorising instrument retained under personal authority schedule PA-190/2.**

Sophie searched the index.

A result appeared.

**PA-190/2 — Personal Authority Schedule.**

**Custodian:** Dr Eleanor Vale.

**Relationship to OI-07:** Supporting authority record.

**Relationship to AR-164/09:** Approval confirmation.

**Release condition:** Originating authority confirmation.

Edward read the fields again.

The same custodian held the original OI-07 authorisation.

The same custodian held the approval certificate for the day-190 review.

That was not necessarily suspicious.

It was, however, significant.

“Two separate sealed records,” Sophie said.

“Both under Eleanor's custody.”

“And both connected to the same originating authority.”

Edward opened the custody history.

The day-190 approval certificate had been transferred to Eleanor at 17:04.

Thirty-two minutes after the confirmation was created.

The transfer had been authorised by the Programme Authority Office.

But the identity of the person who signed the approval remained sealed.

Edward checked the next line.

**Custody instruction:** Preserve separately from the originating personal authority record. Do not merge, substitute or infer common authorship.

He sat back.

The records were deliberately being kept separate.

The OI-07 instruction and the AR-164/09 approval might have been issued under the same authority.

But the custody instruction explicitly prohibited treating them as having the same author.

Edward understood why the restriction existed.

Someone had anticipated that investigators might assume the person who authorised the original review was also the person who approved its final outcome.

The records had been separated to prevent that assumption.

He looked at Sophie.

“We need the two authorisation certificates.”

“Both?”

“Yes. And the underlying authority references.”

She began preparing the request.

Edward added a final note.

**Please do not consolidate the records into a single authorisation summary. Their separate authorship and approval status must remain identifiable.**

At 17:26, the request was submitted.

At 18:03, Eleanor Vale called.

“I've received your request for PA-190/2 and the original OI-07 authorisation.”

“Can you release them?”

“Not yet.”

“Has the originating office refused?”

“No. It has asked for a clarification.”

“Which clarification?”

“Whether the request seeks to establish common authorship between the two records.”

Edward glanced at Sophie's screen.

“That isn't what we asked.”

“I know. But the custody instruction specifically requires that distinction to be preserved.”

“We want to identify the author of each record separately.”

“Then I can confirm that the originating office has been asked to consider the request on that basis.”

Edward paused.

“Dr Vale, may I ask you something?”

“Of course.”

“Have you ever been told who signed either certificate?”

“No.”

“Have you ever been given access to the contents?”

“No.”

“Then how do you know they are separate?”

“Because the custody records identify them as separate instruments with separate release conditions.”

“Do you believe they were issued by the same person?”

Eleanor's answer was immediate.

“I have no basis to believe that.”

Edward looked at the chronology.

“Thank you.”

After the call, he remained seated.

The day-190 review had finally produced a substantive conclusion.

The conclusion had confirmed that the original instruction was valid, that C-19 had no nomination authority, that OC-19 remained unfilled and that PR-06 remained active.

But the person who authorised the original review was still unidentified.

The person who approved the final outcome was also unidentified.

And the two identities were being deliberately kept separate.

Edward closed the file.

On the last page, he wrote:

**The review is complete. The authority behind the review is not yet established.**

Then he added another line.

**No inference of common authorship is permitted.**

He placed the notebook beside the sealed request.

For once, the next step was not to search for another administrative route.

It was to wait for two separate authorities to identify themselves.
