# **Chapter 11 — The Meeting at the Archive**

At nine in the morning, Richard Valmont received a call from the legal archive.

He listened without interrupting.

When the caller finished, he asked only one question.

“Are you certain the notation is original?”

The answer came after a brief pause.

“Yes, Mr. Valmont. It appears to have been written before the envelope was sealed. We have photographed it and included it in the evidence record.”

Richard thanked the custodian and ended the call.

He remained seated behind his desk.

For several seconds, he looked at the telephone.

Then he pressed the intercom.

“Ask Edward and Sebastian to come to my office.”

Edward arrived first.

“What happened?”

Richard handed him the printed photograph.

Edward read the handwritten sentence.

His expression changed.

“Who found this?”

“The archive technician. It was hidden beneath the paper binding.”

Edward read it again.

**If the mother has not received the child, do not deliver this document to the family office.**

“Someone knew the child had not been delivered,” he said.

“Or suspected it.”

Sebastian entered.

“What are we looking at?”

Edward passed him the photograph.

Sebastian read it silently.

“Then the document was not intended for the family office.”

“Apparently not,” Richard said.

Sebastian looked at the bottom of the photograph.

“There's no signature.”

“No.”

“Could it have been written by Charles Vale?”

“We don't know.”

Edward returned to the desk.

“The wording is important. It says the child had not been received. It doesn't say the child was missing.”

Richard looked at him.

“Explain.”

“If someone wrote this after the hospital incident, they may have believed the child was being kept somewhere. If they thought the baby had simply disappeared, they would probably have used different wording.”

Sebastian folded his arms.

“Or the person was being deliberately vague.”

“That's possible.”

Richard picked up the phone.

“Contact Jonathan Pierce. Tell him the new notation must be included in today's record.”

Edward nodded.

“Should we inform Alexander Aurelius?”

Richard considered it.

“He will see it when we open the sleeve.”

“And if he already knows?”

Richard's expression hardened.

“Then we will hear what he has to say.”

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander was reading the same photograph.

Adrian stood beside his desk.

Damien had arrived a few minutes earlier and was examining the archive report.

Victoria sat near the window.

The atmosphere was unusually tense.

“Who found it?” Alexander asked.

“The archive technician,” Adrian replied. “The custodian forwarded the image to all authorised parties.”

Alexander placed the photograph down.

“Did they confirm when it was written?”

“Not yet. The handwriting and ink will need examination.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“Does it mean someone knew where our daughter was?”

“Possibly,” Adrian said.

“Or that someone knew she hadn't reached us,” Damien added.

Victoria looked at him.

“Is there a difference?”

“Yes.”

Damien spoke carefully.

“If the person knew where she was, they may have been directly involved in keeping her away. If they only knew she hadn't been delivered, they might have been trying to prevent the information from reaching the wrong people.”

Victoria looked back at the sentence.

“Either way, someone knew.”

Alexander nodded.

“That's what we need to establish.”

Julian appeared at the doorway.

He had been told that the adults were discussing the old records, but he had not been invited to the meeting.

“Is everyone busy?”

Lucien, who had been standing behind him, answered.

“Apparently.”

Julian looked at his father.

“Are you going to the meeting today?”

“Yes.”

“Will you find out something?”

Alexander looked at his youngest son.

“We hope so.”

Julian hesitated.

“Will they tell you where she is?”

The question was simple.

The answer was not.

“We don't know yet,” Alexander said.

Julian nodded.

“Okay.”

He turned to leave.

Then he stopped.

“If you find out anything, please tell Mother first.”

Victoria's expression softened.

“I will be there, sweetheart.”

Julian gave her a small smile and left with Lucien.

When the door closed, Victoria looked at Alexander.

“He understands more than we think.”

Alexander said nothing.

He was looking at the photograph.

The meeting was scheduled for two in the afternoon at the offices of Vale International Legal Group.

The building was modern, discreet and considerably less imposing than either the Valmont or Aurelius headquarters.

It had been selected because the legal archive was held there and because its records custodian could supervise the opening of the supplementary sleeve.

Richard arrived with Edward and Sebastian.

Jonathan Pierce was waiting in the reception area.

“Mr. Valmont.”

“Mr. Pierce.”

They shook hands.

“Has the archive been prepared?”

“Yes. The original file is in the examination room. The custodian has also arranged for the document to be photographed and recorded during opening.”

Richard nodded.

“Good.”

Edward looked around.

“Are the Aurelius representatives here?”

“Not yet.”

“They're punctual,” Sebastian remarked.

Richard gave him a warning glance.

A few minutes later, the reception doors opened.

Alexander Aurelius entered with Adrian and Damien.

The atmosphere changed immediately.

Richard stepped forward.

“Mr. Aurelius.”

“Mr. Valmont.”

They shook hands.

It was a polite greeting between two men who understood the importance of appearances.

But neither was particularly interested in appearances today.

Alexander glanced at Edward.

“Mr. Valmont.”

“Mr. Aurelius.”

Adrian greeted Sebastian.

Damien exchanged brief nods with Edward.

Jonathan stepped forward.

“Thank you for attending. The archive custodian is ready.”

Alexander nodded.

“Then let's begin.”

The examination room contained a long table, several chairs and a glass cabinet.

A security camera had been positioned above the table.

The original file rested inside a transparent evidence sleeve.

Beside it lay the supplementary envelope.

The senior custodian, a woman named Ms. Eleanor Shaw, introduced herself and explained the procedure.

“Before we proceed, I need to confirm that all parties understand the restrictions. The original file will remain within the examination room. Every document will be photographed before and after handling. No material may be removed without written authorisation.”

Alexander nodded.

Richard did the same.

Ms. Shaw placed the supplementary envelope in the centre of the table.

“This is the outer sleeve. Its seal was broken before the file entered our current archive system. The secondary document sleeve remains sealed.”

She pointed to the small handwritten notation.

“We also discovered this instruction beneath the binding.”

Edward placed the photograph beside the envelope.

Alexander read the sentence.

His face became very still.

Victoria was not present.

She had wanted to attend, but Alexander had asked her to wait until the document could be examined. He had promised to bring her the complete record.

Now he regretted not bringing her.

“Proceed,” he said.

Ms. Shaw photographed the envelope.

Then she carefully removed the secondary sleeve.

It was smaller than expected, made of thick cream-coloured paper and sealed with a dark red wax stamp.

The stamp bore the emblem of the former legal office.

Ms. Shaw examined it.

“The seal is intact.”

“Can it be opened?” Richard asked.

“Yes. However, I must first document its condition.”

She photographed the front, back and edges.

Then she placed it beneath a small examination lamp.

After several minutes, she looked up.

“There is no evidence of tampering.”

Alexander nodded.

Ms. Shaw cut the edge of the sleeve.

Inside was a folded letter, a smaller sheet of paper and a plastic identification pouch.

Everyone became silent.

The custodian removed the contents one by one.

The letter was placed on the table.

The smaller sheet was placed beside it.

The identification pouch remained sealed.

Edward leaned forward.

“Is that a hospital identification record?”

“It appears to be,” Ms. Shaw replied. “We will examine it separately.”

Alexander looked at the letter.

“Read it.”

Ms. Shaw glanced at him.

“Would you prefer to read it yourself?”

Alexander paused.

Then he looked at Richard.

Richard nodded.

Ms. Shaw handed him the letter.

Alexander unfolded it.

The paper was old but well preserved.

The handwriting was neat.

He began reading.

**To the mother of the child,**

**If this letter has reached you, then the arrangements made for your daughter have not been carried out as intended.**

**I cannot explain everything in writing. The person who arranged the transfer has access to the hospital administration, the legal office and the family records. I have reason to believe that the original instructions have been changed.**

**Your daughter was not delivered to the people who were meant to receive her.**

**The second child was recorded under the wrong identity.**

**I do not know whether this was done to protect one child, to conceal another matter, or to fulfil an arrangement that should never have existed.**

**I have retained the original identification record. It is enclosed separately.**

**Do not rely on the hospital's revised records. Do not rely on the family-office copy. Do not confront the person responsible until you know which child was sent where.**

**I am sorry that I could not bring her to you myself.**

**If you are reading this, then I have failed to deliver what I promised.**

**Please find your daughter.**

The letter ended without a signature.

Nobody spoke.

Richard slowly lowered the page.

Edward stared at the words.

Sebastian looked toward Alexander.

Adrian had gone completely still.

Damien was examining the identification pouch.

Finally, Alexander spoke.

“Read it again.”

Ms. Shaw looked at him.

“Sir?”

“The second paragraph. Read it again.”

She repeated the sentence.

*Your daughter was not delivered to the people who were meant to receive her.*

Alexander closed his eyes briefly.

For eighteen years, the family had believed their daughter had been taken from the hospital or lost during the transfer process.

Now they had a letter suggesting something more specific.

She had not been delivered.

The transfer had not been completed.

And the second child had been recorded under the wrong identity.

Richard looked at Edward.

“Two children.”

Edward nodded.

“The records were altered for both.”

Sebastian's expression darkened.

“Then the exchange may not have been accidental.”

Alexander opened his eyes.

“Not may.”

He looked at the letter.

“Someone changed the instructions.”

Ms. Shaw lifted the identification pouch.

“Would you like me to examine this?”

Alexander nodded.

She photographed the pouch and carefully opened it.

Inside was a small hospital identification card.

It had yellowed with age.

The printed information was faint, but still readable.

The card contained:

- A newborn registration number.
- A date of birth.
- A recorded time.
- A hospital ward designation.
- A handwritten note.

Edward leaned forward.

“Can we see it?”

Ms. Shaw placed the card beneath the examination light.

The recorded time was 2:17 a.m.

Edward recognised it immediately.

“That matches the first birth entry.”

Richard moved closer.

“What does the handwritten note say?”

Ms. Shaw adjusted the light.

The writing was difficult to read.

She enlarged the image on her screen.

A few words became clear.

**Mother: V. Aurelius**

**Child: Female**

**Transfer: Pending**

Alexander stared at the card.

For a moment, the room seemed to disappear around him.

Victoria's name.

His daughter's original identification.

A record from the night she was born.

He reached toward the card, then stopped.

“Is this original?”

Ms. Shaw nodded.

“The paper, printing and registration format are consistent with the hospital's historical records. We will need expert examination to establish authenticity conclusively, but it appears genuine.”

Alexander looked at Adrian.

“Take a photograph.”

Adrian did not move.

“Father?”

“Take a photograph. Send it to your mother.”

Adrian nodded and carefully photographed the card.

Richard looked at Edward.

“Does the time match the hospital report?”

“Yes.”

“And the second birth?”

“Five-forty-six.”

Edward looked at the identification card again.

“If this is genuine, then the first child was registered under Victoria Aurelius's name at 2:17.”

Sebastian frowned.

“But what about the second child?”

Ms. Shaw examined the remaining papers.

There was another small document inside the pouch.

It appeared to be a transfer notation.

The paper contained a different registration number.

The time was 5:46 a.m.

The handwritten section was partly smudged.

Only a few words were readable.

**Mother: C. Valmont**

**Child: Female**

**Transfer: Pending**

Catherine's initials.

Richard looked at the page.

His expression changed.

“That's Catherine's name.”

Edward examined the registration number.

It matched the second entry from the hospital records.

For several seconds, nobody moved.

Two children.

Two mothers.

Two original registration numbers.

And a letter claiming that the children had been recorded under the wrong identities.

The room felt smaller.

Richard looked at Alexander.

“Mr. Aurelius, I believe we need to discuss this privately.”

Alexander nodded.

“Yes.”

The meeting was paused.

Ms. Shaw placed the documents back into their protective sleeves.

No one was permitted to leave with copies of the original records. Certified digital images would be distributed to the authorised parties after the examination report was completed.

Richard and Alexander moved into a separate conference room.

Edward, Sebastian, Adrian and Damien remained behind.

For several minutes, nobody spoke.

Then Sebastian broke the silence.

“Your family had an original record showing Victoria Aurelius as the mother at 2:17.”

Adrian nodded.

“Yes.”

“And the hospital's revised record lists a different identity?”

“Yes.”

Edward looked between them.

“The hospital's surviving report shows the birth entries at 2:17 and 5:46, but the original transfer record is missing.”

Damien leaned against the wall.

“Then the original identification records were separated from the revised records.”

“Someone must have had access to both,” Edward said.

Adrian nodded.

“Which suggests the person had access to the maternity ward and the hospital administration.”

Sebastian folded his arms.

“Margaret mentioned a man speaking to the administrator.”

Edward looked at him.

“Yes. But we still don't know whether he was responsible for the changes or merely involved in the transfer.”

Adrian glanced at the closed conference-room door.

“Does your family know anything about the Aurelius file?”

“Not yet,” Edward said.

“Your father?”

“Only what we've discovered through the investigation.”

Adrian looked at him carefully.

“Then why did your family start investigating?”

Edward hesitated.

“Because one of the children was raised in our household.”

Adrian's expression changed.

“One of them?”

Edward nodded.

“Isabelle is Richard and Catherine Valmont's biological daughter. Elena was raised as their daughter, but the DNA results showed she isn't biologically related to them.”

Adrian stared at him.

“What's her name?”

“Her name is Elena.”

The name meant nothing to him.

Not yet.

He had never met her.

He had never seen her photograph.

He did not know what she looked like, what she liked or what she was studying.

He knew only that an eighteen-year-old girl had been raised by the Valmont family.

And that the original hospital records might explain why.

In the conference room, Richard and Alexander sat opposite each other.

The atmosphere was controlled but tense.

Richard spoke first.

“My family has two daughters. One is biologically ours. The other was raised as ours.”

Alexander listened.

“Which one was born at 2:17?”

“We don't know yet. The DNA results confirm that Elena is not biologically related to my wife or me. Isabelle is.”

Alexander looked at him.

“Then Isabelle is your biological daughter.”

“Yes.”

“And Elena?”

“She was raised in our family from infancy.”

Alexander lowered his gaze.

The letter lay between them.

“Your hospital records show that your daughter was born at 5:46.”

“According to the revised records, yes.”

“And mine at 2:17.”

“Yes.”

Richard leaned forward.

“Mr. Aurelius, I think we need to consider the possibility that the children were deliberately switched.”

Alexander's eyes remained on the letter.

“I have considered it.”

“Then we should establish the identities through independent testing.”

“Agreed.”

Richard paused.

“There is another matter.”

Alexander looked up.

“Go on.”

“The girl we raised as our daughter has no idea what this investigation may uncover. She is eighteen years old. She has already had to deal with the discovery that she is not biologically related to us.”

Alexander's expression softened slightly.

“What is her name?”

“Elena.”

Alexander repeated it quietly.

“Elena.”

Richard nodded.

“I would prefer that we do not involve either girl until we have verified the records.”

“I agree.”

“Neither of them should become the subject of rumours or speculation.”

“Agreed.”

Alexander looked down at the document.

“But if the evidence confirms that one of the girls is my daughter, I will want to meet her.”

Richard held his gaze.

“If the evidence confirms it, I will not prevent that.”

For the first time, the two men seemed to understand each other's position.

They were not merely investigating an old legal matter.

They were two fathers who might have spent eighteen years raising the wrong children.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was sitting in the garden with Isabelle.

They had brought their books outside and were taking advantage of the afternoon weather.

Isabelle was reading an article on university accommodation.

Elena was working through a set of practice questions.

“Do you think university housing is worth it?” Isabelle asked.

Elena looked up.

“Depends on the university.”

“Some of the options are quite expensive.”

“Then compare them with private accommodation.”

“Wouldn't that be more complicated?”

“Not necessarily. You just need to check the location, transport and contract terms.”

Isabelle smiled.

“You make everything sound like a financial report.”

“Because I like knowing what I'm agreeing to.”

“That's fair.”

Elena returned to her questions.

A few minutes passed.

Then Isabelle spoke again.

“Do you think we'll still be living here next year?”

Elena looked up.

“Why wouldn't we?”

“I don't know. I suppose things might change.”

Elena considered it.

“Maybe. But I don't think we need to decide that now.”

Isabelle nodded.

She looked toward the mansion.

“Mother seems worried.”

“She's been worried for days.”

“Do you think she knows something?”

“Probably.”

“About the investigation?”

“Yes.”

Isabelle closed her notebook.

“Do you ever feel like everyone knows something except us?”

Elena smiled faintly.

“Every day.”

They laughed.

Then Elena's phone vibrated.

A message from Nicholas appeared.

**Nicholas:** Are you in the garden?

**Elena:** Yes.

**Nicholas:** Good. I need you to settle an argument.

**Elena:** What argument?

**Nicholas:** Edward says I can't borrow his car.

Elena stared at the screen.

**Elena:** Why would you ask me?

**Nicholas:** Because you're reasonable.

**Elena:** What did you do?

**Nicholas:** Nothing.

**Elena:** Nicholas.

**Nicholas:** I may have borrowed it last week without telling him.

Elena put a hand over her face.

Isabelle laughed.

The ordinary world continued.

Neither girl knew that, several kilometres away, a meeting had uncovered the possibility that one of them had been born into a different family.

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian returned home shortly before six.

Victoria was waiting in the sitting room.

She stood as soon as she saw him.

“Did you find anything?”

Adrian nodded.

“Yes.”

He handed her the photograph of the original identification card.

Victoria took it.

For several seconds, she did not move.

Then she read the words.

**Mother: V. Aurelius**

**Child: Female**

**Transfer: Pending**

Her hand began to tremble.

“Is this real?”

“The archive custodian believes it is authentic. The original is undergoing examination.”

Victoria sat down.

“That's her.”

Adrian knelt beside her chair.

“Mother…”

“That's her record.”

He did not correct her.

She held the photograph carefully.

For eighteen years, she had searched for a child whose original hospital record she had never seen.

Now she had a piece of paper that appeared to identify her.

A daughter.

A birth time.

A hospital registration number.

It was not proof of where the child had gone.

It was not proof that she was alive.

But it was more than Victoria had possessed yesterday.

“Did they find the second record?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“Who was the mother?”

“Catherine Valmont.”

Victoria looked at him.

“The Valmont family?”

“Yes.”

“Do they have a daughter?”

Adrian nodded.

“Two girls. One is their biological daughter. The other was raised by them.”

Victoria looked back at the photograph.

“What is the other girl's name?”

Adrian hesitated.

“Elena.”

Victoria repeated the name.

“Elena.”

She looked toward the window.

“Do we know which girl was born at 2:17?”

“Not yet.”

Victoria closed her eyes.

“Then we must find out.”

At seven-thirty, Alexander returned home.

He found Victoria in the sitting room with the photograph still in her hand.

He sat beside her.

“Richard Valmont and I have agreed to arrange independent DNA testing.”

Victoria nodded.

“Good.”

“We will not make any assumptions until the results are confirmed.”

“I understand.”

Alexander looked at the photograph.

“The record is consistent with our daughter's original birth registration.”

Victoria touched the edge of the paper.

“Then she was there.”

“Yes.”

“She was alive. She was born. Someone recorded her.”

Alexander took her hand.

“Yes.”

Victoria turned toward him.

“Did they say where she went?”

“No.”

“Did they find the person who changed the records?”

“No.”

She looked down.

“Then we still don't have her.”

Alexander did not try to soften the truth.

“No.”

Victoria leaned against his shoulder.

For a long time, neither spoke.

At the Valmont Estate, Richard had a different conversation with Catherine.

He had waited until the girls were upstairs.

Catherine sat across from him in the private sitting room.

“What did you find?”

Richard placed the archive photographs on the table.

Catherine read the first document.

Then the second.

Her face lost colour.

“Alexander Aurelius?”

“Yes.”

“And the other child?”

“We don't know which girl is which yet.”

Catherine looked at the second identification record.

Her name was written there.

**Mother: C. Valmont**

**Child: Female**

**Transfer: Pending**

She touched the paper.

“That's my record.”

“Yes.”

Catherine looked up.

“Richard, what does this mean?”

“It means the hospital may have changed the original identities of the two children.”

She stared at him.

“Are you saying Isabelle and Elena were switched?”

“I'm saying the evidence strongly suggests that possibility.”

Catherine's eyes filled with tears.

She looked toward the staircase.

“Have you told Elena?”

“No.”

“Isabelle?”

“No.”

Catherine covered her mouth.

“Richard…”

He reached across the table.

“We need independent testing first.”

Catherine nodded, but she was visibly struggling.

“Both girls deserve to know the truth.”

“They will.”

“And if Elena is the Aurelius child?”

Richard did not answer immediately.

Catherine looked at him.

“She will have another family.”

“Yes.”

“And they will want her.”

Richard looked down.

“I imagine they will.”

Catherine's voice became very quiet.

“Will she still want us?”

Richard had no answer.

For the first time since the investigation began, the possibility of losing Elena no longer felt like a legal complication.

It felt personal.

Late that night, Elena was standing in front of her bedroom mirror, trying to decide whether to attend a university event on Friday.

She had received the invitation earlier that afternoon.

It was a student networking event, with a few business speakers and an opportunity to meet representatives from several companies.

She was considering whether it would be useful.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Clara.

**Clara:** Are you coming on Friday?

**Elena:** Maybe. Still deciding.

**Clara:** You should. There are some interesting speakers.

**Elena:** I'll think about it.

She placed the phone down.

On her desk was a family photograph.

It showed Elena between Nicholas and Edward, with Richard and Catherine standing behind them. Sebastian was looking away from the camera because Nicholas had apparently said something ridiculous at the last moment.

Elena smiled.

She picked up the frame and straightened it.

Then she placed it back.

She did not know that, in another house, a mother had just read her name for the first time.

She did not know that a father had begun wondering whether the child he had searched for was living only a short distance away.

And she did not know that an independent DNA test was about to be arranged.

For eighteen years, two families had lived with different versions of the same missing child.

One family had raised a daughter.

The other had searched for one.

Now the records were beginning to suggest that the girl they had both been waiting for might be the same person.

But there was still one question nobody could answer.

**Which child had actually been born at 2:17 a.m.?**
