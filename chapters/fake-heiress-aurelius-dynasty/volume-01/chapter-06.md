# Chapter 6 — The Photograph from Eighteen Years Ago

At precisely 7:30 the next morning, Edward Valmont was already awake.

This was not unusual.

What was unusual was that he had been awake since four.

The photograph Margaret Ellis had described occupied his thoughts.

A man who was not hospital staff.

A written instruction.

A newborn deliberately kept away from her family.

There were too many possibilities, and none of them were reassuring.

Edward stood beside his desk, reading the message Daniel had sent at 6:45.

**Margaret Ellis has confirmed the meeting for 9:00 a.m. She has agreed to hand over the photograph and written note.**

Edward typed a short reply.

**Arrange secure evidence handling. Do not circulate copies outside the investigation team.**

He put down his phone.

Then he noticed another message.

It was from Nicholas.

**Nicholas:** Are you coming to breakfast or have you decided to become part of the furniture?

Edward replied.

**Edward:** Breakfast.

**Nicholas:** Good. Elena says you're not allowed to skip meals.

Edward frowned.

**Edward:** Why is Elena giving you instructions?

**Nicholas:** Because someone has to look after us.

Edward shook his head.

For all the uncertainty surrounding their family, Nicholas remained Nicholas.

That was something.

The breakfast room was lively when Edward entered.

Nicholas was attempting to convince Elena that he had been unfairly deprived of strawberries.

Again.

"You're still discussing that?" Edward asked.

"It's a serious matter."

"It was one strawberry."

"It was the principle."

Elena put down her spoon.

"I've offered to buy you a whole box."

"That's not the point."

"Then what is the point?"

Nicholas opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

Edward pulled out a chair.

"An excellent argument."

"Thank you," Nicholas said.

"I wasn't praising you."

Elena laughed.

Across the table, Isabelle was reading through a university information booklet.

Catherine noticed Edward.

"Good morning. You're up early."

"I have a meeting."

"At the office?"

"Not exactly."

Richard looked up from his tablet.

"Something related to the hospital?"

Edward paused.

He hadn't intended to discuss the matter at breakfast.

But Richard had noticed his expression.

"Yes," Edward answered.

Sebastian put down his coffee.

"Have they found something?"

"Possibly."

Richard's attention sharpened.

"What?"

"A former maternity nurse has agreed to hand over some old material."

Edward glanced briefly at Elena and Isabelle.

"I'll explain after I've reviewed it."

Richard nodded.

"All right."

Elena looked at him.

"Is it something important?"

Edward smiled faintly.

"We're trying to find out."

She nodded and returned to her breakfast.

Nicholas, however, was watching Edward.

He knew that expression.

It was the expression Edward wore when he had discovered something unpleasant and was deciding how much of it to share.

At 8:45 a.m., Edward and Daniel arrived at Margaret Ellis's apartment.

Margaret was waiting for them.

She had placed the old envelope on the table.

"Good morning, Mrs. Ellis," Edward said.

"Good morning."

Daniel opened a secure evidence case and placed it beside him.

Margaret looked at it.

"You're going to take everything?"

"Only what you agree to provide," Edward replied. "We'll document each item and give you a copy of the receipt."

She nodded.

Edward sat down.

"May I see the photograph first?"

Margaret removed it from the envelope.

It was small, slightly faded, and had a crease running through one corner.

Edward picked it up carefully.

The photograph showed a hospital corridor.

A clock on the wall indicated 5:21 a.m.

Two nurses stood near a door.

A man in a dark suit was speaking with an administrator.

The man's face was partly turned away from the camera.

But Edward could see enough to notice something.

The man wore a small metallic pin on his lapel.

A circular emblem with a stylised letter inside.

Edward enlarged the photograph using a digital scanner.

"Do you recognise the emblem?" he asked.

Margaret shook her head.

"No."

"Did you see the man again?"

"Once."

"When?"

"Later that morning. He was near the records office."

"Did he speak to you?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

Margaret looked down at her hands.

"He asked whether I had completed the paperwork."

"What paperwork?"

"The newborn transfer documentation."

Edward leaned forward.

"And what did you tell him?"

"That I had not been given the original forms."

"How did he respond?"

Margaret's voice became quieter.

"He said I should not concern myself with it."

Edward exchanged a glance with Daniel.

"Did he identify himself?"

"No."

"Did the administrator introduce him?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No. But everyone seemed to know he was important."

Edward looked again at the photograph.

"Mrs. Ellis, do you remember the instruction?"

She took the folded paper from the envelope.

"I've kept it all these years."

Edward opened it carefully.

The handwriting was neat and deliberate.

*The original records must remain separate.*

*The child must not be returned until further instructions.*

Below the two sentences was a date.

And beneath the date were two initials.

**A.V.**

Edward stared at them.

A.V.

The letters meant nothing to him at first.

Then he looked at the emblem on the man's lapel.

The same letters appeared inside the circular design.

He slowly placed the note on the table.

"Mrs. Ellis, did you ever find out who this man was?"

"No."

"Did you tell anyone about this?"

Margaret hesitated.

"I told the administrator that I was uncomfortable with what had happened."

"And?"

"He told me the matter was being handled."

"Who was the administrator?"

"Dr. Samuel Whitmore."

Edward made a note.

"Where is he now?"

Margaret's expression changed.

"He died."

"When?"

"Eleven years ago."

Edward nodded slowly.

There was another silence.

Then Margaret spoke.

"Mr. Valmont, I want you to understand something."

Edward looked up.

"I didn't know a baby had been taken."

"All right."

"I never saw anyone hurt either child. I didn't know what the instruction meant. I thought it was some kind of legal matter."

Edward remained quiet.

Margaret's eyes became wet.

"I was afraid of losing my job. I was young, and I had a family. When the administrator told me not to ask questions, I listened."

Edward set down his pen.

"I am not here to accuse you."

"But I should have said something."

"Perhaps."

He paused.

"But what matters now is that you're helping us establish what happened."

Margaret nodded.

She looked relieved, although not entirely.

Edward placed the photograph and note inside the evidence case.

"Thank you, Mrs. Ellis."

Before leaving, he turned back.

"One more question."

"Yes?"

"Do you remember which child the instruction referred to?"

Margaret closed her eyes.

Then she answered.

"The girl who was supposed to go home with the Aurelius family."

Edward went completely still.

"What did you say?"

Margaret opened her eyes.

"The Aurelius family. I remember the name because the administrator mentioned it."

For a moment, Edward could not speak.

Aurelius.

The name was familiar.

Everyone in the business world knew it.

But why would the Aurelius family be connected to the birth records?

He looked at Margaret.

"Are you certain?"

"I remember hearing it. I cannot promise that I understood everything correctly."

Edward nodded slowly.

That was enough.

He had come looking for evidence of an unexplained hospital error.

He was leaving with a name.

A name that might connect Elena to one of the most powerful families in the world.

Back at Valmont Holdings, Sebastian was reviewing an acquisition proposal when Edward entered his office.

Sebastian looked up.

"You look terrible."

"Good morning to you too."

"Did you find something?"

Edward closed the door.

"Yes."

Sebastian put down his pen.

"What?"

Edward placed the photograph on his desk.

Sebastian examined it.

"Who is this?"

"A man who visited the maternity ward on the night Elena and Isabelle were born."

"Do we know his name?"

"No."

Edward placed the written instruction beside it.

Sebastian read the two sentences.

His expression hardened.

"Someone deliberately separated the records?"

"That's what it appears to suggest."

"Which child?"

Edward looked at him.

"The one who was supposed to go home with the Aurelius family."

Sebastian stared.

"The Aurelius family?"

"Yes."

"Are you certain?"

"The witness remembers hearing the name. It's not conclusive, but the photograph contains an emblem with the initials A.V."

Sebastian picked up the photograph again.

"That could mean anything."

"I know."

"Have you identified the emblem?"

"Not yet."

Sebastian sat back.

"Why would someone from the Aurelius family be at the hospital?"

"That's what we're going to find out."

He opened another file.

"There's something else. The nurse remembers that the original records were taken away from the normal archive."

Sebastian's jaw tightened.

"Taken by whom?"

"We don't know."

"Then we need to find out."

Edward nodded.

For a moment, both brothers remained silent.

The Aurelius name carried considerable weight.

A family whose companies operated across continents, whose investments reached into dozens of industries, and whose private affairs were rarely discussed publicly.

They were not a family one could casually accuse of wrongdoing.

And they were not a family that could be casually ignored.

Sebastian finally spoke.

"Father needs to know."

"Agreed."

Richard was in a conference call when Edward and Sebastian entered his office.

He ended the call after a brief explanation.

"What happened?"

Edward placed the photograph on the desk.

"We met Margaret Ellis."

Richard examined the photograph.

"Who is the man?"

"We don't know. But he was present at the hospital shortly before dawn."

Richard read the handwritten note.

His expression changed.

"Where did she get this?"

"She kept it after the incident."

"What incident?"

Edward explained the meeting.

He described the man, the administrator, the missing records, and Margaret's recollection of the Aurelius family.

Richard remained silent until Edward finished.

Then he asked one question.

"Why would the Aurelius family be involved in our daughter's birth?"

Neither brother answered.

Richard looked at the photograph again.

"Have we verified the initials?"

"Not yet."

"Then do not make assumptions."

"Of course."

Richard stood.

"Contact our external investigators. Quietly. I want the man's identity established before anyone approaches the Aurelius family."

Sebastian nodded.

"And Isabelle?"

Richard paused.

"What about her?"

"She should know that the investigation has uncovered something unusual."

Richard looked at the note.

"Not yet."

Edward frowned.

"Father—"

"We have no confirmed explanation. I will not frighten either girl with a theory."

Edward accepted that.

Richard looked at him.

"Especially Elena."

Edward nodded.

At the Aurelius Global Headquarters, a very different morning was underway.

The building stood in the financial district, surrounded by other corporate towers.

But the Aurelius headquarters occupied an entire block.

Its central tower was connected to two adjoining buildings, one housing the executive offices and the other containing research, legal and administrative divisions.

On the top floor, Alexander Aurelius was reading a report.

He was forty-six years old, but there was very little about his appearance that suggested an ordinary businessman.

His expression was composed.

His questions were precise.

And his employees had learned that he preferred complete answers over reassuring ones.

Across the desk sat Adrian Aurelius, his eldest son.

"Have we received anything from the investigation team?" Alexander asked.

"Nothing conclusive," Adrian replied.

"How long has it been since the last report?"

"Three weeks."

Alexander closed the folder.

"That is unacceptable."

"We've expanded the search to additional private hospitals and regional archives."

"Have we checked the old maternity records?"

"Yes. The team is reviewing them again."

Alexander looked toward the window.

Eighteen years.

Eighteen years since his daughter had disappeared from the hospital records.

Eighteen years since his wife had been told that her newborn daughter could not be located.

Eighteen years of investigations, unanswered questions, false leads and disappointments.

He had never stopped searching.

"Any new information about the hospital staff?" he asked.

Adrian shook his head.

"One former administrator was identified, but he died eleven years ago. We're checking his professional records."

Alexander nodded.

"Continue."

Adrian hesitated.

"Father, Mother wants to know whether there has been any progress."

Alexander's expression softened slightly.

"Tell her the investigation is continuing."

"She'll ask for details."

"Then tell her what we know."

Adrian nodded.

He stood to leave.

"Adrian."

His father stopped him.

"Yes?"

"Do not let the investigation become another exercise in collecting names and documents. I want to know what happened to my daughter."

Adrian's expression became serious.

"We will find her."

Alexander looked down at the report.

"I hope so."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria Aurelius was arranging flowers in the sitting room.

She preferred doing it herself.

The household staff had offered to take over many times, but she enjoyed choosing the flowers and deciding where they should be placed.

On the table beside her lay an old photograph.

It showed a newborn baby wrapped in a pale blanket.

The picture had been taken shortly after the birth.

Victoria looked at it every morning.

A knock sounded.

"Come in."

Her youngest son entered.

He was sixteen, tall for his age, and carried a schoolbag over one shoulder.

"Mother?"

Victoria looked up.

"Good morning, sweetheart."

"Have you seen my headphones?"

"On the table near the library."

He looked toward the hallway.

"Thanks."

Then he noticed the photograph.

"You're looking at it again."

Victoria smiled faintly.

"Yes."

He approached the table.

"Do you think we'll find her?"

Victoria's fingers rested against the photograph.

"I hope so."

"Adrian says the investigators are still working."

"They are."

"Then why haven't they found anything?"

Victoria looked at her son.

"Because sometimes the truth is difficult to find."

He frowned.

"That's not fair."

"No. It isn't."

He sat beside her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then he said, "If she comes home, I'll show her the games room."

Victoria laughed softly.

"That is your first priority?"

"Of course. She'll need to know where everything is."

"And what if she doesn't like games?"

"Then I'll show her the library."

Victoria looked at him with affection.

"That sounds better."

He glanced at the photograph.

"Do you think she'll look like us?"

Victoria's eyes became distant.

"I don't know."

Then she smiled.

"But I hope she has a little of your father's stubbornness."

The boy groaned.

"That's not a compliment."

"It is when you need it."

At the Valmont estate, Elena was having a much less serious problem.

She had misplaced her university identification card.

Again.

She searched her bag.

Then her desk.

Then the drawer beside her bed.

Nothing.

"Where did I put it?"

She checked the desk again.

A knock sounded.

"Elena?"

"Come in."

Isabelle entered.

"Are you looking for something?"

"My university card."

"Have you checked your handbag?"

"Yes."

"Your study folder?"

"Yes."

"Your cardigan pocket?"

Elena froze.

Slowly, she reached into the pocket of the cardigan she had worn the previous evening.

The card was there.

She stared at it.

Isabelle smiled.

"Found it?"

Elena held it up.

"Apparently."

"That's good."

"Thank you."

Isabelle looked at the desk.

"Are you going to university?"

"Yes. I have a lecture this afternoon."

"Can I come with you?"

Elena blinked.

"To university?"

"Not to your lecture. I meant, could I visit the campus with you sometime? I'd like to see the facilities."

Elena smiled.

"Of course. I can show you around."

"Thank you."

"Do you know which course you're interested in?"

"Not yet. I want to look at the available options first."

"Then we can visit the admissions office."

Isabelle nodded.

She seemed pleased.

As she turned to leave, Elena spoke.

"Isabelle?"

"Yes?"

"Would you like to have lunch together tomorrow?"

Isabelle smiled.

"I'd like that."

After she left, Elena placed her identification card carefully inside her university folder.

She was beginning to realise that getting to know Isabelle did not require her to forget the life they had both inherited.

They could simply start somewhere.

That evening, Edward returned home with a new concern.

He had spent the afternoon checking the emblem on the photograph.

The initials A.V. appeared on several historical records connected to the Aurelius family's medical foundation.

But there was a problem.

The emblem was not exclusive to the foundation.

It had also been used by a private legal office that had handled confidential family matters nearly two decades earlier.

Edward had found the office's name.

**Aurelius & Vale Private Counsel.**

The firm no longer existed under that name.

It had been absorbed into a larger international legal group.

But its historical files might still be available.

Edward entered the study and called Sebastian.

"We need to speak."

Sebastian arrived a few minutes later.

"What did you find?"

Edward showed him the emblem.

"I identified the design."

Sebastian studied it.

"What is it?"

"A former private legal office associated with the Aurelius family."

Sebastian looked up.

"Associated how?"

"It handled confidential legal matters for the family."

"Does that prove the man was working for them?"

"No. But it gives us a place to begin."

Sebastian examined the photograph once more.

"Do we contact the firm?"

"Not directly. We need the old records first."

"Then what?"

Edward closed the folder.

"Then we find out why someone connected to the Aurelius family was present when Elena and Isabelle were born."

At 10:36 p.m., Elena was preparing to sleep when her phone rang.

It was Nicholas.

"Hello?"

"Are you asleep?"

"No."

"Good. Important question."

Elena sighed.

"What now?"

"Do you remember where I put my headphones?"

Elena stared at the phone.

"You called me to ask where your headphones are?"

"Yes."

"Did you check your room?"

"Obviously."

"Your desk?"

"Yes."

"Your jacket?"

A pause.

Then Nicholas said, "Why would they be in my jacket?"

"Because you put everything in your pockets."

Another pause.

"Found them."

Elena laughed.

"Good night, Nicholas."

"Wait."

"What?"

"Are you all right?"

Her smile softened.

"Yes."

"Really?"

"Really."

"Good."

He hesitated.

"Tomorrow, let's go out for lunch. Just the two of us."

"Why?"

"Because I want to."

Elena smiled.

"All right."

"Good. Don't eat my strawberries."

"I'll try."

"That isn't reassuring."

"Good night."

"Good night, little sister."

The call ended.

Elena placed the phone on her bedside table.

Outside, the mansion was quiet.

She had no idea that a photograph taken eighteen years ago had just connected her name to a family whose power stretched across the world.

She did not know that a mother she had never met still kept her first photograph beside her bed.

She did not know that four brothers she had never spoken to had grown up believing their youngest sister was lost forever.

And she certainly did not know that the name Aurelius had appeared in the investigation.

For now, she was simply Elena.

A university student.

A daughter.

A sister.

And a girl who had no idea that somewhere, an entire family was still waiting for her.

*End of Chapter 6.*
