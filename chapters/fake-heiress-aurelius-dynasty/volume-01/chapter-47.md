# **Chapter 47 — The Record That Was Never Registered**

The name appeared in three separate records.

**Margaret Voss.**

**Margaret Ellison.**

**Victoria A.**

Three identities.

One investigation.

And possibly three different women.

Edward had spent most of the night reviewing the documents Adrian had forwarded. By morning, he had arranged them across the conference table in chronological order.

The first record was a hospital personnel form.

Margaret Voss had worked in the maternity records department. Her employment began several months before the births and ended twenty-eight days afterward.

The second was a temporary guardianship application.

Margaret Ellison had requested permission to receive a newborn child under temporary protective arrangements.

The third was a rental agreement.

Victoria A. had occupied a property prepared for a baby.

All three records had connections to the same private legal office.

Yet none established that the three names belonged to one person.

Jonathan Pierce entered with two coffees.

"You haven't slept."

"I slept."

"For how long?"

Edward took one cup.

"Enough."

"That's not a number."

"Neither is your definition of enough."

Jonathan placed the remaining documents on the table.

"The registry has responded."

Edward looked up.

"About Margaret Voss?"

"About the temporary guardianship application."

He opened the envelope.

The application had been submitted six months before the births.

It was not an adoption application.

It was a request for temporary placement of an infant whose identity and legal parentage had not yet been established.

The applicant had agreed to provide accommodation, basic care, and medical supervision until a permanent guardian could be identified.

There was one unusual condition.

**The applicant must not receive information concerning the biological identity of the infant.**

Edward read the sentence again.

"She requested not to know?"

"Apparently."

"Why would anyone agree to receive a child without knowing who the child belonged to?"

Jonathan shrugged.

"Perhaps she believed the arrangement was temporary."

"Or perhaps she was told that knowing would put the child at risk."

Edward examined the application.

The signature was clear.

**Margaret Ellison.**

Below it was a handwritten reference.

**External personnel verification: M. Voss.**

Edward placed the application beside the hospital personnel form.

"Two names connected before the children were born."

"Yes."

"That doesn't mean they're the same person."

"No. But it means the connection wasn't created by the later payment records."

Edward looked at the date.

Six months before the births.

Someone had been preparing a temporary receiving arrangement long before the hospital records were altered.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was attempting to conduct a practice interview.

Her interviewer was Nicholas.

This had been a mistake.

"Why do you want to study in Singapore?"

Elena looked at him.

"Because I want to learn in an international academic environment."

Nicholas nodded seriously.

"Good. Next question. Why not study here?"

"Because I applied for an exchange programme abroad."

"That's not a complete answer."

"It wasn't a complete question."

Nicholas put down his imaginary interview notes.

"You're difficult to interview."

"You're difficult to interview with."

"Let's try again."

Elena sighed.

Nicholas leaned forward.

"Tell me about a problem you've solved."

She considered it.

"I helped Isabelle revise the scholarship application guidelines."

"That's not a problem."

"It was before we revised them."

Nicholas laughed.

Elena explained how the original programme assessed students almost entirely through academic results. She had suggested including improvement, financial circumstances, and access to educational resources.

Nicholas listened.

When she finished, he nodded.

"That's a good example."

"Really?"

"Yes. You identified a weakness, considered who might be disadvantaged, and suggested a practical solution."

Elena smiled.

"Thank you."

"Now, what did you learn?"

"That administrative wording can determine whether someone gets an opportunity."

Nicholas stopped smiling.

"That's actually quite good."

"Actually?"

"I mean, very good."

She closed her notebook.

"You're getting better at this."

"I've always been good at this."

"No."

Nicholas laughed.

Their conversation was interrupted by Isabelle, who entered carrying a stack of papers.

"Are you still preparing?"

"Unfortunately," Elena replied.

Isabelle placed the papers on the table.

"I've brought the revised scholarship guidelines."

Elena looked at them.

"You finished already?"

"Almost. Catherine wants me to explain the independent review process at tomorrow's foundation meeting."

Nicholas glanced at the pages.

"How many committees does this programme need?"

"Enough to make sure nobody can quietly favour their friends."

"That's a lot of committees."

"Then perhaps you should volunteer to join one."

Nicholas immediately returned to his coffee.

Elena laughed.

Isabelle sat beside her.

"Could you check the appeal section once more?"

"Of course."

The two girls began reviewing the document.

They were becoming comfortable working together.

Not because the family had instructed them to behave like sisters.

Not because either wanted to prove anything.

They simply had overlapping interests, and each recognised that the other could contribute something useful.

For Elena, that was an unexpected relief.

She did not need to compete with Isabelle every time they occupied the same room.

And Isabelle did not need to turn every opportunity into a comparison.

The temporary guardianship registry was located in an old administrative building on the eastern side of the city.

Edward and Jonathan arrived shortly after eleven.

The records officer, Mr. Howard, had already retrieved the original file.

"I must warn you," he said, "the file is incomplete."

"How incomplete?" Edward asked.

"Several supporting documents were removed during a transfer of records seventeen years ago."

"By whom?"

"We don't know. The transfer was authorised through a legal office."

"Vale Private Counsel?"

Howard checked the cover.

"Yes."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

"Can we see what remains?"

Howard opened the file.

The application was followed by a background assessment, a property inspection, and a preliminary approval.

Margaret Ellison's background assessment was unusually detailed.

She had no recorded criminal history.

No previous guardianship disputes.

No financial irregularities.

She had provided references from two people.

One was a retired physician.

The other was a hospital administrator.

The physician's name was Peter Lang.

Edward recognised it immediately.

Peter Lang had appeared in Daniel Mercer's old correspondence index as a supporting witness.

"Do you have the reference letters?" he asked.

Howard turned several pages.

The physician's letter was present.

It described Margaret Ellison as responsible, financially stable, and capable of providing temporary care.

The hospital administrator's letter was missing.

There was, however, a notation beside the reference field.

**VERIFIED IN PERSON — M. VOSS.**

Edward leaned closer.

"Was this notation part of the original application?"

Howard examined the page.

"It appears so."

"Could we obtain a handwriting comparison?"

"We can arrange that."

Edward pointed to the next section.

"What does this mean?"

The entry read:

**PLACEMENT APPROVED SUBJECT TO ORIGINATING AUTHORITY CONFIRMATION.**

Howard explained.

"The applicant was not authorised to receive a child immediately. Approval was conditional. Someone had to confirm that a specific infant was available for temporary placement."

"Who was responsible for that confirmation?"

"According to the file, the originating representative."

"Name?"

Howard turned the page.

The field was blank.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Again."

Jonathan nodded.

"Another missing name."

Howard closed the file.

"There's one more thing."

He removed a small envelope attached to the back cover.

"This was found inside the file during the last archive inspection. It wasn't indexed."

Edward opened it.

Inside was a single handwritten sentence.

**If the first receiving arrangement fails, the applicant must not be approached again without the mother's written permission.**

There was no signature.

No date.

Only a small mark in the corner.

A circle containing the letters E and V.

Edward recognised the initials.

Eleanor Vale.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander had arranged a meeting with the former head of the Family Office's administrative division.

The man, Thomas Ridley, had retired twelve years earlier.

He arrived carrying a small leather case.

"I was surprised to receive your call," Ridley said.

Alexander gestured towards a chair.

"I understand."

Ridley looked around the room.

"I haven't been here since the old Family Office was reorganised."

"Then perhaps you'll remember more than the current staff."

Ridley smiled faintly.

"I'll try."

Adrian placed several documents on the table.

The first showed the transfer of EFO-SL/07.

The second showed the subsequent use of the authority code.

The third was the old administrative register.

"Do you remember Special Administrative Services?" Adrian asked.

Ridley examined the first page.

"Of course."

"Who operated it?"

"It wasn't a department in the ordinary sense. It was a temporary administrative unit used for sensitive family matters."

"Who supervised it?"

"Originally, the Director of Family Affairs."

"Marcus Morelli?"

"Yes."

"And after he left?"

Ridley looked at the documents.

"That is less clear."

Alexander watched him carefully.

"What do you mean?"

"The office was supposed to close after Morelli's departure. But I remember receiving instructions to preserve its records."

"From whom?"

Ridley hesitated.

"I believe the instruction came from the Chairman's office."

Alexander's expression did not change.

"Did I give it?"

"I can't say, sir."

"Did you speak to me?"

"No."

"Did anyone verify the instruction?"

Ridley looked down.

"Not that I remember."

Adrian leaned forward.

"Then how was it accepted?"

"Those were different times. Some instructions were delivered through sealed internal channels."

"Who could use those channels?"

"The Chairman, the Director of Family Affairs, and certain authorised legal representatives."

Alexander looked at him.

"Could Charles Vale use them?"

Ridley shook his head.

"Not directly."

"Could he arrange for someone else to use them?"

"That would depend on the authorisation."

Adrian placed the transfer record in front of him.

"Does this signature look familiar?"

Ridley examined it.

His expression changed.

"I have seen this before."

"Where?"

"On an internal authorisation form."

"Whose signature is it?"

Ridley looked again.

"I don't know."

He turned the document towards the light.

"But I recognise the way the final letter is written."

Alexander waited.

Ridley continued.

"It resembles the handwriting of the former Deputy Director of Family Affairs."

"Name?"

"Margaret Voss."

The room became silent.

Adrian looked at his father.

Alexander did not move.

"Was Margaret Voss employed by the Aurelius Family Office?" he asked.

Ridley nodded.

"Not permanently. She was seconded to us from the hospital records department."

"Why?"

"She had experience handling maternity and identity records."

"Who requested her appointment?"

Ridley hesitated.

"I believe it was Marcus Morelli."

Alexander leaned back.

"Then she was involved in the Family Office before the children were born."

"Yes."

"How long?"

"Approximately eight months."

Adrian looked at the employment record.

The hospital file had described Margaret Voss as a maternity records clerk.

The Aurelius file described her as a temporary administrative officer.

Neither record mentioned the other.

And both had ended around the same period.

"Do you know where she went after leaving the Family Office?" Adrian asked.

Ridley shook his head.

"She resigned."

"Was the resignation investigated?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Ridley looked uncomfortable.

"Because the Director of Family Affairs handled the matter personally."

Alexander's voice was quiet.

"Then we need to speak to Marcus Morelli again."

Morelli received Edward's call at 3:20 p.m.

He listened without interruption.

When Edward mentioned Margaret Voss, the silence on the other end lasted several seconds.

"Where did you find that name?" Morelli asked.

"In the hospital personnel records."

"That is not where I expected you to find it."

"She worked for you."

"Temporarily."

"She also worked at the Aurelius Family Office."

"Temporarily."

"Why was that arrangement never disclosed?"

"Because it was not relevant to the original investigation."

Edward almost laughed.

"Mr Morelli, it has now appeared in a hospital register, a guardianship application, a rental agreement, and a financial file. I would say it is relevant."

Morelli sighed.

"What do you want to know?"

"Was Margaret Voss the same woman as Margaret Ellison?"

"I don't know."

"Did she receive either child?"

"No."

"Did she arrange the temporary placement?"

"She assisted with the records."

"Did she sign the authorisation documents?"

"Not the original ones."

"Then who did?"

Morelli did not answer.

Edward waited.

Finally, Morelli said, "I will meet you."

"When?"

"Tomorrow."

"At the archive?"

"No."

"Where, then?"

"Somewhere that does not belong to either family."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Send me the location."

Morelli's voice lowered.

"And Edward?"

"Yes?"

"Do not tell the Aurelius family that Margaret Voss was responsible for anything until you know which Margaret Voss you are looking for."

The call ended.

Edward slowly lowered his phone.

Jonathan watched him.

"That sounded deliberate."

"It was."

"Do you think he knows where she is?"

Edward looked at the guardianship application.

"I think he knows why she disappeared from the records."

That evening, Elena received an email from the Singapore programme.

She opened it while sitting in the library.

**International Exchange Programme**

**Subject: Interview Confirmation**

Dear Elena,

We are pleased to confirm your online interview for the International Exchange Programme.

Your interview will take place next week. Please ensure that you have a stable internet connection and a quiet environment.

We look forward to learning more about your academic interests and future plans.

Regards,

Admissions Office

Elena read the email twice.

Then she forwarded it to Clara.

Clara replied with a series of celebratory messages.

A moment later, Nicholas appeared at the library entrance.

"Good news?"

Elena turned the laptop towards him.

"I got the interview confirmation."

Nicholas smiled.

"That's excellent."

"Thank you."

"When?"

"Next week."

"Do you need help preparing?"

Elena raised an eyebrow.

"Are you offering to interview me again?"

"I could."

"Please don't."

"Fine. I'll ask Sebastian."

"Absolutely not."

Nicholas laughed.

He walked over and looked at the confirmation.

"You're going to do well."

Elena closed the email.

"I hope so."

Nicholas paused.

Then he said something she hadn't expected.

"You know, you don't have to make every decision based on what happens with the DNA investigation."

Elena looked at him.

"I know."

"Your future is still yours."

She looked down at her hands.

"Sometimes I forget that."

Nicholas pulled out the chair opposite her.

"Then I'll remind you."

She smiled.

"Not every five minutes."

"Every ten?"

"Every twenty."

"Deal."

The next morning, the Valmont legal office received a call from the temporary guardianship registry.

The records officer had found an additional document.

It had been stored in a separate box under the reference number of Margaret Ellison's application.

Edward arrived within the hour.

The document was a placement assessment.

It described the conditions under which Margaret Ellison would receive a child.

The assessment had been completed before the births.

The applicant had been interviewed twice.

The second interview included a question about whether she would be willing to care for a child without being told the child's biological identity.

Her answer had been recorded.

**I will care for the child as long as necessary. But I will not lie to her if she asks where she came from.**

Edward read the sentence slowly.

It was the first statement in the entire investigation that sounded like it came from someone thinking about the child rather than the arrangement.

Below it was a handwritten addition.

**Applicant understands that contact with the biological mother may not be possible.**

The next line was even more troubling.

**Applicant must not be told that the mother has been informed of the placement.**

Edward looked at the officer.

"Why was this added?"

"We don't know."

"Who wrote it?"

The officer pointed to the bottom of the page.

The signature was incomplete.

Only the initials remained.

**M. V.**

Edward examined the handwriting.

It was different from the disputed rental authorisation.

It was also different from Martha Voss's genuine signature.

He photographed it.

Then he noticed a second sheet beneath the assessment.

It was a copy of an internal communication.

The heading read:

**CONFIDENTIAL — MATERNITY PLACEMENT REVIEW**

The message was dated the sixth day after the births.

It contained only three lines.

*The applicant has been approved.*

*The child has not been assigned.*

*Do not inform the applicant that the child may already be in her care.*

Edward stopped reading.

He looked at the date again.

The sixth day.

The children were still in the private nursery.

Yet the document suggested that someone had already begun preparing a placement.

And the applicant might have been caring for a child without knowing that the child was connected to the case.

He turned the page over.

There was a final handwritten instruction.

**If the child asks about her mother, tell her that the answer is not in the hospital records.**

Edward sat back.

For eighteen years, the investigation had focused on who had taken the children, who had exchanged their identities, and who had concealed the truth.

Now a different possibility was emerging.

Perhaps the most important person in the arrangement had never been told the truth either.

And perhaps the woman who had agreed to care for a child had been deliberately kept ignorant of the very identity she had been trying to protect.
