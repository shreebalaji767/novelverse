# **Chapter 58 — The Woman Who Claimed to Be the Mother**

Alexander read the sentence again.

**SHE CLAIMS TO BE THE MOTHER OF THE CHILD.**

The photograph lay between him and Victoria.

Neither spoke.

Adrian enlarged the image on his tablet.

The woman was standing beside Eleanor Vale in what appeared to be a private drawing room. Her face was turned slightly away from the camera, but her left hand was visible.

A thin scar crossed the base of her thumb.

On her right hand was a silver ring with a dark stone.

Victoria examined the photograph.

"She claimed to be the mother."

"That's what Eleanor wrote," Alexander replied.

"But which child?"

Adrian looked at the date.

"The photograph was taken approximately two months after the ninth-day release."

"Could it have been the woman who received Infant A?" Damien asked.

"Possibly."

"Or the woman who collected the documents?"

Lucien pointed towards the back of the photograph.

"Those might be the same person."

Adrian shook his head.

"We have no proof of that."

Alexander returned to the handwritten sentence.

"Find every surviving document concerning this woman. Not just the hospital records. I want correspondence, payments, property records, legal appointments, everything."

Adrian nodded.

Victoria continued looking at the photograph.

"Why would Eleanor write that sentence?"

Alexander looked at her.

"Because she didn't believe the woman."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

"Or because she wanted someone else to investigate whether it was true."

Edward received the photograph at 8:15 the following morning.

He immediately called Jonathan.

"Get Margaret Voss."

"Again?"

"There's something else she needs to see."

Margaret agreed to meet them that afternoon.

When Edward placed the photograph before her, she recognised the woman immediately.

"That's her."

"The woman you saw at Eleanor's house?"

"Yes."

"Did she tell you her name?"

"No."

"Did Eleanor call her Mrs. Aurelius?"

Margaret nodded.

"Sometimes."

"Did you believe she was an Aurelius?"

"I didn't know."

"Did she ever explain why she was using that name?"

"No."

Edward turned the photograph around.

"Do you recognise the handwriting?"

"Eleanor's."

"Why would she write that this woman claimed to be the mother?"

Margaret studied the sentence.

"Because she had been asking questions."

"What questions?"

"She wanted to know which child had been born at 2:17."

Edward became still.

"Did she say why?"

"She said she had been told that the child was hers."

"By whom?"

"She never said."

"Did Eleanor confirm it?"

"No."

"Did Eleanor deny it?"

Margaret shook her head.

"She told her that nobody had established the biological identity of either child."

Edward looked down at the photograph.

"Then the woman was claiming to be the mother of a child whose identity had never been confirmed."

"Yes."

"Did she accept Eleanor's answer?"

Margaret gave a faint, humourless smile.

"No."

"What did she say?"

Margaret remembered the conversation.

"She said, 'I have waited eighteen years. I am not going to be told that my child is still unidentified.'"

Edward wrote the sentence down.

"Did Eleanor ask her to leave?"

"No. She asked her to sit down."

"Why?"

"Because Eleanor wanted to know what evidence she had."

Edward looked up.

"And did she have any?"

Margaret hesitated.

"She had something."

The Aurelius legal team began a separate investigation into the title *Mrs. Aurelius*.

The family had never maintained a public list of every person authorised to use its name.

There were relatives, former spouses, legal representatives, and individuals who had temporarily acted on behalf of family members.

However, the woman in the photograph did not appear in any of the available records.

Adrian found one possible explanation.

A private representative had been appointed by the Family Office shortly before the births.

The appointment was described as an emergency family liaison.

The representative's name was withheld from the ordinary personnel register.

The authorisation lasted six months.

The document contained a single identifying detail.

**AUTHORISATION ISSUED UNDER THE AUTHORITY OF THE ORIGINATING FAMILY REPRESENTATIVE.**

Alexander examined it.

"I didn't issue this."

"Your authority code appears in the approval field," Adrian said.

"Then it was used without my permission."

Victoria looked at the date.

"Was this before or after the children were born?"

"Four days before."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Who signed the approval?"

Adrian enlarged the scan.

The signature was incomplete.

Only two letters remained visible.

**M. V.**

Victoria looked at him.

"Margaret Voss?"

"Possibly."

Alexander shook his head.

"She was not authorised to appoint family representatives."

Adrian nodded.

"Which means someone gave her authority she wasn't supposed to have."

At the Valmont Estate, Isabelle was reviewing the scholarship programme's first applications.

She had spread several folders across the dining table.

Elena entered carrying two cups of tea.

"Still working?"

"These applications are more complicated than I expected."

Elena placed a cup beside her.

"Good complicated or bad complicated?"

"Both."

Isabelle showed her a form.

"One applicant has excellent academic results but almost no financial difficulty. Another has struggled academically but has improved significantly over the last two years."

"Then the criteria should explain what the programme is trying to achieve."

"That's what I've been trying to establish."

Elena sat down.

"Are you selecting students who need financial help, or students who have the greatest potential to benefit from the programme?"

Isabelle thought about it.

"Both, ideally."

"Then perhaps the panel should consider those separately."

Elena took a sheet of paper and drew two columns.

"Financial circumstances. Academic progress."

She wrote a third heading.

"Personal circumstances."

Isabelle examined the page.

"You're suggesting we assess different things rather than combining everything into one score."

"Yes. Otherwise, someone with a difficult personal situation might receive a low score simply because their examination marks aren't as high."

Isabelle smiled.

"That's actually useful."

Elena shrugged.

"You were already thinking about it."

"But you made it clearer."

They continued reviewing the criteria.

After a while, Isabelle looked towards Elena's laptop.

"Any news from Singapore?"

"Not yet."

"You're checking?"

"Only twice today."

Isabelle laughed.

"That's progress."

Elena smiled.

The two girls returned to their work.

Neither knew that somewhere in the old records, their names had once appeared beside two infant reference numbers that might not have belonged to them.

Edward returned to Margaret Voss's documents.

He had requested the original appointment record for the woman who used the title Mrs. Aurelius.

The surviving copy contained three pages.

The first authorised the representative to communicate with the hospital.

The second permitted her to attend private meetings concerning the newborn children.

The third was a restriction.

**THE REPRESENTATIVE MUST NOT TAKE PHYSICAL CUSTODY OF EITHER INFANT WITHOUT A SEPARATE WRITTEN INSTRUCTION.**

Edward read it carefully.

"She had authority to communicate, but not to take the children."

Jonathan nodded.

"Which means the woman in the photograph was not necessarily a receiving guardian."

"Correct."

"Then why was she asking about the child born at 2:17?"

Edward turned to the final page.

There was a handwritten note.

**THE REPRESENTATIVE HAS BEEN INFORMED THAT THE CHILD'S MOTHER MAY NOT BE THE PERSON WHO CLAIMS THE CHILD.**

Edward stared at it.

"That's unusual."

"Could it refer to Victoria?"

"Possibly."

"Or Catherine."

"Possibly."

Jonathan sat down.

"Every time we think we're getting closer, another person appears who may have been lying."

Edward placed the document aside.

"Perhaps not everyone was lying."

Jonathan looked at him.

"What do you mean?"

"Some of them may genuinely have believed they were protecting the right child. Others may have believed they were following the mother's wishes. Some may have been given false information."

"And some?"

"Some knew exactly what they were doing."

That evening, Margaret Voss telephoned Edward.

Her voice sounded different.

More hurried.

"I found something."

Edward stood immediately.

"What?"

"An old appointment book."

"Where?"

"In a box I didn't give you yesterday."

"What's in it?"

"Names."

"Whose names?"

"People who visited Eleanor."

Edward reached for his coat.

"Don't move anything. I'll come over."

When he arrived, Margaret had placed the appointment book on the dining table.

It was small, leather-bound, and badly worn.

She opened it to a page dated eighteen years earlier.

Three appointments had been written in Eleanor's handwriting.

The first was marked:

**M. MORELLI — 10:00 A.M.**

The second:

**M. VOSS — 1:30 P.M.**

The third:

**MRS. AURELIUS — 4:00 P.M.**

Edward examined the entries.

"Was this the same day as the photograph?"

"Yes."

"Did all three attend?"

"Morelli did. I did. The woman came late."

"How late?"

"Nearly an hour."

"Did you see her arrive?"

"Yes."

"Was she alone?"

Margaret shook her head.

"No."

Edward looked up.

"Who was with her?"

"An older man."

"Do you recognise him?"

"Not clearly. He was wearing a dark suit."

Edward felt a familiar unease.

"Did he have a ring?"

Margaret closed her eyes.

"I remember a ring."

"What kind?"

"Silver. A dark stone."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

The same ring had been described by Catherine years earlier, when she remembered a man approaching her at the hospital.

"Did the man speak to Eleanor?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

Margaret thought carefully.

"He said the arrangement had become too complicated."

Edward waited.

"He said the child should be returned to the mother."

"Which child?"

Margaret shook her head.

"I don't know."

"Did Eleanor agree?"

"No."

"What did she say?"

Margaret's voice became quieter.

"She told him that he had no authority to decide that."

Edward wrote the words down.

"Did the man leave?"

"Eventually."

"And the woman?"

"She stayed."

"How long?"

"Almost two hours."

"Did she take anything with her?"

Margaret nodded.

"An envelope."

"What was inside?"

"I don't know."

Edward examined the appointment book again.

The entry beneath the final appointment had been written in a different hand.

**NO FURTHER MEETINGS WITHOUT CONFIRMATION FROM THE MOTHER.**

He looked at Margaret.

"Who wrote this?"

"Not Eleanor."

"Do you recognise the handwriting?"

Margaret studied it.

Then she shook her head.

"No."

Edward returned to the office with a new question.

The man with the silver ring had appeared in multiple accounts.

He had been seen at the hospital.

He had approached Catherine.

He had attended Eleanor's private meeting.

And he had apparently discussed the return of a child to her mother.

Yet nobody had identified him.

Edward asked the document team to compare the surviving signatures and appointment records.

By the following afternoon, they had found a match.

The man had signed one of the original hospital visitor records.

His name was written in full.

**GABRIEL VALE.**

Edward stared at it.

Jonathan looked over his shoulder.

"Vale?"

"Yes."

"Related to Charles?"

"Not directly, according to the family records."

"Then who was he?"

Edward opened the old personnel file.

Gabriel Vale had been a senior family liaison officer at Vale Private Counsel.

He had worked closely with Charles Vale.

He had also been appointed as a confidential representative to the Aurelius Family Office for a period of six months.

His appointment had begun four days before the births.

It had ended without explanation six months later.

Edward turned to the final page.

There was a handwritten note.

**REPRESENTATIVE REMOVED FOLLOWING DISPUTE CONCERNING MATERNAL IDENTITY.**

Edward read it twice.

Then he looked at Jonathan.

"We've found the man with the ring."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander received the same report.

He recognised the name immediately.

"Gabriel Vale."

Adrian looked at him.

"You know him?"

"I met him twice. He was introduced as a legal liaison."

"Did you authorise him to represent the family?"

"No."

"Then why was he appointed?"

Alexander looked at the document.

"Someone used my authority."

Victoria stood beside him.

"Was he involved in the search for our daughter?"

Alexander shook his head.

"He was supposed to help locate her."

"Then perhaps he knew where she was."

"Perhaps."

Victoria examined the photograph of the woman called Mrs. Aurelius.

"Did he know this woman?"

Alexander studied the image.

"I don't know."

Victoria lowered the photograph.

"Then we need to find him."

Edward located Gabriel Vale through an old professional address.

The building had been converted into private offices.

The receptionist confirmed that Gabriel had not worked there for several years.

However, she remembered that he still received occasional correspondence.

Edward requested the current forwarding address.

The receptionist checked the records.

Then she looked up.

"Mr. Vale no longer receives mail here."

"Do you know where he moved?"

She hesitated.

"I can give you the forwarding address on file."

Edward accepted the paper.

The address belonged to a residential care facility outside the city.

Jonathan looked at it.

"Is he ill?"

"We don't know."

They called ahead.

The facility confirmed that Gabriel Vale was a resident.

He had been admitted eight months earlier.

His records listed a single emergency contact.

Edward asked for the name.

The receptionist checked.

Then she answered.

**Margaret Voss.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

Margaret had been responsible for monitoring the children.

Gabriel had been the representative with the silver ring.

And both were connected to the same confidential arrangement.

But the most troubling detail was yet to come.

The receptionist added:

"Mr. Vale has given strict instructions that he is not to receive visitors from either the Aurelius or Valmont families."

Edward looked at the address again.

"Why?"

The receptionist hesitated.

"Because, according to his instructions, neither family has any right to ask him about the child he took home."
