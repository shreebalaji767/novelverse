# **Chapter 75 — The Mother Who Was Allowed to See a Bracelet**

Isabella Vale remembered the bracelet.

She remembered the narrow white plastic band, the small printed number, and the faint mark on its inner surface.

Most importantly, she remembered what the woman beside her had said.

*You may look at it. You may not take it.*

At the time, Isabella had not understood why a hospital would allow a mother to examine an identification band but refuse to show her the child associated with it.

Eighteen years later, Edward Valmont finally asked her the question nobody had asked before.

"Did you recognise the bracelet?"

Isabella looked at the copy of the Westbridge visitor register.

She had been sitting across from Edward for nearly twenty minutes. She had already confirmed that she visited Westbridge on day 116 with Eleanor Vale.

But the question made her pause.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"How?"

"Because it was mine."

"Your daughter's?"

"My maternity identification band. The one they gave me when I delivered her."

"Did you recognise the number?"

"IV-204."

Edward placed the examination report on the table.

"The band was genuine. It originated from your maternity clinic."

"I know."

He looked at her.

"You knew?"

Isabella gave a small, humourless smile.

"Eleanor told me."

"When?"

"At Westbridge."

Edward waited.

Isabella folded her hands.

"I had spent months asking where my daughter had gone. Charles kept telling me that the records were complicated. Sometimes he said she had died. Sometimes he said she had been transferred for medical care. Eventually, he told me the Aurelius family had claimed one of the children."

"Did you believe him?"

"I didn't know what to believe."

She looked down.

"Then Eleanor contacted me. She said there was an identification matter I needed to see for myself."

"Did she tell you the band belonged to your daughter?"

"No. She said it was connected to my maternity admission."

"Was that enough to make you go?"

"Of course."

Her answer was immediate.

"I had been waiting for someone to give me something real. Not another explanation from Charles. Not another photograph of an unidentified newborn. Something I could recognise."

Edward studied her face.

"What happened when you arrived?"

"Eleanor was already there. A man from the family office was waiting for us. I didn't know his name at the time."

"Adrian Holt?"

"Yes. I remember him now."

"Was the band in his possession?"

"Yes."

"Did he show it to you?"

"He placed it on a table in a small examination room. It was inside a transparent evidence envelope."

"Could you read the number?"

"Yes. IV-204."

"And the initials?"

Isabella looked at him.

"What initials?"

"V.A. They were written inside the band."

She frowned.

"I don't remember seeing those."

"Are you certain?"

"I was looking at the printed number. I didn't turn it over for long."

Edward made a note.

"What did Eleanor tell you?"

Isabella was silent for a moment.

"She told me the band had been found on an infant who had arrived at Westbridge from Meridian."

"Did she say the infant was your daughter?"

"No."

"Did she say it wasn't?"

"No."

"What did she say?"

Isabella looked directly at him.

"She said, 'This proves your child's identification materials were moved. It does not prove where your child is.'"

Edward stopped writing.

That was an unusually careful statement.

"Did she explain how the band had reached Westbridge?"

"She said it had been found on a child whose accompanying documents did not match."

"Did she tell you the child was M-204?"

"Yes."

"Did she say whether M-204 was your daughter?"

"No."

Edward considered the answer.

"Did you ask to see the child?"

"Immediately."

"What happened?"

"Adrian Holt refused."

"On what grounds?"

"He said the child was under medical supervision and that her identity had not been established."

"Did Eleanor support him?"

Isabella nodded.

"She told me that seeing the child would not answer the question. She said I might recognise a face, but that would not establish anything."

Edward remained quiet.

Isabella looked at him.

"I was angry. I thought they were hiding my daughter from me."

"Were they?"

"I don't know."

She took a slow breath.

"But Eleanor said something else. She told me that if I claimed a child without proper identification, I could become part of the same problem that had separated us in the first place."

Edward wrote that down.

It sounded like Eleanor.

She had repeatedly insisted that records must not be merged without verification.

"Did you sign anything that day?" he asked.

"No."

"Did you receive a copy of the examination report?"

"No."

"Did anyone tell you what would happen next?"

"Adrian said the band would be examined and compared with the original maternity records."

"Did he give you a timeframe?"

"He said he would contact me."

"Did he?"

Isabella looked away.

"No."

Edward requested a second meeting with Adrian Holt.

This time, Holt arrived carrying a small notebook.

He looked considerably less comfortable than he had during their previous meeting.

"Isabella Vale says she saw the original band at Westbridge on day 116," Edward began.

"Yes."

"Why wasn't her visit included in the A-17 evidence report?"

"Because she was not an authorised receiving representative."

"She was the mother named on the original maternity record."

"Her biological relationship had not been established through independent testing."

"She had given birth at the clinic."

"Yes."

"She had a maternity identification band."

"Yes."

"And you allowed her to see that band."

Holt nodded.

"At Eleanor's request."

"But you refused to let her see the child."

"Correct."

"Why?"

"Because the child's identity was unresolved."

Edward looked at him.

"Did you believe M-204 might be Isabella's daughter?"

"I considered it a possibility."

"Did you believe it might not be?"

"That was also possible."

"Then why did you allow her to see the band?"

Holt opened his notebook.

"Eleanor believed Isabella had a right to know that her identification material had been recovered."

"Yet she wasn't given the examination report."

"At that point, there was no final report."

"Was she ever given the final report?"

Holt hesitated.

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the comparison was never completed."

Edward placed the receipt on the table.

"You retained the original band and maternity sheet. What prevented you from completing the comparison?"

Holt looked at the document.

"The hospital did not release the complete neonatal register."

"Did you obtain a court order?"

"No."

"Did you ask Isabella to provide consent?"

"No."

"Did you request an independent DNA comparison?"

"No."

"Then what exactly were you waiting for?"

Holt had no immediate answer.

Finally, he said, "I believed the originating family office would provide the missing information."

"Which family office?"

"The Aurelius Family Office."

Edward stared at him.

"Why would the Aurelius Family Office possess Isabella Vale's maternity records?"

Holt lowered his eyes.

"That is something I should have investigated."

The original maternity sheet arrived at the Aurelius estate two days later.

It was delivered in a sealed archival container.

Helena Brooks supervised the opening, and an independent archivist recorded every item removed.

The identification band was still there.

It had been stored in a small transparent evidence sleeve, with the original examination label attached.

The maternity sheet was folded inside a separate envelope.

Edward watched as the archivist unfolded it.

The document was dated eighteen years earlier.

It contained the original admission details from Isabella's maternity clinic.

**Mother: Isabella Vale**

**Admission number: IV-204**

**Delivery time: 2:31 a.m.**

**Infant: Female**

**Birth status: Live birth**

**Temporary identification band: IV-204**

**Initial receiving unit: Neonatal observation**

There was no mention of a death.

No transfer destination appeared in the original field.

But the reverse contained a handwritten notation.

**Transfer to Meridian Children's Medical Centre — Day 6.**

Beneath it was another entry.

**Receiving medical custodian: Eleanor Vale.**

Edward examined the page.

"This confirms the infant associated with IV-204 was transferred to Meridian."

"Yes," Helena replied.

"Does it confirm that M-204 at Westbridge was the same infant?"

"No. Not by itself."

Edward nodded.

The distinction had become almost unbearable, but it was necessary.

The original maternity sheet established the beginning of a documented journey.

It did not establish every step afterward.

He turned the page.

At the bottom, a small box had been left blank.

**Receiving infant identification verified by:**

No name.

No signature.

No date.

"Why was this left blank?" Edward asked.

Helena examined the page.

"I don't know."

The archivist pointed to a faint mark near the lower edge.

"There is something beneath the fold."

Edward leaned closer.

The paper had been folded in such a way that a small handwritten notation was partially concealed.

The archivist carefully flattened it.

Two lines became visible.

**Do not attach maternal identity to temporary receiving number.**

**Await originating comparison.**

Edward looked at the date.

It had been written on the sixth day.

The same day the infant was transferred to Meridian.

The instruction had been issued before the child reached Westbridge.

Which meant the identity separation had begun much earlier than the Northbridge records suggested.

Jonathan arrived with another document shortly afterward.

He placed it beside the maternity sheet.

"Meridian's old transport register."

Edward looked up.

"Where did you find it?"

"Among the archived medical administration records. It was stored separately from the patient files."

The register contained the transfer details for IV-204.

The child had arrived at Meridian on day six.

The receiving medical custodian was listed as Eleanor Vale.

But the next line was unexpected.

**Temporary band received: IV-204.**

**Original band retained pending verification.**

**Replacement band issued: M-204.**

Edward read it twice.

"So the band was replaced at Meridian."

"Yes."

"Not at Westbridge."

"Correct."

The Westbridge record had merely noted that the band was removed and retained.

Jonathan pointed to the next entry.

**Original band placed in sealed identification envelope.**

**Envelope reference: M-204/OR-1.**

Edward looked at the evidence sleeve.

The band had been preserved, but the envelope reference on the original Meridian register was different from the later A-17 reference.

"Where did A-17 come from?" he asked.

Jonathan opened another page.

"Meridian transferred the sealed identification envelope to an external records custodian on day 28."

"Who?"

"Eleanor Vale."

Edward stared at him.

"She collected the original band on day 28?"

"According to this register, yes."

"But she took it to Westbridge on day 116."

"That's what the later visitor register suggests."

Jonathan placed the documents side by side.

"Which means the band may have been in Eleanor's possession for nearly three months before she brought it to Westbridge."

Edward looked at the dates.

Day six: IV-204 arrived at Meridian.

Day six: original band retained.

Day twenty-eight: Eleanor collected the sealed envelope.

Day 116: the band appeared at Westbridge.

The question was no longer simply how the band reached Westbridge.

It was why Eleanor had kept it for so long.

And what had happened to the infant during that period.

Edward returned to the original maternity sheet.

He examined the handwritten note on the reverse.

The instruction not to attach maternal identity to the temporary receiving number had been written in dark blue ink.

A small set of initials appeared beside it.

**E.V.**

Eleanor Vale.

He called her.

She agreed to meet him the following morning.

Eleanor arrived at the Valmont legal office carrying a small handbag and a walking stick.

She was older than Edward remembered from the earlier photographs, but her expression remained composed.

When Edward showed her the maternity sheet, she did not appear surprised.

"You found the original."

"Yes."

"And the band?"

"Yes."

She nodded.

"Then you have finally recovered part of the record."

"Why did you keep the band?"

Eleanor looked at the document.

"Because it was the only original identification item that had travelled with the child from the maternity clinic."

"Why didn't you return it to Isabella?"

"Because I could not establish whether it still belonged to her daughter."

"Why not?"

"Because the band was removed and held separately when the child arrived at Meridian. From that point onward, I could not guarantee that the band had remained associated with the same infant."

Edward leaned forward.

"Did you see the child at Meridian?"

"Yes."

"Did you see the band on her?"

"At the time of arrival, yes."

"Was it attached correctly?"

"It was attached to the infant's wrist. The fastening appeared intact."

"Then why was it removed?"

"Because the transfer documentation contained a discrepancy."

"What discrepancy?"

Eleanor paused.

"The hospital's original record identified the child as IV-204. The receiving transport sheet used another temporary reference."

"Which one?"

"STC-A."

Edward felt his attention sharpen.

"St Catherine's Infant A?"

Eleanor nodded.

"That was the reference written on the transport sheet."

"But Isabella's maternity record identified the infant as IV-204."

"Correct."

"Why was a child from Isabella's clinic being transferred under a St Catherine's reference?"

Eleanor looked at him.

"That was the question I was trying to answer."

Edward studied her face.

"Did you believe the infant was Isabella's daughter?"

"I believed the infant had been born at Isabella's clinic. That much was supported by the original maternity record."

"But?"

"But the transfer sheet claimed a different originating reference. I could not determine whether the infant had been incorrectly labelled, whether the transport document had been attached to the wrong medical file, or whether someone had deliberately moved the identification materials."

"Did you ever establish which explanation was correct?"

"No."

Edward sat back.

The two references had now appeared together on the same transfer document.

IV-204.

STC-A.

The first belonged to Isabella's maternity admission.

The second belonged to the St Catherine's infant record.

And the document had been used to transfer a child from one facility to another.

"Who prepared the transport sheet?" Edward asked.

"Charles Vale's office."

"Who signed it?"

"Gabriel Vale."

Edward looked down.

"Did you challenge him?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"He said the reference had been supplied by the originating office."

"Which originating office?"

"He would not specify."

"Did you contact St Catherine's?"

"Yes."

"And?"

"They said Infant A had already been transferred on the sixth day."

"To Gabriel."

"Yes."

Edward felt the significance of that answer.

Gabriel had taken Infant A from St Catherine's on the sixth day.

The same day IV-204 was transferred from Isabella's maternity clinic to Meridian.

And the transport sheet had used the St Catherine's reference for the child associated with Isabella's maternity record.

Two infants.

Two separate facilities.

Two different birth records.

One administrative reference appearing in both.

"Did you ever see Gabriel with the child from Meridian?" Edward asked.

Eleanor looked at him for a long moment.

"Yes."

Edward did not interrupt.

"On the evening of day six, he came to Meridian with a nurse. He asked to review the transfer documentation."

"Did he take the child?"

"No."

"Did he handle the band?"

"He examined the paperwork. He did not remove the band."

"Was there another child with him?"

Eleanor hesitated.

"Not that evening."

Edward wrote that down.

"Did you tell him the transfer reference was incorrect?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"He told me not to change it until Charles had reviewed the discrepancy."

"Did Charles review it?"

"He sent a written instruction."

"What did it say?"

Eleanor's expression became troubled.

"He said the references must remain separate, but the transport should proceed under the existing administrative arrangement."

Edward stared at her.

"That makes no sense."

"It made no sense to me either."

"Did you refuse?"

"I refused to certify the identity."

"But the child was transferred."

"Yes."

"To Westbridge?"

"No. The child remained at Meridian."

Edward stopped.

"Then what happened?"

Eleanor looked down at her hands.

"I was told to prepare the records for a later residential transfer. But I was never given the authority to move the child myself."

"Who had that authority?"

"Charles Vale arranged the residential placements. Gabriel handled medical coordination. Marianne handled the paperwork."

"Did you know where the child eventually went?"

"No."

Edward studied her.

"Then why did you take the original band to Westbridge?"

"Because the child there had been recorded under M-204, and I wanted to establish whether the band belonged to that child."

"But you had collected the band on day 28."

"Yes."

"Why wait until day 116?"

Eleanor took a breath.

"Because on day 31, I received a letter from the Aurelius Family Office asking me to preserve all original identification materials connected to the disputed infant records."

Edward remembered the letter Margaret Voss had shown him.

Adrian Holt had requested preservation of records and denied Victoria's authorisation.

"Did the letter identify the child?"

"No. It instructed me not to destroy, transfer, or alter the original identification materials."

"Did it name A-17?"

"Not initially."

"Then when did that reference appear?"

"Later."

"Who introduced it?"

Eleanor looked at him.

"Charles."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"What did he say?"

"He told me the records had been consolidated under a protected reference so that the different facilities would not confuse the children."

"Did you agree?"

"I agreed that the records needed to be preserved. I did not agree that they should be consolidated without a proper identity comparison."

"Did you tell Adrian Holt?"

"Yes."

"What did he say?"

"He told me to keep the original band sealed until an independent review could be arranged."

Edward glanced at the report.

"And then you brought it to Westbridge."

"Because I had been told that the child carrying the band was there."

"Who told you?"

Eleanor hesitated.

"Marianne Cross."

"Was she certain?"

"She said the receiving records showed M-204."

"Did you verify that?"

"I asked to see the child and her temporary band. The medical staff confirmed the reference."

Edward remained silent.

Eleanor continued.

"But I could not confirm that the child was Isabella's daughter. The original band might have been transferred, or the child might have been misidentified before the transfer."

"Did you tell Isabella?"

"Yes."

"Did she understand?"

"I believe so."

Edward thought of Isabella's words.

*This proves your child's identification materials were moved. It does not prove where your child is.*

Eleanor had been careful not to give her false hope.

"Why did you allow her to see the band?" he asked.

"Because she had a right to know that her maternity identification material had been found."

"And why not the child?"

"Because I had no authority to release the child to her. Nor could I tell her that the child was hers."

Edward nodded slowly.

For the first time, the sequence of events was becoming clearer.

But the most important question remained.

"Did you ever discover why the St Catherine's reference had been attached to IV-204?"

Eleanor looked at him.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"What did you discover?"

She opened her handbag and removed a small envelope.

"I kept a copy of the original transport instruction."

She placed it on the table.

The document was dated the sixth day after the births.

At the top were two references.

**Originating record: IV-204.**

**Cross-reference: STC-A.**

Below them was a handwritten instruction.

**Do not treat cross-reference as proof of infant identity.**

Edward read it.

Then he looked at the signature.

It belonged to Gabriel Vale.

Eleanor spoke quietly.

"He knew the references were not interchangeable."

Edward looked up.

"Then why did he sign the transfer?"

"That is what I wanted to know."

After Eleanor left, Edward remained alone with the documents.

The investigation had uncovered another contradiction.

Gabriel had signed a transfer instruction linking IV-204 with STC-A.

But he had also explicitly warned that the cross-reference was not proof of infant identity.

He had known the records were connected administratively, not necessarily biologically.

That did not make him innocent.

It did, however, change the question.

Perhaps Gabriel had not been claiming that Isabella's daughter was Infant A.

Perhaps he had been moving a child through a system where two identities were deliberately being kept separate.

Edward opened his notebook.

He wrote:

**IV-204 — Isabella's maternity record.**

**STC-A — St Catherine's Infant A.**

**M-204 — Meridian/Westbridge temporary reference.**

**A-17 — Protected identity file.**

Then he drew lines between the entries.

The lines did not yet form a clear picture.

But one fact stood out.

The original band had travelled from Isabella's maternity clinic to Meridian.

It had been retained there.

It had later appeared at Westbridge.

And the child associated with it had been moved through several institutions without a permanent identity.

Edward turned to the final page of Eleanor's document.

A second handwritten instruction appeared beneath Gabriel's signature.

It had been added later.

**Do not inform the receiving family that the child originated from IV-204 until the St Catherine's comparison is complete.**

There was no signature.

No date.

But the handwriting was familiar.

Edward placed it beside the note from Northbridge:

**Receiving party to be informed that the child is not the Aurelius infant.**

He studied both sentences.

The first instructed someone to withhold Isabella's maternity reference.

The second instructed someone to deny an Aurelius connection.

Together, they created a child with no confirmed family identity.

And someone had ensured that every receiving facility knew what not to say.

Edward picked up his telephone.

"Jonathan, I need the original St Catherine's comparison report."

"Which one?"

"The comparison between Infant A and IV-204."

A pause.

"We've already established that the photograph and bracelet didn't match."

"Not the photograph. The original identification materials."

"Where should I look?"

"Start with the day-12 correction and the day-28 comparison. I want every version, including drafts."

"Understood."

Edward looked at the two handwritten instructions again.

Then he added one final line to his notebook.

**Who ordered the comparison — and who stopped it from being completed?**

For eighteen years, the Aurelius family had searched for a missing daughter.

But someone had known that two different infant records might be connected.

Someone had preserved the evidence.

Someone had prevented the identities from being reconciled.

And someone had made sure that the mothers never received the complete truth.
