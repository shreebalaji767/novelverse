# **Chapter 129 — The Second Contact That Was Never Made**

The photocopy arrived without a sender.

That alone did not make it important.

Edward had received anonymous notes before. Some contained genuine information. Others contained interpretations, incomplete recollections, or attempts to direct the investigation towards a particular person.

He had learned not to confuse an unexpected document with reliable evidence.

But the reference at the bottom of the page bothered him.

**P-04 / SECOND CONTACT — NOT AUTHORISED.**

It suggested that someone had considered contacting the protected principal a second time.

And someone else had stopped it.

Edward placed the photocopy beside the original day-40 dispatch record.

The first contact had been assigned to Gabriel Vale.

The acknowledgement package had been returned on day 43.

The principal's response remained blank.

Now there was evidence of a second contact that had never taken place.

“Find the original reference,” he told Jonathan.

“PR-06?”

“Not just PR-06. Every document mentioning a second contact, repeat notification, or direct principal communication.”

Jonathan opened his laptop.

“Do we know whether the note was written by P-04?”

“No.”

“Could it have been written by Eleanor?”

“Possibly.”

“Or Gabriel?”

“Possibly.”

Edward put the photocopy into a separate sleeve.

“Until we know its origin, it is only a lead.”

The first useful record appeared in the Personal Instructions Division archive.

It was a day-44 correspondence entry.

**PR-06 — SECOND CONTACT REQUEST**

The request had been submitted by Gabriel Vale.

Its stated purpose was to arrange direct communication with the protected principal after the first acknowledgement package had been returned.

The proposed method was straightforward.

Gabriel would arrange a meeting, present the information summary, explain the conditional nature of the receiving arrangement, and obtain a written response.

No child would be presented.

No placement would be discussed as a completed decision.

The principal would simply be given the opportunity to consider the proposal.

Edward read the request twice.

“Gabriel initiated the second contact.”

Jonathan nodded.

“And someone rejected it.”

“Who?”

Jonathan checked the decision field.

**Status: NOT AUTHORISED.**

**Authorising office: PRIVATE INSTRUCTIONS REVIEW.**

There was no named decision-maker.

Only a reference:

**PI-07.**

Edward looked up.

“Private Instruction 07.”

“The same archive we found at V-17.”

“Yes.”

The reference was becoming more significant.

PI-07 had originally governed the preservation of the sealed receiving instruction. It required joint authority before the document could be opened.

Now it appeared to have been used to restrict a direct contact with P-04.

Edward turned to the attached explanation.

*Further communication is not permitted until the receiving authority has been independently verified and the originating identity has been reconciled.*

He frowned.

“That condition makes no sense.”

Jonathan looked at him.

“Why?”

“P-04 was being asked to consider a possible receiving arrangement. Their response was supposed to help determine whether the arrangement could proceed. But this instruction says they cannot be contacted until the receiving authority is verified.”

“Another circular condition.”

“Exactly.”

Gabriel Vale agreed to meet them at his solicitor's office.

He was less guarded than during their previous conversations, but the moment Edward placed the day-44 request in front of him, his expression changed.

“You submitted this?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because the first communication had not been completed.”

“Did you know why?”

“I was told the package had been returned.”

“Did you know the principal had received it?”

“No.”

“Did you know the principal had declined?”

“No.”

“Then why was the request rejected?”

Gabriel looked at the PI-07 reference.

“Because the instruction was issued under the private custody arrangement.”

“Who issued it?”

“I received it through Charles's office.”

“Was it signed?”

“No.”

“Did you question it?”

“I did.”

“What happened?”

“I was told the matter was no longer within my authority.”

Edward leaned forward.

“Who told you that?”

“Charles Vale.”

“Personally?”

“Yes.”

“What did he say?”

Gabriel took a moment before answering.

“He said the proposed recipient could not be approached again until the identity process was complete.”

“But the principal had not even received the original information.”

“I know.”

“Did you tell him that?”

“Yes.”

“What was his response?”

Gabriel looked down at the request.

“He said a second contact could create confusion while the receiving authority remained unappointed.”

Edward studied him.

“Did you agree?”

“No.”

“Then why didn't you contact P-04 anyway?”

“Because my authority was limited. I was not permitted to act outside the written instruction.”

“Could you have resigned from the role and contacted the person privately?”

Gabriel looked at him.

“That would have exposed the principal's protected identity and potentially violated the custody restrictions. I had no independent right to do that.”

Edward understood the problem.

Gabriel's authority was limited.

The person he was supposed to contact remained protected.

And the office controlling the process had refused to authorise the contact.

“Did you ever speak to P-04?” Edward asked.

Gabriel shook his head.

“Not directly.”

“Before day 40?”

“I received instructions concerning the proposed arrangement. I did not meet the principal.”

“Did anyone tell you the principal had already agreed?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell you the principal had refused?”

“No.”

“Then you never knew their position.”

“That is correct.”

Edward closed the folder.

The conditional response had been the only communication attributed to P-04.

But it had come through Gabriel, and it had been limited.

There was still no personal acknowledgement.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander listened to Edward's update.

“Gabriel requested a second contact,” Edward explained. “The request was rejected under PI-07.”

“By whom?” Alexander asked.

“The authorising office is listed, but the individual decision-maker is not identified.”

Victoria looked at the document.

“What reason was given?”

“The receiving authority had not been verified.”

“Then why did they expect the principal to agree to anything?”

“They didn't. Not officially.”

Victoria placed the paper down.

“Then the person was prevented from making a decision, while the administrative records continued to describe a possible receiving arrangement.”

“Yes.”

Alexander studied the PI-07 reference.

“Does this connect to the sealed instruction?”

“Probably. We haven't established who issued the restriction.”

“Could Charles have done it?”

“He had access to the private instruction archive and authorised several related procedures. But we need the original instruction before attributing the decision.”

Victoria looked at the blank acknowledgement field.

“I want the person behind P-04 to have the opportunity to answer, if they are still able to.”

Edward nodded.

“That is what we're trying to establish.”

The original PI-07 archive had been transferred to Meridian.

Rachel Morton located the relevant section.

It was stored separately from the sealed instruction box, but the reference was clearly linked.

The file contained a day-44 restriction notice.

Its wording matched the entry Gabriel had received.

**SECOND CONTACT SUSPENDED.**

**Reason:** Receiving authority not independently verified.

**Further contact:** Not permitted until joint authority established.

The signature field was blank.

There was, however, a small handwritten notation at the bottom.

**Issued under existing private instruction.**

Edward examined it.

“Was this notice attached to the original PI-07 instruction?”

Rachel checked the preservation inventory.

“No. It was added to the correspondence file on day 61.”

“Who transferred it?”

“Eleanor Vale.”

Edward looked at the document again.

“Was the original notice signed?”

“No.”

“Was it authenticated?”

“Not in the ordinary way. The archive accepted it as a copy of an instruction issued under the private custody arrangement.”

“Who verified the copy?”

Rachel turned to the transfer receipt.

The verification signature belonged to Eleanor.

Edward requested the associated correspondence.

A thin sheet was attached.

It was a day-45 note from Eleanor to Charles.

*The second contact request cannot be suspended solely because the receiving authority remains unappointed. The principal has not yet received the information necessary to respond. The absence of an appointment cannot be used as a substitute for the principal's decision.*

Edward read it slowly.

Eleanor had challenged the restriction.

She had recognised the same circular problem.

And she had written to Charles about it.

“What was Charles's reply?” Jonathan asked.

Rachel found the next page.

There was no formal reply.

Only a handwritten notation.

*Maintain suspension pending completion of originating verification.*

No signature.

No date.

Edward stared at the wording.

It had not answered Eleanor's objection.

It had simply repeated the condition.

The next person they needed to speak to was Thomas Greer.

He examined the day-44 restriction notice and the day-45 objection.

“I remember the dispute,” he said.

“Who issued the restriction?” Edward asked.

“Charles instructed that the second contact remain suspended.”

“Did he have authority?”

“Under the private instruction, he controlled access to the protected correspondence archive.”

“That isn't the same as having authority to prevent P-04 from receiving information.”

Greer looked at the notice.

“No.”

“Did you tell him that?”

“I told him the restriction could not be treated as a permanent refusal by the principal.”

“Did he agree?”

“He said it was temporary.”

“How temporary?”

“Until the originating identity had been verified and the receiving authority appointed.”

Edward shook his head.

“That could have taken years.”

Greer did not disagree.

“Did Charles ever explain why he believed P-04 should not be contacted?”

“He said the proposed recipient could not make an informed decision while the child's identity remained unresolved.”

“That sounds reasonable on its own.”

“It was reasonable to withhold unverified claims about the child.”

“But not to withhold the entire proposal.”

Greer looked at him.

“That was Eleanor's position.”

“And yours?”

“I believed the principal should receive a clear explanation of what was known and what remained uncertain.”

“Then why didn't you arrange it?”

“Because I did not control the protected correspondence channel.”

Edward considered the answer.

“Did you know who P-04 was?”

“Yes.”

“Did you know whether the person was related to either family?”

“I cannot disclose the identity.”

“Did you know whether the principal was an adult?”

Greer paused.

“I can tell you that the authority was created for an individual capable of giving a personal response.”

“Was the person legally competent to do so?”

“Yes.”

“Then there was no reason to substitute someone else's decision.”

Greer said nothing.

Edward gathered the papers.

There was still one important question.

“Did P-04 ever receive any information about the child?”

Greer looked at the blank acknowledgement.

“I cannot confirm that.”

In Singapore, Elena's team received feedback on the revised prototype.

The supervisor liked the version history but questioned whether applicants should be allowed to see every internal administrative note.

“Some notes may contain confidential information,” he explained.

Elena nodded.

“Then the system should distinguish between internal review comments and information that applicants are entitled to receive.”

“Would that create two versions of the record?”

“Not exactly. One record, with different access permissions.”

Her teammate Daniel looked at the screen.

“So the system knows the information exists, but only authorised people can see it.”

“Yes. And every access or amendment should be recorded.”

The supervisor considered the idea.

“Good. Add a section explaining who can authorise restricted access.”

Elena wrote it down.

After the meeting, Clara joined her for lunch.

“You look exhausted.”

“I'm fine.”

“You say that every time you're exhausted.”

Elena smiled.

“I have a project deadline.”

“You also have a life.”

“I've heard rumours.”

Clara laughed.

They found a quiet table near the campus garden.

For a while, they discussed classes, accommodation, and the difficulties of adjusting to a new routine.

Then Clara asked about home.

“Do you miss them?”

“Sometimes.”

“Do you regret coming?”

“No.”

Elena looked across the garden.

“I think I needed to do something that was mine. Not because I don't care about my family. I just wanted to know what I could do without depending on what they could give me.”

Clara nodded.

“That makes sense.”

Elena returned to her lunch.

She did not need to explain the feeling further.

Edward received another message from Eleanor that evening.

It contained a scanned page from her personal correspondence archive.

The document was dated day 46.

Its subject was:

**REQUEST FOR DIRECT PRINCIPAL CONTACT — FINAL REVIEW**

Eleanor had written:

*The proposed recipient has not received the principal information summary. Gabriel Vale has confirmed that no direct communication occurred. I therefore request that the second contact be authorised independently of the receiving identity appointment.*

A second paragraph was underlined.

*The principal cannot be expected to provide an acknowledgement for information never received.*

Edward read it twice.

At the bottom was a handwritten response.

**Not approved.**

The handwriting was unfamiliar.

It did not match Charles Vale's known notes.

It did not match Eleanor's.

Nor did it resemble the signatures of the other officials involved.

There was a reference beneath it.

**PI-07 / JOINT CUSTODY DECISION.**

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“Joint custody?”

Jonathan examined the page.

“The private instruction required the originating representative and receiving authority to be present together before access was permitted.”

“But the receiving authority had never been appointed.”

“So the joint decision could never be made.”

Edward stared at the note.

The restriction had not merely been issued by Charles.

It had been attributed to a joint custody decision.

Yet one of the required parties did not exist as an appointed authority.

“Who wrote this?”

Eleanor had attached a separate note.

*I did not issue this refusal. I did not attend any joint custody review on day 46. No receiving identity custodian had been appointed at that time.*

Edward felt the investigation shifting again.

Someone had created the appearance of a joint decision.

Someone had rejected direct contact with P-04.

And the rejection had been recorded under an authority that could not legally have been exercised.

He called Eleanor.

“Do you recognise the handwriting?”

“No.”

“Did you ever see the original?”

“No. I received this copy after the refusal had already been entered.”

“Who sent it?”

“It arrived through the Personal Instructions Division. There was no sender's name.”

Edward looked at the reference.

“Did you challenge it?”

“Yes. I requested the original joint custody record.”

“Was it produced?”

“No.”

“Why?”

Eleanor paused.

“Because the archive said there was no separate joint custody record.”

Edward sat very still.

“Then what was this based on?”

“I don't know.”

The next morning, Edward and Jonathan returned to the Meridian archive.

They requested every document associated with **PI-07 / JOINT CUSTODY DECISION**.

Rachel searched the index.

At first, she found nothing.

Then she opened the historical correspondence register.

One entry appeared.

**Day 46 — Joint custody review initiated.**

**Participants:** Originating representative; receiving identity authority.

**Outcome:** Second contact not authorised.

**Supporting record:** JC-46/ORIGINAL.

**Storage location:** V-17 — West Corridor.

Edward looked at Jonathan.

“The original was at V-17.”

Rachel checked the later transfer inventory.

“It was not included in the day-61 transfer.”

“Was it ever collected?”

She searched again.

A final entry appeared.

**Day 58 — JC-46/ORIGINAL removed under private instruction.**

**Collected by:** C. Vale.

**Destination:** Not recorded.

Edward stared at the screen.

There was now an official record of a joint custody review.

But no record of the participants.

No receiving identity custodian had been appointed.

Eleanor denied attending.

And the original document had been removed by Charles.

Jonathan spoke quietly.

“Could Charles have signed it himself?”

“Possibly.”

“But that would not make it a joint decision.”

“No.”

Edward looked at the empty destination field.

“Find out whether the originating representative was notified of the review.”

Jonathan began searching.

A few minutes later, he stopped.

“There is a notification record.”

“To whom?”

“Victoria Aurelius.”

Edward looked up.

“What does it say?”

Jonathan read the entry.

**Day 46 — Originating representative notified of joint custody review.**

**Acknowledgement: Received.**

**Receiving authority: Not recorded.**

Edward stared at the final line.

Victoria had apparently been notified of a review concerning P-04.

But she had never mentioned receiving such a notice.

And the record still did not identify the other party.

“Get the original notification,” Edward said.

Jonathan opened the next reference.

There was a scanned acknowledgement attached.

The signature looked genuine.

It belonged to Victoria Aurelius.

But beneath it, in a different handwriting, was a sentence that changed the meaning of the entire document:

*I acknowledge receipt of notice only. I have not attended a joint review, approved any receiving arrangement, or accepted the appointment of a receiving authority.*

Edward read the sentence twice.

The notification had been acknowledged.

The review had not been approved.

And someone had used the acknowledgement of receipt to support a decision Victoria said she had never made.

The same mistake had appeared again.

A receipt had become an approval.

A conditional response had become consent.

And now an administrative notification appeared to have become a joint decision.

Edward placed the document on the table.

“Find the person who recorded the joint review as completed.”

Jonathan nodded.

But Edward's attention remained on Victoria's signature.

For the first time, the investigation had uncovered a document suggesting that the Aurelius family had been formally drawn into the P-04 decision process without actually participating in it.

And whoever had done that had known exactly how to make a simple acknowledgement look like something it was not.
