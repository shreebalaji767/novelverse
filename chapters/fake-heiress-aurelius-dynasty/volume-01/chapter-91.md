# **Chapter 91 — The Man Who Refused to Name a Family**

Dr Samuel Hale had retired eleven years ago.

That was the first difficulty.

The second was that his retirement had not made him particularly easy to find.

His former hospital had retained his employment records, professional correspondence, and a small number of archived reports. However, his private address was protected, and the medical institution refused to disclose it without a formal request.

Edward understood the reasoning.

He also understood that the same caution had allowed important questions to remain unanswered for eighteen years.

"We need a legal route," Jonathan said, closing the folder.

"We have one."

Edward placed a document on the table.

It was a copy of the R-04 trust registration.

The name of the settlor was clearly printed.

**DR SAMUEL HALE.**

Below it appeared the date of establishment and the original administrative purpose.

Preservation of records pending verified identification of the receiving family.

Jonathan read the sentence twice.

"He created the trust himself."

"Yes."

"And he was also the medical verification authority."

"Yes."

"Then he was in a position to know why the identities were never confirmed."

Edward looked at the registration document.

"Or he was in a position to prevent them from being confirmed."

Neither possibility was reassuring.

The trust had not been dissolved.

That surprised them.

Its original administrative office had closed, but its legal existence continued under an independent professional trustee.

The company managing it had changed names twice. Its records had been transferred to a larger fiduciary services group, which had inherited the responsibility of preserving the trust's documents.

After two days of formal correspondence, Edward received permission to inspect the registration records and the surviving administrative correspondence.

The permission did not extend to the sealed beneficiary schedule.

That remained restricted.

The first meeting took place in a quiet conference room with a woman named Helen Ward, a senior trust administrator.

She placed three folders in front of Edward.

"The R-04 trust is unusual," she explained. "It was established for a specific administrative purpose, but its beneficiary was never formally designated."

"Was that legally permitted?"

"Under the original deed, yes. The trust was structured to preserve certain records and assets until the relevant identity determination was completed."

"Who was responsible for making that determination?"

"Dr Hale was named as the medical verification authority. However, he was not the sole decision-maker."

Edward leaned forward.

"Who else?"

"The originating records authority and the receiving identity custodian."

"Do you have their names?"

"Not in the general registration file. Their appointments were supposed to be recorded in a separate schedule."

"Was that schedule completed?"

Helen opened the second folder.

"No."

Jonathan exchanged a glance with Edward.

Another incomplete schedule.

Another missing authority.

Another process that appeared to have been designed to reach a conclusion but had never reached one.

"Then why establish the trust?" Jonathan asked.

Helen turned a page.

"Because the documents had already become vulnerable to conflicting instructions. The trust was intended to prevent anyone from removing, transferring, or using the records until the relevant parties had completed verification."

"Did it work?"

Helen hesitated.

"It preserved the records."

"That wasn't my question."

"No," she said quietly. "It did not produce the final identification."

Edward studied the registration.

"Was Dr Hale paid to establish it?"

"The trust received an initial administrative contribution from his professional account. Subsequent preservation costs were paid through a separate reserve."

"Who funded the reserve?"

"That information is in the restricted financial schedule."

"Can we obtain it?"

"With the appropriate authority, yes."

Edward closed the folder.

"Then we will request it."

Helen regarded him carefully.

"May I ask something?"

"Of course."

"Are you investigating the identity of a child, or the conduct of the people who handled the records?"

Edward answered without hesitation.

"Both."

Helen nodded.

"Then I would advise you not to treat the trust as proof of either."

It was the first useful warning he had received that morning.

The financial schedule took another day to obtain.

When it arrived, Edward and Jonathan examined it together.

The initial contribution had been made by Dr Hale.

The later reserve payments had come through a corporate account connected to Vale Private Counsel.

There were also three payments from an unidentified administrative account.

None of the payments contained a child's name.

None identified a biological parent.

But the dates were important.

The first payment had been made on day 18.

The second on day 34.

The third on day 61.

Edward placed the dates beside the Annex C chronology.

Day 18: Eleanor Vale's instruction to keep originating and receiving identities separate.

Day 34: Margaret Voss transferred the sealed Part Two schedule to Adrian Holt.

Day 61: Dr Hale returned Part One to Holt under the Family Office seal.

The payments had not been random.

"Someone was maintaining the trust while the identification process remained incomplete," Jonathan said.

"Someone wanted the documents preserved."

"Or wanted them unavailable."

Edward looked at him.

"Those are not mutually exclusive."

Jonathan leaned back.

"Do you think Hale was protecting the children?"

"I think he may have believed he was."

"And now?"

"Now I want to hear him explain why he never completed the work."

Dr Samuel Hale lived in a modest house on the outskirts of the city.

There was no security gate, no impressive driveway, and no indication that the retired physician had once been involved in a matter connected to two wealthy families and an international corporate dynasty.

The front garden was carefully maintained.

A newspaper lay folded beside the entrance.

Edward rang the bell.

After a short wait, an elderly man opened the door.

His hair had turned almost entirely white. He wore reading glasses and a plain grey cardigan.

"Dr Hale?"

"Yes."

"My name is Edward Valmont. This is Jonathan."

Hale's expression changed slightly.

Not surprise.

Recognition.

He looked at Edward for several seconds.

"Valmont," he repeated.

"Yes."

"I wondered when someone from your family would come."

Edward did not immediately answer.

"May we speak with you?"

Hale stepped aside.

"Come in."

The house was quiet.

A bookshelf covered one wall of the sitting room. Medical journals occupied several shelves, while framed photographs showed a younger Hale at different stages of his career.

There were no obvious reminders of the hospital controversy.

Hale offered tea.

Edward declined.

Jonathan accepted.

Once they were seated, Hale folded his hands.

"I assume this concerns the old neonatal records."

"It concerns the St Catherine's infant records, Annex C, and the R-04 trust."

Hale's eyes lowered briefly.

"So you found it."

"Yes."

"And the trust remains active?"

"It does."

Hale looked troubled.

"Then the records were preserved."

"That is what the trustee told us."

"Good."

Edward studied him.

"Doctor, preserving the records was not the same as identifying the children."

"No," Hale said.

"Why were they never identified?"

Hale removed his glasses.

For a moment, he looked older than he had when he opened the door.

"Because the material required for comparison was never assembled in a form that could support a reliable conclusion."

"We know that."

"Then you know the difficulty."

"We know about the missing neonatal card, the disputed maternity references, the temporary bands, the incomplete recipient schedule, and the various people who transferred documents without completing the necessary verification."

Hale listened without interruption.

Edward continued.

"We also know you were the medical verification authority. You received Part One of Annex C. You returned it to Adrian Holt on day 61. You established R-04. And you never designated a beneficiary."

Hale's expression tightened.

"That is correct."

"Why?"

"Because I did not know which child belonged to which family."

The answer was simple.

It was also insufficient.

"You had eighteen years," Edward said.

Hale looked directly at him.

"Not eighteen years of usable evidence."

"Then why didn't you demand it?"

"I did."

"From whom?"

"From the people responsible for supplying it."

"Names."

Hale was silent.

Edward waited.

Finally, the retired physician said, "Charles Vale. Gabriel Vale. Adrian Holt. And the administrative representatives appointed through the Family Office."

"Did you inform Alexander and Victoria Aurelius?"

"No."

"Did you inform Richard and Catherine Valmont?"

"No."

"Did you inform Isabella Vale?"

Hale looked down.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I was instructed that the originating families were not to be notified until the receiving identities had been confirmed."

Edward's voice became colder.

"That instruction created the very problem you were supposed to solve."

Hale did not argue.

"I know."

Jonathan set down his untouched tea.

"Doctor, what exactly did the R-04 trust contain?"

"Originally? Copies of the records, transfer acknowledgements, and the instructions governing the identification process."

"Copies?"

"Some originals were retained elsewhere. The trust was not the primary hospital archive."

"Then it wasn't protecting the complete evidence."

"No."

"Where were the originals?"

Hale looked at Edward.

"That is what you should be asking."

Edward remained still.

"Where?"

"Some were held by the originating hospitals. Some were transferred to the Family Office. Some were retained by private custodians. The purpose of the trust was to preserve the administrative chain connecting them."

"Did it contain a list of the children?"

"It contained references."

"Did it contain a final list of receiving families?"

"No."

"Did it contain a proposed list?"

Hale paused.

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"Part Two."

Hale nodded.

"The recipient schedule was supposed to identify the proposed receiving arrangements. It was not a declaration of biological parentage."

"We know that now."

"Then you understand why it was not enough."

"I understand why it was dangerous."

Hale looked at him.

"That too."

Edward placed a copy of the schedule reference on the table.

"AV-18/RECIPIENT SCHEDULE. What does AV-18 mean?"

Hale studied the paper.

"I don't know."

"You have seen it before."

"Yes."

"Where?"

"In the trust correspondence."

"Whose correspondence?"

"An instruction that arrived after the trust had been established."

Edward's attention sharpened.

"When?"

Hale considered the question.

"I cannot remember the exact date."

"Was it signed?"

"No."

"Who delivered it?"

"I received it through the trust's administrative address."

"R-04?"

"Yes."

"What did it say?"

Hale looked toward the bookshelf.

"It instructed me not to treat any proposed receiving identity as final until the originating medical records and receiving records had been independently reconciled."

Edward frowned.

"That is consistent with the other instructions."

"It was more specific."

"How?"

Hale turned back.

"It said that the schedule could be preserved, but no child could be assigned to a family merely because that family had been named as a proposed recipient."

The room fell quiet.

Jonathan spoke first.

"Was that instruction followed?"

Hale did not answer immediately.

"Not entirely."

Edward's expression hardened.

"What does that mean?"

"It means the trust preserved the schedule. But other people continued to act as though the proposed arrangements were already settled."

"Who?"

"I cannot say who made every decision."

"Who did you see acting on those assumptions?"

"Charles Vale."

Edward's fingers tightened around the edge of the folder.

"Did he know the identities were unconfirmed?"

"Yes."

"How do you know?"

"Because I told him."

For the next several minutes, Hale described a meeting that had taken place shortly after the trust was created.

Charles Vale had arrived with a document package.

He wanted Hale to approve the medical portion of the recipient schedule.

Hale had refused.

The schedule contained proposed receiving identities, but the originating records had not been reconciled with the receiving records.

Hale had explained that a child's temporary placement reference could not establish biological parentage.

Charles had disagreed.

Not openly.

He had argued that the children needed stable arrangements and that the administrative details could be completed later.

Hale had rejected the request.

"Did Charles threaten you?" Jonathan asked.

"No."

"Offer you money?"

"No."

"Then why did you allow the process to continue?"

Hale looked uncomfortable.

"Because I believed the children were already in temporary care. I thought the records could be reconciled without disturbing their placements."

Edward's voice was controlled.

"You believed the children were safe."

"I believed they were receiving medical and residential care."

"That is not the same thing as knowing who they were."

"No," Hale admitted.

"Did you ever see the children?"

"Two of them."

Edward went still.

"Which two?"

"Two infants in the Northbridge ward."

"On the same day?"

"Yes."

"STC-A and NB-061?"

Hale nodded slowly.

"Those were the references recorded on the ward schedule."

"Could you identify which child was which?"

"No. Both were wearing temporary identification bands. The receiving references were not reliable enough to establish their originating identities."

"Did you compare their medical records?"

"I reviewed the available clinical summaries."

"Did you take photographs?"

"No."

"Did you collect samples?"

"No."

"Did you perform DNA testing?"

"No."

Edward waited.

"Then what did you actually verify?"

Hale's answer came quietly.

"That two female infants were receiving medical care in the ward. Nothing more."

It was an important distinction.

Two infants.

Two temporary references.

No established biological identities.

And a schedule that someone had nevertheless tried to turn into a final arrangement.

Edward took out another document.

"This is the instruction you received on day 18?"

Hale examined the copy.

He nodded.

"Yes."

"Do you recognise the handwriting?"

"I recognise the writing style."

"Who wrote it?"

"I cannot identify the person with certainty."

"Was it Eleanor Vale?"

Hale studied the page again.

"Possibly."

"Marianne Cross?"

"Also possible."

"Did the writer have authority to issue medical instructions?"

"No. The instruction did not authorise a medical decision. It required separation of records."

"Who had authority?"

"The hospital's originating records authority for the clinical material. I had authority to verify the medical comparison. The receiving identity custodian had authority over the proposed recipient schedule."

"And the receiving identity custodian was never appointed."

"Not formally."

"Yet Charles continued submitting schedules."

"Yes."

Edward sat back.

The same pattern appeared again and again.

A document prepared before its supporting evidence existed.

An approval requested from someone who lacked the authority to give it.

A temporary reference treated as though it were a permanent identity.

And a child placed somewhere while the people responsible for identifying that child argued over paperwork.

"Doctor," Edward said, "did you ever suspect that one of the children had been given to the wrong family?"

Hale looked at him for a long time.

"I suspected that the records could not safely establish where either child belonged."

"That wasn't my question."

"I know."

"Did you suspect an actual substitution?"

"I suspected that the administrative process had confused the identity of the originating child with the identity of the receiving child."

Edward's expression changed.

"Meaning?"

"Meaning a family could be listed as the intended recipient of an infant without being the infant's biological family. And a biological mother could be recorded as an originating representative without ever receiving the child."

Jonathan looked down at his notes.

It was the distinction that had been missing from so many earlier assumptions.

Hale continued.

"Once those categories became mixed, every subsequent transfer created another opportunity for error."

"Or manipulation," Edward said.

Hale did not disagree.

The conversation lasted nearly two hours.

When Edward finally stood, he placed one last question before the retired physician.

"Is there anything else in the trust that we have not found?"

Hale's expression became guarded.

"Why do you ask?"

"Because you created it. You know what it was supposed to preserve. You know the beneficiary was never designated. And you have just told us that an instruction arrived through R-04 after the trust was established."

"Yes."

"Was there another document?"

Hale looked toward the window.

"There was a separate instruction."

"What instruction?"

"It concerned the release of the original recipient schedule."

"Part Two?"

"Yes."

"Who issued it?"

"I don't know."

"Where is it?"

"I placed it with the trust correspondence."

"Then the trustee should have it."

"They may."

Edward waited.

Hale continued.

"But there was a condition attached."

"What condition?"

"The document could not be released to any proposed receiving family."

Jonathan frowned.

"Why?"

"Because the proposed receiving family might not be the correct family."

Edward stared at him.

"Then who could receive it?"

Hale's voice was low.

"The originating authority and the receiving authority together."

"The same condition that appeared in the V-17 security report."

Hale nodded.

"Yes."

"Did anyone ever satisfy that condition?"

"No."

"Did you tell Charles?"

"I did."

"What did he say?"

Hale put his glasses back on.

"He said the family arrangements had already been made."

Edward felt the familiar tightening in his chest.

"Which family?"

Hale shook his head.

"He didn't tell me."

Outside the house, Jonathan remained silent until they reached the car.

Then he opened his notebook.

"We have another document."

"Possibly."

"And a condition that was never satisfied."

"Yes."

"Do you believe Hale?"

Edward considered the question.

"He has admitted to things that make him look negligent. That doesn't automatically make him honest about everything else."

"But he didn't deny the central problem."

"No. He didn't."

Jonathan looked back at the house.

"He seemed genuinely troubled."

"Perhaps he is."

Edward opened the car door but did not get inside.

"There's something else."

"What?"

"Hale said the original recipient schedule was placed with the trust correspondence."

"Which means the trustee may still have it."

"Yes."

"And if the release condition was never satisfied, it should still be sealed."

Edward nodded.

"Unless someone removed it."

The trustee's response arrived the following morning.

The original correspondence register contained an entry that had not appeared in the earlier administrative copies.

**R-04 / AV-18**

**DOCUMENT: RECIPIENT SCHEDULE — ORIGINAL**

**STATUS: SEALED**

**RELEASE CONDITION: JOINT AUTHORITY REQUIRED**

Below it was a handwritten note.

**Do not release to either originating family independently.**

Edward read it several times.

The instruction was not a declaration that either family was unsuitable.

It did not establish that the Valmonts or Aureliuses were the receiving families.

It simply meant that neither could receive the document alone.

But the date of the entry troubled him.

The schedule had been placed under seal on day 19.

Annex C had not been formally created until day 28.

Someone had prepared a recipient schedule before the complete identity file existed.

And someone had already decided that neither originating family should see it independently.

Edward called Jonathan.

"We need the original."

"Can the trustee release it?"

"Not yet. They want the joint authority established."

"Then we need to identify the receiving authority."

"Yes."

"Didn't Hale say it was never formally appointed?"

"He said that."

Jonathan was quiet for a moment.

"Then who signed the schedule?"

Edward looked at the scanned register.

There was a small notation beneath the release condition.

Not a name.

Not a signature.

A reference.

**R.V. — CUSTODIAL AUTHORITY PENDING.**

Edward slowly lowered the page.

For eighteen years, they had been searching for the people who had moved the children.

Now they were approaching the person who had been supposed to decide where the records could go.

And that person had never been formally named.

Across the city, Elena sat at her desk with a calculator, three sheets of paper, and an open spreadsheet.

Her Singapore programme acceptance letter was beside her laptop.

She had already completed the first round of formalities.

The remaining tasks were less exciting.

Travel arrangements.

Document verification.

A realistic personal budget.

And the question of how much money she could afford to spend during her first months away.

She entered another figure into the spreadsheet.

Then deleted it.

"No," she murmured. "That is too optimistic."

Nicholas appeared at her open door.

"Are you arguing with your computer?"

"I'm correcting its expectations."

"That sounds serious."

"It is. Apparently, travelling internationally costs money."

Nicholas leaned against the doorframe.

"I could arrange everything."

Elena did not look up.

"I know."

"Flights, accommodation, transport—"

"Accommodation is already included in the programme."

"Then flights."

"I can manage that."

"You're making this unnecessarily difficult."

"I'm making it my responsibility."

Nicholas watched her for a moment.

He had been trying to find the right balance between helping her and allowing her to make her own decisions.

He was not always successful.

"All right," he said eventually. "At least let me review your budget."

Elena considered it.

"Only if you promise not to replace it with an unlimited spending account."

"I make no promises about unlimited spending accounts."

"Then you cannot review it."

He laughed.

She turned the laptop slightly toward him.

"Fine. You can check the calculations."

Nicholas pulled out the chair beside her.

For the next twenty minutes, they argued about transport costs, emergency savings, and whether buying books before arriving in Singapore counted as a necessary academic expense.

Elena insisted that it did.

Nicholas argued that she could use the university library.

She informed him that libraries did not always have the books she wanted.

He eventually conceded, though he made a note to send her a list of second-hand bookstores in Singapore.

It was an ordinary afternoon.

For Elena, that was exactly what made it valuable.

Her future was beginning to take shape through decisions she had made herself.

At the Aurelius residence, Victoria was reading the latest investigation report when Alexander entered the room.

"Any progress?"

"Dr Hale has been located."

Victoria looked up immediately.

"Did he confirm anything?"

"He confirmed that the identities were never medically reconciled."

Alexander took the report from her.

"Did he identify either child?"

"No."

"Did he identify the receiving family?"

"No."

Victoria lowered her eyes.

Another day had passed without an answer.

She had learned to recognise the difference between hope and evidence.

For eighteen years, she had survived on hope.

She would not allow herself to mistake it for proof now.

"What did he say about the trust?" she asked.

"He said the original recipient schedule remains sealed."

"Why?"

"Because neither originating family was permitted to receive it independently. The originating and receiving authorities had to act together."

Victoria looked at the report.

"Then who was the receiving authority?"

"That is what we are trying to establish."

Alexander sat beside her.

"Victoria."

She looked at him.

"We will not force a conclusion simply because we want one."

"I know."

"If the records do not establish that our daughter is Elena, we cannot approach her as though they do."

Victoria was silent.

Then she nodded.

"I don't want to frighten another child with an assumption."

Alexander placed his hand over the report.

"Then we continue carefully."

Victoria looked toward the window.

Somewhere in the world, their daughter might be preparing for university, choosing books, making friends, and building a life that had nothing to do with them.

She wanted to find her.

But she wanted to find her correctly.

That evening, Edward received a second message from the trustee.

It concerned a supplementary document discovered during the preservation company's latest review.

The document had not been included in the original correspondence index because it had been stored separately from the recipient schedule.

Its envelope carried the same reference.

**AV-18.**

But the recipient line was different.

**For the attention of the person holding the receiving authority.**

There was no personal name.

No company designation.

No address beyond the R-04 trust.

The trustee had not opened it.

Edward read the accompanying note.

The envelope had been deposited on day 34.

Five days after the original recipient schedule was sealed.

It had remained untouched ever since.

He immediately called Jonathan.

"There's another envelope."

"From whom?"

"Unknown."

"What's inside?"

"Nothing we know."

"Does it have a release condition?"

Edward examined the final line of the trustee's note.

"Yes."

He read it aloud.

"To be opened only by the person formally appointed as receiving identity custodian."

Jonathan was silent.

Then he asked the obvious question.

"Has that person ever been appointed?"

Edward looked at the blank recipient field.

"No."

He placed the report beside the earlier schedule.

For the first time, the investigation had found not just an unfinished identification process, but a document deliberately preserved for someone whose name had never been entered.

And if the person had never been appointed, then the envelope had been waiting for eighteen years for an authority that might never have existed.

Edward stared at the reference printed across its seal.

**AV-18 — RECEIVING AUTHORITY.**

The next question was no longer simply who had received the children.

It was who had been expected to decide where they belonged.
