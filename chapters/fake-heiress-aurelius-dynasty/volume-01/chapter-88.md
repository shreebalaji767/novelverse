# **Chapter 88 — You Have Not Found the Correct Family**

Edward did not open the envelope.

He placed it on the table, photographed its front and back, and asked the security department to preserve the reception records.

The message was short.

**DO NOT OPEN PART TWO.**

**You have not found the correct family.**

There was no signature.

No reference number.

No explanation.

Jonathan stood beside the table, reading the words.

"Someone knows we found the envelope."

"Yes."

"And they know what is inside?"

"We don't know that."

"They know enough to warn us."

Edward examined the handwriting.

The letters were narrow and evenly spaced. The writer had used a black fountain pen, pressing harder on the first line than on the second.

It was a deliberate message.

Not a hurried warning.

"Could it be from Charles Vale?" Jonathan asked.

"Possible."

"Adrian Holt?"

"Possible."

"Marianne Cross?"

"Also possible."

Jonathan sighed.

"We have managed to identify three people who could have written it, and none of them is a useful answer."

Edward ignored the remark.

He turned the envelope over.

There was a faint impression near the lower edge, as though another document had been placed beneath it when the address was written.

He adjusted the angle of the desk lamp.

A few letters became visible.

**...ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE.**

He leaned closer.

"Get the document examiner."

The examiner arrived shortly before noon.

She introduced herself as Dr. Mira Sen and asked for the envelope to remain untouched until she had completed the preliminary inspection.

She examined the paper, the ink, the adhesive, and the faint impression.

"The envelope is relatively recent," she said.

"Can you determine how recent?" Edward asked.

"Not precisely. The paper stock is still commercially available. The ink is consistent with a modern fountain pen. Nothing here suggests it was prepared eighteen years ago."

"So this was written recently."

"Almost certainly."

She placed a transparent sheet over the faint impression and photographed it.

"The underlying writing is incomplete, but I can make out part of a phrase."

Edward waited.

Dr. Sen adjusted the light.

"Receiving identity must remain separate from originating identity."

Jonathan looked at Edward.

"That's familiar."

"Yes," Edward replied.

The phrase had appeared in several of the old instructions.

Dr. Sen continued.

"There's another impression below it. Possibly a reference."

She enlarged the photograph.

A sequence of characters emerged.

**AV-18...**

The remainder was too faint to read.

Edward looked at the envelope again.

The warning had not merely been written by someone who knew about Part Two.

It had been written by someone familiar with the terminology used in the original custody records.

"Can you tell whether the writer used a template?"

"Not from this alone. But I can compare the handwriting with documents you provide."

Edward gathered the relevant records.

"Compare it with the known correspondence from Charles Vale, Adrian Holt, Marianne Cross, Eleanor Vale, and Margaret Voss."

Dr. Sen accepted the documents.

"I'll examine them. But handwriting comparison may not produce a conclusive identification."

"We understand."

As she left, Jonathan looked at the envelope.

"Someone has spent eighteen years keeping these records separate. Now they are warning us not to reunite them."

Edward returned to his desk.

"Or they are warning us not to reunite them incorrectly."

The legal team was less impressed by the anonymous warning.

Their position was straightforward.

The envelope containing Part Two had been recovered from an authorised hospital archive. Its original seal appeared intact. The custody history was documented. The parties with potential legal interests had been identified.

The message left at reception did not alter those facts.

But it raised a question.

If the correct family had not yet been identified, who was entitled to authorise access?

Edward requested a meeting with the senior legal adviser.

She read the warning twice.

"Do you believe it is genuine?"

"I believe the sender has knowledge of the original records."

"That isn't the same thing."

"No."

She placed the envelope down.

"Then we should not treat it as evidence of a legal restriction."

"I agree."

"However, the existing restriction remains unresolved. We still need to establish who the receiving representative was supposed to be."

Edward opened the Annex C summary.

"The original documents refer to a receiving identity custodian, but the name was never completed."

"Was the custodian an individual or an institution?"

"Unclear."

"Could it have been a family office?"

"Possibly."

"Then we need the designation record."

Edward nodded.

He had already requested it.

The problem was that the designation record was missing from every archive they had examined.

Except, perhaps, one.

Adrian Holt agreed to meet them again.

This time, he looked less comfortable.

Edward placed the anonymous warning on the table.

Holt read it.

Then he looked at Edward.

"You shouldn't have received that."

Jonathan leaned forward.

"Do you know who sent it?"

"No."

"But you recognise the wording?"

Holt returned his attention to the envelope.

"Yes."

Edward's expression sharpened.

"Where have you seen it?"

"In the original instructions."

"Which instructions?"

"The receiving identity schedule."

"Annex C?"

"Not exactly."

Holt took a breath.

"The schedule contained a preliminary classification. It was not the same as the final recipient designation."

Edward opened his notebook.

"Explain."

"Part Two was intended to identify the receiving parties associated with the children. But the receiving identities were not supposed to be treated as biological identities. They were separate administrative categories."

"Why?"

"Because a child could be placed with a temporary guardian before the originating family relationship was verified."

"That much we know."

"Then you also know that the temporary guardian could not automatically become the receiving family."

Jonathan frowned.

"Then who was the receiving family?"

Holt looked at him.

"That was never finalised."

Edward waited.

Holt continued.

"The original arrangement anticipated that the children would eventually be assigned to verified receiving families. Until then, they remained under temporary custody."

"Who was responsible for verifying those families?"

"That depended on the case."

"Who had authority to make the final designation?"

Holt hesitated.

"The originating representative and the receiving identity custodian were both required."

"Who were they?"

"I don't know."

Edward closed his notebook.

"You have repeatedly told us that you received both parts of Annex C. You transferred them to V-17. You signed the clearance certificate. You received the separate transfer reference. Now you are telling us that the final receiving families were never identified."

"That is correct."

"Then why was Part Two sealed as a family document?"

"Because it contained proposed receiving identities."

"Proposed?"

"Not confirmed."

Edward stared at him.

"Did it contain the names of the children?"

"No."

"Did it contain their biological parents' names?"

"Part One contained originating medical records. Part Two contained receiving identity records. The two were designed to be compared."

"Without the comparison, neither part could establish the complete identity."

Holt nodded.

Jonathan looked frustrated.

"So the file was deliberately divided, and then the people responsible for completing it never completed the process."

"That is what the records suggest."

Edward leaned back.

"Then why did you allow the file to be moved to V-17?"

"Because it was supposed to be preserved until the verification process could resume."

"Who told you that?"

"Charles Vale."

"Did he tell you the process would resume?"

Holt looked down.

"No."

After the meeting, Jonathan waited until they were outside before speaking.

"He's still withholding something."

"Probably."

"Do you believe him about the proposed receiving families?"

"I believe the distinction exists in the documents."

"That isn't what I asked."

Edward stopped beside the car.

"I believe Holt knows more than he has told us. I also believe he may genuinely not know the final identities."

"Those two things can both be true?"

"They can."

Jonathan opened the car door.

"Then what does the warning mean?"

Edward looked toward the street.

"It means we should stop assuming that the family named in a record is necessarily the family the child was meant to reach."

At the Valmont residence, Richard had requested a private meeting with Edward.

Catherine and Isabelle were also present.

The investigation had become too complicated for fragmented updates.

Edward placed a summary on the table.

He explained the discovery of Part Two, the anonymous warning, and Holt's latest statement.

Richard listened without interruption.

When Edward finished, he asked a single question.

"Does Part Two identify my biological daughter?"

"Not by itself."

"Does it identify Isabelle's biological mother?"

"Not by itself."

"Then what does it identify?"

"Proposed receiving identities and administrative assignments. The original intention was to compare those records with the originating medical material in Part One."

Catherine looked at the report.

"So the file may contain names of people who were supposed to receive children, but not necessarily their actual parents."

"Correct."

Isabelle had been quiet.

Now she spoke.

"Was my name in it?"

Edward looked at her.

"We don't know."

"Was Elena's?"

"We don't know that either."

"Then what exactly are we trying to prove?"

"Which infant was born to which mother, where each infant was transferred, and whether the identity assigned to each child was ever verified."

Isabelle looked down at her hands.

"That's a lot of questions for one child."

Edward did not correct her.

There were two maternal records, multiple receiving references, and several infants whose identities had been confused by administrative decisions.

But he understood what she meant.

For Isabelle, the investigation was not an abstract historical puzzle.

It concerned the child she had been told was dead.

Richard folded his hands.

"What happens now?"

"We apply for legal authority to inspect Part Two. At the same time, we trace the final recipient designation and identify the person who issued the original instructions."

"Can we do that without opening the envelope?"

"Yes. There are separate administrative records."

Catherine looked at him.

"And if the records don't exist?"

"Then we will have to establish why."

Isabelle looked toward the photograph of the sealed envelope.

"Please don't let anyone decide that we are not entitled to know simply because they have kept us waiting for eighteen years."

Edward met her eyes.

"We won't."

That evening, at the Aurelius Estate, Victoria received a separate update.

Alexander was reviewing the legal team's proposed application when she entered his study.

"Edward has found Part Two," she said.

"Yes."

"Has he opened it?"

"No."

Victoria placed her hand on the back of a chair.

"Do you know what I keep thinking about?"

Alexander looked up.

"What?"

"That we spent years searching for our daughter. We asked hospitals, authorities, investigators, and people who claimed to have information. Every time, someone told us the records were incomplete."

She paused.

"And now we discover that the records were not simply incomplete. They were deliberately separated, and the people responsible never completed the process."

Alexander closed the document.

"That is what we need to establish."

Victoria shook her head.

"I don't want another committee. I don't want another internal review that produces a report explaining why nobody was responsible."

"What do you want?"

"I want the original instructions. The people who signed them. The people who carried them out. And the person who decided our daughter could remain unidentified."

Alexander watched her for a moment.

Then he nodded.

"I'll instruct the legal team to pursue every available route."

Victoria sat down.

After a pause, she asked, "Have you heard anything about the Singapore programme?"

Alexander looked at her.

"Why?"

"Edward mentioned Elena's application in his last update. I wondered whether she had received a decision."

"No. He said the application had progressed to final selection."

Victoria smiled faintly.

"Good."

Alexander returned to his documents.

Neither spoke for a while.

The Aurelius family still had no proof that Elena was their missing daughter.

Victoria refused to allow herself to turn hope into certainty.

But she had begun to notice something about the girl whose identity they were investigating.

Even through second-hand reports, Elena seemed to be building a life of her own.

Victoria found herself hoping that, whatever the records eventually revealed, that part of Elena's life would remain hers.

The following morning, Dr. Mira Sen returned with her preliminary handwriting assessment.

Edward read it at his desk.

The anonymous warning did not match the known handwriting of Charles Vale, Adrian Holt, Marianne Cross, Eleanor Vale, or Margaret Voss.

However, Dr. Sen had identified a partial similarity with another document.

A letter from the original Annex C correspondence.

It was a note attached to the first transfer instruction.

The signature was illegible.

But the letter formation was consistent.

Edward examined the document.

The note contained only two sentences.

**The receiving identity must not be inferred from the originating medical record.**

**Final designation requires confirmation by the originating representative.**

At the bottom was a reference.

**AV-18/RECIPIENT SCHEDULE.**

Edward placed the new warning beside it.

The handwriting was similar.

Not enough to identify the writer conclusively.

But enough to suggest that the same person might have written both documents.

"Who prepared the original note?" Jonathan asked.

"The records say it came from the external custody office."

"Which office?"

"Vale Private Counsel."

Jonathan looked at him.

"So someone from the same organisation that managed the transfers may have sent the warning."

"Possibly."

Edward opened the correspondence index.

The original note had been delivered to Charles Vale.

A copy had been sent to Eleanor Vale.

A second copy had been sent to a recipient whose name had been concealed under the designation:

**O.R. — ORIGINATING REPRESENTATIVE.**

Edward checked the date.

It was the same day that the original recipient schedule had been created.

He turned to the final page.

There was a handwritten addition, almost invisible beneath the faded ink.

**Do not disclose the originating representative's identity to the receiving party until the recipient schedule is complete.**

Jonathan read it.

"That means they were hiding the biological family from the receiving family."

"Or hiding the receiving family from the originating family."

"Why?"

Edward looked at the two sealed records.

"Because someone believed that revealing one identity before confirming the other would compromise the arrangement."

"Or because they wanted to control who learned what."

Edward did not answer.

Both explanations remained possible.

He opened the old Annex C index once more.

The originating representatives were listed under two separate references.

**O.R.-A**

**O.R.-B**

There were no names.

But beside O.R.-A was a small note.

**Maternal identity confirmed by originating facility.**

Beside O.R.-B:

**Maternal identity awaiting independent confirmation.**

Edward read the second entry twice.

One of the two maternal identities had been confirmed by the originating facility.

The other had not.

And if the recipient schedule was never completed, the entire process might have remained unresolved from the beginning.

He reached for the telephone.

"Jonathan, find out who O.R.-A and O.R.-B were assigned to."

Jonathan was already searching.

Then he stopped.

"Edward."

"What?"

"Look at the date of the designation."

Edward turned back to the page.

The date was the ninth day after the births.

The same day Charles Vale had requested the delayed comparison.

The same day the receiving records had begun to diverge.

And beside the two originating references, someone had written a final instruction.

**O.R.-A must not be treated as the receiving mother.**

**O.R.-B must not be treated as the originating mother.**

Edward slowly lowered the page.

For eighteen years, everyone had been searching for the correct family.

But the original records suggested that somebody had made a different mistake.

They had confused the people who gave birth to the children with the people who were supposed to receive them.

And the person who had written the warning now knew that the distinction still mattered.
