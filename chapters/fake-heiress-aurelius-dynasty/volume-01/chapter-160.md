# Chapter 160 — The Replacement Who Was Never Appointed

Family Allocation Review had received the day-144 summary on day 145.

Edward expected to find a decision.

Instead, he found a new entry.

**R-17 — REPRESENTATIVE DECLINED APPOINTMENT.**

**ACTION REQUIRED:** Identify replacement personal-channel representative.

The entry had been created at 10:18.

A second field appeared beneath it.

**PROPOSED REPLACEMENT: PENDING.**

Edward read the two lines again.

"They recorded a refusal and immediately began looking for someone else."

Jonathan checked the source record.

"Yes."

"Was the refusal verified?"

"No."

"Was the original procedure delivered?"

"No."

"Was the individual's identity confirmed?"

"No."

Edward closed the file.

"Then the replacement process began on a conclusion that had never been established."

The replacement search was not part of the receiving-family allocation process.

It belonged to a separate administrative category called Personal Representation Continuity.

The distinction mattered.

A personal-channel representative was supposed to ensure that a protected individual received information, understood the available choices and could communicate a decision without another person supplying that decision on their behalf.

The representative was not supposed to choose a family.

They were not supposed to determine biological identity.

And they were not supposed to make a decision for the protected principal.

Edward found the original continuity procedure.

It required three conditions before a replacement could be appointed:

1. The previous nominee's status had to be independently established.
2. The proposed replacement had to receive the original procedure.
3. The proposed replacement had to accept the limited role through a verified channel.

None of those conditions had been completed for R-17.

Yet the search had begun.

The first proposed replacement was listed as **P.R.-06**.

It was not a person's name.

It was a reference to an external professional service.

Edward requested the original nomination.

The archive response arrived two days later.

The proposed service was operated by a small legal-administrative firm that specialised in protected correspondence, document custody and independent records verification.

Its managing director had been approached on day 146.

The request described the assignment as:

*Temporary personal representation support pending confirmation of the existing representative's status.*

Edward frowned.

"That wording is already misleading."

Jonathan read the request.

"It says the existing representative's status is pending."

"Which means the office was not supposed to treat R-17 as having declined."

"Correct."

"But the request also says a replacement is required."

"Yes."

Edward placed the two documents beside each other.

The first said the previous representative's status was unresolved.

The second treated a replacement as necessary.

Someone had begun arranging a substitute without establishing that the original nominee had withdrawn.

The firm's managing director, Harriet Cole, agreed to speak with Edward.

She brought her original correspondence file.

"I remember receiving the proposal," she said.

"Did you accept?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the proposed assignment was not sufficiently defined."

Edward showed her the request.

Harriet read it.

"The description says I would maintain communication with a protected principal and receive personal responses on their behalf."

"Was that within the role?"

"Not as written. I could preserve documents and verify delivery. I could not accept personal responses as though I were the principal's representative without a defined authority."

"Did you request clarification?"

"Yes."

She produced her reply.

*Before considering the assignment, please provide the original communication procedure, the scope of the proposed authority, the identity-verification method and confirmation that the previous nominee's status has been independently determined.*

Edward examined the date.

Day 147.

"Did they respond?"

"No."

"Did you withdraw?"

Harriet shook her head.

"I never accepted the proposal. There was nothing to withdraw from."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

Another person had been considered for a role without receiving the information necessary to decide whether to accept it.

The Family Allocation Review register recorded Harriet's response differently.

**REPLACEMENT NOMINEE: DECLINED.**

**REASON:** UNWILLING TO ASSUME PERSONAL REPRESENTATION RESPONSIBILITY.

Edward read the entry.

"She didn't decline the role."

Harriet looked at the summary.

"I asked what the role involved."

"Did you receive an explanation?"

"No."

"Did you receive the original procedure?"

"No."

"Then this entry is inaccurate."

Harriet nodded.

"I was willing to consider a properly defined document-preservation assignment. I was not willing to accept an undefined personal authority."

Edward turned to the register.

"Who entered the word declined?"

The operator field contained another shared account.

**FA-ADMIN / REVIEW DESK.**

The same account had been involved in earlier changes to the OR-28 and Schedule 4 records.

Jonathan made a note.

But Edward did not immediately connect the entry to Daniel Mercer or Patricia Moore.

A shared account could identify a system route.

It could not establish who had typed the words.

The second replacement proposal was more unusual.

It named **M.V.**

Margaret Voss.

Edward visited her that afternoon.

She read the proposal without speaking.

Then she placed it on the table.

"I refused this."

"Why?"

"Because I had no authority to represent the protected principal."

"Did you receive the original procedure?"

"No."

"Did you receive the full scope of the assignment?"

"No."

"Did you know who the previous nominee was?"

"No."

"Then why were you approached?"

Margaret looked at the reference.

"Because I held the restricted records and understood the administrative history. Someone may have decided that made me suitable."

"Did you agree to be considered?"

"I agreed to review the written scope if it was properly authorised. I did not agree to become a personal representative."

"Did you send a response?"

"Yes. Day 149."

She retrieved a copy.

*I am willing to clarify the records in my custody and provide a preservation history. I am not willing to assume personal representation authority, make decisions on behalf of the protected principal, or substitute my administrative knowledge for the principal's own acknowledgement.*

Edward read it.

It was precise.

Margaret had not refused to help.

She had refused to be given a role she could not lawfully perform.

"How was your response recorded?" he asked.

Margaret handed him the later summary.

**M.V. — REPLACEMENT REPRESENTATIVE: DECLINED.**

Edward looked at her.

"Did you object?"

"Immediately."

"Was it corrected?"

"No."

Margaret's objection contained an additional attachment.

It was a copy of the day-149 replacement nomination request.

At the bottom, a sentence had been added:

*The replacement shall be permitted to act on the basis of existing administrative continuity until the original representative's status is resolved.*

Edward read it twice.

"Who added this?"

"I don't know."

"Did you receive it before responding?"

"No. It was attached to the later summary."

Jonathan examined the document.

"The sentence would allow a replacement to act before being formally appointed."

"Which is exactly what the procedure prohibits," Margaret said.

Edward nodded.

The proposed replacement was supposed to preserve communication.

But the amendment would allow that person to exercise a representative function before receiving the original procedure or accepting the role.

It would turn a temporary administrative arrangement into authority.

Margaret looked at Edward.

"That was why I refused."

The third replacement proposal was sent to Anna Mercer-Smith.

Edward found the record in the Cross Administrative Services archive.

The request was dated day 150.

Anna had been approached because of her previous work as a temporary records liaison at St Catherine's.

The proposed assignment was described as:

**PERSONAL CHANNEL CONTINUITY AND ORIGINATING RECORDS COORDINATION.**

Anna had written a handwritten response.

*I can assist with the transfer and preservation of originating medical records. I cannot act as a personal representative, confirm the identity of a protected principal, or receive personal communication on another person's behalf.*

She had also written:

*Please do not use my previous employment at St Catherine's as evidence that I possess authority over the identity of any child.*

Edward read the sentence carefully.

It was not a general disclaimer.

Anna was concerned that her former access to hospital records might be used to suggest knowledge or authority she did not possess.

"Did she receive the original procedure?" Jonathan asked.

"No."

"Was her response recorded as a refusal?"

Edward checked the administrative summary.

**A.M. — REPLACEMENT NOMINEE: DECLINED.**

The same pattern.

Three people had been approached.

Three people had asked for proper information or clarified the limits of what they could do.

All three had been recorded as having declined.

None had been appointed.

Edward arranged a meeting with the three women.

Margaret attended in person.

Anna joined through a secure video conference.

Harriet Cole arrived with her firm's original correspondence.

Edward placed the three responses side by side.

"None of you accepted personal representation authority."

"Correct," Harriet said.

"None of you received the original procedure."

Anna nodded.

"Correct."

"None of you was given verified information about the protected principal."

Margaret folded her hands.

"No."

Edward placed the administrative summaries beside them.

"Yet all three were recorded as declining."

Harriet examined the documents.

"That is not what I said."

"Nor I," Anna replied.

Margaret looked at the final summary.

"Someone wanted a list of people who had refused."

Edward considered that.

"Or a list of people who had not accepted."

"The distinction matters," Margaret said.

"Yes."

Anna looked at the three records.

"Did the office ever ask any of us whether we wanted to receive the procedure?"

"No," Edward replied.

"Then it did not establish whether we would have accepted."

Harriet nodded.

"An offer that is never properly made cannot be treated as declined."

Edward wrote down the sentence.

It was a useful distinction.

The replacement search had a final stage.

After the third nominee, the office had prepared a document titled:

**PERSONAL REPRESENTATION CONTINUITY — ADMINISTRATIVE DEFAULT.**

The proposal stated that if no replacement accepted within seven days, the existing document custodian could continue performing limited personal-channel functions.

The proposed custodian was Charles Vale.

Edward read the document in silence.

The authority was described as temporary.

It allowed Charles to:

- Preserve personal-channel correspondence.
- Receive administrative notices.
- Maintain the status register.
- Record the absence of a personal response.
- Coordinate further communication when permitted.

But a separate paragraph gave the custodian permission to record the current administrative position if no personal representative was available.

Edward placed the paper down.

"That last provision is not preservation."

Jonathan read it.

"No. It allows the custodian to record a position without a verified personal response."

"Was it approved?"

The document carried a status stamp.

**APPROVED FOR ADMINISTRATIVE CONTINUITY.**

The approval reference was PC-04.

There was no individual signature.

Thomas Greer examined the document the following morning.

He read the authority clause and shook his head.

"This is not the wording I approved."

"Did you review it?" Edward asked.

"I reviewed an earlier draft."

"Was Charles permitted to maintain the correspondence register?"

"Yes."

"Could he record that a response had not been received?"

"Yes."

"Could he record an administrative position on behalf of the protected principal?"

"No."

"Could he treat the representative's absence as a decision?"

"Absolutely not."

Edward showed him the final paragraph.

Greer read it twice.

"This has expanded the custodian's authority."

"Was the expansion authorised?"

"No."

"Could PC-04 approve it?"

"No. PC-04 was a shared administrative access code, not a legal authority."

Edward placed the approval stamp beside the document.

"Then how did it become approved?"

Greer examined the stamp.

"I cannot tell from this copy."

The original draft was eventually located in Greer's private correspondence archive.

It was dated day 151.

The original had contained a very different clause.

*Where no personal representative has been appointed, the document custodian may continue preservation and administrative notification functions only. No personal response, acceptance, refusal or decision may be inferred.*

The final version had removed the last sentence.

In its place appeared:

*The current administrative position may be retained for continuity until a verified representative is appointed.*

Edward compared the two.

The new wording did not explicitly grant Charles the power to decide for the principal.

But it allowed an unresolved administrative position to remain active without a defined expiry or review.

That was enough to preserve the same arrangement indefinitely.

"Who revised it?" Edward asked.

Greer checked the document history.

"FA-ADMIN / REVIEW DESK."

"Was the revision sent to you?"

"No."

"Did you approve it?"

"No."

"Did Charles?"

"The file contains no signed approval."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"Then we need the distribution record."

The distribution record showed that the revised continuity proposal had been sent to four offices.

V-17 Personal Custody.

Vale Private Counsel.

Northbridge Continuity Trust.

Family Allocation Review.

Northbridge had returned it with a warning.

*Administrative continuity cannot be treated as beneficiary designation, receiving identity verification, or personal consent.*

The Family Allocation Review Office had acknowledged receipt.

It had not accepted the authority.

Vale Private Counsel had filed it as a working instruction.

V-17 had marked it:

**RETAIN PENDING FORMAL APPOINTMENT.**

Edward examined the dates.

The instruction had been prepared before the day-157 migration entry that expanded the R.V. exception.

It had also preceded the day-162 request to move the Original Channel Control Register.

The same unresolved issue was appearing in different forms.

An unappointed representative.

An unverified personal channel.

A document custodian whose role was expanding.

And administrative records that described unfinished processes as completed.

That evening, Elena sat in a quiet study room at her Singapore accommodation.

Her laptop displayed the grant-management platform she had been developing.

She had added a new section to the application history.

**Decision status**

- Not yet considered.
- Information requested.
- Under review.
- Accepted.
- Declined.
- Withdrawn.

Clara leaned closer to the camera.

"Why did you add so many categories?"

"Because I realised that 'not accepted' doesn't necessarily mean 'declined'."

Clara smiled.

"Someone could still be thinking."

"Exactly. Or they might not have received the information. Or the request might never have reached them."

"So you are making a system that refuses to guess."

"That's the idea."

Elena clicked through the workflow.

"If someone asks for more information, the application stays open. If they withdraw, that's recorded separately. And if the invitation was never delivered, the system can't mark them as refusing."

Clara nodded.

"That would prevent quite a few arguments."

"It might also prevent people from pretending a process is complete just because a deadline passed."

Elena leaned back.

She was pleased with the improvement.

It was a small feature, but it made the platform more honest.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Isabelle appeared.

*I reviewed the foundation's grant application workflow. Your distinction between acknowledgement and acceptance would solve one of our recurring problems.*

Elena replied.

*Then perhaps you should steal the idea.*

Isabelle responded almost immediately.

*I would rather credit you and ask permission.*

Elena smiled.

*Permission granted.*

She returned to her work.

For once, the problem she was solving had a clear boundary.

Edward received the final document shortly before midnight.

It was a day-152 objection from the Northbridge Continuity Trust.

The trust had refused to recognise Charles Vale's expanded administrative continuity authority.

Its response was brief.

*The document custodian may preserve correspondence and maintain temporary records. The custodian may not represent the protected principal, create personal consent, appoint a receiving representative, or determine a beneficiary.*

Beneath that was an additional question.

*Please identify the independent authority under which the current administrative position is being maintained.*

The answer field was blank.

But someone had written a note in the margin.

*Do not request a new appointment. Existing continuity remains active.*

The handwriting was the same anonymous hand.

Edward placed the note beside the day-151 revised proposal.

The anonymous writer had not merely been preserving the original procedure.

They had repeatedly intervened whenever someone tried to create a new appointment.

That could have been an attempt to prevent another invalid authority from being established.

Or it could have been a way to keep the existing arrangement alive.

Edward looked at the three rejected replacement responses.

Harriet Cole.

Margaret Voss.

Anna Mercer-Smith.

None had declined a properly explained role.

None had received the original procedure.

And none had been allowed to replace the unresolved R-17 nominee.

He opened the day-152 register again.

A new entry had been added after the trust's objection.

**PERSONAL REPRESENTATION STATUS: CONTINUITY MAINTAINED.**

**RESPONSIBLE CUSTODIAN: C. VALE.**

The entry had been made without a signature.

Edward stared at the screen.

Charles had been the temporary document custodian.

Now the system was listing him as the responsible custodian of the entire personal representation process.

The difference was substantial.

And the entry had been created before any valid appointment had been completed.

Edward reached for his phone.

"Jonathan?"

"Yes?"

"Find every document in which Charles Vale's role changes from document custodian to responsible custodian."

There was a pause.

"Do you think he authorised the change?"

"I don't know."

Edward looked at the unsigned entry.

"But someone has been using his temporary custody role to maintain an authority that was never formally granted."

He ended the call.

On the screen, one final field remained blank.

**DATE OF APPOINTMENT: NOT RECORDED.**

Yet the system continued to describe the appointment as active.
