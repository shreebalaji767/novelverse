# **Chapter 16 — The Package Delivered to the Wrong Mother**

Catherine Valmont had forgotten many things from the days surrounding Elena's birth.

She remembered the hospital room.

She remembered the uncomfortable chair beside the bed, the soft beeping of medical equipment, and Richard's habit of checking his watch every few minutes because he had been worried about the business meeting he had postponed.

She remembered holding her newborn daughter.

She remembered the tiny fingers curling around hers.

And she remembered the hospital administrator apologising for a delay involving the discharge documents.

But she did not remember receiving a mysterious package.

That was why, when Richard called her into his study on Tuesday morning, she initially assumed he wanted to discuss the latest hospital report.

Instead, he placed a single sheet of paper on the desk.

"Do you recognise this?"

Catherine approached.

It was a scanned copy of an old courier register.

She read the date.

Then the delivery description.

Then the recipient's name.

Her expression changed.

"Catherine Valmont."

Richard nodded.

"Do you remember receiving anything from the hospital or the legal office around that time?"

She studied the document more carefully.

"No."

"Think carefully."

"I am thinking."

The sharpness in her voice surprised them both.

Catherine lowered her eyes.

"I'm sorry. I don't mean to sound irritated."

Richard moved closer.

"There's no need to apologise."

She touched the paper.

"Who sent this?"

"We're still establishing that. It was connected to the original maternity identity report."

"Whose report?"

"The report associated with the Aurelius child."

Catherine looked up.

"Why would it have been sent to me?"

"We don't know."

"Could the register be wrong?"

"Possibly."

She looked at the date again.

Three days after the original report had been delivered to Martin Hale.

A package.

Addressed to Victoria Aurelius.

Signed for by Catherine Valmont.

She shook her head.

"I would remember something like this."

Richard said nothing.

Catherine closed her eyes.

"Unless it was delivered to the house and handled by someone else."

"That's possible."

"Mr. Harris wasn't working here then."

"No. He joined us later."

Catherine looked at the paper again.

"Who was managing the household?"

Richard thought for a moment.

"Mrs. Collins. She handled the household administration at the time."

"She's retired."

"I know."

Catherine took a breath.

"Then we should speak to her."

At breakfast, Elena noticed that her mother was distracted.

Catherine had poured tea into her cup without adding any sugar, despite usually taking one spoonful.

She had also been looking at the same page of her tablet for several minutes.

"Mother?"

Catherine looked up.

"Yes, sweetheart?"

"Your tea."

Catherine glanced at the cup.

"What about it?"

"You forgot the sugar."

A faint smile appeared on her face.

"Thank you."

Elena pushed the sugar bowl toward her.

Nicholas watched the exchange.

"Perhaps Mother is finally becoming healthier."

Catherine gave him a look.

"I have never been unhealthy."

"You've been drinking tea with sugar for years. I assumed it was a medical condition."

Sebastian continued reading his newspaper.

"Your ability to speak without thinking remains remarkable."

"Thank you."

"It wasn't praise."

Nicholas looked satisfied anyway.

Elena glanced toward Catherine.

"Is everything all right?"

Catherine hesitated.

"There's something new in the investigation. Richard and Edward are looking into an old document."

"Does it explain what happened?"

"Not yet."

Catherine reached across the table and gently touched Elena's hand.

"Don't worry about it during your classes today."

Elena looked at her.

"I'm trying not to."

"I know."

Isabelle, sitting opposite them, had been listening quietly.

"Does it involve the package Edward mentioned?"

Catherine's hand became still.

Richard looked up.

"What package?"

Isabelle seemed surprised.

"Edward mentioned that they had found an old delivery record. I thought it was related to the report."

Catherine looked at Richard.

He gave a small nod.

"We'll discuss it with Edward before sharing anything further."

Elena noticed the exchange.

She did not ask another question.

Instead, she returned to her breakfast.

But the uneasy feeling remained.

The Aurelius family meeting began at ten.

Alexander, Adrian, Damien and Lucien were present.

Victoria joined them a few minutes later.

Julian was supposed to be attending school, but he had managed to delay leaving by claiming that he needed to collect a document from his father.

The document in question was a drawing he had made of the family.

It contained seven people.

He had drawn an empty space beside himself and written:

**SISTER GOES HERE.**

Victoria had kept it on her desk.

Now Julian was standing near the door, listening.

Alexander noticed him.

"Shouldn't you be at school?"

"I have time."

"Your classes begin at ten."

"Yes."

"It is ten."

Julian glanced at the clock.

"Then I should go."

He did not move.

Lucien smiled.

"Excellent demonstration of responsibility."

Julian ignored him.

Adrian opened the meeting.

"The second dispatch was recorded as delivered to the Valmont residence. The courier register contains a signature matching Catherine Valmont's name."

Victoria looked down at the paper.

"Are we certain it was her signature?"

"No. The signature is difficult to compare because the scan is incomplete. We are requesting the original register."

Alexander folded his hands.

"Could it have been delivered to the household staff?"

"Yes. The recipient field identifies Catherine, but the actual person accepting the package may have been someone else."

Damien spoke.

"Then we need the household records from that period."

"Agreed," Adrian said.

Lucien leaned back.

"Why would someone send the report to the Valmont family?"

No one answered.

"Could it have been an attempt to inform them about the switch?" he continued.

"Or an attempt to involve them," Damien said.

Victoria looked at him.

"Do you think the Valmonts knew?"

"We don't have enough information to say."

Alexander turned toward Adrian.

"Contact Richard. We need to establish whether Catherine remembers the delivery."

Adrian nodded.

"I'll arrange it."

Julian finally spoke.

"Does this mean we might find her soon?"

The room fell quiet.

Alexander looked at his youngest son.

"It means we have another lead."

"That's not what I asked."

"No," Alexander said gently. "It isn't."

Julian lowered his eyes.

Victoria reached for his hand.

"We're getting closer to understanding what happened."

"Will she know about us?"

"If we find her, we'll tell her the truth."

Julian nodded.

Then he looked at the empty space on the family drawing in his hand.

"I hope she likes drawing."

Lucien smiled.

"Perhaps she prefers something else."

"Then I'll learn that too."

At the Valmont Estate, Edward was reviewing the courier record with Daniel, the hospital investigator.

"Have you confirmed the delivery company?"

"Yes. It was a private courier service that handled confidential medical and legal correspondence."

"Does it still exist?"

"Not under the original name. It was acquired by another company fifteen years ago. Their surviving records are incomplete, but the paper registers were transferred to an archive."

Edward pointed to the signature.

"Can they authenticate this?"

"They can examine the original."

"And the delivery address?"

"That is more interesting."

Daniel placed another sheet on the table.

"The package was addressed to the Valmont residence, but the sender's details were incomplete."

Edward frowned.

"How incomplete?"

"The sender's office reference is present. The name is missing."

"Can we trace the reference?"

"Possibly. It was issued by the former Aurelius & Vale legal office."

Edward leaned back.

"So someone connected to the legal office sent a package intended for Victoria Aurelius to the Valmont household."

"That's what the record suggests."

"Why?"

Daniel shook his head.

"There's another detail. The package was marked for personal delivery. It required the named recipient's signature."

Edward looked at the register.

"Yet someone else signed for it."

"Yes."

"Was the courier instructed to leave it anyway?"

"There's no note."

Edward studied the document.

The delivery itself might have been an innocent mistake.

A wrong address.

An incorrect label.

A package received by household staff.

But the timing was difficult to ignore.

Three days after Martin Hale received the original report, another document had been sent.

And the person who had supposedly received it was Catherine Valmont.

"Find out who was working at the courier company that day," Edward said.

Daniel nodded.

"And find the original package, if it still exists."

Daniel hesitated.

"After eighteen years?"

"Try."

That afternoon, Elena and Isabelle were studying in the library.

The room was quiet except for the occasional sound of pages turning.

Elena was working through a set of economics questions.

Isabelle was reading a university prospectus.

After several minutes, Isabelle closed it.

"Can I ask you something?"

Elena looked up.

"Of course."

"Do you ever feel strange about the way everyone talks about the DNA results?"

Elena put down her pen.

"Strange how?"

"As though we're waiting for someone to tell us which family we belong to."

Elena considered that.

"Sometimes."

"Does it bother you?"

"Yes."

Isabelle looked at her.

Elena continued.

"I understand why the tests matter. But sometimes it feels like everyone has stopped talking about anything else."

"That's true."

"I miss discussing ordinary things."

Isabelle smiled faintly.

"Like university?"

"Or Nicholas stealing food."

"That's not ordinary. That's a daily crisis."

Elena laughed.

"Exactly."

Isabelle looked at the prospectus again.

"I've been thinking about what happens if the results confirm everything."

Elena waited.

"I know I'm their biological daughter. But I still feel as though I'm learning how to be part of this family."

"That's normal."

"And you?"

Elena looked toward the window.

"I've been part of it for eighteen years. But now I don't know what everyone expects me to be."

Isabelle's expression softened.

"I don't want you to feel like you have to compete with me."

Elena smiled.

"Then don't compete."

"That's easy for you to say."

"It is. I'm very talented."

Isabelle laughed.

For a moment, the weight of the investigation lifted.

Then Isabelle spoke again.

"Do you think our mothers know more than they're telling us?"

Elena looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because sometimes they look at us as though they're afraid of something."

Elena thought about Catherine's distracted expression that morning.

"I don't know."

"Would you want to know if they did?"

"Yes."

Isabelle nodded.

"So would I."

They returned to their books.

Neither noticed Edward standing outside the library door.

He had come to ask Elena whether she could attend a meeting the following day.

But he had heard enough to understand that the girls were beginning to ask the questions the adults were trying to avoid.

Catherine met Mrs. Collins at a small restaurant near the city centre.

The former household manager had retired several years earlier and now lived with her daughter.

She was delighted to see Catherine.

"You haven't changed at all."

Catherine smiled.

"Neither have you."

Mrs. Collins laughed.

"That's because I have learned the secret of retirement. I simply refuse to be busy."

They ordered tea.

After a few minutes of conversation, Catherine placed the courier record on the table.

"Do you remember anything being delivered to the house on this date?"

Mrs. Collins adjusted her glasses.

"That was a long time ago."

"I know."

She studied the paper.

"A confidential package?"

"Yes."

Mrs. Collins looked at the date.

Then her expression changed.

"I remember something."

Catherine leaned forward.

"What?"

"There was a delivery that week. I don't remember the exact day, but it was a large envelope. It arrived in the afternoon."

"Who received it?"

"I think I did."

Catherine felt her pulse quicken.

"Did you sign for it?"

Mrs. Collins looked at the register.

"Perhaps. I can't be certain."

"Was it addressed to me?"

Mrs. Collins hesitated.

"I remember your name being on it."

Catherine's hands tightened around her teacup.

"What happened to it?"

"I gave it to the office."

"Which office?"

"The private family office. Your husband had asked that all confidential correspondence be taken there."

"Did you see what was inside?"

"No. It was sealed."

Catherine looked down.

"Do you remember who collected it?"

Mrs. Collins thought for a long moment.

"Mr. Hale."

Catherine looked up sharply.

"Martin Hale?"

Mrs. Collins nodded.

"He came to the house that afternoon. I remember because I thought it was unusual for someone from the Aurelius office to collect a package from us."

Catherine went very still.

"Did he say anything?"

"Only that it had been sent to the wrong address and needed to be returned."

"Did you give it to him?"

"Yes."

Catherine stared at her.

"Did you tell Richard?"

"I don't think so. It seemed like an administrative mistake."

Mrs. Collins looked troubled.

"Was it important?"

Catherine did not answer immediately.

Then she said:

"I think it may have been."

Richard received Catherine's call shortly after the meeting ended.

"Martin Hale collected the package from the house," she told him.

Richard stood from his desk.

"Are you certain?"

"Mrs. Collins remembers him. She said he claimed the package had been sent to the wrong address."

"Did he return it?"

"She doesn't know."

Richard looked toward the window.

"Then the courier record may have been deliberately misleading."

"Or the package was genuinely misaddressed and he happened to collect it."

"Possibly."

Catherine was silent.

Then she asked, "Richard, did you know Martin Hale?"

"Only through the Aurelius family office. He handled confidential correspondence."

"Did you ever authorise him to collect documents from our house?"

"No."

Catherine closed her eyes.

"Then why was he there?"

Richard did not answer.

He called Edward immediately.

At the Aurelius Estate, Adrian received a similar update.

Martin Hale had collected the package from the Valmont residence on the same day it was delivered.

The explanation recorded in the courier notes was simple.

**Incorrect destination. Returned to authorised representative.**

But there was no record of the package reaching the legal office afterward.

No return receipt.

No archive entry.

No storage reference.

It had simply disappeared.

Adrian carried the update to Alexander.

"The package never reached the family office."

Alexander read the report.

"Who collected it?"

"Martin Hale."

"From the Valmont residence?"

"Yes."

"Did Catherine Valmont open it?"

"According to the former household manager, no. She never saw it."

Alexander slowly put the paper down.

"Then Catherine may not have known anything about it."

"That seems likely."

Victoria looked at the report.

"Why would Hale collect it himself?"

Adrian answered carefully.

"Perhaps he was correcting an administrative mistake. Or perhaps he wanted to prevent the document from reaching either family."

Alexander looked at the name again.

Martin Hale.

The man who had received the original report.

The man who had collected the second package.

The man who had left the family office twelve years later without any recorded explanation.

"Find him," Alexander said.

"We're working on it."

"No. I want a direct meeting."

Adrian hesitated.

"If he is involved, he may refuse."

"Then ask him what he remembers."

"And if he still refuses?"

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Remind him that he was entrusted with confidential family records. He can explain what happened eighteen years ago, or he can explain why he chose to conceal it."

That evening, Elena was in her bedroom when Catherine came to see her.

She was sitting at her desk, comparing university programmes.

Catherine knocked gently.

"May I come in?"

"Of course."

Catherine entered and sat on the edge of the bed.

"How was your day?"

"Good. Isabelle and I studied together."

"That's nice."

"We're comparing courses. She found a programme I hadn't considered."

Catherine smiled.

"I'm glad you're spending time together."

Elena looked at her.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"Are you worried about something?"

Catherine's smile faded slightly.

"Why do you ask?"

"You've been distracted all day."

Catherine looked at her daughter.

For eighteen years, she had called Elena her daughter.

She had watched her grow, celebrated her achievements, worried about her illnesses, and comforted her after disappointments.

Whatever the hospital records said, those memories were real.

But now there was a question Catherine could not answer.

What if the truth revealed that she had unknowingly received a child who was never meant to be hers?

What if someone had deliberately placed Elena in her arms?

And what if the same person had taken away the child she had given birth to?

Catherine reached for Elena's hand.

"There are still some things we don't understand."

"About the hospital?"

"Yes."

Elena waited.

Catherine wanted to tell her everything.

But she did not yet know what everything was.

"I promise we'll tell you when we have reliable answers."

Elena nodded.

"All right."

Catherine brushed a strand of hair away from her daughter's face.

"Don't forget to rest. You have examinations coming up."

Elena smiled.

"I know."

Catherine stood and moved toward the door.

"Goodnight, sweetheart."

"Goodnight, Mother."

The word followed Catherine into the corridor.

She stopped briefly outside the room.

Then she continued walking.

At 10:15 p.m., Adrian received a message from the private investigator assigned to locate Martin Hale.

**Mr. Hale has been located. He is currently staying at a private residence outside the capital. He has declined to comment on the historical records.**

Adrian read the message.

A second message arrived.

**There is another matter. Mr. Hale recently requested access to a storage unit registered under his former company account. The unit is due to be cleared in four days.**

Adrian immediately called Damien.

"Where is the storage unit?"

Damien checked the location.

"Why?"

"Because it may contain the original report."

"Do we have permission to access it?"

"Not yet."

"Then we need to move carefully."

Adrian looked at the message again.

Four days.

Martin Hale had kept a storage unit connected to his former company account for eighteen years.

And now, shortly after the investigation had reached him, he had requested access to it.

Adrian called his father.

Alexander answered immediately.

"What happened?"

"We've located Hale. He's requested access to an old storage unit. It is scheduled to be cleared in four days."

Alexander was silent.

"Do you think the original report is there?" he asked.

"I think we need to find out before it disappears."

Alexander looked toward the old nursery.

"Arrange a lawful preservation request. Don't let anyone touch the contents until we have the necessary authority."

"Understood."

"And Adrian?"

"Yes?"

"If that report contains the truth about my daughter, I want to see it before anyone else."

Adrian ended the call.

Across the city, Martin Hale stood inside a dimly lit storage unit.

Several boxes were stacked against the walls.

He opened one.

Inside were old office files, envelopes and photographs.

At the bottom lay a sealed brown folder.

Its label had faded, but the writing remained legible.

**AURELIUS — MATERNITY IDENTITY REPORT**

Hale stared at it for a long time.

Then he took out his phone.

He opened a contact he had not called in eighteen years.

The number still worked.

He hesitated before pressing the call button.

When the other person answered, Hale spoke in a low voice.

"They've found the archive."

A pause.

"Yes. The Aurelius family."

Another pause.

"They're asking about the report."

He looked at the sealed folder.

"I can't keep this hidden anymore."

The voice on the other end said something.

Hale closed his eyes.

Then he answered:

"Because the girl they were searching for wasn't the only child involved."

And the call ended.
