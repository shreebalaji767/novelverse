# **Chapter 72 — The Child Who Wasn't the Aurelius Infant**

Edward had learned to distrust sentences that appeared too certain in incomplete records.

**Receiving party to be informed that the child is not the Aurelius infant.**

He read it again.

Then he placed the Northbridge register beside the sixth-day transfer document from St Catherine's.

The same reference appeared on both.

**A-17.**

One document concerned Infant A, who had been taken from St Catherine's by Gabriel Vale on the sixth day after the births.

The other concerned Child 28-B, who had spent seven days at a private residence before being moved to an unnamed external facility.

Between those two records lay eighteen years of missing information.

Jonathan studied the papers across the desk.

"Do you think they're connected?"

"They are connected administratively."

"That isn't what I asked."

Edward leaned back.

"I don't know whether they're the same child. I don't know whether they're different children. And I don't know whether the instruction was true."

Jonathan tapped the handwritten note.

"But someone wanted the receiving party to believe they were different."

"Exactly."

Edward turned the paper over.

The reverse was blank.

There was no signature, no date, and no indication of who had written it.

"Let's find out what A-17 actually means."

The document examiner arrived shortly before eleven.

Her name was Dr. Helen Ward. She had previously examined the reproduced signatures on the Northbridge schedule and the disputed authorisation bearing Victoria Aurelius's name.

She placed the two documents beneath a magnifying lamp.

For several minutes, she said nothing.

Edward waited.

Finally, she pointed to the handwritten instruction.

"This note was added after the register was prepared."

"How can you tell?"

"The ink sits over an existing fold impression. The register had already been folded and stored before this sentence was written."

"Can you date it?"

"Not precisely. I can tell you the sequence, not the calendar date."

She examined the reference.

"The A-17 notation is different."

"Different handwriting?"

"Possibly. More importantly, it appears to have been entered using a different writing instrument. The reference itself was already present when the note was added."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

"So A-17 was not invented to explain the sentence."

"Correct," Helen replied. "It was an existing reference. Someone used it when writing the instruction."

"Can you compare the handwriting with the other documents?"

"I can compare characteristics. I cannot identify an individual from such a short sentence with confidence."

She moved to the sixth-day transfer document.

The paper had been recovered from a sealed administrative envelope. Its original contents were partially faded, but the transfer reference remained legible.

Helen placed it beside the Northbridge register.

"Look at the number."

Edward leaned forward.

The A-17 on both documents had the same unusual shape.

The first character was narrow. The seven had a short horizontal stroke that extended slightly beyond the vertical line.

"Same writer?" Jonathan asked.

"Possibly the same hand, but I wouldn't certify that from two short entries. What I can tell you is that the references were probably entered by someone following the same administrative convention."

Edward looked at the two documents again.

"Then the next question is where that convention came from."

Helen closed her folder.

"That is outside my examination."

Edward thanked her.

After she left, Jonathan gathered the documents.

"We need Gabriel."

"We need more than Gabriel's memory."

"His company records?"

"Everything connected to A-17. Transport invoices, receiving acknowledgements, medical liaison records, and the original instructions."

Jonathan gave him a tired look.

"You make it sound simple."

"It isn't."

Edward picked up the Northbridge register.

"But someone moved a child twice and left paperwork behind. We should be able to find at least one person who remembers the journey."

Gabriel Vale did not look pleased when Edward requested another meeting.

He nevertheless agreed to receive them at his office.

Cross Administrative Services had been dissolved years earlier, but Gabriel still maintained a private consultancy. His office was smaller than the one he had occupied during the period under investigation.

The reception area contained little decoration.

A framed professional certificate hung on one wall. Two filing cabinets stood behind the receptionist's desk.

Gabriel greeted Edward and Jonathan without offering unnecessary pleasantries.

"I understand you have found another reference."

Edward placed a copy of the sixth-day transfer document on the desk.

"A-17."

Gabriel glanced at it.

His expression barely changed.

"That is an administrative route code."

"What did it identify?"

"An approved transport arrangement."

"For which child?"

"That depends on the date and the accompanying transfer instruction."

"Then let's begin with the sixth day."

Gabriel folded his hands.

"I collected Infant A from St Catherine's."

"You have said that before."

"I have also explained that the transfer was conducted under emergency temporary authority."

"Authority that has never been properly established."

Gabriel's jaw tightened.

"That is your interpretation."

"It is the documented position. Alexander Aurelius did not authorise an individual transfer. The signature used on the Northbridge schedule was taken from a general expenditure instruction."

Gabriel said nothing.

Edward continued.

"Did A-17 identify the destination?"

"It identified the transport arrangement."

"Which destination?"

"I would need to see the complete file."

Jonathan opened his folder and produced a copy of the relevant page.

Gabriel studied it.

"This is incomplete."

"We know."

"Then I cannot give you a reliable answer."

Edward watched him carefully.

"You were a shareholder in Cross Administrative Services. You approved invoices for the company. You also acted as medical liaison for Northbridge."

"Yes."

"Did you know that the company received money from an Aurelius Family Office account?"

"I knew the company received payments through an administrative account."

"Did you know the source?"

"Not necessarily. The account was managed by Charles Vale's office."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the answer I can give you."

Edward changed direction.

"Let's discuss Child 28-B."

For the first time, Gabriel's expression shifted.

Only slightly.

But Edward noticed.

"She was at Northbridge from the ninety-sixth to the one hundred and third day after the births," Edward said. "The register lists Margaret Voss as receiving representative, Marianne Cross as administrative coordinator, and you as medical liaison."

"That is what the register says."

"Did you examine the child?"

"I reviewed the medical documents."

"Did you physically see her?"

Gabriel paused.

"I saw several children during that period."

"That wasn't my question."

"I cannot recall every individual visit."

"Did you see Child 28-B?"

Gabriel looked down at the page.

"I cannot confirm that."

Edward let the silence remain.

Then he placed the handwritten instruction on the desk.

**Receiving party to be informed that the child is not the Aurelius infant.**

"Who wrote this?"

Gabriel read it.

"I don't recognise the handwriting."

"Do you recognise the instruction?"

"No."

"Did you tell anyone that Child 28-B was not the Aurelius infant?"

"I never made a biological identification."

"That isn't the same thing."

Gabriel's eyes returned to Edward.

"I was not authorised to determine family identity. My responsibility was medical coordination."

"Yet you approved the invoice for the closure of the child's temporary identity file."

"Administrative approval is not medical identification."

"Then who was responsible for telling the receiving party?"

Gabriel did not answer immediately.

"Marianne handled residential coordination."

"And Charles?"

"Charles controlled Northbridge."

"Three people. Three different responsibilities."

"Yes."

"Convenient."

Gabriel's expression hardened.

"Mr. Valmont, I understand that you are investigating something deeply distressing. But I will not accept accusations simply because several people had different administrative roles."

Edward gathered the papers.

"I haven't accused you of anything."

He stood.

"I am asking who knew where the child went."

Margaret Voss confirmed her signature that afternoon.

She arrived carrying a small document case.

Edward had sent her a copy of the day-103 departure authorisation before the meeting. She had clearly spent considerable time examining it.

"This is my signature," she said.

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"Did you physically release the child?"

Margaret looked at the document again.

"No."

Jonathan glanced at Edward.

Margaret continued.

"I signed the administrative departure authorisation. I did not collect the child, and I did not accompany her to the receiving facility."

"Why did you sign it?"

"Because I believed the medical and residential arrangements had been completed."

"Who told you that?"

"Charles."

"Did you speak to Marianne?"

"She brought me the paperwork. She said the receiving facility had accepted the transfer."

"Did you see an acceptance confirmation?"

"Not at the time."

Edward opened the folder.

"Did you know the destination?"

Margaret hesitated.

"I was told it was a residential facility operating outside the Northbridge network."

"Where?"

"I don't remember the name."

"Was it in the same district?"

"I don't know."

"Was the child present when you signed?"

"No."

The answer came quietly.

Margaret looked down at her hands.

"I had already made that mistake once. I had signed documents for a child I wasn't physically collecting. I knew I should have insisted on seeing the receiving confirmation."

"Yet you signed again."

"Yes."

She did not defend herself.

Edward waited.

Margaret opened her case and removed a folded sheet.

"I kept this."

The paper was a copy of an internal Northbridge instruction.

It was dated the evening before Child 28-B's departure.

At the top was the reference **NB-17/103**.

Below it were three typed lines.

**Administrative responsibility: Margaret Voss.**

**Transport coordination: Cross Administrative Services.**

**Receiving verification: Pending.**

A handwritten addition appeared at the bottom.

**Do not delay transfer for originating identity confirmation.**

Edward read it twice.

"Who wrote this?"

"I don't know. It was attached to the departure papers."

"Did you understand what it meant?"

"I understood that they wanted the child moved before the identity review was completed."

"Why?"

Margaret shook her head.

"Charles said the child could not remain at Northbridge indefinitely. He said the residential arrangement was temporary and that the receiving facility had been selected."

"Did you ask why the originating identity hadn't been confirmed?"

"I did."

"What did he say?"

Margaret looked at Edward.

"He said the child was not the Aurelius infant."

The room became very still.

Jonathan leaned forward.

"Those exact words?"

"Not exactly. He said there was no reason to delay the transfer because the child was not the one the Aurelius family was looking for."

Edward felt his attention sharpen.

"Did he explain how he knew?"

"No."

"Did he show you a DNA report?"

"No."

"Did he show you a hospital identification record?"

"No."

"Did he explain which child he believed the Aureliuses were looking for?"

"No."

Margaret lowered her eyes.

"I accepted his explanation."

"Why?"

"Because I thought he had information I didn't have. I thought he had received confirmation from the originating office."

"Did he?"

"I don't know."

Edward looked at the handwritten instruction.

"Did you ever hear the phrase A-17?"

Margaret considered it.

"Yes."

"Where?"

"On a transport invoice. I believe it was attached to Gabriel's earlier transfer."

"The sixth-day transfer?"

"Possibly. I remember seeing the reference, but I didn't know what it meant."

"Did Charles tell you it identified a particular child?"

"No. He said it was a route designation."

Edward exchanged a glance with Jonathan.

The same explanation Gabriel had given.

Margaret looked up.

"Are you suggesting Child 28-B was the infant taken from St Catherine's?"

"I'm suggesting that someone used the same administrative reference for both records."

"That doesn't mean they were the same child."

"No," Edward said. "It doesn't."

Margaret seemed relieved that he had not made a premature accusation.

Then he asked another question.

"Who received Child 28-B on day 103?"

"I don't know."

"Did you ever see the receiving party's signature?"

"No."

"Did you receive confirmation that the child arrived safely?"

Margaret hesitated.

"Three days later, I received a short message saying the transfer had been completed."

"From whom?"

"Marianne."

"Was there a name?"

"No. Only a reference."

"Which reference?"

Margaret searched her case and produced another sheet.

The message was brief.

**NB-17/103 closed. External receiving confirmation received. Original identity file remains sealed under A-17.**

Edward read it slowly.

"Was there an attachment?"

"Not in the copy I received."

"Did you ask for one?"

"I did."

"And?"

"Marianne said the receiving party had requested confidentiality."

Edward placed the message beside the departure authorisation.

The phrase *original identity file remains sealed under A-17* was new.

A-17 was no longer merely a transport reference.

It was also associated with an identity file.

But that still did not tell him whether the file belonged to Infant A, Child 28-B, or a third administrative record.

The following morning, Jonathan found the invoice.

It was buried in a collection of Northbridge accounts that had been overlooked during the earlier financial review.

The invoice had been issued by Cross Administrative Services on the one hundred and fourth day after the births.

Its description was unusually detailed.

**External residential transfer — NB-17/103.**

**Transport coordination and receiving verification.**

**Temporary identity file retention — A-17.**

**Medical liaison clearance.**

The payment had been made from the same consolidated administrative account used for earlier Aurelius-related expenses.

But there was something else.

At the bottom of the invoice, beneath the approval stamp, appeared a small notation.

**Receiving party: M.C.**

Jonathan read it twice.

"Marianne Cross?"

"Possibly."

"She was the administrative coordinator. Why would she also be the receiving party?"

"She may have been the person who signed on behalf of the facility."

"Or the person who arranged the transfer."

Edward examined the invoice.

"Find the payment instruction."

Jonathan searched the attached records.

A few minutes later, he found it.

The payment had been approved by Gabriel Vale.

Not Charles.

Edward stared at the signature.

"That's interesting."

"Why?"

"Because Gabriel told us he had no role in identifying the child. He said his responsibility was medical coordination."

Jonathan looked at the approval.

"Perhaps this was simply payment for medical clearance."

"Perhaps."

Edward pointed to the final line.

"Then why did he approve the retention of the identity file?"

Jonathan read the wording again.

**Temporary identity file retention — A-17.**

"Maybe he knew the file was connected to the transport."

"That is exactly what we need to establish."

Edward closed the folder.

"Get the original payment record. Not the copy."

Marianne Cross was harder to locate than Gabriel.

Her former business address had been closed, and her last known residence was registered under a different name.

Jonathan eventually found an address through an old company filing.

It belonged to a modest apartment building in the western part of the city.

They arrived shortly before six in the evening.

The woman who opened the door was in her late fifties. Her hair was tied back, and she wore a plain cardigan over a dark blouse.

She looked at Edward.

"Yes?"

"Marianne Cross?"

Her expression changed.

"Who is asking?"

"Edward Valmont."

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she opened the door wider.

"Come in."

The apartment was orderly, almost excessively so. There were no family photographs on the walls. A small collection of books occupied one shelf, while several neatly labelled folders sat on a desk near the window.

Marianne offered them tea.

They declined.

She sat opposite them.

"I wondered when someone would come."

Edward studied her.

"You knew we were investigating?"

"I knew someone had begun asking questions about the old Northbridge records."

"Did you expect us?"

"Not specifically."

Edward placed the invoice on the table.

"Did you coordinate the transfer of Child 28-B on day 103?"

"Yes."

"Did you personally receive the child?"

Marianne looked at the document.

"I signed the external receiving acknowledgement."

"Where?"

"I cannot give you a reliable answer without seeing the original record."

"Was it a residential facility?"

"Yes."

"Which one?"

She hesitated.

"Northbridge used several external facilities. Some were temporary, some were medical, and some were private residential arrangements."

"Which one received this child?"

Marianne looked at him.

"You already know that the destination was deliberately omitted from the Northbridge register."

"We know the register was incomplete. We don't know why."

"Because the facility did not want its name included in the central records."

"Who was responsible for that decision?"

"Charles."

"Did you agree?"

"I followed the instruction."

"That isn't an answer."

Marianne's fingers tightened around one another.

"I was an administrative coordinator. I was not the person making decisions about the children's identities."

"Yet you signed the receiving acknowledgement."

"Because the receiving facility required a single representative to sign."

"Who was physically present?"

Marianne did not answer.

Edward changed the subject.

"What does A-17 mean?"

She looked at the invoice again.

"It was a protected identity route."

"Explain."

"It was used when a child's original identification documents could not accompany the child through the ordinary residential process."

"Why couldn't they?"

"Because the records were disputed."

"Which records?"

Marianne looked at him for a long moment.

"The originating records."

Edward leaned forward.

"Was A-17 assigned to Infant A?"

"It was used in the transfer arrangement."

"Was it assigned to Child 28-B?"

"It was used in the residential arrangement."

"Are those the same thing?"

"No."

"Then what connected them?"

Marianne looked toward the window.

"An identity file."

Edward waited.

She finally continued.

"A-17 was not a child's name. It was not a birth number. It was a protected file reference. The same file could contain several documents relating to a child's movement, but that did not mean every document belonged to the same child."

Jonathan exchanged a glance with Edward.

That distinction changed the shape of the investigation.

"Could two different children have appeared under the same reference?" Edward asked.

"Yes."

"Could one child have appeared under different references?"

"Yes."

"Then A-17 proves nothing about biological identity."

"No."

"Why was it used for Infant A's transfer?"

"Because the transfer was being handled outside the ordinary hospital discharge process."

"And Child 28-B?"

"Because the residential identity file had not been completed."

"Who decided to place both arrangements under A-17?"

Marianne lowered her eyes.

"Charles."

"On whose authority?"

"I don't know."

Edward let the silence settle.

Then he placed the handwritten note before her.

**Receiving party to be informed that the child is not the Aurelius infant.**

Marianne's face changed.

It was not surprise.

It was recognition.

"You have seen this before," Edward said.

"No."

"But you recognise the wording."

She read it again.

"Yes."

"Who wrote it?"

"I don't know."

"Who asked you to communicate it?"

Marianne looked at him.

"Charles."

"When?"

"Before the transfer."

"What exactly did he say?"

Marianne took a breath.

"He told me the receiving party had to understand that the child was not the Aurelius infant. He said there must be no confusion when the child arrived."

Edward felt his attention sharpen.

"Did he explain why?"

"He said the Aurelius family was searching for a particular child, and that this child was not to be associated with that search."

"Did he provide proof?"

"No."

"Did he identify the child the Aureliuses were supposedly searching for?"

"No."

"Did you ask?"

Marianne's expression became guarded.

"I did not ask questions about biological identity."

"Why not?"

"Because I had been told repeatedly that the identity records were incomplete. I believed the medical and legal representatives were handling the matter."

"Did you tell the receiving party?"

"Yes."

"What did they say?"

Marianne looked down.

"They asked whether the child had been tested."

Edward was silent.

"Tested how?"

"DNA."

"Did you have a report?"

"No."

"What did you tell them?"

"That I had been instructed to communicate the classification."

"Classification?"

"That was the word Charles used."

Edward glanced at Jonathan.

"Did the receiving party accept it?"

Marianne shook her head.

"They asked for the originating records. I told them those records were sealed."

"Who told you to say that?"

"Charles."

"Did you ever see the child?"

Marianne's eyes moved toward the window again.

"Yes."

"When?"

"At the receiving facility."

"Was she healthy?"

"She was small, but she appeared well. A nurse was present."

"Was it definitely Child 28-B?"

Marianne paused.

"The transport documents said 28-B."

"That isn't what I asked."

"I cannot prove that the child I saw was the same child described in the Northbridge register."

Edward leaned back.

The answer was frustrating, but it was also the first direct acknowledgement that the paperwork might not have followed the child accurately.

"Did you see a bracelet?"

Marianne thought for several seconds.

"Yes."

"What was written on it?"

"I don't remember."

"Did it say 28-B?"

"I don't think so."

"Was there another number?"

Marianne closed her eyes briefly.

"I remember two letters and three numbers."

Edward waited.

"Could they have been IV-204?"

"No. I don't remember the letters clearly enough."

"Could they have been STC-A?"

"I don't know."

"Then what do you remember?"

Marianne opened her eyes.

"I remember that the nurse refused to remove the bracelet until the receiving medical officer had checked the file."

Edward wrote that down.

"Was the medical officer Gabriel Vale?"

"No."

"Do you know who it was?"

"Only his surname."

"Which was?"

"Dr. Hale."

Jonathan immediately made a note.

Edward continued.

"Did Dr. Hale sign the receiving record?"

"I believe so."

"Where is that record now?"

Marianne looked at the folders on her desk.

"I kept a copy."

For the first time since they had entered the apartment, she stood.

She went to the desk, opened a drawer, and removed a thin folder.

Marianne returned and placed them on the table.

The first was a transport acknowledgement.

The second was a medical clearance note.

The third was a receiving record.

Edward read the first page.

**Transfer reference: NB-17/103.**

**Protected file: A-17.**

**Receiving facility: Westbridge Children's Residence.**

The name was familiar.

Jonathan looked up.

"Westbridge?"

Marianne nodded.

"That was the facility."

Edward continued reading.

The receiving representative field contained a name.

**Dr. Samuel Hale.**

But the signature beneath it was not a doctor's signature.

It was a short administrative mark, followed by a handwritten instruction.

**Do not enter originating family details. Await independent verification.**

Edward turned to the medical clearance note.

It recorded that the female child had arrived without acute medical concerns. The nurse had checked the temporary identification band, but the original identity materials were not included in the accompanying file.

At the bottom was a separate note.

**Band reference: 17-04.**

Edward stopped.

Jonathan noticed immediately.

"What is it?"

Edward placed the page beside the old schedule.

The reference 17-04 had appeared before.

Not on the child's bracelet.

On the Northbridge financial instruction.

He looked at Marianne.

"Who assigned band reference 17-04?"

"I don't know."

"Was it used at Westbridge?"

"Yes."

"Was it a permanent identification number?"

"No. It was temporary."

"Did you ever see the originating identity file?"

Marianne shook her head.

"Only its cover."

"What was written on it?"

She looked uncomfortable.

"I don't remember everything."

"Try."

Marianne closed her eyes.

Then she spoke.

"Two lines."

Edward waited.

"One said that the child's original maternal record was unresolved."

She paused.

"The other said that the child must not be released to any family representative until the identity review was complete."

Edward looked at Jonathan.

That instruction was important.

It meant the receiving facility had not been given permission to deliver the child to a family merely because someone claimed her.

But it also meant someone had deliberately preserved a barrier between the child and any possible relatives.

"Who was supposed to complete the identity review?" Edward asked.

Marianne opened her eyes.

"Charles said the originating office would send confirmation."

"Did it?"

"No."

"Did Westbridge ask for it?"

"Several times."

"To whom?"

"Northbridge. Then Cross Administrative Services. Finally, they sent a request directly to Vale Private Counsel."

"Who answered?"

"Gabriel."

"What did he say?"

Marianne looked down at the papers.

"He said the child was not the Aurelius infant."

Edward did not move.

The words had appeared in a handwritten Northbridge instruction.

Charles had repeated them to Margaret.

Marianne had communicated them to Westbridge.

And Gabriel had repeated them when the receiving facility asked for identity confirmation.

But none of them had produced a biological report.

"Did he explain how he knew?" Edward asked.

"No."

"Did he identify the Aurelius infant?"

"No."

"Did he say which child the family was searching for?"

"No."

Edward slowly closed the folder.

For eighteen years, the investigation had revolved around the possibility that a child had been moved under the wrong name.

Now he was beginning to see another possibility.

Perhaps the people handling the records had not merely confused the identities.

Perhaps they had deliberately created separate administrative identities so that nobody at any one facility could reconstruct the full movement of a child.

And if that was true, the phrase *not the Aurelius infant* might never have been a medical conclusion.

It might have been a method of preventing questions.

That evening, Edward called Jonathan after returning to the Valmont estate.

"Westbridge is the next priority," he said.

"I've already found the company registration."

"Who owns it?"

"That's the problem. The facility changed operators twice. The original company was dissolved, and the records were transferred to a successor organisation."

"Can we access them?"

"Not without establishing our legal basis."

"Then establish it."

Jonathan hesitated.

"There's another issue."

"What?"

"Westbridge's original patient index contains two temporary entries from the same week."

Edward looked toward the darkened window.

"Both female?"

"Yes."

"Both connected to A-17?"

"One is."

"And the other?"

"That's what makes it interesting."

Jonathan paused.

"The second entry has no A-17 reference at all. It has a handwritten note saying that the child's original identification band was replaced on arrival."

Edward's fingers tightened around the phone.

"Do we have a name?"

"No."

"An originating facility?"

"Yes."

Jonathan read it from his notes.

"Meridian Children's Medical Centre."

Edward stood very still.

Meridian.

The same hospital that had received the infant associated with Isabella Vale's bracelet, IV-204.

The same hospital that had received Child 28-B on day 92.

Two female children.

One connected to A-17.

The other arriving with a replaced identification band.

And both appearing in Westbridge's records during the same period.

"What's the date?" Edward asked.

"Day 103."

Edward looked down at the copy of the Northbridge transfer acknowledgement.

Child 28-B had arrived at Westbridge on day 103.

If the second child had also arrived that day, the two entries could be connected.

Or they could be entirely separate.

"Send me the full index," Edward said.

"I'm trying to get it."

"Don't assume the two entries refer to the same child."

"I won't."

Edward ended the call.

For a long moment, he remained beside the window.

Somewhere in the old records, two children had been reduced to temporary numbers.

One had been told she was not the Aurelius infant.

The other had arrived with her identification band replaced.

And eighteen years later, nobody could confidently say which record belonged to which child.

Edward returned to his desk and opened his notebook.

He wrote two lines.

**WESTBRIDGE — A-17 — Child 28-B.**

**WESTBRIDGE — Meridian transfer — band replaced.**

Then he added a third.

**Find out whether the two entries ever shared a room.**

Because if they had, someone at Westbridge might remember something the documents had failed to preserve.

Not a name.

Not a number.

Perhaps only the sight of two newborn girls in the same nursery.

But for the first time, Edward had a reason to believe the mystery did not end with the child who had been moved.

It might begin with the child who had been left behind.
