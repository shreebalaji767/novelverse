# Chapter 7 — The Family Behind the Initials

The Valmont family had a rule about Sundays.

No business meetings before lunch.

Richard had introduced the rule years ago after Sebastian attempted to discuss a company acquisition during a family birthday celebration.

Nicholas had supported it enthusiastically.

Mostly because he disliked meetings.

Unfortunately, the rule had never prevented Nicholas from turning breakfast into a negotiation.

"I want to go out today."

Sebastian continued reading his tablet.

"Then go."

"I want everyone to come."

"Then invite everyone."

"I did."

"Then why are you still talking?"

Nicholas ignored him.

He turned toward Elena.

"You're coming, aren't you?"

Elena looked up from her toast.

"Where?"

"Somewhere interesting."

"That isn't a location."

"Shopping district. Lunch. Bookstore. Maybe the cinema."

"That's three separate plans."

"Four, if we include dessert."

Elena smiled.

"You're very enthusiastic."

"I've been deprived of family outings."

Edward glanced at him.

"You went out with us last week."

"That was a business dinner."

"You spent most of it complaining about the food."

"Exactly. It was work."

Catherine laughed.

"Perhaps we can all go after lunch."

Isabelle looked up from her cup.

"Would it be all right if I came too?"

Nicholas immediately nodded.

"Of course."

Elena smiled.

"That would be nice."

Isabelle returned the smile.

Richard looked around the table.

"Then let's make it an afternoon outing. We haven't done that in some time."

Nicholas raised his glass.

"Excellent. A successful proposal."

Sebastian looked at him.

"You haven't proposed anything."

"I proposed an outing."

"You announced one."

"Details."

Elena laughed.

For the first time in several days, the family seemed to be planning something without discussing hospital records, legal documents, or the complicated circumstances of two newborn girls.

She was looking forward to it.

At 9:20 a.m., Edward received a call from Daniel.

He answered while standing in the study.

"Go ahead."

"Sir, we've located the legal firm's successor."

"Which firm?"

"Vale International Legal Group. It absorbed Aurelius & Vale Private Counsel fifteen years ago."

Edward looked at the notes on his desk.

"Have they confirmed that the old files still exist?"

"Some of them. The company says its historical records are being digitised."

"Can we access them?"

"Not without legal authorisation."

Edward frowned.

"Can we establish who represented the Aurelius family eighteen years ago?"

"Possibly. I've found a former partner's name."

"Who?"

"Jonathan Pierce."

Edward wrote it down.

"Find out where he is now."

"He's retired. He lives outside the capital."

"Arrange a meeting."

Daniel hesitated.

"There's something else."

Edward waited.

"The old legal office handled several confidential matters involving the Aurelius family during that period. One of the files is marked with the same emblem as the photograph."

Edward straightened.

"What is the file number?"

Daniel gave him the reference.

Edward wrote it down.

"Don't request the file yet. First, find out who authorised it."

"Understood."

The call ended.

Edward looked at the photograph again.

A man at a hospital.

An instruction to keep records separate.

A legal office connected to the Aurelius family.

He had no proof that the family itself had been involved.

But the connection was becoming difficult to ignore.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander was having breakfast with his family.

It was one of the few occasions when all his children were expected to be present.

Adrian sat to his right, reading a report.

Lucien was talking to Damien about a new technology investment.

Damien was listening with only half his attention.

The youngest brother, Julian, was trying to convince their mother that he needed a new gaming computer.

Victoria was pouring tea.

"Julian, you received a new computer last year."

"That was last year."

"Which is not very long ago."

"Technology changes."

Lucien grinned.

"Your brother has discovered the art of financial persuasion."

Adrian didn't look up.

"He's discovered the art of spending other people's money."

Julian frowned.

"I thought this was a family."

"It is," Damien replied. "That doesn't make your argument stronger."

Victoria laughed.

Alexander placed his newspaper aside.

"Julian, if your grades remain satisfactory, we can discuss it."

Julian brightened.

"Really?"

"After your examinations."

His expression fell.

"That's months away."

"Then you have months to improve your argument."

Lucien raised his coffee cup.

"An excellent lesson in corporate negotiation."

Julian glared at him.

The conversation moved on.

But Victoria's gaze eventually returned to the photograph resting beside her.

Adrian noticed.

"Mother?"

She looked up.

"Yes?"

"Have you received the latest report?"

"Not yet."

Alexander glanced at Adrian.

"Did the investigation team give you a date?"

"They promised an update this week."

Victoria lowered her cup.

"I wish they would stop promising updates and bring us something useful."

Nobody answered.

She looked down at the photograph.

"Every year, I tell myself that this will be the year."

Alexander reached across the table and covered her hand with his.

"We haven't stopped looking."

"I know."

Victoria smiled faintly.

"But sometimes I wonder whether she is somewhere wondering why nobody came for her."

The room became quiet.

Adrian looked down.

Lucien stopped playing with his spoon.

Damien's expression hardened.

Julian looked at the photograph.

"If she is, we'll find her."

Victoria looked at him.

"Yes," she whispered.

"We will."

By noon, the Valmont family was preparing to leave.

Nicholas had somehow managed to turn a simple outing into a logistical operation.

He had arranged two cars, a restaurant reservation, and a list of places he wanted to visit.

Elena looked at the list.

"Why are there twelve places?"

"Because we have an entire afternoon."

"We cannot visit twelve places in one afternoon."

"Not with that attitude."

Isabelle stood beside them, looking at the list.

"There's a bookstore here."

Elena pointed.

"That's a good one. I've been there before."

"Is it large?"

"Quite large. They have a second floor with academic books and a little café."

Isabelle smiled.

"I'd like to see it."

Nicholas crossed out two restaurants.

"Then we'll go there after lunch."

Sebastian appeared at the entrance.

"Are we leaving?"

Nicholas nodded.

"Yes."

"Then stop rearranging the list."

"You're not helping."

"I'm not supposed to."

Edward came downstairs carrying his car keys.

Richard and Catherine followed.

For a moment, the family stood together in the entrance hall.

Elena looked around.

She had stood in this same place thousands of times.

But today there was something new.

Isabelle stood beside her.

Not as a visitor.

Not quite as a familiar sister.

Somewhere between the two.

Elena smiled at her.

"Ready?"

Isabelle nodded.

"Ready."

The afternoon went better than Nicholas expected.

Which was unfortunate for his reputation.

Lunch was held at a quiet restaurant overlooking the river.

Nicholas ordered too much food.

Sebastian complained about the portion sizes.

Edward corrected a minor detail in the restaurant's description of a dish.

Richard ignored all three.

Catherine and Isabelle discussed the university's international programme.

Elena listened while looking through the dessert menu.

Nicholas leaned toward her.

"You're planning dessert already?"

"Yes."

"We haven't finished lunch."

"Planning ahead is important."

He nodded approvingly.

"Excellent. You're learning."

Isabelle glanced at the menu.

"What do you recommend?"

Elena pointed toward one of the desserts.

"The lemon tart. It's very good."

Nicholas shook his head.

"Don't listen to her. The chocolate cake is better."

Elena looked at him.

"You always order chocolate cake."

"Because I have excellent taste."

Sebastian put down his fork.

"You have predictable taste."

"That is not a criticism."

Isabelle smiled.

"I think I'll try the lemon tart."

Nicholas looked betrayed.

"Another one."

Elena laughed.

After lunch, they visited the bookstore.

Elena led Isabelle through the entrance and toward the second floor.

The store was quiet, with tall shelves and large windows.

Isabelle stopped in front of the fiction section.

"You come here often?"

"When I have time."

"Do you buy books every time?"

"Not every time."

Nicholas, who had followed them, picked up a thick volume.

"She says that, but she has an entire bookshelf at home that looks like a small library."

Elena took the book from him.

"Put that back."

"Why?"

"Because you picked it up without looking at the title."

Nicholas glanced at the cover.

"Advanced Corporate Taxation."

Elena stared at him.

"Exactly."

He returned it to the shelf.

"Not my genre."

Isabelle laughed.

They spent nearly an hour browsing.

Isabelle bought two books about economics and one novel.

Elena bought a book about financial history.

Nicholas bought nothing.

He claimed the bookstore had failed to meet his standards.

At 4:15 p.m., Edward's phone rang.

He stepped away from the group to answer.

"Daniel?"

"Sir, we've located Jonathan Pierce."

"Good."

"He has agreed to meet tomorrow."

"Did he recognise the reference number?"

"Yes."

Edward's expression changed.

"What did he say?"

"He said he remembers the file."

Edward looked toward the bookstore window.

"What else?"

"He asked whether the Aurelius family had been informed."

Edward went still.

"Did you tell him anything?"

"No, sir."

"Good."

Daniel hesitated.

"There's one more detail."

"Go ahead."

"The file was created under a confidential family matter designation. It was not a standard hospital representation."

Edward lowered his voice.

"Who requested it?"

"We haven't confirmed that yet."

"Find out."

"Understood."

Edward ended the call.

He looked at his family.

Elena was showing Isabelle a book.

Nicholas was pretending to be offended because nobody had asked for his recommendation.

Sebastian was checking his phone.

Richard and Catherine were speaking near the entrance.

For a moment, Edward considered telling Richard everything.

Then he decided to wait.

He needed facts.

Not suspicions.

The following morning, Jonathan Pierce arrived at a private meeting room arranged by Vale International Legal Group.

He was an elderly man with a neatly trimmed grey beard and a careful manner of speaking.

Edward and Daniel were already waiting.

"Mr. Pierce," Edward greeted him.

"Mr. Valmont."

They shook hands.

Pierce sat down.

"I understand you're investigating a matter involving St. Catherine's Hospital."

"Yes."

"Something from eighteen years ago."

"Correct."

Pierce placed his briefcase on the table.

"I was wondering when someone would ask about that file."

Edward watched him carefully.

"You remember it?"

"I remember the designation."

"Who created it?"

Pierce opened his briefcase.

"Not me personally. I was a junior partner at the time. The matter was assigned to the senior partner, Mr. Charles Vale."

"Is he still alive?"

"No. He passed away several years ago."

"Do you remember the client?"

Pierce hesitated.

"I remember the name Aurelius."

Edward's expression remained neutral.

"Which member of the family?"

"I cannot say without reviewing the authorisation."

"Can you review it?"

Pierce nodded.

"I have already requested access to the archived index."

"How long will that take?"

"Possibly a few days."

Edward leaned forward.

"Mr. Pierce, a child was separated from her family. We need to establish why."

Pierce looked at him for a long moment.

Then he spoke.

"I understand."

He opened another folder.

"There is something I can tell you now."

Edward waited.

"The file was not opened because of a hospital complaint."

"What was it opened for?"

Pierce looked down at his notes.

"It was opened as a family identity protection matter."

Edward frowned.

"Identity protection?"

"Yes."

"Whose identity?"

Pierce shook his head.

"That is what I need to verify."

Edward sat back.

He had expected a hospital dispute.

Perhaps a financial settlement.

Perhaps an attempt to hide a medical mistake.

Instead, he was hearing phrases that suggested someone had been trying to protect the identity of a newborn child.

And the matter had been handled by a legal office connected to the Aurelius family.

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander received a message from Adrian.

**We have located an old legal file connected to the hospital investigation. The former firm has been contacted.**

Alexander read it twice.

He immediately called his son.

"How did you find it?"

"The investigation team located a former employee of Aurelius & Vale."

"Does he remember the matter?"

"Yes."

Alexander stood.

"Have we established what it concerns?"

"Not yet."

"Then get the records."

Adrian hesitated.

"Father, there's something else."

"What?"

"The file was classified as an identity protection matter."

Alexander's expression changed.

For a few seconds, he said nothing.

Then he asked, "Who authorised it?"

"We're trying to establish that."

Alexander looked toward the framed photograph on his desk.

His daughter's photograph.

A small baby wrapped in a pale blanket.

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"If this file concerns our daughter, I want every page."

"Understood."

"And don't let anyone interfere with the investigation."

"I won't."

The call ended.

Alexander remained standing.

Identity protection.

The phrase brought back a memory he had spent years trying to understand.

The hospital had told him that his daughter could not be located.

He had asked whether she had been taken.

They had said there was no evidence.

He had asked whether someone had accessed the records.

They had said the archive showed no irregularities.

But now there was a legal file.

A confidential file.

Opened under the Aurelius name.

He walked to the window.

For the first time in eighteen years, he wondered whether someone had known exactly where his daughter was.

And had chosen not to tell him.

That evening, Elena was sitting at her desk when Catherine entered.

"Are you busy?"

"Just finishing some reading."

"May I sit?"

"Of course."

Catherine sat on the edge of the sofa.

"I wanted to ask how you felt about yesterday."

Elena looked up.

"The outing?"

"Yes."

"It was nice."

"Did you enjoy spending time with Isabelle?"

Elena smiled.

"Yes. She's easy to talk to."

Catherine looked relieved.

"I'm glad."

Elena returned to her book.

After a moment, Catherine spoke again.

"Elena, I want you to know something."

She put down the book.

"What?"

"Whatever the hospital investigation discovers, you are not going to be treated as though you suddenly stopped mattering."

Elena was quiet.

Catherine continued.

"You have been part of this family for eighteen years. No document can erase the memories we share."

Elena looked at her mother.

"Thank you."

Catherine reached for her hand.

"I know things have been difficult."

"They have."

"You can tell me when they are."

Elena smiled faintly.

"I will."

Catherine squeezed her fingers.

Then she stood.

"Come downstairs when you're ready. Nicholas is insisting that he needs help choosing a new laptop."

Elena stared.

"Why does he need my help?"

"Apparently, you are the only person he trusts to understand the specifications."

Elena sighed.

"That's unfortunate."

Catherine laughed.

"Very."

Later that night, Edward received the first page of the archived legal index.

He opened the document.

The file number matched the reference Daniel had found.

The date was correct.

The classification was correct.

Then he reached the authorisation section.

His eyes stopped.

**Client: Aurelius Family Office**

**Authorised representative: Alexander Aurelius**

Edward read the name again.

Alexander Aurelius.

The chairman of Aurelius Global.

The father of the missing newborn girl.

Edward sat back.

For eighteen years, Alexander Aurelius had been searching for his daughter.

And eighteen years ago, his own family office had authorised a confidential legal file connected to the hospital.

Edward stared at the document.

Why would Alexander Aurelius authorise an identity protection matter involving his own newborn daughter?

Had he known about the missing records?

Had he been told something the Valmont family had never been told?

Or had someone used his authority without his knowledge?

There was only one way to find out.

Edward picked up his phone and called Richard.

"Father, we have a name."

Richard answered immediately.

"Whose?"

"Alexander Aurelius."

Silence.

Then Richard asked, very quietly:

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

Edward looked at the document again.

"The file was authorised by the Aurelius family office."

Richard did not speak for several seconds.

Then he said, "Bring everything you have to my study tomorrow morning."

"I will."

The call ended.

Edward closed the document.

The investigation had finally reached the family whose name had appeared in the hospital records.

But the discovery had created a far more troubling question.

**If Alexander Aurelius had authorised the file, why had he spent eighteen years searching for a daughter whose disappearance might have been connected to his own office?**

*End of Chapter 7.*
