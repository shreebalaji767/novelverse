# **Chapter 209 — The Register That Preserved the Originals**

The Medical Records Preservation Office responded at 7:46 the following morning.

Edward had expected a formal acknowledgement.

Instead, the message contained a warning.

**HIST-27/06 is a preservation register, not an identity register.**

**Access to original materials does not establish permission to compare, disclose or associate the identities recorded within them.**

Edward read it twice.

“They're making the distinction before we even see the file,” Sophie said.

“They have reason to.”

He opened the attached access conditions.

The preservation office required three separate approvals:

- Authority to inspect the original records.
- Authority to compare records belonging to different originating institutions.
- Authority to disclose any resulting identity conclusion.

The approvals were independent.

An investigator could receive permission to inspect a document without being authorised to compare its contents against another person's record.

Edward recognised the structure.

It was the same distinction that had appeared throughout the investigation.

Access was not authority.

Custody was not ownership.

And an administrative connection was not proof of identity.

He submitted the first request.

At 9:15, Dr Eleanor Vale joined the review remotely.

“The preservation office is correct,” she said. “The originals were deposited under separate preservation instructions.”

“Who deposited them?”

“That information is available in the register, but the depositors' identities are restricted.”

“Can we establish whether the same person deposited all the records?”

“No. The register records separate deposits. It does not establish a common depositor.”

Edward opened HIST-27/06.

The visible index contained five entries.

**HIST-27/06-A:** Original maternity record preservation.

**HIST-27/06-B:** Neonatal identity material preservation.

**HIST-27/06-C:** Receiving institution documentation.

**HIST-27/06-D:** Personal identity comparison materials.

**HIST-27/06-E:** Chain-of-custody and transfer records.

Edward looked at the entries.

“Which one relates to F-02?”

“D appears in the Northbridge comparison reference,” Eleanor replied. “But that does not mean the other entries belong to the same person.”

“Can we inspect A and B?”

“Only after the preservation office confirms the scope of the authority.”

Edward nodded.

He did not want another administrative summary that appeared to connect records simply because they had been filed together.

At 10:40, the preservation office approved access to the register's deposit history.

The first entry was dated day 27.

The second was dated day 28.

The third was dated day 31.

The fourth was dated day 34.

The fifth was dated day 47.

Edward examined the descriptions.

The maternity record materials had been deposited first.

The neonatal identity materials had been deposited separately the following day.

The receiving institution documentation had arrived three days later.

The personal identity comparison materials had arrived after the original family communication was initiated.

The chain-of-custody records had been added much later.

Sophie pointed to the fourth entry.

“Day 34. That's after Northbridge suspended the delivery assignment.”

“Yes.”

“So someone preserved additional identity material after the first verification attempt failed.”

“Possibly. We need the deposit instruction before we can say why.”

Edward opened the fourth entry.

**HIST-27/06-D — Personal Identity Comparison Materials**

**Deposit purpose:** Preserve original material pending independent verification.

**Restriction:** No conclusion concerning identity, family relationship or receiving eligibility may be entered without approval from the originating authority.

There was a handwritten note beneath it.

**Comparison must begin with the source record, not the proposed recipient reference.**

Edward stopped.

That instruction was unusually precise.

It suggested that someone had already recognised the danger of beginning with a presumed identity and searching backward for documents that appeared to support it.

At 11:30, Elena's pilot demonstration began.

Six organisations had submitted test applications.

Two reviewers had completed their assessments.

One application had been returned because its supporting financial statement did not match the declared reporting period.

The platform had preserved both the original submission and the replacement document.

The coordinator examined the audit trail.

“Can the reviewer change the original file?”

“No.”

“Can the applicant delete it?”

“No. They can submit a corrected version, but the original remains in the record.”

“And if the reviewer makes a mistake?”

“The reviewer can submit an amended assessment. The earlier assessment remains visible, with the reason for the change.”

The coordinator nodded.

“What happens if the person approving the grant has not been assigned the proper authority?”

“The decision remains pending.”

Elena opened the permissions page.

The system showed the programme officer's role, the date it had been granted and the date it would expire.

“Your system won't let us proceed?”

“It won't let the application appear approved. Someone with the correct authority must confirm the decision.”

The coordinator smiled.

“That is exactly what we wanted to test.”

Elena did not smile immediately.

She was pleased, but she also understood what the pilot meant.

If the system worked, it would be useful.

If it failed, the failure would be hers to explain.

She closed the demonstration.

“Then let's test the failure cases properly.”

At 13:05, Edward received the deposit instruction for HIST-27/06-D.

The document was signed by a preservation officer.

The depositor was identified through a restricted institutional reference.

The instruction contained one sentence that immediately drew his attention.

**The comparison materials are not to be arranged according to the provisional receiving identity, as the receiving identity has not been independently established.**

Edward read it aloud.

Sophie looked at the earlier Northbridge report.

“They were aware of the problem before the comparison.”

“Someone was.”

“Could that have been Northbridge?”

“No. The deposit predates its final review.”

He checked the signature.

The officer had not been authorised to decide the identity question.

The officer's role was limited to preserving the materials and ensuring that the original sequence remained intact.

Edward opened a request for the identity of the restricted depositor.

The system rejected it.

**Identity disclosure requires originating authority approval.**

He leaned back.

“Another sealed identity.”

“Not necessarily the person we're looking for,” Sophie said.

“Agreed.”

He marked the restriction.

The investigation had encountered too many records in which a sealed identity had been treated as if it were the missing answer.

This time, he would not repeat the mistake.

At 14:20, Catherine called Edward.

“Is there anything new?”

“There is a preservation register containing original maternity and identity materials.”

“Does it mention Elena?”

“No.”

“Does it mention the Aurelius family?”

“Not in the accessible extract.”

Catherine was silent for a moment.

“Then what does it establish?”

“That original records were preserved separately and that an independent comparison was expected.”

“Can they be examined?”

“Some can. Others require additional authority.”

“Are you closer?”

Edward considered the question.

“We are closer to understanding what evidence was preserved. We are not yet closer to proving who Elena is.”

Catherine accepted the distinction.

“Don't let anyone turn the existence of a file into an answer.”

“I won't.”

After the call, Edward returned to the register.

He wondered whether Catherine understood how difficult that instruction was becoming.

Every record seemed to point toward another record.

Every record had been preserved for a reason.

But the reasons were rarely complete.

At 15:45, the preservation office released the chain-of-custody summary for HIST-27/06-A and HIST-27/06-B.

The two deposits had separate receipt numbers.

The maternity record materials had been sealed in an institutional envelope.

The neonatal identity materials had been deposited in a separate container.

Neither container had been opened after receipt.

Both seals were intact at the last inspection.

The records had never been physically combined.

Edward read the final statement.

**No authorised cross-comparison has been conducted between deposit A and deposit B.**

Sophie looked at him.

“So the original records have never been compared.”

“Not under this preservation register.”

“Could they have been compared elsewhere?”

“We don't know.”

Edward opened the older investigation reports.

Several contained references to comparisons.

But none identified the original materials as their source.

They relied on copies, extracts, administrative summaries and secondary schedules.

The distinction mattered.

A copied record could accurately reproduce a document while still omitting the information needed to establish its origin.

And a comparison between two copies could not repair an incomplete chain of custody.

At 16:30, Eleanor sent a further clarification.

The preservation office could authorise a neutral examination of the original materials if the originating institutions approved.

The examiner would be permitted to:

- Confirm the documents' physical condition.
- Verify whether the seals and original markings were intact.
- Establish the chronology of each record.
- Identify missing pages, attachments or alterations.
- Report whether the records were suitable for a later independent comparison.

The examiner would not be permitted to decide whether two infants were the same child.

That would require a separate authorisation.

Edward read the conditions.

It was a sensible first step.

He requested an independent records examiner.

At 17:10, the Aurelius family received an update.

Alexander listened without interrupting.

Victoria sat beside him, reading the preservation register summary.

Adrian asked the first question.

“Do the records establish that the missing family communication concerned our family?”

“No,” Edward replied over the call. “The originating family identity remains sealed.”

“And Elena?”

“Nothing establishes a connection to her.”

Lucien leaned forward.

“Then what have we actually learned?”

“That original materials were preserved before several later administrative arrangements were created. Their chain of custody appears intact, but they have not been compared under an authorised independent process.”

Damien considered that.

“So we may finally be able to establish what the original records say, without relying on later summaries.”

“Yes.”

Julian was quiet.

He had been holding his notebook.

“Does this mean we're closer to finding her?”

Alexander looked at his youngest son.

“It means we're closer to knowing what happened.”

Julian lowered his eyes.

“That isn't the same thing.”

“No,” Victoria said gently. “It isn't.”

He opened his notebook to the last page.

Item fifty remained unfinished.

**Ask her what she wants to do, instead of deciding everything myself.**

He closed it again.

At 18:25, Edward received the examiner's acceptance.

The examination would take place at the Medical Records Preservation Office.

Two originating institutions had approved inspection.

The third had not yet responded.

The appointment was provisional.

Edward reviewed the list of materials.

HIST-27/06-A.

HIST-27/06-B.

The separate neonatal identity container.

He checked the approval conditions one last time.

Then he noticed something missing.

The original deposit inventory referred to an additional attachment.

**Supplemental source description: SD-27/06.**

It had not been included in the accessible register.

He opened the preservation office's catalogue.

The entry existed.

But its status was different.

**SD-27/06 — Source description supplied by originating institution.**

**Access:** Restricted.

**Purpose:** Establish provenance of original identity materials.

Edward requested the document.

A response appeared almost immediately.

**Access cannot be granted through the Medical Records Preservation Office.**

**The source description remains under the authority of the original depositing institution.**

Edward stared at the screen.

The original records were preserved.

Their seals were intact.

Their chronology could be examined.

But the document explaining where the materials had come from remained with the institution that had deposited them.

He opened the institutional reference.

**Originating institution: St. Catherine's Maternity and Records Hospital.**

Edward slowly closed the catalogue.

St. Catherine's had been the hospital associated with Victoria Aurelius's maternity record.

That did not prove the preserved materials belonged to Victoria.

It did not prove they concerned the missing child.

But it established the next place where the investigation had to begin.

And this time, they would need the original source description—not another summary of what someone believed it contained.
