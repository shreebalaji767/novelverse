# **Chapter 21 — The Man Who Was Not Supposed to Deliver Her**

Edward read the recovered sentence again.

*He must not be allowed to deliver the child.*

Beneath it was the partial name.

**MARTIN HA—**

There was no question about the identity.

Martin Hale.

The former administrative director of the Aurelius Family Office.

The man who had received the original maternity identity report.

The man who had collected the package intended for Victoria Aurelius from the Valmont residence.

The man who had kept the folder containing both children's names for eighteen years.

And now, according to a handwritten warning, the man who was not supposed to deliver a child.

Edward forwarded the image to Adrian.

Then he called him.

"Have you seen the latest forensic result?"

"Yes."

"Do you know what it means?"

"I know what it says."

"That's not the same thing."

Adrian was silent for a moment.

"Have the examiners recovered the complete sentence?"

"Not yet. The paper impression becomes too faint after the word 'child'. There may be more, but they need additional processing."

"Could the sentence refer to something other than a physical delivery?"

"Possibly. We need the surrounding text."

Adrian exhaled.

"Then we need to speak to Hale again."

Edward looked at the image.

"I agree."

"Don't approach him alone this time."

"I wasn't planning to."

"Good."

Adrian ended the call.

Edward remained at his desk, staring at the document.

There was something deeply uncomfortable about the fact that nearly every important piece of evidence seemed to lead back to the same few people.

Charles Vale.

Eleanor Vale.

Martin Hale.

The unidentified liaison officer.

And an unknown person who had apparently been able to use Alexander Aurelius's authority.

The mystery was no longer simply about two children being exchanged at a hospital.

It was about an arrangement that had been made, suspended, concealed and perhaps deliberately prevented from being completed.

And someone had spent eighteen years ensuring that the full story remained buried.

Martin Hale was expecting the Aurelius investigators.

He had received a message from Adrian's office the previous evening, informing him that they wished to discuss the newly recovered correspondence.

He had not replied.

Instead, he had spent most of the night searching through his remaining papers.

There were boxes in his study, a locked cabinet in the spare room and a metal storage case beneath his bed.

Most contained ordinary administrative records.

Some were incomplete.

Others had been kept because he had never been certain whether destroying them would be safer than preserving them.

He opened the metal case.

Inside were several envelopes.

One was marked with the old Aurelius Family Office seal.

Another carried the name of Charles Vale.

The third had no label.

Hale picked it up.

He recognised the handwriting.

Eleanor Vale.

He had not opened it in eighteen years.

He placed it back.

Then he sat on the edge of the bed and looked at his hands.

He had spent years telling himself that he had done what he was instructed to do.

He had not made the hospital arrangements.

He had not altered the birth registrations.

He had not chosen where either child would live.

He had only handled documents.

That had been his explanation.

It had also been his excuse.

His phone rang.

The caller ID showed an unknown number.

Hale stared at it.

Then he answered.

"Hello?"

A man's voice spoke.

"Have they found the second instruction?"

Hale stood.

"Who is this?"

"You know who I am."

"No, I don't."

"Then you have forgotten more than I expected."

Hale tightened his grip on the phone.

"What do you want?"

"To remind you that the original arrangement was never supposed to become public."

Hale glanced towards the metal case.

"The children were involved. The families have a right to know."

There was a short silence.

"That is not what you said eighteen years ago."

Hale went pale.

"Who is this?"

The caller ignored the question.

"Do not give them the remaining documents."

The call ended.

Hale stood motionless.

Then he looked at the metal case again.

For the first time in eighteen years, he considered taking everything to the authorities himself.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena had discovered that the family library was an excellent place to avoid Nicholas.

Unfortunately, Nicholas had discovered it too.

He entered carrying a plate.

"Peace offering."

Elena looked up from her book.

"What did you break?"

"Why must you assume I have done something wrong?"

"Because you brought food."

"Perhaps I'm becoming generous."

"That would be a major personality development."

Nicholas placed the plate beside her.

It contained small pastries from the kitchen.

Elena selected one.

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

He sat opposite her.

"What are you reading?"

"A book about economic development."

Nicholas looked at the cover.

"That sounds exciting."

"It is."

"Really?"

"Yes."

"You're saying that because you don't want to admit you're bored."

Elena turned a page.

"Some of us enjoy reading."

"Some of us enjoy living."

She threw him a look.

He smiled.

For a few minutes, he watched her read.

Then he spoke.

"Have you decided about the exchange programme?"

Elena looked up.

"How did you know?"

"Clara mentioned it when she visited."

"She shouldn't have told you."

"She didn't. I overheard you discussing it with Edward."

Elena sighed.

"Yes. I'm thinking about applying."

"Where?"

"Singapore is one option. There are a few universities in Europe too."

Nicholas nodded.

"That sounds good."

"That's all?"

"What else should I say?"

"I thought you might tell me not to go."

"Why would I?"

Elena shrugged.

"Because everyone has been behaving as though I might disappear."

Nicholas's expression softened.

"You're allowed to go somewhere for a semester without disappearing."

She smiled slightly.

"Good."

"Besides, I can visit."

"That would be expensive."

Nicholas looked offended.

"Are you questioning my financial capacity?"

"I'm questioning your ability to plan a trip without turning it into a shopping expedition."

"Unfair."

"Accurate."

He leaned back.

"Seriously, though. Apply."

Elena studied him.

"You're sure?"

"Of course."

He paused.

"You're not going to spend your entire life sitting in this house because the adults haven't figured out what happened eighteen years ago."

Elena looked down at her book.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she smiled.

"Thank you."

Nicholas reached for a pastry.

"Don't mention it."

She immediately pushed the plate away.

"That's mine."

He grinned.

"Now we're back to normal."

Catherine was in the foundation office with Isabelle.

The building was quieter than the Valmont Estate, and the atmosphere was very different from the family business offices.

The foundation supported educational programmes, healthcare initiatives and several community development projects.

Catherine had wanted Isabelle to see the work rather than simply hear about it.

They had spent the morning reviewing scholarship applications.

Isabelle was surprised by the number of students supported by the foundation.

"I didn't realise there were so many."

"Every year, we receive thousands of applications," Catherine explained. "We cannot support everyone, but we try to make the selection process fair."

Isabelle examined a file.

"Do you personally review these?"

"Not all of them. The scholarship committee handles the initial assessments. I review the final recommendations."

Isabelle nodded.

"Can I ask something?"

"Of course."

"Would I be allowed to work here?"

Catherine looked at her.

"Of course. If you're interested."

"Not because I'm your daughter."

Catherine understood immediately.

"You would go through the same process as anyone else."

Isabelle looked relieved.

"I'd prefer that."

"Then that's what we'll arrange."

They continued reviewing the documents.

After a while, Isabelle asked, "Does Elena help with the foundation too?"

"Occasionally. She has helped organise educational materials and review some of our student resources."

"She seems to enjoy that."

"She does."

Catherine smiled faintly.

"She has always liked projects where she can see a practical result."

Isabelle looked down at the file.

"I'd like to understand the work better."

"Then you can start with the scholarship programme."

Catherine handed her another folder.

"Take your time. There's no expectation that you know everything immediately."

Isabelle nodded.

For the first time since arriving at the Valmont Estate, she felt that she was being invited into something that was not simply a family title or inheritance.

It was work.

Something she could learn.

Something she could contribute to.

At the Aurelius Global headquarters, Adrian and Edward met Martin Hale in a private conference room.

Hale arrived carrying a small case.

He looked tired.

Adrian noticed immediately.

"Mr Hale."

"Mr Aurelius."

Edward gestured towards a chair.

"Thank you for coming."

Hale placed the case beside him.

"I thought you might want to see something."

Adrian looked at it.

"What is it?"

"Documents."

"Related to the hospital?"

"Yes."

Edward leaned forward.

"Why didn't you bring these when we first met?"

Hale looked at him.

"Because I wasn't sure I should."

"What's changed?"

Hale glanced towards the closed door.

"Someone called me last night."

Adrian's expression sharpened.

"Who?"

"I don't know."

"What did they say?"

"They asked whether the second instruction had been found."

Edward and Adrian exchanged a glance.

"What else?" Edward asked.

"They told me not to give you the remaining documents."

"Did they threaten you?"

Hale looked down.

"Not directly."

Adrian's voice became colder.

"Then why are you here?"

"Because I have spent eighteen years telling myself that I was only following instructions."

He opened the case.

Inside were three envelopes and a thin folder.

"I no longer believe that explanation is sufficient."

Edward examined the folder.

"What's in here?"

"Copies of correspondence between the Aurelius Family Office and Charles Vale's legal office. There are also several internal authorisation requests."

Adrian looked at him.

"Are these original?"

"Some are copies. The originals were transferred to a separate archive."

"Where?"

Hale hesitated.

"I don't know whether it still exists."

Edward opened the first envelope.

It contained a typed letter dated eighteen years earlier.

The sender was the Aurelius Family Office.

The recipient was Charles Vale.

The subject line read:

**CONFIDENTIAL — MATERNITY IDENTITY MATTER**

Edward read the first paragraph.

Then he stopped.

"Adrian."

Adrian took the letter.

His eyes moved across the page.

The letter did not authorise a transfer.

It did not authorise a change of records.

It authorised an investigation into the disappearance of an infant.

The request had been submitted after the hospital reported that the child recorded under the Aurelius name could not be located.

Alexander Aurelius's signature appeared at the bottom.

Adrian looked at Hale.

"Did my father sign this?"

"Yes."

"Then why did you tell us he may not have known about the file?"

"Because this was the first document he signed. The other authorisation was submitted later."

"By whom?"

Hale pointed to the second envelope.

Adrian opened it.

Inside was a form authorising the external liaison office to act on behalf of the family office in matters concerning the maternity identity investigation.

The signature field was completed.

But the name of the authorised representative was blank.

At the bottom was a handwritten reference.

**EFO-SL/07.**

Edward looked at Hale.

"Who was this?"

"I never knew the person's full name."

"You're telling us an unidentified person had authority to act on behalf of the Aurelius Family Office?"

"That was what the paperwork claimed."

"Who approved it?"

Hale looked uncomfortable.

"The authorisation appeared in the system."

"That isn't an answer."

"I know."

Adrian placed the document down.

"Was the signature verified?"

Hale shook his head.

"I wasn't responsible for verification."

"Who was?"

"Charles Vale's office."

Edward opened the third envelope.

It contained a short letter.

This one was handwritten.

The date was six days after the births.

The first sentence was clear.

*The original report has been received. Do not deliver the child until the second instruction is confirmed.*

Edward looked at Hale.

"Who wrote this?"

Hale shook his head.

"I don't know."

"Did you receive it?"

"Yes."

"Then why did you collect the report from the hospital?"

Hale looked at him.

"Because I was told to secure it."

"By whom?"

"By the external liaison office."

"Did you deliver a child?"

"No."

"Did you arrange a transfer?"

"No."

"Then what does this instruction mean?"

Hale lowered his eyes.

"I don't know."

Edward leaned back.

For a moment, the only sound in the room was the ventilation system.

Then Adrian asked a question that neither of them had considered.

"Mr Hale, did you ever see the children?"

Hale looked up.

"Which children?"

"Both."

The older man hesitated.

Then he nodded.

"Once."

Edward immediately sat forward.

"Where?"

"At the hospital."

"When?"

"About a week after they were born."

"Why were you there?"

Hale looked at the documents.

"To confirm that the children were still in their assigned locations."

Adrian's expression changed.

"Who asked you to do that?"

Hale gave him the same answer.

"The liaison office."

"Did you see both children together?"

"No."

"Did you see their mothers?"

"No."

"Who was caring for them?"

Hale was silent.

Then he said something that changed the direction of the investigation.

"I didn't see either family."

Edward frowned.

"Then where were the children?"

Hale looked at him.

"They were in a private hospital nursery."

At the Aurelius Estate, Alexander received the update shortly before noon.

He listened without interrupting.

When Adrian finished, Alexander asked, "He saw both children?"

"Yes."

"At the hospital?"

"Yes."

"One week after the births?"

"According to his statement."

Alexander walked to his desk.

"Was there a private nursery?"

"There was a temporary observation unit used for infants requiring additional monitoring."

"Why were the children there?"

"Hale claims he was told they were awaiting identity verification."

Alexander stopped.

"That is not a normal procedure."

"No."

"Who authorised it?"

"We don't know yet."

Alexander looked towards the window.

His daughter had been born at the hospital.

She had been recorded as awaiting transfer.

Then her identity had apparently become the subject of an investigation.

And now, a week later, she had still been inside the same institution.

"Did Hale recognise either child?"

"He says he wasn't given identifying details."

"Did he see their identification bands?"

"One was covered. He couldn't read the other."

Alexander's jaw tightened.

"Find the nursery records."

"We've requested them."

"And the staff?"

"Also being identified."

Alexander turned.

"Adrian, I want the names of everyone who had access to that unit during those seven days."

"Understood."

That evening, Elena found Catherine in the drawing room.

Her mother was reading a document.

Elena approached quietly.

"Are you busy?"

Catherine looked up.

"No, sweetheart. Come here."

Elena sat beside her.

For a while, they spoke about ordinary things.

The foundation.

Isabelle's visit.

Elena's university application.

Then Catherine asked, "Have you thought any more about the exchange programme?"

"Yes. I'm going to apply."

Catherine smiled.

"That's wonderful."

"I'll need to prepare some documents."

"Tell me if you need help."

"I will."

Catherine put the papers aside.

"Elena."

"Yes?"

"Whatever happens with the investigation, I don't want you to feel that your future is uncertain."

Elena looked at her.

"I know."

"Your education will continue. Your plans will continue. You don't have to wait for the adults to settle everything."

Elena smiled faintly.

"That's what Nicholas said."

Catherine laughed softly.

"Then perhaps your brother has finally said something sensible."

"Don't tell him. He'll become unbearable."

Catherine reached for her hand.

Elena allowed her to hold it.

For a moment, the room was quiet.

Then Elena asked, "Were you happy when I was born?"

Catherine's expression changed.

The question was simple.

But it carried eighteen years of history.

"Yes," she answered.

"Even before you knew what I would be like?"

Catherine smiled through the sudden emotion in her eyes.

"Especially then."

Elena looked down at their joined hands.

"Do you remember the day?"

"Of course."

"What do you remember?"

Catherine hesitated.

"I remember being exhausted. I remember wanting to hold you. I remember the nurse telling me you were healthy."

She paused.

"And I remember thinking that I would spend the rest of my life worrying about you."

Elena smiled.

"You've done a very good job."

Catherine laughed quietly.

But after Elena left, she remained seated for a long time.

Because the question had reminded her of something she had not thought about in years.

The day Elena was brought home.

The hospital had told her that the baby was ready.

She remembered the blanket.

The little cap.

The nurse who had carried the infant into the room.

But she did not remember signing the final transfer document.

And suddenly, that absence in her memory seemed important.

Late that night, Edward received a message from the hospital archive team.

The original private nursery register had been located.

It had been stored separately from the maternity records.

The register contained seven days of entries.

Two infant identification numbers appeared repeatedly.

One was associated with the Aurelius maternity registration.

The other with the Valmont maternity registration.

Both had been admitted to the private nursery.

Both had been discharged.

Edward opened the scanned discharge pages.

The first infant had been discharged on the seventh day.

The second on the eighth.

He examined the names entered in the guardian section.

The first page listed a hospital-appointed representative.

The second listed a private family representative.

Neither name belonged to Richard Valmont, Catherine Valmont, Alexander Aurelius or Victoria Aurelius.

Edward enlarged the second page.

The signature was clear.

**MARTIN HALE.**

But beneath it was another entry.

A handwritten note, added several hours later.

*Infant released to receiving guardian. Identity confirmation pending.*

Edward read it again.

Then he opened the first discharge page.

The same note appeared.

*Infant released to receiving guardian. Identity confirmation pending.*

Both children had been discharged.

Neither discharge had been supported by completed identity verification.

And the receiving guardians were not named.

Edward picked up his phone and called Adrian.

"Adrian, we have a new problem."

"What is it?"

"Hale wasn't just holding documents. He signed the nursery discharge register."

Silence.

Then Adrian asked, "For which child?"

Edward looked at the two scanned pages.

"For both."

He paused.

"And neither record identifies who actually took the children home."

The call remained connected.

Edward stared at the discharge dates.

Two children.

Two separate discharges.

One man signing both records.

And two unknown receiving guardians.

Then another message appeared on his screen.

The archive team had recovered an additional page from the back of the register.

It was a handwritten correction.

The original entry had been crossed out.

The replacement entry contained only one sentence:

**THE CHILDREN WERE NOT RELEASED TO THE GUARDIANS LISTED IN THE ORIGINAL INSTRUCTIONS.**

Beneath it was a date.

And a signature.

**CHARLES VALE.**
