# Chapter 15 — The Person Who Received the Report

The first thing Adrian Aurelius did after leaving his father's office was cancel two meetings.

The second was instruct his secretary not to transfer any calls to him unless they were directly related to the historical archive investigation.

The third was call the head of Aurelius Technologies' records division.

He was beginning to suspect that the investigation had been treated as an ordinary archival recovery for too long.

It was not ordinary.

Someone had used the family's name, accessed confidential records, and apparently arranged for Alexander Aurelius to be kept away from information concerning his own newborn daughter.

That was no longer merely a hospital matter.

It was a question of who had been permitted to act in the family's name.

"Mr. Aurelius," the records director answered.

"I've sent you a document recovered from the Meridian archive."

"Yes, sir. I've reviewed it."

"Who received the original report?"

"We're checking the transfer logs."

"Don't check only the digital logs. I want the physical dispatch registers, courier receipts, storage inventories, and any correspondence relating to the file."

"Understood."

"And I want the names of everyone who had access to the archive between the date of the original report and its classification."

"That will be a substantial list."

"Then send me a substantial list."

Adrian ended the call.

He looked at the document again.

*Do not allow Alexander to receive the original report.*

There were only two possibilities that made sense.

Either someone had deliberately kept the information from Alexander, or the instruction had been written to protect him from something.

Neither possibility was comforting.

At the Valmont Estate, Elena was attempting to establish a new study schedule.

It was not going well.

She had divided her afternoon into four sections.

- University assignments.
- Examination preparation.
- Reviewing possible degree programmes.
- Personal reading.

The schedule had lasted precisely eleven minutes before Nicholas appeared at her door.

"Do you want to go shopping?"

"No."

"Would you like to reconsider?"

"No."

"Would you like to hear the details before refusing?"

Elena looked up from her notebook.

"Are you asking me to go shopping, or are you asking me to accompany you while you shop?"

Nicholas paused.

"That's an important distinction."

"Which one?"

"Both."

She returned to her notebook.

"I'm studying."

"Isabelle is coming."

Elena stopped writing.

"Is she?"

"Yes. She mentioned wanting a few things for university. Mother suggested we take her."

"Then why are you asking me?"

Nicholas looked offended.

"Because I am a thoughtful brother."

"You're a bored brother."

"Those things are not mutually exclusive."

Elena considered her schedule.

She had been studying for nearly two hours. A short break would not hurt.

"Fine. But I'm not spending the entire afternoon in clothing stores."

"Agreed."

"And I have to be back by six."

"Agreed."

"And you're paying for lunch."

Nicholas smiled.

"Now you're negotiating."

"Learning from the best."

He pointed at her notebook.

"Bring that."

"Why?"

"Because if you start talking about university programmes during lunch, I can pretend to read something."

Elena threw a cushion at him.

He caught it.

Then, very deliberately, placed it back on the chair.

"I'll be downstairs."

Isabelle was waiting near the entrance when Elena arrived.

She wore a simple cream blouse and dark trousers, carrying a small handbag.

"Are you ready?" she asked.

"Almost."

Nicholas appeared behind them.

"Excellent. Everyone is ready. We can leave."

Elena looked at him.

"You haven't even picked up your keys."

Nicholas checked his pockets.

"An insignificant detail."

"Your phone too."

He checked again.

"Why do you notice everything?"

"Because otherwise we'd never leave."

Isabelle laughed.

They travelled to a shopping district in the city centre.

Nicholas had arranged for a car, but Elena insisted that they walk between the shops instead of being driven from one entrance to another.

"You know we have a car," he reminded her.

"I know."

"And the car can take us to the next building."

"I also know that."

"Then why are we walking?"

"Because the buildings are next to each other."

Nicholas looked at Isabelle.

"Do you see what I deal with?"

Isabelle smiled.

"I think she's right."

Nicholas sighed.

"Two against one. This is unfair."

They visited a bookstore first.

Elena spent more time than either of the others expected in the academic section. Isabelle joined her, comparing several university guides and looking through a book about financial markets.

Nicholas wandered toward the fiction shelves.

When he returned, he was carrying three books.

Elena looked at them.

"Are you buying those?"

"Yes."

"Do you even know what they're about?"

"Not yet."

"Then why?"

"The covers looked interesting."

Elena shook her head.

"That's a terrible purchasing strategy."

"It has worked for me before."

They eventually settled at a café.

Isabelle placed her university guide on the table.

"I've been thinking about something."

Elena looked up.

"What?"

"If the DNA results confirm what everyone suspects, will things change between us?"

Nicholas stopped examining the dessert menu.

Elena took a moment to answer.

"I don't know."

Isabelle looked down.

"That's honest."

"I don't want to promise something I can't predict."

"Do you think you'll resent me?"

Elena frowned.

"Why would I?"

"Because I came into the family and everything became complicated."

"Everything was already complicated before you arrived."

Isabelle looked at her.

Elena continued.

"You're not responsible for what happened at the hospital. Neither am I. We didn't choose where we were born."

Isabelle was quiet.

"I know that."

"Knowing something and feeling it are different."

Isabelle gave a small, surprised smile.

"You're beginning to sound like a counsellor."

"Don't tell Clara. She'll start charging me for advice."

Nicholas finally looked up.

"Can we discuss something important?"

Both girls turned toward him.

"Which dessert are we ordering?"

Elena stared at him.

"That was not important."

"It is to me."

Isabelle laughed.

And the conversation moved on.

At Aurelius Global Headquarters, the archive investigation had produced its first substantial list.

Adrian read through it in the presence of Damien.

There were seventeen individuals connected to the Meridian records during the relevant period.

Most were routine employees.

Four had administrative authority.

Three had been involved in the transfer of the hospital archive.

One name appeared more than once.

**Martin Hale.**

Damien leaned forward.

"Who is he?"

"Former family office administrative director."

"Was he employed here when the girls were born?"

"Yes. He handled confidential correspondence and external liaison requests."

"Does he still work for us?"

"No. He resigned twelve years ago."

"Why?"

Adrian scrolled through the personnel record.

"Officially, personal reasons."

"Any disciplinary issues?"

"None recorded."

Damien looked at the list.

"Then why is his name appearing in the archive?"

"Because he signed for the original report."

Damien's expression changed.

"Personally?"

"According to the dispatch register."

Adrian turned the screen toward him.

A scanned entry showed a courier delivery made eighteen years earlier.

The recipient was Martin Hale.

The delivery description read:

**Original maternity identity report — confidential.**

The delivery had been made six days after the birth.

Damien read the entry twice.

"Was the report ever returned?"

"There's no record of it."

"Was it stored in the family office?"

"Not according to the current inventory."

"Then where is it?"

Adrian closed the document.

"That's what we're going to find out."

Damien leaned back.

"Do we contact him?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"Because if he was involved in concealing the report, an unexpected call might give him time to destroy whatever remains."

Damien nodded.

"Do we know where he is?"

"His last registered address is in the capital. I'm having it verified."

Damien stood.

"I'll handle the security side."

Adrian looked at him.

"Quietly."

"I know."

"And no intimidation."

Damien gave him a flat look.

"Do you think I threaten retired administrators for entertainment?"

Adrian returned to his screen.

"I think you enjoy making people uncomfortable."

"Only when necessary."

At the Valmont Estate, dinner was unusually peaceful.

Nicholas had returned with several shopping bags.

Most belonged to Isabelle.

One contained books Elena had purchased.

Another contained a box of pastries.

Richard glanced at the bags.

"Did you buy the entire shopping district?"

"Only a small section," Nicholas replied.

Sebastian looked at Isabelle.

"Did you find what you needed?"

"Yes. Thank you."

Catherine smiled.

"I'm glad."

Elena placed her book beside her chair.

Richard noticed it.

"What did you buy?"

"A book about financial systems. I'm comparing a few courses."

"Have you decided what you want to study?"

"Not completely."

Richard nodded.

"Take your time."

Sebastian looked at her.

"Economics would be useful. So would business administration. You should compare the programmes rather than choosing based on the university's reputation."

Elena nodded.

"I've been doing that."

Nicholas leaned toward her.

"She has a spreadsheet."

Everyone looked at him.

Elena frowned.

"How do you know?"

"Because you showed it to me."

"I showed you the course list."

"Same thing."

"Not remotely."

Isabelle smiled.

"Could I see it sometime?"

"Of course."

Richard returned to his meal.

For a moment, Elena felt a familiar warmth.

This was still the family with whom she had grown up.

The voices were familiar. The table was familiar. Even the arguments were familiar.

But there was something else now.

She was aware of every small change.

The way Catherine looked at Isabelle when she spoke.

The way Richard sometimes paused before addressing Elena.

The way Sebastian had become more careful with his words.

None of it was necessarily cruel.

That almost made it harder to understand.

Because she could not point to one thing and say, *There. That is when everything changed.*

It had happened gradually.

And perhaps that was why she sometimes felt as though she were watching her own life from a short distance away.

Nicholas nudged her beneath the table.

She looked at him.

He pointed toward the dessert.

There were two chocolate cakes.

He silently indicated that one was hers.

Elena smiled.

Some things had not changed.

Later that evening, Edward received another call from Jonathan Pierce.

"I found something in the physical archive," Jonathan said.

"What?"

"The original dispatch register contains a second entry."

Edward sat down.

"Related to the maternity report?"

"Yes. The first entry records delivery to Martin Hale. The second records a separate transfer three days later."

"To whom?"

"That's the problem."

Jonathan sent a scanned copy.

Edward opened it.

The recipient's name had been partly obscured by a fold in the paper.

Only the first name remained visible.

**Victoria.**

Edward frowned.

"Victoria Aurelius?"

"Possibly."

"Does the entry include an address?"

"There's a reference number. We're trying to decode it."

Edward looked at the date.

Three days after Martin Hale received the original report, another copy or related document had apparently been dispatched.

If the recipient was Victoria Aurelius, then someone had sent information to the child's mother.

But why?

And what had she received?

"Jonathan, can you verify the courier record?"

"I'm working on it."

"Was the package delivered?"

"The register says it was."

Edward leaned forward.

"Then we may have a second record of what happened."

"Possibly."

Edward looked at the name again.

Victoria.

He imagined the woman who had spent eighteen years waiting for her daughter.

Had she received a document that told her the child was alive?

Had she received something else entirely?

Or had the package never reached her?

"Send me everything as soon as you have it."

"I will."

At the Aurelius Estate, Victoria was sitting with Alexander when Adrian entered.

He carried a folder.

"We've identified the person who received the original report."

Alexander looked up.

"Who?"

"Martin Hale. He was the administrative director of the family office at the time."

Victoria straightened.

"Did he work with you?"

Alexander nodded slowly.

"Yes. He handled confidential correspondence. I remember him."

"Do you know why he left?"

"Not in detail. He resigned years later."

Adrian placed the folder on the table.

"We have also found evidence of a second dispatch three days after the original report was delivered to him."

Alexander opened the folder.

The scanned register lay on top.

Victoria looked at the partly visible name.

Her face changed.

"That's my name."

Adrian nodded.

"The entry may refer to you. We're still verifying the recipient details."

Victoria stared at the paper.

"Someone sent me something?"

"According to the register, yes."

"Did I receive it?"

"We don't know yet."

Alexander looked at the date.

"Three days after Hale received the original report."

Adrian nodded.

"Correct."

Victoria stood.

"Then find the package."

"We're tracing the courier records."

"Don't stop at the courier records. Check the household mail register, the private office, and every document that came into the estate during that period."

"Already instructed."

Victoria looked at Alexander.

For eighteen years, she had remembered the days after her daughter's birth as a blur of hospital rooms, medical explanations and unbearable silence.

She remembered being told there had been complications with the transfer records.

She remembered being told that the hospital was investigating.

She remembered waiting for someone to bring her daughter.

But she had never remembered receiving a package.

"Alexander," she whispered.

He took her hand.

"We'll find out."

"What if it was sent to me?"

"Then we'll find out what was inside."

Victoria looked down at the register.

"What if someone made sure I never saw it?"

Alexander had no answer.

At 11:36 p.m., a former records clerk named Martin Hale received a telephone call.

He had retired from administrative work years ago and lived quietly in a residential neighbourhood outside the capital.

He rarely received calls after dinner.

The number was unfamiliar.

He answered.

"Hello?"

There was silence.

"Mr. Hale?"

"Yes."

"My name is Jonathan Pierce. I am contacting you regarding a confidential archive from the former Aurelius & Vale legal office."

Hale did not respond.

"Mr. Hale?"

"Who gave you my number?"

"We are reviewing a historical file from eighteen years ago. It concerns a maternity hospital and a confidential report delivered to your office."

A long silence followed.

Then Hale spoke.

"I don't remember anything about that."

"We have a signed dispatch receipt."

"I handled thousands of documents."

"This one was marked as an original maternity identity report."

Hale's breathing changed slightly.

"I think you've got the wrong person."

"Mr. Hale—"

The call ended.

Jonathan stared at his phone.

He immediately contacted Edward.

Edward was asleep when his phone rang.

He answered after the second call.

"What happened?"

"Martin Hale."

Edward sat up.

"What about him?"

"I contacted him. He denied remembering the report."

"And?"

"He hung up."

Edward looked at the clock.

"Did you threaten him?"

"No. I simply asked about the document."

"Good. Don't contact him again tonight."

"I wasn't planning to."

Edward ended the call and opened his notebook.

He wrote:

**Martin Hale — original report recipient.**

Then he added:

**Reaction: immediate denial.**

He looked at the second dispatch entry.

The partly obscured name.

Victoria.

If that package had reached the Aurelius Estate, then the family might already have been given an explanation eighteen years ago.

An explanation that had somehow failed to reach them.

Or perhaps it had reached the wrong person.

Edward picked up his phone and called Adrian.

The following morning, the Aurelius family received an unexpected message from their archive department.

The courier company responsible for the second dispatch had been identified.

Its surviving delivery register contained a recipient signature.

The package had not been delivered to Victoria Aurelius.

It had been signed for by someone else.

The name was clear.

**Catherine Valmont.**

Adrian read it twice.

Then he called Alexander.

For several seconds, neither man spoke.

The second package, sent three days after the original report, had been addressed to the mother of the Aurelius child.

But the person who had signed for it was the mother of the Valmont child.

And eighteen years later, both women were raising the two girls whose identities had been switched.

The question was no longer simply where the original report had gone.

It was what Catherine Valmont had received—and whether she had ever known whose daughter she was holding.
