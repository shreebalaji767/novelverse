# **Chapter 67 — The Signature at the Bottom**

Edward looked at the signature for the fourth time.

The name was unmistakable.

**Alexander Aurelius.**

It appeared beneath the three entries on the Northbridge schedule, written in dark ink against a narrow line at the bottom of the page.

For a moment, the room seemed unusually quiet.

Jonathan leaned over the table.

"That's his signature."

"It resembles his signature," Edward corrected.

Jonathan straightened.

"You think it's forged?"

"I think we have spent several weeks discovering that documents can look genuine while meaning something entirely different from what they claim."

Edward placed the schedule beneath the desk lamp.

The page was old, slightly yellowed at the edges. Three columns contained the child references, temporary representatives, and administrative instructions. The bottom section was separated from the main table by a thin horizontal line.

Alexander's name appeared below it.

There was no visible heading.

No date beside the signature.

No explanation of what he had supposedly approved.

Edward tapped the empty space immediately above the name.

"Where is the label?"

Jonathan examined it.

"Which label?"

"Whatever this signature was supposed to acknowledge."

Jonathan studied the page again.

"Authorised by. Approved by. Received by."

"Exactly."

"And none of those appears here."

"Which means we don't know what he signed."

Edward sat back.

The signature was potentially the most important discovery they had made.

It was also potentially meaningless.

He disliked both possibilities.

At Northbridge, the archive supervisor had initially refused to release anything further.

The company's records had been transferred twice, its original administrators were either retired or deceased, and its legal successor had little interest in explaining transactions conducted eighteen years earlier.

However, the discovery of the Aurelius name changed the conversation.

By eleven in the morning, Edward and Jonathan were sitting in a private records room with a representative of Northbridge's legal successor.

Her name was Helen Ward.

She had brought a sealed envelope containing documents recovered from the company's old accounting department.

"I want to make one thing clear," Helen said. "Northbridge was not an adoption agency. It provided temporary accommodation, administrative services, and private residential arrangements. Any medical or guardianship decisions were supposed to remain with the relevant institutions."

"Supposed to?" Jonathan asked.

Helen gave him a tired look.

"I have spent three months reviewing the old company records. I would not use that phrase if I believed every department followed the rules."

Edward placed a copy of the schedule on the table.

"Can you identify this document?"

Helen examined it.

Her expression changed slightly.

"Yes."

"How?"

"It is a receiving and expenditure reconciliation schedule."

Edward waited.

Helen turned the paper toward herself.

"Northbridge maintained accounts for several temporary residential arrangements. The company received funds from different private offices and distributed them to facilities, medical providers, and approved representatives."

"Was this particular schedule connected to children?"

"Apparently."

"Why apparently?"

"Because the original schedule was not classified as a medical record. It was an administrative account document. The child references were used to distinguish expenses."

Jonathan frowned.

"So this wasn't originally a list of children being transferred?"

"No. Not according to its classification."

Edward looked at the three entries.

"Then what were these representatives receiving?"

Helen examined the document more closely.

"That depends on the accompanying instructions."

"Which we don't have."

"Not all of them."

She opened the sealed envelope.

Inside were six pages.

The first two contained routine expenditure summaries.

The third was a payment reconciliation.

The fourth was a carbon copy of an administrative instruction.

The fifth was a receipt.

The sixth was an incomplete photocopy of the schedule Edward had brought.

Helen separated the pages.

"These were found together in the same archive box."

Edward examined the carbon copy.

It contained the same three references.

Child 28-B.

Child IV-204.

Child 28-C.

But the bottom section was different.

There was no signature.

Instead, a handwritten notation appeared in the margin.

**Pending originating-office confirmation.**

Edward read it twice.

"Is this the earlier version?"

"Most likely."

"Can you establish the date?"

Helen pointed to the upper corner.

"Twenty-eighth day after the recorded births."

Edward's eyes narrowed.

The same day Margaret Voss had received the comparison report.

"Was the signed version prepared on the same date?"

Helen searched through the papers.

"No date is visible on that copy."

"Can you tell whether it was produced later?"

"The paper stock is different. The photocopy is also of a different generation."

Jonathan leaned forward.

"Meaning?"

"The signed page appears to have been copied from another document and attached to the reconciliation schedule at some later point. I cannot tell you when without a proper examination."

Edward looked again at the bottom.

"Then the signature may not belong to the schedule."

Helen nodded.

"That is possible."

The forensic document examiner arrived shortly after noon.

Dr. Samuel Reed had worked with the Aurelius legal office before, but Edward had specifically requested an independent examination.

He spent nearly an hour photographing the pages, comparing the printing patterns, and examining the signature under different forms of magnification.

Alexander Aurelius had provided several authenticated signatures from the relevant period.

There were also bank instructions, board approvals, and personal correspondence.

The examiner placed the schedule beside a genuine letter.

"Mr. Aurelius's signature is consistent with the signature on this page."

Jonathan looked at Edward.

"Consistent?"

Dr. Reed continued before either man could ask another question.

"That does not mean he signed this particular document."

He pointed to the signature.

"Look at the ink distribution. The signature on the Northbridge page has no independent writing pressure. There are no corresponding indentations on the paper beneath it. The ink pattern is reproduced rather than deposited directly."

"So it's a copy," Edward said.

"Yes. A mechanically reproduced signature."

"Could it have been copied from an authentic document?"

"Almost certainly. The line characteristics are consistent with a genuine signature from the same period."

Jonathan frowned.

"Then someone took his real signature and put it here."

"That is the most likely explanation."

"Can you identify the original document?"

"Not from the signature alone."

Dr. Reed turned to another page.

"However, I noticed something else."

He placed the signed schedule beside the unsigned carbon copy.

"The lower section of this document was reproduced separately. The horizontal dividing line does not align perfectly with the original table."

Edward leaned closer.

"How much of the original page was removed?"

"I cannot determine that from these copies. But the signed strip appears to have been attached to a document that did not originally contain it."

"Was the signature altered?"

"No. The signature itself appears to have been reproduced without substantial alteration."

Dr. Reed paused.

"But the context was changed."

Edward stared at the page.

That distinction mattered.

Someone had taken a genuine signature, removed it from its original context, and placed it beneath a schedule containing three child references.

It did not prove Alexander had authorised anything.

It proved someone wanted the document to appear as though he had.

Alexander Aurelius read the forensic report without speaking.

Adrian stood beside the window.

Damien had taken a seat near the opposite end of the conference table, while Lucien leaned against a cabinet with his arms folded.

Victoria sat beside her husband.

The report lay open between them.

Alexander reached the final page.

"Show me the signature."

Edward passed him the enlarged photograph.

Alexander examined it carefully.

"I recognise it."

"Do you remember signing the document?" Edward asked.

"No."

"Do you recognise the page?"

"No."

"Have you ever seen this Northbridge schedule?"

"Not that I remember."

Edward nodded.

He had expected that answer, but he did not allow himself to interpret it as proof.

"Dr. Reed believes the signature was copied from an authentic document."

Alexander looked at the photograph again.

"Then find the original."

"We are trying."

Alexander placed the photograph down.

"Whoever assembled this wanted the signature to appear connected to the three entries."

"Yes."

"Was there an authorisation heading?"

"No. The original carbon copy has no signature field at all. It contains a note that originating-office confirmation was still pending."

Alexander's expression hardened.

"Then the signature was added afterward."

"That is what the evidence suggests."

Victoria had been silent throughout the examination.

Now she asked, "Could the original document have been an instruction from your office?"

"It could have been."

"Would you have authorised payments to Northbridge?"

Alexander considered the question.

"Possibly. I signed hundreds of administrative and financial documents during that period."

Adrian turned away from the window.

"Would those payments have been made under your personal approval?"

"Not necessarily. The Family Office operated under several delegated financial authorities. I signed consolidated instructions, annual allocations, and exceptional expenditure approvals."

Damien looked at the report.

"And EFO-SL/07?"

Alexander's eyes moved toward him.

"That reference was used by the external family liaison office. It was not a personal authorisation code."

"Could it have appeared on a routine payment instruction?"

"Yes."

Lucien straightened.

"So someone could have taken a payment document, copied Father's signature, and attached it to the Northbridge schedule?"

"That is one possibility," Edward replied.

Alexander looked at him.

"Do not make the mistake of treating a possibility as a conclusion."

"I won't."

The chairman nodded once.

Then he turned toward Adrian.

"Find every financial document bearing the Northbridge reference. Not just the child-related records. Every payment, invoice, receipt, and instruction."

Adrian took out his tablet.

"I'll have the finance archive searched."

"Include the original Family Office records. I want the documents from before the reference was assigned to external representatives."

Victoria looked at her husband.

"And the people who had access to your signature?"

Alexander's answer came after a brief pause.

"That list will be longer than we would like."

Nobody disagreed.

At the Valmont Estate, Edward's absence had become noticeable.

He had left before breakfast and had not returned for lunch.

Nicholas had asked twice whether the investigation had produced anything useful.

Both times, Sebastian had told him to stop disturbing their brother.

By early afternoon, Elena was in the library with a laptop open and several documents spread across the table.

She had been reviewing the financial section of her university application.

The application itself was straightforward.

The supporting documentation was not.

She needed to clarify her educational funding, provide proof of her current address, and submit a statement explaining her intended course of study.

She had spent twenty minutes deciding how to phrase the final statement.

Then she deleted the entire paragraph.

It sounded too much like something written by a person trying to impress an admissions committee.

Elena leaned back.

"Why is writing about yourself harder than writing about anything else?"

From the nearby sofa, Isabelle looked up from a scholarship programme report.

"Because you know too much about yourself."

"That sounds suspiciously philosophical."

"It wasn't meant to be."

Elena turned the laptop slightly.

"Can you read this?"

Isabelle stood and walked over.

She read the statement silently.

Then she pointed to the final paragraph.

"You are trying to explain what you intend to do after graduation."

"Yes."

"But you haven't decided."

"I have some ideas."

"Then write that."

"Universities prefer certainty."

"Universities also prefer honesty."

Elena considered this.

"You're becoming rather inconvenient."

"I've been told that before."

Elena smiled and began rewriting the paragraph.

Isabelle returned to her report.

For a while, the library was quiet except for the occasional sound of a keyboard.

Then Isabelle spoke.

"Do you think they will accept you?"

Elena glanced up.

"I hope so."

"That's not what I asked."

"I know."

Elena saved the document.

"I think I have a reasonable chance. But I don't want to plan my entire life around getting accepted."

Isabelle nodded.

"That's sensible."

"What about you?"

"Economics, probably. Maybe business administration afterward."

"You sound more certain."

"I'm not."

Elena smiled.

"Good. I was beginning to feel inadequate."

Isabelle rolled her eyes.

Their conversation continued in the ordinary way of people who were still learning how to share a home.

Neither knew that, several floors above the library, Edward's investigation had reached another turning point.

That evening, Jonathan returned to the Northbridge archive.

He had not been satisfied with the carbon copy.

If the signature strip had been attached later, there had to be an original document from which it had been copied.

He asked Helen Ward to search the financial archive again, this time using the reference number found in the margin of the reconciliation schedule.

**NB-17/04.**

The number appeared on a payment ledger dated eighteen years earlier.

It was connected to a quarterly family-office expenditure report.

Helen opened the corresponding digital index.

"There are three supporting documents."

Jonathan leaned closer.

"Can we retrieve them?"

"Two are available. The third is marked as transferred."

"Transferred where?"

Helen examined the entry.

"Private accounting archive. Vale Family Office."

Jonathan went still.

"Charles Vale?"

"The original company was dissolved, but its accounting archive was transferred to a legal custodian."

"Do we know who?"

Helen looked at the record.

"Vale Private Counsel."

Jonathan immediately called Edward.

The call came while Edward was reviewing the newly discovered financial references with Alexander.

Edward listened without interrupting.

When Jonathan finished, he asked one question.

"What's the document description?"

"A quarterly consolidated expenditure instruction."

Edward looked toward Alexander.

"Can you send me the reference?"

Jonathan did.

Edward placed the phone on speaker.

Alexander read the number.

His expression changed.

"I remember that report."

Edward looked up.

"What was it?"

"A consolidated expenditure instruction for several external family-office operations. Northbridge was one of the listed service providers."

"Did you sign it?"

"I may have."

"Was it connected to the missing children?"

"No. Not directly."

Alexander paused.

Then he added, "The report covered multiple operations. Security accommodation, medical contingencies, temporary residences, and administrative services."

Victoria's attention sharpened.

"Medical contingencies?"

"That category was broad. It included emergency accommodation and expenses for people under temporary protection."

Edward looked at the three child references on the schedule.

"Could the Northbridge payments have been included?"

"Yes."

"Could you have known what the payments were for?"

Alexander was silent for a moment.

"I would have known the general allocation. Not necessarily the individual recipients."

"Would the instruction have contained your signature?"

"If it was an exceptional consolidated allocation, yes."

Edward understood the possibility.

A genuine signature.

A legitimate financial document.

A copied signature strip.

An administrative schedule altered to make it appear that the chairman had personally approved three specific child placements.

The original document might explain the signature.

It would not necessarily explain the children.

"Can you remember who prepared the report?" Edward asked.

"Charles Vale's office submitted the external expenditure summaries. The Family Office reviewed them before consolidation."

"Did you review the individual supporting records?"

"No. That was the responsibility of the finance and compliance departments."

"Who was responsible for compliance?"

Alexander's expression grew colder.

"At the time?"

"Yes."

"Gabriel Vale was the external liaison. Margaret Voss handled part of the internal monitoring. The compliance department had its own director."

"Name?"

"Peter Langford."

Edward wrote it down.

Alexander continued.

"But do not assume the report was fraudulent simply because its signature was reused. The original expenditure could have been legitimate."

"I understand."

"No," Alexander said quietly. "I want you to understand something more important."

Edward looked at him.

"An authorised payment does not authorise every action taken by the person receiving it."

The room fell silent.

Alexander looked once more at the photograph of his copied signature.

"If someone used money allocated for temporary protection to move a child without proper authority, the financial approval would not make that transfer lawful."

Edward nodded.

That was the distinction they needed to preserve.

Later that night, the first recovered page from the original expenditure instruction arrived.

It was incomplete.

The upper half contained the heading, the accounting period, and the reference number.

The lower half contained a list of service providers.

Northbridge appeared on the third line.

Beside it was a financial allocation.

The final page carried Alexander's signature.

But the heading above the signature was not *Authorised by*.

It read:

**Chairman's approval of consolidated expenditure ceiling.**

Edward read the line twice.

Then he looked at the amount.

It was considerably larger than the amount listed on the Northbridge schedule.

The consolidated instruction covered several facilities and external service providers.

It did not name Child 28-B.

It did not name IV-204.

It did not name Child 28-C.

It did not authorise any individual transfer.

The signature was genuine.

The financial approval was genuine.

The connection to the three child records had been manufactured afterward.

Edward placed the recovered page beside the Northbridge schedule.

The documents now told a more precise story.

Someone had taken a legitimate financial approval and used its signature to create the appearance of personal authorisation for a separate administrative record.

But the person who had done it still had not been identified.

And there was another problem.

The original expenditure instruction had been prepared by the external liaison office.

The preparer's name appeared in a small box beneath the accounting reference.

Edward leaned closer.

The handwriting was familiar.

He had seen it in the hospital corrections, the receiving instructions, and the verification papers.

He read the name aloud.

"Charles Vale."

Jonathan looked at him.

"Again?"

Edward nodded.

Charles had submitted the financial report.

Charles had concealed the correction to Victoria's false signature.

Charles had known about the ninth-day instruction.

And now his office had prepared the document from which Alexander's signature had been copied.

It was not proof that Charles had arranged every transfer.

But it placed him much closer to the centre of the operation than before.

Edward picked up his phone.

He needed to speak with Margaret Voss again.

This time, he would not ask her whether she had seen the signature.

He would ask her what she knew about the money.

Because if the Northbridge schedule had been assembled after the original financial approval, someone had needed access to both documents.

And someone had needed a reason to make three uncertain child records look like three officially approved placements.

Edward looked down at the final line of the expenditure instruction.

Beneath Charles Vale's name was another notation.

**Supporting beneficiary schedule retained separately — original custodian: M. Voss.**

Margaret Voss had not merely received the comparison report.

She had been entrusted with the original beneficiary schedule.

And that schedule had still not been found.
