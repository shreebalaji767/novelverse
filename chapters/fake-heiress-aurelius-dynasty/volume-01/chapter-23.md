# **Chapter 23 — The Box Reserved for a Mother**

The words were printed in black ink.

They were ordinary words, arranged in an ordinary administrative format.

Yet nobody in the archive room seemed willing to speak after reading them.

**DO NOT RELEASE TO AURELIUS FAMILY OFFICE.**

**AUTHORISED RECIPIENT: THE MOTHER OF THE CHILDREN.**

Edward read the second line again.

Then he looked at the first.

"That's an unusual restriction."

Jonathan Pierce stood beside the archive table, his hands resting on the back of an empty chair.

"Unusual is an understatement. The file was created under Vale Private Counsel. It was later transferred to the firm's restricted legal repository. The restriction was carried forward through three separate archival reviews."

Adrian glanced at the document.

"Who maintained it?"

"Initially, Charles Vale. After his death, the repository's legal custodian. The most recent review was conducted six years ago."

"And nobody questioned the wording?"

"They questioned it. The answer was always the same. No release without authorisation from the originating representative or an appropriate legal order."

Richard Valmont leaned back.

"Then we obtain the legal order."

"That may be possible," Pierce replied. "But the difficulty is establishing who has the right to request it. The document doesn't identify the mother by name."

Alexander Aurelius had been silent for several minutes.

He looked at the two lines.

"The mother of the children."

His voice was quiet.

"Which mother?"

Nobody answered.

There were two women whose names appeared in the original hospital records.

Victoria Aurelius.

Catherine Valmont.

One had given birth at 2:17 in the morning. The other at 5:46.

But the hospital's altered records had made those facts unreliable as a guide to the children's identities.

The archive contained two infants, two registration numbers, two provisional placements and a private nursery discharge register that did not identify the people who had actually received either child.

Now there was a box reserved for a mother.

And nobody knew which one.

"Could it mean both?" Edward asked.

Pierce considered the possibility.

"Perhaps. The plural wording could refer to two mothers. Or it could be a general description of the intended recipient. We cannot interpret a legal restriction based on assumptions."

Sebastian folded his arms.

"If it means both mothers, why prohibit the Aurelius Family Office?"

"Because the office and the mother are not necessarily the same legal recipient," Pierce said. "The restriction may have been intended to protect the information from institutional access, even if the originating family was involved."

Adrian looked at his father.

"Or someone wanted to prevent the office from discovering what had happened."

Alexander's expression did not change.

"That possibility has been under consideration for some time."

Richard turned towards Pierce.

"Can you identify the originating representative?"

"Not from the repository index. The name field was removed from the accessible copy. The original designation is sealed inside the box's access record."

Edward frowned.

"So the box contains the document that identifies who is allowed to open the box."

"Not necessarily. It may contain a signed instruction, a legal designation or a different kind of authority record. We won't know until we obtain lawful access."

Nicholas, who had been standing near the window, finally spoke.

"That's remarkably inconvenient."

Nobody laughed.

He looked at the document again.

"Sorry. I know this isn't a joke."

"It isn't," Edward said.

Nicholas nodded.

"Then let's find the person who decided that only a mother could receive it."

Pierce closed the folder.

"That is probably where we should begin."

### **1. The Problem with an Old Legal File**

The meeting continued for another hour.

Pierce explained that Vale Private Counsel had maintained several categories of restricted files. Some involved inheritance disputes, others involved protected witnesses, confidential medical arrangements and family identity matters.

Box 114 had been classified under the last category.

Its creation date was two days before the births.

Its first review had taken place nine days later.

Its second review had occurred six weeks after that.

The file had then remained sealed.

"Who requested the first review?" Adrian asked.

"Charles Vale."

"And the second?"

Pierce checked his notes.

"Eleanor Vale."

Edward looked up.

"Charles's wife?"

"Yes."

"Was she a lawyer?"

"Not formally. She worked in the firm's administrative division before marrying Charles. She understood the internal procedures, however. She had access to several restricted archival systems through her previous position."

Richard tapped one finger against the table.

"Did she have authority to change the recipient?"

"No. But she could request a review, submit supporting documents and place a temporary preservation hold if she believed a file was at risk."

"Could she have placed the restriction herself?"

"Possibly. The archive record does not show who originally entered it."

Adrian looked at the document again.

"Can we find her?"

Pierce shook his head.

"Eleanor Vale died seven years ago."

"Then we speak to whoever handled her estate."

"That is already being arranged."

Alexander finally moved away from the table.

"Find every document she submitted regarding Box 114. Not only the correspondence that survived in the archive. I want the original submission records, the dates, the receiving officers and every person who reviewed them."

Pierce nodded.

"I'll begin immediately."

Richard stood.

"We should also contact the hospital."

Edward gave him a questioning look.

"The hospital records have already been reviewed."

"Not the current hospital administration. The original institution has changed ownership twice, but its legal records should still have a custodian. If Box 114 was connected to a birth arrangement, there may be a corresponding document in its files."

"That's possible," Edward said.

Alexander looked towards Richard.

"Then our legal teams can coordinate."

Richard gave a brief nod.

For the first time since the investigation began, the two family heads seemed to be approaching the problem from the same direction.

They still did not trust each other completely.

But neither could afford to investigate alone.

### **2. A Question at Breakfast**

At the Valmont Estate, breakfast was considerably less complicated.

At least, that was what Elena hoped.

She sat at the dining table with a notebook open beside her plate.

A laptop displayed a university exchange programme application.

Nicholas was eating toast.

Edward's chair was empty.

Sebastian was reading the financial section of a newspaper on his tablet.

Catherine was reviewing a message from the foundation.

Richard had already finished his coffee.

Isabelle arrived several minutes after Elena.

"Good morning."

"Morning," Elena replied.

Nicholas glanced at the clock.

"You're late."

Isabelle looked at him.

"By three minutes."

"Three minutes is the beginning of a dangerous habit."

Elena closed her notebook.

"Coming from the person who once arrived forty minutes late to his own birthday lunch, that's an interesting statement."

"That was different."

"How?"

"I had a reason."

"What reason?"

Nicholas considered it.

"I didn't want to attend."

Sebastian lowered his tablet.

"That isn't a reason. That's an admission."

Isabelle smiled faintly as she sat down.

Catherine looked at Elena's laptop.

"Are you working on the exchange application?"

"Yes. I found the programme details yesterday."

"Which university?"

"One in Singapore. There's another programme in Europe, but the application requirements are slightly different."

"Have you decided which one you prefer?" Richard asked.

"Not yet. I want to compare the courses first."

Sebastian glanced at the screen.

"That's sensible. Look at the actual modules, not just the university's reputation. Also check whether the credits transfer."

"I've already made a comparison sheet."

Nicholas looked offended.

"Of course you have."

Elena blinked.

"What?"

"You've made a spreadsheet."

"Yes?"

"You're eighteen."

"That's generally when people apply for university programmes."

"Normal eighteen-year-olds don't make spreadsheets for fun."

"I don't make them for fun."

Sebastian looked at him.

"She makes them because she is organised. You should try it."

Nicholas took another bite of toast.

"I organise things mentally."

"Your room is evidence against that claim," Elena said.

Isabelle laughed.

It was a small sound, but it came easily.

Nicholas pointed at her.

"You're supposed to be new here. You shouldn't be joining their side so quickly."

"Which side?" Isabelle asked.

"The side that attacks me before breakfast."

"Perhaps you're making it too easy," she replied.

Elena hid her smile behind her glass.

For a few minutes, the conversation remained ordinary.

Then Catherine noticed Elena looking at the university application again.

"Have you considered what it would be like to study abroad?"

Elena looked up.

"A little."

"Would you be comfortable staying away from home?"

"I think so. It would be different, but that's part of the experience."

Catherine nodded slowly.

"Then you should apply."

Elena studied her mother's expression.

There was something unusually careful about the way Catherine had said it.

"You're sure?"

"Of course."

"I don't want everyone to feel that I'm leaving because of everything happening here."

Catherine put down her phone.

"Elena, studying abroad is not an act of abandonment."

Nicholas nodded.

"Besides, you'll have to return eventually. Someone has to stop Edward from becoming completely unbearable."

"Edward is already unbearable," Sebastian said.

"See? It's a family responsibility."

Isabelle looked between them.

"Does Edward know about this responsibility?"

"Unfortunately," Nicholas replied. "He has been informed many times."

Elena smiled.

She returned to her application.

But Catherine continued watching her for a moment.

The investigation was becoming more complicated with every discovery.

And Elena was still trying to decide which university would offer her the best economics programme.

Catherine wondered how long they could protect that ordinary part of her life.

### **3. The Other Mother**

Victoria Aurelius was sitting in the family library when Adrian found her.

A book lay open in front of her.

She had not turned a page in nearly twenty minutes.

"Mother?"

She looked up.

"Yes?"

Adrian approached and placed a thin folder on the table.

"We found something else."

Victoria looked at the folder.

"About the hospital?"

"About the sealed legal archive."

He sat opposite her and explained the discovery.

The restriction.

The wording.

The unidentified recipient.

The prohibition against release to the Aurelius Family Office.

Victoria listened without interrupting.

When he finished, she looked towards the window.

"The mother of the children."

"Yes."

"Does that mean me?"

"We don't know."

Victoria lowered her gaze.

For eighteen years, she had been told that her daughter had disappeared.

At first, she had believed the hospital had made an administrative mistake.

Then the explanations had changed.

A missing transfer form.

A misplaced identification record.

A failed attempt to locate a receiving guardian.

Eventually, the hospital had stopped providing useful answers.

She had spent years looking for a child whose identity had become a collection of contradictory documents.

Now she was being told that someone had reserved a sealed box for a mother.

But even that simple description had become uncertain.

"Was Catherine Valmont also listed as a mother in the original records?" she asked.

"Yes."

Victoria nodded.

"Then she has as much reason to want the contents as I do."

Adrian did not answer immediately.

"Would you be comfortable meeting her?"

Victoria looked at him.

"Why wouldn't I be?"

"Because we don't know what the documents contain."

"That is precisely why we should speak."

She closed the book.

"I don't want another family to become an enemy simply because our children may be connected to the same mistake."

Adrian watched her carefully.

His mother had spent eighteen years waiting for a daughter.

He knew what that waiting had done to her.

But she was still able to consider another woman's feelings.

"I'll arrange a meeting," he said.

Victoria shook her head gently.

"Not yet."

"Mother?"

"I would like to know more about the legal restriction first. I don't want Catherine to feel that I'm asking her to surrender something that may belong to her."

Adrian nodded.

"All right."

Victoria looked at the folder again.

"One more thing."

"Yes?"

"If we discover that the box was intended for me, I want to know why."

Her voice became quieter.

"Not only who authorised it. I want to know why someone thought I should receive the truth privately, instead of through my own family."

### **4. The Woman Who Had Kept the Correspondence**

Jonathan Pierce arrived at the former Vale residence shortly after noon.

The house had been sold years earlier, but Eleanor Vale's personal papers had been transferred to a private estate archive maintained by the family's surviving relatives.

The current custodian was Eleanor's niece, Margaret Vale.

She was in her early fifties and had agreed to meet Pierce after receiving a formal request.

"You said this concerns my aunt's old legal work," Margaret began.

"Yes. Specifically, correspondence related to a file created eighteen years ago."

"She kept a great deal of correspondence."

"We're looking for material connected to a maternity identity matter."

Margaret's expression changed slightly.

"That sounds familiar."

Pierce leaned forward.

"You remember something?"

"Not the exact file. But I remember my aunt mentioning a hospital."

"When?"

"Years ago. I was visiting her after my uncle died. She had several boxes of old papers in the study. I asked why she hadn't disposed of them."

"And what did she say?"

"She said some things should not be destroyed until the people involved had a chance to understand what happened."

Pierce remained still.

"Did she mention the children?"

"No. She never gave me details."

"Did she mention a mother?"

Margaret looked at him.

"She said there was a woman who had been denied an answer."

Pierce's attention sharpened.

"Did she say who?"

"No."

"Did she identify the family?"

Margaret shook her head.

"She was very careful. She said that even after all those years, the wrong person might still be asking the right questions."

Pierce took out a notebook.

"Do you remember anything else?"

Margaret thought for a moment.

"She once said that a mother should not have to prove she was allowed to know what happened to her own child."

Pierce stopped writing.

The sentence was too close to the restriction on Box 114 to ignore.

"Did she keep any documents related to that statement?"

"I don't know. But I can check."

Margaret stood and walked towards a cabinet.

She returned several minutes later carrying a small archival box.

It contained letters, photographs and old administrative papers.

Pierce did not touch anything until she had reviewed the contents.

Near the bottom was a brown envelope.

The writing on the front was unmistakable.

**BOX 114 — CORRESPONDENCE REVIEW**

Pierce looked at Margaret.

"May I examine this?"

She nodded.

He opened the envelope carefully.

Inside were three pages.

The first was a formal request for a review of the restriction.

The second was a short handwritten note.

The third was a copy of an internal message.

Pierce read the first page.

It had been written by Eleanor Vale.

The request was dated six weeks after the hospital births.

It asked the firm to maintain the sealed status of Box 114 until the identities of both infants could be independently verified.

That was consistent with the other documents.

The handwritten note, however, contained something new.

> The recipient must not be selected by reference to the family office, the hospital registration or the person who arranged the transfer. The recipient must be identified through the original maternity records.

Pierce read it twice.

Then he looked at the final page.

It was a short internal message from Charles Vale.

The message contained only one sentence.

**Do not ask either family to nominate the recipient. The originating instruction must identify her.**

Pierce slowly lowered the paper.

For the first time, he understood that the restriction was not simply preventing the Aurelius Family Office from accessing the archive.

It was preventing both families from choosing who would receive it.

The intended mother had already been designated somewhere.

The identity was simply hidden.

### **5. The Original Maternity Records**

Edward received Pierce's call that afternoon.

He listened carefully as Pierce described the documents.

"So Eleanor explicitly said the recipient must be identified through the original maternity records?"

"Correct."

"Not the revised hospital records?"

"Correct."

"And Charles instructed the firm not to let either family nominate someone?"

"Yes."

Edward stood beside his office window.

"Then the original records are more important than we thought."

"They are the only documents the instruction recognises."

"Do we still have access to the originals?"

"Not all of them. The hospital's maternity ledger has been preserved, but some associated documents remain missing."

Edward thought about the two birth entries.

2:17 a.m.

5:46 a.m.

The registration numbers.

The private nursery.

The missing transfer signatures.

The children who had been discharged without their receiving guardians being identified.

"There's something else," Edward said.

"What?"

"The hospital's original admission records may include a separate maternal identification section. We have not obtained the complete version."

Pierce was silent for a moment.

"Then we should request it."

"I'll speak to the hospital's legal custodian."

"Be careful. If the restriction was created for a reason, someone may still be watching the records."

Edward glanced towards the door.

"Do you think the person who arranged the original transfer is still involved?"

"I don't know."

"Martin Hale was threatened."

"Yes."

"And someone used Alexander Aurelius's authority without his knowledge."

"Possibly. We still haven't proved whether the authorisation was forged or misrepresented."

Edward rubbed his forehead.

Every answer seemed to create another question.

"Send me copies of Eleanor's correspondence."

"I will."

The call ended.

Edward remained at the window for a moment.

Then he opened the investigation file and added a new heading.

**BOX 114 — INTENDED RECIPIENT IDENTIFICATION**

Under it, he wrote:

*Original maternity records required.*

### **6. Two Girls and a University Application**

That evening, Elena found Isabelle sitting at the small table in the library.

Several university brochures were spread around her.

Elena stopped at the doorway.

"Are you researching universities?"

Isabelle looked up.

"Yes. I thought I should start looking properly."

"Have you chosen a subject?"

"Business administration, probably. Maybe economics."

Elena smiled.

"Those are close to my choices."

"I noticed."

Isabelle moved one brochure aside.

"Do you mind if I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"Did you always know what you wanted to study?"

Elena considered the question.

"Not always. I liked several subjects. I used to think I might study literature, then I became interested in economics. Eventually I realised I wanted something that would help me understand how businesses and organisations actually work."

"Because of the Valmont family?"

"Partly, perhaps. But not only because of them."

Elena sat opposite her.

"I've always been curious about why some businesses succeed while others fail. How people make decisions, how money moves, why certain projects receive funding and others don't."

Isabelle nodded.

"I understand that."

"What about you?"

Isabelle looked down at the brochure.

"I want to build something that belongs to me."

Elena waited.

"I've spent a lot of time trying to prove that I can do things without someone giving me an opportunity out of sympathy. I don't want my future to depend on being somebody's daughter."

Elena understood that feeling more than she expected.

"Then we have something in common."

Isabelle gave a small smile.

"Do you think people will treat us differently at university?"

"Probably."

"Because of the family?"

"Because of everything. The Valmont name, the Aurelius investigation, the rumours if anyone discovers what's happening."

Isabelle leaned back.

"That sounds exhausting."

"It might be."

Elena closed one of the brochures.

"But we don't have to make every decision based on what other people think."

Isabelle studied her.

"You're surprisingly calm about this."

"I'm not calm."

"You seem calm."

"That's because I'm very good at looking calm while worrying about five different things."

Isabelle laughed.

Elena smiled.

"Besides, I have an application deadline. That is a much more immediate problem."

"How immediate?"

"Next Friday."

Isabelle stared at her.

"Why didn't you say that earlier?"

"Because I was discussing your university plans."

"Your application is due next Friday?"

"Yes."

"And you're sitting here talking to me?"

Elena looked at her notebook.

"I can do both."

Isabelle shook her head.

"You're impossible."

"That's what Nicholas says."

"Then Nicholas might be right about something."

Elena gasped in mock offence.

Isabelle laughed again.

For a while, they worked side by side.

They compared programme requirements, discussed tuition arrangements and looked at exchange opportunities.

Neither girl mentioned the investigation.

Neither asked whether the other might eventually leave.

For that evening, they were simply two eighteen-year-olds trying to decide what to study.

### **7. The Name Behind the Restriction**

At 8:40 p.m., Adrian received the report from Jonathan Pierce.

He read it in the Aurelius Estate's private study.

Alexander stood beside the desk.

Victoria sat nearby.

Lucien had joined them after finishing a business call, while Damien was reviewing a separate security report.

Julian was supposed to be studying.

He had been told not to interrupt.

He had interrupted twice.

This time, he was standing outside the open doorway.

"Is it about my sister?"

Alexander looked towards him.

"Come in, Julian."

Julian entered immediately.

Adrian placed the report on the desk.

"Eleanor Vale's correspondence confirms that the recipient of Box 114 was supposed to be identified through the original maternity records. Neither family was permitted to nominate the recipient."

Victoria read the copied note.

"The original maternity records," she repeated.

"Yes," Adrian said.

"Then the person who wrote this expected the hospital to have preserved something that would identify her."

"That's our current interpretation."

Julian looked between them.

"Does that mean you might find out where she is?"

Nobody answered immediately.

Adrian chose his words carefully.

"It may help us establish what happened to both children."

Julian looked disappointed.

"But it doesn't tell us where she is."

"Not yet."

He lowered his eyes.

"All right."

Victoria reached out and touched his arm.

"We are closer than we were yesterday."

Julian nodded, though he still looked unconvinced.

Lucien leaned back in his chair.

"At least we have a direction now."

Damien closed his security report.

"There's another concern."

Alexander looked at him.

"What is it?"

"The recent calls to Martin Hale. We have been tracing the numbers, but the caller used temporary routing services. We haven't identified the person."

"Any connection to the former family office?"

"Not yet."

"Continue."

Damien nodded.

Adrian returned to the report.

"There is one more thing. Eleanor's request was made six weeks after the births. That means she had access to information we haven't recovered yet."

Victoria looked at him.

"She knew something."

"Yes."

"Something important enough to keep the box sealed."

Adrian nodded.

Alexander walked to the window.

Outside, the estate gardens were illuminated by low lights.

For eighteen years, he had believed that finding the original hospital records would answer the central question.

Now he wondered whether the records themselves had been deliberately divided to keep the answer out of reach.

"Contact the hospital tomorrow," he said.

"We will," Adrian replied.

"And Pierce should continue examining Eleanor's personal correspondence."

"Already arranged."

Alexander turned.

"Do not let anyone approach the mothers or children with unverified conclusions."

Julian looked up.

"Even if we find out which one is our sister?"

"Especially then."

Julian frowned.

"But if she's our sister—"

"Then she deserves the truth," Alexander said. "Not a collection of guesses delivered to her as facts."

Julian was quiet.

After a moment, he nodded.

Victoria looked down at Eleanor's handwritten note.

The recipient must be identified through the original maternity records.

She wondered whether the answer had been waiting for her all these years.

And whether someone had deliberately ensured that she would never be allowed to find it.

### **8. The Page That Had Been Removed**

At the Valmont Estate, Edward was still working.

He had requested the hospital's complete maternity admission records and was waiting for confirmation from the legal custodian.

A notification appeared on his screen.

It was from the hospital's archival department.

**Subject: Additional Material Located — Historical Maternity Records**

Edward opened it.

The message was brief.

The archivist had located a separate folder stored alongside the original maternity ledger. It had not been included in the earlier document transfer because it was classified under maternal admission rather than infant transfer.

The folder contained admission forms, identification verification sheets and a series of supplementary pages.

One page had been removed.

The remaining sheets showed two entries.

**Mother: V. Aurelius**

**Mother: C. Valmont**

Both entries included a reference to an attached identification verification form.

Neither form was present.

Edward read the message again.

Then he noticed the final paragraph.

*The missing pages appear to have been removed before the records were transferred to the hospital's central archive. The removal was documented in a handwritten preservation note dated eighteen years ago.*

His eyes narrowed.

The note had been signed.

Not by Margaret Ellis.

Not by Dr. Samuel Whitmore.

Not by Charles Vale.

The signature belonged to a name that had appeared repeatedly throughout the investigation.

**Martin Hale.**

Edward leaned back.

Hale had signed the nursery discharge entries.

He had received the original identity report.

He had collected the package addressed to Victoria Aurelius.

He had admitted seeing both infants.

And now his signature appeared on a note documenting the removal of the original maternal identification pages.

Edward immediately called Adrian.

When Adrian answered, Edward spoke without greeting.

"We found the missing maternity records."

A brief silence followed.

"Are they complete?"

"No. The identification pages were removed eighteen years ago."

"By whom?"

Edward looked at the signature on his screen.

"Martin Hale."

For several seconds, neither man spoke.

Then Adrian asked the question Edward had been dreading.

"Did he remove them before or after the children left the hospital?"

Edward read the date.

His expression hardened.

"Before."

He looked at the final line of the preservation note.

*Original maternal identification pages transferred to authorised recipient for confidential verification.*

There was no recipient name.

Only a reference number.

**EFO-SL/07.**

And beneath it, in smaller handwriting, was one final instruction:

**Do not return these pages to the hospital until both mothers have been identified.**
