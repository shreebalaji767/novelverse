# Chapter 42 — The Station That Existed in the Budget

The name A-2 appeared in three documents.

The first was the original six-facility funding summary.

The second was a regional equipment allocation schedule.

The third was a Treasury reserve authorization signed by Arthur Fenwick.

All three described the same facility.

Ashenford Western Relay Station A-2.

None established where it was.

Seraphina placed the documents side by side.

"How much money was allocated?"

Mr Bellamy checked the Treasury schedule.

"Six hundred crowns for initial equipment and preparatory work."

"Was the money released?"

"Not all of it. Four hundred and twenty crowns were transferred to the regional infrastructure account. The remaining one hundred and eighty were retained centrally."

"Do we know what the four hundred and twenty paid for?"

"Not yet."

Seraphina looked at the funding summary.

"Was there a construction contract?"

"None has been located."

"Land acquisition?"

"No record."

"An engineering appointment?"

"There is a reference to a preliminary survey."

"Was the survey completed?"

Bellamy turned a page.

"The payment was authorized. The completion certificate is missing."

Seraphina leaned back.

A facility had received an allocation.

Equipment had been reserved for it.

Money had been transferred.

A survey had apparently been commissioned.

But there was no confirmed location, construction contract or completion certificate.

It was not enough to call the facility imaginary.

It was enough to ask why the kingdom had spent money preparing for it.

Theodore convened a meeting the following morning.

The committee's six-facility register was displayed on the table.

The first three entries were relatively familiar.

N-4 had a confirmed physical location.

W-2 had a confirmed physical location.

E-1 remained a catalogue reference without a verified site.

The remaining entries were less certain.

S-3 appeared in southern infrastructure records.

A-2 appeared in Ashenford funding and equipment schedules.

The sixth facility remained an unidentified reserve entry.

Theodore pointed to A-2.

"We now know that a serviceable regulating casing was reserved for this station."

"Yes," Seraphina said.

"And that an initial allocation was made."

"Yes."

"Then why is its location unknown?"

"Because the records we have do not identify it."

Minister Garrick frowned.

"Could the station have been cancelled?"

"Possibly."

"Then why was the equipment reservation not released?"

"We don't know."

"Could the allocation have been transferred to another project?"

"Possibly."

"Could the station have been constructed under a different name?"

"Also possible."

Garrick looked increasingly dissatisfied.

"Are there any answers?"

Seraphina considered.

"Yes. We know that the project existed in the administrative system. We do not yet know whether the physical facility was built."

Lucien nodded.

"That is an important distinction."

Theodore turned to Holt.

"Find the preliminary survey."

Holt made a note.

The first survey record was located in the Ashenford regional archive.

It was a payment certificate.

The document authorized a payment to a surveying firm called **Northvale Cartographic Services**.

The work was described as:

*Preliminary western corridor assessment for proposed relay infrastructure.*

There was no station number on the certificate.

No map was attached.

The payment had been made.

The firm had signed the receipt.

But the survey report itself was missing.

Seraphina read the certificate.

"Was Northvale Cartographic Services a registered company?"

"Yes," Holt replied. "It operated in Ashenford for approximately thirty years."

"Does it still exist?"

"No. It closed nineteen years ago."

"Who owned it?"

"The surviving commercial register lists two partners. One was Edmund Northvale. The other was his brother, Samuel."

Seraphina looked at the date.

"Can either be contacted?"

"Edmund died several years ago. Samuel is believed to be living in the capital."

"Then we should ask him about the survey."

Samuel Northvale arrived two days later.

He was an elderly gentleman with a careful manner and an unusually strong memory for dates.

When Holt showed him the payment certificate, he adjusted his spectacles.

"Yes. I remember this assignment."

"Do you remember what was surveyed?" Seraphina asked.

"The western corridor."

"Was a relay station included?"

"That was the proposed purpose."

"Did you identify a location?"

"We identified several possible locations."

"How many?"

"Three."

Holt wrote this down.

Northvale continued.

"The first was near the old military road. The second was closer to the river crossing. The third was further north, beyond the abandoned quarry."

"Which one was selected?"

Northvale looked at the certificate.

"I don't believe we were told."

"Did you submit a report?"

"Yes."

"To whom?"

"The regional infrastructure office."

"Do you remember the recipient?"

"Not the individual's name. I remember the office was undergoing a reorganization."

Seraphina exchanged a glance with Holt.

"Was the report accepted?"

"It was received. Whether it was formally approved is another matter."

"Do you have a copy?"

Northvale shook his head.

"My brother kept the technical drawings. I kept the commercial records. After the company closed, the papers were divided."

"Where are your brother's drawings?"

"Some were donated to the regional survey archive. Others went to the family estate."

"Can we examine them?"

"Of course. I have no objection."

The regional survey archive contained two maps.

The first showed the western corridor before the proposed infrastructure project.

The second showed the same area after the preliminary survey.

Three locations were marked with small circles.

None was labelled A-2.

Each had a reference number.

**Site W-1.**

**Site W-2.**

**Site W-3.**

Seraphina looked at the map.

"Could these be the three possible locations?"

"Yes," Northvale confirmed.

"Was one selected?"

"I cannot say."

Lucien examined the markings.

"These are not engineering site numbers. They are survey references."

"Could any of them support a relay station?"

"That would depend on the terrain, the mana flow and the final circuit design."

Seraphina looked at the first location.

It lay close to the old military road.

The second was near the river.

The third was north of the abandoned quarry.

"Do we know whether any of these locations were later used for another facility?"

Holt checked the regional maps.

"The military road site is currently marked as a storage compound. The river site is unoccupied. The quarry site is listed as an abandoned extraction area."

"Then we need to inspect the maps from the year after the survey."

The next set of maps showed something unexpected.

The proposed northern site had been marked with a new boundary line.

A small rectangular structure appeared near the quarry road.

It was labelled:

**Western Service Shelter — 14.**

There was no reference to A-2.

No magical infrastructure notation.

No circuit symbol.

Just a service shelter.

Seraphina studied the map.

"Was this structure built?"

Holt checked the regional property register.

"Yes. It was constructed on land leased by the regional authority."

"Who owns it now?"

"The land remains under regional administration. The building is listed as disused."

Lucien leaned over the map.

"Could this have been A-2?"

"Possibly," Seraphina replied. "But the map does not establish that."

He examined the location.

"It is close to one of the proposed survey sites."

"Close is not the same as identical."

"Agreed."

Seraphina made a note.

**Western Service Shelter 14 — possible relationship to A-2. Physical inspection required.**

Theodore approved a limited inspection.

The committee would not authorize excavation, restoration or equipment installation.

It would send a small team to examine the site, confirm the building's identity and compare it with the old survey drawings.

Seraphina was not required to attend.

She was also not particularly interested in spending several days travelling through the western marches.

Unfortunately, the site records were incomplete, and the committee wanted someone familiar with the administrative history present.

She considered refusing.

Then she remembered the original six-facility register.

If A-2 had been built under another name, the inspection could clarify the entire project.

"How long would the journey take?" she asked.

"Three days each way," Holt replied.

Seraphina looked at him.

"That is not a short inspection."

"No."

"Could the local authority examine it first?"

"They could confirm the building's condition, but they may not have access to the historical records."

Seraphina sighed.

"Then I will go."

Evelyne was not pleased.

"You have only just returned from the previous inspection."

"It is a different site."

"That is precisely the problem. Every time one site produces a question, you travel to another."

"I am not responsible for every facility."

"Yet you keep accepting responsibility for the records."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

"The committee is responsible for the inspection. I am only attending as an administrative adviser."

Evelyne gave her a measured look.

"That is what you said before the last journey."

"Because it was true."

"And how many days were you away?"

"Six."

"Seven."

Seraphina considered.

"The journey back was delayed."

"Of course."

Evelyne closed the travel schedule.

"Take enough warm clothing. The western roads are colder than the capital."

"I know."

"And do not let Lucien persuade you to inspect any magical structure."

"I have no intention of doing that."

"Good."

Evelyne handed the schedule back.

"Then make sure the people responsible for the technical inspection understand that."

The party departed four days later.

It consisted of Seraphina, Holt, Lucien, two Academy technicians, a regional surveyor and a small security escort.

Theodore remained in the capital.

He had sent a formal letter to the Ashenford regional authority confirming the committee's limited purpose.

No repairs were authorized.

No equipment could be removed.

No changes to the property were permitted without written approval.

Seraphina appreciated the clarity.

The first two days passed without incident.

The third was less pleasant.

A section of the western road had been damaged by heavy rain.

The carriage slowed to a crawl while the local workers repaired a drainage channel.

Seraphina watched them from the window.

"How long will it take?"

The driver glanced ahead.

"Perhaps an hour."

Lucien leaned across the seat.

"Would you like me to stabilize the ground?"

"No."

"It would be a simple spell."

"The workers are already repairing it."

"It might save time."

"It might also interfere with their drainage work."

Lucien looked disappointed.

The driver smiled.

Seraphina returned to her papers.

Western Service Shelter 14 was smaller than the maps suggested.

It stood on a low rise beyond the abandoned quarry, surrounded by scrubland and old fencing.

The building had two rooms, a storage area and a narrow underground chamber.

Its roof was damaged.

The entrance door had been replaced at some point.

A faded regional authority sign remained above the doorway.

The local surveyor introduced himself as Master Colin Reeve.

"This was used as a maintenance shelter for the quarry road," he explained. "It has been empty for at least fifteen years."

Seraphina examined the structure.

"Was it ever used for magical maintenance?"

"Not according to our local records."

Lucien entered the building.

He inspected the walls and floor.

After several minutes, he called for the Academy technicians.

"There are signs of an old mounting platform."

Seraphina remained outside.

"Is it a relay structure?"

"I don't know yet."

The technicians examined the platform.

It was made of reinforced stone, with four metal anchor points.

One corner had been damaged.

A narrow channel ran beneath it.

Lucien studied the channel.

"It may have been designed to carry a low-volume mana conduit."

"May have been?"

"The shape is consistent with older infrastructure."

"Can you identify the station?"

"Not from this alone."

Seraphina looked toward the surveyor.

"Does the building have a basement inventory?"

"No. Only the original property register."

"Can we see it?"

Reeve nodded.

The property register was kept at the regional office.

It listed the shelter's construction as a public maintenance project.

The building had been commissioned twenty years earlier.

Its original purpose was described as:

*Roadside service accommodation and equipment storage.*

There was no mention of A-2.

However, the construction schedule included a separate entry.

*Additional foundation works, eastern chamber.*

Seraphina examined the wording.

"Was this part of the original building?"

Reeve checked the attached plan.

"No. It was added during construction."

"Who authorized it?"

"The regional infrastructure office."

"Under which project?"

The reference was difficult to read.

It appeared to begin with the letters RA.

Lucien leaned closer.

"RA-6."

Seraphina looked at him.

The same allocation reference.

The eastern chamber had been built under the A-2 reserve allocation.

The building was not merely a roadside shelter.

It had been modified for a second purpose.

The inspection continued the following morning.

The underground chamber was cleared of loose debris.

It contained an empty equipment platform and several mounting brackets.

There were no regulating assemblies.

No active magical circuit.

No sign that the structure had ever been commissioned.

One of the technicians discovered a metal plate fixed to the inside wall.

It was badly corroded.

After careful cleaning, several letters became visible.

**A-2.**

Seraphina stared at the plate.

The station existed.

It had been built under another name.

But its official records described it as a roadside shelter.

Lucien examined the mounting platform.

"This was intended for a relay assembly."

"Can you confirm that?"

"The dimensions match the old preliminary design."

"Does that mean the station was completed?"

"No. The platform is present, but the circuit was never installed."

Seraphina looked at the empty chamber.

A facility had been surveyed.

Money had been allocated.

A building had been constructed.

A serviceable casing had been reserved.

Yet the station had never become operational.

The original project had not vanished.

It had been hidden beneath an ordinary building description.

Not necessarily deliberately.

But the difference mattered.

The regional surveyor examined the property register again.

"Why would a relay station be listed as a service shelter?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"Perhaps the building had more than one purpose."

"Then why was the second purpose omitted?"

"That is what we need to find out."

Lucien looked at the old mounting platform.

"The technical installation may have been cancelled before completion."

"Was the cancellation recorded?"

"Not in the documents we have."

Seraphina turned to Holt.

"Please request the original construction authorization and the final inspection report."

Holt wrote it down.

"Also the lease agreement for the land. If the facility had a separate infrastructure purpose, there may have been an additional property schedule."

Reeve nodded.

"I will ask the regional office."

That evening, Seraphina sat outside the shelter.

The western sky was turning orange.

The abandoned quarry lay beyond the low hills, its old stone walls partly covered by vegetation.

Lucien joined her.

"Do you think the station was ever intended to be connected?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"The platform, the chamber, the allocation and the equipment reservation all suggest it."

"Then what stopped it?"

Seraphina looked at the building.

"We don't know."

Lucien sat on the stone step.

"Perhaps the project was abandoned when the regional authority was reorganized."

"Perhaps."

"Or the funds were redirected."

"Possibly."

"Or the technical design changed."

"Also possible."

He looked at her.

"You are not going to guess."

"No."

"Even though you have enough evidence to suspect something?"

"Especially because we have enough evidence to suspect several different things."

Lucien laughed quietly.

Seraphina looked toward the chamber.

There was something unsettling about finding a facility that had existed for twenty years without being properly identified.

But there was also something useful about it.

A-2 was no longer merely a line in a budget.

It had a location.

A structure.

A purpose.

And a history that could finally be examined.

The next morning, the regional office delivered a copy of the original construction authorization.

It contained a reference to the Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office.

The authorization described the work as:

*Preparation of a western relay facility under reserve programme RA-6/19.*

The structure was to include a service shelter, equipment chamber and foundation for a future regulating assembly.

The construction work had been approved.

The equipment installation was listed as a separate stage.

The final line read:

*Further work subject to technical confirmation and release of reserved equipment.*

Seraphina examined the date.

The authorization had been signed by Arthur Fenwick.

She placed the document beside the equipment reservation.

The same officer had approved the allocation and the construction preparation.

This was not proof of wrongdoing.

It did, however, establish that A-2 had been a real project.

The equipment had been reserved.

The building had been constructed.

The installation had been planned.

The final stage had never been completed.

Seraphina looked at Holt.

"Can we locate the technical confirmation?"

"We will request it from the Academy and the former regional engineering office."

"And the equipment release?"

"We will trace the Central Stores records."

She nodded.

Before leaving, Seraphina asked the regional surveyor one final question.

"Was the building ever officially reclassified?"

Reeve checked the property register.

"Yes. Six months after construction, it was reclassified as a roadside maintenance shelter."

"Who authorized the change?"

"The regional property office."

"Was the infrastructure department consulted?"

"There is no record of consultation."

Seraphina examined the entry.

The reclassification had removed A-2 from the regional property index.

The building had remained.

The equipment chamber had remained.

The mounting platform had remained.

Only the official description had changed.

She copied the reference.

The journey back to the capital took three days.

Seraphina used most of it to organize the findings.

The committee's register would need to be revised.

A-2 was now a confirmed physical location.

Its construction was confirmed.

Its intended infrastructure purpose was confirmed.

Its circuit remained uninstalled.

Its equipment reservation was confirmed.

Its final technical approval was missing.

Its later reclassification as a roadside shelter had no recorded consultation with the infrastructure department.

That last detail deserved investigation.

She wrote it carefully.

**A-2: Physical location confirmed. Infrastructure foundation and equipment chamber confirmed. No evidence of circuit installation or commissioning. Reclassified as roadside maintenance shelter six months after construction. Reason and authorization chain require further review.**

She closed the folder.

The committee had now confirmed four of the six facilities in some form.

N-4.

W-2.

A-2.

And the unidentified southern reference still waiting to be examined.

Theodore would want the report immediately.

Seraphina looked out of the carriage window.

She hoped the remaining two facilities were less complicated.

It was an optimistic hope.

She knew better than to trust it.
