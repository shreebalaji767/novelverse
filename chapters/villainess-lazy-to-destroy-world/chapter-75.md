# **Chapter 75 — The Inspection That Had Been Scheduled Before Anyone Asked**

Seraphina did not open the letter immediately.

She held it between two fingers and examined the seal.

The Royal Infrastructure Department had apparently decided that ordinary correspondence was insufficient for its purposes. The envelope was thick, the paper expensive, and the seal large enough to suggest that whatever it contained had been approved by at least three people who would never have to carry it.

Mira waited.

"Should I bring the letter opener, my lady?"

"No."

Seraphina placed the envelope on the nearest table.

"Bring Cedric."

Mira hesitated.

"Master Cedric?"

"Yes."

"And the letter opener?"

Seraphina looked at the envelope.

"Unfortunately."

Cedric arrived fifteen minutes later.

He examined the seal.

"Royal Infrastructure Department."

"I noticed."

"Perhaps it concerns the NTR-4 inspection."

"It concerns an inspection of winter mana regulation facilities."

"That sounds like NTR-4."

"It sounds like several facilities."

Cedric opened the letter.

The Department was preparing a regional inspection programme covering historical winter regulation systems in the northern territories.

The programme would examine surviving infrastructure, identify facilities requiring maintenance, and establish whether any inactive systems remained connected to current networks.

The Department proposed beginning with House Veyr's NTR-4 chamber.

It also requested access to two additional sites.

The first was an old agricultural regulation station near Westmere.

The second was a transport signal facility close to the northern road.

The letter stated that the inspections had been scheduled for the following week.

Seraphina looked up.

"Scheduled?"

Cedric nodded.

"Apparently."

"Who agreed?"

Cedric continued reading.

"The Department has requested provisional cooperation from the regional authority."

"That was not my question."

"No, my lady."

The letter included a preliminary schedule.

It listed the inspection team, travel arrangements, equipment requirements, and estimated duration.

It also listed House Veyr as the local host.

Seraphina leaned back.

"How considerate."

Cedric looked at her.

"They may have assumed the landowner would cooperate."

"Cooperation is not the same as hosting."

"Would you like me to write a reply?"

"Yes. Ask them to distinguish between a request for access and a confirmed arrangement."

Cedric nodded.

"And tell them that House Veyr has not agreed to host the inspection."

"Correct."

"Should we refuse access?"

Seraphina considered it.

"No. We have already agreed to a preliminary surface survey of NTR-4. We should not contradict that agreement."

She pointed to the letter.

"But the additional sites require separate confirmation. We are not responsible for granting access to land we do not own."

Cedric smiled.

"Understood."

Seraphina looked at the schedule again.

The Department had also requested that House Veyr provide accommodation, transport, meals, and local guides.

She tapped the page.

"Who wrote this?"

"The Department's regional coordinator."

"Did he write it before or after deciding that we would provide everything?"

Cedric examined the signature.

"Before, apparently."

Seraphina pushed the letter toward him.

"Please tell him that the estate's kitchens are not a branch of the Royal Infrastructure Department."

Cedric laughed.

The reply was sent the following morning.

It was polite.

It was also very clear.

House Veyr would permit the previously agreed surface survey of NTR-4 under the conditions already established.

The estate would not provide accommodation, meals, or transport unless separately agreed.

Access to the other sites would have to be requested from their respective landowners.

The Department was also asked to provide the names and responsibilities of all inspectors, the equipment they intended to bring, and the technical scope of the proposed work.

The schedule was not accepted as a confirmed arrangement.

Three days later, the Department replied.

It apologized.

The schedule had been prepared by the regional coordination office using an outdated template.

The template assumed that the estate containing a facility would automatically provide local support.

The Department acknowledged that this assumption had no basis in the current agreement.

It also confirmed that the inspection team would be responsible for its own equipment and transport.

Seraphina read the letter.

"Excellent."

Cedric nodded.

"They have corrected the arrangement."

"Yes."

"Will you attend the inspection?"

"No."

Cedric smiled.

"I thought so."

The inspection team arrived the following Monday.

There were four members.

Lucien Arkwright represented the Royal Academy.

Engineer Darius Veylan represented the Technical Standards Directorate.

Master Harlan represented the regional maintenance authority.

And a young surveyor named Elias Trent had been assigned to record the physical condition of the sites.

Cedric met them at the estate gate.

Seraphina received them in the administrative room.

She had no intention of attending the underground inspection.

She did, however, want to make sure everyone understood the limits of the arrangement.

Lucien greeted her.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Master Arkwright."

Darius Veylan bowed.

"Your Ladyship."

Seraphina recognized the surname.

The engineer had no connection to the old contractor, at least none established by the records.

She did not ask.

Instead, she indicated the chairs.

"Before you begin, I would like to confirm the purpose of this inspection."

Lucien nodded.

"The Department wishes to establish the current status of the historical winter regulation network."

"Not restore it?"

"Not at this stage."

"Not activate it?"

"Certainly not."

Darius opened his folder.

"Our immediate task is to identify the physical condition of the remaining facilities, compare their present state with the historical technical records, and determine whether any current circuits depend on them."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Will you be opening NTR-4?"

"No."

"Entering it?"

"No."

"Removing equipment?"

"No."

"Testing the old control ring?"

"No."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we understand each other."

Lucien looked amused.

"Your caution is becoming famous."

"I would prefer it to become unnecessary."

The team began at Westmere.

The agricultural regulation station was located beside an old irrigation channel.

Unlike NTR-4, it remained active.

Its current equipment was comparatively modern.

The original winter regulation chamber had been dismantled during a reconstruction twenty-six years earlier.

The replacement system used a different distribution arrangement.

Master Harlan led the inspection.

The station's heating circuit was functioning.

The irrigation regulator responded within its normal range.

The signal indicator was stable.

The team recorded the current equipment numbers and compared them with the historical plans.

Darius found an old mounting bracket beneath the eastern platform.

It had been left in place after the reconstruction.

The bracket's original function was uncertain.

Lucien examined the technical drawing.

"It may have supported an older balancing assembly."

"Can you confirm that?" Cedric asked.

"No. The drawing identifies the position, not the exact equipment."

The surveyor recorded it as an unidentified historical fitting.

Nobody attempted to remove it.

Nobody declared it dangerous.

The inspection continued.

Seraphina received the report two days later.

The station was functioning normally.

The old chamber had been removed.

The current system was independent.

The bracket remained unidentified.

There was no recommendation for immediate work.

It was an uneventful result.

Seraphina appreciated it.

The second site was less straightforward.

The northern transport signal facility stood beside the old road.

Its original building had been converted into a small maintenance shelter.

The signal system had been replaced, but a section of the old conduit remained beneath the floor.

The current maintenance team had known about it.

They had assumed it was disconnected.

The historical register described it as a winter regulation connection.

The modern site map did not show it.

Darius inspected the visible section.

He found no active flow.

Lucien performed a separate technical reading.

The result was consistent with a disconnected conduit.

Master Harlan asked the local maintenance supervisor when the old section had been sealed.

The supervisor checked his records.

"Twenty-two years ago, during the signal replacement."

"Was the disconnection documented?"

"There is a maintenance entry."

"Does it describe the work?"

The supervisor turned a page.

"It says the old conduit was isolated."

Darius examined the entry.

"Was it physically removed?"

The supervisor shook his head.

"The instruction says isolated, not removed."

Lucien looked toward the floor.

"That distinction matters."

The supervisor frowned.

"Is it unsafe?"

"Not necessarily. We have not found an active connection."

Darius added that a disconnected conduit could remain in place without creating a problem, provided its isolation had been properly completed and its condition remained stable.

The team recorded the uncertainty.

The facility would not be altered until the original isolation procedure could be located.

The local maintenance supervisor looked relieved.

He had apparently expected to be ordered to remove the entire floor.

Seraphina heard about this later.

She was not surprised.

A great deal of trouble could be avoided by distinguishing between something that existed and something that needed to be changed.

The third inspection was NTR-4.

Seraphina remained at the estate.

Cedric accompanied the team as the landowner's representative.

The northern entrance remained blocked.

The southern entrance was accessible only from the slope.

The inspection team examined the surrounding ground.

They found no new cracks, movement, or surface discharge.

The sealed maintenance shaft remained undisturbed.

Darius examined the visible stonework.

The construction appeared stable.

He warned that this did not establish the condition of the underground chamber.

Lucien agreed.

The team took measurements.

The Academy recorded the surface readings.

The maintenance authority checked the drainage around the entrance.

The surveyor updated the location map.

No one opened the chamber.

No one entered the shaft.

The inspection ended before sunset.

Cedric returned with the team.

Seraphina was waiting in the administrative room.

"Well?"

Lucien placed a folder on the table.

"The surface condition is stable."

"Good."

"The chamber remains inaccessible."

"Also good."

"The current circuits are independent."

"Excellent."

Darius opened the folder.

"There is one additional finding."

Seraphina looked at him.

She had been expecting this.

"Go on."

The additional finding concerned the old winter network's distribution diagrams.

The diagrams showed that NTR-4 had once supplied several local circuits through a shared regulating arrangement.

The network had been dismantled in stages.

Some connections had been removed.

Others had been isolated.

A few had been replaced with newer systems.

The historical records did not consistently identify the exact dates of those changes.

Darius had compared the old plans with the current regional maps.

He had found that three modern circuits followed routes close to the original network.

They were not necessarily connected.

Their designs, however, appeared to have inherited parts of the earlier distribution arrangement.

Lucien placed three diagrams side by side.

"The circuits are independent in their current operation," he said.

"Then what is the concern?" Cedric asked.

"Their maintenance standards may have been based on the same old design assumptions."

Seraphina looked at the diagrams.

"Explain that without using words that make me regret inviting you."

Lucien smiled.

"Imagine three separate water channels. Each has its own gate and its own maintenance team. They are not connected anymore. But all three were built using measurements taken from the same old water system."

Seraphina nodded.

"That is understandable."

"If the original measurements were incomplete, the later designs might share the same weakness."

"Even if they are separate?"

"Yes."

Darius added that the problem was not necessarily structural.

The old design might have used assumptions about winter mana flow that no longer held under modern operating conditions.

The current circuits could be perfectly safe.

But the technical standards used to inspect them might be outdated.

Seraphina examined the diagrams.

"Have they been inspected recently?"

"Yes," Master Harlan replied.

"Under which standards?"

He looked at his notes.

"The current regional maintenance standards."

"Are those standards based on the old design?"

"Partially."

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Does that mean the inspections were wrong?"

"No. It means we need to confirm that the standards remain suitable."

She considered this.

That was a different question.

A circuit could be functioning normally while the rules used to evaluate it were no longer sufficient.

The Academy had not discovered a failure.

It had discovered a possible weakness in the assumptions behind the inspection process.

Seraphina did not know enough about magic to judge the technical implications.

She did know that changing standards without understanding the systems could create its own problems.

"How many circuits are affected?"

Darius answered.

"Three confirmed. Possibly more."

"How many have you inspected?"

"Two during this programme. The third is scheduled for next month."

"Can you identify the technical standards used for each?"

"Yes."

"Can you compare the current standards with the original design requirements?"

Lucien nodded.

"That is what the Academy intends to do."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then do that before recommending changes."

Lucien smiled.

"Agreed."

The inspection report took another week.

It contained thirty-two pages.

Seraphina read the summary.

The three facilities had been classified as follows:

**Westmere Agricultural Regulation Station**

The modern system was active and functioning within current operating limits.

The historical winter chamber had been removed.

An unidentified mounting bracket remained.

No immediate action was required.

**Northern Transport Signal Facility**

The modern signal system was active.

The old conduit was believed to be isolated.

The physical removal of the conduit had not been documented.

Further review of the isolation record was recommended.

**NTR-4 Northern Thermal Regulation Chamber**

The chamber remained dormant.

Its surface structure was stable.

Its internal condition was unverified.

No activation or restoration was recommended.

The report also identified three current circuits whose inspection standards were derived partly from historical winter regulation designs.

No immediate faults had been identified.

A technical comparison was recommended.

Seraphina reached the final section.

It contained a proposed schedule for a second inspection programme.

She put the report down.

"No."

Cedric looked up from his own copy.

"What is it?"

"They have included another schedule."

He examined the final page.

The Department proposed a three-month review covering the three modern circuits and two additional historical sites.

It also requested a representative from House Veyr to attend the technical meetings.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Why?"

"Because NTR-4 is on our land."

"That does not make us technical representatives."

Cedric nodded.

The Department's request did not explicitly assign responsibility.

It merely suggested that House Veyr should remain involved.

Seraphina dictated a reply.

House Veyr acknowledged the report.

It would retain its existing responsibility as landowner for NTR-4.

It would not participate in technical decisions, inspections of unrelated facilities, or changes to regional maintenance standards.

The estate would consider future access requests individually.

No permanent representative would be appointed.

The Department was welcome to communicate directly with the Academy and the regional authority regarding technical matters.

Cedric wrote it down.

"Would you like to include a copy of the inspection report?"

"Yes. The entire report."

"Even the proposed schedule?"

"Especially the proposed schedule."

Cedric smiled.

The Department's response arrived several days later.

It accepted the distinction.

The second inspection programme would proceed under the Academy and regional maintenance authority.

House Veyr would not be required to attend.

The Department also agreed that technical standards should not be revised until the historical comparison was complete.

Lucien sent a separate note.

He thanked Seraphina for insisting that the report distinguish between confirmed findings and unresolved questions.

He also mentioned that the Academy had discovered an old technical standards manual.

The manual dated from the period when NTR-4 had been constructed.

Its winter operating limits had been copied into several later regional maintenance guides.

The Academy intended to examine whether those limits remained appropriate.

Seraphina placed the note in the archive.

It was an important discovery.

It was also a matter for the Academy.

She was beginning to appreciate that distinction.

Two days later, the estate physician visited the administrative wing.

Master Rowan had been called to inspect a worker who had developed dizziness while repairing a heating conduit in the eastern service building.

The worker was resting in the infirmary.

His condition was stable.

The problem appeared to have been caused by prolonged exposure to heat and poor ventilation.

The conduit itself was functioning normally.

Seraphina asked whether the worker had received medical attention.

"Yes," Master Rowan replied.

"Is the room safe?"

"The heating is stable, but the ventilation grille is partly obstructed."

"Then the maintenance team should clear it before anyone returns to work."

Master Rowan nodded.

The incident had nothing to do with the old winter regulation network.

It was an ordinary maintenance problem.

But Seraphina found herself looking at the old diagrams again.

The eastern service building used a modern heating circuit.

Its design had been approved years earlier.

Its inspection records were complete.

Yet the room had become uncomfortable because a ventilation grille had been neglected.

Not every problem required a historical explanation.

Sometimes a worker became dizzy because somebody had failed to clear a vent.

She closed the technical report.

"Please tell Master Harlan that the room should not be used until the ventilation has been checked."

"I will."

Master Rowan left.

Seraphina looked at the window.

Outside, the snow had almost disappeared.

The winter had not finished, but it was losing its authority.

That evening, Cedric brought her the Department's latest notice.

The technical comparison had begun.

The Academy had identified a discrepancy between the historical winter operating limits and the limits used by the current regional maintenance guides.

The difference was not large.

It was not yet known whether it affected safety.

The Academy wanted to test the calculations against present-day measurements.

The first test would be conducted at a controlled facility.

No active regional circuit would be altered.

Seraphina read the notice.

"Good."

Cedric looked at her.

"You sound relieved."

"I am relieved that they intend to test their calculations before changing anything."

He nodded.

The notice included one additional sentence.

The Academy had also found that several historical maintenance guides referred to a shared regulating principle that was not described in the surviving technical manuals.

The principle appeared in operating instructions from different regions.

The Academy did not yet know whether it represented a standard design practice or an assumption inherited from the original winter network.

Lucien had requested access to the central technical archive.

Seraphina folded the letter.

"That is interesting."

"Yes."

"Is it dangerous?"

Cedric shook his head.

"No indication of that."

"Then we wait for the test results."

Cedric smiled.

"I will inform the Department that House Veyr has no further comments."

"Please do."

He stood to leave.

Seraphina stopped him.

"One thing."

"Yes?"

"Did Margaret order the new curtains?"

Cedric paused.

"I believe she did."

"How much?"

He told her.

Seraphina stared at him.

"That is an unreasonable price."

Cedric smiled.

"Margaret says the old curtains have begun to lose their insulation."

"They have survived three winters."

"She says they are no longer adequate."

Seraphina leaned back.

The Royal Infrastructure Department had found a discrepancy in fifty-year-old magical engineering principles.

The Academy was testing old assumptions.

The regional authority was reviewing its maintenance standards.

And Margaret Ellison had apparently decided that the curtains were now a matter of public safety.

Seraphina sighed.

"Fine. Replace them."

Cedric bowed.

"Thank you, my lady."

"Do not thank me. Negotiate the price."

He laughed and left.

Seraphina returned to her tea.

The old chamber remained closed.

The modern circuits continued to operate.

The technical investigation had begun without making her responsible for it.

For the first time in several weeks, the kingdom's infrastructure seemed to be behaving like infrastructure.

Which meant, naturally, that she was beginning to suspect it would not last.
