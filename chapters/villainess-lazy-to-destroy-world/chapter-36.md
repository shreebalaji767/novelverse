# **Chapter 36 — The File That Had Been Transferred**

The phrase appeared three times.

*File transferred.*

Seraphina had found it in the original inspection register, written beneath the instruction that the final settlement should not be released until commissioning was complete.

The first occurrence was in the margin.

The second appeared in a later index.

The third was in a Treasury correspondence summary.

None of the three entries identified the destination.

It was an impressively consistent failure.

Seraphina sat at her desk, comparing the documents.

Mira placed breakfast beside her.

"My lady, you have been looking at those papers since yesterday."

"I know."

"Did you sleep?"

"Somewhat."

"Did you eat?"

Seraphina looked at the breakfast.

"I am about to."

Mira folded her arms.

"That is what you said an hour ago."

Seraphina glanced at the clock.

It was, unfortunately, true.

She put down her pen.

"Fine."

Mira pulled the chair away from the desk.

"Breakfast first."

Seraphina sat.

There were limits to how much resistance one could reasonably offer a maid who had been employed long enough to recognize every form of procrastination.

After breakfast, Seraphina returned to the inspection register.

The instruction signed by Darius Veylan was important.

It established that the engineer had not considered W-2 ready for final commissioning.

The settlement should have remained incomplete until corrective work and a final report were received.

Yet the Treasury had later approved a reduced payment.

The deduction concerned equipment valued at 320 crowns.

It was possible that the reduced payment reflected an agreed settlement.

It was also possible that someone had misunderstood the contract.

The missing file might explain which.

Seraphina wrote three questions in her notebook:

1. Who received the transferred file?
2. What did the file contain?
3. Was the transfer connected to the final settlement?

She underlined the third question.

It was the most important.

The first two could be answered by locating the document.

The third required understanding why it had been moved.

Roland arrived shortly before noon.

He carried a small bundle of correspondence.

"We have found a transfer register."

Seraphina looked up.

"From which office?"

"The former Regional Engineering Office."

"Does it identify the file?"

"Partially."

He placed the register on the desk.

The entry was dated twenty-one years earlier.

It referred to:

*W-2 technical completion papers, commissioning correspondence and related settlement documents.*

The destination was abbreviated:

*Central Project Review Office.*

Seraphina examined the entry.

"Was there such an office?"

"Yes. It was established during the reorganization of the regional engineering administration."

"How long did it operate?"

"Approximately eighteen months."

"And after that?"

"It was dissolved. Its responsibilities were divided between the Royal Infrastructure Department and the Treasury."

"Where did its records go?"

Roland placed another document on the desk.

"That is less clear."

Seraphina sighed.

Of course it was.

The Central Project Review Office had been established to review outstanding regional infrastructure projects.

Its responsibilities included examining incomplete contracts, disputed payments, delayed inspections and projects transferred between administrative authorities.

It had not been a permanent institution.

It had existed to resolve a backlog.

When the backlog was supposedly cleared, the office was dissolved.

Its staff were reassigned.

Its records were divided.

Some went to the Treasury.

Some went to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Others were returned to the regional authorities.

The transfer register indicated that the W-2 file had been sent to the Central Project Review Office.

There was no record of its return.

Seraphina read the organizational history.

"Who was responsible for the office?"

Roland checked the document.

"An administrator named Lord Pembroke."

"Was he responsible for technical inspections?"

"No. He supervised the administrative review."

"Then he would not have determined whether W-2 was operational."

"Correct."

"Who handled the technical papers?"

"The office had several reviewing officers. Their assignments are listed in a separate personnel register."

"Can we obtain it?"

"I have requested a copy."

Seraphina nodded.

"Good. We should also obtain the office's record-transfer schedule."

Roland looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because the file might have been divided again when the office was dissolved."

He understood.

"We may need to search more than one archive."

"Yes."

Roland looked at the papers.

"Do you ever wish these records had simply been kept together?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"Every day."

In Aurelion, Adrian Holt received the same discovery.

He was less pleased.

The committee's search had already involved the Academy, Treasury, Royal Logistics Department and several regional offices.

Now it would have to examine the records of an institution that had existed for less than two years.

He took the transfer register to Theodore.

The Crown Prince examined it.

"Central Project Review Office."

"Yes, Your Highness."

"Why was it established?"

"To resolve outstanding regional projects during the reorganization."

"Was W-2 considered outstanding?"

"The transfer register suggests so."

Theodore looked at the entry.

"Then why did the Treasury later process the settlement?"

"We don't yet know."

"Was the file transferred before or after the payment authorization?"

"Before."

Theodore looked up.

"Then the Treasury may have processed the payment without the technical papers."

Holt hesitated.

"That is possible. But we need to examine the payment procedure before drawing that conclusion."

Theodore nodded.

"Good. Find the office's transfer schedule and the names of the reviewing officers."

Holt gathered the papers.

As he reached the door, Theodore called him back.

"One more thing."

"Yes, Your Highness?"

"Do not describe the office as having cleared the backlog until we know what happened to the unresolved cases."

Holt paused.

"Understood."

Theodore returned to the register.

The old programme had not necessarily been abandoned because of one dramatic decision.

It might have been weakened by a series of ordinary administrative choices.

Each office had handled its assigned portion.

Each transfer had appeared reasonable.

And somewhere between them, responsibility had become difficult to identify.

The Central Project Review Office personnel register arrived four days later.

The reviewing officers were listed by department.

There were two engineers, three financial clerks, four administrative officers and a records supervisor.

One of the engineers had been assigned to W-2.

His name was **Darius Veylan**.

Seraphina examined the page.

"Was he transferred to the review office?"

Roland nodded.

"According to the register, he was temporarily assigned as a technical reviewer."

She looked at the date.

It was six weeks after the original commissioning instruction.

"Then he did not simply disappear from the programme."

"Apparently not."

"Does the register say what he reviewed?"

"No. It only records his assignment."

Seraphina leaned back.

The information changed the picture.

Darius Veylan had been responsible for the technical work at W-2.

He had requested that final settlement be withheld pending commissioning.

Then he had been assigned to the very office that received the file.

That did not establish that he had made the decision to transfer it.

Nor did it establish that he had approved the reduced settlement.

But it meant he might have known what happened to the missing documents.

Seraphina wrote a note to Holt.

*Please establish the dates and terms of Engineer Veylan's temporary assignment to the Central Project Review Office. We should also identify whether he participated in the technical review of W-2 or was assigned to other projects.*

The answer came from an unexpected source.

Lucien had located a technical review schedule in the Academy archive.

It listed Darius Veylan's assignments during his time at the Central Project Review Office.

W-2 was included.

So were two other projects.

The schedule showed that he had reviewed the technical status of the western station and submitted recommendations.

The actual report was missing.

However, a summary entry survived.

*W-2: Structural completion accepted. Equipment installation incomplete. Commissioning deferred pending corrective work.*

Seraphina read it.

The summary matched the earlier inspection register.

But there was another line.

*Further action assigned to regional maintenance authority.*

She looked up.

"Which authority?"

Lucien shook his head.

"Not specified."

"Was the corrective work completed?"

"No report has been found."

"Was the regional maintenance authority still operating?"

"At the time, yes."

Seraphina looked at the date.

The office had been dissolved the following year.

The responsibility had been transferred again.

She closed the report.

"Then we need the maintenance authority's work orders."

Lucien nodded.

"I suspected you would say that."

"Why?"

"Because every time we find a document, it sends us to another office."

Seraphina considered this.

"At least this one has a name."

The committee meeting was held the following week.

Holt presented the new findings.

Darius Veylan had been temporarily assigned to the Central Project Review Office.

He had reviewed W-2.

His surviving technical summary stated that commissioning remained deferred.

Further action had been assigned to the regional maintenance authority.

Minister Garrick examined the summary.

"Then the engineer knew the station was incomplete."

"Yes," Seraphina replied.

"And the review office knew."

"Yes."

"Then why was the settlement processed?"

Mr Bellamy opened the Treasury file.

"The payment authorization was issued after the review office had begun examining the project."

"Who approved it?"

"The authorization bears the signature of the Treasury's regional expenditure officer."

"Was that officer aware of the technical status?"

"We do not know."

Theodore turned toward Holt.

"Could the payment have been processed under the construction milestone rather than final commissioning?"

"Possibly."

Seraphina nodded.

"That is why we need the original payment schedule."

Garrick looked impatient.

"Surely the summary is sufficient to establish that the station was incomplete."

"It establishes that commissioning was incomplete," Seraphina said. "It does not establish whether the payment was improper."

Professor Vale supported her.

"The contract may have allowed payment for completed structural work while commissioning remained pending."

Lucien added, "But the final settlement should still have been withheld if the contract required commissioning."

Theodore looked at Bellamy.

"Find the original payment schedule."

Mr Bellamy agreed.

The next subject was the missing review report.

Holt explained that the Central Project Review Office's files had been divided when the institution closed.

The financial papers had been sent to the Treasury.

The technical papers had been sent to the Academy.

The correspondence had been returned to the Regional Infrastructure Department.

The records-transfer schedule contained a list of boxes.

The W-2 file appeared in Box 17.

Box 17 had been transferred to the Treasury.

However, the Treasury's archive inventory described it as:

*Regional maintenance correspondence, assorted.*

There was no specific reference to W-2.

Seraphina looked at Holt.

"Have you requested Box 17?"

"Yes."

"Has it been found?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"The archive inventory lists the box, but its current shelf location has not been confirmed."

Minister Garrick looked at him.

"How can an archive lose a box?"

Holt answered carefully.

"The archive did not necessarily lose it. The box may have been renumbered during a later transfer."

Garrick closed his eyes briefly.

Seraphina felt a faint sympathy for him.

Only faint.

After the meeting, Lucien approached Seraphina.

"I have been thinking about the old station."

"That sounds dangerous."

He ignored her.

"If the regulating assemblies were delivered but not installed, and commissioning was deferred, then the station may have remained structurally complete for years without becoming operational."

"Yes."

"That would explain why the later maps continued to show the building while the technical records described the circuit as inactive."

"Possibly."

"But there is another possibility."

Seraphina waited.

"The station may have been used for storage or maintenance even after the original circuit project was suspended."

She considered this.

"Do we have evidence?"

"Not yet. But Master Whitlock mentioned that the station had a maintenance room."

"Then we should check whether the building was used after the programme ended."

Lucien nodded.

"I can ask the Academy to examine the surviving regional maps."

"Please do."

He smiled.

"You know, you are becoming quite interested in this project."

Seraphina gave him a flat look.

"I am interested in knowing when I can stop reading about it."

Lucien laughed.

The first practical consequence of the inquiry arrived from Westmere.

Alden Mercer had sent a second letter.

He had remembered something after their meeting.

The maintenance room at W-2 had contained a large wooden cabinet.

The cabinet held equipment logs and work orders.

When Mercer left his assignment, he had been instructed to hand the keys to the incoming supervisor.

He remembered the man's name.

**Peter Alden.**

Seraphina paused.

Peter Alden.

She knew the name from the old grain records.

A Peter Hales had signed the receipt for a temporary grain depot.

But this was a different name.

Different spelling.

Different person, as far as the records showed.

She did not connect them.

She wrote down the name.

Then she asked Roland to search the regional maintenance personnel register.

The search produced a Peter Alden.

He had worked as a maintenance supervisor in the western district.

His employment ended nineteen years ago.

The personnel record included a final address.

A village near the western junction.

He was still listed as living there.

Seraphina looked at the date.

He had taken over the station keys shortly before the Central Project Review Office was dissolved.

He might have received the maintenance cabinet.

He might have been responsible for the work orders.

He might remember nothing.

But he was another person whose name could be checked.

Seraphina wrote to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

She recommended that the committee request a statement from Peter Alden.

The request should be limited to his assignment at W-2, his receipt of the station keys and the location of the maintenance records.

Theodore approved it.

The inquiry was sent.

Then, for several days, nothing happened.

Seraphina used the time to return to estate matters.

The winter transport budget required review.

A road repair contractor had submitted a revised estimate.

The northern grain stores needed their annual inspection.

Cedric wanted her opinion on a proposal to replace several old carts.

Seraphina found these tasks almost pleasant.

At least the carts existed.

At least the road had a location.

And when someone said a cart had been delivered, there was usually a cart.

Peter Alden's reply arrived on a rainy afternoon.

He confirmed that he had taken over maintenance responsibility for W-2 after Alden Mercer's transfer.

He also confirmed that he had received the keys to the maintenance room.

But he had never received the equipment logbook.

According to his recollection, the cabinet had been empty.

He had been told that the records were being held at the Central Project Review Office.

He had assumed they would be returned.

They never were.

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she looked at Roland.

"Does he remember who told him?"

"He names the regional office administrator."

"Is that person still alive?"

"Unknown."

"Can we identify the position?"

"Yes. The record lists an administrative officer named Henry Cole."

Seraphina looked at the letter again.

The equipment logbook had not been transferred to the incoming maintenance supervisor.

It had been held elsewhere.

The Central Project Review Office had received the W-2 file.

Darius Veylan had reviewed the technical status.

The office had assigned corrective work to the regional maintenance authority.

Then the office had been dissolved.

And the records had been divided.

Seraphina closed the letter.

The missing file was beginning to look less like one lost document and more like a collection of documents that had been separated at exactly the moment they were needed.

That evening, Cedric found her studying the personnel register.

"Another name?"

"Henry Cole."

"Who is he?"

"An administrative officer who handled the transfer of the W-2 maintenance records."

Cedric looked at the papers.

"Do you think he deliberately withheld the logbook?"

"I don't know."

"Could he have forgotten to return it?"

"Yes."

"Could the records have been sent to another office?"

"Yes."

"Could the cabinet have been empty because the logbook had already been removed?"

"Yes."

Cedric sighed.

"That is a very long list."

"Which is why we should find the transfer record before deciding what happened."

He sat opposite her.

"You have been at this for weeks."

"Yes."

"And the original programme ended twenty-two years ago."

"Yes."

"Do you think it is worth all this effort?"

Seraphina looked at the papers.

The old stations might never be restored.

The original programme might have been too expensive, too ambitious or too poorly managed to continue.

But people had made decisions based on its existence.

Money had been allocated.

Equipment had been purchased.

Engineers had been assigned.

Buildings had been constructed.

And the records had been left incomplete.

"I think the kingdom should know what it paid for," she said.

Cedric nodded.

"That is fair."

She pushed the papers aside.

"And I think someone should eventually explain why a maintenance cabinet can travel through three departments without anyone recording where it went."

Cedric laughed.

"That may be more difficult."

"Probably."

The next morning, the Royal Infrastructure Department sent another message.

Peter Alden had included a small detail in his reply.

When he received the station keys, he had also been given a verbal instruction.

The maintenance room was not to be used until the regional office had completed its final review.

The instruction had come from Henry Cole.

There was no written order.

No reason was recorded.

Seraphina read the message slowly.

The station's equipment had been delivered.

Some components had been installed.

The circuit had not been commissioned.

The maintenance records had been removed.

The incoming supervisor had been told not to use the maintenance room.

And the final review had never appeared in the surviving files.

She placed the message beside the earlier records.

Then she noticed a line in the personnel register.

Henry Cole had not retired.

He had been transferred.

His destination was listed as:

**Central Project Review Office — Records Administration.**

Seraphina sat very still.

Henry Cole had handled the transfer of the maintenance records.

He had later worked in the office that received the W-2 file.

And when the office was dissolved, its records had been divided again.

He was not merely another name in the archive.

He might have been one of the few people who understood where the missing documents had gone.

Seraphina took a fresh sheet of paper.

This time, she did not write a list of possible explanations.

She wrote a single request.

*Please establish the current whereabouts of former Records Administrator Henry Cole and determine whether he retained responsibility for the W-2 file during the dissolution of the Central Project Review Office.*

She signed it.

Then she placed it in the outgoing correspondence tray.

For once, she had a name that connected the beginning of the transfer to its final destination.

Whether that connection would lead to an answer remained to be seen.

But the old file had not vanished by itself.

Someone had been responsible for moving it.

And Seraphina was beginning to understand that the most important person in the investigation might not have been the engineer who designed the station.

It might have been the clerk who decided where the papers went.

**End of Chapter 36**
