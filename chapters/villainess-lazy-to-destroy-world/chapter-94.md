# **Chapter 94 — The Three Containers Were Not Where the Inventory Said**

At seven in the morning, the Royal Academy received a request for permission to enter a building that had been closed for six years.

At seven twelve, the request was returned.

Not rejected.

Returned.

The difference mattered to the clerk who had prepared it, though nobody else in the room seemed particularly interested.

The Eastern Materials Laboratory had been closed under a formal administrative order. Its remaining equipment had supposedly been transferred to the Academy's secondary stores. The building itself had been placed under the authority of the Royal Infrastructure Department because its eastern wing shared a service corridor with an older underground distribution passage.

The request had been addressed to the Academy.

The building belonged to the Department.

The materials had supposedly been transferred to the Academy.

The corridor belonged to neither office in the sense that mattered.

The clerk had therefore sent the request to the wrong authority.

At seven thirty, Lucien Arkwright read the returned paper.

At seven thirty-one, he put it down.

At seven thirty-two, he asked for the person who had prepared it.

At seven thirty-three, he was informed that the person was currently attempting to determine which office was responsible for the eastern wing.

Lucien closed his eyes.

"Of course."

Seraphina learned about the problem during breakfast.

She had returned to the Veyr residence shortly before two in the morning and had been asleep for approximately four hours when Mira brought in the morning correspondence.

There were three letters from the estate, one from Duchess Evelyne, a short note from Cedric, and an official request bearing the Royal Infrastructure Department's seal.

Seraphina looked at the request.

Then she looked at Mira.

"Why is the kingdom writing to me before breakfast?"

"Perhaps it has learned your schedule."

"Then it has learned incorrectly."

Mira placed the tray on the table.

"The message says the Crown Prince requests your assistance in reviewing the old laboratory's transfer records."

Seraphina unfolded the letter.

Theodore's handwriting was neat, direct and entirely too legible for someone who had probably not slept much more than she had.

The letter explained that the Academy had located an old transfer index linking the three containers of C-4 stabilizing compound to the Eastern Materials Laboratory. The laboratory's closure inventory referred to them as technical samples, but no final reconciliation had been found.

Theodore requested Seraphina's help distinguishing the original procurement records, the laboratory's sample records and the later storage transfer.

He also emphasized that she was not being asked to supervise the technical investigation.

She read that sentence twice.

"At least someone is learning."

Mira poured tea.

"Will you go?"

"After breakfast."

"Master Arkwright may be waiting."

"Then he will have an opportunity to appreciate patience."

Mira placed a plate beside her.

"Would you like another piece of bread?"

Seraphina looked at the bread.

"Yes."

The kingdom could wait another five minutes.

The Academy's secondary stores were located in a long stone building behind the eastern lecture halls.

Unlike the central stores, which handled ordinary equipment and frequently used materials, the secondary stores held items that had been retained for reference, evaluation or future inspection.

It was not a particularly exciting place.

There were shelves of old instruments, sealed containers, obsolete measuring devices, damaged brackets, pieces of conduit and several boxes whose labels had become more informative than their contents.

A storekeeper named Edwin Clarke met Seraphina and Lucien near the entrance.

"I have located the closure inventory," he said.

"Good," Lucien replied. "And the three containers?"

"Not yet."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then what have you located?"

"The inventory."

"Ah."

Edwin seemed unsure whether she was making a joke.

She was not entirely sure herself.

He led them to a small office containing several cabinets and a table.

The closure inventory was a thick ledger, its cover marked:

**EASTERN MATERIALS LABORATORY — FINAL TRANSFER, SIX YEARS PRIOR**

Lucien opened it.

The first section listed instruments and testing equipment.

The second listed laboratory furniture.

The third contained consumable materials.

The C-4 compound appeared on page forty-two.

Three containers.

Batch reference C4-18.

Status: retained for technical reference.

Destination: Academy Secondary Stores.

Receiving authority: pending confirmation.

Seraphina leaned closer.

"Why does it say pending confirmation?"

Edwin adjusted his spectacles.

"Because the receiving office had not signed the final page when this inventory was prepared."

"Did it sign later?"

"I haven't found a later copy."

Lucien turned the page.

There was an attachment.

A handwritten note explained that the containers had been placed in a temporary holding area while the Academy determined whether they could be retained for future comparison.

The note was dated six years earlier.

"Where was the temporary holding area?" Lucien asked.

Edwin opened another register.

"Room E-12."

"Is that here?"

"No. That was part of the laboratory."

Seraphina sat down.

"Then the inventory does not confirm that the containers arrived here."

"No, my lady."

"Does it confirm that they left the laboratory?"

Edwin considered the question.

"The closure team recorded them as packed."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

"Does it confirm who received them?"

"No."

Lucien looked increasingly dissatisfied.

"Can we inspect the old laboratory?"

"That requires departmental authorization."

Seraphina closed the ledger.

"Then the first task is to establish whether the containers were ever transferred. We should not begin by searching a building for something that might not have left it."

Lucien looked at her.

"I agree."

Edwin nodded.

"I can prepare the original packing list."

"Please do," Seraphina said. "And look for the dispatch record, not another copy of this inventory."

The Crown Prince joined them shortly before ten.

He had already spoken to the Infrastructure Department and obtained permission for a limited inspection of the laboratory's former storage room.

The authorization was narrow.

No excavation.

No opening of sealed underground passages.

No movement of equipment without an Academy representative.

No removal of materials until their identity had been confirmed.

Seraphina read the conditions.

"That is reasonable."

Theodore glanced at her.

"You sound disappointed."

"I expected at least one person to request permission to dismantle a wall."

Lucien ignored them.

"Has the building been inspected recently?"

"Only externally," Theodore replied. "The eastern wing is structurally stable. The interior rooms were closed after the transfer."

"Then we will need a building survey before entering."

Theodore nodded.

"Already arranged."

Seraphina folded the authorization.

"Who is responsible for the building?"

"The Royal Infrastructure Department."

"And the materials?"

"The Academy."

"And the service corridor?"

"Departmental maintenance, with Academy technical access."

She looked at him.

"That arrangement must be very pleasant for everyone involved."

"It has been described as efficient."

"By whom?"

Theodore did not answer.

Lucien began gathering the registers.

They departed.

The Eastern Materials Laboratory stood behind a low iron fence.

Its eastern wing was built from pale stone, with narrow windows and a roof that had been repaired recently. The main entrance was secured with a new lock. The old laboratory sign remained above the doorway, though the lettering had faded.

A departmental surveyor named Master Colin Reeve waited beside the gate.

He inspected the authorization, confirmed their identities and explained the building's condition.

"The western rooms were cleared six years ago. The eastern storage section was sealed after the final inventory. The lower service corridor remains closed."

"Why?" Lucien asked.

"It was not part of the laboratory's retained facilities. The corridor belongs to an older infrastructure structure."

"Does it connect to the capital network?"

"That has not been established."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then we will not establish it today."

Reeve nodded.

The group entered through the main door.

The laboratory smelled of dust, stone and old timber.

Most of the equipment had been removed. Empty worktables stood against the walls. Several shelves remained, their contents marked with numbered tags.

The storage room was at the far end of the eastern wing.

A second door stood between it and the main corridor.

Reeve examined the lock.

"Seal intact."

Lucien checked the closure record.

"Open it."

The door was unlocked under supervision.

Inside, the room contained six wooden storage frames, a small metal cabinet and several empty crates.

There were no catalyst containers.

Edwin Clarke, who had accompanied them with the records, checked the room against the inventory.

"Nothing here."

Seraphina examined the storage frames.

"Are these numbered?"

"Yes."

"Do the numbers match the closure inventory?"

Edwin checked.

"Four do. Two were removed before the closure."

"Where?"

He turned to the register.

"Listed as transferred to the Academy's instrument workshop."

"Are they relevant?"

"No. They held testing equipment."

Lucien examined the cabinet.

It was empty.

"Where would the technical samples have been kept?"

Edwin pointed to the second frame.

"Here, according to the room plan."

The frame had three narrow compartments.

Each was labelled with an old material reference.

The middle compartment bore the marking C4-18.

It was empty.

Lucien crouched to inspect it.

"The containers were stored here."

Seraphina looked at the floor.

There were no obvious marks of recent movement.

The wooden frame was dusty, but the compartment had a cleaner rectangular outline where something had once rested.

"Does the closure inventory list the containers as sealed?"

Edwin checked.

"Yes."

"Did anyone record their condition?"

"Not in this book."

"Then we need the packing list."

Lucien stood.

"Agreed."

The absence of the containers was not evidence of theft.

It was evidence that the old laboratory's final inventory had recorded materials that were no longer in the room.

The question was where they had gone.

The packing list arrived shortly after noon.

It was kept in a separate records cabinet at the Infrastructure Department.

The document was brief.

Three containers of C-4 compound.

Batch C4-18.

Packed by laboratory assistant Hester Vale.

Witnessed by stores clerk Robert Fenwick.

Transfer destination: Academy Secondary Stores, temporary technical reference.

Vehicle: Departmental transport cart 6.

Dispatch date: four days before the laboratory's closure.

There was also a receiving signature.

It belonged to an Academy storekeeper named Thomas Baird.

Lucien read the document.

"That confirms receipt."

"Does it?" Seraphina asked.

He looked at her.

"The receiving signature is present."

"Yes. But what did he receive?"

"Three containers."

"According to the packing list."

Lucien read the line again.

Seraphina pointed to the final column.

"Look at the description."

He leaned closer.

The packing list described the material as:

**Three sealed reference containers — C4-18.**

The receiving record described:

**Three sealed reference containers — technical samples.**

The batch number was absent from the receiving record.

It was possible they were the same containers.

It was also possible that the receiving office had accepted three different containers under the same broad description.

"Could the receiving clerk have copied the description from the dispatch sheet?" Theodore asked.

"Certainly," Seraphina replied. "But we should not treat a copied description as independent confirmation."

Lucien looked at the signature.

"Thomas Baird worked in the secondary stores."

"Then find his stock register."

Edwin nodded.

"I know where it is."

Thomas Baird's stock register was not difficult to locate.

It was difficult to understand.

The former storekeeper had recorded incoming materials by date and temporary shelf location. Items retained for technical reference were grouped separately from ordinary supplies. Some entries had later annotations in different handwriting.

The three containers appeared under a general category:

**Stabilizing Compound — Reference Samples**

Quantity: three.

Location: Temporary Shelf E.

No batch number.

No issue record.

No disposal record.

No transfer-out entry.

Seraphina read the page.

"So the storekeeper recorded three containers."

"Yes," Edwin said.

"But not the batch."

"Correct."

"And the temporary shelf?"

"That was an intermediate location."

"Where did the materials go afterward?"

Edwin searched the next pages.

There was an entry dated eleven days later.

**Reference samples relocated during secondary stores reorganization.**

Destination: Cabinet R-6.

The entry had been initialled.

There was no separate movement form.

Lucien frowned.

"Was Cabinet R-6 here?"

Edwin checked the old storage plan.

"No. It was in the Academy's former materials annex."

"Where is that now?"

"The annex was converted into a teaching laboratory."

Theodore looked at him.

"Are the cabinets still there?"

Edwin hesitated.

"Some are."

Seraphina looked at the page.

"Then we have a location."

"We have a cabinet number," Lucien said.

"That is more than we had this morning."

The former materials annex had been converted into a teaching laboratory nine months after the closure.

Its cabinets had been retained, though their contents were reorganized.

The current laboratory supervisor, Mistress Elara Finch, was understandably surprised when a prince, a royal magician, a records clerk and a noblewoman arrived asking about a cabinet that had been out of use for six years.

She listened to the explanation.

Then she said, "Cabinet R-6 was removed."

Lucien stared at her.

"When?"

"Three years ago."

"Where was it sent?"

"The Academy's general equipment workshop."

"Why?"

"It was damaged."

"Was its contents removed?"

"Of course."

"Where were they sent?"

Mistress Finch paused.

"I would have to check."

Seraphina sat down.

"That would be helpful."

The laboratory supervisor retrieved the relocation register.

Cabinet R-6 had been dismantled because one of its rear supports had cracked.

Its contents had been removed and placed in temporary storage.

The register listed several categories:

- obsolete glassware;
- calibration weights;
- reference samples;
- damaged testing instruments;
- unidentified containers.

The reference samples were assigned to Shelf 3 of the general equipment workshop.

The unidentified containers were assigned to a separate holding area.

There was no batch number.

Lucien read the entries.

"Why were reference samples and unidentified containers separated?"

"Different handling requirements," Mistress Finch replied.

"Did the reference samples include catalyst materials?"

"Possibly."

"Can we inspect Shelf 3?"

"Not without the workshop supervisor."

Theodore turned to the coordinator accompanying them.

"Contact the workshop."

The general equipment workshop was still operating.

Its supervisor, Master Oren Bell, received the group with the strained expression of a man who had been informed that a prince was coming to inspect a shelf.

He checked the relocation register.

Then he checked the workshop inventory.

Then he sent an assistant to the storage room.

The assistant returned carrying a small wooden box.

It was not one of the containers.

It held glass sample vials, two calibration weights and a metal instrument case.

"These were on Shelf 3," the assistant said.

Bell checked the label.

"Reference samples."

Lucien examined the box.

"No C-4 containers."

The assistant shook his head.

"Not here."

Seraphina looked at the relocation register.

"Where were the unidentified containers?"

Bell checked the second entry.

"Temporary holding area, east workshop."

"Is that still in use?"

"No. It was cleared last year."

"Where were its contents sent?"

Bell looked through the records.

The answer was not immediate.

It took another twenty minutes and two calls to the Academy's equipment office.

The unidentified containers had been transferred to the central technical disposal room.

Not disposed of.

Transferred for classification.

The three containers might have been among them.

The workshop's records did not say.

Lucien leaned against the table.

"We have followed these containers through four locations."

"Five," Seraphina said.

He looked at her.

"The laboratory, temporary shelf, Cabinet R-6, Shelf 3, and the holding area."

"That is five."

"Thank you."

"You're welcome."

Theodore examined the latest register.

"Can the disposal room be inspected?"

Bell nodded.

"With the equipment office's permission."

The prince turned to his coordinator.

"Obtain it."

The central technical disposal room was not a disposal room in the ordinary sense.

It was a classification facility.

Materials that were no longer needed but could not yet be destroyed were placed there for identification, safety assessment and formal disposition.

The room contained old metal components, damaged measuring instruments, sealed containers, laboratory samples and several items whose labels were no longer readable.

The three containers were not immediately visible.

A technician checked the room's inventory.

Then he checked a supplementary register.

Then he called for the person responsible for chemical and catalyst materials.

The person arrived carrying a narrow ledger.

"Three sealed containers of C-4 compound?" she asked.

Lucien nodded.

"Batch C4-18."

She searched the register.

"Three containers were received from the former equipment workshop."

Seraphina looked at the entry.

"Does it identify the batch?"

"No. The transfer was recorded under the category of unidentified stabilizing materials."

"Are they still here?"

The technician turned several pages.

"Two were sent for testing."

"And the third?"

"Retained."

"Where?"

"Cabinet D-11."

Lucien looked up.

"Can we inspect it?"

"Yes. But the containers will need to be identified before they are opened."

The technician led them to the cabinet.

It was secured with a numbered seal.

The seal number matched the current inventory.

The cabinet was opened under supervision.

Inside were three containers.

Two were small and sealed with dark wax.

The third was slightly larger, with a faded label.

Lucien approached.

The label read:

**C4 — REFERENCE MATERIAL**

There was no batch number.

Seraphina looked at the inventory sheet.

"How many containers were recorded here?"

"Three."

"How many are present?"

The technician counted.

"Three."

"Good."

Lucien examined the containers without touching them.

"These may be the original samples."

"May be?" Theodore asked.

"The description is consistent. But we need the original batch markings or a laboratory comparison."

The technician nodded.

"We can arrange that."

Seraphina looked at the cabinet.

"So the containers were not necessarily lost."

"No," Lucien replied.

"But the records did not establish where they were."

"Correct."

"That is a different problem."

He looked at her.

"Yes."

Theodore turned to the technician.

"Keep the containers sealed. Record their present condition. No material is to be used until the Academy confirms their identity."

The technician bowed.

For the first time that day, the group had found something that could plausibly be the missing material.

It was not yet proof.

It was a location.

The Academy's technical team began the identification process that afternoon.

The first comparison was physical.

The containers' seals, markings and dimensions were compared with the original laboratory packing list.

Two matched the expected dimensions.

The third had a different outer casing, but the internal container specification was consistent with the old laboratory record.

The labels were incomplete.

The material itself remained sealed.

A preliminary examination suggested that the contents had been stored properly.

No one declared the investigation complete.

At five in the afternoon, Lucien received the first report.

"Two containers are consistent with the original batch," he told Theodore. "The third is likely from the same evaluation group, but the identification is not conclusive."

"Can the material be tested?"

"Yes, but we need to determine whether a sample can be removed without compromising the containers."

"How long?"

"At least a day for the preliminary procedure."

Theodore nodded.

"Then take the time."

Lucien glanced at the report.

"There is another issue."

"Which?"

"The two containers sent for testing were not returned to the cabinet."

"Where are they?"

"One was consumed during a laboratory comparison. The other was transferred to a separate research collection."

"Is that transfer recorded?"

"Yes."

"Then find it."

Lucien looked at him.

"I intend to."

Seraphina, who had been reviewing the stores comparison, set down her pen.

"How much of the original forty-eight containers remains?"

Lucien looked at the old procurement figures.

"Fourteen were used during scheduled maintenance. Three were transferred for technical evaluation. The rest should have remained in central stores."

"Should have?"

"The central stores inventory currently lists thirty-one."

"That accounts for forty-five."

"Yes."

"Three more were transferred."

"Yes."

"Then the original forty-eight are accounted for in the broadest possible sense."

Seraphina considered the figures.

"Not necessarily by location or condition."

"No."

"But the arithmetic is not evidence of a missing supply."

Lucien nodded.

"Agreed."

Theodore looked at the report.

"Then the material investigation will continue. But tonight's disturbance cannot be attributed to those containers."

"Not yet," Lucien said.

The capital's protective network remained under reduced demand for the rest of the day.

The southern zone showed no further interruptions.

The western zone recorded two minor variations, both corrected by the reserve.

The central assembly continued to respond slowly.

The northern zone remained stable.

The Academy's preliminary technical report stated that the network was functioning within its approved limits, but its operating margin was narrower than expected during sustained evening demand.

The report recommended continued monitoring and an expedited inspection of the central, western and southern replacement components.

It did not recommend shutting down the network.

It did not recommend reducing the reserve.

It did not identify a confirmed cause.

The King approved the recommendations.

Theodore ordered the city watch to maintain the additional lanterns and asked the hospitals to report any further interruptions immediately.

Liora sent word that the southern infirmary had returned to normal operation, although the staff intended to keep their reserve lamps ready.

Seraphina received the message while reviewing the final stores report.

She read it, placed it beside the others and rubbed her forehead.

The day's work had established that the three containers of C-4 compound had passed through several locations.

Two were likely part of the original batch.

A third remained under identification.

The other containers had been accounted for through maintenance records and existing inventory.

There was no proof that catalyst had been stolen.

There was no proof that the missing containers had been installed in the capital network.

There was, however, a serious weakness in the kingdom's ability to trace technical materials after they left their original stores.

That weakness had existed long before the current emergency.

Seraphina wrote a note at the bottom of her report.

**Transfer recorded does not equal final receipt. Final receipt does not equal continued possession.**

She paused.

Then she added:

**Require separate confirmation at each stage.**

It was not a technical solution.

It was not even an especially clever one.

It was simply the sort of distinction that should have been made years ago.

At seven in the evening, another report arrived from the Academy.

The central assembly's response time had increased again.

Only slightly.

The reading remained within the approved range, and the reserve was compensating.

But the increase had occurred despite the reduced demand schedule.

Lucien read the report in the monitoring room.

"That is not good."

Fenwick stood beside him.

"No."

"Could the residue be causing the delay?"

"It may be contributing."

"And the replacement lining?"

"Possibly."

"What about the western and southern components?"

"We have not completed their inspections."

Lucien stared at the board.

Seven columns.

Seven sets of measurements.

The central assembly's line had begun to drift away from its earlier operating pattern.

The western and southern zones continued to show intermittent variations.

The reserve remained active, but the latest readings suggested it was working harder than the previous evening.

Fenwick turned to the technician.

"Continue the observation. No adjustment."

"Yes, Master."

"Notify the prince if the central response exceeds the approved threshold."

The technician nodded.

Lucien looked toward the closed laboratory reports stacked on the table.

"We have found the containers."

"Some of them."

"We have found the records."

"Some of them."

"We have identified the maintenance gaps."

"Some of them."

Lucien gave him a weary look.

"Must you qualify everything?"

Fenwick did not look away from the board.

"Yes."

A bell sounded from the eastern observation station.

The technician checked the indicator.

"Western zone variation."

Lucien turned.

"Reserve response?"

"Active."

"Central assembly?"

"Delayed."

"How delayed?"

The technician checked the secondary instrument.

His expression changed.

"Beyond the previous evening's reading."

Fenwick stepped closer.

"Confirm it."

The second technician checked the measurement.

Then nodded.

The figure was correct.

The central assembly had responded.

But it had taken longer.

Lucien looked at the reserve column.

Its contribution was rising.

Not dramatically.

Not yet.

But steadily.

And for the first time, the technicians watching the board could no longer describe the increasing demand as the only reason the system was struggling.

The network was still functioning.

The capital was still protected.

But something inside the system was changing faster than the people responsible for maintaining it could explain.

At the bottom of the observation sheet, the technician recorded the time.

**19:46 — Central response delay increased. Reserve compensation active. Further assessment required.**

No one in the room crossed out the entry.

No one called it routine.

And nobody went home.
