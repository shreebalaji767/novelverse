# **Chapter 34 — The Amount That Did Not Belong to Anyone**

Seraphina had developed a particular dislike for financial discrepancies.

Not because they were difficult to understand.

Most were perfectly understandable once someone bothered to explain the circumstances.

She disliked them because they attracted people who were eager to explain them before checking the records.

The latest discrepancy concerned Veylan Works.

The company had claimed an outstanding settlement twenty-one years earlier. The Treasury had located a payment authorization, but its amount did not match the figure in the company's correspondence.

Seraphina had received the relevant documents that morning.

There were four sheets.

The first was a contractor's letter.

The second was a payment authorization.

The third was an account summary.

The fourth was a handwritten calculation attached to the summary.

She read them in silence.

Then she read them again.

"Mira."

"Yes, my lady?"

"Do you know where Cedric is?"

"In the west office, reviewing the estate transport accounts."

"Please ask him to come here."

Mira glanced at the documents.

"Is something wrong?"

"Something is inconsistent."

"That sounds serious."

"It may be."

Mira left.

Seraphina returned to the papers.

The contractor's letter requested payment of 1,840 silver crowns.

The Treasury authorization approved 1,520 crowns.

The account summary showed that 1,520 crowns had been entered as paid.

The handwritten calculation contained a deduction of 320 crowns.

The difference was exact.

That was the first reassuring thing.

The second was that the deduction had a written explanation.

The third was that the explanation referred to a separate delivery.

The problem was that the separate delivery had no surviving receipt.

Seraphina leaned back.

There it was.

Not a missing payment.

Not necessarily an unpaid debt.

A deduction whose supporting document could not be found.

She disliked the situation already.

Cedric arrived with his sleeves rolled up.

"You sent for me?"

"Yes. Please sit."

He looked at the documents.

"Veylan Works?"

"Yes."

"Have they found the original contract?"

"Not yet. But we have the payment records."

Seraphina handed him the sheets.

Cedric examined them.

"The company requested 1,840 crowns."

"Correct."

"The Treasury approved 1,520."

"Correct."

"And the account shows 1,520 paid."

"Correct."

He looked at the handwritten calculation.

"Three hundred and twenty deducted."

"Apparently."

"Why?"

"According to this note, the company had failed to return equipment valued at 320 crowns."

Cedric looked up.

"That sounds reasonable."

"It might be."

"Then what is the problem?"

"There is no equipment receipt."

Cedric examined the page again.

"Perhaps the equipment was never returned."

"Perhaps."

"Or perhaps the deduction was recorded incorrectly."

"Perhaps."

He looked at her.

"You are being unusually careful with your answers."

"I would prefer not to accuse a dead company of withholding equipment based on a note written by someone whose name we cannot read."

Cedric gave a short laugh.

"That is fair."

Seraphina pointed to the calculation.

"Look at the date."

Cedric checked.

"It was written six days after the payment authorization."

"Which means it may have been added during settlement."

"Or during reconciliation."

"Yes."

Cedric placed the documents together.

"We need the original settlement file."

"Exactly."

The Royal Infrastructure Department's request for the original settlement file had already been submitted.

Unfortunately, the file was not held in the Treasury's main archive.

It had been transferred to the former Regional Engineering Office.

That office had been dissolved twenty-two years earlier.

Its records had been divided among three institutions.

The technical documents had gone to the Royal Academy.

The financial records had gone to the Treasury.

The remaining correspondence had been transferred to the Regional Stores Authority.

Nobody had recorded the full division of the archive.

Adrian Holt's letter described the situation with remarkable restraint.

*The original settlement file has not yet been located. Several associated records may exist in separate collections. A coordinated search has been requested.*

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she folded it.

"At least he has stopped pretending the records are all in one place."

Mira was arranging the correspondence.

"Was he pretending?"

"Not intentionally."

"Then why?"

"Because people tend to believe that an archive is a room containing every document in the correct order."

Mira considered this.

"Is it not?"

Seraphina looked at her.

"No."

Mira appeared disappointed.

"That seems inconvenient."

"It is."

The committee met four days later.

Theodore was already seated when Seraphina entered.

Minister Garrick stood beside the window, speaking quietly with Mr Bellamy.

Lucien had brought several Academy registers.

Professor Vale was examining a map.

Adrian Holt was arranging the latest correspondence.

Cedric sat beside Seraphina.

Theodore began the meeting.

"We have received the first response from the Treasury regarding the Veylan settlement."

Mr Bellamy opened a folder.

"The authorization for 1,520 crowns was approved by the Regional Engineering Office. The amount was subsequently entered into the Treasury's consolidated expenditure account."

"Who authorized the deduction?" Seraphina asked.

Mr Bellamy checked the paper.

"The surviving calculation does not contain a full name."

"An office?"

"Regional equipment reconciliation."

"Does that office appear in the organizational register?"

"It does."

"Was it responsible for inspecting returned equipment?"

"Yes."

"Could it deduct the value of equipment that had not been returned?"

"Under the contract terms, yes."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the deduction was within the office's authority, provided the equipment was actually missing and properly valued."

"That is correct."

"Do we have an inventory?"

Mr Bellamy shook his head.

"Not yet."

Theodore looked at Holt.

"Can we locate the equipment reconciliation register?"

"We have requested it from the Regional Stores Authority."

"How long?"

"They have not provided an estimate."

Minister Garrick frowned.

"Why not?"

Holt answered carefully.

"The authority's older records are stored separately from its current inventory. They are being reviewed before access is granted."

Garrick looked dissatisfied.

Seraphina understood the expression.

It was the expression of a man who had spent years believing that issuing an instruction was the same as completing a task.

She said nothing.

Mr Bellamy placed the handwritten calculation on the table.

"The deduction may have been applied because the contractor failed to return a set of regulating components."

Lucien leaned forward.

"What kind of components?"

"That is not specified."

"Could they have been used at W-2?"

"Possibly."

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Do we know whether those components were included in the station's original equipment list?"

"No. The Academy has not found the complete list."

"Then we should not assume they were."

Lucien nodded.

Professor Vale pointed to the map.

"Could the missing equipment have been intended for another station?"

"Possibly," Seraphina replied.

"Or it may have been ordinary workshop equipment unrelated to the relay system."

"Also possible."

Theodore looked around the table.

"Then we have three questions. What equipment was deducted? Where was it supposed to be? And what happened to it?"

Holt wrote them down.

Seraphina added a fourth.

"Who verified the deduction?"

Mr Bellamy looked at her.

"The equipment reconciliation office."

"Which person?"

"The record does not say."

"Then that is also unresolved."

Minister Garrick sighed.

"How many questions can one payment create?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"Apparently four."

Cedric lowered his head.

His shoulders moved once.

Seraphina ignored him.

The meeting continued with the other facilities.

The northern station's land ownership had been confirmed.

The western station's location was established, but its ownership remained unclear.

The eastern station existed in an old catalogue, but no physical structure had been identified.

The southern storehouse reference had been traced to a regional circuit supply depot, though its relationship to the original programme was still uncertain.

The Ashenford station remained unverified.

The sixth facility appeared only in the financial allocation summary.

The committee was making progress.

Slow progress.

But progress nonetheless.

Then Lucien placed a separate document on the table.

"The Academy has located a technical note concerning W-2."

Seraphina looked at him.

"An inspection report?"

"No. A preliminary assessment."

"What does it say?"

Lucien unfolded the paper.

"It records irregular mana distribution during the initial testing phase."

Professor Vale looked up.

"How irregular?"

"Not enough to classify the circuit as unsafe. But the report recommended a further calibration."

"Was the calibration completed?"

Lucien shook his head.

"There is no surviving completion report."

Seraphina examined the date.

It was twenty-one years old.

The same year the contractor suspended operations.

"Does it mention Veylan Works?"

Lucien checked.

"No."

"Does it identify the equipment?"

"Only by category."

"Then it should remain a separate technical reference until we can establish the connection."

Lucien nodded.

Theodore looked toward Seraphina.

"You believe these records may be related?"

"I believe they concern the same period and the same station. That makes them worth comparing. It does not make them proof of one another."

Theodore accepted the distinction.

"Continue the comparison."

After the meeting, Seraphina and Cedric remained behind.

Holt was collecting the documents.

"Lady Seraphina," he said, "may I ask whether you would review the Treasury's settlement summary when it arrives?"

"Yes, provided it is limited to the historical records."

"Of course."

"And I would prefer the original calculation sheets, not just a summary."

Holt nodded.

"I will request them."

Seraphina watched him place the papers into a folder.

"Mr Holt?"

"Yes?"

"Please make sure the unresolved entries are not described as confirmed losses."

He looked puzzled.

"Why?"

"Because a missing receipt is not proof that equipment disappeared. A missing inventory is not proof that equipment was stolen. A payment deduction is not proof that the deduction was correct."

Holt considered this.

"Understood."

Cedric waited until Holt had left.

"You have said that several times."

"Because several people keep forgetting it."

"Do you think the committee will eventually find the truth?"

Seraphina looked at the papers.

"I think it will find more documents."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

She gathered her notes.

"Sometimes it is enough to discover which questions can actually be answered."

The following morning, a letter arrived from the Royal Logistics Department.

The old receiving registers had been located.

They were stored in a warehouse outside the capital.

The records covered twenty-seven years.

The department could not confirm whether the Veylan equipment transfer was included.

However, one register contained a reference to a group of regulating components received from the former Regional Engineering Office.

The entry was dated twenty-one years earlier.

The quantity was not recorded.

The recipient's name was illegible.

The reference number was partially damaged.

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she read it again.

"Roland."

"Yes, my lady?"

"Can you arrange a copy of the register?"

"I can request it."

"Please do."

He looked at the letter.

"Is this the equipment?"

"We do not know."

"But it could be."

"Yes."

Roland smiled.

"That is something."

"It is a reference."

"Still."

Seraphina allowed herself a small smile.

"Yes. Still."

The copy arrived two days later.

The original register had been damaged by moisture.

Several lines were unreadable.

The entry concerning the regulating components was partly visible.

The description read:

*Regulating assemblies, assorted.*

The source was listed as:

*Former Regional Engineering Office.*

The receiving location was:

*Central Stores Annex.*

The date matched the company's transfer correspondence.

But there was no contractor name.

No quantity.

No signature that could be read.

And no indication of which station the components were intended for.

Seraphina placed the copy beside the company's letter.

The company had asked permission to transfer equipment to the regional stores authority.

The receiving register showed that regulating assemblies had entered central stores on the same date.

It was a promising connection.

It was not yet a confirmed one.

She wrote to Holt.

*Please compare the receiving entry with the Veylan Works correspondence. The matching date and general equipment description warrant further examination, but the source and destination should remain unconfirmed until the original transfer authorization is located.*

She sent the letter.

Then she turned to the remaining papers.

That evening, the household received a visitor.

The visitor was a middle-aged man carrying a leather case.

He introduced himself as Mr Thomas Weller, a retired clerk from the former Regional Engineering Office.

Seraphina recognized the name.

He had previously assisted with the old grain records.

She received him in the smaller sitting room.

"Mr Weller. What brings you here?"

He placed the case beside his chair.

"I heard that the Royal Infrastructure Department was looking into the old western programme."

"That is correct."

"I worked in the regional office during its final years."

Seraphina sat forward.

"Did you handle the Veylan Works settlement?"

"Not directly. But I remember the file."

"Do you remember why the company suspended operations?"

Weller considered the question.

"I remember a disagreement."

"About payment?"

"Partly."

"Equipment?"

"Yes."

"Can you explain?"

He opened his case and removed a small notebook.

"The company had delivered several components. Some were accepted. Others were left in storage because the station had not completed its calibration."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Which station?"

"W-2, I believe."

"Are you certain?"

"No. I remember the western project, but I cannot guarantee that every component belonged to the relay station."

She nodded.

"What happened to the equipment?"

"Some was transferred to stores. Some remained at the site."

"Was the transfer recorded?"

"I believe so."

"Do you remember who handled it?"

Weller looked at his notebook.

"An assistant clerk named Robert Fenwick."

Seraphina wrote down the name.

"Was he responsible for the equipment inspection?"

"No. He prepared the transfer papers. The technical inspection was handled by the engineering office."

"Who conducted it?"

Weller hesitated.

"I cannot remember."

Seraphina waited.

"Was it Darius Veylan?"

"I do not know."

"Was Darius Veylan employed by Veylan Works?"

"I believe he was. But I did not deal with him personally."

The answers were limited.

Still, they were useful.

"Do you have any documents?"

Weller tapped the leather case.

"I kept a few copies of correspondence when the office was closing. I thought they might be useful someday."

Seraphina looked at the case.

"May I see them?"

"Certainly."

The documents were not extensive.

There were two letters, a transfer slip and a list of equipment categories.

The first letter was from the regional office to Veylan Works.

It stated that the company could not retrieve certain components until the technical inspection had been completed.

The second letter was from Veylan Works.

It requested confirmation that the equipment would be transferred to central stores.

The transfer slip was dated six days later.

Its reference number matched the damaged entry in the Royal Logistics register.

Seraphina examined it carefully.

The document listed:

- Three regulating assemblies.
- Two mounting frames.
- A collection of workshop tools.
- Several unclassified metal components.

The receiving signature was clear.

Robert Fenwick.

Seraphina looked up.

"Mr Weller, is this the same Robert Fenwick you mentioned?"

"Yes."

"And does this establish that the equipment was received?"

"It establishes that he signed for it."

"Does it establish that the equipment was later delivered to W-2?"

"No."

"Do you know where it went?"

Weller shook his head.

"Not personally."

Seraphina placed the transfer slip beside the register copy.

The signature was now identifiable.

The reference number matched.

The date matched.

The equipment categories matched.

For the first time, they had a clear link between Veylan Works' equipment and the central stores authority.

But the next destination remained unknown.

She looked at Weller.

"Would you be willing to provide a written statement about these documents?"

"Yes."

"Please distinguish what you personally remember from what the documents show."

Weller nodded.

"That is reasonable."

Seraphina thanked him.

After Weller departed, Cedric came into the sitting room.

He examined the transfer slip.

"That is a significant discovery."

"Yes."

"We have the company correspondence, the transfer authorization and the receiving signature."

"Yes."

"Then the equipment reached central stores."

"That is what the documents indicate."

Cedric smiled.

"You're not going to let me say anything more, are you?"

"No."

He laughed.

Seraphina gathered the papers.

"We still do not know whether the equipment was later sent to W-2, another station, or retained in storage."

"Could it have been used elsewhere?"

"Yes."

"Could it have been discarded?"

"Yes."

"Could it still be in the warehouse?"

"Also yes."

Cedric looked at her.

"You have a remarkable ability to make a discovery sound like another beginning."

Seraphina considered that.

"That is because it is another beginning."

The committee received the new documents the following week.

Holt confirmed that the transfer reference matched the Royal Logistics register.

Theodore authorized a request for the subsequent distribution records.

Mr Bellamy agreed to search the Treasury's capital equipment accounts for any later valuation or disposal entry.

Lucien requested a technical review of the three regulating assemblies.

Professor Vale asked whether the components could be identified from their original specifications.

The committee now had a documented sequence:

Veylan Works had supplied equipment.

The regional office had withheld permission for collection pending inspection.

The equipment had later been transferred to central stores.

A receiving clerk had signed for it.

What happened after that remained unknown.

It was not the complete answer.

But it was the first part of the old programme's history that could be followed through matching references rather than assumptions.

Seraphina placed the transfer slip into the committee file.

She wrote one final note beneath the entry:

*Receipt at central stores confirmed. Subsequent allocation unknown.*

Then she closed the folder.

The next morning, she received a short letter from the Crown Prince.

It contained only one sentence.

*The Royal Logistics Department has found a second transfer record bearing Robert Fenwick's signature, dated three months after the first.*

Seraphina read it.

She turned the page.

The second record concerned equipment sent from central stores to a regional maintenance location.

The destination was abbreviated.

The final two characters were clearly visible.

**W-2.**

She stared at the page for a long moment.

Then she called for Mira.

"Please bring me the committee correspondence book."

Mira entered.

"Another document, my lady?"

"Yes."

"Is it important?"

Seraphina looked again at the destination.

"I think we may finally know where the equipment went."

Mira smiled.

"That is good news."

"It is."

Seraphina placed the letter on top of the file.

Then she noticed the date.

The equipment had been sent to W-2 three months after the contractor's settlement.

And the receiving signature at the station was not Robert Fenwick's.

It belonged to someone else.

Someone whose name had not appeared in any of the records so far.

**A. Mercer.**

Seraphina slowly set down her pen.

The old station had not simply been left without equipment.

Someone had received it.

And that meant someone might still know what happened next.

**End of Chapter 34**
