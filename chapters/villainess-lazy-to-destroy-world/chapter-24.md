# Chapter 24 — The Academy's Unfinished Demonstration

The first warning arrived in the form of a letter.

The second arrived in the form of a person.

Seraphina considered the second considerably more troublesome.

The letter had come from the Royal Academy three days after the royal reception guest arrangements reached the Veyr estate. It requested permission for a technical team to conduct a preliminary examination of the western mana conduit before the larger regional inspection began.

The request was properly written.

It identified the proposed inspection site, listed the Academy personnel involved, explained the instruments they intended to bring, and specified that no alterations would be made to the conduit.

It even included an estimated duration.

Seraphina had read it twice and found no immediate reason to object.

Then Lucien Arkwright arrived at the estate.

He was carrying a leather case, two rolled diagrams and an expression suggesting that he had slept very little.

"Lady Veyr."

"Master Arkwright."

"I apologize for arriving without more notice."

"Your letter arrived yesterday."

"Yes."

"That is notice."

"I meant personal notice."

Seraphina looked at the case in his hand.

"Is that related to the inspection?"

"Partly."

"And the diagrams?"

"Also."

"Are you carrying anything that isn't related to the inspection?"

Lucien considered the question.

"My lunch."

"Then I am relieved."

Mira, standing behind Seraphina, lowered her eyes.

Lucien seemed not to notice.

"I would like to explain something before the technical team arrives."

Seraphina gestured toward the sitting room.

"Please do."

Lucien spread the first diagram across the table.

It showed the western conduit system beneath the northern estate. Several lines represented mana channels, while small symbols indicated junctions, regulators and access points.

Seraphina recognized the section near Westmere.

She had seen a simpler version during the Academy's earlier presentation.

"This is the current map?" she asked.

"Partly."

"Partly?"

"The central conduit route is based on the original construction plan. The branch connections have been updated from later inspection records."

"How old is the original plan?"

"Forty-three years."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Has the route changed?"

"Not according to the records."

"That wasn't my question."

Lucien paused.

"Some sections have been modified. The main route should remain consistent."

"Should?"

"Yes."

She leaned closer.

The map showed three branch connections. One passed near the old mill road. Another crossed agricultural land. The third approached a small maintenance building that no longer appeared on the current estate map.

"What is this building?"

Lucien followed her finger.

"An old inspection station."

"Is it still there?"

"I don't know."

Seraphina looked up.

"You don't know whether a building shown on your inspection map still exists?"

"The Academy's copy was based on the original plan."

"Then perhaps the first task should be finding out whether the building exists."

Lucien looked at the map again.

"That is reasonable."

"Thank you."

He rolled up his sleeves and began writing a note.

Seraphina watched him.

"Is that the purpose of today's visit?"

"Not entirely."

Lucien opened the leather case.

Inside were several small instruments, a collection of measuring rods and a metal device with a circular glass face.

Seraphina looked at it.

"What does that do?"

"It measures fluctuations in the mana current."

"How?"

"The needle responds to changes in the surrounding field. A stable flow produces a narrow range of movement. An irregular flow causes wider variation."

"Does it measure the amount of mana?"

"Not directly. It measures relative fluctuation."

"Can it distinguish between a damaged conduit and a faulty instrument?"

Lucien stopped.

He looked at the device.

Then at her.

"Not by itself."

"Can it distinguish between a normal seasonal variation and an abnormal one?"

"Only when compared with a reliable baseline."

"Do you have one?"

Lucien slowly closed the case.

"Not for every section."

Seraphina folded her hands.

"Then what exactly will your team be demonstrating?"

"How the inspection equipment works."

"That is different from demonstrating that the conduit is safe."

"Yes."

"Good. I would prefer that distinction be written into the report."

Lucien looked almost pleased.

"You are more careful than most people who request these inspections."

"I have seen what happens when people confuse a measurement with a conclusion."

"From the estate records?"

"Among other things."

He nodded.

The conversation had taken less than ten minutes.

Seraphina suspected that the Academy had not expected the inspection to begin with questions about the limitations of its instruments.

She considered this a promising start.

The technical team arrived shortly before noon.

There were four members.

Lucien introduced them as Master Alistair Fenwick, an Academy engineer; Helena Voss, a junior researcher; Bram Ellery, an instrument specialist; and Professor Edmund Vale, who had accompanied the group to observe the procedure.

Professor Vale greeted Seraphina with a polite bow.

"Lady Veyr. I hope we are not inconveniencing you."

"Not yet, Professor."

His eyes moved briefly toward Lucien.

"That is encouraging."

Master Fenwick was a broad-shouldered man in his late forties, with greying hair and a practical manner. He carried a heavy wooden case and looked more interested in the equipment than in the formalities.

"Your Grace has approved access to the western site?" he asked.

"Limited access for preliminary inspection," Seraphina replied. "No repair work, no alteration of the conduit and no interruption of local services without separate permission."

"Understood."

"And the inspection team will remain with the estate's assigned guide."

"Naturally."

"Good."

Master Fenwick looked at Lucien.

"She has already given you the conditions?"

"Yes."

"Then we may proceed."

The group travelled by carriage to the old mill road.

The western conduit access point was located beyond a low stone wall, near a stretch of farmland. A small wooden shelter stood beside it, its roof recently repaired.

A few estate workers were clearing vegetation around the entrance.

Seraphina stepped down from the carriage.

The afternoon was cool, and a light wind moved through the fields.

"That shelter is new," she said.

"Yes," Lucien replied. "The old access structure was replaced several years ago."

"Do you know when?"

"Not precisely."

"Then record that."

Helena Voss opened her notebook.

Seraphina glanced at her.

"Do you have the replacement record?"

Helena checked the documents.

"Not with us."

"Then note that the date remains unverified."

The junior researcher wrote it down.

Master Fenwick removed a set of measuring tools from his case.

"We will begin with an external examination. The access chamber will be opened only after the surrounding structure is checked."

Seraphina nodded.

That was sensible.

The engineer inspected the stonework, tested the hinges and examined the protective seal around the access cover.

Bram Ellery checked the instruments.

Lucien reviewed the diagram.

For several minutes, everyone appeared to be doing exactly what they were trained to do.

Seraphina found this reassuring.

Then Master Fenwick stopped.

"Who authorized this replacement?"

The estate worker beside him looked uncertain.

"I don't know, sir. It was already here when I joined the maintenance team."

"How long ago?"

"Six years."

Fenwick examined the outer frame.

"The bolts are newer than the surrounding metal."

Lucien approached.

"Could they have been replaced during the last inspection?"

"Possibly."

"Do we have the inspection record?"

Helena searched through the documents.

"Not in the packet."

Seraphina looked at the estate worker.

"Who maintains this access point?"

"The western maintenance crew, my lady."

"Who supervises them?"

"Mr. Thomas Wren."

"Then we should ask him for the maintenance history before assuming the bolts were changed by the Academy."

Lucien nodded.

"Agreed."

Master Fenwick continued his inspection.

The access cover was opened carefully.

A faint blue glow appeared beneath it.

The conduit itself was a narrow channel lined with pale mineral material. Thin metal bands crossed the inner surface at regular intervals. Mana moved through it as a faint shimmer, almost like light passing beneath water.

Seraphina had seen magical objects before, but she still found the sight unusual.

It was beautiful.

It was also apparently expensive to maintain.

"Is that the normal appearance?" she asked.

"Approximately," Lucien replied.

"Approximately?"

"The brightness varies with the surrounding mana concentration."

"Does the colour change indicate anything?"

"Not necessarily."

"Then what should we look for?"

Lucien crouched beside the access chamber.

"Changes in the movement pattern, pressure fluctuations, interruptions in the circuit and abnormal heat accumulation."

"Can any of those be identified visually?"

"Some."

"And the others?"

"Require instruments."

Seraphina nodded.

She was beginning to understand why the Academy needed trained personnel.

A faint glow was not a diagnosis.

The first measurement took twenty minutes.

Bram Ellery placed the instrument beside the conduit and adjusted two small metal rings.

The needle moved.

It settled.

Then it moved again.

He recorded the readings.

Master Fenwick examined the results.

"Within the expected range."

Lucien frowned.

"That is lower than the previous measurement."

"By how much?"

"Approximately four percent."

"Could be seasonal."

"Possibly."

"Could also be instrument variation."

"Yes."

Seraphina looked at the two men.

"Do you disagree?"

"Not exactly," Fenwick said. "We have a previous reading from the Academy's visit, but the conditions were not identical."

"Then the measurements cannot be compared directly."

"Not without qualification."

"Will the report say that?"

Fenwick nodded.

"It should."

Lucien studied the instrument again.

"There is still a variation at the junction."

"How significant?" Fenwick asked.

"Small."

"Consistent?"

"Not yet."

"Then we need another reading."

Bram moved the instrument to a second position.

The needle shifted.

This time, it moved farther than before.

Helena leaned forward.

"That is outside the first range."

"Only slightly," Bram said.

Lucien examined the position.

"Try the secondary calibration."

Bram adjusted the instrument.

The needle returned toward the earlier range.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Does that mean the first reading was wrong?"

"It means the instrument may have been affected by its position."

"Then the first result should not be treated as a confirmed fluctuation."

Lucien nodded.

"Correct."

Master Fenwick looked mildly irritated.

"Which is why we are taking multiple readings."

Seraphina did not respond.

The engineer was right.

She was also beginning to notice that the technical team was learning the same lesson as the estate administrators: a procedure was only as reliable as the conditions under which it was performed.

The team continued for another hour.

They examined the visible sections of the conduit, checked the outer casing and recorded the condition of the access chamber.

No immediate structural danger was identified.

However, the junction's readings remained inconsistent.

The team could not determine whether the variation was caused by a minor defect, seasonal conditions, instrument positioning or an older modification in the circuit.

Their conclusion was appropriately limited.

The conduit required further examination.

It was not declared unsafe.

It was not declared fully sound.

Seraphina considered that a satisfactory result.

The trouble began when a stable-looking section produced an unexpected sound.

It was faint.

A short metallic click.

Bram stopped moving the instrument.

"Did you hear that?"

Everyone turned.

The sound came from somewhere inside the access chamber.

Lucien crouched closer.

"Again."

They waited.

Nothing happened.

Master Fenwick examined the outer casing.

"Could be a loose fastening."

"Or a pressure adjustment," Lucien said.

"Can we identify it without opening the secondary panel?"

"Not from here."

Seraphina looked at the panel.

"Would opening it require a separate authorization?"

"Yes," Fenwick replied. "It would expose a section of the circuit that is not included in today's inspection."

"Then it remains closed."

Lucien looked disappointed.

"We could examine the panel without disturbing the circuit."

"Would that answer the question?"

"Not necessarily."

"Then we should record the sound and request permission for a separate examination."

Fenwick nodded.

"That is the correct procedure."

The team completed its notes.

Before leaving, Lucien examined the area around the access chamber.

He found a small indentation in the stone beside the wall.

It was rectangular, with two narrow holes near the corners.

He crouched.

"This may be where the old maintenance marker was mounted."

Seraphina approached.

"May be?"

"The dimensions are consistent with the old diagrams."

"Can you confirm?"

"Not yet."

Master Fenwick examined the indentation.

"Could have been a bracket."

"Or a sign," Helena suggested.

Lucien shook his head.

"The old plans show a metal marker here."

Seraphina looked at the empty space.

"Is it possible that the marker was removed when the access structure was replaced?"

"Yes."

"Then we need the replacement records."

Lucien stared at the indentation.

"Yes."

For the first time that afternoon, he seemed less interested in the technical reading than in the missing object.

Seraphina understood.

The marker might have contained a maintenance number, a construction reference or the name of the contractor responsible for the junction.

Without it, the physical site could not be matched easily to the older records.

That did not prove deliberate concealment.

It did make the missing record more important.

The team returned to the estate shortly before sunset.

Seraphina invited them to take tea in the smaller reception room.

Master Fenwick accepted.

Professor Vale seemed relieved.

Lucien looked as though he would rather return to the access chamber.

Seraphina sat across from him.

"Master Arkwright."

"Yes?"

"Your report will distinguish between confirmed observations and possible explanations?"

"Certainly."

"And it will identify which questions remain unanswered?"

"Yes."

"Good."

Lucien looked at her.

"You are concerned that the findings will be misunderstood."

"I am concerned that someone will read a sentence saying the conduit showed a variation and decide it needs immediate reconstruction."

Professor Vale smiled.

"That has happened before."

Seraphina turned to him.

"Has it?"

"Not here. But technical reports are often simplified when passed between departments. A cautious observation becomes a warning. A warning becomes a request for funds. A request for funds becomes an approved project before anyone has agreed on the actual problem."

"That sounds familiar."

"It should. It happens in many institutions."

Lucien set down his cup.

"The Academy has been trying to improve how its findings are communicated."

"Then perhaps the report should include a summary of what the findings do not establish."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"That would be useful."

Professor Vale considered it.

"An explicit limitations section?"

"Yes," Seraphina replied. "Something that says what has been examined, what has not been examined, and what conclusions cannot yet be drawn."

Lucien picked up his notebook.

"I can include that."

Seraphina looked at him.

"You do not need to write it down immediately."

"I might forget."

"Then your memory is worse than mine."

"I have been told that before."

Mira, who was serving tea, glanced at Seraphina.

Seraphina ignored her.

After the Academy team departed, Seraphina went to her father's study.

Alaric was speaking with General Cassian when she entered.

The general had a map spread across the desk.

"Am I interrupting?"

"Only a discussion about the western road," Alaric replied.

Seraphina stopped.

"The western road?"

Cassian looked up.

"The section near the old mill junction. The surveyors found that part of the drainage channel is obstructed."

"Is it affecting the road?"

"Not yet. But if the blockage remains through the next heavy rainfall, water may undermine the outer edge."

Alaric tapped the map.

"We intend to repair the drainage before the road itself deteriorates."

Seraphina studied the location.

It was not far from the conduit access point.

"Does the drainage channel pass near the old inspection station?"

Cassian frowned.

"Which station?"

"The one shown on the Academy's original conduit map. It may no longer exist."

The general looked at the map.

"There is an old stone foundation near the junction. We assumed it belonged to a former road shelter."

"Has anyone inspected it?"

"Not recently."

Alaric looked at her.

"Why?"

Seraphina explained the day's inspection, including the missing marker and the uncertainty around the old access structure.

Cassian listened carefully.

When she finished, he leaned over the map.

"That foundation may be worth examining."

"Would it be related to the conduit?"

"Possibly. The old road and the original maintenance route were built around the same period."

Alaric considered this.

"We should not assume they are connected."

"No," Cassian agreed. "But the surveyors can record the foundation's position while they are inspecting the drainage."

Seraphina nodded.

"That would be useful."

Alaric glanced at her.

"You seem to have developed an interest in old foundations."

"I have developed an interest in knowing what is beneath the ground before someone decides to build over it."

General Cassian laughed.

"That is a sensible interest."

Seraphina had the uncomfortable feeling that sensible interests were becoming increasingly expensive.

Two days later, Lucien sent his preliminary report.

It was short, carefully worded and accompanied by the raw measurements.

The report confirmed:

- No visible structural failure was identified during the external examination.
- The measured mana flow remained broadly stable during the inspection period.
- A small variation was recorded near the junction, but its cause was undetermined.
- A brief metallic sound was heard within the access chamber.
- The secondary panel was not opened.
- The old maintenance marker was absent.
- The original inspection station shown on the historical map had not yet been located.

The report included a separate section titled *Limitations of the Preliminary Examination*.

Seraphina read it with approval.

It did not claim more than the team had established.

At the bottom, Lucien had added a handwritten note.

*Master Whitlock has agreed to meet us in Aurelion before the royal reception. He says he remembers the junction modification, but not the contractor's name. He believes the original work order may have been retained by the regional maintenance office.*

Seraphina read the note twice.

Then she placed the report in the growing file.

The next step was clear.

They needed to find the old work order.

Not because it would necessarily explain the missing marker.

Not because it would prove wrongdoing.

But because the conduit had been modified twenty-two years ago, and the records describing that modification were incomplete.

The Academy could measure the current condition.

The estate could identify the land and maintenance responsibilities.

The old work order might explain who had been responsible for the original change.

Three different sources.

None sufficient by itself.

Together, perhaps, they could establish something reliable.

Seraphina closed the file.

That evening, a second letter arrived.

This one was from Liora.

The saintess had written that the Church's charitable office had received a request from the Academy for assistance in reviewing the medical provisions available to regional inspection teams.

The Academy had apparently discovered that its emergency supplies differed considerably between regions.

Some teams carried basic treatment materials.

Others relied on local medical facilities.

A few had no clearly recorded arrangement at all.

Liora was concerned that the inspection programme might expand without anyone establishing minimum medical support for the people involved.

She asked whether Seraphina knew who handled medical procurement for the Veyr estate.

Seraphina read the letter and looked toward Mira.

"Do we have a medical supplies officer?"

"We have a physician, my lady. The infirmary stores are managed by Mistress Eleanor."

"Does she purchase supplies?"

"She prepares the requirements. The estate's purchasing office handles the orders."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we should ask her how the system works."

Mira hesitated.

"Are you going to help the Academy with this as well?"

"No."

Mira looked unconvinced.

"I am going to ask how our own medical supplies are managed."

"That sounds similar."

"It is not."

"Of course, my lady."

Seraphina unfolded Liora's letter again.

The saintess had not asked her to take responsibility for the national medical supply system.

She had asked a specific question.

That was manageable.

Seraphina took out a sheet of paper and began writing her reply.

She would provide the name of the estate's medical supply supervisor and offer to share the relevant procurement procedure, provided the physician agreed.

Nothing more.

She placed the letter beside Lucien's report.

The western conduit had not failed.

The old marker remained missing.

The Academy had discovered that its instruments had limitations.

And now its emergency medical arrangements were being questioned.

Seraphina looked at the two documents.

She had intended to avoid the Academy.

Instead, the Academy seemed to be discovering that almost every institution had a problem it had not thought to examine.

She picked up her notebook and added one final sentence beneath her rules for the royal reception.

*Do not confuse being asked a question with being responsible for the answer.*

She considered it an excellent rule.

Then she closed the notebook, went to dinner, and deliberately did not ask what other letters had arrived.

**End of Chapter 24**
