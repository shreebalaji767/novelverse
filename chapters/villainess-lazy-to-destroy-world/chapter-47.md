# Chapter 47 — The Company That Had Already Closed

Seraphina read the company name three times.

**Veylan Works.**

She placed the Treasury letter beside the old W-2 settlement records.

The company had appeared in the investigation before.

It had been responsible for the construction work at W-2, where Engineer Darius Veylan had supervised the installation of regulating equipment. Its contract had ended with an incomplete commissioning process, an unresolved equipment deduction and a final settlement that had never been approved.

The company had ceased trading twenty-one years earlier.

The sixth reserve allocation had been made before that.

Seraphina checked the payment date.

It was twenty-three years old.

"So the company was still operating when the payment was issued," she said.

Holt nodded.

"According to the commercial register, yes."

"Then the fact that it was later dissolved tells us nothing about whether this payment was legitimate."

"Agreed."

Seraphina picked up the Treasury voucher.

The payment had been issued under the description:

*Technical preparation and reserve installation services — Eastern and Western Regional Infrastructure Programme.*

The amount was 960 crowns.

The second payment, issued to Central Stores, was 420 crowns.

Together, they accounted for the sixth reserve allocation listed in the historical funding summary.

"Was the money paid in full?" she asked.

"The Treasury ledger records both payments as completed."

"Does that mean the work was completed?"

"No."

"Good. We should keep those two things separate."

Holt made a note.

Seraphina leaned back.

She had hoped the sixth allocation would turn out to be a contingency reserve that had never been used.

Instead, it had paid a company already connected to one of the incomplete facilities.

There was no evidence of wrongdoing.

There was, however, another trail to follow.

The next committee meeting began with the two payment vouchers.

Minister Garrick examined them first.

"Nine hundred and sixty crowns to Veylan Works."

"Yes," Seraphina replied.

"And four hundred and twenty to Central Stores."

"Yes."

"What was Veylan Works paid to provide?"

"The voucher says technical preparation and reserve installation services."

"That is not a useful description."

"It is not."

Lucien examined the second page.

"Could this have been for spare components?"

"Possibly," Holt said.

"Or preliminary engineering work," Seraphina added.

Garrick looked at her.

"Do you believe this payment was connected to W-2?"

"I believe the company was involved in W-2. I do not yet know whether this payment was."

"Could the same engineers have worked on both?"

"Yes. But that does not establish that the contracts were connected."

Theodore looked at the payment date.

"Was the sixth allocation part of the same programme?"

"The funding summary lists it alongside the other five facilities," Holt replied. "But the original authorization has not been found."

"Then we should locate it."

Seraphina nodded.

"And we should establish what the payment actually purchased before deciding where it belongs."

Garrick placed the voucher down.

"Very well. Treasury will provide the underlying payment authorization and supporting invoices."

The Central Stores voucher was easier to trace.

Its reference led to a procurement register from the old regional stores authority.

The payment had covered three categories:

- Protective storage containers.
- Replacement fittings.
- Transport and handling.

The quantities were listed.

The destination was not.

Instead, the register contained a general reference:

*Reserve programme — regional distribution.*

Seraphina examined the figures.

"How many containers?"

"Twelve," Holt replied.

"Replacement fittings?"

"Twenty-four sets."

"Transport?"

"Four separate consignments."

"Do we have dispatch records?"

"Not yet."

Lucien looked at the procurement description.

"These could have been used for several facilities."

"Or for the general reserve," Seraphina said.

"Yes."

"Then we cannot assign them to any particular station."

Holt wrote down the qualification.

The committee had become increasingly careful about such things.

It was progress, although Seraphina was beginning to suspect that the most significant achievement of the entire review would be teaching officials to write the words *not confirmed*.

The Veylan Works invoice arrived four days later.

It was a single sheet.

The company had charged for:

*Preliminary engineering assessment, preparation of reserve installation materials, equipment compatibility review and regional technical coordination.*

The invoice was signed by Edmund Veylan.

Darius Veylan was listed as technical representative.

Seraphina recognized both names.

Edmund had owned the company.

Darius had supervised the W-2 work.

The invoice did not identify a facility.

It did not identify a specific component.

It did not include an attached technical report.

At the bottom was a reference:

**VW/RP/06.**

Seraphina showed it to Lucien.

"Does this resemble an ordinary engineering invoice?"

"The description is broad, but not unusual for preliminary work."

"Would you expect a technical report?"

"Yes. Especially for an equipment compatibility review."

"Could it have been prepared without one?"

"It could. But we would have no way to verify the work."

Seraphina placed the invoice beside the Treasury voucher.

"Then we need the company's project register."

Veylan Works had maintained its main office in the eastern commercial district.

The building was now occupied by a merchant association.

Its former business records had been transferred to a private archive when the company ceased trading.

The archive belonged to a retired solicitor named Mr. Percival Dunn.

He replied to the committee's request with a short letter.

He still held several boxes of Veylan Works documents.

However, the company had been dissolved through an ordinary commercial closure, and its records had not been preserved according to any government archive standard.

Some documents had been discarded.

Others had been transferred to former employees.

A few boxes remained in his possession.

He was willing to permit an examination.

Seraphina read the letter.

"That is more cooperation than I expected."

Holt looked at the archive address.

"The office is in the eastern district."

"How far?"

"Approximately half a day's journey."

Seraphina closed the letter.

"Then we will need to arrange a visit."

Lucien looked at her.

"Will you attend?"

"I would prefer not to."

"That was not what I asked."

Seraphina sighed.

"Yes."

The journey east took place on a clear morning.

The road was busy with carts, merchant wagons and delivery coaches.

Seraphina spent most of the journey reading the company's dissolution record.

Veylan Works had been registered twenty-seven years earlier.

It had initially specialized in industrial magical equipment, including regulating assemblies, protective casings and conduit fittings.

The company had gradually expanded into regional infrastructure contracts.

Its final major projects included W-2 and several smaller installations.

The dissolution notice cited financial difficulties following the loss of two contracts and the return of incomplete equipment.

There was no mention of criminal proceedings.

No government seizure.

No unexplained disappearance of assets.

The company had simply stopped trading.

Seraphina closed the record.

"Do we know why it lost the contracts?"

Holt checked his notes.

"One was cancelled after a regional design change. The other was disputed over completion requirements."

"Was that W-2?"

"Yes."

Lucien looked out of the carriage window.

"That does not mean the company was incompetent in every project."

"No," Seraphina replied. "It means we should not assume that either."

Mr. Dunn's archive occupied two rooms above a legal office.

The first contained shelves of bound registers.

The second held boxes arranged according to the names of former clients.

Mr. Dunn greeted them with a polite bow.

"I apologize for the condition of the records."

Seraphina looked at the shelves.

"They appear better organized than several government archives we have visited."

Mr. Dunn smiled.

"That is a rather low standard."

"It is."

He led them to a table.

Three boxes had been prepared.

The first contained commercial accounts.

The second contained contracts.

The third contained technical correspondence.

Seraphina began with the commercial accounts.

The company had maintained separate ledgers for each major contract.

W-2 appeared in one.

The reserve programme appeared in another.

She turned several pages.

The reserve account was marked:

**RP-06 — GENERAL RESERVE PREPARATION.**

There were entries for materials, staff time, preliminary design work and equipment compatibility assessment.

The total matched the 960-crown invoice.

Seraphina checked the individual entries.

The company had assigned two engineers to the work for six weeks.

It had purchased specialized fittings.

It had conducted an assessment of three regulating assembly types.

It had also prepared a list of possible storage locations.

The work appeared genuine.

But the destination remained unclear.

Lucien examined the technical correspondence.

He found a document titled:

*Reserve Equipment Compatibility — Preliminary Findings.*

The report was dated three weeks before the invoice.

It identified three types of regulating assemblies.

One was suitable for a medium-capacity regional relay station.

Another was designed for smaller local distribution points.

The third was intended for temporary use during maintenance operations.

Lucien read the report carefully.

"This is useful."

Seraphina looked up.

"Does it identify the sixth facility?"

"No. It describes equipment categories, not locations."

"Does it mention E-1, W-2 or A-2?"

"Only as examples of existing or proposed installations."

"Then it was a general assessment."

"That is what it appears to be."

Seraphina examined the final page.

A handwritten note read:

*Final allocation subject to confirmation of regional requirements.*

There was no confirmation attached.

"Was the report accepted?" she asked Mr. Dunn.

He searched the contract box.

"I have a letter acknowledging receipt."

"From whom?"

"The Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office."

Seraphina looked at Holt.

He wrote down the reference.

The acknowledgment letter was dated twenty-three years earlier.

It thanked Veylan Works for submitting the preliminary report and stated that the findings would be reviewed before any reserve equipment was assigned.

The letter also requested clarification about the proposed storage arrangements.

Veylan Works had replied.

The company recommended that reserve components be held in regional stores until individual installation sites were confirmed.

Seraphina read the reply.

"That is sensible."

Lucien nodded.

"It would prevent equipment from being sent to sites before they were ready."

"Was that recommendation followed?"

"We know some equipment was placed in Central Stores," Holt said.

"Some," Seraphina repeated.

The letter identified a proposed arrangement:

- Central storage for specialized regulating assemblies.
- Regional storage for common replacement fittings.
- Release only after site authorization and technical confirmation.

The company had not been asked to construct a sixth facility.

It had been asked to prepare a general reserve plan.

Seraphina looked at the original funding summary.

The sixth reserve allocation might not have represented a facility at all.

It might have been a programme category.

There was still the matter of the payment.

The invoice described technical preparation.

The acknowledgment letter confirmed that the report had been received.

But there was no final acceptance certificate.

Holt found another document in the commercial ledger.

It was a payment confirmation from the Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office.

The office had approved the 960 crowns for the preliminary assessment and reserve preparation.

The payment had not been contingent on construction or installation.

It had been authorized against the submitted work.

Seraphina read the approval carefully.

The signature belonged to Arthur Fenwick.

The same Senior Expenditure Officer whose name appeared in the W-2 settlement records.

She placed the document on the table.

"Another appearance."

Lucien examined the signature.

"Does that mean the same programme?"

"It means the same official approved both payments."

"Could he have been responsible for the whole reserve arrangement?"

"Possibly. But we need to establish the extent of his authority."

Holt checked the approval.

"His title was Senior Expenditure Officer. The authorization was within the stated financial ceiling."

"Was he also responsible for technical approval?"

"No."

"Then we should not treat his payment authorization as technical acceptance."

Holt wrote that down.

The remaining records concerned the equipment itself.

The company had recommended three storage categories.

The Central Stores Authority had received twelve protective containers and twenty-four sets of fittings.

A separate equipment register showed that two regulating assemblies were placed in reserve.

One was the casing later identified as RC-17-051.

The other had a different number.

**RC-17-063.**

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Have we encountered that number?"

He checked the committee's register.

"No."

"Could it be another component from the same procurement?"

"Yes."

"Do we know where it went?"

Holt searched the transfer schedule.

There was an entry.

The assembly had been sent to the Western Regional Maintenance Authority.

The receiving signature was difficult to read.

The date was twenty-two years earlier.

Seraphina studied it.

"Does the document identify the intended station?"

"No."

"Then it could have gone to any western project."

Lucien looked at the equipment specification.

"Not necessarily. Its capacity would narrow the possibilities."

"Would it identify one facility?"

"Only if we had the original design requirements."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we have another equipment trail."

Mr. Dunn brought them tea while they examined the final box.

He had found a letter from Edmund Veylan to the Allocation Office.

It was dated several months after the reserve payment.

The company had asked whether the preliminary assessment had been accepted and whether additional work was required.

The reply was brief.

The office confirmed that the report had been received.

Further instructions would follow after the regional authorities confirmed their requirements.

No further instructions appeared in the company's correspondence.

The letter had been filed under *Completed Accounts*.

Seraphina looked at it.

"Why was it filed as completed?"

Mr. Dunn checked the ledger.

"Because the invoice had been paid and the preliminary work had been delivered."

"That makes sense from the company's perspective."

"Yes."

"It does not mean the entire reserve programme was completed."

"No."

Seraphina returned the letter to its folder.

For once, the distinction was clear.

The company had completed the work it had been paid to do.

The government had never completed the next stage.

On the journey back, Lucien reviewed the findings.

"So Veylan Works was paid for a preliminary reserve assessment."

"Yes."

"The company prepared compatibility recommendations and a storage plan."

"Yes."

"The report was received by the Allocation Office."

"Yes."

"But no final regional requirements were issued."

"Not in the records we found."

Lucien looked at the western transfer entry.

"And RC-17-063 was transferred to the Western Regional Maintenance Authority."

"Yes."

"That may be connected to the sixth reserve allocation."

"Or it may have been part of the general equipment reserve."

"Either way, we need its receiving record."

Seraphina looked at the countryside.

The original mystery had seemed straightforward.

A company had received money.

A reserve facility had appeared in the funding summary.

Equipment had moved between regions.

Now the records suggested something less dramatic but more complicated.

The company had done the preliminary work.

The government had paid for it.

The programme had never progressed to a final allocation.

Equipment had nevertheless been distributed.

And the administrative records had gradually treated the reserve category as though it were a sixth facility.

Nobody had needed to falsify anything for the confusion to develop.

A sequence of unfinished decisions was apparently sufficient.

The committee reviewed the archive findings three days later.

Minister Garrick read the report in silence.

When he finished, he placed it on the table.

"Then the sixth facility did not exist."

"We cannot establish that one was ever planned as a physical facility," Seraphina replied.

"The reserve allocation was for equipment preparation and storage."

"That is what the surviving documents support."

Theodore examined the original funding summary.

"Why was it listed alongside the five facilities?"

"We do not know. It may have been included because the reserve programme supported their construction and maintenance."

Garrick looked at Holt.

"Can we correct the register?"

"Yes. We can classify it as a reserve programme allocation, not a separate facility."

Lucien added:

"That would also prevent the committee from searching for a building that may never have been intended to exist."

Seraphina nodded.

"Please record the distinction."

The committee also agreed to investigate the transfer of RC-17-063.

The Western Regional Maintenance Authority was asked to locate the original receiving register, current storage record and any subsequent transfer documentation.

The equipment was not to be moved or used until its identity and condition had been confirmed.

The revised register now contained six entries:

- N-4: confirmed location, disconnected status believed, closure details incomplete.
- W-2: confirmed location, commissioning incomplete, equipment history partially reconstructed.
- E-1: confirmed structure, equipment uncalibrated and held in storage.
- S-3: uncompleted proposed facility, land access never secured.
- A-2: confirmed structure, equipment installation not completed.
- Reserve programme: funding and preliminary work confirmed, not a physical facility.

Theodore read the list.

"We have made considerable progress."

Seraphina looked at the six entries.

"We have made considerable progress in establishing how much remains unresolved."

Garrick laughed.

"That is one way of describing it."

"It is the accurate way."

After the meeting, Theodore asked Seraphina to remain.

Holt and Lucien left with the revised register.

The prince placed a letter on the table.

"The committee's review has changed the way the department handles historical infrastructure projects."

Seraphina looked at the letter.

"Has it?"

"New projects will require a unified record containing technical status, financial status, equipment location and responsible authority."

"That is sensible."

"It will also require confirmation when a project is suspended, relocated or cancelled."

"Also sensible."

"And equipment transfers will require a receiving office to confirm the intended use."

Seraphina nodded.

"That would have prevented several of the problems we found."

Theodore studied her.

"I would like you to remain involved in the review of the new procedure."

Seraphina looked at him.

"How involved?"

"An advisory role."

"That phrase has become suspicious."

He almost smiled.

"Your existing restrictions would remain."

"Then I will consider it once the written proposal arrives."

"Fair enough."

He hesitated.

"Your appointment ends in three weeks."

"I am aware."

"Would you prefer to conclude the historical review before deciding?"

"Yes."

Theodore accepted that.

Seraphina returned to the Veyr estate two days later.

Her father was waiting in the study.

Alaric looked at the stack of papers she carried.

"More royal documents?"

"Unfortunately."

"Have they found another station?"

"Not yet."

"Another building?"

"Several."

He regarded her for a moment.

"Do you regret accepting the appointment?"

Seraphina thought about it.

She had discovered that the old programme had not been one continuous failure.

Some work had been completed properly.

Some had been paid for but never followed by the next stage.

Some equipment had been transferred to projects that were not ready.

Some facilities had been built but never commissioned.

And some records had survived only because a clerk had kept a copy that nobody had asked for.

She had also learned that the people involved were not all careless or dishonest.

Some had followed the instructions available to them.

Others had tried to warn their superiors.

A few had simply left offices without ensuring that anyone understood what remained unfinished.

"No," she said eventually. "I regret agreeing to attend so many meetings."

Alaric gave a short laugh.

"That sounds more like you."

Seraphina placed the documents on the table.

"How are the northern roads?"

"Two bridges need repairs before winter."

She looked at him.

"Have the contractors provided estimates?"

"Yes."

"Are they detailed?"

Alaric stared at her.

Then he smiled.

"Your mother told me not to ask you about the roads."

"That was wise."

That evening, Seraphina received a letter from the Western Regional Maintenance Authority.

The original receiving register for RC-17-063 had been found.

The equipment had been delivered to the western region twenty-two years earlier.

The receiving clerk had signed for it.

A storage location was listed.

But the following line contained an instruction that had never appeared in any other record.

*Do not release pending inspection by the appointed regional engineer.*

The appointed engineer's name was written beneath it.

**Darius Veylan.**

Seraphina stared at the page.

The same engineer who had supervised W-2.

The same engineer whose company had prepared the reserve equipment assessment.

And the same engineer whose final commissioning report had never been found.

She read the date again.

The instruction had been issued before Veylan Works ceased trading.

She placed the letter beside the earlier W-2 records.

The committee had not yet established whether the two projects were connected.

But the same engineer had been assigned responsibility for equipment in both.

And the equipment in the western storehouse had apparently been waiting for his inspection.

For twenty-two years.
