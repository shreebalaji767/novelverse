# **Chapter 81 — The Capital Had Been Protected by a System Nobody Fully Understood**

The royal capital had three protective networks.

Seraphina learned this before breakfast.

She had assumed there were two.

The first protected the outer walls and principal gates. The second regulated the magical distribution beneath the central districts, supporting public lighting, communication relays, and several civic facilities.

The third, according to the briefing invitation, was an older control network that had been incorporated into the modern system during a series of renovations.

Nobody seemed entirely certain what it still did.

This was not an encouraging way to begin a royal meeting.

"Perhaps it is only a historical designation," Evelyne suggested.

Seraphina stood in front of the wardrobe while Mira examined two dresses.

"Perhaps."

"Then there is no reason to worry."

"That depends on whether the system is still connected."

Evelyne looked at her daughter.

"You are attending a briefing, not an inspection."

"I know."

"Then you do not need to solve anything."

Seraphina turned.

"That is what I intend to do."

Mira held up a dark blue dress.

"Your Ladyship, this one?"

Seraphina examined it.

"Is it comfortable?"

"Yes."

"Can I sit through a long meeting in it?"

"Certainly."

"Then that one."

Evelyne smiled.

"You have developed very practical standards for royal occasions."

"I have developed practical standards for everything."

"Your father will be pleased to hear that."

Seraphina suspected her father would be more pleased if she did not ask how many protective networks the palace possessed.

Unfortunately, she already knew the answer.

Three.

And the third one was apparently a mystery.

The royal capital looked different in winter.

The streets were crowded with merchants selling hot food, woollen clothing, and small household supplies. Workers cleared snow from the main roads. Carriages moved slowly through the central district, while guards directed traffic around a section of the western avenue undergoing repairs.

Seraphina watched the streets through the carriage window.

The capital's magical lighting remained steady.

Small blue lamps illuminated the covered walkways.

A public communication marker near the eastern square flashed twice, then settled.

Nothing appeared unusual.

Alaric sat opposite her.

"The King has asked for an initial briefing," he said. "The technical discussion will follow."

"Who is attending?"

"King Edmund, the Crown Prince, Minister Garrick, the head of the Royal Infrastructure Department, Lucien Arkwright, and several technical officers."

"Will the Academy present its findings?"

"Yes."

"Then I expect they will answer most questions."

Alaric glanced at her.

"You have become very confident in other people's competence."

"I have always preferred it."

He gave a quiet laugh.

The carriage entered the palace grounds.

King Edmund III received them in a smaller council chamber.

Queen Adelaide was present, along with Crown Prince Theodore.

Minister Garrick stood beside a table covered in maps.

Lucien had several folders.

A man Seraphina did not recognize was speaking with the Director of the Royal Infrastructure Department.

He was introduced as Master Alistair Fenwick, Director of the Technical Standards Directorate.

Seraphina greeted him politely.

The King gestured toward the chairs.

"Please sit. We have a matter that requires careful examination."

Seraphina sat beside her father.

Theodore stood near the table.

"Before we begin," he said, "I want to make one distinction. This is not an emergency meeting. The capital network is functioning normally. We are examining an uncertainty in its historical construction records."

Seraphina nodded.

That was a useful opening.

The King looked at Lucien.

"Please explain."

Lucien placed a map on the table.

It showed the capital's protective infrastructure.

The outer network formed a broad ring around the city.

The inner distribution system extended through the central districts.

A third set of lines appeared beneath them, marked in a different colour.

"This is the historical control network," Lucien explained. "It was constructed before the current protective system."

He pointed to a central structure beneath the royal district.

"Originally, this network regulated the distribution of mana between several civic circuits. It was not a source of energy. It controlled the flow through existing conduits."

Seraphina looked at the map.

"Like a distribution station?"

"Yes. A regulating structure."

"Was it replaced?"

"Partially."

"Partially?"

Lucien pointed to three sections.

"The outer control channels were disconnected during the first modernization. The inner channels were modified during the second. Some of the old conduits were retained because they were structurally sound."

Seraphina examined the lines.

"And the third network?"

Lucien hesitated.

"It is not a separate network in the modern sense. It is a historical control arrangement that may still influence parts of the newer system."

Theodore spoke.

"The problem is that the final commissioning record for the inner distribution network cannot be found."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Does the Academy know whether the old controls were fully removed?"

"Not yet."

"Does the Department know?"

"No."

"Does the Directorate know?"

Master Fenwick answered.

"We know what the approved construction plans required. We do not yet know whether every stage was completed exactly as specified."

Seraphina nodded.

That was the first important distinction.

A plan could describe what should have happened.

An inspection report could describe what someone had seen.

A commissioning certificate could confirm that the completed system had been tested and accepted.

They were not interchangeable.

She looked at the map again.

"How old is the original network?"

"Approximately eighty years," Lucien said.

"And the modern system?"

"The first major replacement began thirty-two years ago. The second stage followed twelve years later."

"Then there should be records from three different periods."

Theodore nodded.

"There are."

"Are they complete?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at the King.

"Then I assume the purpose of this meeting is to establish which records are missing and who is responsible for finding them."

King Edmund smiled faintly.

"That is an excellent starting point."

Seraphina had not intended to say anything particularly impressive.

She was simply relieved that nobody had asked her to explain mana regulation.

The first technical presentation lasted forty minutes.

Lucien explained the original design.

Master Fenwick explained the modern standards.

The Department presented its construction records.

The Academy showed the surviving inspection reports.

The main historical control chamber had been built beneath the central administrative district.

It had originally served five major circuits.

One supported the royal district's protective barrier.

One supported the central communication network.

One supplied the principal civic lighting system.

One served the eastern public buildings.

The fifth was connected to the old inner distribution network.

Over time, the circuits had been separated and modernized.

The outer protective barrier had received an independent regulator.

The communication network had been rebuilt.

The civic lighting system had been divided into smaller sections.

The eastern public buildings had received their own control structure.

But the old central chamber had remained in use during the transition.

The problem was not that the chamber had been forgotten.

It was that the records did not clearly establish what had happened to every one of its original connections.

"How many are still connected?" Theodore asked.

Lucien answered carefully.

"We have confirmed that two historical conduits remain physically present. Their operational status is not established."

"Could they be active?"

"They could carry residual mana under certain conditions. We have not confirmed that they are connected to an active circuit."

Seraphina looked at the map.

"Could they simply be sealed conduits?"

"Yes."

"Could they be connected to the newer distribution system?"

"That is one possibility."

"Could they be disconnected at the chamber but remain connected elsewhere?"

Lucien nodded.

"Also possible."

Seraphina leaned back.

The meeting was becoming more complicated.

But at least the Academy was not pretending to know more than it did.

Minister Garrick opened a financial folder.

"The original modernization programme was funded through three separate allocations."

He placed the documents on the table.

The first covered structural modifications.

The second covered equipment replacement.

The third covered commissioning and final acceptance.

The Treasury had records of the first two.

The third allocation had been partially reconciled, but the final expenditure schedule was missing.

Seraphina examined the figures.

"Was the money paid?"

"Some of it."

"To whom?"

"Several contractors and technical suppliers."

"Was the final stage completed?"

"We cannot establish that from the financial records alone."

She nodded.

"Then the Treasury cannot confirm the technical completion."

"Correct."

"Can the Academy confirm it?"

Lucien shook his head.

"Not without the commissioning record or an equivalent technical examination."

Seraphina looked at the King.

"The departments are answering different questions."

King Edmund nodded.

"Explain."

"The Treasury can confirm expenditure. The Department can confirm authorized construction and recorded transfers. The Academy can assess the technical condition. The Directorate can establish whether the completed system meets current standards."

She looked at the papers.

"None of those answers alone proves that the whole system was properly commissioned."

There was a short silence.

Master Fenwick nodded.

"That is correct."

Theodore looked at Seraphina.

"Then we need a consolidated review."

"Yes."

Lucien appeared relieved.

He had probably expected someone to suggest that the Academy simply activate the old chamber and see what happened.

Seraphina was glad nobody had suggested it.

The King asked the Department to present its proposed review structure.

The plan divided the work into four parts.

First, the original construction and modernization records would be assembled.

Second, the current physical condition of the old chamber and its conduits would be examined.

Third, the modern circuits would be tested independently.

Fourth, the results would be compared before any changes were authorized.

The proposal also established restrictions.

No historical conduit could be activated without written technical authorization.

No modern circuit could be disconnected merely to test a historical connection.

No equipment could be removed from the old chamber without an inventory.

Any structural access would require approval from the property authority.

The review would not authorize repairs or reconstruction.

Those decisions would require separate technical and financial approval.

Seraphina read the proposal.

It was considerably better than the original winter plan.

"Who coordinates the records?" she asked.

The Department's director answered.

"Adrian Holt."

Seraphina nodded.

She remembered the name.

He had coordinated earlier regional infrastructure work.

"Who coordinates the technical examination?"

"Lucien Arkwright, under the Academy's authority."

"And the physical access?"

"The Royal Infrastructure Department, with the royal property office."

"Who confirms the expenditure records?"

"Minister Garrick's office."

Seraphina returned the proposal.

"That seems clear."

The King studied her.

"You have no objections?"

"Not to the division of responsibility."

"And the review itself?"

"It should proceed."

Theodore smiled slightly.

Seraphina added, "Provided the technical team does not treat missing documents as proof of missing work."

The smile disappeared from his face.

Then he nodded.

"That is an important qualification."

The meeting concluded with a decision to begin the review.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would establish a records register.

The Academy would prepare a technical inspection plan.

The Treasury would identify the original allocations and their supporting accounts.

The royal property office would confirm access rights.

A separate team would examine the old chamber's physical condition.

Seraphina was not assigned a role.

She noticed this.

She appreciated it.

Then King Edmund spoke.

"Lady Seraphina."

She looked up.

"Your Majesty?"

"You have experience distinguishing between historical records, administrative summaries, and completed work. Would you be willing to examine the proposed records register once it has been prepared?"

Seraphina paused.

That was a narrower request than she had expected.

"What would the review involve?"

"Checking whether the register distinguishes the source of each entry and whether uncertain information is clearly identified."

"Would I be responsible for verifying the technical findings?"

"No."

"Or confirming the expenditure?"

"No."

"Or coordinating the departments?"

The King smiled.

"Certainly not."

Seraphina looked at Theodore.

He appeared to be trying not to smile.

She considered the request.

A records register could be checked without becoming responsible for the infrastructure itself.

But she wanted the boundaries in writing.

"I can review one draft, Your Majesty. I would not supervise its preparation or certify its contents."

"Agreed."

Theodore made a note.

"One draft," he repeated.

Seraphina nodded.

That was acceptable.

After the meeting, Queen Adelaide invited Seraphina to remain for tea.

Alaric was speaking with the King.

Theodore had been called away by Minister Garrick.

Lucien was already discussing the next inspection with Master Fenwick.

Seraphina sat beside the Queen.

"How do you find the capital this winter?" Adelaide asked.

"Busy."

"That is not an answer."

"It is an accurate one."

The Queen laughed.

"Your mother tells me you prefer accurate answers."

"My mother tells people many things."

"She also tells me you dislike unnecessary attention."

Seraphina looked at her.

"That is accurate too."

Adelaide poured tea.

"Then I will not ask you to attend any more meetings than necessary."

Seraphina accepted the cup.

"Thank you, Your Majesty."

The Queen glanced toward the technical chamber.

"They are worried."

"About the old network?"

"About what they might find."

Seraphina considered this.

"Do they have reason to believe it is failing?"

"No. The capital's protective system is functioning normally."

"Then what worries them?"

Adelaide looked toward the window.

"The possibility that something important was never properly documented. People become uncomfortable when they cannot distinguish between what was completed and what was merely intended."

Seraphina took a sip of tea.

"That is understandable."

The Queen smiled.

"You sound like your mother."

Seraphina was not sure whether this was a compliment.

She decided to accept it as one.

The first physical inspection was scheduled for the following week.

The old chamber was located beneath the eastern side of the royal administrative district.

Its entrance had been sealed during the second modernization.

The property office confirmed that the chamber remained Crown property.

The Academy would lead the technical examination.

The Department would provide structural engineers.

A royal maintenance team would manage access.

The inspection would begin with the entrance and surrounding foundations.

No internal equipment would be touched until the chamber had been assessed for structural and magical safety.

Lucien sent Seraphina a copy of the preliminary access report.

The entrance was stable.

The surrounding masonry showed minor age-related deterioration.

The old ventilation shaft had been partially blocked.

There was no evidence of recent structural movement.

The chamber remained sealed.

Seraphina read the report.

Then she noticed a line in the historical description.

**Access restriction: temporary, pending completion of internal control replacement.**

She looked at the date.

Thirty-two years earlier.

She read the next paragraph.

The access restriction had been renewed twelve years later.

The second renewal referred to the inner distribution modernization.

The final record stated:

*Access restriction to remain in force until commissioning and final technical acceptance.*

There was no subsequent entry.

Seraphina put down the report.

She sent a note to Lucien.

*The access restriction appears to have been tied to final commissioning. Has the royal property office confirmed whether a later release was issued?*

His reply arrived that afternoon.

*No release order has been located. The chamber was treated as permanently restricted after the second modernization.*

Seraphina read the answer.

That did not prove anything was wrong.

But it raised a practical question.

If the chamber had been intended to remain sealed until commissioning, had commissioning ever been formally completed?

Or had the restriction simply become permanent because nobody remembered why it existed?

She sent another note.

*Please retain the original restriction and renewal records in the register. Do not describe the chamber as formally decommissioned unless a separate order is found.*

Lucien replied:

*Agreed.*

The inspection took place on Thursday.

Seraphina did not attend.

She received the report the following morning.

The outer chamber was structurally sound.

The ventilation shaft required cleaning before prolonged access.

The entrance seal was intact.

The Academy detected a very low residual mana reading near the chamber's southern wall.

The reading was not sufficient to establish an active connection.

The technical team recommended a second survey after the ventilation work.

The report also identified two conduits entering the chamber.

One had been sealed with a modern isolation plate.

The other had an older closure.

The closure was intact, but its original installation date could not be confirmed.

The Academy did not open either conduit.

Seraphina read the report carefully.

There was no failure.

No sudden surge.

No dangerous discovery.

Just an old chamber, two conduits, and a question about whether the original system had been fully disconnected.

She placed the report beside her tea.

Then she noticed the inspection diagram.

The southern conduit was marked **C-2**.

The northern conduit was marked **C-1**.

The old construction map used the same labels.

But the modern renovation plan had reversed them.

Seraphina looked again.

She was not an engineer.

She could not determine whether the labels were wrong.

But she could see that the documents disagreed.

She sent the diagram to Lucien with one question.

*Are the C-1 and C-2 labels consistent across the original and modern plans?*

His reply came an hour later.

*No. The second modernization reversed the labels in its working drawings. We are checking whether the physical identifiers were changed as well.*

Seraphina leaned back.

There it was.

Not a catastrophe.

Not proof of a hidden connection.

A discrepancy in the naming of two conduits.

The sort of discrepancy that could be harmless.

Or the sort that could make an otherwise competent team examine the wrong pipe.

She wrote back.

*Please confirm the physical identifiers before the next inspection.*

Lucien replied:

*Already requested.*

That evening, Alaric joined her in the library.

He had received the inspection summary.

"The chamber appears stable," he said.

"Yes."

"Lucien says the team is checking the conduit labels."

"Yes."

"Did you notice the discrepancy?"

"In the plans."

Alaric nodded.

"Good catch."

Seraphina closed her book.

"I did not catch anything technical. I noticed two documents used different labels."

"That is still useful."

She considered that.

Perhaps it was.

Alaric sat opposite her.

"The King asked whether you would be willing to attend the next briefing."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why?"

"He wants the records register reviewed before the next technical stage."

"That is the one draft I agreed to examine."

"Yes."

"Then I will examine it when it is ready."

Alaric smiled.

"I told him you would say that."

Seraphina returned to her book.

The old chamber had not been opened.

The conduits had not been activated.

The protective network was functioning.

The technical team had found no immediate danger.

For now, the kingdom's capital remained protected.

But the original network had been built eighty years earlier, modified twice, and partially incorporated into a newer system.

Some of its records had survived.

Some had not.

And the people responsible for maintaining it were beginning to discover that the difference between an old system and a properly retired system was not always as obvious as they had assumed.

Seraphina turned a page.

She hoped the next report would contain fewer questions.

It did not.

The following morning, Lucien sent a message.

The physical conduit labels had been checked.

The modern isolation plate was marked C-1.

The older closure was marked C-2.

According to the original construction map, C-2 had once connected to the inner distribution network.

According to the second modernization plan, C-2 had been designated for removal.

But the removal certificate was missing.

The inspection team had not opened it.

Lucien's final sentence was brief.

*We have not established whether C-2 was physically removed, permanently sealed, or retained as a dormant conduit.*

Seraphina read it twice.

Then she folded the letter.

"Tea," she said.

Mira looked up.

"Now?"

"Yes."

"Anything else?"

Seraphina glanced at the letter.

"Please tell Cedric that I will be in the library this afternoon."

"Shall I tell him why?"

"No."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina returned to her breakfast.

She had agreed to review one register.

She had not agreed to become the keeper of every old pipe in the kingdom.

And she intended to maintain that distinction.
