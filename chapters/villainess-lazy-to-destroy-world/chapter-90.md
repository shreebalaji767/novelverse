# **Chapter 90 — The Inspection That Had Been Postponed Until Spring**

The old drainage passage was not magical.

Seraphina considered this an encouraging development.

It was also not particularly mysterious.

The northern storage building stood on a slight rise above the lower agricultural fields. Its western roof had begun to sag, the northern wall required repointing, and the yard sloped toward a stone outlet that had been blocked for longer than anyone currently employed at the estate could remember.

The passage beneath the wall was part of an ordinary drainage arrangement.

At least, that was what the first inspection suggested.

Cedric had arranged for the estate's senior engineer to examine the structure.

Master Hobb, who had spent more than forty years repairing bridges, walls and drainage channels, had gone with two assistants. They had inspected the accessible stonework, measured the yard's slope and examined the blocked outlet.

They had not entered the passage.

The entrance was too narrow, the air inside had not been tested, and the stone cover was partially supported by deteriorated masonry.

Master Hobb had returned with a short report.

Seraphina read it at breakfast.

"His recommendation is to leave it alone until the ground is dry," she said.

Cedric sat opposite her.

"That is what I expected."

"Then why did you ask?"

"Because I wanted to know whether there was a reason to clear it immediately."

"There isn't."

"The western field has been holding water."

"That is a separate problem."

Cedric nodded.

The report identified two possible causes of the standing water.

The first was the blocked outlet.

The second was a shallow depression in the field beyond the storage yard.

Clearing the passage might improve drainage, but it would not necessarily correct the field's slope.

The estate engineer recommended a surface survey before any excavation.

Seraphina placed the report down.

"Do that first."

Cedric made a note.

"And the old work order?"

"The archive is searching."

"Good."

He looked at the report.

"Master Hobb also wants the storage building's roof repaired."

"That is reasonable."

"He estimates the work will cost more than the original maintenance allowance."

"Then ask him for a separate estimate."

Cedric looked faintly amused.

"That is exactly what he has requested."

"Then he has saved us a conversation."

The capital network review continued without Seraphina.

The Academy had begun examining the installation histories of the remaining modern protective zones.

The review was limited to the capital's protective infrastructure.

It was not an invitation to inspect every magical structure in the kingdom.

That distinction had been written into the Council's instructions after the King had expressed concern that a focused review might expand into a general administrative exercise.

The Directorate identified three categories of installations requiring examination.

First, installations where the final physical arrangement differed from the approved drawing.

Second, installations where the commissioning record did not clearly distinguish initial testing from formal technical acceptance.

Third, installations where routine maintenance had replaced or altered a component without a corresponding update to the technical register.

The review would begin with the eastern and central zones.

The eastern circuit's corrective work had demonstrated why the categories mattered.

A system could be operating within its approved range while the records failed to describe its actual physical arrangement.

That did not mean every difference represented a dangerous defect.

It meant the difference needed to be understood.

Lucien supported the review.

Master Fenwick supported it with conditions.

Master Harlan supported it after requesting that the maintenance teams not be required to complete three different inspection forms for the same component.

The request was accepted.

A single revised form would be introduced.

It would record:

- The approved design.
- The actual installed arrangement.
- Any authorized variation.
- The measured condition of critical components.
- The date and result of the latest technical inspection.
- Any outstanding confirmation.

The Directorate also agreed to distinguish between work completed, work inspected and work formally accepted.

Seraphina received the revised format for administrative comment.

She made only two corrections.

The first concerned the wording of the acceptance field.

The second concerned the source of the information.

A maintenance worker should not be required to certify that an installation had been technically approved merely because the worker had inspected it.

Lucien accepted both changes.

The first review outside the eastern circuit concerned the central distribution zone.

It had been modernized twelve years earlier.

Unlike the eastern installation, its physical arrangement matched the approved drawings.

The internal components were within tolerance.

The maintenance records were complete.

There was, however, a minor discrepancy in the commissioning summary.

The summary described the zone as having passed its final operating assessment.

The underlying test records showed that the assessment had been completed under ordinary demand.

A separate elevated-demand test had been performed three weeks later.

The results were acceptable, but the summary did not reference the second test.

The Academy recommended correcting the register.

No repair was required.

The second zone produced a similar administrative discrepancy.

Its final commissioning records were complete, but one maintenance adjustment had been recorded under an outdated component reference.

The equipment itself was correctly identified.

The record was corrected.

By the end of the first week, the review had found two documentary errors and no physical defects requiring corrective work.

Lucien sent the results to Theodore.

The Crown Prince read the summary in his office.

"Then the review is finding what it was supposed to find."

"Some incomplete records," Lucien said.

"And some functioning installations."

"Yes."

"Good. I would prefer that the report contain both."

Lucien nodded.

Theodore looked through the pages.

"How long until the remaining zones are reviewed?"

"Several weeks."

"Will the reserve zone require a separate assessment?"

"Yes. Its balancing function was not adequately represented in the older central model. We need to confirm that the revised operating description matches the current installation."

"Can it be done without reducing its contribution?"

"Yes. We have already prepared the observation procedure."

"Then proceed."

Lucien gathered the documents.

Theodore stopped him.

"One more thing. Have the regional authorities been informed that the review is limited to the capital?"

"Yes."

"All of them?"

Lucien paused.

"I believe so."

Theodore raised an eyebrow.

Lucien sighed.

"I will confirm."

The confirmation revealed that one regional office had misunderstood the instruction.

The Western Infrastructure Authority had received a copy of the revised inspection form.

It had interpreted the form as a new national requirement.

The office had already begun compiling a list of magical structures across the western marches.

The list included military signal shelters, agricultural regulators, private estate conduits and two abandoned water mills.

The Directorate had not requested this work.

The western office had also asked three landowners to provide access to private installations.

One of them had refused.

The refusal had been polite but firm.

Theodore was not pleased.

"Why was the form sent without an accompanying instruction?"

The Department representative looked uncomfortable.

"It was included in the technical circular."

"The circular concerned the capital network."

"Yes."

"Then why was it distributed as a general maintenance form?"

The representative examined the document.

"The covering letter described it as a revised inspection standard."

"Was that accurate?"

"No, Your Highness."

Theodore leaned back.

"Then correct it."

The Department issued a clarification.

The revised form was to be used for the capital protective network review only.

Regional authorities were not required to conduct additional inspections unless a separate technical instruction was issued.

The western office withdrew its requests.

The landowners were informed.

No penalties or access demands were made.

Seraphina received a copy of the clarification because her earlier comments had been attached to the revised form.

She read the opening paragraph.

Then she looked at the final sentence.

*The distribution of the document does not create an obligation to conduct inspections beyond the scope specified in the original instruction.*

She approved the wording.

Mira, who was sorting the morning correspondence, glanced at the page.

"Did someone misunderstand the form?"

"Yes."

"Did you write it?"

"No."

"Then why are you correcting it?"

"Because someone else misunderstood what it was for."

Mira considered that.

"That sounds tiring."

"It is."

The northern storage archive finally located the original drainage work order.

It had been filed under the old agricultural property number rather than the building's current name.

The archive clerk sent Cedric a copy.

The document was dated thirty-one years earlier.

It recorded the clearing of the eastern outlet, replacement of two damaged stone covers and repair of the drainage channel near the storage yard.

The work had been performed by a local mason.

The final entry read:

*Passage cleared. Water flow restored. Stone covers replaced.*

The inspection field was blank.

Cedric brought the copy to Seraphina.

"At least we know what the earlier work involved."

"Yes."

"The mason's name is listed."

"Is he still alive?"

"No. But his son runs a masonry workshop in the village."

"That might be useful if we need to identify the construction method."

Cedric nodded.

He looked at the final line.

"The work was completed."

"The record says so."

"But it wasn't inspected."

"Not according to this document."

Cedric placed the page on the table.

"Would you call that a problem?"

"Not yet."

He looked at her.

"Why not?"

"Because the present condition is already being inspected. We don't need to decide whether a thirty-one-year-old form was adequate before we know what the structure looks like now."

Cedric nodded slowly.

It was a sensible answer.

He had become more accustomed to receiving such answers from Seraphina.

He was also learning that she did not always want to investigate the past.

Sometimes she wanted to repair the present.

Master Hobb returned to the storage building two weeks later.

The surface survey confirmed that the western field's standing water was caused by both the blocked outlet and the shallow depression.

The drainage passage could be cleared, but doing so before the roof and entrance masonry were repaired would be unsafe.

The stone cover needed temporary support.

The passage required ventilation.

The outlet needed to be opened from the exterior before anyone attempted internal inspection.

Master Hobb recommended a staged repair.

First, stabilize the entrance.

Second, clear the exterior outlet.

Third, test the passage's airflow.

Fourth, inspect the interior using appropriate safety precautions.

Fifth, repair the channel if necessary.

The work would begin after the ground dried.

Cedric approved the sequence.

Seraphina approved the budget.

The storage building's roof would be repaired separately.

No one requested an Academy inspection.

No one suggested that the passage concealed a magical facility.

The estate engineer had found a drainage problem.

He intended to repair it.

Seraphina considered this an excellent outcome.

The capital's technical review reached the reserve zone during the same period.

The Academy's observations confirmed that the reserve zone performed two related functions.

It maintained the approved balancing contribution during ordinary operation.

It also compensated for differences between eastern and western distribution under elevated demand.

The old central model had described the zone as transitional.

The current technical assessment described it as an active balancing component.

The revised model included the scheduled adjustment interval and the conditions under which the reserve contribution could be reduced.

The earlier six-year-old test was also added to the technical history.

Its results were not treated as proof of failure.

They were recorded as evidence that the eastern circuit required reserve compensation under sustained demand until a later assessment established otherwise.

The four-year-old operating schedule amendment was retained in the history.

It was not erased.

Instead, the register explained that the amendment had relied on a technical summary that did not contain the complete seasonal assessment.

The new operating procedure restored the appropriate restriction.

Lucien reviewed the document.

"That is better."

Master Fenwick examined the revised diagram.

"Does it reflect the actual operating arrangement?"

"Yes."

"Has it been checked by the maintenance team?"

"Yes."

"Has it been checked by the Directorate?"

"Yes."

"Then issue it."

The document was approved.

For the first time, the reserve zone's actual function, maintenance procedure and operating limitations appeared together in one current technical record.

It was not a dramatic achievement.

No one applauded.

The Academy's administrative clerk complained that the new diagram was difficult to fold.

Lucien told him to stop folding it.

The remaining review continued.

The southern protective zone had complete installation records but an outdated maintenance schedule.

The northern zone had an incomplete equipment inventory, although the physical components matched the current design.

The western zone had one replacement component recorded under the supplier's catalogue number rather than the Academy's internal reference.

Each discrepancy was corrected.

No additional physical defects were identified.

The Directorate did not declare the entire network perfect.

It declared that the reviewed installations had been compared against their approved designs and that identified discrepancies had been recorded and addressed according to their significance.

The distinction mattered.

The capital's protective network had been functioning.

The review had not discovered a hidden catastrophe.

It had discovered that the documentation supporting a complex system was less complete than the officials had assumed.

That was enough to justify the work.

At the end of the month, the King requested a final briefing on the first stage of the review.

Seraphina attended because Theodore had asked her to examine the administrative summary.

She sat beside Duchess Evelyne, who had accompanied Alaric to the capital for a separate meeting with the northern council.

The King listened to the technical report.

Lucien described the eastern circuit correction.

Master Fenwick summarized the remaining installation reviews.

Master Harlan explained the revised maintenance forms.

Theodore presented the updated register.

The King asked whether the network was safe.

Lucien answered carefully.

"The reviewed zones are operating within their approved limits. The eastern circuit has passed its corrective assessment. The reserve zone has been restored to its documented operating function. No immediate safety defect has been identified in the remaining reviewed installations."

"Are there further inspections?"

"Yes. The next stage will examine the historical relationship between the original protective network and the modern distribution system."

"Does that mean the old network is still connected?"

"No, Your Majesty. The historical conduits previously identified have been confirmed inactive or removed. The next stage concerns the documentation of how the modern system was designed around the earlier infrastructure."

The King nodded.

"And how long?"

"Several months."

The King looked toward Theodore.

"Then we will receive a progress report every month."

Theodore agreed.

The King turned to Seraphina.

"Lady Veyr. Does the administrative record reflect the work completed?"

"Mostly, Your Majesty."

The room became attentive.

Seraphina looked at the summary.

"The report accurately distinguishes the physical inspections, corrective work and revised operating instructions. However, the expenditure summary describes the eastern circuit work as a repair. It should also identify the undocumented installation variation that made the correction necessary."

The Treasury representative shifted in his seat.

The King looked at him.

"Can that be amended?"

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Then amend it."

Seraphina closed the document.

That was all she had to say.

After the briefing, Evelyne found her daughter in the palace corridor.

"You spoke very little."

"I was asked to review a summary."

"That has never prevented you from speaking more."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Are you criticizing my efficiency?"

"I am observing it."

They walked toward the waiting carriage.

Evelyne glanced at the report under Seraphina's arm.

"Has the eastern circuit been repaired?"

"Yes."

"Was it dangerous?"

"Not according to the current assessment."

"And the remaining network?"

"Still under review."

Evelyne nodded.

"Then perhaps you should return home."

"That is my intention."

"Your father has already asked whether you will be attending the next Council session."

"No."

"I told him that."

"Thank you."

Evelyne smiled faintly.

"He seemed disappointed."

"He can be disappointed in the comfort of his own office."

"That is precisely what I suggested."

They reached the carriage.

Before Seraphina climbed in, Evelyne touched her arm.

"You did well."

Seraphina paused.

She was not entirely sure what to say.

So she settled for the truth.

"I corrected one sentence."

"Sometimes that is enough."

The following morning, Seraphina returned to the Veyr estate.

Cedric met her in the entrance hall.

"The northern storage building's exterior outlet has been cleared."

"Any damage?"

"Two stone covers need replacing. The drainage passage is still closed."

"Good."

"Master Hobb says the airflow test can be performed after the new covers are installed."

"Then let him proceed."

Cedric handed her another report.

It concerned the western field.

The surface survey had identified an old channel running along the lower boundary.

It had been partially filled with soil during an earlier cultivation project.

The channel appeared on a map from forty-seven years ago.

The current field plan showed it as a natural depression.

Seraphina looked at the drawing.

"Who filled it?"

"We don't know."

"Was it deliberately closed?"

"The surveyor cannot determine that yet."

"Then don't describe it as a filled drainage channel in the work order. Call it an unidentified surface feature pending inspection."

Cedric smiled slightly.

"You are becoming predictable."

"I am becoming tired."

He laughed.

Seraphina returned the report.

"Have the surveyor compare the old map with the current field measurements. No excavation until Master Hobb has assessed the soil."

Cedric nodded.

As he left, Seraphina looked again at the drawing.

The old channel was not particularly unusual.

Agricultural land changed.

Drainage arrangements were modified.

Fields were divided, roads moved and boundaries adjusted.

A line on an old map was not proof that a structure remained beneath the soil.

She had learned not to treat every incomplete record as a mystery.

Some were simply incomplete records.

That evening, a messenger arrived from the capital.

He carried a sealed letter from Theodore.

The message was brief.

The second-stage review had begun.

The Academy had recovered a historical design schedule from the original capital protection programme.

The schedule listed five control points.

Four were identified in the surviving modern design records.

The fifth appeared under a different reference number.

The Directorate had not yet established whether it referred to a separate physical installation or an earlier name for one of the four confirmed points.

The letter did not suggest that the fifth control point was active.

It did not suggest that the current network was unsafe.

It merely requested that the historical record be compared with the original construction maps.

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she read it again.

Mira waited beside the desk.

"Is there another problem?"

"Not yet."

"That sounds like a problem waiting to happen."

"It sounds like an old design schedule."

Seraphina folded the letter.

She had no appointment in the capital next week.

She had no intention of accepting another advisory position.

And she had no reason to believe that the fifth control point represented anything more than an outdated reference.

Still, she placed the letter in the correspondence folder rather than discarding it.

Outside, the northern wind moved across the estate grounds.

The storage building's roof was scheduled for repair.

The drainage passage remained closed.

The western field still held water.

And in the capital, the engineers had begun comparing a list of five control points with a network that officially contained four.

For now, the discrepancy existed only on paper.
