# Chapter 28 — The Deliveries That Arrived on Paper

Seraphina had developed a particular dislike for the phrase *marked as received*.

It was not an unreasonable phrase.

Goods were delivered. Someone accepted them. A record was made.

The difficulty arose when the record existed but the goods did not.

She read Liora's letter again, paying particular attention to the final paragraph.

The southern charitable infirmary had reported that several medical supply deliveries appeared in the Church's central distribution records. The local infirmary, however, had no corresponding receiving certificates.

The supplies included bandage cloth, antiseptic preparations, clean linen and basic surgical instruments.

There were no claims of theft.

There was no confirmed loss.

The regional office had simply been unable to reconcile the records.

Seraphina put the letter down.

"How many deliveries?" she asked.

Mira, who was arranging the correspondence tray, checked the accompanying note.

"Six."

"Over what period?"

"Four months."

"From the same supplier?"

"Two suppliers."

Seraphina considered this.

"Then it may not be one problem."

"Would you like me to ask the Church for more information?"

"No. Liora has already asked its regional office to investigate. We should not interfere with an inquiry simply because we have heard about it."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina returned to her correspondence.

Then she stopped.

"Actually, send a reply."

"What should it say?"

"Thank her for informing us. Tell her that we have no direct knowledge of the southern deliveries, but we can share our experience with receiving records if the regional office requests assistance."

Mira wrote it down.

"Anything else?"

"Yes. Ask whether the Church has confirmed that the supplies were dispatched."

Mira looked up.

"Is that important?"

"Very."

Seraphina leaned back.

A delivery recorded as received could have several explanations.

Perhaps the supplier had dispatched the goods and the local office had failed to record them.

Perhaps the central office had marked the goods as received when they were only dispatched.

Perhaps the goods had arrived at a regional store but never reached the infirmary.

Perhaps the quantities had been divided among several destinations.

There were many possibilities.

The first question was not where the supplies had gone.

It was whether they had left the supplier at all.

Liora's response arrived three days later.

The Church had confirmed that all six deliveries had been entered into the central distribution register as completed.

However, the original dispatch documents were incomplete.

Four deliveries had supplier invoices.

Two had only summary entries.

The local infirmary had confirmed that it received none of the six consignments in their recorded quantities.

A regional storehouse had acknowledged receiving two smaller consignments, but the quantities did not match the central register.

Seraphina read the details.

There was another distinction.

The Church's central register recorded the deliveries as *fulfilled*.

It did not clearly state whether that meant dispatched, delivered to a regional store or accepted by the final destination.

Seraphina placed the letter beside Eleanor's infirmary ledger.

It was the same problem in a different form.

Different offices were using the same word for different events.

She sent a copy to Eleanor.

The infirmary supervisor arrived that afternoon.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Mistress Eleanor."

Eleanor examined the letter.

"Do they know where the supplies went?"

"No."

"Do they know whether they were sent?"

"Not for all six deliveries."

Eleanor turned another page.

"Then they should not be calling them completed."

"That is what I thought."

Eleanor looked at the list of supplies.

"These are ordinary medical goods. Nothing particularly difficult to obtain."

"Could they have been distributed through another store?"

"Certainly. But the receiving record should identify the destination."

"Would you be willing to review the Church's receiving format?"

Eleanor closed the letter.

"If the Church asks me to."

Seraphina smiled.

"I thought you might say that."

Eleanor returned the letter.

"Your Grace's estate has enough trouble keeping its own records consistent. I would rather not become responsible for the Church's."

Seraphina nodded.

"A reasonable boundary."

In the southern provinces, the problem was causing a different kind of frustration.

The charitable infirmary at Bellford was managed by Sister Agnes, a woman in her early sixties who had spent most of her life providing medical care to the surrounding villages.

She was not particularly interested in administrative disputes.

She was interested in having enough clean bandages.

The six missing deliveries had been expected to replenish the infirmary's emergency supplies before the winter season.

Instead, the shelves were nearly empty.

Sister Agnes stood in the storage room with a regional Church administrator named Brother Matthias.

"I am not asking for additional supplies," she said.

Matthias examined the inventory.

"I understand."

"I am asking where the supplies listed in your register went."

"The central office has recorded them as fulfilled."

"Then perhaps the central office should explain what fulfilled means."

Matthias sighed.

"The records are being examined."

"They have been examined for three weeks."

"We are waiting for the supplier confirmations."

"And while we wait?"

Matthias looked at the shelves.

Sister Agnes continued.

"We have enough bandages for ordinary treatment. We do not have enough to replenish the emergency stock. If there is an accident on the southern road, we will need to borrow supplies from another infirmary."

"That is being arranged."

"By whom?"

"The regional office."

"When?"

Matthias had no immediate answer.

Sister Agnes looked at him.

"I am not angry with you, Brother Matthias. But a promise that supplies are being arranged is not a supply."

He lowered his eyes.

"I know."

She softened slightly.

"Then help me find out what happened."

The first investigation meeting was held at the southern regional Church office.

Representatives from the central distribution department, two suppliers and the Bellford infirmary attended.

The central register was placed on the table.

It showed six deliveries.

The supplier representatives examined the entries.

One recognized the first four.

The remaining two had been entered under a joint purchasing arrangement.

"These four consignments were dispatched," the first supplier said.

"To Bellford?" asked Matthias.

"Not directly. They were sent to the southern regional store."

"Do you have the receiving certificates?"

The supplier produced copies.

The quantities were recorded.

The receiving signatures were visible.

Matthias examined them.

"These are not from Bellford."

"No," the supplier replied. "The storehouse accepted the deliveries."

"Then why does the central register say they were delivered to the infirmary?"

The central distribution clerk looked uncomfortable.

"The register uses the final destination field."

"But the goods were delivered to the regional store."

"Yes."

"Then why is the storehouse not recorded as the receiving location?"

The clerk turned several pages.

"I would need to check the original instructions."

The second supplier explained that its two consignments had been dispatched together with other medical goods.

The receiving record listed the combined shipment.

However, the individual quantities for Bellford were not separated.

The regional storehouse had acknowledged the combined delivery.

It had not confirmed the distribution to each destination.

By the end of the meeting, the six deliveries had become three different problems.

Four consignments had reached the regional storehouse but had been incorrectly described in the central register as delivered to Bellford.

Two consignments had been included in a larger shipment, but the allocation to Bellford had not been confirmed.

The supplies had not vanished.

But neither had the local infirmary received them.

The difference mattered.

A week later, Liora sent Seraphina a more detailed account.

The Church had confirmed that four consignments were still in the southern regional storehouse.

The goods had been accepted, inspected and entered into inventory.

They had not been released for distribution because the allocation instructions were incomplete.

The remaining two consignments had been received as part of a combined shipment. The storehouse inventory showed the total quantity, but no separate allocation had been recorded for Bellford.

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she looked at Eleanor.

"So the supplies are there."

"Some of them."

"And the others?"

"Included in a larger shipment."

"Which means they may also be there."

"Possibly."

Eleanor returned the letter.

"At least now we know where to look."

Seraphina nodded.

The Church had made a mistake.

But it was not necessarily a malicious one.

The central office had recorded the intended destination rather than the actual receiving location. The storehouse had accepted the goods but had not completed the allocation. The local infirmary had been waiting for supplies that had never been released.

It was a chain of incomplete actions.

Each office had a record.

None of the records described the entire journey.

Seraphina wrote back to Liora.

She suggested that the Church distinguish between three stages:

- Dispatched by the supplier.
- Received by the regional storehouse.
- Delivered to the final infirmary.

She also recommended that the distribution register identify the person responsible for confirming each stage.

Liora replied that the Church intended to revise its forms.

She added that the Bellford infirmary would receive the four confirmed consignments first, while the remaining two would be reconciled against the combined shipment.

Seraphina was pleased.

Then she read the final paragraph.

The Church had discovered a similar discrepancy involving medical supplies in two other southern districts.

Those records were being examined separately.

Seraphina placed the letter down.

"How many districts?" she asked.

Mira checked.

"Three."

"How many deliveries?"

"Fourteen."

Seraphina stared at her.

"Why does the number keep increasing?"

Mira thought about it.

"Perhaps because they are finally counting them."

Seraphina looked at her.

"That is not comforting."

"No, my lady."

Meanwhile, the western conduit inspection was approaching.

The Academy had confirmed its technical team.

Lucien would lead the inspection, accompanied by Master Fenwick and two circuit specialists.

The estate would provide Cedric's appointed representative, the maintenance superintendent and two local workers.

Seraphina had no intention of attending the technical examination.

She had already sent the relevant records.

She had also made it clear that the Academy would be responsible for its own measurements and conclusions.

On the morning before the inspection, Cedric arrived with a final report.

"The Academy has accepted the access arrangements," he said.

"Good."

"They have also requested permission to examine the old maintenance station foundation."

"Does that require additional access?"

"Yes. It is on estate land."

Seraphina read the request.

The examination would involve measuring the foundation and comparing the remaining mounting points with the recovered bracket.

No excavation was proposed.

She approved the access.

"Make sure they understand that the approval is for examination only."

Cedric nodded.

"I will."

"Have they confirmed whether the conduit will remain in operation during the inspection?"

"Yes. Lucien says the circuit will be monitored continuously. No changes will be made."

"Good."

Cedric gathered the papers.

"That should be everything."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Are you certain?"

Cedric paused.

"Yes."

"Then I hope you have a pleasant day."

He smiled.

"Thank you, my lady."

The inspection began shortly after sunrise.

Lucien arrived with the Academy team and was met by the regional superintendent.

The access chamber was opened under the supervision of the estate workers.

The Academy specialists began by measuring the mana flow at several points along the conduit.

The readings were stable.

There were minor fluctuations, but nothing outside the expected range for an older system.

Lucien recorded the measurements.

Master Fenwick examined the junction.

"The regulating circuit is still functioning."

"Yes," Lucien replied.

"But the distribution is uneven."

"Within the expected limits."

Fenwick looked at the old maintenance map.

"That may explain why the original modification was necessary."

Lucien nodded.

They continued the examination.

The missing marker was not present.

The mounting position was visible, but the surrounding stone showed signs of later repair.

One of the specialists took measurements.

"The bracket could have been removed during the repair."

Lucien looked at him.

"Could have been?"

"Yes. The mounting holes are older than the surrounding mortar."

"Can you establish when the mortar was replaced?"

"Not precisely."

Lucien made a note.

They examined the access chamber again.

The conduit showed no immediate sign of structural failure.

The regulating circuit was operating.

The missing marker did not affect its function.

The Academy team completed its measurements and moved to the old maintenance station.

The foundation was overgrown, but the remaining stonework was intact.

Master Fenwick compared the measurements with the old drawing.

"The building was larger than the current access shelter."

"Yes," the superintendent replied.

Lucien examined the foundation.

"Could the old station have contained the regulating components?"

"According to the inventory, yes."

One specialist pointed toward a shallow recess in the western wall.

"Was that a storage niche?"

The superintendent looked closer.

"It may have been."

Lucien examined it.

The recess was narrow and deep enough to hold a small metal container.

There were no visible markings.

He took measurements.

Then he stepped back.

"We should compare this with the old inventory before drawing conclusions."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Agreed."

The inspection continued for another hour.

No hidden chamber was discovered.

No secret mechanism was found.

No dangerous magical disturbance occurred.

The Academy simply recorded what remained.

For Lucien, that was sufficient.

The report arrived at the estate four days later.

It confirmed that the western conduit was functioning within acceptable limits.

The junction showed evidence of an earlier modification.

The modification appeared to be stable, though the Academy could not confirm whether the final inspection had ever been completed.

The recovered bracket was consistent with the mounting arrangement at the former maintenance station.

The missing marker had not been located.

The foundation contained a possible storage recess, but its original purpose could not be established without further evidence.

The Academy recommended a second archival review before any additional physical investigation.

Seraphina read the report.

She was relieved.

There was no immediate emergency.

No reason to close the conduit.

No justification for an expensive repair.

The next step was administrative and historical, not magical.

She sent a copy to Alaric.

Then she wrote to Lucien, thanking him for the careful examination.

She also asked that the report distinguish between confirmed findings and possible explanations.

Lucien's reply was brief.

*That distinction is included in the final version.*

Seraphina smiled.

Good.

Two days later, Roland arrived with a different report.

The old procurement register had been found.

Veylan Works had received an initial payment for the western junction modification.

A second payment was listed in a separate ledger.

The second payment had been authorized.

But the entry did not identify the final recipient.

It referred to a transfer through the regional stores office.

Roland had also located a supplier invoice for regulating components.

The quantity matched the old maintenance inventory.

However, the regional stores register still had not been found.

Seraphina examined the documents.

"Does the second payment prove the contractor completed the work?"

"No."

"Does the invoice prove the components reached the maintenance station?"

"No."

"Does the inventory prove they were transferred to the regional stores?"

"It records a transfer instruction. We do not yet have the receiving confirmation."

She nodded.

"Then we still have the same gap."

Roland folded his hands.

"Yes."

Seraphina looked at the payment entry.

"Who authorized the second payment?"

"The regional finance officer."

"Do we have the signature?"

"Yes."

"Can it be authenticated?"

"Not yet. We have requested the original authorization register."

Seraphina placed the report down.

"Thank you, Roland."

He bowed.

"Is there anything else, my lady?"

"No. Continue with the archive search."

Roland hesitated.

"May I make a suggestion?"

"Of course."

"We should search the regional stores records by transfer reference, not by the contractor's name."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because the components may have been entered under a general stores category. If we search only for Veylan Works, we may miss the receiving record."

Seraphina smiled.

"That is an excellent suggestion."

Roland looked mildly surprised.

"I will proceed with it."

After he left, Seraphina opened her notebook.

She wrote:

*The contractor was paid.*

Then she paused.

She crossed it out.

The evidence showed that two payments had been authorized. It did not yet establish who ultimately received the money.

She wrote instead:

*Two payments were recorded as authorized. Final recipient not confirmed.*

It was a small distinction.

But it was an important one.

The next letter from Liora arrived a week later.

The Church had completed the first stage of its southern supply review.

The four consignments at the regional storehouse had been released to Bellford.

The two combined consignments had been reconciled against the larger shipment. The final quantities were confirmed, and the missing allocation records had been corrected.

The other two districts had also located several delayed deliveries.

There was no evidence of deliberate theft.

However, the review had revealed that the central distribution office had been using a form that combined supplier dispatch, regional receipt and final delivery into one entry.

The Church intended to replace it.

Liora ended the letter with a personal note.

*I am beginning to understand why you dislike vague records.*

Seraphina smiled.

She wrote back:

*I do not dislike vague records. I dislike when people use them to make decisions that require precise information.*

She considered the sentence.

Then she added:

*I am pleased the infirmaries have received their supplies. Please thank Sister Agnes for insisting on a proper answer.*

The following morning, Seraphina visited the estate infirmary.

Eleanor was checking the emergency stock.

"How are the new records?" Seraphina asked.

"Good."

"Any problems?"

"The purchasing office has started including the batch numbers."

"That is encouraging."

"They have also asked whether we can reduce the number of separate columns."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Why?"

"They say the form is too complicated."

Eleanor folded her arms.

"What did you tell them?"

"That I am willing to discuss the layout, but I will not remove the distinction between delivered and usable stock."

Seraphina nodded.

"Good."

Eleanor looked at her.

"Your Grace, may I ask something?"

"Of course."

"Why did you become interested in these records?"

Seraphina paused.

There was no easy answer.

"I noticed that the numbers did not agree."

"That is all?"

"At first."

Eleanor looked at her thoughtfully.

"Most people would have asked which number was correct."

Seraphina smiled faintly.

"I have learned that sometimes the more useful question is why there are three numbers in the first place."

Eleanor nodded.

"That is a better question."

They continued through the storage room.

The shelves were orderly.

The stock was counted.

The records were clear.

And nobody had to guess whether a bandage existed simply because it appeared in a purchasing ledger.

Seraphina considered this a respectable achievement.

That evening, she received a message from the southern regional office.

The Church had found an additional discrepancy.

It concerned a shipment of medical instruments intended for a rural infirmary.

The central register showed that the instruments had been delivered.

The regional storehouse register showed that they had been received.

But the infirmary had no record of collecting them.

Unlike the earlier deliveries, this shipment included a set of surgical instruments that had been specially ordered.

The regional office had asked the supplier to confirm whether the instruments were still under warranty.

Seraphina read the message.

She placed it beside Liora's letter.

The supply review was continuing.

It was not her responsibility.

She did not have the authority to investigate it.

And the Church had already established a process for doing so.

She wrote a short reply to Liora, wishing the regional office success.

Then she closed the correspondence.

For once, she did not ask for the next report.

She did not request copies.

She did not offer to examine the records.

She simply left the matter where it belonged.

Later that night, Mira entered the sitting room.

"My lady, the Duchess asks whether you will join her for breakfast tomorrow."

Seraphina looked up from her book.

"Does she have a reason?"

"She said she would like to discuss the charitable gathering."

Seraphina sighed.

"Of course."

Mira smiled.

"Would you like me to prepare the dress she selected?"

"Yes."

"Which one?"

"The least uncomfortable."

"I believe that is the blue one."

"Then the blue one."

Mira bowed and left.

Seraphina returned to her book.

The Academy had inspected the conduit.

The Church had corrected its supply records.

The estate had improved its own infirmary procedures.

The old junction investigation remained unresolved, but it had become more precise.

There were still missing documents.

There were still unanswered questions.

And somewhere in the archives, a regional stores ledger might contain the record they needed.

Seraphina turned a page.

She was not going to think about it tonight.

Not even if the missing ledger was probably somewhere in a building full of people who had spent twenty years insisting that somebody else had it.

She had a book.

She had a warm room.

And tomorrow, apparently, she had a charitable gathering.

She closed her eyes.

The kingdom could wait until breakfast.

**End of Chapter 28**
