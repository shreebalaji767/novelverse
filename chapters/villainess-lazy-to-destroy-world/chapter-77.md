# **Chapter 77 — The Reception Where Nobody Wanted to Discuss the Weather**

Seraphina had one month to prepare for the royal winter reception.

She considered this excessive.

The invitation had arrived with enough time for a person to arrange an entire wardrobe, consult a tailor, select suitable accessories, and perhaps reconsider several life choices.

Seraphina intended to do none of those things.

Unfortunately, her mother had other plans.

"The reception will be attended by the Crown Prince, the Royal Academy, the Royal Infrastructure Department, and representatives from several regional authorities," Duchess Evelyne explained.

Seraphina sat before her dressing table.

"Then they should discuss infrastructure."

"They may."

"Excellent. I can stay home."

Evelyne continued examining the fabric samples.

"You are the heir of House Veyr. Your presence is expected."

"Is my absence expected to cause an infrastructure failure?"

"No."

"Then I am pleased."

Evelyne smiled.

"Your presence may prevent a social failure."

Seraphina looked at her mother through the mirror.

"That sounds less important."

"It is not."

Evelyne selected a dark burgundy fabric.

Seraphina examined it.

"Is that necessary?"

"It is appropriate."

"That is not the same thing."

"Neither is being comfortable and being presentable."

Seraphina sighed.

The dress was chosen.

The accessories were chosen.

The tailor was summoned.

The royal reception had claimed its first victim.

The invitation list was considerably more interesting than the clothing arrangements.

The Crown had invited members of the Royal Council, the Royal Academy, the technical departments, several noble families, and representatives from the five regions.

The official description called it a winter gathering intended to encourage cooperation between regional institutions.

The unofficial description, according to Evelyne, was that the Crown wanted everyone to speak to one another without requiring a formal committee.

Seraphina was not convinced.

"Will Minister Garrick attend?"

"Yes."

"Lucien?"

"Yes."

"Master Darius Veylan?"

"His name appears on the list."

"Master Harlan?"

"Probably."

Seraphina looked at the invitation.

"And the Crown Prince?"

Evelyne raised an eyebrow.

"He is hosting."

"Then I suppose he will be there."

Her mother laughed.

Seraphina returned the invitation to the table.

There were also several noble families from the western and southern regions.

Some were associated with infrastructure projects.

Others had contributed to regional maintenance funds.

A few had no obvious connection to the winter regulation review.

The gathering was not a technical conference.

It was a royal reception.

That meant people would talk about technical matters while pretending they were discussing something else.

Seraphina disliked this arrangement.

The morning of the reception, Mira entered with a small box.

"Your Ladyship, the dress has arrived."

Seraphina looked up from her breakfast.

"Does it have to be worn today?"

Mira paused.

"Yes."

"That is unfortunate."

Mira opened the box.

The dress was dark burgundy, with a modest embroidered pattern along the sleeves.

It was elegant without being excessively decorated.

Seraphina examined it.

"At least it is not covered in flowers."

"Her Grace selected it."

"I know."

Mira placed the accessories beside it.

A pair of small earrings.

A simple necklace.

A matching hair ornament.

Seraphina looked at the collection.

"How long will this take?"

"Approximately forty minutes."

"Why?"

Mira looked at her.

"Because you have hair."

Seraphina considered this an unfair observation.

The royal reception was held in the western hall of the palace.

The room had been decorated with winter branches, silver lanterns, and pale blue fabric.

Large windows overlooked the palace gardens.

The floor was polished marble.

The chandeliers were expensive enough to make Seraphina wonder whether the kingdom had a separate budget for lighting things that were already visible.

Alaric and Evelyne entered with her.

The Duke greeted several northern nobles.

The Duchess was immediately surrounded by acquaintances.

Seraphina found herself beside a familiar column.

It was a useful position.

She could observe the room without appearing to avoid anyone.

She had barely settled when Crown Prince Theodore approached.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Your Highness."

He glanced toward her parents.

"Your family has arrived."

"Yes."

"How is the northern region?"

"Cold."

Theodore looked at her.

She looked back.

He smiled.

"I suppose that is accurate."

"Very."

He gestured toward the room.

"The Crown has invited representatives from several regions. The intention is to encourage cooperation on winter infrastructure."

"Will there be a formal discussion?"

"Not tonight."

"Then I am relieved."

Theodore's smile widened slightly.

"Minister Garrick has already complained that the reception may be less productive than a committee meeting."

"Perhaps he should have organized a committee meeting."

"He says the Crown specifically forbade it."

"An excellent decision."

Theodore laughed.

Then he lowered his voice.

"The Academy's technical review has attracted considerable attention."

Seraphina glanced at him.

"Because of the old operating manual?"

"Among other things."

"Has anything dangerous been discovered?"

"Nothing confirmed."

"Then I hope everyone remembers that."

Theodore nodded.

"That is one reason I wanted to speak with you."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because the Department has received several requests for immediate revisions to regional maintenance standards."

"Before the technical review is complete?"

"Yes."

"That seems premature."

"It does."

Theodore glanced toward Minister Garrick.

"The Treasury is concerned about the cost of replacing equipment that may not need replacement. The Academy is concerned about continuing to use standards that may be incomplete. The regional authorities are concerned about being given new instructions without additional funding."

Seraphina considered this.

"Those are three different problems."

"Exactly."

"Then they should not be combined into one decision."

Theodore nodded.

"I thought you might say that."

She looked at him.

"Have you asked the technical departments what they recommend?"

"Yes. Their answers differ."

"Then the disagreement is probably useful."

Theodore looked faintly amused.

"You have an unusual appreciation for disagreement."

"I appreciate people explaining why they disagree."

He nodded.

"That is rarer than it should be."

Minister Garrick joined them a few minutes later.

He was holding a glass of water and looked as though he had already been forced to discuss the budget with three people who had no intention of paying it.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Minister."

"I hope the north is recovering from the early snow."

"Mostly."

"And the military supply matter?"

"Still under investigation."

Garrick nodded.

"The Council has not asked me to become involved."

"That sounds fortunate."

He gave a short laugh.

Then he looked toward the Crown Prince.

"Your Highness, I was just explaining the financial implications of the proposed review."

Theodore sighed.

"I remember."

Garrick turned back to Seraphina.

"The Academy has recommended a comprehensive comparison of the historical and modern maintenance standards. The regional authorities have asked whether the Treasury will fund additional inspections."

"Will it?"

"Not yet."

"Then the inspections should be planned according to the available funding."

Garrick looked at her.

"That is precisely the difficulty. The technical departments want the work completed before the next winter. The Treasury wants an estimate before approving expenditure. The regional authorities want to know which facilities will be included before preparing the estimate."

Seraphina considered this.

"Then begin with the facilities already identified."

Garrick waited.

"Separate the confirmed technical concerns from the historical records review. Estimate the cost of each stage. Do not make every region responsible for every facility."

The Minister looked at Theodore.

Theodore nodded slowly.

"That may be workable."

Garrick considered the suggestion.

"The difficulty is that some facilities serve more than one region."

"Then identify those separately."

"And where the ownership is unclear?"

"Do not assign costs until the responsibility is established."

Garrick exhaled.

"That is annoyingly reasonable."

Seraphina looked at him.

"I am sorry."

"No, you are not."

She was not.

The next person to approach was Lucien.

He looked considerably more comfortable than the Treasury Minister.

He had apparently spent the first part of the reception explaining the old manual to a group of nobles who had asked whether it contained any useful spells.

Seraphina looked at him.

"Did you tell them?"

"That it was a technical maintenance manual."

"Did they understand?"

"One of them asked whether the balancing circuit could be used to warm a ballroom."

Seraphina nodded.

"That sounds like a nobleman."

Lucien laughed.

He introduced her to Master Darius Veylan, who was standing beside him.

Darius bowed.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Master Veylan."

"I have heard that your estate permitted the inspection of NTR-4."

"We permitted a surface survey."

"That distinction was included in the report."

"Good."

Darius smiled.

He was younger than Seraphina had expected, perhaps in his early thirties.

He carried himself like an engineer rather than a court official.

His attention was focused on the discussion.

"The Academy's review has been useful," he said. "We have discovered that several regional guides retained historical operating limits without preserving their original conditions."

"Yes. Lucien explained."

"We are now comparing those limits with current circuit measurements."

"Have you found a problem?"

"One circuit near the western marches has a worn balancing assembly. It remains stable, but the replacement requires compatibility testing."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then I hope the test is completed before anyone attempts to install the component."

Darius looked at her.

"It will be."

Lucien smiled.

"She has been particularly insistent about that."

"I am pleased to hear it."

Darius continued.

"There is another matter. The old technical review group recommended a revised maintenance procedure thirty-four years ago. The central standards office never formally acknowledged it."

"Does that mean the current guides are invalid?"

"No. The regional department had authority to issue them. But the absence of formal consolidation has allowed different regions to interpret the recommendation differently."

Seraphina considered this.

"Then the problem is not only the manual."

"No. It is also the way the instructions were distributed and adopted."

Lucien nodded.

"The Academy is preparing a comparison."

"Good."

Seraphina glanced toward the ballroom.

A group of nobles had gathered around a display table.

The table contained miniature models of old and modern mana circuits.

She looked back at Lucien.

"Are those working models?"

"Only demonstration models."

"Are they safe?"

"Yes."

"Are they expensive?"

Lucien smiled.

"Not particularly."

"Then I may look at them."

The models were arranged in three groups.

The first showed an old winter regulation circuit.

The second showed a modern replacement system.

The third demonstrated a balancing assembly.

Lucien explained the differences.

The old system used a central regulating chamber to distribute mana among several smaller circuits.

The modern system used independent regulators.

The old arrangement was easier to maintain when the entire network was functioning.

The newer design was more flexible because individual circuits could operate independently.

Seraphina examined the models.

"Why was the old system retained for so long?"

Darius answered.

"Because it was reliable under the conditions for which it had been designed. Replacing it required money, engineering work, and regional coordination."

"Was the replacement programme completed?"

"Not entirely."

"Why?"

Darius glanced at Lucien.

Lucien answered.

"Some regions replaced their systems earlier than others. Some facilities were abandoned. Others were adapted. The records are incomplete."

Seraphina looked at the model.

"So the kingdom has several generations of equipment operating under different standards."

"Yes."

"That sounds difficult."

"It is."

She examined the miniature balancing assembly.

"Could the modern system fail because someone uses an old component?"

Darius nodded.

"That is possible. Physical dimensions are not enough to establish compatibility."

"Then why are old components still in reserve?"

"Because some may remain useful for historical systems or approved replacements. But they must be inspected before use."

Seraphina nodded.

She did not need to understand the internal mechanism to understand the principle.

An old part was not automatically useful merely because it had been kept.

Lucien smiled.

"You are becoming familiar with the technical side."

"No."

He looked surprised.

"I am becoming familiar with the fact that I should not touch it."

Darius laughed.

The reception became more complicated after dinner.

The King and Queen arrived.

The room quieted.

King Edmund III greeted the assembled guests and spoke briefly about the importance of regional cooperation.

He did not mention any specific technical findings.

He praised the work of the Academy, the infrastructure departments, and the regional authorities.

He also thanked the noble families that had permitted inspections on their land.

Seraphina listened.

The King was careful.

He did not suggest that the old systems were dangerous.

He did not announce a new national programme.

He did not promise funding that had not been approved.

He simply stated that the kingdom needed accurate information before making decisions.

Seraphina approved.

Then the Crown Prince stepped forward.

He announced that the Royal Council would receive a preliminary report after the technical comparison was complete.

The report would separate immediate maintenance needs from long-term modernization proposals.

The Treasury would prepare a staged funding estimate.

The regional authorities would identify their own priorities.

The Academy would continue its technical assessment.

No new operating standard would be issued until the review was complete.

It was a sensible arrangement.

It was also a public announcement.

Seraphina could already imagine the number of people who would misunderstand it.

After the speech, Baroness Amelia Rosenthal approached Seraphina.

The baroness was an older woman from the eastern territories.

Her family owned several manufacturing estates and had contributed equipment to regional transport projects.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Baroness."

"I understand your family has an old regulation chamber on its land."

"Yes."

"Have you opened it?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because the Academy has not established that it is safe to enter."

The baroness looked thoughtful.

"That seems sensible."

Seraphina was relieved.

The baroness continued.

"My family has a similar structure beneath one of our old workshops. We have been told that it may still contain useful equipment."

"Has it been inspected?"

"Not properly."

"Then I would recommend asking the technical authority before allowing anyone to enter."

The baroness nodded.

"That is what I intend to do."

She glanced toward the model table.

"The old systems were built to last."

"Apparently."

"Some of the equipment is still functioning."

"Some of it may be."

The baroness smiled.

"You are cautious."

"I am not an engineer."

"That is probably why."

Seraphina considered the statement.

It was possible that being unqualified made her more cautious.

It was also possible that she had simply seen enough problems caused by people pretending to know things they did not.

The baroness continued.

"Would your estate be interested in sharing the inspection format?"

Seraphina blinked.

"The format?"

"Your estate's records are apparently quite clear. The Academy mentioned the distinction between confirmed findings, historical references, and unresolved questions."

Seraphina understood.

The estate's earlier administrative work had become known beyond the north.

She looked toward Lucien.

He appeared to be pretending not to listen.

"House Veyr can share the format," she said. "But it is not a technical inspection procedure."

"Of course."

"It only records what has been examined, who examined it, and what remains uncertain."

"That would still be useful."

Seraphina nodded.

The baroness thanked her.

It was a small request.

It did not require her to travel.

It did not make her responsible for another facility.

It could be handled through Cedric.

Seraphina decided she could tolerate it.

Later in the evening, Theodore approached her again.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Your Highness."

"I wanted to ask whether House Veyr would be willing to provide the administrative format to the Royal Infrastructure Department."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Is this a request?"

"It is."

"Not an appointment?"

"No."

"Not a committee position?"

"No."

"Not a permanent advisory role?"

Theodore smiled.

"No."

She considered him.

"Then House Veyr can provide the format."

"Thank you."

"With one condition."

"Which is?"

"The Department must not present it as a technical standard."

Theodore nodded.

"Agreed."

"It is a recordkeeping format. It does not determine whether a circuit is safe, whether equipment is compatible, or whether a facility should be restored."

"I understand."

"Good."

Theodore looked toward the other guests.

"The technical review has become more complicated than I expected."

Seraphina glanced at the models.

"That is usually what happens when people discover that an old system was not as simple as they believed."

Theodore smiled.

"Do you think the kingdom will manage it?"

"I think the people responsible for the work should be given enough time and information to do it properly."

"That is not quite an answer."

"It is the only one I have."

He nodded.

"Fair enough."

The reception ended shortly before midnight.

Seraphina returned to the carriage with her parents.

Alaric looked satisfied.

Evelyne looked pleased.

Seraphina looked tired.

"Did you enjoy the evening?" Evelyne asked.

"It was acceptable."

"High praise."

"Nobody asked me to open an underground chamber."

Alaric laughed.

"That is a successful reception."

Seraphina leaned back.

The carriage moved through the palace gates.

She had spoken to the Crown Prince.

She had answered questions from a noblewoman.

She had discussed old technical manuals with two engineers.

She had agreed to share an administrative format.

She had not joined a committee.

She had not accepted an appointment.

She had not promised to repair anything.

It had been a productive evening.

Which was precisely why she suspected it would create more work.

The following morning, Cedric arrived with a letter from the Royal Infrastructure Department.

It thanked House Veyr for agreeing to share its administrative format.

The Department also requested a copy of the estate's existing historical infrastructure register.

Seraphina read the request.

"Why?"

Cedric checked the attached note.

"They want to use it as a reference while designing the national register."

"Have they explained how it will be used?"

"They say it will help distinguish confirmed locations, unverified references, ownership, maintenance responsibility, and technical status."

Seraphina nodded.

"That is reasonable."

She read the next paragraph.

The Department intended to circulate the proposed format to the regional authorities.

It would also use the Veyr register as an example during the first planning meeting.

Seraphina put the letter down.

"Do they want the whole register?"

"Yes."

"No."

Cedric waited.

"Send them a redacted version. Remove private estate details, financial arrangements, and any information unrelated to the technical register."

"Understood."

"And include a note explaining that the document is an example, not an approved national standard."

Cedric smiled.

"I will."

He began gathering the papers.

Seraphina returned to her breakfast.

Then Cedric stopped.

"There is one more thing."

She looked up.

"Of course."

"The Royal Academy has requested a copy of the original technical review group's report."

"Why?"

"They want to compare its recommendations with the current maintenance guides."

"That seems appropriate."

Cedric placed another letter beside her.

The report had been found.

But its original attachments were missing.

The distribution list referred to three technical appendices.

One described the revised operating limits.

One contained the recommended inspection procedure.

The third was titled:

**Regional Winter Network Dependency Schedule.**

The Academy had never received it.

The Department's archive had no copy.

The regional authorities had not retained it.

The technical standards office had no record of its receipt.

Seraphina read the final line.

*The missing schedule may contain the original list of facilities and connections considered necessary for the transition away from the winter regulation network.*

She put the letter down.

The old chamber was closed.

The modern circuits were functioning.

The technical standards were being reviewed.

But somewhere, an appendix had disappeared.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"How many facilities were listed?"

"We do not know."

"How many connections?"

"Unknown."

"Does the Academy know where the schedule was last seen?"

"The original review group's minutes refer to it as an attachment to the final report."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

The kingdom had managed to lose the document that explained which parts of its old magical network were supposed to have been disconnected.

She opened her eyes.

"Tell Lucien to search the Academy's copy of the review group's minutes."

Cedric nodded.

"And tell the Department not to announce a national transition programme until the schedule is found."

"Would they do that?"

"I hope so."

Seraphina picked up her tea.

The reception had been pleasant.

The Crown Prince had been reasonable.

The engineers had been competent.

The Treasury had not promised money it did not have.

For one evening, the kingdom had behaved like a functioning institution.

Apparently, that had been enough excitement for the month.
