# Chapter 49 — The Committee That Had Never Met Again

The review group had existed for eleven days.

That was the first thing Seraphina discovered.

Its establishment order had been issued on the third day of the autumn quarter, twenty-one years earlier. Its first meeting had been scheduled for the fourteenth day.

The postponement notice had been issued on the fifteenth.

There was no record of a second meeting.

Seraphina placed the documents on her desk.

"Eleven days."

Mira looked up from arranging the correspondence.

"Is that important, my lady?"

"It is an impressive lifespan for a committee."

"Perhaps it completed its work quickly."

"It postponed its first meeting."

Mira considered that.

"Then perhaps it was efficient in a different way."

Seraphina gave her a flat look.

Mira returned to the correspondence.

The establishment order named five members.

- Arthur Fenwick, Senior Expenditure Officer.
- Master Colin Reeve, regional surveyor.
- Darius Veylan, technical engineer.
- Master Elias Rook, maintenance engineer.
- Henry Cole, records administrator.

Seraphina recognized every name.

Fenwick had authorized payments connected to W-2 and the reserve programme.

Reeve had conducted surveys for E-1 and A-2.

Veylan had inspected equipment and supervised W-2.

Rook had reviewed the incomplete regulating assemblies.

Cole had retained copies of transfer schedules when the records were divided among offices.

They had all appeared in the investigation independently.

Now they were listed together.

Seraphina read the committee's stated purpose.

*To review the status of regional infrastructure works, identify incomplete installations, reconcile outstanding equipment allocations and recommend the administrative closure or continuation of affected projects.*

She looked at the postponement notice.

The reason was simple.

The committee had been instructed to wait until the original project completion schedules were assembled.

The schedules had not been assembled.

The meeting had never taken place.

Holt arrived shortly before noon.

He read the establishment order and checked the names.

"This may be the missing administrative link."

"Possibly."

"The members were selected because they had worked on different parts of the programme."

"Yes."

"Then the review group may have been intended to bring their information together."

Seraphina nodded.

"That would have been sensible."

Holt examined the postponement notice.

"Why was it never reconvened?"

"That is what we need to establish."

"Should I contact the former members?"

"Yes. Start with Henry Cole. He may remember receiving the original notice."

Holt made a note.

"And Arthur Fenwick?"

"Ask for his surviving correspondence."

"Master Reeve?"

"Same."

Holt looked at the list.

"Some of them may not remember."

"Then we will rely on records."

Henry Cole replied first.

He was willing to meet the committee again.

The meeting was arranged in the capital, in a small conference room at the Central Project Review Office.

Cole arrived with a folder of old correspondence.

He looked older than when Seraphina had last seen him, but his memory remained precise.

"I remember the review group," he said.

"Did you attend its first meeting?" Theodore asked.

"There was no first meeting."

"Why was it postponed?"

Cole opened his folder.

"The original project schedules were supposed to be assembled by the Allocation Office. They were not ready."

"Did you receive an explanation?"

"Yes. The regional records had not been reconciled."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Was that the only reason?"

Cole paused.

"No."

He removed another document.

"The committee was also missing a formal mandate for examining financial settlements."

Garrick frowned.

"But Arthur Fenwick was a member."

"He was a member in his capacity as Senior Expenditure Officer. That did not give the review group authority to reopen Treasury decisions."

"Could it have reviewed the records without changing those decisions?" Seraphina asked.

"Yes. But the distinction had not been written into the establishment order."

Theodore read the document.

"So the group was established before its responsibilities had been clearly defined."

Cole nodded.

"That was my understanding."

Seraphina examined the postponement correspondence.

The Allocation Office had requested additional time to collect the completion schedules.

The Treasury had asked whether the group intended to review unpaid balances.

The Technical Evaluation Board had requested clarification about whether it was expected to issue new technical assessments.

The Regional Maintenance Authority had asked whether its officers were required to attend.

Four separate offices had asked four different questions.

Nobody had answered them.

Instead, the meeting was postponed.

"Was there a later instruction?" Seraphina asked.

Cole shook his head.

"I sent two reminders."

"To whom?"

"The Allocation Office."

"Did they reply?"

"Only to acknowledge receipt."

"Did you follow up?"

"Yes. I asked whether the review group remained active."

He found the letter.

The response was written several months later.

*The matter remains under administrative consideration.*

Seraphina stared at it.

"That is not an answer."

"No," Cole said. "It was not."

The second reminder had been more specific.

Cole had requested confirmation of the group's status and asked whether the original project schedules were still required.

The response contained a handwritten instruction.

*Retain papers pending further direction.*

No further direction appeared.

The review group's file was placed in storage.

The members continued with their ordinary duties.

Some transferred to other offices.

Some retired.

The programme itself continued to appear in regional expenditure summaries.

The review group was never formally dissolved.

It was simply no longer used.

Seraphina looked at Cole.

"Did you ever consider that the group might have been cancelled?"

"Yes."

"Did you receive a cancellation notice?"

"No."

"Then why did you stop sending reminders?"

Cole looked down at his papers.

"Because I was instructed to retain the file pending further direction. I was not authorized to compel the Allocation Office to convene the group."

Seraphina nodded.

He had followed the instruction he had received.

It was another example of a process that had stopped without anyone formally ending it.

Master Colin Reeve sent a written response.

He remembered being appointed to the review group but did not remember receiving the postponement notice personally.

His office had received a copy.

He had assumed the meeting would be rescheduled.

It never was.

His own records contained a preliminary report listing four unresolved matters:

1. The E-1 installation had not reached final calibration.
2. A-2 construction had been completed only to the structural stage.
3. W-2 had not received final commissioning approval.
4. N-4's equipment status remained uncertain.

The report was dated two days before the scheduled meeting.

Seraphina read it.

The list was remarkably familiar.

The same unresolved matters had taken months to reconstruct from separate archives.

They had been identified twenty-one years earlier.

The review group had been created to examine them.

And the meeting had never happened.

Master Elias Rook was more difficult to contact.

He had retired to a small village in the northern foothills.

His first reply stated that he was willing to provide information but did not wish to travel to the capital.

Seraphina arranged a written interview.

His response arrived a week later.

He remembered the review group.

He had been asked to prepare a summary of the W-2 and N-4 technical issues.

He had submitted the summary.

The document had been received by the Allocation Office.

But he had never been asked to present it.

His summary contained an important distinction.

W-2 required a replacement component and final calibration.

N-4 required an inspection of the stored regulating assembly before any decision about installation could be made.

The two stations had different technical problems.

They had not been treated as a single failed project.

Rook had warned that combining them in one completion schedule could create confusion.

Seraphina placed his letter beside Reeve's report.

The old review group had not lacked technical information entirely.

It had lacked a process for bringing that information together.

Arthur Fenwick's records took longer to locate.

He had retired from the Treasury and moved to the eastern district.

His surviving correspondence had been transferred to the Office of Public Financial Records.

The archive found three letters.

The first was the original appointment.

The second was a request for clarification about the review group's financial authority.

The third was a reply from the Allocation Office.

It stated that the review group was not authorized to alter existing payment decisions.

It could identify discrepancies and recommend further examination.

Seraphina read the reply.

"Then the group could have reviewed the financial records."

"Yes," Holt said.

"Without reopening payments."

"Correct."

"Why was the mandate never clarified?"

Holt examined the date.

"The clarification arrived two days after the scheduled meeting had been postponed."

Seraphina looked at the document.

The answer had arrived.

The meeting had not been rescheduled.

Fenwick agreed to a written interview.

He remembered the financial purpose of the group.

He had been asked to identify outstanding balances, incomplete settlements and reserve payments.

He had also been asked to distinguish between payments made for completed work and payments made for preliminary services.

That last instruction was significant.

It was the same distinction Seraphina had made when reviewing the Veylan Works reserve invoice.

Fenwick had understood that a payment could be legitimate even when a wider project remained incomplete.

His notes had warned against treating every unpaid balance as evidence of misconduct.

He had also identified a concern.

The programme's financial summaries combined several categories:

- Construction expenditure.
- Equipment procurement.
- Technical evaluation.
- Preliminary engineering services.
- Regional maintenance contributions.

The summaries did not always show whether a project had reached its final stage.

Seraphina read the note.

It was not a discovery of corruption.

It was a warning about accounting categories.

A warning that had not been incorporated into the programme's central records.

The committee met again after all five responses had arrived.

Theodore reviewed the old establishment order.

"Then the review group was intended to reconcile the programme's technical, financial and administrative status."

"Yes," Seraphina replied.

"It had the necessary people."

"Mostly."

"It had reports."

"Several."

"It had authority to recommend further review."

"Yes."

"And it never met."

"Correct."

Garrick examined the postponement notice.

"Why was no one responsible for reconvening it?"

Seraphina looked at the order.

"Because the order did not assign that responsibility clearly."

Holt added:

"The Allocation Office was expected to assemble the schedules. The records administrator was expected to retain the papers. The technical members were expected to prepare reports. No one was explicitly tasked with calling the next meeting."

Garrick leaned back.

"An administrative failure."

"An incomplete administrative arrangement," Seraphina corrected.

"Is there a difference?"

"Yes. The first sounds as though someone failed to perform an assigned task. The second recognizes that the task may never have been assigned."

Theodore nodded.

"That distinction should be included in the report."

Lucien examined the old technical summaries.

"There is another issue."

He placed Rook's report beside Reeve's.

"These records show that the technical problems were known."

"Yes," Seraphina said.

"But they were not necessarily understood by the same people."

"Explain."

"The E-1 report concerned calibration. The W-2 report concerned a missing component. N-4 required a separate inspection. Each office had information about one part of the programme."

"Could the review group have resolved that?"

"It could have created a unified technical picture. It could not have repaired the equipment or approved installation."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the committee should not assume that a meeting would have completed the programme."

"No. It would have clarified what remained to be done."

Theodore looked at the old reports.

"That alone might have prevented years of confusion."

The committee agreed to add the review group's history to the programme report.

It would be recorded as an established administrative body whose first meeting had been postponed and which had never been formally reconvened.

The reasons were documented:

- Original project schedules had not been assembled.
- Financial review authority required clarification.
- Technical responsibilities had not been defined.
- No officer had been explicitly assigned to reconvene the group.

No individual was identified as responsible for the entire failure.

The committee also agreed that the original reports should be preserved together.

They were placed in a new archive file under a single reference.

For the first time in twenty-one years, the reports from Fenwick, Reeve, Veylan, Rook and Cole were held in the same place.

Seraphina watched Holt place the final folder into the cabinet.

"That is one problem solved."

Holt smiled.

"At least the records are together."

"Yes."

"Will that make the programme easier to understand?"

"Considerably."

He closed the cabinet.

"Then perhaps we have achieved something."

Seraphina considered that.

"Perhaps."

The next morning, the Royal Infrastructure Department sent a request for a final status review.

The department wanted the committee to determine which unresolved projects required further action and which could be closed administratively.

Seraphina read the request.

It was a reasonable next step.

It was also more work.

She called Holt.

"Have they included a proposed list?"

"Yes."

"How many entries?"

"Fourteen."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

"Why fourteen?"

"The department included the six facility references, the reserve allocation, the old review group, two equipment transfers, three regional maintenance matters and the historical contractor accounts."

"That is not a list of projects."

"No."

"It is a list of everything anyone has remembered."

Holt remained silent.

Seraphina opened the document again.

The first six entries were the known facilities.

The seventh was the reserve programme.

The eighth was the review group.

The remaining entries were administrative issues.

She took out her pen.

"We need to separate physical projects from records and administrative actions."

Holt nodded.

"I can prepare a revised register."

"And don't put the review group under facilities."

"I won't."

Later that day, Liora visited the estate.

She had returned from the southern provinces and brought a small parcel.

Seraphina opened it.

Inside was a narrow wooden instrument box.

It had been made by a local carpenter and was intended for the Bellford infirmary.

"The infirmary asked for something that could hold their smaller instruments separately," Liora explained.

Seraphina examined the compartments.

"They have labeled sections."

"Yes. The head nurse insisted on it."

"Good."

Liora smiled.

"They also wanted a record of which instruments were stored in each compartment."

Seraphina looked up.

"Did they make one?"

"They did. It is attached to the lid."

Seraphina turned the box over.

A small paper label listed the contents, the date of inspection and the person responsible for checking them.

She nodded.

"That should help."

Liora sat opposite her.

"Your committee has been busy."

"Yes."

"Have you finished?"

"No."

"Are you close?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"Closer to understanding what happened. Not necessarily closer to finishing everything."

Liora nodded.

"Sometimes that is still progress."

Seraphina looked at the instrument box.

"That depends on what happens afterward."

Liora told her about the southern infirmary's latest difficulties.

The supply procedure had improved.

The staff now recorded what arrived, what was usable and what needed replacement.

However, transportation remained unreliable.

A shipment of surgical instruments had been delayed because the regional carrier had changed its route without notifying the infirmary.

The supplies had eventually arrived.

But the infirmary had spent two days believing the shipment was missing.

Seraphina listened.

"Did the carrier record the change?"

"Yes. In its own transport register."

"Did the infirmary receive a copy?"

"No."

"Then the problem is communication, not necessarily delivery."

"Exactly."

Liora placed a letter on the table.

"We have asked the regional authority to notify receiving institutions whenever a route changes."

"Good."

"We are also asking them to identify a contact person for each district."

Seraphina nodded.

"That sounds more useful than sending another general instruction."

Liora smiled.

"I thought you might say that."

After Liora left, Seraphina returned to the fourteen-entry list.

She separated the entries into three categories.

**Physical facilities and equipment.**

**Financial and contractual records.**

**Administrative responsibilities and historical decisions.**

The list became easier to understand.

It also became longer.

She examined the final section.

One entry referred to the former Allocation Office's correspondence concerning the review group.

Another referred to the absence of a formal closure decision.

The third referred to the transfer of programme responsibility after the regional engineering office had been reorganized.

Seraphina looked at the last entry.

There was a reference number.

**RAO/TRANSFER/21.**

It had not appeared in the original programme register.

Holt had found it in a general correspondence index.

Seraphina read the description.

*Transfer of unfinished regional infrastructure matters to successor departments.*

She looked at the date.

It was the same year the review group had been postponed.

The document might explain what happened next.

The transfer schedule was located in the Allocation Office's successor archive.

It was not a project report.

It was an administrative handover.

The old Allocation Office had been reorganized.

Its responsibilities were divided between the Royal Infrastructure Department, the Treasury and the Technical Evaluation Board.

The schedule listed several unfinished matters.

W-2 was transferred to the Technical Evaluation Board for technical review and Treasury for financial settlement.

E-1 was transferred to the Royal Academy for calibration records and the Eastern Regional Infrastructure Office for site responsibility.

A-2 was transferred to the Western Regional Infrastructure Authority.

S-3 was transferred to the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office.

The reserve programme was transferred to Central Stores.

The old review group's papers were transferred to the Central Project Review Office.

Seraphina read the schedule carefully.

The responsibilities had been distributed.

But there was no overall coordinating office.

The handover had divided the unfinished programme into separate responsibilities.

It had not transferred the programme as a whole.

Theodore arrived at the estate the following afternoon.

He had received the same transfer schedule.

"Then the reorganization explains why the records became scattered."

"Yes."

"Each department received the matters within its own authority."

"Yes."

"But no department received responsibility for the entire programme."

"Correct."

Theodore studied the schedule.

"Was that deliberate?"

"We don't know."

"Could the old Allocation Office have expected the departments to coordinate?"

"Possibly. But the handover does not say so."

He looked at her.

"What would you recommend?"

Seraphina took out the revised register.

"First, we should not recreate the old programme simply because its records were divided. We need to establish whether any of the facilities are still required."

"Agreed."

"Second, each physical site needs one clearly identified authority responsible for its current condition."

"Agreed."

"Third, technical approval, financial settlement and property ownership should remain separate responsibilities, but they need a common record."

Theodore nodded.

"And the historical review?"

"It should be closed once the records are preserved, the unresolved matters are assigned and the responsible departments confirm receipt."

Theodore looked at her.

"That would bring your appointment to an end."

"That is the intention."

He smiled faintly.

"I thought it might be."

That evening, Seraphina received a final document from the western archive.

It was a letter written by Darius Veylan shortly before Veylan Works ceased trading.

He had addressed it to the Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office.

The letter concerned the reserve programme and the equipment he had inspected.

He wrote that several components remained in storage because their intended installation sites had not been confirmed.

He warned that releasing them without approved technical requirements could create safety problems.

Then he made a recommendation.

*The reserve programme should remain open only until each component has been assigned to a confirmed installation site or formally returned to central storage. No equipment should remain under indefinite restriction without a designated authority responsible for its review.*

Seraphina read the letter slowly.

The recommendation was clear.

It was also precisely what had failed to happen.

She placed the letter beside the old review group's establishment order.

The people responsible for the programme had identified many of the problems.

They had requested clarification.

They had prepared reports.

They had warned against premature installation.

But the administrative system had never brought their work together.

The committee that was supposed to do so had never met again.

And the handover that followed had divided the unfinished responsibilities without assigning anyone to oversee the whole.

Seraphina closed the file.

For the first time, she understood the central failure of the old programme.

It had not been one missing signature, one dishonest contractor or one lost piece of equipment.

It had been the absence of a final decision.

And now, twenty-one years later, the kingdom was still living with the consequences.

The following morning, the committee received a new instruction from the Crown Prince.

The historical review was to prepare its final report within the month.

Every unresolved item would require one of three outcomes:

- Assigned to a responsible department for further action.
- Formally closed with supporting evidence.
- Retained as unresolved, with a named authority responsible for future review.

Seraphina read the instruction.

It was the first order she had seen that explicitly required every unfinished matter to have an owner.

She placed it beside her revised register.

Then she wrote a note at the top of the page.

*No unresolved matter is to be recorded without identifying who is responsible for its next review.*

She underlined it once.

It was not a particularly grand reform.

It would not repair a single magical conduit.

It would not install an equipment assembly.

It would not restore a road or build an infirmary.

But it might prevent another twenty-one years of silence.

Seraphina closed her notebook.

Her appointment had three weeks remaining.

For once, she could see the end of the work.

Unfortunately, the next letter came from the Treasury.

It concerned the original W-2 contract.

The Treasury had found a final settlement schedule that had not appeared in any previous archive.

It was dated one year after the company had ceased trading.

And the amount listed as outstanding was not 320 crowns.

It was **1,160 crowns**.

Seraphina stared at the figure.

Then she slowly reopened the W-2 file.
