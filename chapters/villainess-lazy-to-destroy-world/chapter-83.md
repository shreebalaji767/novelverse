# **Chapter 83 — The Seventh Zone That Was Never Quite Idle**

The Academy had scheduled the test for nine in the morning.

Seraphina considered this unnecessarily early.

Not because she intended to participate. She had been explicitly told that her presence was optional, and she had no technical responsibilities whatsoever.

But the invitation had arrived at breakfast, and her father had read it aloud with the solemnity usually reserved for military orders.

"Controlled assessment of the Central Protective Reserve," Alaric had said.

Seraphina had continued buttering her bread.

"That sounds technical."

"It is."

"Then why am I invited?"

"Because you reviewed the administrative register."

"That sounds like a mistake."

Evelyne had lowered her teacup.

"Perhaps they would like someone to confirm that the administrative register continues to describe what the engineers are actually doing."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

"That sounds like a mistake with a longer explanation."

Alaric folded the invitation.

"You may decline."

She considered it.

Then she remembered the seventh zone.

Central Protective Reserve — Transitional Operation.

A name that sounded less like an established part of the capital's protective network and more like something an exhausted clerk had written on a temporary label forty years ago.

"Will the test involve changing the network's configuration?"

"Temporarily," Alaric replied.

"Then I would like to observe the briefing."

Her father nodded.

"Very well."

"Not the test itself."

"That is your decision."

"And I am not responsible if something goes wrong."

"I had not suggested otherwise."

Seraphina placed her knife beside her plate.

"Good. I would hate to discover that breakfast had become a contractual obligation."

Evelyne smiled into her tea.

The Academy's central testing chamber was considerably less impressive than the palace's formal halls.

It was a long, reinforced room beneath the Royal Academy's eastern research building, with stone walls, copper-lined observation panels and a raised platform containing a collection of instruments.

There were no grand magical circles painted across the floor.

No dramatic pillars.

No mysterious blue flames.

There were, however, three separate safety notices, two locked cabinets and a large board displaying the testing sequence.

Seraphina liked the board.

It suggested that someone had considered the possibility of people forgetting what they were supposed to do.

Lucien Arkwright was standing beside it.

He looked as though he had slept poorly.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Master Arkwright."

"You came."

"I was invited."

"That is not always the same thing."

"Then perhaps your invitation system needs improvement."

Lucien glanced at the board.

"Possibly."

He had apparently decided not to argue.

Master Alistair Fenwick, Director of the Technical Standards Directorate, was examining a set of instruments near the platform. He was accompanied by two Academy specialists, a representative from the Royal Infrastructure Department and three members of the capital's maintenance staff.

Theodore stood near the rear of the chamber, speaking with Minister Garrick.

Alaric had arrived separately and was discussing access arrangements with a royal official.

Seraphina approached Lucien.

"Has the network been stable?"

"Yes. The seven zones are operating within their established ranges."

"Then why test the reserve?"

"Because operating within range does not tell us whether every part of the system still serves its recorded purpose."

He pointed toward the board.

"The seventh zone has been classified as transitional for nearly twelve years. It is maintained, monitored and included in the network's operating schedule. However, its original purpose was described as temporary load balancing during the second modernization."

"Temporary for twelve years."

"Yes."

"That is an impressive amount of temporary."

Lucien ignored her.

"The test is intended to determine whether the zone can be isolated briefly without affecting the other six zones, and whether the network behaves as the current technical model predicts."

Seraphina studied the board.

There were four stages.

**Stage One:** Record ordinary operating conditions.

**Stage Two:** Reduce the reserve zone's contribution within approved limits.

**Stage Three:** Observe the response of the six primary zones.

**Stage Four:** Restore the reserve configuration and compare readings.

She pointed to the second stage.

"Who can stop it?"

"Master Fenwick, myself, and the Academy's safety officer."

"Can the maintenance team stop it?"

"Any operator can trigger the emergency restoration procedure."

"Good."

Lucien raised an eyebrow.

"Did you expect otherwise?"

"I prefer arrangements in which the people closest to a problem can stop making it worse."

Master Fenwick had apparently heard her.

He turned from the instruments.

"That is a reasonable principle, Lady Seraphina. The maintenance team has independent authority to initiate restoration if any monitored value exceeds its safety threshold."

"Even if the senior researchers disagree?"

"Especially then."

Seraphina nodded.

She had no further questions about that part.

The safety officer, a broad-shouldered woman named Master Elara Venn, approached with a slate.

"All personnel have received the procedure. No one is to approach the control platform during a test stage unless instructed. The chamber's outer protective circuit remains untouched. The reserve zone's contribution will be adjusted through the modern control assembly, not through the old underground chamber."

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"That distinction is in the test record?"

"It will be."

"Will be?"

Lucien sighed.

"It is in the technical procedure."

"Then it should also be in the record."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Agreed. We will distinguish the modern reserve zone from the historical chamber and the old C-2 conduit. They are not interchangeable."

"Thank you."

Seraphina stepped back.

She had come to observe a test, not become its unofficial secretary.

The first stage began at nine fifteen.

The testing chamber quieted as the Academy specialists activated the monitoring instruments.

A low vibration passed through the floor.

It was not loud enough to be frightening. It resembled the distant movement of a heavy carriage over a stone road.

Lucien watched the primary readings.

Master Fenwick stood beside him, while Master Venn monitored the emergency restoration panel.

The six primary zones remained within their ordinary ranges.

The seventh zone showed a steady contribution.

Seraphina sat beside her father.

"Does that look normal?" Alaric asked quietly.

"I have no idea."

"At least you are honest."

"I have always found that pretending to understand unfamiliar machinery creates unnecessary work."

Alaric watched the instruments.

"Your mother would say the same about certain members of the council."

"Mother would say it more politely."

"She would."

At the front of the chamber, Lucien spoke.

"Stage One complete. Ordinary operating conditions recorded."

A clerk entered the result on the test sheet.

Master Fenwick reviewed the figures before giving approval for the next stage.

"Proceed to the initial reduction."

The operator adjusted the reserve zone's contribution.

Nothing dramatic happened.

The floor vibration changed slightly.

One of the instruments produced a brief amber indication, then returned to its ordinary mark.

Lucien leaned closer.

"Hold."

The operator stopped adjusting.

Master Venn checked the emergency panel.

"No threshold exceeded."

Master Fenwick examined the readings.

"Record the change. Do not proceed until the values settle."

The chamber waited.

For nearly a minute, the readings fluctuated within a narrow band.

Then they stabilized.

"Initial reduction stable," Lucien said.

The clerk wrote it down.

Seraphina watched the operator.

He had not looked frightened. He had looked annoyed, which was probably a more useful reaction when dealing with equipment.

The second reduction began.

This time, the difference was more noticeable.

A small rise appeared in the eastern distribution reading. At the same moment, the western zone registered a corresponding decline.

Lucien frowned.

"That is not the expected response."

"How far outside the model?" Master Fenwick asked.

"It is not outside the approved safety range. But the relationship is unexpected."

Master Venn checked the secondary instruments.

"All values remain within limits."

Lucien looked toward the operator.

"Hold the current setting."

The operator did so.

For several seconds, the readings continued to shift.

Then the eastern value rose slightly again.

Master Venn moved to the restoration panel.

"Recommend restoration."

Lucien studied the display.

"Agreed. Restore the reserve contribution."

The operator followed the approved sequence.

The seventh zone returned to its original setting.

The eastern and western readings gradually settled.

The floor vibration returned to its previous level.

No alarm sounded.

No protective barrier failed.

No one was injured.

The test was stopped.

Master Venn recorded the time.

"All monitored values remain within the established safety range. Normal configuration restored."

Seraphina released a breath she had not realized she was holding.

Alaric glanced at her.

"That was uneventful."

"It was stopped because it was not behaving as expected."

"That is a good reason."

"Yes."

She looked back toward Lucien.

He was already examining the readings.

The test had lasted less than twenty minutes.

The investigation, judging by his expression, would last considerably longer.

The technical team gathered around the central table.

Seraphina remained near the rear of the chamber.

She had no intention of joining a discussion about magical distribution.

Lucien placed three sheets beside one another.

"The eastern zone increased when the reserve contribution was reduced. The western zone decreased. The relationship was not predicted by the simplified model."

"Could the instruments be misaligned?" Theodore asked.

"We checked the secondary readings. The instruments agree."

"Could the eastern zone have received an unrelated load change?"

"Possible, but the timing is suspicious."

Master Fenwick folded his arms.

"We should not call it a fault until we know what it is."

"I did not."

"You were about to."

Lucien looked irritated.

"I was about to say the model is incomplete."

"That is more accurate."

Theodore stepped closer.

"What does this mean for the capital's protective network?"

"Nothing immediate," Lucien answered. "The network is operating normally. The test was stopped well within the approved limits, and the reserve configuration has been restored."

"Could we continue testing?"

"Not until we understand the readings."

Master Fenwick agreed.

"No further reduction today. The Academy will compare the test results with the maintenance records and the original design documents."

Theodore nodded.

"Then that is the decision."

Seraphina was pleased.

A decision had been made without anyone declaring the situation an emergency or proposing an immediate committee with twelve members and a ceremonial name.

It was a promising morning.

Unfortunately, the discussion continued.

The maintenance superintendent, Master Harlan, brought forward a bound operating log.

"This is the reserve zone's routine maintenance record for the past eight years."

Lucien opened it.

"Why eight?"

"Earlier logs were transferred to the central maintenance archive. These are the records kept by the current operating team."

"Any major adjustments?"

"None."

"Any periods when the reserve contribution was reduced?"

"Not deliberately."

Lucien turned several pages.

"Not deliberately?"

Harlan pointed to a series of entries.

"Routine balancing adjustments. The reserve contribution was altered during normal operation, usually when the eastern or western distribution readings approached their upper operating range."

"How often?"

"Several times a month, depending on demand."

Lucien stopped turning pages.

"Several times?"

"Yes."

Master Fenwick moved closer.

"These were automatic adjustments?"

"No. The operating staff made them according to the maintenance instructions."

"Which instructions?"

Harlan opened the back of the log and removed a smaller sheet.

It contained a table of permitted adjustments and corresponding operating conditions.

Lucien read it.

Then he read it again.

"These instructions are not in the current operating model."

Harlan looked uncertain.

"They are in the maintenance handbook."

"Which edition?"

"The second revised edition."

"That is twelve years old."

"Yes."

Theodore looked toward Master Fenwick.

"Was this adjustment procedure included in the modernization handover?"

"We have not established that."

Lucien returned to the table.

The instructions described a balancing procedure under which the reserve zone could temporarily compensate for uneven distribution between the eastern and western circuits.

It was not described as an emergency function.

It was not described as an occasional contingency.

It was part of ordinary operation.

Seraphina glanced at the original test board.

The stated purpose of the test had been to determine whether the seventh zone could be isolated without affecting the other six.

But the maintenance staff had been using it to correct differences between them for years.

She did not know whether that was technically necessary.

She did know that the two descriptions did not match.

"Master Harlan," she said.

He turned.

"Yes, my lady?"

"Do the operators know why they make these adjustments?"

"Some do. It is part of the training."

"Do they know what happens if they do not?"

Harlan hesitated.

"They know the readings may become uneven."

"Has anyone tested that?"

"Not recently."

Lucien looked up.

"Not in the present configuration."

"Then the procedure may be preserving a function that the model does not describe."

"Possibly."

Seraphina nodded.

That was enough.

She had no reason to decide what the reserve zone did. The engineers had instruments, records and a test procedure for that purpose.

But someone needed to reconcile the documents.

She looked at the operating log.

"Is this procedure included in the central maintenance register?"

Harlan checked the cover.

"It is referenced."

"Does the register describe it as routine balancing?"

"No. It says the reserve zone is maintained in transitional readiness."

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"That sounds different."

"It is different."

"Then the register is incomplete."

Master Fenwick considered the log.

"That is an administrative finding, not a technical conclusion."

"Yes."

"And we should not assume the operating procedure is correct simply because it has been followed for years."

"I agree."

Lucien gave her a brief look of approval.

It was almost certainly the most flattering expression he could manage before lunch.

The next hour was spent comparing the maintenance handbook with the central operating model.

The differences were not limited to the reserve zone.

One instruction referred to the eastern circuit as the primary balancing recipient. Another described the western circuit as the preferred recipient under higher demand.

The current model treated both as independent distribution zones.

Neither description was necessarily wrong.

They simply appeared to describe different operating arrangements.

The maintenance team also used a seasonal adjustment table that did not appear in the simplified model.

Master Fenwick asked for its source.

Harlan produced a reference number.

The reference led to an earlier operating directive, issued during the second modernization.

The directive itself was not in the chamber.

The Royal Infrastructure Department's representative wrote down the reference and promised to retrieve it.

Seraphina watched the growing pile of documents.

She was beginning to understand why the reserve zone had remained transitional.

The designation might have survived because the network had never been formally handed over as a single, fully documented system.

Individual circuits had been tested, adjusted and maintained.

The central register had recorded the resulting structure.

But the people operating it had inherited instructions that were not fully reflected in the register.

That was not proof of negligence.

It was not proof of danger.

It was a reason to stop treating a simplified description as a complete explanation.

Theodore approached her while the technical team continued its discussion.

"You noticed the discrepancy."

"I noticed that the register says one thing and the maintenance log says another."

"That is a discrepancy."

"An ordinary one."

"Not necessarily."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Your Highness, I am not going to tell the Academy how to operate its circuits."

"I did not ask you to."

"Good."

Theodore glanced toward the instruments.

"Still, the test has changed the scope of the review."

"It should. The original question was whether the reserve could be isolated. Now they know they need to understand what it has been doing."

"Would you recommend another test?"

"No."

He raised an eyebrow.

"I would recommend that the engineers decide whether another test is justified after reviewing the operating instructions."

Theodore smiled faintly.

"That is a very careful answer."

"It is a very obvious answer."

"Obvious answers are not always popular."

"Then perhaps people should stop asking questions they do not want answered."

Theodore looked as though he might laugh.

Instead, he returned to the table.

By early afternoon, the Academy issued a short preliminary record.

The controlled test had been stopped after an unexpected relationship appeared between the eastern and western distribution readings during reduction of the reserve zone's contribution.

All readings remained within approved safety limits.

The reserve configuration had been restored.

No interruption to the capital's protective network had occurred.

Further testing was suspended pending review of the operating model, historical instructions and routine maintenance records.

The report also included a separate administrative note:

**The Central Protective Reserve is recorded as transitional in the central register. Current maintenance records indicate routine balancing activity under conditions not fully represented in the simplified operating model. The technical purpose and operating requirements remain under review.**

Seraphina read it once.

Then she read the final paragraph.

It did not claim that the reserve was faulty.

It did not claim that the old network remained active.

It did not connect the unexpected readings to the removed C-2 conduit.

That was important.

The historical chamber had been inspected. C-2 had been removed from the central chamber, its remaining sleeve sealed, and the eastern junction confirmed inactive.

The seventh zone belonged to the modern protective network.

It was a separate matter.

Lucien came over as she finished reading.

"We will need the older operating directives."

"Then retrieve them."

"We may also need to compare the original commissioning sequence."

"That sounds sensible."

"And the seasonal instructions."

"Also sensible."

He looked at her.

"You are not going to offer an opinion?"

"I have several opinions. None of them would help you calibrate a protective circuit."

Lucien gave a tired laugh.

"At least you know that."

"I have been told it is one of my better qualities."

That evening, Seraphina returned to the palace with her father.

Theodore remained at the Academy to discuss the next steps with the technical team.

Alaric was quiet during the carriage ride.

Eventually, he asked, "Do you think the network is unsafe?"

Seraphina looked out of the window.

"I don't know."

"That is not reassuring."

"Neither would be an invented answer."

Alaric considered that.

"Your mother will ask."

"Tell her the test was stopped safely, the network remains stable, and the engineers found an operating procedure that needs to be compared with the current model."

"She will ask whether the capital is in danger."

"Then tell her the Academy has not identified an immediate danger."

"And if she asks what happens next?"

"The engineers investigate."

Alaric nodded.

"She will dislike that answer."

"She is welcome to dislike it."

When they reached the palace, Evelyne was waiting in the drawing room.

She listened to the account without interrupting.

When Alaric finished, she looked at Seraphina.

"So the reserve zone is used during ordinary operation?"

"According to the maintenance log."

"But the central register calls it transitional."

"Yes."

"And the test showed that reducing its contribution changed the balance between two other zones."

"Yes."

Evelyne leaned back.

"Then it may not be redundant."

"That is one possibility."

"Or the operating procedure may have continued after the original need disappeared."

"Also possible."

"Or the simplified model may be missing an important condition."

"That is what the Academy intends to determine."

Evelyne nodded.

"Good. I would rather have three possible explanations than one premature accusation."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

That was probably the most sensible thing anyone had said all day.

A servant entered with a small tray.

On it lay a folded letter bearing the seal of the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Seraphina looked at it.

"No."

Alaric glanced at the envelope.

"You haven't read it."

"I recognize the seal."

Evelyne took the letter before her daughter could push it away.

She opened it and read.

Then she passed it to Alaric.

He examined the contents.

"The Department is requesting copies of the maintenance handbook and the older operating directive."

"That is reasonable."

"They also request the names of the staff who performed routine balancing adjustments."

Seraphina frowned.

"Why?"

"To establish the operating history," Alaric said.

"Then they should ask the maintenance superintendent for the records, not treat the operators as suspects."

Evelyne studied the letter.

"It does not say they are suspects."

"Good. Then the wording should make that clear."

Alaric nodded.

"I will mention it to the Department."

Seraphina stood.

"Excellent. I have contributed everything I can."

"Where are you going?" Evelyne asked.

"To sleep."

"It is not yet late."

"That is why I am going early."

She reached the door.

Then Alaric spoke again.

"Seraphina."

She turned.

"Yes?"

"The Academy's preliminary report includes a reference to an operating directive from the second modernization. It is dated twelve years ago."

"All right."

"That was the year the seventh zone was formally classified as transitional."

Seraphina looked back at the letter.

For twelve years, the zone had carried a designation suggesting that its role was temporary.

For twelve years, the maintenance team had continued using it as part of ordinary operation.

And now the controlled test had shown that reducing its contribution changed the relationship between two other circuits.

She did not know what that meant.

But she knew what question the engineers needed to answer.

"Ask them to compare the date of the directive with the date of the register amendment," she said.

Alaric nodded.

"Why?"

"Because if the operating instructions were changed after the zone was classified as transitional, the register may have been describing an older arrangement."

Evelyne considered this.

"And if they were changed before?"

"Then the register may have been wrong from the beginning."

Neither answer was comforting.

But both were testable.

Seraphina left the room.

Behind her, her parents continued discussing the letter.

For once, she did not feel the need to remain and listen.

The capital's protective network had not failed.

The old underground system remained inactive.

The modern reserve zone had been restored to its ordinary configuration.

The engineers had discovered a discrepancy and stopped before turning it into a larger experiment.

That was a reasonable day's work.

Unfortunately, the next morning, Lucien sent a second message.

The operating directive had been issued eleven months before the register amendment.

And the amendment had not simply changed the reserve zone's designation.

It had also removed a reference to a **scheduled balancing interval** from the central operating model.

The maintenance handbook still contained it.

The central model did not.

The Academy had not yet determined whether the omission was deliberate, accidental, or based on a technical change that had never been completed.

Seraphina read the message twice.

Then she placed it on her breakfast table.

"Is it bad news?" Evelyne asked.

"Not yet."

Alaric looked up.

"What does it say?"

Seraphina folded the message.

"That someone changed the description of the system without changing all the instructions used to operate it."

Her father was silent for a moment.

Then he asked, "And what does that mean?"

Seraphina picked up her spoon.

"It means the engineers have more work."

She took a bite of porridge.

"And I hope they enjoy it."
