# Chapter 98 — The Committee That Had Been Created Before Its Members Were Chosen

The first meeting of the new administrative review committee was scheduled for Monday.

The appointment letters were issued on Saturday.

The membership list was completed on Sunday.

The meeting agenda arrived on Monday morning.

At nine fifteen, Seraphina discovered that the committee had already been established under a different name three years earlier.

At nine twenty, the Department discovered that the earlier committee had never formally been dissolved.

At nine thirty, the Treasury asked whether the new committee was therefore a continuation of the old one.

At nine forty, nobody could answer.

Seraphina looked at the three documents spread across the table.

"Is this a joke?"

Theodore, seated opposite her, examined the membership list.

"I hope not."

"That is not reassuring."

Minister Garrick cleared his throat.

"The earlier committee was established to coordinate maintenance records following the second modernization."

"Was it active?"

"It met twice."

"Did it complete its work?"

"That is unclear."

"Was it dissolved?"

"The records do not show a formal dissolution."

Seraphina looked at the new committee's title.

**Capital Protective Network Administrative Review Committee.**

Then she looked at the old one.

**Capital Infrastructure Maintenance Coordination Committee.**

"Why was a new committee created?"

Theodore glanced toward the Department's coordinator.

The coordinator opened his folder.

"The new committee was intended to review the recent interruption and establish a permanent administrative register."

"Then why does the old committee have the same responsibilities?"

"It may not have."

"Has anyone compared the terms of reference?"

The coordinator hesitated.

Garrick looked at the ceiling.

Seraphina closed the folder.

"Before we begin, I would like to know whether we are creating a new committee, reopening an old one or pretending that an unfinished committee never existed."

Nobody answered immediately.

The meeting had been scheduled for nine.

It was now nine forty-three.

The old committee's first record was found in the Royal Infrastructure Department's archive.

It had been established twelve years earlier during the second modernization.

Its original purpose was to coordinate the transfer of maintenance responsibilities from the central modernization office to the regional authorities.

The committee had included representatives from the Academy, Treasury, Infrastructure Department and three regional maintenance offices.

Its first meeting had established a list of documents to be transferred.

Its second meeting had reviewed the transfer schedule.

The third meeting had been postponed.

The fourth had never been called.

No final report had been issued.

No formal dissolution had been recorded.

The committee's administrative file had simply been closed when the modernization project ended.

Seraphina read the final entry.

"Who closed the file?"

The coordinator checked.

"The Department's former records office."

"On whose instruction?"

"The entry does not say."

"Then the file was closed administratively, not by a committee decision."

"Apparently."

"Does the old committee still have legal authority?"

Garrick shook his head.

"No. Its authority was tied to the modernization programme, which has ended."

"Then why are we discussing whether it is still active?"

"Because its administrative record was never formally closed."

Seraphina looked at him.

"That is a records problem, not a legal mystery."

Garrick gave her a tired smile.

"I am beginning to appreciate the distinction."

Theodore called the meeting to order at ten.

The revised committee would have four principal responsibilities:

1. Establish a complete administrative register of the seven modern zones.
2. Reconcile maintenance records with installation and technical acceptance records.
3. Confirm the location and status of materials used during the recent repair.
4. Recommend a permanent process for maintaining and reviewing the records.

The committee would not direct technical work.

It would not approve expenditure.

It would not authorize the activation, isolation or modification of any magical circuit.

Those responsibilities remained with the relevant authorities.

Seraphina read the terms carefully.

"Who is responsible for the final register?"

The coordinator answered.

"The Royal Infrastructure Department."

"Who confirms technical information?"

"The Academy."

"Who confirms expenditure and procurement?"

"The Treasury."

"Who confirms local inspection records?"

"The regional maintenance authorities."

"And who resolves a disagreement between those offices?"

Theodore answered.

"The committee will record the disagreement and refer it to the responsible authority."

"That is acceptable."

The coordinator looked relieved.

Seraphina continued.

"Who maintains the committee's own records?"

The coordinator raised his hand slightly.

"I have been appointed secretary."

"Good. Then your office should also maintain the version history."

"Yes."

"And no one should overwrite an earlier entry."

The coordinator nodded.

"Agreed."

Theodore looked around the table.

"Any objections?"

Nobody spoke.

"Then the committee will proceed."

The first issue was the old transfer register.

It contained thirty-seven entries.

Some were complete.

Others recorded only that a document had been sent.

Several entries showed that a document had been received without identifying the person who received it.

One entry listed a technical drawing as transferred to the Academy, but the Academy's archive catalogue contained no corresponding reference.

Another listed a Treasury expenditure schedule as complete, although the Treasury's copy was marked provisional.

Theodore looked at Seraphina.

"Where do we begin?"

"With the documents that affect the current system."

"Which are?"

"The component specifications, maintenance instructions, installation variations and acceptance records."

The Academy representative nodded.

"Those are the technical priorities."

Seraphina turned to the coordinator.

"Do we have a list of the records already confirmed?"

"Yes."

"Then separate confirmed records from references that have not been located."

"That will take time."

"Yes."

The coordinator looked at the thirty-seven entries.

"Could we simply mark the missing documents as unavailable?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because unavailable is not the same as nonexistent. We should record the last confirmed location, the office responsible for the transfer and whether the document was expected to be retained."

Garrick nodded.

"That is sensible."

Seraphina pointed to one entry.

"Here, the Treasury schedule was transferred to the Department. The Department says it received a copy. The Academy's version is marked provisional. We need to establish which version was used for the final expenditure record."

The Treasury representative opened his folder.

"I can provide the original schedule."

"Then do so."

The first task was assigned.

The meeting had been running for nearly two hours.

Nobody had yet argued about anything important.

Seraphina considered this an encouraging development.

At noon, the committee reviewed the materials inventory.

The eighteen containers of C-4 reserve compound had been accounted for.

Six remained at the chemical reference store.

Six were held by the catalyst laboratory.

Four were held by the Department.

One had been consumed during testing.

One had been transferred to another research section.

The transferred container's location had now been confirmed.

It remained sealed in the research section's controlled store.

The inventory was complete.

The Academy had also verified that the three containers used during the recent central assembly repair came from the catalyst laboratory's reserve.

Their batch numbers, quantities and issue records matched.

The remaining catalyst had been returned to controlled storage.

The committee accepted the inventory.

Then the Department's representative raised another matter.

"The western depot's three containers remain unresolved."

Seraphina looked up.

"Those were recorded as used in emergency maintenance."

"Yes."

"Do we have the maintenance reports?"

"Two have been found."

"And the third?"

"Not yet."

"Then keep the third entry open."

The representative looked dissatisfied.

"Would it not be better to close the matter if the materials were probably used?"

"No."

"We have no evidence that they were removed."

"We also have no evidence that they were used."

Theodore intervened.

"Continue the search. We will not treat an incomplete record as a completed one."

The representative nodded.

Seraphina moved to the next page.

There was no reason to spend the entire day discussing three containers whose whereabouts had not affected the recent repair.

They mattered.

But they did not require immediate drama.

The second half of the meeting concerned the permanent maintenance register.

The Academy proposed a technical document containing the current condition of every component.

The Department proposed an administrative register listing all installations, maintenance actions and transfers.

The Treasury wanted a separate expenditure record.

The regional authorities preferred a single form.

All four proposals had advantages.

All four also created the possibility of maintaining four different versions of the same information.

Seraphina looked at the draft forms.

"Why do we need four registers?"

The Academy representative answered.

"Because technical information should not be edited by an administrative office."

"The Department does not need to edit technical information. It needs to record that a technical assessment has been completed."

"That is true."

"And the Treasury can record expenditure without changing the technical status."

Garrick nodded.

"That is how we currently operate."

"Then we need one central index with references to the separate records, not four competing summaries."

The coordinator began writing.

"One central index?"

"Yes. It should identify the component, its location, the responsible authority, the current technical status, the latest inspection reference, the latest maintenance reference and the financial record."

Lucien, who had joined the meeting as the Academy's technical representative, considered the proposal.

"Would it include the technical measurements?"

"No. Those belong in the technical report."

"Then how will the index show whether a component has passed inspection?"

"It will show the report reference and the conclusion confirmed by the Academy."

Lucien nodded.

"That is acceptable."

The Department's representative raised a concern.

"What if the report is later amended?"

"Then the index should show the amended reference and preserve the previous entry."

"That will create a large register."

"It is a seven-zone system, not an empire."

Garrick coughed.

Theodore looked down at his notes.

The coordinator wrote the proposal into the minutes.

At three, the committee encountered its first genuine disagreement.

The Academy had classified the central assembly as operational under monitoring.

The Department had classified it as restored.

The Treasury had classified the repair expenditure as completed.

The regional maintenance authority had classified the central assembly as awaiting final review.

Each classification was reasonable within its own records.

Together, they created confusion.

Theodore placed the four documents side by side.

"Which status should the central index use?"

Seraphina examined them.

"Operational under monitoring."

"Why?"

"Because that is the most accurate description of the current technical condition."

The Department representative objected.

"The repair has been completed."

"That describes the maintenance work."

"The expenditure is complete."

"That describes the payment."

"The regional authority has finished its assigned inspection."

"That describes its inspection."

The Academy representative nodded.

"None of those statements establishes that the wider technical review is complete."

Theodore looked at the documents again.

"Then the index should record all four."

Seraphina nodded.

"With separate fields."

The coordinator wrote:

- Maintenance work: completed.
- Expenditure: recorded as complete.
- Local inspection: completed.
- Technical status: operational under monitoring.
- Wider assessment: ongoing.

The disagreement ended.

No one was particularly satisfied.

That was usually a sign that the distinction was accurate.

At four fifteen, the committee received a report from the Academy's monitoring office.

The central assembly had maintained stable readings throughout the day.

The reserve contribution remained within the approved range.

The western and southern zones had experienced small variations during the afternoon demand increase.

Both remained within their operating limits.

The eastern circuit continued to perform normally after the earlier corrective work.

The network was stable.

Fenwick's recommendation remained unchanged.

The reserve was to remain active.

No further experimental reductions were authorized.

The committee recorded the report.

Then the Academy representative added something.

"There is one further issue."

Seraphina looked at him.

"What?"

"The component register contains a discrepancy in the western zone's installation date."

"How large?"

"Six months."

"Which record?"

"The Department's installation summary lists the replacement guide as installed in the third quarter of the second modernization. The Academy's technical archive lists it in the first quarter of the following year."

Theodore looked toward the Department representative.

"Which is correct?"

"We need to inspect the original installation file."

Seraphina closed her notebook.

"Then that is the next record to locate."

Lucien nodded.

"It may also affect the maintenance interval."

"Does it?"

"We don't know."

"Then don't assume it does."

He gave her a look.

"I was not going to."

"Good."

The committee's first day ended at five thirty.

It had completed the following:

- confirmed the current location of all eighteen reserve catalyst containers;
- established the current administrative status of the central assembly;
- identified the records required for the seven-zone register;
- separated technical, administrative and financial responsibilities;
- located an unresolved installation-date discrepancy;
- assigned responsibility for the outstanding transfer records.

It had not completed the historical review.

It had not resolved the three containers recorded as used at the western depot.

It had not confirmed the final commissioning status of the second modernization.

It had not established a permanent maintenance register.

Theodore reviewed the minutes.

"Not a bad first day."

Seraphina looked at the document.

"We spent nearly eight hours deciding how to describe the current condition of one assembly."

"That is an exaggeration."

"Only slightly."

He smiled.

"Would you prefer that we had spent eight hours arguing about who was allowed to describe it?"

"No."

"Then we made progress."

Seraphina gathered her papers.

"That is a generous interpretation."

At the palace entrance, Cedric was waiting.

He had arrived with an estate report and a small package from Evelyne.

Seraphina looked at the package.

"What is it?"

"Your mother sent food."

"Why?"

"She believes you have been eating badly."

"That is becoming a common accusation."

Cedric handed her the package.

"She also asked me to remind you that the thirty-day appointment has a defined end date."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Did she use those exact words?"

"Yes."

"That is reassuring."

Cedric handed her the estate report.

"The northern maintenance schedule has been revised. The village council accepted the bridge postponement. The eastern garden wall remains postponed. The household accounts are balanced."

"Any problems?"

"One."

Seraphina waited.

"The laundry supervisor has asked for a new drying room roof."

"Is the existing one unsafe?"

"Not yet, but the timber is deteriorating."

"Then ask Martin Cole to inspect it and provide a cost estimate."

Cedric nodded.

"I thought you would say that."

"Then why ask?"

"Because I wanted to see whether you would."

Seraphina looked at him.

He was becoming insufferable.

It was probably good for the estate.

That evening, Evelyne received a copy of the committee's first minutes.

She read them in her sitting room while Alaric reviewed the northern accounts.

"How was the meeting?" he asked.

"Long."

"Useful?"

"Apparently."

"That is not an answer."

Evelyne placed the minutes down.

"They have finally separated technical status from financial completion and administrative responsibility."

Alaric looked up.

"That sounds obvious."

"It should have been."

"Was it not?"

"Not in the records."

He returned to his accounts.

Evelyne turned another page.

"The committee also discovered that an earlier committee had never been formally closed."

Alaric paused.

"How does that happen?"

"An office closed the file when the project ended."

"Was that incorrect?"

"Not necessarily. But the committee's final status was never recorded."

Alaric looked at her.

"Do you think the same thing happened with the maintenance records?"

"I think several offices believed another office had completed the work."

"That is not the same as deliberate wrongdoing."

"No. It is not."

Evelyne closed the minutes.

"But it is enough to cause serious problems."

On Tuesday, the committee met again.

The Department had located the original western installation file.

It was not in the technical archive.

It was in the contractor's completion records.

The file contained the installation schedule, the delivery record and a site supervisor's report.

The replacement guide had been delivered in the third quarter of the second modernization.

It had not been installed immediately.

The site was still undergoing structural work.

The installation occurred six months later.

The Department's summary had used the delivery date.

The Academy's archive had used the installation date.

Both records were correct.

They were describing different events.

Seraphina read the documents.

"Then there is no discrepancy."

Lucien examined the site report.

"Not in the date, no."

Theodore looked at the file.

"Does this affect the maintenance interval?"

"It might," Lucien said. "The supplier's recommended interval begins from installation, not delivery."

The Academy representative corrected the component record.

The maintenance schedule was recalculated.

The next inspection date changed by six months.

A small adjustment.

But an important one.

The committee recorded the distinction.

Seraphina looked at the old summary.

"Another example of two correct records creating one incorrect conclusion."

Garrick nodded.

"That seems to be the theme of this review."

The next issue concerned the final commissioning certificate.

The second modernization had produced several records.

The central installation had passed its initial tests.

The financial expenditure had been completed.

The maintenance teams had begun operating the network.

The technical board had issued conditional acceptance for the revised eastern circuit design.

But the final commissioning certificate for the full modernized network had not been located.

The committee had known this before.

What it had not established was whether the certificate had ever been issued.

The Academy searched its archive.

The Department searched the project office.

The Treasury searched the expenditure records.

The former Secretariat's register was also examined.

No certificate was found.

A document reference appeared in the Secretariat's correspondence book.

**CAP/COM/32-F — Final Network Commissioning Confirmation.**

The entry stated that the document had been prepared and sent to the Technical Standards Directorate.

The receiving office had acknowledged the correspondence.

But the final decision was not recorded.

Theodore read the entry.

"Could the certificate have been issued?"

"Yes," the Academy representative replied.

"Could it have been rejected?"

"Possibly."

"Could it have remained pending?"

"Also possible."

Seraphina looked at the correspondence register.

"Do we know what happened after the Directorate received it?"

"No."

"Then that is what we need to establish."

The coordinator wrote the task into the minutes.

The committee did not declare the network improperly commissioned.

It did not declare the certificate unnecessary.

It recorded that the final status remained unverified.

By Wednesday afternoon, the committee had reached an uncomfortable conclusion.

The capital protective network had been operating for years.

It had undergone several rounds of modernization.

It had passed initial tests.

It had received maintenance.

It had been used daily.

But the documentary record did not establish that the final commissioning process had been completed in the form required by the original approval.

The current network was functioning.

That did not answer the historical question.

The King was informed.

He did not order an immediate shutdown.

He did not authorize an experimental test.

He asked the Academy to determine whether the absence of the certificate affected the current operating authority.

Fenwick's preliminary answer was that the network had continued operating under subsequent maintenance approvals and technical instructions, but the historical commissioning record remained incomplete.

The King ordered the matter to be examined separately from the immediate operational review.

The current configuration would remain unchanged.

The reserve would remain active.

No new modifications would be authorized until the technical and administrative position was clear.

On Thursday, Seraphina received a letter from the Department.

It contained the official minutes of the first three committee meetings.

There was also a separate request.

The committee secretary wanted her to approve the proposed central index format.

She read the form.

It contained the fields they had discussed.

It also contained a new field.

**Overall Project Status.**

Seraphina stared at it.

Then she wrote in the margin:

*Remove. The project contains multiple independent statuses. Use separate fields for installation, technical acceptance, maintenance, expenditure and administrative responsibility.*

She returned the form.

Five minutes later, the coordinator arrived personally.

"Why do you want the field removed?"

"Because it invites someone to compress five different conditions into one word."

"Would that be harmful?"

"It already has been."

He looked at the form.

"Could we use a summary field instead?"

"Yes, if it is explicitly marked as a summary and does not replace the individual statuses."

The coordinator wrote the correction.

"Thank you."

Seraphina returned to her work.

He hesitated.

"Lady Seraphina?"

"Yes?"

"The committee secretary has requested that you review the revised form before the next meeting."

"Send it through the normal office."

"Of course."

He bowed and left.

Mira entered a moment later.

"Your Ladyship, the Duchess has sent another message."

Seraphina closed her eyes briefly.

"What now?"

"She asks whether you intend to return home for the weekend."

"Yes."

"She also says that she expects you to arrive before supper."

Seraphina looked at the clock.

"That is a reasonable expectation."

Mira smiled.

"Shall I prepare the carriage?"

"Please."

On Friday morning, the Academy issued its latest monitoring report.

The central assembly remained stable.

The reserve continued operating within its approved range.

The western and southern components had passed their initial post-repair observations.

The remaining maintenance inspections were scheduled.

The final commissioning certificate remained unlocated.

The historical review continued.

The committee's administrative register was still being prepared.

Seraphina read the report, signed the receipt and placed it in the correct file.

Then she noticed something at the bottom.

A new reference had been added.

**CAP/COM/32-F — Supplementary Secretariat Register, Appendix B.**

She read it again.

The final commissioning certificate had not been found.

But the supplementary register had not yet been examined.

The document reference was associated with a different administrative office.

Not the Technical Standards Directorate.

Not the Academy.

The Royal Secretariat's old correspondence division.

Seraphina set down her pen.

"Of course."

Mira looked over.

"Is there another problem?"

"Not yet."

"Then what is it?"

Seraphina folded the report.

"The kingdom has discovered another office."

Mira considered that.

"Is that unusual?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at the growing pile of papers.

"That is precisely the problem."
