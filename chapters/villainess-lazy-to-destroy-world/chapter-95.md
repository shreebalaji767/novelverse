# **Chapter 95 — The Night the Capital Lost Its Outer Light**

The first thing to fail was not the protective network.

It was the morning report.

At six o'clock, the Royal Academy's monitoring office sent its overnight summary to the Crown Prince's administrative chamber. The document stated that the central assembly had experienced a further response delay, that the western and southern zones had registered repeated fluctuations, and that the reserve had compensated for each recorded disturbance.

The final line read:

*No interruption to the capital's primary protective coverage has been confirmed.*

At six seventeen, a messenger from the western district arrived with a report that contradicted it.

At six twenty-three, a second messenger arrived from the southern quarter.

At six thirty, the Academy withdrew its morning summary.

The capital had not lost its entire protective network.

But two outer sections had stopped responding.

And this time, the reserve had not restored them.

Seraphina was getting dressed when Mira entered.

"Your Ladyship, a messenger from the palace."

Seraphina looked at the clock.

"Is it an emergency?"

"He says it is."

"Does he look frightened?"

"Very."

"Then it probably is."

Mira handed her the sealed message.

Theodore's note contained only a few lines.

*The capital's protective network has suffered a partial interruption. Please come to the administrative building. Do not travel through the western district.*

Seraphina read it.

Then she read it again.

"Prepare the carriage."

"Immediately?"

"Yes."

Mira moved toward the door.

"Should I inform the Duke and Duchess?"

"Yes. Tell them I am going to the palace. Ask them to remain available, but not to send anything until we know what is needed."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina pulled on her gloves.

"Also tell the household that no one is to travel toward the western district."

"Yes, my lady."

The message was short.

That made it worse.

The capital's eastern streets were crowded with people who had been turned away from the western routes.

The city watch had established temporary barriers at several intersections. Ordinary lanterns illuminated the affected streets, and public buildings had opened their ground-floor halls to people who did not wish to remain near the outer districts.

The protective lamps along the western approach were dark.

Not all of them.

Some remained lit, though their light was weaker than usual. Others had stopped responding altogether.

The streets themselves were intact.

The buildings were standing.

There had been no explosion, no collapse and no visible destruction.

The interruption was concentrated along two sections of the outer protective line.

But the protective line mattered.

It had been designed to reduce the risk of uncontrolled mana disturbances entering the capital's connected distribution network. It did not make every street invulnerable, nor did it replace ordinary guards, fire precautions or structural safeguards.

Without it, the affected districts had lost an important layer of protection.

The city watch was therefore treating the interruption seriously.

Seraphina reached the administrative building shortly after seven.

Theodore was waiting near the entrance.

"You're here."

"I was invited."

He looked tired.

"Two outer sections have stopped responding. The northern section is unstable but functioning. The western and southern zones are still connected to the central assembly, but their protective output has fallen."

"Casualties?"

"None confirmed."

"Buildings?"

"No structural damage reported. The city watch has checked the affected streets."

"Then what is the immediate problem?"

"The Academy cannot restore the sections without determining whether the interruption is caused by the central assembly, the local components or the distribution connection."

Seraphina glanced toward the Academy building across the courtyard.

"And they cannot determine that from here?"

"They are trying."

"How many people have been moved?"

"Several public buildings have been temporarily cleared as a precaution. The hospitals remain operational."

"Good."

Theodore handed her a second report.

"There's another problem."

She opened it.

The report concerned the reserve zone.

During the latest disturbance, the reserve had reached its permitted contribution limit. It had compensated for the initial variation, but the central assembly's response had continued to slow. When the western and southern sections experienced another demand increase, the reserve could not restore both within the required interval.

The affected sections had automatically entered their protective fallback state.

The system had done what it was designed to do.

It had prevented an unstable section from continuing to draw from the network.

Unfortunately, it had also left part of the capital without its outer protective coverage.

Seraphina read the report.

"Can the sections be reconnected?"

"Not until the Academy confirms their condition."

"Then don't reconnect them."

"I have already issued that instruction."

"Good."

Theodore gave her a brief look.

"That was not a request for your approval."

"I know."

For the first time that morning, he almost smiled.

Then a messenger entered.

"Your Highness. Master Arkwright requests your presence at the Academy monitoring room."

Theodore turned immediately.

"Tell him I am coming."

The monitoring room was crowded.

Technicians stood at the observation board. Several engineers had arrived from the Royal Infrastructure Department. Master Alistair Fenwick was examining a series of measurements with two assistants.

Lucien stood beside the central assembly diagram.

"The western and southern interruptions occurred within fourteen seconds of each other," he explained.

"Could one have caused the other?" Theodore asked.

"Possibly. Both draw through the central distribution assembly, but their local control components are separate."

Fenwick pointed to the diagram.

"The reserve reached its permitted contribution limit. The automatic protective mechanism isolated the two affected sections when their output fell below the minimum operating threshold."

"Was the isolation successful?" Theodore asked.

"Yes. The other zones remain stable."

"Then why can't we restore the affected sections?"

"Because we don't know whether the central assembly can maintain a stable response under the present conditions."

Lucien placed another sheet on the table.

"The central response time increased again before the interruption. The latest measurement is significantly slower than yesterday's."

"How much slower?"

Lucien gave him the figure.

Theodore examined it.

"Could the mineral residue have caused this?"

"It may have contributed to the gradual delay. We cannot yet establish whether it is the primary cause of the current failure."

"Then what do you recommend?"

Fenwick answered.

"Maintain the present configuration. Keep the two affected sections isolated. Continue monitoring the remaining zones. We will inspect the central assembly and determine whether a controlled restoration is possible."

"How long?"

"We cannot give you a reliable estimate."

Theodore nodded.

"Proceed."

Seraphina had been studying the diagram.

"Which sections have lost their outer coverage?"

Lucien pointed.

"The western approach and the southern administrative quarter."

"Are they both connected to the same replacement assembly?"

"No. They have separate local regulators."

"Then why did they fail together?"

Lucien looked at the diagram again.

"They share the central distribution assembly and the reserve response."

"Could the central assembly be failing to distribute the reserve contribution properly?"

"That is one possibility."

"Then the local regulators may not be the first things to inspect."

Fenwick nodded.

"Correct. We need to establish whether the central assembly is delivering the expected response before examining the local sections."

Seraphina stepped back.

She had no more useful questions.

The engineers needed to do their work.

She turned toward Theodore.

"What do you need from me?"

"Help with the records and resource coordination. The Department is preparing a list of materials required for the inspection."

"Then I need the list, not an instruction to obtain everything in the kingdom."

Theodore looked at her.

"Agreed."

The first emergency supply request arrived twenty minutes later.

It contained six categories of materials.

Some were ordinary maintenance supplies.

Others were specialized components used in the central distribution assembly.

The list included:

- replacement contact lining;
- approved cleaning compounds;
- calibrated measuring instruments;
- temporary isolation seals;
- stabilizing catalyst;
- replacement control guides.

Seraphina read the quantities.

Then she looked at the stores representative.

"How much is immediately available?"

"That depends on the required specification."

"Then why have you given me one total?"

The representative looked uncomfortable.

"The materials are listed under the emergency allocation."

"That is a funding category. It is not a storage location."

He took the paper back.

"I will prepare a revised list."

"Please do."

Theodore turned toward the Infrastructure Department.

"I want the central stores, Academy stores and regional maintenance stores checked separately."

The representative bowed.

"Yes, Your Highness."

Seraphina looked at the catalyst entry.

The three containers from the old laboratory had not yet been fully identified.

The Academy had confirmed that two were consistent with the original batch, while the third remained uncertain.

None had been authorized for use.

"Do you have enough catalyst for the inspection?" she asked.

Lucien shook his head.

"Enough for limited testing. Not enough to replace the affected assemblies."

"How much would replacement require?"

"We don't know until we complete the inspection."

"Then don't issue a replacement request yet."

The stores representative looked relieved.

Theodore did not.

"We may need it urgently."

"Then we should know what we need before asking for it."

The prince considered this.

"Prepare a provisional request, but do not authorize the issue until the technical team confirms the quantities."

Seraphina nodded.

That was a reasonable compromise.

At nine o'clock, the western district reported a new problem.

A public mana lamp had cracked.

It was not connected to the protective network's central assembly, but its housing had been damaged when the local protective output fell and a separate street circuit experienced an irregular fluctuation.

The lamp did not explode.

It did not injure anyone.

However, its damaged casing had exposed part of the internal conductor.

The city watch closed the immediate area and called the municipal maintenance crew.

The incident was small.

The response was not.

The district office requested permission to isolate the street circuit. The municipal maintenance crew wanted the Academy to confirm whether the damaged lamp was connected to the affected protective section. The Academy wanted a local diagram. The diagram had been updated three years earlier, but the municipal office held only an older copy.

Theodore received the request.

He read it once.

Then he passed it to the Infrastructure Department.

"Resolve the local safety issue first. The city watch has authority to keep the street closed. The Academy can confirm the network relationship afterward."

The official bowed.

Seraphina watched him leave.

"That was sensible."

Theodore looked at her.

"Was it?"

"Yes. The lamp doesn't need to know which department owns the paperwork before someone stops walking underneath it."

He rubbed his forehead.

"I am beginning to understand why the Department finds you difficult."

"I have never claimed to be easy."

At the southern infirmary, Liora was organizing additional lighting and patient movement.

The building's internal circuits remained functional, but the outer protective line had been interrupted.

Sister Agnes had decided to keep the patients in their existing rooms. Moving them would have created more risk than remaining where they were.

The infirmary had sufficient ordinary lamps, but its emergency supplies were limited.

Liora sent a request to the Church's regional storehouse.

The first response stated that additional lamps were available.

The second response stated that the lamps were reserved for another district.

The third response clarified that the reservation had been made six months earlier and that the original district no longer required them.

Liora stared at the three messages.

Nessa looked over her shoulder.

"Should I send another request?"

"No," Liora replied. "Ask the storehouse to confirm the current physical quantity."

"Wouldn't that be the same thing?"

"Apparently not."

The request was sent.

At noon, the storehouse confirmed that twenty-four lamps were available.

They were dispatched with ordinary candles, oil and replacement glass.

Liora arranged for the supplies to be divided between the infirmary and two nearby public shelters.

She did not send them to the palace.

She did not wait for a royal announcement.

She simply ensured that people who needed light received it.

At one in the afternoon, the Academy completed its first inspection of the central assembly.

The outer casing was intact.

The seals were undamaged.

The stabilizing rings remained within their permitted tolerances.

The regulator, however, showed further deterioration in response time.

The mineral residue had increased.

The replacement contact lining retained more residue than expected.

The central assembly had not suffered a sudden structural break.

It had gradually become less responsive.

The engineers could not yet determine whether cleaning the contact surface would restore sufficient performance.

The lining might also require replacement.

The inspection report contained another finding.

The catalyst distribution channel was not delivering material evenly across the central regulator.

It was not blocked.

It was not broken.

But its measured flow differed from the original specification.

Lucien read the report.

"That could explain the delayed response."

"Could it explain the western and southern interruptions?" Theodore asked.

"Possibly. The uneven distribution may have affected the regulator's ability to stabilize the demand increase."

"Can it be repaired?"

"Yes, but we need the correct replacement components and catalyst specification."

Fenwick pointed to the report.

"The current catalyst supply may be insufficient for the full procedure."

Theodore turned to the stores representative.

"How much is available?"

The man consulted his revised inventory.

"Central stores have a quantity of C-4 compound, but the recorded batch is not the same as the original evaluation material."

"How much?"

"Enough for limited work."

"And the secondary stores?"

"Two containers are under identification. The third is still being examined."

Lucien frowned.

"We cannot use unidentified material."

"I know," Theodore said.

The stores representative continued.

"There may be additional supplies held by the Royal Infrastructure Department."

"Then locate them."

The man bowed.

The next report arrived from the Department's old regional stores office.

It contained an unexpected reference.

A shipment of C-4 stabilizing compound had been delivered to the capital's western maintenance depot four years earlier.

The original order had been for twelve containers.

The depot's receiving record listed twelve.

The central stores ledger recorded a transfer of twelve.

But the depot's current inventory listed only five.

Seven had been recorded as used during maintenance.

The maintenance schedule listed four.

Three containers remained unexplained.

Seraphina examined the records.

"Another difference."

The stores representative nodded.

"Yes, my lady."

"Are these the same batch?"

"No. The procurement reference is different."

"Then don't combine the figures."

"I haven't."

She looked at the original order.

The material specification was similar to the C-4 compound used in the central assembly, but the supplier's reference included an additional designation.

C-4/R.

"What does the R mean?"

The representative checked the supplier catalogue.

"Regional formulation."

Lucien took the document.

"That is not necessarily interchangeable with the central formulation."

"Could it be used in the capital network?" Theodore asked.

"Not without checking the technical specification."

Seraphina returned to the ledger.

"Where are the seven containers recorded as used?"

The representative found the relevant entries.

Four were linked to scheduled maintenance.

Three were listed under emergency repairs.

The emergency repair references were incomplete.

"Which repairs?" Seraphina asked.

"The western depot's maintenance record."

"Do you have the original reports?"

"Not yet."

"Then we cannot conclude that the three containers were lost."

Theodore looked at the report.

"Could the depot have retained them?"

"Possibly."

"Could they have been used elsewhere?"

"Possibly."

Lucien looked impatient.

"We do not need another general inventory problem. We need material for the central assembly."

Seraphina turned toward him.

"Then we need the correct material, not merely a container with a familiar name."

He stopped.

She continued.

"The regional formulation may be unsuitable. If the depot used it in a different assembly, it may not be available for this repair. We need the technical specification and the maintenance records."

Lucien looked at the catalogue again.

"She's right."

Theodore nodded.

"Find the records."

At four in the afternoon, the King arrived at the Academy.

He had received the latest technical report and wanted to hear directly from the engineers.

Fenwick explained the central assembly's condition.

The network remained partially functional.

The reserve was still active.

Two outer sections remained isolated.

The central regulator's response time had deteriorated.

The catalyst distribution channel was operating unevenly.

The western and southern components required separate inspection.

The King listened.

"Can the capital remain protected while repairs are undertaken?"

"Most of it can," Fenwick replied. "The isolated sections will remain without their outer protective coverage until we can restore them safely."

"How long?"

"We cannot give a reliable estimate."

The King looked at Theodore.

"What resources are required?"

"Specialized catalyst, replacement contact lining, calibrated instruments and additional technical staff."

"Can the Treasury provide them?"

Minister Garrick answered.

"We can authorize emergency expenditure. The difficulty is identifying which materials are suitable and where they are located."

The King turned toward Seraphina.

"You have been reviewing the stores records."

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Can you tell us what is missing?"

"Not yet. Some quantities are unresolved because the transfer records are incomplete. Others are recorded as used, but the corresponding maintenance reports have not been located."

"Can you determine whether any of the materials were diverted?"

"No."

The answer was immediate.

The King studied her.

"Why not?"

"Because a missing transfer record does not prove diversion. A difference between a receipt and an inventory does not prove theft. We have several possible explanations, and we have not ruled them out."

"Then what can you establish?"

"Which materials were ordered, which were received, which were issued, which were recorded as used and which remain physically present. We can also identify where the records stop agreeing."

The King nodded.

"Do that."

He turned to the Treasury minister.

"Provide emergency funds for confirmed requirements. Do not wait for the entire historical reconciliation before authorizing necessary work."

Garrick bowed.

The King then addressed Fenwick.

"Restore the network as safely as possible. The capital does not require a perfect report. It requires a functioning protective system."

Fenwick bowed.

"We understand, Your Majesty."

The evening brought another complication.

The western district's local protective line had remained isolated for nearly twelve hours.

The southern section was still disconnected.

The remaining network was functioning, but the reserve was carrying a greater share of the demand.

The Academy recommended reducing nonessential use further.

The city complied.

Public demonstration halls closed.

Several administrative buildings reduced their circuit use.

The royal gardens' decorative lamps were switched off.

The palace's ceremonial wing was placed on ordinary lighting.

The royal household complained briefly.

The King ignored them.

At the Veyr residence, Duchess Evelyne received a request from the Crown Prince's office.

It asked whether House Veyr could provide temporary financial support for the procurement of specialized materials if the Treasury's emergency allocation proved insufficient.

Evelyne read the request carefully.

Then she called Alaric.

"The capital needs funds."

"How much?"

"They have not given a final amount."

"Then we cannot provide a final amount."

"No."

She placed the request beside the estate accounts.

"But we can establish what is available."

Alaric looked at her.

"Are you considering using the family's reserve?"

"I am considering making it available if the Crown confirms the requirement."

"That reserve exists for the duchy."

"Yes."

"Winter roads, grain, transport and the northern maintenance obligations."

"I know what it exists for."

Alaric studied her expression.

"Then why?"

"Because the capital's protective network is not an ornamental expense. If the interruption spreads, the consequences will not stop at the palace gates."

He was silent.

Evelyne continued.

"I will not release funds without a defined request, a repayment arrangement or a formal allocation. But I will not pretend that our reserves are more important simply because they are ours."

Alaric nodded slowly.

"Prepare the figures."

Evelyne called for Cedric.

The reserve would not be handed over immediately.

But House Veyr was prepared to help.

At nine in the evening, the Academy began preparing the central assembly for controlled maintenance.

The procedure required a temporary isolation of one internal section while the remaining network continued operating.

It was not a repair yet.

It was a diagnostic step.

The technicians needed to determine whether the uneven catalyst distribution could be corrected through cleaning or whether the regulator's contact lining had deteriorated beyond safe use.

Theodore attended the briefing.

Lucien explained the sequence.

"First, we will confirm that the reserve can maintain the unaffected zones. Then we will isolate the central regulator's maintenance section. The remaining network will continue under the approved reduced-demand schedule."

"And if the reserve cannot maintain the load?"

"We will stop the procedure and restore the existing configuration."

"How long will the isolation last?"

"Only as long as necessary to complete the inspection."

Theodore looked at Fenwick.

"Do you approve?"

"I do, provided the reserve readings remain within the specified margin."

Theodore turned to Lucien.

"Proceed when ready."

At ten forty-three, the isolation began.

The Academy's technicians followed the approved sequence.

The central regulator's maintenance section was separated.

The reserve accepted the additional load.

For several minutes, the readings remained stable.

Then the northern zone registered a sudden fluctuation.

The technician reported it immediately.

Lucien checked the reserve.

"Hold."

The reserve contribution increased.

The northern reading recovered.

But the central assembly's response time had worsened.

Fenwick stepped toward the observation board.

"Stop the procedure."

Lucien looked at him.

"We are almost finished."

"Stop."

The technicians restored the previous configuration.

The reserve returned to its earlier operating state.

The northern zone stabilized.

The maintenance section remained untouched.

The procedure had not caused a new interruption.

But it had demonstrated something the Academy had not expected.

The network was now so dependent on the reserve that even a controlled isolation of one maintenance section created a response problem elsewhere.

Theodore looked at the board.

"Can we continue?"

Fenwick shook his head.

"Not tonight."

Lucien removed his gloves.

"We need the central assembly operating normally before we attempt another isolation."

"How do we restore the two outer sections?"

"We cannot do it safely until the central response improves."

Theodore's jaw tightened.

"Then what is the next step?"

Lucien looked at the inspection report.

"Find the correct catalyst supply. Confirm the replacement lining. Prepare the central regulator for repair."

The prince nodded.

"Do it."

Near midnight, Seraphina was given the revised materials list.

The central stores had confirmed a limited quantity of suitable catalyst.

The Academy's secondary stores had two containers awaiting final identification.

The third container was still under examination.

The western depot's C-4/R material was not approved for use in the central assembly.

The three containers recorded as used in emergency maintenance remained under investigation.

The list was not reassuring.

It was, however, clearer than the previous one.

Seraphina placed it beside the technical report.

Then she noticed a small notation near the bottom.

**Additional C-4 stabilizing compound — reserve allocation, original project reference: CAP/PROT/12-6.**

The quantity was not stated.

Neither was the storage location.

She looked at the stores representative.

"What is this?"

He leaned over.

"An older reserve allocation."

"Is it included in the current inventory?"

"I don't believe so."

"Why not?"

"It was held under a separate project account."

"Where?"

He searched the attached record.

"Central Reserve Materials Depot."

"Is that the same as central stores?"

"No, my lady. It was a separate facility."

Lucien looked up.

"How much material?"

The representative shook his head.

"The record does not say."

Seraphina read the reference again.

CAP/PROT/12-6.

The capital protective network's second modernization.

Twelve years earlier.

The same period in which the central maintenance schedule had been revised.

"Can the depot be inspected?"

The representative looked toward Theodore.

The prince answered.

"Yes. First thing tomorrow."

Seraphina folded the paper.

She had no idea whether the material still existed.

She did not know whether it was suitable for the current network.

She did not know whether it had ever been used.

But it was the first record that suggested the kingdom might have another source of the required catalyst.

Outside, the western and southern districts remained without their outer protective coverage.

The city watch continued its patrols.

The infirmaries kept their ordinary lamps burning.

The Academy's technicians watched the central assembly.

And the reserve, still active, continued carrying more responsibility than it had been designed to bear for so long.

At twelve minutes past midnight, a new observation was entered.

**Central response delay continues. Reserve contribution elevated. Two outer sections remain isolated.**

Below it, Master Fenwick added a handwritten instruction.

**No further isolation until adequate replacement materials are confirmed.**

The capital had not fallen.

But the people responsible for protecting it had finally run out of room to pretend that another inspection would be enough.
