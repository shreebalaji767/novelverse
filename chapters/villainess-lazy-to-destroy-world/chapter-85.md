# **Chapter 85 — The Test That Had Ended Without an Answer**

The Royal Maintenance Office had a peculiar way of responding to requests.

It acknowledged them.

Then it acknowledged that it had acknowledged them.

Then, several days later, it sometimes explained that the original request had been sent to another department.

Seraphina had encountered this procedure before.

She did not enjoy encountering it again.

"The equipment log has not been found?" she asked.

Cedric looked at the letter in his hand.

"No."

"Has the office searched the correct archive?"

"It says the archive was searched."

"Which archive?"

Cedric read the next paragraph.

"The central maintenance records."

"That is not an archive. That is a description of several rooms."

"I had the same concern."

"Did you ask for the inventory?"

"Yes."

"And?"

"They have sent us a list of the records they searched."

Seraphina held out her hand.

Cedric passed her the document.

It contained fourteen entries.

Six concerned modern network maintenance. Four concerned historical equipment. Two concerned staff assignments. One concerned a general transfer register.

The final entry read:

*Royal Maintenance Office — Central Protective Network — Technical Test Attachments.*

Seraphina looked at it.

"That sounds promising."

"It is the only entry that might contain the log."

"Then why did they not find it?"

"The record states that the attachment was not present."

"Was the folder present?"

Cedric looked down again.

"The folder is not mentioned."

Seraphina returned the list.

"Ask them whether they searched the folder or only the index."

Cedric nodded.

"Anything else?"

"Ask whether the equipment log was expected to be filed with the test summary or separately."

"That is a reasonable question."

"Yes. It is also the kind of question that should have been asked before someone declared the archive searched."

Cedric folded the list.

"I will write to them."

Seraphina returned to her breakfast.

She had no intention of spending her morning chasing a missing attachment.

The Academy could do that.

The Royal Maintenance Office could do that.

The Royal Infrastructure Department could do that.

There were enough people in the capital whose entire careers involved finding things that other people had misplaced.

She hoped they were being paid appropriately.

The Royal Maintenance Office replied three days later.

The folder had been found.

It had been stored under the test's equipment reference rather than the final review reference.

The folder contained the installation record, removal record, instrument calibration sheets and a transfer receipt for the monitoring assembly.

The equipment log itself was missing.

However, the folder included a note from the test supervisor.

**Final assessment deferred pending comparison with the eastern distribution circuit's revised operating conditions.**

The note was dated six years earlier.

Beneath it was a second entry.

**Comparison to be completed after receipt of the revised regional operating schedule.**

There was no date.

There was no signature.

There was no indication that the comparison had taken place.

Lucien brought the folder to the Academy.

Master Fenwick examined it with the patience of a man who had learned that expressing frustration did not make documents appear.

"At least we have the test conditions," he said.

Lucien placed a second folder beside it.

"And the instrument readings."

"Where did you find those?"

"The Academy's calibration archive. They were filed with the instruments rather than the test."

Master Fenwick looked at the documents.

"Then we can reconstruct the test."

"Most of it."

"What is missing?"

"The operator's observation log."

"Could the readings be interpreted without it?"

"Some of them. Not all."

Master Fenwick turned a page.

"Then we will distinguish what can be established from what cannot."

Lucien nodded.

The test had been conducted using a temporary monitoring assembly connected between the eastern distribution circuit and the reserve zone.

The purpose was to measure how the two behaved under a revised load arrangement.

The initial readings were stable.

The reserve contribution was then reduced in stages.

At the second stage, the eastern circuit showed a rise in distribution pressure.

The reserve zone compensated.

The test supervisor stopped the reduction before the readings reached the upper operating threshold.

No damage was recorded.

The temporary assembly was removed.

The final assessment was deferred.

The Academy's surviving data did not show whether the eastern circuit could operate independently under the revised conditions.

It showed only that the reserve zone had responded to the change.

Lucien placed the readings beside the current network model.

"The current model assumes the eastern and western circuits can operate independently within their ordinary ranges."

"Does the test contradict that assumption?" Master Fenwick asked.

"Not conclusively."

"Does it support it?"

"Not conclusively."

Master Fenwick looked at him.

"Then we have established that the test was inconclusive."

Lucien sighed.

"Yes."

"That is still a finding."

"It is a very unsatisfying one."

"Most accurate findings are."

The Academy's technical team requested the original regional operating schedule used during the six-year-old test.

The Department located a copy.

It was not the same schedule currently used by the maintenance team.

The older version contained an additional instruction:

**Where the eastern circuit exhibits sustained upward variation during reduction of reserve contribution, the test shall be suspended pending confirmation of the circuit's independent balancing capacity.**

The newer version retained the ordinary balancing procedure but omitted the instruction about independent capacity.

The change had been made four years earlier.

No accompanying technical report had been found.

Lucien read the amendment.

"Someone removed the restriction."

Master Fenwick looked at him.

"That is not what the document says."

"It removes the sentence."

"It does not establish why."

"Perhaps it was considered unnecessary."

"Perhaps."

"Perhaps the eastern circuit had been modified."

"Also possible."

Lucien put the paper down.

"Then we need the technical report."

"Yes."

Theodore, who had joined the review, examined the amendment date.

"Who approved it?"

The Department's representative checked the reference.

"The amendment was issued by the Regional Maintenance Authority under a delegated operating procedure."

"Who signed it?"

"Master Elias Venn, then acting superintendent."

"Is he still in service?"

"No. He retired three years ago."

"Can he be contacted?"

"Yes."

Theodore nodded.

"Arrange an interview. Not an accusation. We need to know what information supported the change."

Master Fenwick agreed.

Seraphina was not present at the meeting.

She was at the palace garden, where Evelyne had insisted that she accompany her for a short walk.

The Duchess had a habit of arranging walks whenever she believed her daughter had spent too much time indoors.

Seraphina suspected that this was a form of punishment disguised as concern.

"You have been reading the same book for four days," Evelyne said.

"I am reading it carefully."

"You have not turned a page since yesterday."

"I am considering the author's choices."

Evelyne looked amused.

"Are the choices particularly difficult?"

"The protagonist has accepted responsibility for an entire province after discovering that its accounts are inaccurate."

"Perhaps she is ambitious."

"Perhaps she has never met an estate administrator."

Evelyne laughed.

They continued along the garden path.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Evelyne asked, "Has the Academy made progress?"

"Some."

"Is that good?"

"It means they have found enough information to understand what they still don't know."

"That sounds like progress."

"It is, apparently."

Evelyne stopped beside a stone bench.

"Do you feel responsible for the investigation?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"No."

"Not even a little?"

"I noticed that the register and the maintenance instructions disagreed. I did not design the network, operate it, approve its modifications or remove its records."

"Then why do you keep following the reports?"

"Because the capital's protective network is important, and I would prefer people not make decisions based on incomplete information."

Evelyne nodded.

"That is a reasonable answer."

Seraphina sat down.

"Please do not tell anyone. It may damage my reputation."

Master Elias Venn arrived at the Academy the following week.

He was a thin, elderly man with neatly combed grey hair and a habit of reading every document before answering a question about it.

The interview was attended by Lucien, Master Fenwick, Theodore and two Department representatives.

Seraphina received a summary afterward.

Venn confirmed that he had approved the operating schedule amendment four years earlier.

He had not personally removed the original restriction.

The amendment had been prepared by the Regional Maintenance Authority's technical review section.

The authority had received a report stating that the eastern circuit had been adjusted to operate within the revised distribution range.

The report recommended removing the old restriction because the circuit was no longer expected to require the same level of reserve compensation.

Venn had reviewed the recommendation and approved the revised schedule.

"Did he inspect the supporting measurements?" Seraphina asked when Lucien explained the interview.

"According to his statement, he reviewed the summary."

"Not the original readings?"

"No."

"Who prepared the summary?"

"Master Venn could not recall the individual. He believes it came from the authority's technical review section."

"Did he have authority to approve the amendment?"

"Yes."

"Then the question is whether the technical review section had adequate evidence."

Lucien nodded.

"Precisely."

"What happened to the report?"

"Venn believes it was attached to the amendment file."

"Was it?"

"The file contains the recommendation, but not the supporting report."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Another missing attachment."

"Yes."

"At least this one has a known purpose."

Lucien did not appear comforted.

The Academy had now established three distinct stages.

The original network used a scheduled balancing interval.

The six-year-old test showed that reducing the reserve contribution affected the eastern circuit, but the test was inconclusive.

Four years ago, the restriction was removed after a technical review recommended that the eastern circuit could operate under revised conditions.

The supporting report was missing.

The modern network had continued operating with the reserve zone active.

Nothing in the evidence proved that the system was unsafe.

But the decision to remove the restriction had not yet been independently verified.

The Royal Infrastructure Department requested a temporary suspension of any further changes to the reserve zone.

The request was approved.

The maintenance team would continue its established routine balancing procedure.

The Academy would not repeat the isolation test.

The Directorate would review the original design assumptions and the later technical amendment.

The Department would retrieve the supporting report or establish whether it had ever been completed.

Theodore asked for a public-facing summary that avoided unnecessary alarm.

The first draft described the review as an investigation into "potential deficiencies in the capital protective network."

Seraphina saw the wording during a brief administrative review.

She placed a line through the phrase.

The Department's representative looked surprised.

"Is there a problem?"

"Yes."

"What would you prefer?"

"An assessment of the reserve zone's operating requirements and the documentation supporting its current designation."

"That sounds less urgent."

"It is less dramatic."

"Would that not understate the issue?"

"It would describe the issue we actually have."

The representative considered this.

"Some members of the Council may want stronger wording."

"Then they should wait until the technical findings justify it."

Theodore, standing near the window, looked at the revised sentence.

"Use it."

The representative nodded.

Seraphina returned the draft.

She had no desire to become responsible for the public description of a technical review.

She had already learned that a poorly worded sentence could travel farther than the evidence supporting it.

The missing report was located at the end of the month.

It had not been destroyed.

It had been filed under a different project reference.

The document was titled:

**Eastern Distribution Circuit — Independent Capacity Assessment.**

It was twenty-three pages long.

The report described modifications made to the eastern circuit four years earlier.

The modifications had increased its distribution capacity and altered the relationship between its control assembly and the central reserve zone.

The assessment concluded that the eastern circuit could operate independently under ordinary demand conditions.

However, it also identified a limitation.

Under sustained high demand, the eastern circuit's distribution pressure could rise more quickly than the revised model predicted.

The reserve zone was therefore retained as a balancing support.

The report recommended that the scheduled balancing interval continue until a further assessment could establish whether the eastern circuit's revised control assembly performed consistently under seasonal variations.

The final paragraph was particularly important.

**The revised operating model may be adopted for ordinary planning purposes. This assessment does not authorize removal of the reserve balancing procedure or independent operation of the eastern circuit under sustained high demand.**

Lucien read the paragraph aloud.

Master Fenwick closed his eyes briefly.

"Then the amendment four years ago was not supported by the report."

"Not entirely," Lucien said.

"Did the report recommend removing the restriction?"

"No. It recommended retaining the procedure."

"Then why was the restriction removed?"

"We still need to establish that."

Theodore examined the document.

"Could the amendment have been based on another report?"

"Possibly," the Department representative replied. "But we have not found one."

"Then we should not assume the amendment was authorized by this assessment."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Agreed."

The technical team compared the report's measurements with the current maintenance records.

The reserve zone's routine balancing activity was consistent with the report's recommendation.

The central model, however, did not reflect that recommendation.

The maintenance schedule had preserved the procedure.

The central register had not.

The four-year-old amendment had removed the restriction without the supporting assessment authorizing that change.

There was no evidence that the maintenance staff had been instructed to stop balancing.

There was no evidence that the reserve zone had been deliberately disabled.

There was also no evidence that the capital's network had suffered an interruption.

But the administrative decision had created a gap between what the system was designed to do and what its central records claimed it could do.

The King received the preliminary findings in a closed Council meeting.

Seraphina was not invited.

Alaric attended as a northern representative because the capital's protective network was part of the wider national infrastructure review.

When he returned, he found Seraphina in the family library.

"The Academy has completed its comparison," he said.

She looked up.

"Is the network safe?"

"The network remains operational. The reserve zone is being maintained under its existing procedure."

"Good."

"The technical report confirms that the eastern circuit may require reserve balancing under sustained high demand."

"That sounds important."

"It is."

"Has the central model been corrected?"

"Not yet. The Directorate will prepare a revised version after the technical review is completed."

"Has the four-year-old amendment been withdrawn?"

"Temporarily suspended pending a formal review of its authority."

Seraphina nodded.

"And the person who approved it?"

"Master Venn followed the recommendation submitted to him. He has cooperated with the review."

"Then he should not be blamed for the missing supporting report."

Alaric looked at her.

"The Council has not accused him."

"Good."

"There will be further questions about how the technical review section prepared its recommendation."

"That is reasonable."

Alaric sat across from her.

"The King wants the capital network fully reassessed before the next major winter period."

Seraphina closed her book.

"How long?"

"Several months, perhaps longer."

"Then they should begin soon."

"They have."

She looked at him.

"Does the King intend to appoint another committee?"

Alaric smiled.

"Yes."

"How many people?"

"Nine."

Seraphina stared at him.

"Why?"

"Technical specialists, maintenance representatives, the Department, the Academy, the Directorate, Treasury and the Royal Council."

"That is a lot of people."

"It is a complicated system."

"Then I hope they have a large table."

Alaric laughed.

The new review group began work the following week.

Its first decision was to preserve the existing operating arrangement until the technical assessment was complete.

Its second was to reconstruct the sequence of modifications made to the eastern circuit over the past twelve years.

Its third was to identify all current procedures that depended on the reserve zone.

No one proposed an immediate shutdown.

No one proposed activating the old underground network.

The old C-2 conduit remained confirmed inactive.

The seventh zone remained part of the modern protective network.

The review was not a search for a hidden magical catastrophe.

It was an attempt to establish whether the capital's current system had been properly understood, documented and maintained.

Seraphina received a copy of the new administrative register two weeks later.

It contained separate entries for:

- Original design.
- Modernization authorization.
- Technical assessment.
- Operating instructions.
- Maintenance procedure.
- Current operating status.
- Outstanding technical questions.
- Administrative approval.

For the first time, the reserve zone's entry included a clear note:

**Current status: Active within the modern capital protective network. Routine balancing function retained. Independent operation under sustained high demand not yet established.**

Seraphina read it twice.

Then she placed the register aside.

Mira, who had been waiting with a fresh cup of tea, looked at her.

"Is it finally finished?"

"No."

"Is something wrong?"

"Not with the register."

Mira waited.

Seraphina picked up her cup.

"The engineers have discovered that the eastern circuit's control assembly was modified again during the first year of the last modernization."

Mira blinked.

"Again?"

"Yes."

"Does that mean another report is missing?"

Seraphina looked at her.

"Why would you say that?"

Mira hesitated.

"Because the last few reports were."

Seraphina took a sip of tea.

"That is a deeply unfortunate pattern."

She opened the new register.

The latest modification had been authorized twelve years earlier, shortly before the reserve zone was classified as transitional.

Its purpose was listed as improving distribution stability during seasonal load changes.

The work had been completed.

The installation had been inspected.

The maintenance team had accepted the modified assembly.

But the technical assessment attached to the modification referred to a separate test of the eastern circuit's response under a reduced reserve contribution.

That test had been conducted in the same month as the original reserve-zone designation.

Its reference number was different from the six-year-old test.

And the Academy had not yet found its results.

Seraphina closed the register.

"Perhaps," she said, "they should stop modifying things until they can find the paperwork for the previous modification."

Mira considered this.

"Would that be possible?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because that would be sensible."

Outside, the capital continued its ordinary work.

The protective network remained active.

The eastern and western districts received their usual supply.

The maintenance staff followed the existing balancing schedule.

Nothing had failed.

But the review group now had a new task: establish what the eastern circuit had been designed to do after its final modification, and whether the reserve zone's role had changed at the same time.

The next technical file was requested from the Royal Infrastructure Department.

It had been transferred to the Central Technical Evaluation Board.

The Board's archive index recorded that the test results had been reviewed.

The final recommendation, however, was listed as **not issued**.

And the test supervisor's name had been crossed out of the original assignment register.
