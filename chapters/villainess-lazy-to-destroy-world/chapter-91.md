# **Chapter 91 — The Fifth Control Point Was Not on the Map**

The fifth control point had three names.

This was the first difficulty.

The original capital protection schedule called it **Central Junction Five**.

A later construction record referred to it as **Northern Distribution Interface**.

The modern technical register listed something called **Central Interface C**, although no one had yet established whether the three references described the same location.

Lucien disliked all three names.

"They are not interchangeable," he said.

Master Fenwick looked at the documents spread across the conference table.

"Then why have they been treated as interchangeable?"

"Because the modern register copied the reference from the later construction summary."

"Which copied it from?"

"The twelve-year modernization report."

"And that report?"

Lucien turned a page.

"It refers to the Northern Distribution Interface."

Theodore looked at the original schedule.

"Which is called Central Junction Five here."

"Yes."

"Can we establish whether they are the same place?"

"Not yet."

Seraphina was present only to review the administrative register.

She had already learned that old infrastructure documents could contain several names for one physical object, one name for several objects, or a name that referred to a proposed structure that had never been built.

She looked at the five entries.

"Do we have coordinates for the original control points?"

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Approximate locations."

"Do we have construction measurements?"

"For four."

"And the fifth?"

"Only a distance from the old central chamber."

Seraphina examined the schedule.

The fifth point was described as being located north-east of the original control chamber, near the old administrative aqueduct.

"Was the aqueduct part of the protective network?"

"No," Lucien said. "It was an ordinary water distribution structure."

"Then why is it used as a reference?"

"Because it was a permanent landmark."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Was it?"

Lucien paused.

The aqueduct had been altered twice.

Its northern section had been replaced during a road expansion, and the original stone channel had been enclosed beneath a later administrative building.

The landmark was not entirely permanent.

Seraphina returned to the register.

"Then the location estimate needs to account for the changes to the aqueduct."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"We can compare the old survey with the present street plan."

"That sounds like a task for the survey office."

"It is."

Seraphina closed the folder.

"Excellent."

Lucien looked faintly amused.

She had not intended to contribute anything further.

The survey office began with the original construction map.

The map was eighty years old.

Its northern edge included the aqueduct, an administrative courtyard and a service road that no longer existed.

The fifth control point was marked with a small square.

There was no detailed drawing.

The accompanying schedule described it as a junction where the northern protective branch met the central distribution route.

The surveyors compared the measurements with the present street layout.

The old courtyard had been divided into three properties.

The service road had become a covered passage.

The aqueduct had been rerouted.

The approximate position of the fifth control point fell beneath the western section of the current administrative records building.

That did not establish that anything remained there.

The building had been constructed thirty-six years earlier.

Its foundations had been inspected during construction.

The surviving structural report contained no reference to a magical control chamber.

The Department requested the original foundation plans.

They arrived two days later.

The plans showed a reinforced service corridor beneath the building.

It contained water pipes, drainage channels, electrical conduits and a narrow stone passage.

There was no marked magical chamber.

Lucien examined the plans.

"Could the control point have been incorporated into the foundation?"

"Possibly," Master Fenwick said. "But we need more than a possible location."

Theodore looked at the building's structural report.

"Was the site examined before construction?"

"Yes."

"By whom?"

"The Royal Works Department."

"Did they know about the historical schedule?"

The representative searched the file.

"No reference to it appears in the construction correspondence."

"Then the original control point may have been removed before the building was constructed."

"Or it may never have existed as a separate chamber," Lucien said.

Theodore nodded.

"Continue the comparison."

The building's current facilities manager was not pleased when the Department requested access to the lower service corridor.

He had recently arranged a replacement of the main drainage pump.

The corridor was already crowded with equipment, and the proposed inspection would require temporary access restrictions.

He read the request twice.

"Are you planning to excavate?"

"No," Lucien replied.

"Are you planning to remove any walls?"

"Not at this stage."

"Will the water supply be interrupted?"

"Not for the initial inspection."

The facilities manager looked at the documents.

"Then why do you need access?"

"To compare the existing structure with the historical location reference."

The manager sighed.

"I have a building full of clerks who will ask why their work has been interrupted."

"We will keep the inspection limited."

"How limited?"

"Two hours."

The manager looked at the schedule.

"Three. I need time to arrange access."

Lucien agreed.

The inspection was scheduled for Thursday morning.

The facilities manager sent a notice to the building's occupants.

The notice described the work as a structural and historical survey of the lower service corridor.

No one mentioned a missing magical control point.

The inspection revealed a narrow stone recess behind a later masonry partition.

It was not a chamber.

It was not an active magical installation.

It contained no visible circuit.

The stonework was older than the building.

A small rectangular plate had been fixed to the inner wall.

Its inscription was partly obscured by mineral deposits.

Lucien examined it without touching the surface.

The visible characters appeared to read:

**C.J. — V**

The fifth control point was listed as Central Junction Five.

The mark was suggestive.

It was not conclusive.

The Academy's conservator photographed the plate and examined the surrounding masonry.

The recess appeared to have been incorporated into the later building foundation.

No active mana flow was detected.

No circuit components were visible.

The stonework showed signs of an earlier opening that had been sealed.

Master Fenwick examined the photographs.

"Could this be the control point?"

"It could be a remnant associated with it," Lucien replied.

"Is that the same thing?"

"No."

Theodore looked at the plate.

"Can we establish what was behind the sealed section?"

"Not without a separate structural assessment."

"Would that require opening the wall?"

"Possibly."

"Is there a reason to do so immediately?"

Lucien considered the question.

"Not yet."

The inspection was concluded.

The recess was photographed, measured and entered into the building's structural register.

The Academy did not request excavation.

The fifth control point remained unconfirmed.

Seraphina received the inspection summary on Friday.

She was in the estate accounts room, reviewing a proposal from the northern agricultural office.

The proposal concerned the repair of a shared irrigation channel.

The channel served four fields belonging to three different landowners.

The proposed cost distribution was based on the length of the channel passing through each property.

Seraphina had already rejected the first calculation.

The length of a channel did not necessarily reflect the amount of water each field received.

She was waiting for the revised estimate when Mira brought in the Academy's report.

Seraphina read it.

The report described a sealed stone recess, an old plate and an uncertain relationship to Central Junction Five.

The conclusion stated:

*The location contains historic masonry and a possible remnant associated with the original capital protection programme. No active circuit or functioning control assembly has been identified. Further investigation would require structural authorization and is not currently recommended.*

Seraphina placed the report beside the irrigation proposal.

Mira glanced at it.

"Did they find the missing point?"

"No."

"Did they find something?"

"An old wall."

Mira looked at the report.

"That sounds less impressive."

"It is probably more useful."

She returned to the irrigation calculation.

The capital network's second-stage review continued.

The central and southern zones had passed their inspections.

The northern zone required a minor correction to its maintenance register.

The western zone had a replacement component whose supplier reference had been updated.

The reserve zone remained under enhanced observation.

The eastern circuit's response had improved after the support bracket correction.

The revised operating schedule had been issued.

The system was functioning within its established limits.

There was no immediate reason to interrupt normal operation.

The Academy's work was therefore divided into two parts.

The first concerned the modern network's present condition.

The second concerned the historical structures that had been incorporated, modified or removed during earlier construction.

The fifth control point belonged to the second category.

So did the old sealed recess.

Neither was considered an active part of the current protective network.

The distinction was included in the monthly report to the King.

He approved the next stage.

A week later, the Royal Infrastructure Department received an unusual request.

It came from the Office of Administrative Buildings.

The office wanted to know whether the Academy's inspection of the lower service corridor had identified any structure that could affect the building's foundations.

The request was understandable.

The facilities manager had noticed that the inspection report referred to a sealed recess and historic masonry.

He did not want a future construction project to encounter an undocumented structure.

The Department responded that no immediate structural concern had been identified.

However, the sealed section had not been opened.

Any future work affecting that wall would require a separate assessment.

The building manager accepted the response.

The matter was added to the building's maintenance file.

No further action was taken.

The first warning came from the western zone.

It was not dramatic.

A maintenance operator recorded a brief fluctuation in the outer protective reading during a routine morning check.

The reading returned to its previous level within seconds.

The operator repeated the measurement.

The result was normal.

He recorded the fluctuation and submitted the report.

The regional maintenance supervisor reviewed it.

The report was classified as an isolated variation.

No operating limit had been approached.

The central monitoring office received a copy.

It was added to the weekly record.

Lucien saw it during his review.

He compared it with the previous readings.

There was no clear pattern.

The western zone had recorded occasional short variations in the past.

Most had occurred during changes in environmental mana conditions.

The Academy requested the operator's original measurements.

They arrived two days later.

The first reading had been taken during a period of increased demand in the western administrative district.

The second had been taken after the demand had fallen.

The difference was small.

The Academy found no reason to alter the operating schedule.

The incident was closed as an isolated variation.

Three days later, the northern zone recorded a similar fluctuation.

This time the reading did not return to its previous level immediately.

It stabilized after the reserve system adjusted.

The maintenance operator reported the event.

The supervisor compared it with the western report.

Both had occurred during periods of increased demand.

Neither had exceeded its operating limit.

The central monitoring office requested additional readings.

Lucien received the reports that evening.

He read them carefully.

The two events were separated by several days and involved different zones.

The circumstances were not identical.

They were also not entirely unrelated.

He requested the reserve zone's recent observation records.

The records showed that the reserve system had made several small adjustments during the same period.

Each adjustment had remained within the approved range.

There was no evidence of a fault.

Lucien brought the reports to Master Fenwick.

"Do you think the reserve zone is compensating for an unexpected change?"

"I don't know."

"Could the two fluctuations be unrelated?"

"Yes."

"Could they be connected through the central distribution system?"

"Possibly."

Master Fenwick looked at the readings.

"Then we need more information before we decide."

Lucien agreed.

The Academy prepared a short observation schedule.

It would compare all seven zones over several operating periods.

The network would remain in its ordinary configuration.

No deliberate reduction of reserve contribution would be performed.

The test would be observational.

Theodore approved it.

The observation began on Monday.

For the first two days, the readings were normal.

The western and northern zones showed ordinary variations.

The eastern circuit remained stable.

The reserve zone made small adjustments.

Nothing unusual was recorded.

On Wednesday afternoon, the central monitoring office reported a short interruption in the data from the northern zone.

The monitoring instrument had stopped transmitting for less than a minute.

The local operator confirmed that the physical circuit had remained active.

The monitoring instrument was inspected.

A loose connection was found in its external signal cable.

The cable was replaced.

The event was recorded as an instrumentation fault.

Lucien reviewed the report.

It was a reasonable explanation.

But he requested that the Academy compare the local instrument readings with the central monitoring records for the same period.

The comparison showed a brief difference.

The local instrument had recorded a small fluctuation.

The central record had not.

The missing data interval explained why.

The circuit itself had not stopped operating.

The two reports were consistent once the monitoring interruption was accounted for.

Lucien closed the review.

He was careful not to turn every discrepancy into a network failure.

On Thursday morning, the southern zone recorded a short variation.

It was smaller than the northern event.

The operator submitted the report immediately.

The reserve system adjusted.

The readings returned to their established range.

The central monitoring office requested a second measurement.

The second measurement showed a similar variation.

The Academy compared the result with the previous week's records.

The pattern was still too weak to establish a common cause.

Lucien reviewed the reports in silence.

Three zones had now recorded brief variations over a period of eleven days.

The reserve zone had compensated each time.

The capital network remained stable.

But the events were no longer isolated to one region.

Master Fenwick examined the combined chart.

"Could this be a change in demand?"

"Possibly."

"Environmental conditions?"

"Also possible."

"Instrument error?"

"We have ruled it out for two of the events. The southern readings are consistent."

"Then what do you recommend?"

"Continue observation. Compare the central distribution readings against the original operating model."

Fenwick nodded.

"Do that."

The comparison took longer than expected.

The modern operating model described the seven zones as separate distribution areas connected through a central balancing structure.

The reserve zone adjusted the differences between the eastern and western circuits.

The central structure maintained the overall distribution.

The model assumed that the zones would respond within their approved ranges under ordinary variations in demand.

The recent readings suggested that several zones were experiencing small changes at roughly the same time.

The changes were not large enough to indicate a fault.

They were, however, distributed across the network.

Lucien examined the model.

The central distribution structure was functioning within its approved range.

The reserve zone was also functioning correctly.

No individual zone showed a sustained abnormal reading.

The difficulty was that the variations did not appear to originate from one zone.

They appeared to be occurring across several.

Master Fenwick looked at the chart.

"Could the central structure be responding to the combined demand?"

"That is what we need to determine."

"Is there a safe way to measure it?"

"Yes. We can compare the current readings against the historical baseline without changing the operating configuration."

"Then do that."

The historical baseline was retrieved from the Academy's archive.

It contained five years of monitoring records.

The first comparison showed that the current readings were slightly more variable than the earlier baseline.

The difference was small.

The old records had also been collected using instruments with different calibration intervals.

A direct comparison would therefore be unreliable.

The Academy requested the original instrument calibration records.

Some were available.

Others were stored under the central monitoring office's maintenance reference.

The Department began retrieving them.

Meanwhile, the network continued operating normally.

The King received a brief update.

He asked whether the recent variations required any public notice.

Lucien advised against it.

"There is no evidence of a public service interruption or immediate safety concern. The observations are being reviewed."

The King accepted the recommendation.

Theodore instructed the Department to continue monitoring and to keep the regional maintenance teams informed.

No emergency declaration was issued.

No restrictions were placed on the city.

On Friday evening, Lucien received the first calibrated comparison.

The readings from the western, northern and southern zones showed a small but consistent increase in variation during periods of elevated demand.

The difference was within the approved operating range.

The reserve zone had compensated each time.

The central distribution structure had not exceeded its limits.

However, the variation was present in all three zones.

Lucien checked the timestamps.

The three events had occurred at different times.

The demand conditions were not identical.

The environmental readings were also different.

He compared the data again.

The variation was not explained by one local event.

He sent the results to Master Fenwick.

Fenwick arrived at the Academy shortly afterward.

"Have you found the cause?"

"No."

"Have you found a common pattern?"

"Yes."

"How significant?"

"Not enough to declare a fault. Enough to justify a closer examination of the central distribution structure."

Fenwick looked at the chart.

"Could the reserve zone continue compensating?"

"Under the conditions observed, yes."

"And if the variations increase?"

"We would need to reassess."

Fenwick studied the readings.

"Then we should not wait for them to increase."

Lucien nodded.

The Academy prepared a controlled inspection of the central distribution structure.

It would be performed without reducing the reserve contribution.

The inspection required access to the central monitoring chamber and the adjacent distribution assembly.

The Department approved the work.

The inspection was scheduled for the following Tuesday.

Seraphina received the request for administrative review on Saturday.

The document described the proposed inspection as a routine comparison of the central distribution structure against its historical operating baseline.

She read the scope.

It included physical inspection, instrument calibration, maintenance history and comparison of recent readings.

It did not include dismantling the central assembly.

It did not include reducing the reserve zone's contribution.

It did not include altering the operating schedule.

She approved the administrative description.

Then she noticed a reference at the bottom.

**Inspection access: Central Monitoring Chamber — Lower Service Level.**

She looked at the building plan attached to the request.

The chamber was beneath the Royal Infrastructure Department's main administrative building.

It was not the old sealed recess beneath the records building.

It was a separate modern facility.

She returned to the request.

The inspection was properly scoped.

The access arrangements were clear.

The technical team had identified the necessary stopping conditions.

She signed the administrative review.

Mira placed the next letter on her desk.

"Is that the last one?"

"For today."

"Then perhaps you can rest."

Seraphina looked at the stack of estate correspondence.

"Perhaps."

She put the Academy request aside.

Outside, the afternoon light faded over the northern grounds.

The capital's protective network continued to operate.

The eastern circuit was stable.

The reserve zone was compensating normally.

The three reported fluctuations remained within the approved limits.

And on Tuesday, the engineers would examine the central distribution structure.

They expected to find an ordinary maintenance issue.

They had no reason yet to expect anything else.
