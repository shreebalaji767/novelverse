# **Chapter 76 — The Manual That Had Been Correct for the Wrong Reason**

The Royal Academy sent its preliminary findings on a Wednesday.

Seraphina was not expecting them.

She had expected a letter from Margaret Ellison about the curtains, a delivery notice from the northern grain merchants, and possibly another request from the military office.

She had not expected a technical report.

Mira placed the document beside her breakfast.

"Master Arkwright sent this personally."

Seraphina looked at the envelope.

"Did he say whether it was important?"

"He said it was preliminary."

"That was not my question."

Mira considered.

"He also said the Academy would appreciate your attention."

Seraphina sighed.

That was close enough to an answer.

She opened the report.

The Academy had completed its first comparison between the historical winter operating manual and the modern regional maintenance guides.

The findings were not alarming.

They were, however, unusual.

The modern guides contained several operating limits that had originally appeared in the old manual.

Those limits had been copied into later documents without the technical explanation that accompanied them.

Over time, the numbers had become standard references.

The underlying assumptions had not.

Seraphina read the summary twice.

Then she turned to the final page.

The Academy had identified one particular limit that required further examination.

It concerned the acceptable difference in mana flow between a primary circuit and its balancing circuit during periods of severe cold.

The historical manual gave a maximum permitted difference.

The modern guide repeated the same figure.

But the original manual described the figure as a temporary operating limit under specific conditions.

The modern guide described it as a general maintenance threshold.

Seraphina put down her spoon.

"That is not the same thing."

Mira looked at the document.

"Is something wrong?"

"Possibly."

She read the paragraph again.

"Or someone copied a number without copying the reason for it."

Mira nodded politely.

Seraphina doubted that she understood the distinction.

That was acceptable.

Neither did Seraphina understand the magical calculations.

But she understood that a rule could become misleading when separated from the conditions under which it had been written.

She folded the report.

"Send a reply thanking Master Arkwright."

"Anything else?"

"Ask him to send the complete historical manual, including its appendices."

Mira wrote it down.

Seraphina returned to breakfast.

The kingdom had discovered another problem.

At least this one was being examined by people who knew what they were doing.

Lucien arrived two days later.

He brought the original manual.

It was an old leather-bound volume with a cracked spine and several loose pages.

Seraphina examined it.

"How old?"

"Fifty-two years."

"That is older than some of the estate staff."

Lucien smiled.

"Some of the estate staff may find that offensive."

"Then they should avoid being compared to books."

He opened the manual carefully.

The first section described the construction of the northern winter regulation network.

The second contained operating instructions.

The third contained maintenance procedures.

The fourth was a collection of technical notes.

The final section included a series of amendments.

Seraphina turned the pages.

"Where is the relevant limit?"

Lucien pointed to a table.

The figure appeared beside a note explaining that the maximum difference applied only when the balancing circuit was operating under a reduced winter load.

A separate table described normal operating conditions.

The modern maintenance guide had retained the first figure.

It had not retained the distinction.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Why?"

"That is what we are trying to establish."

"Was the second table removed?"

"No. It appears in the original manual."

"Was it included in the modern guide?"

"No."

"Was the modern guide based on a different technical standard?"

"Not entirely. It was compiled from several regional manuals."

Seraphina looked at the pages.

"So someone copied the first table and left out the second."

"That is possible."

"Can you tell who?"

"Not yet."

Lucien turned to the amendments.

There were four.

The first concerned replacement materials.

The second concerned the maintenance interval.

The third changed the winter operating procedure.

The fourth referred to a technical review.

The fourth amendment was incomplete.

It contained a reference number but no attached report.

Lucien tapped the page.

"That is our next lead."

Seraphina looked at the number.

**WMR/TECH/18-4.**

"Do you know what it means?"

"Not yet."

"Then why are you smiling?"

Lucien looked at her.

"Because we have found a reference."

Seraphina closed the book.

"References are not answers."

"No."

"Please remember that."

He laughed.

The Academy's first technical test took place at a controlled facility outside Aurelion.

The test used a model circuit built to reproduce the conditions described in the old manual.

It did not involve any active regional infrastructure.

The Academy tested the historical operating limit under three conditions.

Normal flow.

Reduced winter flow.

And fluctuating load.

The first two produced results consistent with the old manual.

The third produced a wider variation than expected.

The difference was not dangerous within the controlled model.

But it was larger than the modern maintenance guide would have permitted.

The Academy repeated the test.

The result was similar.

Lucien sent the findings to Seraphina.

She read them in the afternoon.

The technical report explained that the modern guide's general threshold was too restrictive under fluctuating conditions.

It could cause maintenance teams to classify harmless variation as a fault.

But the same guide could also fail to identify a different kind of instability because it did not account for the relationship between the primary and balancing circuits.

The issue was not that the threshold was simply too high or too low.

It was that the threshold was being used without the conditions that gave it meaning.

Seraphina read the conclusion.

The Academy recommended a review of the modern maintenance guides.

It did not recommend changing the limits immediately.

The regional authority would need to compare the revised guidance with actual circuit measurements.

Seraphina placed the report down.

"That is sensible."

Cedric, who had been reading the same report, nodded.

"They are not proposing immediate changes."

"Good."

"Would the current inspections be invalid?"

"That is a question for Lucien."

Cedric smiled.

"I believe he has answered it."

Seraphina looked at the report again.

The inspections were not invalid.

The current circuits had been examined using the standards in force at the time.

The results remained valid within the scope of those inspections.

The new findings simply showed that the standards might need a more complete technical basis.

It was a subtle distinction.

It was also important.

An old rule could be incomplete without making every person who had followed it incompetent.

Seraphina appreciated that.

The missing technical review was harder to locate.

Lucien had already searched the Academy's central archive.

The reference WMR/TECH/18-4 appeared in the old manual and in two later maintenance guides.

Neither contained the report.

The Academy's index listed the review as transferred to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The Department's archive listed it as received.

But the file itself was missing.

The transfer entry identified a technical review group.

Its members included an Academy researcher, a regional engineer, a maintenance inspector, and a Treasury representative.

The review had taken place thirty-four years earlier.

Its purpose was to evaluate whether the winter regulation network should be replaced, modernized, or retained.

The report had apparently recommended a change in the way regional maintenance teams assessed fluctuating mana flow.

But the final approval was not recorded.

Lucien brought the information to Seraphina.

She looked at the documents.

"Did the review group finish its work?"

"The meeting records indicate that it did."

"Was the recommendation approved?"

"Unknown."

"Was the modern guide based on the recommendation?"

"Possibly."

"Was the report transferred?"

"Yes."

"Was it received?"

"Yes."

"Then why is it missing?"

Lucien shrugged.

"That is what the Department is trying to establish."

Seraphina leaned back.

The old report might explain the modern guide.

It might also explain why a particular technical limit had been copied without its original conditions.

But the missing document did not prove that anyone had deliberately removed it.

It could have been filed incorrectly.

It could have been separated from its attachments.

It could have been retained under a different title.

The Academy had found a reference.

Now it needed the report.

She looked at Lucien.

"Has the Department searched the technical archive?"

"Yes."

"The regional archive?"

"Not yet."

"Then perhaps the next step is obvious."

Lucien smiled.

"I was hoping you would say that."

"I am beginning to regret being helpful."

The Royal Infrastructure Department replied a week later.

The missing review had been located in a secondary archive.

It was filed under the title:

**Assessment of Winter Circuit Maintenance Procedures — Regional Standards Review.**

The report was forty-six pages long.

Its conclusion was clear.

The old winter regulation network was no longer suitable for continued operation.

The facilities were to be disconnected or replaced.

The surviving circuits were to be assessed individually.

The report also recommended that future maintenance guides distinguish between normal operating limits and temporary limits used during reduced winter loads.

The recommendation had been accepted by the technical review group.

However, the final implementation record was incomplete.

The Department had approved a revised maintenance guide.

The Academy had received a copy.

The regional authorities had received instructions to update their local procedures.

But the final distribution list showed that several regions had not acknowledged receipt.

The northern authority was among them.

Seraphina read the report.

"That explains something."

Cedric looked up.

"What?"

"The modern guide was based on the revised recommendation, but the northern authority may have continued using the older version."

Cedric nodded.

"Could that explain the current discrepancy?"

"Possibly."

Lucien, who had arrived that afternoon, shook his head.

"Not entirely."

He opened the report.

"The northern authority received a later maintenance guide. It contains the revised wording in one section."

"Then why does it still contain the old threshold?"

"Because the revised guide retained the old table as a reference."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Without the original conditions?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Lucien examined the report.

"The review group recommended retaining the table for historical comparison. The guide's editor appears to have placed it in the operating section rather than the reference appendix."

Seraphina stared at him.

"That is a remarkably ordinary mistake."

Lucien laughed.

"It is."

The report had been approved.

The revised guide had been distributed.

The old threshold had been retained.

But it had been placed in the wrong section.

The error was not in the calculation.

It was in the presentation.

A technical reference had been mistaken for an operating instruction.

The northern maintenance authority had followed the guide as written.

The Academy had now identified the confusion.

No one had needed to conceal a document.

No one had needed to sabotage a circuit.

The problem had survived because a page had been placed in the wrong part of a manual.

Seraphina looked at the report.

She was beginning to understand why Lucien found old records interesting.

She was also beginning to understand why he was terrible at organizing them.

The next question concerned the three modern circuits.

The regional maintenance authority wanted to know whether the old threshold had affected their inspection results.

The Academy compared the available readings.

The Westmere agricultural circuit had been operating within its actual technical limits.

Its inspection records had included additional measurements beyond the old threshold.

The northern transport signal circuit had been isolated from the old network and was no longer governed by the historical winter procedure.

The third circuit, located near the western marches, had been inspected under the older guide.

Its readings showed occasional variation during severe cold.

The variation had been recorded as a maintenance warning.

No failure had occurred.

The circuit remained operational.

The Academy recommended a new controlled inspection to determine whether the variation was normal under the revised standards.

It also recommended checking the circuit's balancing assembly.

The assembly was old.

It had been replaced once.

The replacement's installation record was incomplete.

Darius Veylan was assigned to review the technical history.

Master Harlan was assigned to coordinate the regional maintenance inspection.

Lucien would supervise the Academy's measurements.

Seraphina was not included.

She approved of this.

The western circuit inspection took place three weeks later.

The facility stood near a small settlement on the edge of the western marches.

It supplied a group of roadside signal lamps and a nearby maintenance shelter.

The lamps were not particularly powerful.

Their purpose was to provide guidance during snow and poor visibility.

The circuit had been built to withstand severe winter conditions.

Its current equipment was older than the modern systems at Westmere.

The inspection team arrived in the morning.

They checked the primary regulator.

They examined the balancing assembly.

They measured the mana flow under ordinary conditions.

The first readings were stable.

The second set showed a slight variation.

The third set, taken after the circuit had been exposed to a controlled increase in load, showed a wider difference.

Lucien repeated the measurement.

The result remained consistent.

Darius examined the balancing assembly.

"The response is slower than the expected range."

Master Harlan looked at him.

"Is that a fault?"

"Not necessarily."

Lucien reviewed the readings.

"The variation is greater than the modern guide's general threshold, but the circuit remains stable."

Darius nodded.

"The assembly may be responding more slowly because of wear."

The team inspected the external casing.

There was no visible damage.

The internal components required a separate examination.

The circuit was placed under a reduced operating load.

The roadside lamps remained active.

The maintenance shelter retained its normal heating supply.

The team did not shut down the entire facility.

They recorded the readings and prepared a controlled maintenance plan.

No emergency was declared.

No evacuation was required.

The circuit was functioning.

But its balancing assembly needed attention.

The report arrived at the estate several days later.

Lucien's summary was brief.

The western circuit had not failed.

Its balancing assembly showed signs of age-related wear.

The historical threshold had caused the variation to be classified as a warning rather than a fault.

The revised technical assessment recommended replacing the assembly during the next planned maintenance period.

The replacement would require a compatible component.

The regional depot had one in storage.

However, the component's technical classification was incomplete.

It had been recorded as a reserve regulating assembly.

Its compatibility with the western circuit had not been confirmed.

Seraphina read the summary.

"Do they know where it came from?"

Lucien nodded.

"It was part of the old winter network reserve."

Seraphina looked up.

"The same network as NTR-4?"

"Yes."

"Was it ever installed?"

"Not according to the records."

"Was it inspected?"

"Once, twenty-nine years ago."

"Was it approved for use?"

Lucien hesitated.

"Only for the original design."

Seraphina put down the report.

"Then it cannot simply be installed in the modern circuit."

"No."

"Good."

Lucien smiled.

"I knew you would understand."

"I understand that old equipment should not be connected to a different system merely because it fits."

Darius, who had joined the meeting, nodded.

"That is correct. Physical compatibility is not technical compatibility."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then the depot should keep it in storage until the Academy completes the assessment."

"That is our recommendation."

The component would be inspected.

Its internal specifications would be compared with the western circuit.

If compatible, it could be approved for use.

If not, a replacement would need to be manufactured.

The western circuit would continue operating under a reduced load until the work was complete.

The maintenance authority accepted the recommendation.

The following week, Seraphina received a message from the regional maintenance office.

The western circuit had been inspected again.

The balancing assembly's wear was confirmed.

The circuit remained stable.

The replacement component was unsuitable for direct installation.

Its internal guide had been designed for the old winter network.

The Academy recommended retaining it as a historical reserve item.

A new component would be manufactured to match the current circuit.

The maintenance work was scheduled for the following month.

Seraphina read the notice.

"At least they checked."

Cedric nodded.

"It seems the process is working."

"Slowly."

"Yes."

"Expensively."

"Probably."

She returned the notice.

The matter was not urgent.

The circuit was stable.

The replacement would be made correctly.

No one had attempted to force an old component into a modern system.

That was progress.

At the end of the month, Lucien sent one final document.

It was a copy of the original technical review group's distribution list.

The list contained six recipients.

The Academy.

The Royal Infrastructure Department.

The Northern Regional Maintenance Authority.

The Western Regional Maintenance Authority.

The Southern Regional Infrastructure Office.

And the Central Technical Standards Directorate.

The first five had acknowledged receipt.

The sixth had not.

The Directorate's current archive contained the report, but no record of its formal adoption.

The modern maintenance guide had therefore been issued using the review group's recommendation without a confirmed adoption record from the central standards office.

Lucien explained that this did not automatically invalidate the guide.

The Department had possessed authority to issue regional operating instructions.

But the absence of the central acknowledgment meant that the technical standard had never been formally consolidated.

The same recommendation had been implemented in different ways across the regions.

The Academy wanted to investigate the consequences.

Seraphina read the final paragraph.

It referred to a broader review of the kingdom's winter infrastructure standards.

The review would include the original regulation network, later replacements, regional maintenance practices, and the status of reserve equipment.

She placed the document on the table.

"How many people are involved?"

Lucien thought.

"Several departments."

"How many facilities?"

"Not yet known."

"How many years of records?"

"Possibly fifty."

Seraphina closed the folder.

"Then I hope they have enough paper."

Lucien laughed.

But he did not disagree.

That evening, Seraphina sat with her mother in the family sitting room.

Evelyne was reviewing invitations.

Alaric was reading a report from the northern mines.

Cedric had gone to inspect the new curtain fittings.

Seraphina had brought no documents.

She considered this an achievement.

Evelyne looked up.

"How is the Academy's investigation?"

"Proceeding."

"Have they found anything serious?"

"Not yet."

"Have they asked you to attend another meeting?"

"Not recently."

Evelyne smiled.

"Then perhaps they have learned."

Seraphina considered this.

"Perhaps."

Alaric lowered his report.

"Is the old chamber still closed?"

"Yes."

"Good."

He returned to the mining report.

Seraphina looked at the fire.

The kingdom's old magical infrastructure had not collapsed.

The western circuit had not failed.

The old reserve component had not been installed incorrectly.

The technical guide had contained a confusing reference.

The missing review had been found.

The central standards office had not formally acknowledged it.

There was still much to investigate.

But nothing required her immediate attention.

She reached for a biscuit.

Then Evelyne placed an invitation beside her.

Seraphina looked at it.

"What is this?"

"A winter reception in Aurelion."

"Why?"

"Because the Crown is hosting it."

"That does not explain why I must attend."

Evelyne smiled.

"It is a gathering of regional representatives, technical officials, and several noble families."

Seraphina looked at the invitation.

She recognized the names of the Royal Infrastructure Department and the Academy.

She also recognized the Crown Prince's name.

The reception was scheduled for the following month.

Seraphina slowly put down her biscuit.

"Is this about the winter infrastructure review?"

Evelyne examined the invitation.

"It does not say so."

"Then why are the technical officials attending?"

"Because the Crown has invited them."

Seraphina stared at her mother.

Evelyne smiled.

"Perhaps they simply wish to discuss the weather."

Seraphina looked at the fire.

The kingdom had spent fifty years preserving an incomplete technical standard.

It had taken several weeks to discover that a table had been placed in the wrong section of a manual.

The old chamber remained closed.

The modern circuits were being reviewed.

And now the Crown had invited everyone involved to a reception.

She had a terrible feeling that the next problem would arrive wearing formal clothing.
