# Chapter 31 — The Crown Prince Has a Very Long List

The third station was in the western marches.

Seraphina found this fact deeply inconvenient.

Not because the western marches were particularly dangerous, although they were certainly more troublesome than the northern estate.

Not because she had any desire to visit them.

She did not.

The problem was that the discovery changed the nature of the investigation.

One abandoned maintenance station might be an isolated administrative failure.

Two might have indicated a neglected regional programme.

Three, spread across different territories, suggested that the original infrastructure project had been considerably larger than the surviving records implied.

And if the same engineering office had supervised all three, the missing documents might not be confined to the northern archives.

Seraphina stared at Lucien's letter.

Then she read it again.

The western station was designated **Western Relay Station W-2**.

It had been listed in an engineering catalogue twenty-four years earlier.

Its current status was unknown.

The Academy had found no record of its formal closure.

There was also a reference to a fourth location, but the catalogue entry was incomplete.

Seraphina put the letter down.

"How many stations did this programme have?"

Mira, who was arranging the correspondence, considered the question.

"Three that we know of?"

"That is not an answer."

"It is the only answer I have, my lady."

Seraphina sighed.

"Fair."

She reached for the bell.

"Please ask Cedric to come here."

Cedric arrived with a folder.

He had become accustomed to being summoned whenever the Academy discovered another old document.

This time, however, he looked less concerned than usual.

"I have already reviewed the catalogue entry."

"Of course you have."

"The western station is not connected to the estate's current conduit."

"That is reassuring."

"It was part of the same regional maintenance programme, but it appears to have served a different circuit."

Seraphina examined the map.

The western marches were far from Veyr territory.

The station was marked near an old military road.

"Was it connected to a military facility?"

"Possibly. The catalogue does not specify."

"Does the Royal Military have records?"

"We do not know."

Seraphina looked up.

"Then why is the Academy asking us?"

"It is not asking us to investigate the western station. Lucien sent the information because it may help explain the northern records."

"That is reasonable."

Cedric opened the folder.

"There is another matter."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Naturally."

"The Academy has requested a meeting with the Crown Prince."

She leaned back.

"Why?"

"Because the original maintenance programme was funded partly through the Royal Infrastructure Allocation."

"Which means?"

"Some of the records may be held by the Treasury."

"Does the Treasury have the records?"

"Possibly."

"Does the Academy know?"

"It has requested confirmation."

"Then why is the Crown Prince involved?"

Cedric looked at the invitation.

"Because the Royal Infrastructure Allocation was overseen by the Royal Council at the time."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

The Royal Council.

The Crown Prince.

The Treasury.

The Academy.

A regional engineering office that no longer existed.

And several buildings nobody had officially closed.

She opened her eyes.

"Are we required to attend?"

"No."

"Excellent."

Cedric hesitated.

"The Crown Prince has requested your presence."

Seraphina looked at him.

"That is less excellent."

The invitation was formal.

It requested Seraphina's attendance at a meeting in Aurelion to discuss the historical infrastructure programme and the Academy's ongoing inspection work.

The meeting would include the Crown Prince, representatives of the Royal Academy, the Treasury and the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Seraphina read the invitation carefully.

The stated purpose was to establish the current ownership and maintenance responsibilities of the remaining facilities.

It also requested that House Veyr provide copies of its surviving records.

She considered the wording.

At least it was specific.

She wrote a reply confirming her attendance, with one condition.

Cedric would accompany her as the estate's administrative representative.

She would not be responsible for technical conclusions or funding decisions.

She would provide the estate's records and answer questions concerning its own land.

She also requested that the meeting agenda be provided in advance.

Cedric read the draft.

"That is sensible."

"Thank you."

"Do you think the Crown Prince will accept the conditions?"

"He can refuse."

"And if he does?"

"I will not attend."

Cedric smiled.

"That is also sensible."

The journey to Aurelion took two days.

Seraphina travelled with Mira and Cedric, accompanied by a small household escort.

The roads were in reasonable condition.

The weather remained clear.

There were no unexpected delays, no broken carriage wheels and no disputes over tolls.

Seraphina considered the journey a remarkable success.

They arrived at the Veyr residence shortly before sunset.

The capital was busy.

Merchants moved through the main streets, carts crowded the market roads, and government messengers hurried between administrative buildings.

Seraphina watched the city from the carriage window.

It had been several weeks since her last visit.

The streets looked much the same.

The people looked much the same.

The Academy's towers remained visible above the surrounding buildings.

She wondered how many people in the city knew that three old maintenance stations had recently been rediscovered.

Probably very few.

And that was probably for the best.

The meeting was scheduled for the following morning.

Cedric reviewed the documents with her over breakfast.

The first folder contained the estate's surviving maps.

The second contained the western junction reports.

The third contained the records concerning Northern Relay Station N-4.

The fourth contained the correspondence with the Academy.

Seraphina checked the order.

"Where are the regional stores records?"

"Still in the archive."

"Then we cannot include them."

"Correct."

"Where is the final payment confirmation for Veylan Works?"

"Still missing."

"Then we cannot describe the payment as completed."

Cedric nodded.

"Understood."

Seraphina looked at him.

"You seem very calm."

"I have learned that if I panic every time the Academy finds an old building, I will accomplish very little."

She considered that.

"That is a useful skill."

"I recommend it."

The Royal Council chamber was smaller than Seraphina expected.

It was not the grand ceremonial hall used for royal proclamations.

Instead, it was a practical meeting room with a long table, several cabinets and large windows overlooking the inner courtyard.

Crown Prince Theodore was already present.

He stood beside the table, reviewing a document with Minister Garrick, the royal finance minister.

Lucien sat near the opposite end with Professor Vale and Master Fenwick.

Two Treasury officials were arranging their papers.

Theodore looked up as Seraphina entered.

"Lady Seraphina. Thank you for coming."

"Your Highness."

He greeted Cedric as well.

Then he gestured toward the table.

"Please take your seats."

Seraphina sat beside Cedric.

She placed her folders in front of her.

Theodore looked at the documents.

"You have brought the estate's surviving records."

"Yes. The records are incomplete, so I have marked the missing sections."

"That will be useful."

Minister Garrick examined the top sheet.

"House Veyr has maintained its own archive of regional infrastructure documents?"

"Some of them," Cedric replied. "The records are scattered across several departments."

"Why?"

Cedric looked at Seraphina.

She answered.

"Because the original documents were created by different offices, and their responsibilities changed over time."

Garrick nodded.

"That is a familiar problem."

Seraphina wondered whether the minister considered it familiar because he had encountered it before or because he had helped create it.

She decided not to ask.

Theodore opened the meeting.

"The Academy has identified three facilities associated with the old regional maintenance programme. We need to establish whether these facilities remain part of the kingdom's infrastructure, whether they were formally decommissioned, and which offices inherited their responsibilities."

Professor Vale explained the original programme.

It had been established twenty-four years earlier to support the maintenance of several regional mana circuits.

The project had been funded through a combination of Royal Infrastructure Allocation and regional contributions.

Its purpose was to improve inspection access, reduce travel time for maintenance teams and provide local storage for replacement components.

The programme had operated across several regions.

The northern and western facilities were only part of the system.

Seraphina looked up.

"How many facilities were included?"

Professor Vale checked his notes.

"The original proposal lists six."

The room became quiet.

Six.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

He did not appear surprised.

"How many have been located?" she asked.

"Three confirmed locations," Lucien replied. "Two additional references remain unverified. The sixth is mentioned only in a funding summary."

"Do we know where the sixth was?"

"No."

Seraphina nodded.

Theodore looked toward the Treasury officials.

"Do we have the original allocation records?"

One official answered.

"We have the annual funding summaries."

"Not the detailed records?"

"Not yet."

"Where are they?"

The official consulted his notes.

"The original allocation files were transferred during the reorganization of the Royal Infrastructure Department."

Theodore waited.

"To where?"

"The Treasury archive."

"Have they been located?"

"Some of the files."

"Which ones?"

The official looked down.

"We are still identifying them."

Seraphina quietly opened her notebook.

She was beginning to understand why the Crown Prince had requested her presence.

The kingdom had six facilities.

Three were known.

Several offices had records.

Nobody could establish where all the records were.

And the Treasury had not yet confirmed which files it possessed.

Minister Garrick leaned forward.

"Before we discuss the missing documents, we should establish whether the facilities require expenditure."

Lucien looked at him.

"The Academy cannot determine that without inspection."

"Then perhaps we should inspect them before discussing historical responsibility."

Professor Vale nodded.

"That would be reasonable."

Seraphina looked at the minister.

"Would the inspections be authorized by the Academy or the Royal Infrastructure Department?"

Garrick turned toward her.

"Why does that matter?"

"Because the estate can provide access to land under its control, but we cannot authorize work on a facility that may belong to the Crown or another institution."

The minister considered this.

"That is correct."

"Then the inspection permissions should identify the responsible landholder and the office authorizing the technical work."

Theodore nodded.

"Agreed."

Lucien added, "The Academy can provide the technical team. It cannot assume ownership of the facilities simply because it inspects them."

Garrick looked at the papers.

"Then we should establish ownership before any repair expenditure is considered."

Seraphina nodded.

"That would prevent the inspection from being mistaken for a transfer of responsibility."

Theodore looked at her.

"Precisely."

The discussion continued.

The Academy would prepare a list of confirmed facilities.

The Treasury would search its allocation records.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would identify the offices that had inherited responsibility.

The relevant regional authorities would be asked to confirm land ownership and access.

No repair work would be authorized until the inspection findings and ownership records were reviewed.

Seraphina found the arrangement sensible.

It was also considerably more complicated than simply asking someone to repair an old building.

The first disagreement concerned the funding summaries.

One Treasury official argued that the original programme had been fully funded.

Lucien disagreed.

"The annual summary records the allocation ceiling, not the final expenditure."

The official looked irritated.

"The funds were approved."

"That does not establish that every project was completed."

Minister Garrick intervened.

"Can you provide the detailed records?"

Lucien shook his head.

"Not yet."

Seraphina examined the summary.

There were six facility entries.

Each had a budget allocation.

The northern relay station had received a modest amount.

The western station had received more.

Two other facilities were listed under general regional maintenance.

The final entry was labelled *contingency and reserve*.

She looked at the figures.

"Does the reserve include the sixth facility?"

The Treasury official checked.

"It may."

"Is there a separate project reference?"

"No."

"Then the summary does not establish that a sixth facility was constructed."

The official frowned.

"Nobody has claimed that it was."

"Good. I wanted to make sure we were not treating an allocation as proof of a completed building."

Theodore glanced at her.

"That distinction will be included in the meeting record."

Seraphina returned to her notes.

The meeting was becoming more useful.

It was also becoming longer.

After the first break, Professor Vale presented the old engineering catalogue.

The entries described the six facilities in varying detail.

Northern Relay Station N-4 was listed as a temporary maintenance station.

Western Relay Station W-2 was associated with a separate regional circuit.

A third facility had been identified near the eastern trade road.

The remaining references were incomplete.

The catalogue also listed the old engineering office responsible for supervision.

It had been dissolved twenty-two years earlier.

"Who inherited its responsibilities?" Theodore asked.

A Treasury official replied.

"The Royal Infrastructure Department."

"And who inherited the physical records?"

"The Treasury archive received the financial records. The engineering records were transferred to the central engineering archive."

"Then why do we have incomplete records in both places?"

The official hesitated.

"Some records may have been transferred more than once."

Seraphina wrote a note.

*Transfer history incomplete.*

She underlined it.

Theodore looked at the official.

"Can you provide a complete list of transferred files?"

"We can begin preparing one."

"How long?"

The official hesitated.

"Several weeks."

Theodore did not look pleased.

"Then begin immediately."

During the second break, Seraphina moved toward the windows.

The meeting had been productive, but she had begun to feel that the kingdom's entire administrative structure was built around the principle of moving documents from one office to another until nobody remembered why they had been created.

Lucien joined her.

"You seem tired."

"I am."

"Was the meeting what you expected?"

"No. I expected fewer buildings."

Lucien laughed.

"The original programme was ambitious."

"Was it successful?"

He considered the question.

"Parts of it were. The facilities improved maintenance access in several regions. But the programme was reorganized before all the long-term arrangements were completed."

"Why?"

"Funding priorities changed. Several senior engineers retired. The department was dissolved."

"And the facilities?"

"Some continued operating. Others were abandoned."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Was that considered acceptable?"

"At the time, perhaps."

"That is a worrying answer."

Lucien leaned against the window frame.

"The kingdom has changed considerably in twenty-four years. Some of the old systems were replaced. Others were simply left behind."

Seraphina looked out at the courtyard.

"Then we should find out which is which."

"That is what we are trying to do."

She nodded.

"Good."

Lucien glanced toward the meeting room.

"The Crown Prince has asked the Academy to prepare a wider inspection plan."

Seraphina sighed.

"How wide?"

"All confirmed facilities."

"That sounds expensive."

"It will be."

"Does the Academy have a budget?"

Lucien smiled.

"Not yet."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

"Of course."

When the meeting resumed, Theodore had a new proposal.

He wanted to establish a temporary working group.

It would include representatives from the Royal Infrastructure Department, Treasury, Academy and regional authorities.

Its purpose would be to locate the original records, confirm the status of the known facilities and recommend the next steps.

The proposal was sensible.

It was also missing one important detail.

"Who will coordinate the working group?" Seraphina asked.

Theodore looked at her.

"The Royal Infrastructure Department."

"Does it have a designated officer?"

"Not yet."

"Then the first meeting should not be scheduled until one is appointed."

Minister Garrick looked mildly surprised.

"Why?"

"Because the working group will have several institutions represented. If nobody has authority to collect the reports and circulate decisions, it will become another series of separate meetings."

Professor Vale nodded.

"She has a point."

Theodore considered the matter.

"Minister Garrick, can your department appoint a coordinating officer?"

"Yes."

"Then please do so before the next meeting."

The minister nodded.

Seraphina returned to her seat.

She had not intended to become involved in the working group's organization.

But the problem was obvious.

A group without a coordinator was simply a room full of people waiting for someone else to write the minutes.

The meeting concluded in the late afternoon.

Theodore summarized the agreed actions.

The Academy would prepare a list of confirmed facilities and inspection requirements.

The Treasury would search its allocation records and identify the transferred financial files.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would appoint a coordinator and examine its inherited responsibilities.

Regional authorities would confirm access and land ownership.

No repair work or funding commitment would be made before the technical inspections and documentary review were complete.

Seraphina listened carefully.

The actions were clear.

The responsibilities were assigned.

The deadlines were reasonable.

For once, a meeting had ended with more than a promise to hold another meeting.

Theodore looked toward her.

"Lady Seraphina, I would appreciate your continued cooperation in providing the Veyr estate records."

"Of course. We will provide the records relevant to our land and surviving archives."

"And your assistance in reviewing the working group's administrative arrangements?"

Seraphina paused.

"Only if the group requests a limited review of its procedures."

Theodore smiled slightly.

"That is acceptable."

She bowed.

"Then I look forward to receiving the written request."

Lucien looked amused.

Cedric looked relieved.

Minister Garrick looked as though he had just discovered that inviting Seraphina to a meeting was not the same as obtaining an unlimited supply of her time.

After the meeting, Theodore asked Seraphina to remain for a few minutes.

Cedric waited outside with the documents.

Lucien and Professor Vale had already left.

The Crown Prince stood beside the table.

"I wanted to ask you something."

Seraphina waited.

"Do you believe the old programme was deliberately abandoned?"

She considered the question.

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because we do not have enough evidence to establish that."

Theodore nodded.

"What do you believe happened?"

"I believe the programme was reorganized, the responsible offices changed, and some of the facilities were not properly transferred or closed."

"That sounds like a conclusion."

"It is a possibility supported by the records. It is not yet a complete explanation."

Theodore looked at her for a moment.

"You are careful with your conclusions."

"I have learned that being wrong in a confident manner creates additional work."

Theodore laughed quietly.

"That is an unusually practical observation."

"Thank you."

He returned to the table.

"There is another matter. The Royal Council has several ongoing infrastructure reviews. The Academy's findings may be relevant to them."

Seraphina's expression remained neutral.

"Then the Academy should submit its findings to the appropriate department."

"I was considering asking you to assist with the broader review."

She looked at him.

"Your Highness, I am the heir to a northern duchy. I can provide regional records and explain our estate's administrative arrangements. I cannot supervise the kingdom's infrastructure programme."

"I was not asking you to supervise it."

"Then what would you ask me to do?"

"Review the proposed responsibilities and identify areas where the institutions may be duplicating or neglecting work."

Seraphina considered this.

It was not an unreasonable request.

It was also not a small one.

"Would the review be limited to the infrastructure programme?"

"Yes."

"Would it have a defined end date?"

"We can establish one."

"Would I be expected to attend every meeting?"

"No."

"Would I be responsible for implementing the recommendations?"

"Certainly not."

She thought for a moment.

"Then I will consider a written proposal."

Theodore nodded.

"That is fair."

Seraphina bowed.

She was beginning to suspect that the Crown Prince had a very long list of problems.

Fortunately, he appeared to be learning that she was not available to solve all of them.

Cedric was waiting in the corridor.

"How did it go?"

"He asked whether I would review the broader infrastructure programme."

Cedric looked at her.

"And?"

"I asked for a written proposal."

He smiled.

"Good."

They walked toward the exit.

"Do you think he will send one?"

"Probably."

"Will you accept?"

"That depends on what it says."

Cedric nodded.

"That seems reasonable."

Seraphina glanced at him.

"You are agreeing with me quite frequently today."

"I have discovered that it saves time."

She looked at him.

"That is a dangerous habit."

Cedric laughed.

Back at the residence, Seraphina found a letter from Evelyne.

It asked whether the meeting had gone well.

She replied with a brief account.

The Crown Prince had established a working group.

The Academy would prepare inspection requirements.

The Treasury would search for the original financial records.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would appoint a coordinator.

The Veyr estate would provide its surviving documents.

She did not mention the possibility of a broader review.

That was not yet a decision.

After sealing the letter, she placed the remaining documents on her desk.

The meeting had revealed that the original programme involved six facilities.

Three had been located.

Two more were referenced in incomplete records.

The sixth existed only as a funding entry.

There was no proof that all six had ever been constructed.

There was no proof that the missing facilities had been deliberately concealed.

And there was no proof that the old programme had caused any current danger.

The investigation had become larger.

But at least the questions were becoming clearer.

The next morning, Seraphina prepared to leave Aurelion.

Before the carriage departed, Cedric brought her one final document.

"The Academy sent this overnight."

She took it.

It was a preliminary list of the six facilities.

The first three were familiar.

Northern Relay Station N-4.

Western Relay Station W-2.

Eastern Road Maintenance Station E-1.

The fourth was listed as **Southern Circuit Storehouse S-3**.

The fifth was listed as **Ashenford Relay Station A-2**.

The sixth was marked only as **Reserve Facility — Location Unconfirmed**.

Seraphina studied the list.

The southern storehouse was particularly interesting.

It was not described as a relay station.

It was a storage facility.

That meant the original programme had included more than maintenance access.

It had also included the storage and distribution of replacement components.

She looked at Cedric.

"Does the Academy know where S-3 is?"

"Not yet."

"Does the Southern Church have any connection to it?"

"That has not been established."

"Then we should not assume one."

"Agreed."

She placed the document in her folder.

The southern storehouse might be unrelated to the medical supply problems.

It might have been used only for magical equipment.

Or it might have been a shared regional facility.

There was no evidence yet.

She would not connect two separate investigations simply because both involved the southern provinces.

The carriage door opened.

Mira climbed inside.

Cedric followed.

As they began the journey north, Seraphina looked once more at the list.

Six facilities.

Three located.

Two referenced.

One uncertain.

She folded the paper and placed it in her notebook.

The Crown Prince had a long list.

The Academy had a longer one.

And Seraphina had a growing suspicion that the kingdom's greatest magical mystery was not an ancient spell or a forgotten prophecy.

It was the question of who had been responsible for keeping the buildings in good condition.

She leaned back against the carriage seat.

At least nobody had asked her to become the permanent coordinator.

Yet.

**End of Chapter 31**
