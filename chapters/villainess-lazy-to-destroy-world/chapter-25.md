# Chapter 25 — The Saintess's List of Necessary Things

The first thing Seraphina learned about the estate infirmary was that nobody agreed on how many bandages it contained.

The second thing she learned was that the disagreement had existed for considerably longer than anyone cared to admit.

The third thing was that Mistress Eleanor, who managed the infirmary, was perfectly capable of explaining the problem without once raising her voice.

Seraphina found this particularly impressive.

"I have counted the supplies three times," Eleanor said, placing a ledger on the table. "The purchasing office has counted them twice. The physician has counted them once. We still have three different totals."

Seraphina looked at the ledger.

"How?"

"Different counting dates."

"That explains different totals if supplies were used between counts."

"Yes."

"Then what is the problem?"

Eleanor turned a page.

"The purchasing office records the number of packages delivered. I record the number of usable items. The physician records the number of items available for immediate treatment."

Seraphina paused.

"Those are three different things."

"Exactly."

Eleanor was a woman in her late fifties, with greying hair and a firm expression. She had worked at the Veyr estate for more than twenty years and had no patience for people who assumed that medical supplies could be managed by looking at the number written on a delivery receipt.

"The purchasing office says we received two hundred rolls of bandage cloth," Eleanor continued. "I recorded one hundred and eighty-six usable rolls. The physician recorded one hundred and forty-two available for immediate use."

"Where are the remaining forty-four?"

"Some are reserved for the infirmary's emergency stock. Some are awaiting sterilization. Some are too damaged for use."

Seraphina looked at the figures.

"Then none of those numbers is necessarily incorrect."

"No."

"Yet they appear contradictory."

"Because nobody records what the number represents."

Seraphina leaned back.

She had seen this problem before in other forms.

A delivery quantity was not the same as usable stock. Usable stock was not the same as immediately available stock. A purchase record did not tell anyone what remained after treatment, damage or storage.

The problem was not that people could not count.

It was that they were counting different things and pretending the results were interchangeable.

"Who prepared the current system?" Seraphina asked.

"An earlier physician, I believe. The purchasing office adopted its own format afterward."

"And nobody combined them?"

"Not successfully."

Seraphina looked at Eleanor.

"Would you be willing to help design a clearer record?"

"I already have one."

Eleanor reached beneath the ledger and produced a smaller notebook.

Seraphina examined it.

It was simple and well organized.

There were separate columns for quantities received, quantities inspected, quantities made available, quantities used and quantities discarded. Each entry included a date and the name of the person responsible.

"Why isn't this the official record?"

"Because the purchasing office uses its own ledger."

"Could the two records be compared?"

"Yes, if someone gives us time to do it."

Seraphina closed the notebook.

"Then I believe we have found the actual problem."

Eleanor gave her a level look.

"That is what I have been saying for four years."

Seraphina felt an immediate and sincere respect for her.

The next morning, Seraphina sent a reply to Liora.

She explained that the Veyr infirmary used separate records for delivered, usable and immediately available supplies. She offered to share the procedure with the Academy and the Church, but made it clear that the estate could not certify the medical arrangements of other regions.

She also included Eleanor's name and asked whether Liora would be willing to communicate directly with her.

The reply arrived two days later.

Liora accepted.

She also requested permission to visit the infirmary during her next journey north.

Seraphina approved.

That was how a relatively ordinary conversation about bandages became a planned meeting between the estate's infirmary supervisor, the saintess and two representatives of the Academy.

Seraphina was beginning to understand why simple questions rarely remained simple.

Liora arrived on the fourth morning.

She travelled with a small Church escort and one assistant, a young woman named Nessa who carried a collection of medical records.

Seraphina met them in the entrance hall.

"Lady Veyr," Liora greeted her.

"Saintess Everwyn."

"You look well."

"I have been sleeping."

"That is encouraging."

"Apparently it is an increasingly unusual achievement."

Liora smiled.

Mira stepped forward to take Nessa's luggage.

The young assistant looked relieved.

"Thank you. I was afraid I would have to carry all of this through the entire estate."

"What is in it?" Seraphina asked.

"Medical supply records from three charitable offices, two regional infirmaries and the Church's central distribution store."

Seraphina looked at the stack of folders.

"How many pages?"

Nessa hesitated.

"Several hundred."

Seraphina turned to Liora.

"You brought several hundred pages?"

"I brought the records that were available."

"Why?"

"Because the Academy asked the Church to help review the emergency provisions for its inspection teams."

"Could they not have sent a summary?"

"They did."

"And?"

"It did not include the original quantities."

Seraphina closed her eyes briefly.

"Of course it didn't."

Liora laughed.

"Would you like to see the summary?"

"No."

"Why?"

"I would prefer to remain happy for another few minutes."

The infirmary was located in the eastern wing of the estate, away from the main household rooms.

It was not large, but it was well maintained. There were six treatment rooms, a small examination chamber, a storage area and a separate room for patients who needed observation.

The physician, Master Rowan, greeted them.

He was a middle-aged man with a tired face and an attentive manner.

"Mistress Eleanor told me we were expecting you."

"Thank you for receiving us," Liora said.

Master Rowan bowed.

"I understand you wish to discuss emergency medical provisions."

"Yes. The Academy is reviewing the supplies available to its regional teams. We want to identify what is necessary, what is already provided and where the gaps remain."

"That is sensible."

He glanced toward the folders Nessa carried.

"Have you brought the current regional lists?"

"Yes."

"Then we should begin with the actual conditions in the field."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because a list can tell us what an office believes it has. It cannot always tell us what a physician can use."

Eleanor, who had joined them, nodded.

"That is precisely the problem."

Seraphina decided to remain quiet.

For once, she was surrounded by people who understood the subject better than she did.

She intended to take advantage of this.

The discussion began with a simple question.

"What should every inspection team carry?" Liora asked.

Master Rowan answered first.

"Clean bandages, antiseptic preparations, basic wound dressings, splints, clean water and a means of contacting the nearest physician."

Eleanor added:

"Gloves, soap, clean cloth, storage containers and a way to keep supplies dry."

Nessa wrote everything down.

Liora considered the list.

"What about healing catalysts?"

Master Rowan shook his head.

"Not every team needs them."

"But some may."

"Yes. A team inspecting an unstable magical site should have access to someone trained in mana-related injuries. That does not mean every ordinary inspection team should carry advanced catalysts."

Lucien's name appeared in Seraphina's thoughts.

He had mentioned the limitations of magical instruments.

Perhaps he would have a similar opinion about emergency medical equipment.

"Would the Academy provide a specialist?" Seraphina asked.

Liora nodded.

"That is one possibility. The Church could help identify trained healers in regions where the Academy does not have sufficient personnel."

"Would those healers be expected to travel with every team?"

"No. That would be impractical."

Master Rowan spoke again.

"We should distinguish between supplies carried by the team and medical assistance available nearby."

Liora looked pleased.

"Exactly."

Seraphina watched the discussion.

The participants were not arguing about whether medical care mattered. They were trying to determine what kind of care could realistically be provided under different conditions.

That was a more useful conversation.

"Could the Academy establish three levels of provision?" Seraphina asked.

Everyone looked at her.

She continued carefully.

"An ordinary inspection team might carry basic first-aid supplies and have a confirmed route to local medical assistance. A team working near a hazardous site might require additional protective equipment and a trained medical attendant. A major operation could require a dedicated medical station."

Master Rowan nodded slowly.

"That would be more practical than one universal list."

Eleanor added, "Provided the list also identifies who checks the supplies."

"And who replaces them," Nessa said.

"And who pays for them," Seraphina added.

Liora smiled.

"That is why I wanted to speak with you."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Because I mentioned bandages?"

"Because you asked who was responsible for the records."

Seraphina sighed.

"I was hoping that would not become a reputation."

They spent the next two hours reviewing the regional supply summaries.

The differences were considerable.

The northern teams generally had access to estate or military physicians, though the quality of supplies varied. The western territories relied more heavily on Academy facilities, but several inspection routes were too far from the nearest established medical centre.

In the southern provinces, Church-supported infirmaries provided basic treatment in many villages. However, some charitable stores had no regular replacement schedule.

The eastern territories had access to commercial suppliers, but smaller towns sometimes depended on individual merchants for essential materials.

The capital had several large hospitals and specialist facilities, yet even there the Academy's records did not clearly distinguish between supplies held centrally and those assigned to individual teams.

Seraphina studied the map.

"These are not all the same problem."

"No," Liora agreed.

"The southern provinces have a distribution problem. The western territories have an access problem. The capital has a record-keeping problem."

"And the northern region?" Nessa asked.

Seraphina looked at Eleanor.

"Apparently, we have a disagreement about bandages."

Eleanor gave her a dry look.

"We have a disagreement about what the word 'available' means."

Liora laughed.

Seraphina pointed to the western map.

"How far is the nearest medical station from this inspection route?"

Nessa checked the records.

"Approximately twelve miles."

"Is there a physician there?"

"Yes, but the route may be difficult during heavy rain."

"How long would it take to reach the station?"

"On foot, several hours."

"By carriage?"

"Depending on the road."

Seraphina looked at the map again.

"Then the team needs a different arrangement."

Liora nodded.

"Perhaps a local physician could be assigned on the days of inspection."

"Would that interfere with the village's normal medical services?"

"It could."

Master Rowan leaned forward.

"We should not solve one problem by creating another. If a physician leaves a village without replacement, the village loses access to care."

"Could a second practitioner be assigned?" Nessa asked.

"Possibly. But we need to know whether one is available."

Liora made a note.

The discussion continued.

By the time they finished, they had not produced a national medical plan.

They had, however, identified several questions that the Academy and Church needed to answer before the inspection programme expanded.

Seraphina found this considerably more satisfying than a grand declaration promising medical care for everyone.

A promise was easy.

A reliable arrangement required people, supplies, transport and money.

After the meeting, Liora asked Seraphina to walk with her through the garden.

The afternoon was bright, and the air smelled faintly of wet earth.

Mira followed at a respectful distance.

For several minutes, neither woman spoke.

Then Liora said, "You seem different from when we first met."

Seraphina glanced at her.

"That is not usually considered a compliment."

"I meant that you listen more."

"I always listened."

"Perhaps. But before, you seemed to be waiting for people to finish speaking so you could tell them what they should do."

Seraphina considered this.

"That sounds unpleasant."

"It sometimes was."

"Thank you for telling me."

Liora smiled.

"I am not trying to criticize you."

"I know."

They walked past a flowerbed where several late-season blossoms had begun to fade.

Liora stopped.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Yes."

"Why have you become so careful about taking responsibility?"

Seraphina looked at the flowers.

It was not an easy question.

She could not explain the fever, the memories or the terrible certainty she had once felt about the future. She could not explain why she sometimes woke with the sense that a small mistake might lead to something much larger.

She could only answer with what she understood.

"Because I used to think that wanting something to be fixed meant I was qualified to fix it."

Liora was quiet.

Seraphina continued.

"I thought that if I noticed a problem, I should take control of it. If I was dissatisfied with how something worked, I should change it. I didn't always consider whether I understood the situation."

"That must have been difficult for the people around you."

"Probably."

"And now?"

"Now I am trying to ask who understands it better than I do."

Liora looked at her.

"That is a good question."

"It is also an efficient one."

"Is efficiency your favourite virtue?"

"No. Sleeping is."

Liora laughed.

The sound carried across the garden.

Seraphina found herself smiling.

For a moment, there were no missing records, no royal invitations and no old magical conduits.

There were only two young women walking through a garden, discussing the inconvenience of being expected to solve problems simply because they had noticed them.

The following morning, Liora and Nessa departed for the capital.

Before leaving, Liora handed Seraphina a short note.

"We will send the revised medical requirements once the Church and Academy have reviewed them."

"Thank you."

"And Mistress Eleanor's record format was extremely helpful."

"She will be pleased to hear that."

"I hope the Academy uses it."

"That depends on whether the Academy can persuade its departments to use the same words for the same things."

Liora smiled.

"That may be harder than finding the supplies."

"Probably."

After the carriage departed, Seraphina returned to the infirmary.

Eleanor was checking a delivery.

A clerk stood beside the storage shelves, holding a receipt.

"The order says forty packages," he explained.

Eleanor pointed to the crates.

"Thirty-eight arrived."

"Perhaps two are in the second carriage."

"Then we will record thirty-eight received and amend the entry when the remaining two arrive."

The clerk frowned.

"Would it not be simpler to wait until the entire order is delivered?"

"No," Eleanor replied. "It would be simpler to record what has actually happened."

Seraphina stood quietly by the doorway.

The clerk noticed her and bowed.

Eleanor returned to the receipt.

"Please make the correction."

The clerk did so.

Seraphina smiled faintly.

The system was not perfect.

But at least it was beginning to describe reality.

Three days later, a message arrived from Aurelion.

Lucien had arranged a meeting with Master Oren Whitlock.

The retired engineer would be available on the afternoon before the royal reception.

The message included a second detail.

Master Whitlock had located an old maintenance register.

It contained an entry dated twenty-two years earlier.

The entry referred to a modification of the western conduit junction.

The contractor's name was missing.

But the register included a reference number.

**WM-22/06.**

Seraphina read the number carefully.

She recognized the format.

It resembled the reference numbers used in the older regional maintenance records examined by Roland.

She immediately sent a copy to her father.

Then she wrote to Lucien, asking him to bring the original register to the meeting if possible.

She did not suggest that the missing contractor was connected to the current maintenance discrepancy.

She did not suggest that the junction had been deliberately altered.

She did not even know whether the reference number was unique.

But it was a lead.

A modest one.

The kind that required patience.

The next week passed with unusual calm.

The Westmere roof repairs were completed.

The northern road survey identified two drainage sections requiring attention before winter.

Brant Trading House delivered another shipment under the revised receiving procedure. The quantity was recorded correctly, and the payment was approved after the receiving certificate was filed.

The old grain investigation remained unresolved.

Roland had received confirmation that the Millhaven supplemental delivery existed, but the village receipt still had not been located. The Alder's Crossing allocation remained uncertain.

The separate maintenance authorization inquiry was also ongoing. The estate's legal adviser was examining the copied document, while Roland compared the authorization signatures against the original registers.

Seraphina did not interfere.

She had asked for updates.

She had not asked to take over.

It was a small victory.

Then the royal reception arrived.

On the morning of the reception, Seraphina stood before the mirror while Mira adjusted the fastening at the back of her dress.

The gown was dark green, with understated embroidery along the sleeves. Evelyne had chosen it.

Seraphina had initially suggested wearing something less noticeable.

Her mother had replied that looking deliberately insignificant was still a way of attracting attention.

Seraphina had no answer to that.

"Is it comfortable?" Mira asked.

"Yes."

"Can you move easily?"

Seraphina raised both arms.

"Yes."

"Can you sit without difficulty?"

"Yes."

"Then I believe it is suitable."

Seraphina examined her reflection.

Her hair had been arranged in a simple style, with a few loose strands framing her face. She wore no elaborate jewellery beyond a small family pendant.

She looked like a young noblewoman.

That was unfortunate.

She would have preferred to look like someone who had come to deliver a parcel.

A knock sounded at the door.

Evelyne entered.

"Are you ready?"

"Almost."

Her mother inspected the dress.

"Very good."

"Will I be expected to dance?"

"No."

"Will I be expected to give a speech?"

"No."

"Will I be expected to join a committee?"

"Not unless you agree."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Mother."

Evelyne smiled.

"I am joking."

"I am not certain that is reassuring."

The royal palace was already illuminated when the Veyr carriage arrived.

Carriages lined the entrance courtyard, and servants moved between them with practiced efficiency.

The reception hall was decorated with banners representing the five regions of Elarion. A raised platform stood at one end, where the royal family would receive the assembled nobles and officials.

Seraphina stepped down after her parents.

The palace was magnificent.

It was also full of people.

She immediately regretted her decision.

Alaric offered his arm to Evelyne.

Seraphina walked beside them.

They greeted the royal chamberlain, then the king and queen, before being directed toward their assigned place.

King Edmund III was older than Seraphina remembered from the few descriptions she had heard in the capital. He had a reserved expression and a careful manner.

Queen Adelaide greeted Evelyne warmly.

The Crown Prince stood beside them.

"Lady Veyr," Theodore said.

"Your Highness."

"I trust your journey was comfortable."

"Very much so."

"Your father has been corresponding with the northern administration."

"He has."

"I look forward to hearing his report."

Alaric bowed.

"Your Majesty, Your Highness."

The king acknowledged him.

Then the Veyr family moved on.

Seraphina had survived the first five minutes.

She considered this a promising beginning.

The reception proceeded according to schedule.

There were formal greetings, brief speeches and several introductions.

The Crown Prince spoke about administrative cooperation between the regions. The Royal Academy presented its preliminary inspection priorities. Treasury representatives discussed the need for clearer cost estimates before any major expenditure was approved.

Seraphina listened without volunteering comments.

She noticed Lucien standing beside Professor Vale.

He looked uncomfortable in formal clothing.

Liora stood nearby, speaking with Queen Adelaide and two senior Church officials.

When the speeches ended, guests began moving through the hall.

Seraphina had just accepted a glass of water when Baroness Amelia Rosenthal approached.

"Lady Veyr."

"Baroness."

"I hear you have been very busy in the north."

"Not particularly."

Amelia smiled.

"Your name has appeared in several reports."

"That sounds unfortunate."

"Not necessarily. People are interested in the Academy's regional inspection programme."

"Then they should speak with the Academy."

"And the northern estate's cooperation?"

"That is a matter for my father."

Amelia studied her.

"You have become very careful."

"I have become very tired."

The baroness laughed.

"That may be the most honest answer I have heard this evening."

Seraphina looked around the hall.

"Have you enjoyed the reception?"

"I have attended enough of them to know that enjoyment is not always the purpose."

"Then what is?"

"To remain visible without appearing eager to be seen."

Seraphina considered this.

"That sounds exhausting."

"It is a useful skill."

"Everyone keeps telling me that."

Amelia smiled.

"Perhaps you should listen."

Before Seraphina could reply, a palace official approached.

"Lady Veyr, Master Arkwright would like to speak with you."

Seraphina looked toward Lucien.

He was holding a folded document.

Of course he was.

She excused herself and crossed the hall.

"Master Arkwright."

"Lady Veyr. I apologize for interrupting."

"Is this about the inspection?"

"Yes."

"Then I assume something has happened."

Lucien unfolded the document.

"Master Whitlock has confirmed the reference number in the maintenance register. He also found a second entry."

Seraphina looked down.

The first entry read:

*Western junction modification, WM-22/06. Work supervised by Master Oren Whitlock.*

The second entry was dated four months later.

*Final inspection deferred pending receipt of contractor's completion certificate.*

There was no completion certificate attached.

Seraphina read the line again.

"Was the inspection ever completed?"

"Master Whitlock does not remember. He believes the work was finished, but he cannot confirm that the final inspection took place."

"Does the register identify the contractor?"

"No."

"Does it identify the official who accepted the work?"

Lucien shook his head.

"Only the regional maintenance office."

Seraphina looked at the document.

It was an important gap.

But it was still only a gap.

"Have you checked the office's surviving records?"

"Not yet. Master Whitlock believes the old files may have been transferred when the office was reorganized."

"Then we need to find out where they went."

Lucien nodded.

"I was hoping you might help identify the relevant regional archive."

"I can ask my father's administration."

"Thank you."

Seraphina returned the document.

"Do not describe the missing certificate as evidence of an unsafe modification."

"I won't."

"Do not assume the work was improperly completed."

"I won't."

"And do not let anyone present this as proof that the conduit was deliberately altered."

Lucien gave a small nod.

"I understand."

For a moment, they stood in silence.

Then Lucien glanced toward the raised platform.

"The Crown Prince intends to announce the next stage of the inspection programme."

Seraphina followed his gaze.

Theodore was speaking with the Royal Chancellor.

"Is that a problem?"

"Not necessarily."

"Then why do you look concerned?"

Lucien lowered his voice.

"Because the announcement includes a proposed schedule for the first regional inspections."

Seraphina looked at him.

"And?"

"The western conduit is listed."

"That is expected."

"Yes. But the schedule also includes two sites that have not yet been assessed for access or ownership."

Seraphina frowned.

"Who approved that?"

"I don't know."

The royal musicians began playing again.

Guests turned toward the platform.

Theodore stepped forward.

Seraphina looked at the document in Lucien's hand.

The Academy had not completed its assessment.

The regions had not confirmed every access arrangement.

The Treasury had not approved a full budget.

Yet the programme was already being presented publicly.

She felt a familiar tension settle behind her eyes.

The evening had been going so well.

The Crown Prince began his announcement.

He spoke of cooperation, public safety and the importance of understanding the kingdom's ageing magical infrastructure.

He explained that the Academy would begin a staged inspection programme across the five regions.

The first stage would focus on identifying the condition of existing conduits, access points and maintenance facilities.

No major repairs would begin without separate authorization.

Seraphina relaxed slightly.

That was at least consistent with the revised proposal.

Then Theodore announced the preliminary schedule.

The western conduit was listed for inspection.

So were the eastern trade junctions, a southern agricultural regulator and two sites in the western marches.

Seraphina recognized the locations from the incomplete map.

One of the western sites had not yet been examined.

Another crossed land whose ownership remained disputed.

She looked toward Lucien.

He was watching Theodore with a troubled expression.

The announcement ended.

Applause followed.

Seraphina did not join immediately.

She was still looking at the printed schedule.

It was not a disaster.

It was not even necessarily a mistake.

But the schedule had moved ahead of the evidence.

And once a public announcement had been made, correcting it would become considerably more difficult.

Theodore approached the Veyr family after the applause.

"Your Grace. Duchess. Lady Veyr."

Alaric bowed.

"Your Highness."

"The programme will require cooperation from the regional authorities."

"Of course," Alaric replied. "We will review the proposed schedule."

Theodore nodded.

Seraphina glanced at the printed document.

"Your Highness, may I ask one question?"

"Certainly."

"Are these confirmed inspection locations, or proposed locations awaiting access approval?"

Theodore looked at the schedule.

"They are the initial locations selected for assessment."

"Then the document should perhaps distinguish between confirmed sites and provisional ones."

Theodore paused.

Lucien stepped forward.

"That is correct. Two locations remain subject to access and ownership review."

The Crown Prince looked at him.

"Was that not included in the explanatory notes?"

"It was included in the technical draft, Your Highness. I am not certain it was retained in the public summary."

Theodore took the printed schedule from his secretary.

He read it carefully.

Then he looked back at Seraphina.

"Thank you. I will have the wording reviewed before the schedule is circulated further."

Seraphina bowed.

"That is all I wanted to clarify."

Theodore nodded.

No argument followed.

No public confrontation occurred.

The reception continued.

But Seraphina had learned something important.

Even when institutions agreed on a careful procedure, the summary presented to everyone else could still remove the very qualifications that made the procedure safe.

She looked toward the hall.

The Academy's representatives were already speaking with officials from the regions.

Liora was discussing medical provisions with a Church administrator.

Her father was speaking with the Chancellor.

Her mother had been approached by two noblewomen.

And Lucien was examining the schedule again.

Seraphina took another sip of water.

She had attended the reception.

She had greeted the king.

She had made no promises.

She had not joined a committee.

By every reasonable measure, she had succeeded.

Unfortunately, she had also discovered that the Academy's next problem had already been announced in front of half the kingdom.

She looked at the ceiling.

Perhaps she should have stayed home after all.
