# **Chapter 79 — The Equipment That Had Been Counted Without Being Named**

The Central Stores Authority had received the reserve equipment list.



It had also received the equipment.



At least, that was what the surviving transfer register suggested.



The problem was that the register did not identify individual pieces.



It listed categories, quantities, and estimated values.



There were twenty-four connecting assemblies, twelve internal guides, eight calibration instruments, and an unspecified number of protective fittings.



The total estimated value was 3,860 crowns.



The receiving clerk had signed the transfer.



The inventory clerk had recorded the package.



The accounting office had acknowledged the value.



But the individual equipment entries were absent.



Lucien explained this to Seraphina on a cold Tuesday morning.



She was eating breakfast.



He was standing beside the table with three documents.



"You have brought this here because?"



"Because the equipment list refers to the northern winter network."



"And?"



"Because the Academy believes some of the equipment may have been transferred to regional stores."



"That is a reasonable possibility."



"Some of it may have been used in later infrastructure projects."



"Also reasonable."



"And we have not yet identified the destination of the calibration instruments."



Seraphina took another bite of toast.



"That is unfortunate."



Lucien looked at her.



"You are not going to ask what happened to them?"



"I assume you are going to tell me."



He placed the first document on the table.



"The original reserve schedule."



The second document followed.



"The Central Stores receiving register."



The third was a later inventory summary.



Seraphina glanced at the dates.



The reserve equipment had been transferred thirty-four years earlier.



The later inventory had been prepared nineteen years after that.



"Why is the inventory summary relevant?"



"It contains a reference to the original transfer."



"Does it identify the equipment?"



"Not individually."



Seraphina looked up.



"Then what does it identify?"



"Twenty-four connecting assemblies, twelve internal guides, and eight calibration instruments."



She looked at the document again.



The quantities were identical.



"That is encouraging."



"It is."



"Does the total value match?"



"Almost."



"Almost?"



"The later inventory records 3,810 crowns."



Seraphina set down her toast.



"That is a difference of fifty crowns."



"Yes."



"Is that significant?"



"We do not know."



"Could it be depreciation?"



"Possibly."



"Could it be an accounting adjustment?"



"Possibly."



"Could it be a missing item?"



Lucien hesitated.



"Possibly."



Seraphina looked at him.



"You have become very fond of that word."



He smiled.



"The evidence is incomplete."



"Then the evidence should be completed."



She pushed the papers back toward him.



"Please continue."

The Central Stores Authority's records were kept in a building behind the Royal Infrastructure Department.



Unlike the old archives, this building was still in regular use.



It contained shelves of current inventory, sealed rooms for specialized equipment, and a separate section for historical transfer records.



The building also contained a surprisingly large number of clerks who appeared to dislike being asked questions about old equipment.



Lucien visited with Master Harlan and a representative from the Technical Standards Directorate.



Seraphina did not attend.



She had no appointment, no authority over the stores, and no desire to spend an afternoon watching people search boxes.



Instead, she received a letter from Cedric.



The letter contained a short summary.



The original transfer had been recorded as a single package.



The receiving register listed four categories.



The later inventory had copied the same quantities.



However, the later inventory did not confirm that the equipment remained physically present.



It was a reconciliation of historical values.



The fifty-crown difference came from a depreciation adjustment applied to the entire reserve package.



No individual item had been written off.



Seraphina read the letter twice.



"That is better than losing fifty crowns," she said.



Mira, who was arranging the room, glanced at her.



"Was someone looking for fifty crowns?"



"Apparently."



"That sounds exhausting."



"It is."



Mira folded a tablecloth.



"Would you like me to bring the afternoon tea?"



"Yes."



Seraphina put the letter aside.



The equipment was still unaccounted for.



But at least the accounting difference was no longer pretending to be a mystery.

Two days later, Lucien returned.



This time he brought a retired stores clerk.



His name was Master Robert Fenwick.



He was seventy-one, had worked for Central Stores for thirty-eight years, and had retired after the regional inventory consolidation.



He carried a small notebook.



"I was not responsible for the original transfer," he explained. "I joined the stores office several years later."



Seraphina nodded.



"Then what do you remember?"



"I remember the reserve equipment."



"Why?"



"Because it was kept separately."



Lucien leaned forward.



"Where?"



"In the old technical stores section. It had a restricted allocation label."



Seraphina looked at him.



"Was it the same allocation as the western reserve components?"



"No. That was a later system. The old winter network equipment had a separate reference."



Robert opened his notebook.



"TRANS/RES/31-B."



Lucien nodded.



"That is the reference."



Robert continued.



"The package was divided after the first inventory. The connecting assemblies were sent to three regional depots. The internal guides were retained centrally. The calibration instruments were placed in the technical equipment room."



Seraphina looked at the notebook.



"Do you remember the quantities?"



"Not exactly. I remember the distribution was uneven."



"Was there a transfer record?"



"There should have been."



"Can you identify the clerk responsible?"



Robert considered this.



"Master Edwin Clarke."



Seraphina looked at Lucien.



The name was familiar.



Edwin Clarke had served as a receiving clerk in an earlier investigation, but that had been a different office and a different period.



Lucien clarified.



"This is another Edwin Clarke. He worked in Central Stores during the transition."



Seraphina nodded.



"Good. We have enough people with similar names already."



Robert gave a brief laugh.



"That is true."



He turned another page.



"There was also a technical instruction attached to the distribution list."



"What did it say?" Lucien asked.



Robert frowned.



"I cannot remember the exact wording. Something about not issuing the equipment until the receiving regions confirmed compatibility."



Seraphina looked at him.



"Was that a general instruction?"



"Yes."



"Did it apply to all the equipment?"



"I believe so."



Lucien wrote it down.



"That would explain why some of the equipment remained in storage."



Robert nodded.



"Possibly."



Seraphina looked at him.



He smiled apologetically.



"I see I have adopted the Academy's vocabulary."

The next set of records was located in the Central Stores historical inventory.



The connecting assemblies had been distributed to three regional depots.



Eight went to the Northern Regional Stores Depot.



Ten went to the Western Regional Stores Depot.



Six went to the Eastern Regional Stores Depot.



Each transfer had a receiving signature.



The internal guides remained in central storage.



The calibration instruments were transferred to the Technical Evaluation Unit.



The total quantities matched the original schedule.



There was no missing equipment in the initial distribution.



However, the regional depots had later reorganized their inventories.



The northern depot had been merged into the Northern Maintenance Authority.



The western depot had been renamed twice.



The eastern depot had transferred its specialized equipment to the Eastern Technical Institute.



The internal guides had been recorded as obsolete components during a later equipment review.



The calibration instruments had been classified as general technical tools.



The original reserve reference had disappeared from the current inventory system.



Lucien read the summary.



"The equipment was not necessarily lost."



Seraphina looked at him.



"That is encouraging."



"The problem is that its later use was not consistently recorded against the original reserve schedule."



"Then we need to establish where it went."



"Yes."



"Do the regional authorities have records?"



"Some."



"Then ask them."



Lucien nodded.



He had already begun.

The Northern Regional Stores Depot responded first.



It confirmed that eight connecting assemblies had been received.



Five had later been issued for agricultural regulation maintenance.



Two had been transferred to the northern transport authority.



One had been retained as an emergency spare.



The agricultural assemblies had been installed in two separate facilities.



The transport authority's two assemblies had been used during a signal-system replacement.



The remaining spare had been moved into the current regional maintenance warehouse.



The records were incomplete, but the physical inventory matched the surviving entries.



The western depot responded three days later.



Its ten assemblies had been divided between two maintenance projects.



Four had been installed in the western agricultural network.



Three had been sent to a road-maintenance facility.



Two had been classified as unsuitable for modern systems and placed in long-term storage.



The tenth had been transferred to the regional technical workshop.



The eastern depot's records were more complicated.



Of its six assemblies, three had been used in an industrial regulator replacement.



Two had been transferred to the Eastern Technical Institute.



One had been returned to Central Stores after failing an inspection.



The returned assembly had later been dismantled for parts.



There was no evidence that the original reserve package had been misappropriated.



The equipment had been distributed and used over several decades.



The original allocation reference had simply not followed every later transfer.



Seraphina read the three reports.



"That seems reasonably clear."



Lucien nodded.



"Most of it."



"Most?"



He turned to the final page.



"The two assemblies held in western storage require further inspection."



Seraphina sighed.



"Of course they do."

The two assemblies had been stored in a locked section of the Western Regional Technical Warehouse.



They were old.



Their outer casings were intact.



Their internal guides were still present.



But the equipment had been designed for the old winter network.



The Technical Standards Directorate did not consider them suitable for installation without testing.



One assembly had been inspected years earlier and marked for possible reuse after modification.



The other had no surviving inspection report.



The warehouse manager, Master Harlan, arranged a supervised examination.



Seraphina was not invited.



She was grateful.



The report arrived a week later.



The first assembly could be dismantled for parts, but its casing was no longer suitable for a pressure-balancing system.



The second was structurally intact, but its internal guide had a different alignment from the modern standard.



Both were classified as historical equipment.



Neither was suitable for immediate installation.



The Directorate recommended retaining one as a reference specimen and disposing of the other under ordinary technical waste procedures.



The old reserve list could finally be closed.



The equipment had not vanished.



It had been used, transferred, stored, modified, or retired.



The administrative problem was that later inventories had not consistently retained the original reference.



Seraphina read the report.



"That is a fairly ordinary ending."



Lucien looked disappointed.



"Ordinary?"



"Yes. The equipment was old, people used it, offices changed, and records stopped following the original list."



"That is not entirely satisfying."



"It is not supposed to be satisfying. It is supposed to be accurate."



Lucien considered that.



Then he nodded.



"Perhaps."

The technical review continued without Seraphina's participation.



The Academy prepared a revised historical register.



The Central Stores Authority attached the later transfer records.



The Technical Standards Directorate classified the remaining equipment.



The Royal Infrastructure Department updated the current maintenance guidance.



The report also included a recommendation.



Historical equipment references should remain visible in later inventory records, even when the equipment had been reused or reclassified.



The recommendation was simple.



It was also likely to be ignored by someone who considered the extra field unnecessary.



Seraphina hoped the Directorate would insist.

The northern winter, meanwhile, continued.



The first serious snowfall came earlier than expected.



The road authority restricted heavy traffic on the eastern route.



Westmere's grain deliveries were reduced to smaller loads.



The temporary storage arrangement was reactivated.



This time, the village storehouse was inspected before the first wagon arrived.



The roof had been repaired.



The floor had been raised.



The separate merchant spaces were marked.



The weighing procedure was already in place.



Cedric received the first delivery report.



It was correct.



The second was correct.



The third contained a minor discrepancy in the recorded weight of two sacks.



The discrepancy was checked.



The weighing scale had been adjusted after the previous inspection, but the new calibration mark had not been entered into the register.



The weight itself was accurate.



The record was corrected.



Cedric sent Seraphina a note.



*The storehouse is operating normally. The new procedures appear to be working.*



She replied:



*Excellent. Please do not create another committee to celebrate.*



Cedric's response arrived the following morning.



*I had not intended to.*



Seraphina was pleased.

A letter from Liora arrived that afternoon.



It contained a brief account of the southern infirmaries.



The winter supply arrangements had been adjusted after the previous year's delivery difficulties.



Several infirmaries had established small emergency stocks of blankets, clean linen, and basic medical supplies.



The Church had also revised its distribution schedule to account for roads that became unreliable during heavy rain or snow.



Liora had visited three districts.



She had found the new procedures helpful, though the staff were still struggling with shortages of trained workers.



Her letter ended with a complaint about the number of people who considered a written schedule a substitute for actually sending supplies.



Seraphina smiled.



She wrote back.



*I agree. A schedule cannot carry a box of bandages.*



Mira collected the letter.



"Shall I send it with the afternoon courier?"



"Yes."



"Would you like to include anything else?"



Seraphina thought.



"Ask whether she has received the wool blankets from the northern merchants."



Mira nodded.



Seraphina returned to her book.



For several minutes, the estate remained quiet.



Then Cedric entered.



He was holding a letter from the Royal Infrastructure Department.



Seraphina looked at it.



"No."



"I have not said anything."



"You are holding a government letter."



"It concerns the technical review."



"That does not improve matters."



Cedric placed it on the table.



The Department had completed its preliminary historical review.



The report confirmed that the old winter network had been progressively disconnected or replaced.



The surviving facilities were not part of one functioning system.



The old northern chamber remained dormant.



The Westmere station had been rebuilt independently.



The northern transport signal circuit had been isolated.



The southern regulator had been dismantled.



The western relay still required final confirmation.



The unidentified reserve connection remained unverified.



The report also identified a modern infrastructure issue.



Several current regional circuits had been built using design principles inherited from the old network.



They were independent systems.



But some of their maintenance assumptions had been copied from older technical guidance.



The revised manual corrected the operating limits.



It did not yet address every difference in the newer systems.



The Directorate proposed a further inspection of three modern circuits before issuing the final national maintenance standard.



Seraphina read the final paragraph.



The first inspection would take place in the western marches.



The second would take place in the northern agricultural district.



The third would take place near the capital.



She looked up.



"Why is the northern agricultural district included?"



Cedric answered.



"Because its regulator was built using the same revised design."



"Is it unstable?"



"The report does not say that."



"Then why inspect it?"



"To establish whether the maintenance guidance is suitable."



Seraphina nodded.



That was sensible.



She read the next page.



The Department requested permission to access the northern agricultural circuit because a section of its maintenance route crossed Veyr land.



Seraphina placed the letter down.



"That is a land-access request."



"Yes."



"Not a request for House Veyr to supervise the inspection?"



"No."



"Not a request for funding?"



"No."



"Not a request for me to attend?"



Cedric smiled.



"Not yet."



She gave him a flat look.



"Do not encourage them."

The following morning, Seraphina met with Master Colin Armitage.



The estate surveyor had already reviewed the access map.



The northern agricultural circuit crossed a strip of Veyr land before reaching the regional maintenance road.



The land was not used for cultivation.



It was a narrow section of pasture beside a drainage ditch.



The circuit itself was buried.



The Department wanted access to two inspection points and a maintenance chamber.



Armitage had no objection to the survey.



He did, however, identify a problem.



"The access map is outdated," he said.



Seraphina examined it.



The map showed a maintenance path running directly across the pasture.



In reality, the path had been moved several years earlier to avoid a waterlogged section.



The old route was still marked on the Department's plan.



"Does the new path reach both inspection points?" she asked.



"Yes."



"Does it cross any other property?"



"No. It remains within Veyr land until it joins the regional road."



"Then update the access map."



Armitage nodded.



"The maintenance chamber is also marked on the wrong side of the ditch."



Seraphina looked at him.



"How far?"



"Approximately twelve paces."



"Is that significant?"



"For the inspection, yes. For ownership, no."



"Then make the distinction clear."



Armitage smiled.



"I will."



Seraphina returned the map.



"Once the revised plan is ready, send it to the Department. They can arrange the inspection."



"Will you attend?"



"No."



"Will Cedric?"



"If the Department requires a land representative, he can appoint someone."



Armitage nodded.



He understood.



The estate would provide access.



It would not become responsible for the technical review.

The inspection took place nine days later.



The weather was cold but clear.



A small team from the Royal Infrastructure Department arrived with Lucien, two engineers, and a regional maintenance officer.



Cedric assigned Martin Cole to accompany them as the estate's land representative.



The inspection points were accessible.



The maintenance chamber was dry.



The circuit's outer structure showed no significant damage.



The engineers examined the regulator and its connecting lines.



The regional maintenance officer checked the isolation points.



The circuit remained stable throughout the inspection.



No old winter network connection was found.



However, the engineers identified a discrepancy between the original construction drawing and the current maintenance map.



A short section of conduit had been moved during a repair sixteen years earlier.



The repair itself was properly recorded.



But the updated route had never been added to the central technical map.



The difference did not affect the circuit's operation.



It did affect future maintenance access.



The engineers corrected the local map.



They also recommended checking two similar sites.



The inspection report described the circuit as operational and independent.



No immediate repairs were required.



Lucien sent Seraphina a brief message.



*The northern agricultural circuit is functioning normally. The old network is not connected. One mapping discrepancy has been corrected.*



Seraphina read it.



Then she placed the letter beside her breakfast plate.



Mira looked at her.



"Good news?"



"Yes."



"Then why do you look unhappy?"



"Because the next report will probably contain another map."



Mira considered this.



"Would you like more tea?"



"Yes."

The Department's preliminary review was published internally at the end of the month.



It was not a public report.



It was a technical document intended for the regional authorities and the Academy.



Its findings were cautious.



The old winter network had been retired in stages.



Most known connections had been disconnected or replaced.



The surviving modern circuits were independent.



The western relay required final verification.



The reserve equipment had been accounted for.



The old operating manual had contained valid instructions for its original conditions, but its limits had been repeated without their original qualifications.



The revised guidance corrected that error.



Three modern circuits still required comparison before the final standard could be approved.



The report also included a warning.



The modern circuits had been designed by different regional teams.



Although they shared several technical principles, they did not necessarily respond identically under winter conditions.



A single operating limit should not be applied to all of them without further testing.



Seraphina read the conclusion.



"That seems sensible."



Cedric nodded.



"Lucien says the Academy agrees."



"Good."



"Minister Garrick has asked whether House Veyr would consider hosting the next technical meeting."



Seraphina looked at him.



"No."



Cedric smiled.



"I thought you would say that."



"Where will it be held?"



"The Royal Academy."



"Excellent. They have rooms."



"And staff."



"And a kitchen."



"And technical equipment."



"Then I see no reason to move it here."



Cedric folded the letter.



"I will inform the Department."



Seraphina returned to her book.



The old equipment had been accounted for.



The missing appendix had been found.



The northern circuit had been inspected.



The winter roads were functioning.



For once, the various offices appeared to be doing their own work.



It was almost peaceful.



Then Mira entered with another letter.



"This arrived from the capital."



Seraphina looked at the seal.



The Royal Infrastructure Department.



She stared at it.



Mira placed it beside her tea.



"Would you like me to open it?"



"No."



"Shall I send it to Cedric?"



Seraphina considered the question.



"Yes."



Cedric, who had not yet left the room, looked at her.



"You have not even read it."



"I recognize the seal."



He picked it up.



The letter was addressed to the Duke of Veyr.



Its subject was the upcoming national technical standards review.



The Department was requesting permission to use the northern agricultural circuit as one of the comparison sites for the final winter operating standard.



It was a reasonable request.



The inspection would be conducted by the Academy.



The regional authority would provide maintenance records.



The Department would cover all expenses.



House Veyr would only be asked to permit access to the land.



Cedric read the final paragraph.



Then he looked at Seraphina.



"There is one additional point."



"What?"



"The comparison includes the western marches circuit."



Seraphina nodded.



"That was expected."



"The western circuit's maintenance report contains an unresolved fluctuation."



She looked up.



"What kind?"



"Small variation in the balancing readings. It remains within the temporary operating range."



"Is it stable?"



"According to the report."



"Has the cause been identified?"



"Not yet."



Seraphina considered the letter.



The western circuit had already been inspected.



The worn balancing assembly had been identified.



A replacement component was being manufactured.



The new technical review would compare it with the northern and capital circuits.



There was no immediate danger.



But the final standard would depend on understanding why the three systems behaved differently.



"Send the access permission," she said.



Cedric nodded.



"And the technical questions?"



"To the Academy."



"Not you?"



"Not me."



He smiled.



"Understood."



Seraphina took her tea.



Outside, the first proper winter wind moved across the estate.



The northern roads remained open.



The grain stores were holding.



The old winter network was mostly understood.



And somewhere in the western marches, an independent circuit was producing readings that did not quite match the expectations of the people who had designed it.



Seraphina hoped they would discover why before anyone decided to solve the problem by changing the operating limit again.



She had developed a strong dislike of operating limits.



They were apparently very easy to write.



And very difficult to explain.
