# **Chapter 86 — The Engineer Who Had Been Removed from the Assignment**

The name had been crossed out with a single black line.

It was not particularly dramatic.

There was no torn page, no suspicious stain, no mysterious symbol in the margin.

Just a name that had once occupied a space in an assignment register and no longer did.

Seraphina found this irritating.

Not because she believed the name had been removed for some sinister reason. She had learned to distrust that kind of conclusion.

It was irritating because the register did not explain why.

The document had arrived at the palace with the Academy's latest administrative summary. The eastern circuit's final modification, twelve years earlier, had required a technical assessment of its response under reduced reserve contribution.

The assessment had been conducted.

The results had been reviewed.

The final recommendation had never been issued.

The test supervisor's name had been crossed out.

Seraphina read the summary again.

"Who is this?"

Lucien, sitting opposite her, examined the page.

"Master Roland Edrick."

"Was he dismissed?"

"We don't know."

"Did he resign?"

"We don't know."

"Did he retire?"

"We don't know."

"Did he die?"

Lucien looked at her.

"We don't know that either."

Seraphina set the page down.

"Your records are remarkably committed to uncertainty."

"The Central Technical Evaluation Board was reorganized twice after the test."

"That explains why the records may be difficult to locate. It does not explain the crossed-out name."

"No."

"Have you asked the Board?"

"They no longer exist under that name. Their functions were divided between the Technical Standards Directorate and the Academy."

"Then ask both."

"We have."

"And?"

"The Directorate says the assignment register belongs to the former Board. The Academy says it received only the technical reports."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Have you asked who received the original assignment register?"

Lucien paused.

"No."

"Then perhaps start there."

He considered the suggestion.

"That is reasonable."

"Please do not sound surprised."

The Royal Academy's new review group had been working for twelve days.

It had already established that the eastern circuit's final modification was authorized, completed and inspected. The reserve zone remained active because the maintenance team continued using it under the old balancing procedure.

However, the technical assessment of the eastern circuit's response under reduced reserve contribution remained unresolved.

The test had been conducted twelve years earlier.

Its results had been reviewed by the Central Technical Evaluation Board.

No final recommendation had been issued.

The Board's assignment register listed three examiners:

- Master Roland Edrick, senior technical examiner.
- Master Julian Harcourt, calibration specialist.
- Master Elias Rook, regional maintenance representative.

Two names remained intact.

The third had been crossed out.

Master Julian Harcourt was still alive and working as a technical adviser.

He had previously examined equipment from the old E-1 project, including the uncalibrated assembly ER-22-091.

His recollection was limited but useful.

He remembered the assessment.

He remembered that the eastern circuit had produced a response that differed from the simplified model.

He remembered Master Edrick's concern that the result could not be interpreted without additional measurements under sustained load.

He did not remember why the final recommendation had not been issued.

He also remembered that the assignment had been interrupted.

"Interrupted how?" Lucien had asked.

Harcourt had considered the question.

"The Board received a request to suspend the assessment. I cannot remember whether it came from the Department or the Board's chair."

"Was the request written?"

"I believe so."

"Do you remember the reason?"

"Not precisely. There was some disagreement about the test conditions."

"What disagreement?"

"The Academy's model used a standard load assumption. The maintenance authority believed the eastern circuit was operating under a different seasonal profile."

"Was that disagreement resolved?"

"Not to my knowledge."

Lucien had written everything down.

The interview had produced no accusation.

It had produced a possible explanation.

The assessment had not necessarily been abandoned because someone wanted its result hidden.

It might simply have been suspended because the participating offices disagreed about the conditions under which the results should be interpreted.

But the crossed-out name still required an explanation.

The Royal Infrastructure Department eventually located the original assignment register.

It had been transferred during the Board's reorganization to the Directorate's historical personnel archive.

The register contained a handwritten note beside Master Edrick's name.

**Assignment withdrawn — replacement examiner to be appointed.**

There was no date.

There was no replacement examiner.

There was no reference to the authority responsible for withdrawing the assignment.

The other two examiners had remained assigned.

The technical review had not been formally cancelled.

Seraphina read the entry during the next administrative meeting.

"Does the Directorate have the withdrawal order?"

"No," the archive officer replied.

"Does the Academy?"

"No."

"Does the Department?"

"We have not found it."

"Then how does the Directorate know the assignment was withdrawn?"

The archive officer looked uncomfortable.

"The register says so."

"Yes. But who wrote it?"

"We don't know."

"Was the handwriting identified?"

"Not yet."

Master Fenwick intervened.

"Lady Seraphina, the personnel archive is not your responsibility."

"I know."

"We can have the archive team examine it."

"That would be preferable."

She closed the register.

There was no reason for her to continue.

The meeting returned to technical matters.

Lucien presented the surviving measurements.

The eastern circuit's response under reduced reserve contribution had been greater than the simplified model predicted, but the test had been stopped before the circuit reached its upper operating limit.

The result could indicate that the circuit required continued balancing support.

It could also indicate that the test's load assumptions had not accurately represented the circuit's operating conditions.

The Academy needed additional evidence before deciding.

Master Fenwick recommended that the current configuration remain unchanged.

Theodore approved the recommendation.

The meeting ended without a new test being scheduled.

Seraphina considered that a satisfactory outcome.

Two days later, the Directorate found a personnel record for Master Roland Edrick.

He had not died.

He had not been dismissed.

He had not retired.

He had been transferred.

Twelve years earlier, shortly after the assessment began, he had been appointed to a temporary technical review assignment in the western marches.

The assignment lasted six months.

He returned to the capital afterward, but his position at the Central Technical Evaluation Board had been filled by another examiner.

The personnel record did not mention the eastern circuit assessment.

It did not say that he had been removed because of the assessment.

It did not say that the transfer was disciplinary.

It was simply a transfer.

Lucien examined the record.

"Why was his assignment withdrawn?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"Do you know?"

"No."

"Then we should not assume the transfer explains it."

"I am not assuming."

"You have been staring at the record for three minutes."

"I am considering the sequence."

"Consider it after someone asks him."

Lucien sighed.

The Directorate arranged an interview.

Master Roland Edrick lived in a quiet district south of the Academy.

He was sixty-three, retired from active service and employed part-time as a technical adviser to a private engineering school.

He received Lucien and Master Fenwick in a modest office filled with old instruments and technical books.

Seraphina was not invited.

She was pleased.

The interview lasted nearly two hours.

Edrick remembered the eastern circuit assessment clearly.

He had been the senior examiner.

The test had been designed to determine whether the eastern circuit could maintain stable operation with a reduced contribution from the reserve zone.

The first stage had produced ordinary results.

The second had shown a gradual rise in eastern distribution pressure.

The reserve zone had compensated.

The third stage was never completed.

Edrick had requested additional measurements.

He believed the test conditions did not sufficiently account for the seasonal demand profile.

The Academy's model had assumed an average load.

The maintenance authority had provided records showing that the eastern circuit experienced higher demand during certain periods.

The two sets of information had not been reconciled.

Edrick had recommended a second test under a more representative load profile.

The Board had not approved it.

Then he had been transferred west.

"Did you request the transfer?" Lucien asked.

"No."

"Did you object?"

"I asked whether the eastern circuit assessment would be reassigned."

"What was the answer?"

"That the matter would be reviewed after my return."

"Was it?"

Edrick gave a small, humourless smile.

"I returned. The Board had been reorganized. The file was no longer under my department."

"Did you try to recover it?"

"Once. I was told the assessment had been transferred to the Academy."

"Did you contact the Academy?"

"I sent a letter."

"Did you receive a reply?"

"No."

Lucien exchanged a glance with Master Fenwick.

"Do you still have a copy of the letter?"

Edrick considered the question.

"Possibly."

He rose and crossed to a cabinet.

After several minutes, he returned with a thin folder.

Inside was a copy of his letter.

It was dated eleven years earlier.

The letter asked the Academy to confirm whether the additional test had been conducted and whether the revised operating conditions had been formally adopted.

A reply had been attached.

It was short.

*The Academy has received the inquiry. The eastern circuit assessment remains under review by the relevant technical authority. No further action is required from the former examiner at this stage.*

The reply had been signed by a records officer.

Not a technical examiner.

Edrick had kept the letter because he had never understood what it meant.

Lucien read it twice.

"Did you consider the matter closed?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because I had asked whether the test had been completed. The reply did not answer that question."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Did you contact the Board again?"

"By then, the Board no longer existed in its former form. I had no clear office to approach."

"Did you believe the eastern circuit was unsafe?"

Edrick shook his head.

"No. I believed the assessment was incomplete. That is not the same thing."

Lucien wrote down the distinction.

"Do you know who requested your transfer?"

"No. The personnel office assigned me. I was told there was a shortage of technical examiners in the western marches."

"Was there?"

"Yes. Several projects were delayed."

"Do you think the transfer was related to your assessment?"

Edrick looked at him.

"I have no evidence of that."

The interview ended with a request for permission to examine his remaining technical notes.

He agreed.

The notes were delivered to the Academy the following week.

They contained sketches, load calculations, instrument readings and a short summary of the unfinished assessment.

The most important page was not the one Lucien expected.

It was a table comparing the eastern circuit's response under three conditions.

The first condition showed ordinary operation with the reserve zone active.

The second showed reduced reserve contribution under average demand.

The third showed reduced reserve contribution under sustained high demand.

The third column had not been completed.

At the bottom, Edrick had written:

**The circuit's independent operating capacity cannot be established from the present test. The reserve contribution appears to compensate for a difference that increases under sustained eastern demand. Additional measurements required before any change to operating procedure.**

The Academy compared the table with the surviving instrument readings.

The values matched.

The current maintenance records also showed that the reserve zone had continued to compensate for eastern distribution changes during periods of higher demand.

This did not prove that the eastern circuit could never operate independently.

It did establish that the original assessment had not demonstrated that it could.

Master Fenwick placed the page beside the current central model.

"The four-year-old amendment should never have been treated as a technical authorization to remove the restriction."

Lucien nodded.

"It was based on a summary that did not include the unfinished assessment."

"Do we know who prepared the summary?"

"Not yet."

Theodore looked at the report.

"Was the amendment deliberately misleading?"

"We cannot establish that," Master Fenwick replied. "The person who prepared it may have misunderstood the earlier assessment. The approving officer may have relied on an incomplete summary. The technical authority may have assumed the additional test was completed."

"And the result?"

"The reserve procedure was removed from the central model, but it continued in maintenance practice."

Theodore was quiet.

"Then the immediate correction is clear."

"Yes," Lucien said. "The central model must be revised to reflect the existing balancing procedure until the Academy completes a new assessment."

"Can that be done without changing the network?"

"Yes. It is an administrative and technical documentation correction. The operating configuration will remain unchanged."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Then that should be our first action."

The revised operating model was prepared over the next three weeks.

It included the reserve zone's balancing function, the conditions under which it was to be used, and the limits of what had been established about the eastern circuit's independent capacity.

The document also identified the unfinished assessment.

It did not claim that the original test had failed.

It did not claim that the eastern circuit was defective.

It stated that the test had been incomplete and that no final recommendation had been issued.

The Directorate formally suspended the four-year-old amendment.

The maintenance authority retained its existing operating procedure.

The Academy began planning a new assessment using a more representative load profile.

The review group also recommended that any future changes to the central operating model include a reference to the technical evidence supporting them.

Theodore approved the recommendation.

The King received the corrected model.

The capital's protective network continued operating normally.

For the first time in several weeks, Lucien sent Seraphina a message that did not contain the words *missing attachment*, *unconfirmed receipt* or *historical reference*.

It said:

*The central model has been corrected. The reserve zone's current balancing function is now accurately recorded. No changes to the operating configuration have been made.*

Seraphina read it at breakfast.

"Good," she said.

Evelyne looked up.

"Is that the end?"

"Of this part."

"Only this part?"

"The Academy still needs to conduct the new assessment."

Evelyne smiled.

"Then perhaps you can return to your book."

Seraphina looked at the book beside her plate.

She had finally reached the chapter where the protagonist was about to reorganize an entire province.

She closed it.

"I have decided the protagonist is irresponsible."

"Why?"

"She keeps accepting responsibilities without asking who will maintain them afterward."

Alaric lowered his newspaper.

"That sounds familiar."

Seraphina gave him a look.

"Are you referring to me?"

"I would never."

Evelyne laughed.

The new technical assessment was scheduled for the following month.

It would use a controlled load arrangement, with the reserve zone remaining active throughout the initial stages.

The Academy would measure the eastern circuit under ordinary and elevated demand conditions.

Only after those results were reviewed would the team consider a limited reduction in reserve contribution.

Master Venn would supervise the maintenance side.

Lucien would lead the technical assessment.

Master Fenwick would oversee compliance with the approved procedure.

Theodore would receive the findings through the Council.

Seraphina was not appointed to the team.

She was not invited to supervise the test.

She was asked only to review the final administrative register after the technical report had been approved.

She accepted.

That was the arrangement she had wanted from the beginning.

A week before the test, however, Master Fenwick requested one additional document from the Department.

The original authorization for the eastern circuit's final modification.

The existing record confirmed that the work had been completed.

But the modification's authorization contained a reference to an earlier design review.

The design review had recommended changes to the eastern control assembly and a revised relationship with the reserve zone.

Its final page contained a handwritten instruction:

**Implementation subject to confirmation of the central distribution measurements following installation.**

The installation had been completed.

The measurements had been taken.

The final assessment had not been issued.

And the design review's approval sheet listed two signatures.

One belonged to the former Directorate deputy standards officer.

The other belonged to a technical reviewer whose name did not appear in the later assignment register.

The signature was unfamiliar.

The title beneath it was not.

**Senior Examiner, Central Technical Evaluation Board.**

The Academy checked the personnel index.

The examiner had been appointed temporarily.

The appointment lasted four months.

No permanent assignment followed.

No transfer record existed.

No retirement record existed.

And unlike Master Roland Edrick, this examiner's name had never appeared in the Board's final personnel register.

Lucien read the entry several times.

Then he placed the old approval sheet beside the new assessment schedule.

The first test had been incomplete.

The second had been suspended.

The third had never been formally authorized.

And the person who had reviewed the original design had disappeared from the surviving personnel records.

The network remained stable.

The reserve zone continued to operate.

But the Academy now had a new question.

Who had approved the design that made the seventh zone necessary in the first place?
