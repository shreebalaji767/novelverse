# Chapter 6 — A Very Unfortunate Breakfast

Seraphina Veyr had developed a new appreciation for breakfast.

It was one of the few occasions when nobody expected her to examine a ledger, approve a document, attend a lesson, or explain why she had suddenly become interested in the administrative structure of a duchy.

Breakfast was simple.

There was bread.

There was fruit.

There were eggs.

There was tea.

And, occasionally, there was the pleasant illusion that the day might proceed without complications.

Unfortunately, the illusion ended when her father placed a letter beside her plate.

“Seraphina.”

She looked at the letter.

Then at him.

“Good morning, Father.”

“Good morning.”

“Is that for me?”

“Yes.”

Seraphina slowly put down her spoon.

“What is it?”

“A request from Roland Ashford.”

She looked at the letter again.

The financial office's seal was unmistakable.

She had seen enough of it over the last few days to recognize it immediately.

“Does it concern the northern maintenance allocation?”

“It does.”

Seraphina sighed.

Across the table, Duchess Evelyne glanced up from her tea.

“Is something wrong?”

“Nothing is wrong,” Seraphina replied. “That is precisely what I am hoping.”

Alaric unfolded the letter.

“Roland has found the original authorization. He would like to discuss the discrepancy with us.”

Seraphina blinked.

“With us?”

“Yes.”

“Why me?”

“Because your questions led him to examine the records.”

Seraphina looked at her breakfast.

She had been trying very hard not to become important.

Apparently, she had failed.

“Could I simply receive a written explanation?”

Alaric considered it.

“Perhaps. But Roland believes the original documents should be reviewed together.”

Seraphina picked up her tea.

“Then I suppose I should attend.”

Evelyne smiled.

“You sound very enthusiastic.”

“I am trying to maintain a positive attitude.”

“That is admirable.”

Seraphina took a sip.

The tea was excellent.

It was also the last peaceful thing she would enjoy that morning.

### A Morning of Terrible Timing

The meeting had been scheduled for the tenth bell.

Seraphina arrived at the eastern office five minutes early.

She had brought a notebook, two pens, and absolutely no intention of making any decisions.

Roland Ashford stood beside the desk.

A large archive box occupied one corner.

Several documents had been arranged in chronological order.

Alaric was already seated.

“Good morning,” Seraphina said.

“Good morning,” Roland replied.

The accountant looked tired.

Seraphina wondered whether he had slept.

She decided not to ask.

“Please sit,” Alaric said.

Seraphina took the chair beside her father.

Roland opened the archive box.

“The issue concerns an additional maintenance allocation requested by the northern property office three months ago.”

He placed a document on the desk.

“This is the original authorization.”

Seraphina leaned forward.

The paper contained a list of properties and a general expenditure ceiling.

At the bottom was the signature of the duke's administrative office.

“Is this the same document mentioned in the regional summary?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“Then what is the discrepancy?”

Roland placed another paper beside it.

“This is the copy attached to the regional expenditure report.”

Seraphina compared the two.

The wording was similar.

The dates were identical.

But the second document contained an additional paragraph authorizing a specific adjustment.

She looked up.

“This paragraph is not in the original.”

“Correct.”

“Was it added later?”

“That is what I am investigating.”

Alaric's expression hardened.

“Who prepared the copy?”

“The northern property office.”

“And who approved it?”

Roland placed a third document on the desk.

“The approval is attributed to an assistant administrator named Peter Hales.”

Seraphina studied the signature.

“Is he authorized to approve this type of adjustment?”

“Not independently.”

“Then why is his signature here?”

Roland looked at her.

“That is the question.”

Seraphina sat back.

She did not like the direction of the conversation.

It was beginning to sound like a mystery.

Mysteries were dangerous.

They involved missing documents, suspicious signatures, people who refused to answer questions, and eventually someone standing in a dark corridor holding a knife.

She preferred furniture inventories.

Furniture inventories were harmless.

“Could this have been an administrative mistake?” she asked.

“It could,” Roland replied. “The copied document may have been prepared from an earlier draft.”

“Or someone may have attached the wrong page.”

“That is possible.”

Alaric folded his arms.

“Have you contacted Hales?”

“I have sent a request for clarification. He has not yet replied.”

“Then we wait for his explanation.”

Roland nodded.

Seraphina looked at the documents again.

“Is the money missing?”

“No confirmed loss has been established.”

“Has the contractor been paid?”

“Yes.”

“Was the work completed?”

“The contractor submitted a completion report. The regional inspection record is still being verified.”

Seraphina nodded.

“Then we should not assume that the payment was improper.”

Roland's shoulders relaxed slightly.

“Precisely.”

Alaric glanced at his daughter.

“You are being careful.”

Seraphina looked at him.

“I do not know enough to be anything else.”

The duke gave a faint smile.

“An excellent habit.”

Seraphina hoped it would remain useful.

### The Uninvited Guest

The meeting was interrupted by a knock.

The secretary entered.

“Your Grace, Master Cedric Veyr has arrived.”

Alaric looked toward the door.

“Send him in.”

Cedric entered carrying a folder.

He bowed to the duke, greeted Seraphina, and then looked at Roland.

“I was told there was a discussion about the northern property allocation.”

Roland nodded.

“Yes. I requested the original records.”

Cedric placed his folder on the desk.

“I have brought the property office's correspondence register.”

Seraphina looked at it.

“Does it include the original request?”

“Yes.”

Cedric opened the folder.

The first letter was dated three months earlier.

It requested additional funds for repairs to several storage buildings.

The second letter acknowledged receipt.

The third requested clarification about the allocation.

The fourth contained an updated estimate.

Seraphina read them in order.

Then she stopped.

“Who received this letter?”

Cedric leaned forward.

“The property office.”

“Was it answered?”

“According to the register, yes.”

“Where is the reply?”

Cedric checked the next page.

“There is a reference number, but no attached copy.”

Roland reached for the register.

“Let me see.”

He examined the entry.

His expression darkened.

“This is not the original correspondence file.”

Cedric frowned.

“It is the office register.”

“The register records the movement of letters. It does not contain the letters themselves.”

“Then where are the letters?”

“That is what I would like to know.”

Seraphina stared at the page.

She was beginning to understand the problem.

There were several documents.

Each referred to another document.

Some were original.

Some were copies.

Some were summaries.

And a few appeared to exist only as references in other records.

It was a bureaucratic maze.

A very expensive bureaucratic maze.

“Could we request the correspondence from the property office?” she asked.

Cedric nodded.

“I can arrange that.”

“Please do.”

Roland closed the register.

“There is another concern.”

Everyone looked at him.

“The additional paragraph in the copied authorization refers to a second maintenance allocation. That allocation was processed separately.”

Alaric's expression became colder.

“Was it also approved by Hales?”

“Yes.”

“Then we will need to examine that payment as well.”

Seraphina slowly lowered her pen.

She had a terrible feeling that this was no longer a small discrepancy.

### Breakfast Was Supposed to Be Peaceful

By the time the meeting ended, Seraphina had learned three things.

First, the northern property office had submitted an authorization that differed from the original.

Second, the person who had signed the additional approval might not have possessed the authority to do so.

Third, the estate's correspondence records were incomplete.

None of these facts proved wrongdoing.

But together they justified a closer examination.

Roland collected the documents.

“I will continue reviewing the payments.”

Alaric nodded.

“Keep the original papers secure. Do not circulate copies until we understand the situation.”

“Understood, Your Grace.”

Cedric gathered his folder.

“I will contact the property office.”

“Thank you,” Alaric said.

Seraphina stood.

“May I go?”

Her father looked at her.

“Of course.”

She bowed and left.

For the first time in several days, she felt genuinely tired.

Not physically.

Mentally.

She had wanted to avoid the original story's political conflicts.

Instead, she was now involved in a financial investigation.

She had no idea whether the two were connected.

She did not even know whether the financial issue was serious.

But she knew one thing.

She was not going to become an investigator.

She would not follow people.

She would not interrogate servants.

She would not search secret rooms.

She would not spend nights examining old documents.

She would leave the matter to Roland and Cedric.

They were experienced.

They knew the estate.

They could handle it.

She would return to her schedule.

She would read.

She would ride.

She would attend the spring reception.

And she would eat breakfast without discussing financial discrepancies.

That was the plan.

It lasted until the following morning.

### The Wrong Breakfast

Seraphina entered the family dining room at the usual hour.

The table was arranged beautifully.

Fresh bread.

Butter.

Fruit.

Eggs.

Tea.

Her father was already seated.

Her mother was speaking with a servant.

Everything appeared normal.

Seraphina took her place.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning,” Alaric replied.

Evelyne smiled.

“Did you sleep well?”

“Very well.”

“Good.”

Seraphina reached for the bread.

Then the doors opened.

A servant entered carrying a silver tray.

“My apologies, Your Grace. A message has arrived from the northern property office.”

Alaric looked up.

“Now?”

“Yes, Your Grace. It was marked urgent.”

Seraphina closed her eyes.

The universe had developed a personal interest in ruining her breakfast.

Alaric accepted the letter.

He read it once.

Then again.

His expression changed.

“What is it?” Evelyne asked.

“The property office claims the additional authorization was issued under an instruction from the central administration.”

Roland's earlier concern returned to Seraphina's mind.

“Do they name the person who issued it?” she asked.

Alaric handed her the letter.

She read it.

The document referred to an administrative instruction issued by the ducal office.

But the reference number did not match the number in the original archive record.

She looked up.

“Could it be a different instruction?”

“That is what the property office claims.”

“Do we have it?”

Alaric shook his head.

“Not yet.”

Seraphina put down the letter.

“Then we need the original.”

Alaric nodded.

He rang the bell.

The secretary appeared.

“Send for Roland. Ask him to bring the original authorization and the correspondence file.”

The secretary bowed and withdrew.

Evelyne looked between them.

“Is this serious?”

Alaric answered carefully.

“It may be a mistake. But we need to establish how the payment was approved.”

Seraphina reached for her tea.

She had not even taken a bite of breakfast.

A second servant entered.

“My lady, there is also a message from the spring reception committee.”

Seraphina looked at him.

“Why?”

“They wish to confirm your attendance.”

She stared.

The servant held out the envelope.

She accepted it.

Inside was a formal invitation and a request for confirmation.

The event was scheduled for three weeks later.

She had already agreed to attend.

There was no reason for the committee to ask again.

She looked at the paper.

Then at the financial letter.

Then at her breakfast.

“Is there anything else?” she asked.

The servant hesitated.

“A message from your riding instructor, my lady. He would like to reschedule tomorrow's lesson.”

Seraphina put her tea down.

“Why?”

“The weather.”

She looked toward the window.

The sky was perfectly clear.

“Of course.”

Evelyne covered her mouth.

Alaric looked suspiciously close to laughing.

Seraphina slowly folded the invitation.

“I believe,” she said with great dignity, “that I shall eat my breakfast before the next disaster arrives.”

### The Duke's Daughter

After breakfast, Seraphina returned to the library.

She had decided that the safest place in the estate was somewhere without a desk full of financial records.

The library was ideal.

It contained books.

Books did not ask questions.

Books did not send urgent messages.

Books did not require signatures.

She selected a volume about the history of the northern duchy and settled into a chair.

Mira brought tea.

Seraphina thanked her.

For nearly an hour, everything was peaceful.

Then Mira spoke.

“Your father asked whether you would attend the afternoon meeting.”

Seraphina closed her eyes.

“About the property office?”

“Yes.”

“Did he say why?”

“He said your observations may be useful.”

Seraphina looked at the ceiling.

She had made a mistake.

A terrible mistake.

She had allowed herself to become useful.

And now the duke had discovered it.

“Could you tell him that my observations are currently unavailable?”

Mira smiled.

“I will convey your message.”

Seraphina opened her book.

“Thank you.”

Mira waited.

Then she added, “He also said you may decline if you wish.”

Seraphina looked at her.

“Really?”

“Yes.”

That was unexpected.

She considered it.

If she attended, she would have to read more documents.

If she declined, the investigation would continue without her.

That was probably better.

She had no authority over the property office.

She had no expertise in legal administration.

And she had no desire to become involved in a matter that might turn out to be a simple clerical error.

“I shall decline,” she said.

Mira nodded.

“Very well.”

She turned to leave.

Then Seraphina spoke again.

“Wait.”

Mira stopped.

“Please tell my father that if he needs me to understand something specific, I will be available tomorrow.”

“Of course, my lady.”

Mira left.

Seraphina looked at her book.

She had successfully refused a meeting.

It was a small victory.

She decided to celebrate by reading another chapter.

### The First Rule of Administration

The afternoon meeting took place without Seraphina.

Roland presented the original authorization.

Cedric presented the correspondence register.

The duke listened.

The property office's explanation was incomplete.

The reference number did not match.

The additional paragraph was not present in the original document.

And the person who had signed the approval had not yet provided a statement.

Alaric ordered a formal review.

No accusation was made.

No one was dismissed.

The relevant records were secured, and the property office was instructed to provide the original correspondence.

Roland left the meeting with more work than he had entered with.

Cedric left with a list of documents to retrieve.

Alaric remained in the office.

He looked at the empty chair where his daughter would usually have sat.

“She declined,” he said.

Cedric nodded.

“She said she would be available tomorrow if you needed her to understand something specific.”

Alaric smiled faintly.

“That sounds sensible.”

Roland gathered his papers.

“Your Grace, I would recommend that Lady Seraphina not be involved in the formal review.”

“I agree.”

“Her questions were useful, but she has no responsibility for investigating the property office.”

Alaric nodded.

“Then she will not be asked to do so.”

Cedric looked at the duke.

“You seem pleased with her.”

“I am.”

“Even though she has created more work for Roland?”

Roland gave him a tired look.

Alaric laughed.

“She did not create the problem. She merely noticed that the records were unclear.”

Roland sighed.

“That is an unfortunate distinction.”

### A Quiet Decision

That evening, Seraphina found a new schedule on her desk.

It had been prepared by Mira.

The riding lesson had been moved.

The estate administration lesson remained.

The spring reception was still listed.

The household correspondence had been reduced.

And beside the afternoon study period, someone had written:

**Private time. No interruptions unless necessary.**

Seraphina stared at the words.

“Who added this?”

“Your mother,” Mira replied.

Seraphina looked up.

“Did she?”

“Yes.”

For a moment, Seraphina did not know what to say.

Her mother had noticed.

She had noticed that the schedule was becoming crowded.

That Seraphina was trying to understand her responsibilities without allowing them to consume every hour of her day.

It was a small kindness.

A very welcome one.

“Thank her for me,” Seraphina said.

“I will.”

Mira prepared the bed.

Seraphina changed into her nightclothes and sat beside the window.

Outside, the estate was quiet.

The guards moved along the paths.

The lights in the offices were gradually disappearing.

Somewhere in the financial department, Roland Ashford was probably still examining documents.

Seraphina felt a little guilty.

Then she remembered that Roland was a senior accountant who had chosen his profession.

She decided not to feel guilty.

She opened her notebook.

Under her list of rules, she added another line.

**11. Let competent people do their jobs.**

She looked at it.

Then she added:

**12. Eat breakfast before discussing financial irregularities.**

That was a rule worth keeping.

She closed the notebook.

Tomorrow would be better.

Tomorrow would be quiet.

Tomorrow would contain no urgent messages, no missing authorizations, and no mysterious payments.

She climbed into bed.

Outside, the wind moved through the trees.

And in the northern property office, a sealed document was being prepared for delivery to the ducal estate.

It contained the name of the official who had issued the disputed instruction.

The name was not the one Roland expected.

Nor was it the one the property office had mentioned.

For now, however, Seraphina knew nothing about it.

She was asleep.

And breakfast, at least, was over.
