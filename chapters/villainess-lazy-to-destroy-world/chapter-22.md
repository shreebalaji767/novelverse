# Chapter 22 — The Crown Prince's Unbudgeted Question

Seraphina read the royal invitation three times.

The first reading confirmed that it was addressed to her.

The second confirmed that it requested her presence the following morning.

The third confirmed that it did not contain the words *optional*, *informal*, or *brief*.

She placed it on the desk.

Mira stood nearby, waiting.

"How long does a private administrative discussion usually last?" Seraphina asked.

"That depends on the subject, my lady."

"And the Crown Prince?"

"That depends on His Highness."

Seraphina looked at her.

Mira lowered her eyes.

It was not a useful answer.

"Did the messenger say anything?"

"He said His Highness expected a reply before the evening."

"Of course he did."

Seraphina picked up her pen.

She considered refusing.

Then she considered the consequences of refusing a royal request without a reasonable explanation.

Then she considered attending and discovering that the discussion had nothing to do with the Academy's inspection.

She looked at the letter again.

The subject was the regional infrastructure programme.

She could attend a meeting about that.

She could answer questions about the conditions under which House Veyr had permitted the inspection.

She could not authorize expenditure, promise access to other properties, or commit her father's administration to anything.

That was manageable.

Probably.

She wrote her reply.

*Your Highness,*

*I shall attend the discussion tomorrow morning. I would be grateful if the subject could remain limited to the Academy's regional infrastructure proposal and the inspection arrangements already discussed.*

*Respectfully,*

*Lady Seraphina Veyr*

She read it twice.

Then she handed it to Mira.

"Send that."

"Yes, my lady."

"And Mira?"

"Yes?"

"If anyone asks whether I am looking forward to the meeting, the answer is no."

Mira smiled.

"I shall not volunteer that information."

The royal palace was not as Seraphina remembered it.

The novel had described the palace in terms of splendour, ceremony, and political importance.

It had not described the waiting rooms.

There were several.

The first was for visitors who had arrived without an appointment.

The second was for visitors with appointments but without the appropriate credentials.

The third was for visitors whose credentials were valid but whose receiving official had not yet been informed of their arrival.

Seraphina was escorted into the third.

A palace attendant took her invitation.

"Please wait here, Lady Veyr."

"How long?"

The attendant looked mildly uncomfortable.

"His Highness has been informed of your arrival."

"That was not my question."

The attendant blinked.

"Approximately ten minutes."

"Thank you."

The attendant left.

Seraphina sat down.

Mira remained beside the door.

The room contained four chairs, a narrow table, two paintings, and a vase of flowers.

A small clock ticked on the wall.

Seraphina studied it.

The hands moved correctly.

That was reassuring.

After nine minutes, the door opened.

A young palace clerk entered carrying a folder.

"Lady Veyr?"

"Yes."

"His Highness will receive you shortly. I have been asked to provide you with the preliminary proposal."

He placed the folder on the table.

Seraphina opened it.

The first page contained a summary of the Academy's regional inspection programme.

The second contained a proposed list of participating territories.

The third contained a preliminary schedule.

She turned the page.

Then stopped.

The proposed programme included:

- Northern Veyr conduit network.
- Eastern Merrow industrial regulators.
- Southern Solmere agricultural protection circuits.
- Western Ashenford military signal conduits.
- Several royal properties in Aurelion.

Seraphina read the list again.

This was not a minor inspection programme.

It was a national initiative.

She turned to the financial section.

There was no budget.

She closed the folder.

The clerk looked at her.

"Is there a problem, Lady Veyr?"

"Does this proposal have a financial estimate?"

"Not yet."

"Has the Academy requested one?"

"I believe so."

"From whom?"

The clerk checked his notes.

"The Royal Treasury."

Seraphina nodded.

"Has the Treasury responded?"

"Not yet."

"Then why is the proposal being circulated to the participating territories?"

The clerk hesitated.

"I am not certain."

Seraphina looked at the folder.

She was beginning to understand why the Crown Prince wanted to discuss the matter.

Unfortunately, she was also beginning to understand why the Crown Prince might not enjoy the discussion.

Crown Prince Theodore Aurelian entered without ceremony.

He was accompanied by a secretary carrying another folder.

"Lady Veyr."

"Your Highness."

"Thank you for coming."

"Thank you for receiving me."

Theodore gestured toward the table.

"Please sit."

Seraphina sat.

The secretary placed his folder beside hers.

The Crown Prince took the chair opposite her.

"I have read the report from the Academy's review."

"Then you know House Veyr has not agreed to participate in the wider programme."

"I do."

"And that our authorization applies only to the inspection of the western conduit."

"I do."

Theodore glanced at the folder.

"Your reply to my invitation was very precise."

"I thought it best to avoid misunderstandings."

"That is precisely why I wanted to speak with you."

Seraphina waited.

Theodore opened the proposal.

"The Academy believes the kingdom's regional mana infrastructure requires a coordinated review. Several conduits were constructed decades ago. Their maintenance records are incomplete, and some sections have been modified without consistent documentation."

"That is what I understood from the presentation."

"The Academy has proposed inspections across all five regions."

"Yes."

"The Royal Council is interested."

"That is understandable."

"The Treasury has reservations."

Seraphina nodded.

"Also understandable."

Theodore looked at her.

"You do not seem surprised."

"The proposal does not contain a budget."

The secretary looked down.

The Crown Prince gave a faint smile.

"You noticed."

"It is difficult not to."

"The Academy believes that a detailed budget cannot be prepared until the inspection programme begins."

"That may be true."

"The Treasury believes that no inspection programme should begin without an estimate of the likely cost."

"That may also be true."

Theodore leaned back.

"And what do you believe?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"I believe the two institutions are discussing different stages of the same process."

Theodore raised an eyebrow.

"Explain."

"The Academy wants to know what needs repairing before estimating the cost. The Treasury wants to know the cost before approving the work. Both positions are reasonable, but neither answers the question of how the initial inspections will be funded."

Theodore said nothing.

Seraphina continued.

"Perhaps the programme should be divided into stages. The first stage would establish which sites require inspection, what information is needed, and what the inspections themselves would cost. The second stage would identify necessary repairs and provide estimates. The third stage would concern funding and implementation."

The secretary began writing.

Theodore looked at him.

"Please continue."

Seraphina looked back at the Crown Prince.

"That would also prevent a territory from being treated as having agreed to repairs simply because it permitted an inspection."

Theodore nodded slowly.

"That distinction is important."

"It is particularly important when the Academy is inspecting property it does not own."

"Yes."

He turned a page.

"The Royal Council has also raised the question of responsibility. Some regional authorities claim that the Academy should maintain the conduits because it designed them. The Academy argues that the landowners and regional administrations are responsible for maintenance."

Seraphina looked at the document.

"Who owns the conduits?"

"That is part of the problem."

"Then the ownership should be established before maintenance responsibility is assigned."

Theodore looked at her.

"Would that not delay the programme?"

"It would prevent disputes later."

The secretary stopped writing.

The Crown Prince tapped the table once.

"That is a familiar answer."

Seraphina glanced at him.

"Is it?"

"You have said something similar about the western inspection."

"Because it is the same problem."

"Perhaps."

He turned another page.

"There is also the matter of older agreements. Several conduits cross private estates, public roads, and land administered by the Church. Some were built under agreements that have since been amended."

Seraphina looked at the list.

She recognized one of the regions.

Solmere.

The southern provinces.

She had heard enough about the region's agricultural and medical institutions to know that the Church maintained several charitable properties there.

"Would the Church be involved in the inspections?"

"Possibly."

"Then its property records should be included."

"That is being considered."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Has anyone asked the Church?"

Theodore paused.

The secretary looked down again.

Seraphina took a breath.

"Forgive me, Your Highness. I do not mean to suggest that the Council has neglected the matter. But a programme involving several institutions should establish who has been consulted and who has not."

Theodore gave a small nod.

"That is why I asked you here."

Seraphina was not sure whether that was reassuring.

The Crown Prince's secretary placed a second document on the table.

"This is the proposed framework for regional participation."

Seraphina opened it.

The language was formal.

Very formal.

It described participating territories as *cooperating administrative partners*.

It referred to *facilitating access*.

It mentioned *reasonable assistance*.

It did not specify whether reasonable assistance included paying for repairs, providing workers, supplying materials, or allowing the Academy to conduct work without further authorization.

Seraphina read the document twice.

Then she looked at the secretary.

"Who drafted this?"

"An interdepartmental committee."

"Was it reviewed by the legal office?"

"Not yet."

"Was it reviewed by the Treasury?"

"Not yet."

"Was it reviewed by the regional authorities?"

The secretary hesitated.

"No."

Seraphina placed the document down.

"Then I would not recommend circulating it as a formal agreement."

Theodore looked at her.

"Why?"

"Because it uses language that can be interpreted in several ways. If the intention is to request access for inspections, it should say that. If the intention is to request financial support, that should be stated separately."

"Would you suggest rewriting it?"

"I would suggest deciding what it is meant to accomplish first."

Theodore looked at the secretary.

"Make a note of that."

The secretary wrote.

Seraphina continued.

"House Veyr is willing to consider a properly defined inspection request. That does not mean we are agreeing to provide labour, materials, or funding for the entire programme."

"Understood."

"And the same distinction should apply to the other territories."

Theodore closed the document.

"Agreed."

Seraphina relaxed slightly.

The discussion was going better than she expected.

Then the Crown Prince asked his next question.

"Would you be willing to advise the Council during the programme's development?"

Seraphina stared at him.

There it was.

The additional responsibility.

She had known it was coming.

She had simply hoped it would arrive in a different form.

"What would that involve?"

"Attending selected meetings and reviewing the regional participation framework."

"How many meetings?"

"That has not been determined."

"How much time?"

"That has not been determined."

"Would I be expected to travel to other regions?"

"Possibly."

"Would I have authority to approve expenditure?"

"No."

"Would I be responsible for the programme's results?"

"No."

Seraphina considered this.

"Then I cannot agree until the responsibilities and limits are written down."

Theodore looked at her for a moment.

Then he laughed.

Not loudly.

But sincerely.

"Lady Veyr, I suspect you have made several officials very uncomfortable."

"I am sorry to hear that."

"Are you?"

"Not particularly."

Theodore smiled.

"At least you are honest."

The meeting lasted another forty minutes.

By the end, several matters had been clarified.

The Academy would prepare a staged inspection proposal.

The Treasury would provide a preliminary funding framework for the first stage.

The legal office would review the participation agreement.

Regional authorities would be asked to identify which conduits crossed public, private, and religious property.

No territory would be considered committed to repairs merely because it permitted inspection.

Seraphina would take the revised proposal back to House Veyr.

She would not become an adviser to the Royal Council.

Not yet.

Theodore seemed satisfied.

The secretary looked exhausted.

Seraphina considered that a reasonable outcome.

As she rose to leave, the Crown Prince spoke again.

"One more matter, Lady Veyr."

She stopped.

"Yes, Your Highness?"

"Your observations about the programme's administrative structure are useful. But I would also like to understand something."

She waited.

"Why have you become so interested in these matters?"

Seraphina looked at him.

The question was not hostile.

It was simply direct.

She considered several answers.

She could say that she had become interested in regional administration because it was necessary.

She could say that she disliked incomplete records.

She could say that she had discovered how much ordinary people were affected by decisions made in distant offices.

All of those things were true.

But they did not explain everything.

"I used to assume that the people responsible for managing things understood what they were doing," she said.

Theodore listened.

"Sometimes they do. Sometimes they do not. Sometimes they are doing their best with incomplete information. Sometimes the information is wrong. Sometimes nobody knows who is responsible."

"And you wish to correct that?"

"Not everything."

"Then what?"

Seraphina glanced at the documents on the table.

"I would like fewer people to suffer because two offices failed to speak to each other."

Theodore was quiet for a moment.

Then he nodded.

"That is a reasonable ambition."

Seraphina bowed.

"Thank you, Your Highness."

She left the room.

Outside the palace, Mira was waiting beside the carriage.

"How was the meeting, my lady?"

"Productive."

"Did His Highness ask you to do anything?"

Seraphina stopped.

"Yes."

Mira looked concerned.

"What?"

"He asked me to advise the Royal Council."

Mira's expression changed.

Seraphina continued walking.

"I declined."

Mira hurried after her.

"Was he displeased?"

"No."

"Was he surprised?"

"Possibly."

"Was the meeting difficult?"

"Not particularly."

Mira looked at her.

Seraphina sighed.

"It was only forty minutes of explaining that permission to inspect something does not mean permission to repair it."

Mira considered this.

"That sounds reasonable."

"It is."

"Then why did it take forty minutes?"

Seraphina looked at the palace.

"Because the kingdom has many people who need to agree that something is reasonable before anyone can do it."

Mira nodded.

"Would you like to return to the residence?"

"Yes."

The carriage door opened.

Seraphina climbed inside.

She had completed her meeting.

She had not agreed to a new position.

She had not signed an undefined contract.

She had not been invited to a reception.

She was beginning to feel that the visit might still end successfully.

The next morning, Seraphina visited the Academy to collect the revised inspection notes.

Lucien was waiting in the review chamber.

He had several documents spread across the table.

"Lady Veyr."

"Master Arkwright."

"I heard you met with the Crown Prince."

"Yes."

"And?"

"The Academy will need to prepare a staged proposal."

Lucien frowned.

"That will take time."

"Yes."

"We were hoping to begin the wider inspections before the end of the season."

"Then perhaps the proposal should be prepared quickly."

He looked at her.

"That is not particularly helpful."

"It was not intended to be."

Lucien sighed.

Seraphina took a seat.

"What did you find in the old mill-road junction?"

"The conduit is stable. However, we discovered that the maintenance marker was removed deliberately."

Seraphina looked up.

"Deliberately?"

"It was detached from its mounting. The fixing bolts were removed."

"Could it have fallen?"

"Not without leaving damage to the mounting."

"Could it have been taken for repair?"

"Possibly. But there is no record of removal."

Seraphina considered this.

"Does the missing marker affect the conduit?"

"Not directly. It identifies the junction and its original circuit configuration."

"So the conduit can still function without it."

"Yes."

"Then we should not assume that its removal caused the irregular flow."

Lucien looked pleased.

"Exactly."

He handed her a diagram.

The junction had been modified twenty-two years earlier.

The missing marker was associated with the original configuration.

The Academy could not yet determine whether the marker had been removed during the modification or at a later date.

"Do you have a list of the people who worked on the junction?"

"We have the supervising engineer's name. The contractor is still unknown."

"Who was the engineer?"

"Master Oren Whitlock."

Seraphina looked at the name.

It meant nothing to her.

"Is he still employed by the Academy?"

"No. He retired eleven years ago."

"Is he available for questions?"

"Possibly. We have not contacted him yet."

"Then perhaps you should."

Lucien smiled.

"I was planning to."

Seraphina returned the diagram.

"Good."

He gathered the papers.

"Would you be interested in seeing the junction?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"Is there any practical reason for me to visit it?"

"Not technically."

"Then no."

Lucien looked disappointed.

Seraphina stood.

"I am not an engineer."

"You are familiar with the local arrangements."

"Which is why I can tell you where the workers are repairing the irrigation channel. I do not need to stand beside the conduit to do that."

Lucien considered her answer.

Then he nodded.

"Fair enough."

She left him with his documents.

That afternoon, Seraphina received a message from the north.

Cedric had located a further record concerning the old emergency grain distribution.

The Greyford village office had confirmed the delivery of thirty measures.

The record included a receiving signature and a distribution list.

Three villages confirmed.

One hundred and thirty measures accounted for.

Millhaven remained uncertain.

Alder's Crossing remained uncertain.

The remaining balance of 105 measures was still unresolved.

The message also contained a small detail.

The Greyford distribution list referred to an additional emergency shipment that had been sent from the temporary depot after the original allocation.

Its quantity was not recorded.

The shipment was described only as *supplemental provisions for households affected by late frost*.

Seraphina read the entry carefully.

"Another shipment," she murmured.

Mira looked up.

"From the same depot?"

"Apparently."

"Does that mean the missing grain was distributed?"

"Possibly. But we do not know how much."

Seraphina wrote a note.

The original 260 measures had been received.

Some had been distributed through village allocations.

At least one supplemental shipment had also been recorded.

The total could not yet be reconciled.

She looked at the documents.

The investigation was beginning to show that the emergency distribution had been more complicated than the surviving summary suggested.

There might have been multiple deliveries.

There might have been additional allocations.

There might have been grain retained in reserve.

Or there might have been losses.

The records were insufficient to decide.

She added a note:

**Do not treat the original allocation list as the complete distribution record.**

Then she put the letter away.

The day was almost over.

She had two days before returning to Veyr.

The Academy review had gone reasonably well.

The Crown Prince had not asked her to attend another reception.

The royal proposal was being revised.

And the grain investigation had confirmed another village delivery.

It was, by any sensible measure, a productive visit.

She allowed herself to enjoy the thought.

Then Mira entered with a final message.

"Lady Seraphina, the Duchess has received a request from the Royal Palace."

Seraphina looked up.

"What kind of request?"

Mira held out the letter.

"It concerns the spring reception."

Seraphina stared at her.

"Which spring reception?"

"The royal one."

Seraphina slowly closed her eyes.

She had not agreed to attend.

She had not been asked.

And yet, somehow, the invitation had found her.

She opened the letter.

The royal palace had requested the attendance of House Veyr at a formal gathering to mark the opening of the new administrative season.

The invitation was addressed to Duke Alaric, Duchess Evelyne, and Lady Seraphina.

She looked at the date.

Three weeks away.

Seraphina placed the letter on the desk.

Then she looked at Mira.

"Please tell my mother I have a question."

"Yes, my lady?"

"Does refusing a royal invitation require a better excuse than refusing an ordinary one?"

Mira considered this carefully.

"I believe so."

Seraphina sighed.

"Of course it does."

**End of Chapter 22**
