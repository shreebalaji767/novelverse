# Chapter 32 — The Proposal With Seventeen Responsibilities

The Crown Prince's proposal arrived nine days after Seraphina returned to the northern estate.

It was delivered in a substantial envelope bearing the royal seal.

Seraphina knew it would be troublesome before she opened it.

Not because royal documents were necessarily troublesome.

Some were perfectly reasonable.

Others contained seventeen responsibilities, three vague obligations and a sentence that somehow managed to make an unpaid position sound like a privilege.

She broke the seal.

The proposal was six pages long.

She read the first page.

Then the second.

By the third, she had stopped drinking her tea.

By the fourth, she had reached for her notebook.

By the sixth, she put the entire document down.

"Mira."

"Yes, my lady?"

"How many pages?"

"Six."

"How many responsibilities?"

Mira glanced at the document.

"Would you like me to count them?"

"No. I am afraid of the answer."

The proposal concerned the temporary Royal Infrastructure Review Committee.

Its stated purpose was to examine the administration of the old regional maintenance programme, identify gaps in institutional responsibility, and recommend procedures for future inspections.

Seraphina was invited to serve as an external administrative adviser.

The position sounded limited.

The responsibilities were not.

She was expected to:

- Review the surviving regional records.
- Compare the original programme with current infrastructure arrangements.
- Identify missing administrative transfers.
- Examine regional inspection procedures.
- Review the proposed ownership register.
- Advise on coordination between the Academy and regional authorities.
- Attend committee meetings.
- Review the committee's minutes.
- Prepare summaries for the Crown Prince.
- Advise on the allocation of future inspection resources.
- Identify potential administrative risks.
- Review the proposed maintenance schedule.
- Assist in resolving disagreements between regional offices.
- Recommend improvements to reporting procedures.
- Provide quarterly progress assessments.
- Review the committee's final report.
- Remain available for additional duties requested by the Crown Prince.

Seraphina read the last responsibility twice.

"Additional duties."

Mira nodded.

"It sounds broad."

"It is a large hole disguised as a sentence."

Mira tried not to smile.

Seraphina turned to the first page.

The position had no defined end date.

It also did not specify how many hours she would be expected to contribute.

She picked up her pen.

"Bring me a fresh sheet."

Cedric arrived shortly afterward.

He read the proposal in silence.

Then he turned to Seraphina.

"They want you to do quite a lot."

"Yes."

"Perhaps more than they realize."

"Perhaps more than they admit."

Cedric returned to the document.

"The Crown Prince may simply want someone to ensure the committee remains organized."

"Then he should appoint a secretary."

"He may believe your experience would be useful."

"My experience consists of discovering that a delivery receipt does not prove a delivery."

Cedric looked at her.

"That is not an insignificant skill."

"It is also not sufficient qualification to supervise six regional infrastructure facilities."

He nodded.

Seraphina placed the proposal between them.

"Let us separate the responsibilities."

She took a fresh sheet.

"The first group concerns documents."

Cedric followed.

"Reviewing records, comparing registers, identifying missing transfers."

"That is reasonable, provided the committee supplies the documents and the work is limited to the original maintenance programme."

She wrote.

"The second group concerns coordination."

"Meetings, minutes, regional disagreements."

"That is not our responsibility. The Royal Infrastructure Department should appoint its own coordinator."

Cedric nodded.

"The third group concerns technical and financial decisions."

"Inspection resources, maintenance schedules and future allocations."

"Those should remain with the Academy and Treasury."

"Exactly."

She wrote the final group.

"Additional duties."

Cedric looked at the phrase.

"I would remove it."

"So would I."

Seraphina leaned back.

"An adviser should advise on the assigned matter. An adviser should not become an alternative department."

Cedric smiled.

"That is a useful way to put it."

The revised response took most of the morning.

Seraphina did not reject the invitation.

She also did not accept it.

Instead, she proposed a narrower role.

She would serve as an external administrative adviser for the historical records review only.

The appointment would last three months, with the possibility of extension by mutual agreement.

The committee would provide the relevant documents in an organized form.

She would review the records, identify inconsistencies and submit written observations.

She would attend no more than two meetings per month unless a specific issue required additional discussion.

She would not make technical assessments, approve expenditures, supervise regional staff or resolve disputes between institutions.

She would not be responsible for implementing recommendations.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would appoint a coordinator.

The Academy would remain responsible for technical inspections.

The Treasury would remain responsible for financial verification.

The committee would have to provide a clear statement of its authority and reporting arrangements.

Cedric read the final draft.

"It is very clear."

"Good."

"It may also disappoint them."

"That is not a reason to make it unclear."

Cedric laughed.

Seraphina sealed the letter.

"Send it."

The Crown Prince's reply came five days later.

He accepted the proposed limits.

The appointment would be for three months.

The committee coordinator would be appointed by the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The Academy and Treasury would retain their respective responsibilities.

The first review would concern only the historical maintenance programme.

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she placed it in her correspondence file.

"That was easier than expected."

Mira looked at her.

"Are you pleased?"

"Yes."

"Then perhaps the committee will be useful."

Seraphina considered that.

"Perhaps."

She did not say that she was looking forward to it.

She was not.

The first committee meeting was scheduled for the following week in Aurelion.

Seraphina travelled with Cedric.

Mira accompanied them as usual.

The meeting room was different from the one used by the Crown Prince.

This was an administrative chamber within the Royal Infrastructure Department.

It had a long table, several cabinets and a large wall map showing the kingdom's five regions.

The map was old.

Someone had added several newer road routes in a different shade of ink.

Seraphina noticed that the western marches had been marked with a red border.

She wondered whether it was a military designation or an administrative one.

Then she decided not to ask.

She had promised herself not to investigate every mark on every map.

The committee members were already assembling.

Theodore sat at the head of the table.

Minister Garrick was present.

Lucien represented the Academy.

A Treasury official named Mr. Bellamy had brought several ledgers.

The Royal Infrastructure Department was represented by its newly appointed coordinator, **Mr. Adrian Holt**.

Holt was a middle-aged official with a narrow face and an orderly stack of papers.

He greeted Seraphina.

"Lady Seraphina. Thank you for accepting the appointment."

"Thank you for clarifying its scope."

He smiled.

"I believe that will make the work easier for everyone."

Seraphina hoped he was right.

Theodore opened the meeting.

"Today's purpose is to establish the procedure for reviewing the historical records. We will not make decisions about repairs, funding or ownership today."

Seraphina nodded.

A sensible beginning.

Holt presented the committee's proposed working method.

The historical documents would be divided into six categories:

1. Original project authorization.
2. Financial allocation and expenditure.
3. Construction and procurement.
4. Inspection and maintenance.
5. Transfer of responsibility.
6. Closure or decommissioning.

Each institution would provide the records it held.

The committee would compare the records and identify gaps.

A consolidated register would be prepared.

Seraphina examined the proposed register.

It was well organized.

But one column concerned her.

"Why does the register have a single status column?"

Holt looked at the page.

"It is intended to show the current status of each facility."

"Current status according to whom?"

He paused.

"The relevant authority."

"Which authority?"

Holt examined the form.

"The Academy for technical status. The Treasury for financial status. The regional authority for land and access."

"Then one status column may be insufficient."

Lucien nodded.

"Technical status and administrative status can differ."

Garrick added, "Financial closure is another separate matter."

Holt looked at the form again.

"Then we should divide the column."

Seraphina made a note.

Theodore glanced at her.

"That is the purpose of the review."

She nodded.

At least they were beginning with the right questions.

The first facility discussed was Northern Relay Station N-4.

The Academy confirmed that the building existed and had been inspected.

The available measurements suggested that its magical circuit had been disconnected.

However, the exact date and method of disconnection remained unknown.

The land belonged to House Veyr.

The estate had no record of the station's original construction or formal closure.

The Treasury had not yet located the detailed financial file.

Holt entered the information into the register.

Then he stopped.

"Who should be listed as responsible for the facility?"

Seraphina answered carefully.

"House Veyr owns the land. That does not establish ownership of the former equipment or responsibility for its original operation."

Lucien nodded.

"The Academy can report the technical findings, but it cannot claim the building."

Garrick added, "The Treasury cannot establish ownership through a funding record alone."

Holt looked at the Crown Prince.

"Then the ownership entry remains unconfirmed."

Theodore nodded.

"Record it that way."

Seraphina approved.

The meeting was proceeding well.

The second facility was Western Relay Station W-2.

The Academy had confirmed its location.

Its structure was damaged, but no active circuit had been detected.

The western regional authority had not yet confirmed land ownership.

The Royal Military had been asked to examine the old road records because the facility was located near a former military route.

The military response had not arrived.

Holt looked at the table.

"Should we delay the entry until the military confirms the land boundary?"

"No," Seraphina said.

He turned toward her.

"We can record the location as confirmed and the ownership as pending."

Holt nodded.

"Yes. That would be more accurate."

Theodore watched the exchange.

"Continue."

The third facility was Eastern Road Maintenance Station E-1.

The Academy had found the reference in an engineering catalogue.

No physical location had been confirmed.

The eastern regional office had not located the relevant land survey.

The Treasury had a budget entry.

It did not have a construction certificate.

Seraphina examined the entry.

"Does the funding record identify a contractor?"

Mr. Bellamy checked his notes.

"No."

"Does it identify a payment recipient?"

"Not in the summary."

"Then we should not classify it as constructed."

Holt made another note.

Theodore looked toward the Treasury official.

"Can you search for the detailed payment record?"

"Yes, Your Highness."

"Please do."

The fourth facility was Southern Circuit Storehouse S-3.

Lucien explained that it had been listed as a regional storage facility for replacement magical components.

Its location remained uncertain.

The southern regional authority had been asked to search its old infrastructure maps.

The Church had not been contacted.

Seraphina looked at the entry.

"Why not?"

Lucien turned toward her.

"The Church was not listed as an original participant."

"Then there is no reason to contact it yet."

She closed the folder.

The southern medical supply inquiry was separate.

The fact that both matters involved a regional storehouse did not establish any connection.

She would not create one.

The fifth facility was Ashenford Relay Station A-2.

The Royal Military had confirmed that an old military road passed near the recorded location.

It had not confirmed whether the station still existed.

The western military command had requested additional time.

Theodore instructed Holt to leave the entry open.

The sixth facility remained a funding reference without a confirmed location.

No one knew whether it had been constructed.

The committee recorded it as an unverified project entry.

When the list was complete, Seraphina examined the register.

Six entries.

Six different levels of certainty.

Not one had a complete record.

She looked at Holt.

"Could we add a column for the source of each status?"

He considered the suggestion.

"Such as the Academy inspection report or Treasury allocation register?"

"Exactly. Otherwise, someone reading this later may not know whether a statement comes from a physical inspection, an old catalogue or a financial summary."

Holt nodded.

"That is sensible."

He added the column.

Seraphina looked at the page.

The register was becoming more useful.

It was also becoming longer.

That was acceptable.

A short document that concealed uncertainty was not necessarily better than a longer one that explained it.

The next item concerned the missing regional engineering office.

Its records had been divided between the central engineering archive and the Treasury.

The committee wanted to establish whether all documents had been transferred.

The archive had located several boxes.

The Treasury had found allocation summaries.

The Royal Infrastructure Department had identified a transfer reference.

But nobody had confirmed whether the complete set of records had survived.

Holt proposed that the three offices prepare separate inventories.

Seraphina examined the proposal.

"Will the inventories use the same document references?"

Holt nodded.

"They should."

"Should they, or will they?"

He looked at her.

She continued.

"If each office creates its own numbering system, we may end up with three inventories that cannot be compared."

Lucien smiled.

"That is likely."

Holt made a note.

"We can use the original file references where available and assign temporary identifiers to unnumbered documents."

"Good. The temporary identifiers should not replace the original references."

"Agreed."

Theodore leaned back.

"Please include that in the procedure."

Seraphina wrote another note.

She was beginning to understand why the Crown Prince had wanted her on the committee.

The work was not particularly glamorous.

It was also not particularly difficult.

It required patience, consistency and a willingness to admit that a document did not prove more than it actually said.

She could manage that.

Provided nobody expanded her responsibilities again.

The meeting ended after three hours.

Holt summarized the assigned actions.

The Academy would complete the technical status entries.

The Treasury would search for detailed payment and allocation records.

The central engineering archive would prepare an inventory of the surviving boxes.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would coordinate the document references.

The regional authorities would continue checking land and access records.

Seraphina would review the proposed historical register and submit observations.

No one had asked her to supervise repairs.

No one had asked her to allocate funds.

No one had asked her to become responsible for the sixth facility.

She considered this a successful meeting.

As the participants began leaving, Minister Garrick approached her.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Minister."

"You seem to have a particular talent for identifying weaknesses in administrative arrangements."

Seraphina looked at him.

"I have had practice."

He smiled.

"Would you consider reviewing some of the Treasury's older infrastructure procedures?"

She paused.

The proposal was not entirely unexpected.

It was also not within the scope of her appointment.

"I would be willing to consider a separate written request, provided it is limited and clearly defined."

Garrick nodded.

"Of course."

Seraphina suspected that he had expected a different answer.

She did not apologize.

Outside the meeting room, Cedric was waiting.

"How was it?"

"Reasonable."

"That sounds promising."

"It was."

"Any additional responsibilities?"

"One minister asked whether I would review Treasury procedures."

Cedric sighed.

"Of course."

"I told him to submit a separate request."

Cedric smiled.

"Then perhaps he will reconsider."

"Perhaps."

They walked toward the entrance.

Lucien caught up with them.

"Lady Seraphina, may I ask you something?"

She turned.

"Yes?"

"The Academy has found a reference to a maintenance register that may identify the original engineers assigned to the six facilities."

"Where?"

"In the archives of the Royal Academy."

"Is it complete?"

"No. It appears to be a copy."

Seraphina waited.

Lucien continued.

"The original register contains a list of engineers, their assignments and the dates of their inspections. The copy omits the final page."

"Does the missing page include the western stations?"

"We do not know."

"Then find the original."

Lucien smiled.

"We are trying."

Seraphina nodded.

"Please send me the relevant reference when you have it."

"I will."

He departed.

Cedric looked at her.

"Another document?"

"Another document."

"At least it is not another building."

Seraphina considered that.

"Give it time."

That evening, the committee's first working register arrived by messenger.

Seraphina reviewed it after dinner.

The document was better than she expected.

Each facility had separate fields for:

- Physical location.
- Land ownership.
- Technical status.
- Financial status.
- Administrative responsibility.
- Source of information.
- Outstanding questions.

The uncertain entries were clearly marked.

The register did not pretend that the missing information had been found.

Seraphina approved the structure.

She sent a short response to Holt, recommending that the same format be used for the remaining records.

Then she put the document away.

Her three-month appointment had begun.

It was limited.

It was defined.

And, so far, it had not become a permanent position.

She considered that an encouraging start.

The next morning, a messenger arrived from the capital.

The Crown Prince had sent a personal note.

The Royal Infrastructure Department had received the first inventory from the central engineering archive.

Among the records was a document dated twenty-four years earlier.

It was titled:

**Regional Maintenance Programme — Final Allocation of Personnel and Equipment.**

The document contained a list of six facilities.

Beside each facility was the name of a supervising engineer.

Five names were legible.

The sixth had been crossed out and replaced with a handwritten entry.

The name beside Northern Relay Station N-4 was familiar.

**Master Oren Whitlock.**

The name beside Western Relay Station W-2 was unfamiliar.

**Engineer Darius Veylan.**

Seraphina read the name again.

Veylan.

The same name as the contractor whose records they had been examining.

She did not immediately assume the engineer and the contractor were connected.

The name could belong to a family member, a relative or an unrelated person.

But it was worth checking.

She placed the document on her desk.

Then she wrote to Roland.

*Please examine the surviving Veylan Works records for any reference to an engineer named Darius Veylan. Do not assume that the name establishes a connection. I would like confirmation of identity, employment and any recorded project assignment.*

She sealed the letter.

The investigation had produced another name.

Not an answer.

Not yet.

And for the first time, Seraphina found herself wondering whether the old infrastructure programme had been abandoned because its administrators had failed to complete it—or because the people responsible for completing it had disappeared from the records.

She looked at the clock.

It was nearly midnight.

She closed the file.

Whatever the answer was, it could wait until morning.

**End of Chapter 32**
