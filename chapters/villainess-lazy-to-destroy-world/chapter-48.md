# Chapter 48 — The Equipment That Had Been Waiting for an Engineer

The western authority's letter contained three attachments.

The first was the original receiving register for equipment RC-17-063.

The second was a storage inventory.

The third was a maintenance instruction bearing Darius Veylan's name.

Seraphina placed the three documents side by side.

The equipment had been delivered to the Western Regional Maintenance Authority twenty-two years earlier.

It had been placed in Storage Annex 4.

Its release had been restricted until inspection by the appointed regional engineer.

The appointed engineer was Darius Veylan.

The restriction had never been formally lifted.

Seraphina read the last page again.

"Was he actually assigned to the western region?"

Holt, who had come to the estate to collect the committee's correspondence, checked his notes.

"Yes. His temporary transfer began twenty-two years ago."

"How long was it supposed to last?"

"Six months."

"And did he return?"

"There is no formal return entry in the personnel register."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then we need to find out what happened to his assignment."

Holt nodded.

"Would you like me to request the personnel file?"

"Yes. Also the original appointment order, any extension, and the authority responsible for his work."

She paused.

"And please don't describe this as a missing engineer."

Holt raised an eyebrow.

"Why not?"

"Because he may have returned home, retired or been transferred somewhere else. We have a missing record, not necessarily a missing person."

Holt made a note.

The personnel file arrived two days later.

It was incomplete.

Darius Veylan had been appointed technical representative of Veylan Works before the company received its regional contracts.

He had worked on several infrastructure projects, including W-2.

His temporary western assignment had been approved after the company requested additional engineering support for the reserve programme.

The order specified six months of work.

His responsibilities included:

- Reviewing equipment compatibility.
- Inspecting reserve assemblies.
- Advising regional maintenance teams.
- Reporting whether equipment could be released for installation.

The appointment had been approved by the Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office.

The final page contained a note:

*Further assignment subject to regional project requirements.*

There was no extension.

There was no completion report.

There was no transfer back to the company.

Seraphina looked at the personnel record.

"Does the company register show that he continued working for Veylan Works?"

"Yes," Holt replied. "His name appears in the commercial records until the company's dissolution."

"Then he may have been working for the company while also carrying out the temporary government assignment."

"That is possible."

"Was he paid separately?"

Holt checked the Treasury record.

"There was a temporary engineering allowance."

"Was it paid for six months?"

"Yes."

"After that?"

"No further payment appears under that appointment."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we cannot assume he remained employed by the government."

Lucien arrived that afternoon.

He had reviewed the equipment specification and brought a copy of the old western maintenance schedule.

"RC-17-063 was a regulating assembly," he explained. "It was intended for a medium-capacity installation."

"Like E-1?"

"Similar capacity, but not necessarily the same design. The specifications are not identical."

"Could it have been intended for W-2?"

Lucien examined the equipment reference.

"Possibly. We need the original regional requirements."

Seraphina looked at the transfer instruction.

"Why would Darius Veylan have been assigned to inspect it?"

"Because he was familiar with the equipment."

"Was he the only engineer qualified to do so?"

"No. But he may have been responsible for the compatibility assessment."

Lucien turned the page.

"There is something else."

He pointed to the storage instruction.

*Do not release pending inspection by the appointed regional engineer.*

"That wording suggests the equipment was not approved for general distribution."

"Because it needed inspection?"

"Yes. But it does not tell us whether the inspection was never conducted or whether the result was recorded elsewhere."

Seraphina considered that.

"Then the next question is whether the equipment was ever examined."

The western office sent a list of surviving inspection registers.

There were three.

The first covered equipment received by the authority.

The second recorded equipment issued to installation teams.

The third contained maintenance inspections.

RC-17-063 appeared in the receiving register.

It did not appear in the issue register.

It appeared once in the maintenance register, fourteen years later.

The entry was brief.

*Storage condition satisfactory. Identification confirmed. Release restriction unchanged.*

The inspector was Master Colin Reeve.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Reeve again."

Lucien nodded.

"He was involved in E-1 and several eastern surveys. He may have worked across regional projects."

"Did he inspect the internal assembly?"

"The entry doesn't say."

"Then it was a storage inspection, not a technical assessment."

"Correct."

Seraphina looked at the date.

Fourteen years after the equipment had arrived.

The restriction had remained in place.

Nobody had released it.

Nobody had recorded a technical result.

And the equipment had apparently been preserved without being used.

The western authority provided the storage location.

Storage Annex 4 still existed.

It had been converted into a general maintenance depot, but one section remained reserved for older technical equipment.

The authority agreed to arrange an inspection.

Lucien requested that the equipment not be moved before the technical team arrived.

Seraphina asked for a copy of the building's structural assessment.

The authority supplied it.

The annex was stable.

The storage section had been dry and ventilated for years.

There was no evidence of water damage.

That was encouraging.

It was also the first time in several weeks that Seraphina had received a document without discovering an entirely new administrative problem hidden in its margins.

She almost enjoyed the moment.

Almost.

The journey to the western region took two days.

The road passed through agricultural settlements, low wooded hills and several old military supply routes.

Seraphina travelled with Lucien, Holt and a small technical team from the Royal Academy.

Theodore had remained in the capital.

He had sent a short letter authorizing the visit and reminding everyone that the committee's purpose remained historical review.

Seraphina appreciated the reminder.

She had no desire to arrive in the west and discover that someone expected her to supervise a magical installation.

Storage Annex 4 stood behind a larger maintenance yard.

The building was long and low, with thick stone walls and a reinforced roof.

Its original entrance had been widened to accommodate supply carts.

The western authority's depot manager, Mr. Edwin Marsh, greeted the group.

"Welcome. The equipment is in the restricted section."

"Has it been moved recently?" Lucien asked.

"No. We were instructed not to move it until your team arrived."

"Good."

Marsh led them through the building.

The restricted section was separated by a heavy wooden door.

Inside were several storage racks and a row of metal cabinets.

The air was cool.

The equipment was covered with protective cloth.

A label hung from one rack.

**RC-17-063 — RESTRICTED.**

Lucien checked the number.

"It matches."

Seraphina looked at the cabinet.

"How many people have access to this room?"

"Three depot employees and the regional maintenance supervisor."

"Is access recorded?"

Marsh showed them the key register.

Every entry had been signed.

The last recorded visit was six months earlier.

It had been a routine storage inspection.

Seraphina nodded.

At least the equipment had not been casually handled.

The technical team began the inspection.

They checked the outer casing, identification plate and protective seals.

The assembly was intact.

The internal alignment panel remained secured.

Lucien examined the inspection markings.

"The casing appears to be in good condition."

"Can we open it?" Seraphina asked.

"Not yet. We need to confirm the original storage condition and compare the inspection marks with the old specification."

The team spent several hours reviewing the equipment.

Eventually, Lucien authorized the opening of the inspection panel.

The interior was clean.

The alignment assembly was present.

The regulating core had been secured separately.

There was no visible corrosion.

However, one of the internal guide fittings had been marked with a small red line.

Lucien examined it closely.

"This was inspected before."

"By whom?" Seraphina asked.

"I don't know yet."

The marking was not part of the standard manufacturing code.

The technical team photographed it and recorded its position.

The inspection continued.

An hour later, the team had completed the preliminary assessment.

The assembly appeared structurally sound.

Its internal components were present.

But it could not be approved for installation without a full compatibility test.

Lucien placed the preliminary report on the table.

"That is the most we can say today."

"Could it be repaired if necessary?"

"Possibly. But we should not decide that until the testing is complete."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the equipment remains restricted."

Marsh looked relieved.

"I was worried you would ask us to move it."

"No. We have no reason to move it."

Lucien looked at the red marking again.

"I would like to know who performed the earlier inspection."

"Can the mark identify them?" Seraphina asked.

"Perhaps. We should compare it with the old workshop records."

Seraphina looked at the depot manager.

"Do you have any historical maintenance notes for this storage section?"

Marsh considered the question.

"We have the current records. The older ones were transferred to the regional office."

"Could we request them?"

"Of course."

The regional office sent the historical maintenance notes the next morning.

They contained a record from twenty-two years earlier.

It was not an inspection report.

It was a short instruction from Darius Veylan.

*Assembly to remain sealed until compatibility review is completed. Do not release to installation team.*

Seraphina read it carefully.

The instruction was dated two weeks after the equipment arrived.

The handwriting matched the signature on the original receiving register.

Darius had personally inspected the assembly.

Or at least, he had examined it sufficiently to issue a storage restriction.

Lucien compared the note with the red marking.

"The marking may have been made during his inspection."

"Does it mean the assembly failed?"

"No. It could indicate a point requiring further examination."

"Could you tell?"

"Not without the original technical notes."

Seraphina looked at the date.

"Was a report ever submitted?"

Holt searched the archive references.

"There is a technical review listed under the same date."

"Where?"

"The Western Regional Maintenance Authority."

"Then we should ask for it."

The technical review had not been found in the central archive.

It had been found in the western authority's old correspondence register.

The entry stated:

*Inspection conducted by Engineer Darius Veylan. Equipment retained pending review of regional installation requirements.*

The next line recorded a request for the regional engineering office to confirm which project would receive the assembly.

There was no answer.

Seraphina read the entry.

"Then he did inspect it."

Lucien nodded.

"But he did not approve it for installation."

"Because the destination had not been confirmed."

"That is what the record suggests."

Theodore's secretary sent a message from the capital requesting an update.

Holt prepared a summary.

Seraphina reviewed it before it was sent.

She made one correction.

The draft stated:

*Darius Veylan inspected RC-17-063 and withheld approval pending confirmation of the intended installation site.*

She changed it to:

*The surviving register records an inspection by Darius Veylan and an instruction to retain the equipment pending confirmation of regional installation requirements. No final compatibility approval has been located.*

The distinction was small.

It was also important.

That afternoon, the depot manager brought another document.

It was a transfer request.

The western authority had asked for RC-17-063 to be moved to a regional project site.

The request had been submitted one month after Darius's inspection.

It had been refused.

The reason was written in the margin.

*No installation site approved.*

Seraphina examined the request.

"Who submitted this?"

"Regional maintenance superintendent," Holt said.

"Was it the same superintendent responsible for W-2?"

Holt checked the name.

"No. It was a different officer."

"Then we should not assume it was connected to the W-2 dispute."

Lucien nodded.

"Agreed."

The refusal had been signed by the regional engineering office.

There was no evidence of pressure to release the equipment.

If anything, the record showed that someone had prevented an unauthorized transfer.

Seraphina added the document to the file.

The equipment had been inspected.

Its release had been restricted.

A later attempt to move it had been refused.

And the restriction had remained in place for twenty-two years.

The process was incomplete.

But at least one part of it had worked.

That evening, Seraphina sat in the small guest room assigned to the committee.

The western authority had arranged a simple dinner.

Lucien was still in the technical office reviewing the inspection photographs.

Holt had returned to the capital with the updated records.

Seraphina had the room to herself.

She opened her notebook.

The old reserve programme had produced several different outcomes.

Some equipment had been installed.

Some had been redirected.

Some had been stored.

Some had never received final approval.

The records did not support a single explanation.

There had been delays, incomplete decisions, reorganized offices and missing correspondence.

But there were also people who had followed restrictions and refused to release equipment without authorization.

She wrote a short note.

*Do not treat every unfinished project as evidence of wrongdoing.*

Then she closed the notebook.

The next morning, Lucien brought the final preliminary inspection report.

RC-17-063 was structurally intact.

The internal components were present.

No immediate signs of deterioration had been found.

The red marking was consistent with an earlier inspection notation, but its exact meaning remained unconfirmed.

The assembly could be subjected to a full compatibility assessment.

It could not yet be approved for installation.

Seraphina read the conclusion.

"That is clear."

Lucien nodded.

"The technical team recommends retaining it in secure storage until the responsible authority identifies a valid installation requirement."

"Good."

He hesitated.

"There is another point."

Seraphina looked up.

"The internal guide fitting is a different type from the one used in the W-2 assembly."

"Different manufacturer?"

"Possibly. The design is older."

"Would that make it incompatible?"

"Not necessarily. But it means we should not assume the equipment was intended for W-2."

Seraphina nodded.

The temptation to connect everything was becoming dangerous.

Darius Veylan had worked on W-2.

He had inspected RC-17-063.

His company had prepared the reserve assessment.

Those facts did not prove that the equipment was intended for W-2.

Nor did they prove that the same problem had affected both projects.

She added the technical qualification to the report.

Before leaving the western region, Seraphina visited the regional office.

The maintenance superintendent, Mr. Peter Alden, received them.

He was a practical man with a habit of checking every document before signing it.

Seraphina recognized his name from the earlier W-2 records.

He had been appointed after Alden Mercer transferred away.

"Do you remember Darius Veylan?" she asked.

Alden nodded.

"Yes. He was here briefly."

"Did you work with him?"

"Not directly. I arrived after his initial assignment."

"Did you ever receive his inspection report?"

Alden considered the question.

"I received the equipment restriction."

"Not the full report?"

"No. The technical office retained the original."

"Did you ask for it?"

"Yes."

"Was it supplied?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why was the equipment not transferred elsewhere?"

"Because the restriction remained in place."

"Even after the original engineer left?"

"Especially after he left."

Alden opened a drawer and removed a copy of the old instruction.

"I was not going to release a regulating assembly without technical approval."

Seraphina examined the copy.

The wording was clear.

The equipment was to remain sealed pending compatibility review.

Alden had followed the instruction.

She looked at him.

"Thank you for keeping the record."

He seemed surprised.

"It was my responsibility."

"Yes. And you carried it out."

The carriage left the western office shortly before noon.

Seraphina looked back at the maintenance yard.

The assembly remained in Storage Annex 4.

It had not been lost.

It had not been installed.

It had not been declared unusable.

It had simply waited for a decision that never arrived.

Lucien sat opposite her.

"Do you think we will find the original engineer's full report?"

"I hope so."

"Do you think it will explain why the equipment was never assigned?"

"Perhaps."

"And if it doesn't?"

Seraphina looked out of the window.

"Then we will record that too."

Lucien smiled faintly.

"You have become very comfortable with incomplete answers."

"No. I have become tired of pretending they are complete."

Back at the estate, a letter awaited her.

It was from the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The department had located a separate correspondence file concerning Darius Veylan's temporary assignment.

The file included a letter from the engineer himself.

It had been written twenty-two years earlier.

The letter stated that his inspection duties were complete, but he could not submit a final recommendation because the regional installation requirements had not been confirmed.

He had requested clarification.

There was no reply.

Then, at the bottom of the letter, he had added one sentence.

*I cannot recommend release of the reserve assembly until the intended site and operating conditions have been established.*

Seraphina read it twice.

The engineer had not simply forgotten to finish the work.

He had refused to approve equipment without knowing where it would be used.

She placed the letter beside the other records.

For the first time, the committee had found a clear explanation for why one piece of equipment had remained in storage.

It had not been an administrative accident.

It had been a deliberate technical restriction.

The failure had occurred afterward, when no one had taken responsibility for resolving it.

That evening, Seraphina received a second letter.

This one came from the capital.

The Royal Infrastructure Department had found a reference to Darius Veylan's final assignment.

It was not a transfer back to Veylan Works.

It was not a resignation.

It was an appointment to a separate technical review group.

The group had been established to examine the unfinished western and northern infrastructure projects.

Its first meeting had been scheduled for twenty-one years earlier.

Its second meeting had never taken place.

The reason was written in the final line.

*Review postponed pending receipt of the original project completion schedules.*

Seraphina stared at the letter.

The review group had been created to examine unfinished projects.

It had been postponed because it lacked the records needed to examine them.

And no surviving document showed that it had ever reconvened.

She set the letter down.

"Of course," she murmured.

The old programme had not merely lost its documents.

It had once attempted to investigate the loss.

Even that investigation had been left unfinished.
