# **Chapter 72 — The Caravan That Had Arrived Without Its Last Three Wagons**

The western military road was not broken.

This was the first useful fact in the report.

The second useful fact was that nobody knew where the last three wagons of a supply caravan had gone.

Seraphina read the message twice.

Then she looked at Cedric.

"How many wagons were in the caravan?"

"Twenty-one."

"How many arrived?"

"Eighteen."

"How long have the other three been missing?"

"Two days."

She put the report down.

"Are they lost?"

Cedric hesitated.

"The military transport office says they are unaccounted for."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

Seraphina leaned back.

The caravan had left the western supply depot carrying winter provisions, replacement equipment, and medical stores for the border garrisons.

It had been expected to reach the northern staging post three days earlier.

The first eighteen wagons had arrived.

The final three had not.

The military transport office had sent a notice to the regional administration.

The regional administration had sent a notice to House Veyr.

House Veyr had sent a notice to Seraphina.

She stared at the report.

"Why?"

Cedric looked at the final paragraph.

"The road passes through the northern Veyr district."

"That explains the location."

He smiled faintly.

Seraphina continued.

"It does not explain why we are responsible for finding military wagons."

Cedric placed another sheet on the table.

"The transport office has asked whether the estate has received any reports of abandoned vehicles, damaged equipment, or unusual traffic."

Seraphina read it.

The request was reasonable.

It was also broad enough to make the entire northern duchy sound like a collection of people waiting beside roads with binoculars.

"Send the request to the estate's road supervisors and village offices."

Cedric nodded.

"Ask them to report anything relevant."

"Not to search the entire district."

"Understood."

Seraphina picked up her tea.

"Twenty-one wagons."

"Yes."

"Eighteen arrived."

"Yes."

"Three did not."

"Yes."

She took a sip.

"Perhaps the missing wagons are very committed to being missing."

Cedric laughed.

Seraphina did not.

The matter involved military supplies.

It could become serious.

The first report came from the village of Greyford.

A road supervisor had seen three wagons passing through the western junction shortly after dusk.

They had been moving slowly.

One wagon had a broken wheel.

The supervisor had not stopped them because the road was crowded with returning grain carts.

He assumed they belonged to a private merchant.

The report included a description of the wagon covers.

They were dark green.

The military transport office confirmed that the missing wagons had dark green covers.

Seraphina read the message.

"Then they were probably the missing wagons."

Cedric nodded.

"Probably."

"Did the supervisor see the military markings?"

"No."

"Did he record the drivers?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then we know that three wagons with similar covers passed through the junction."

"Yes."

She placed the report down.

"Ask the military office whether it can confirm the wagon numbers."

Cedric wrote the instruction.

The office replied that the missing wagons were numbered 14, 17, and 19.

The supervisor had not recorded numbers.

The description remained useful.

But it was not confirmation.

Seraphina disliked how quickly a reasonable observation could become a conclusion.

She had seen it happen with historical records.

She had seen it happen with newspapers.

She had seen it happen with twelve estates that had merely acknowledged letters.

Now three wagons had dark green covers.

That was all.

She returned the report.

"Keep the description in the file. Do not mark the wagons as found."

Cedric nodded.

The second report came from a roadside inn.

The innkeeper had seen a military wagon arrive late at night.

Its driver had asked for a place to repair a wheel.

The innkeeper had provided a stable yard.

The wagon had left the following morning.

The driver had not paid for the repair.

He had promised to settle the account at the northern staging post.

The innkeeper wanted to know whether the estate would reimburse him.

Seraphina read the letter.

"Was the wagon numbered?"

"No."

"Did it have military markings?"

"The innkeeper says it had a crest on the side."

"Which crest?"

Cedric checked the description.

"The royal transport seal."

Seraphina nodded.

"That is more useful."

The innkeeper had also recorded the driver's name.

It was **Thomas Reed**.

The military office confirmed that a driver named Thomas Reed had been assigned to wagon 17.

The missing wagon might have been found.

But the innkeeper had not recorded its number.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Can the office confirm the driver's assignment and route?"

"Yes."

"Then ask it."

The reply arrived the following afternoon.

Thomas Reed had been assigned to wagon 17.

He had reported a wheel failure at the western junction.

He had been instructed to continue to the northern staging post after repairs.

The military office had not received his arrival report.

The innkeeper's description matched.

The wagon was almost certainly one of the missing three.

Seraphina placed the letter beside the first report.

"That is one."

Cedric nodded.

"One wagon located."

"Not yet."

He looked at her.

She continued.

"We know where it was. We do not know whether it reached the staging post."

Cedric smiled.

"Yes, my lady."

The third report was more troubling.

A village elder from Alder's Crossing had reported a wagon standing beside the old western road.

It had been there since the previous evening.

The wagon was empty.

Its cover had been removed.

The wheels were intact.

The elder had not approached it because the military office had previously instructed villagers not to interfere with abandoned equipment.

Seraphina read the report.

"Was there a driver?"

"No."

"Any sign of damage?"

"None."

"Any military markings?"

"The wagon had a transport seal."

She looked at Cedric.

"Which number?"

"Fourteen."

Seraphina sat upright.

Wagon 14.

The first missing wagon.

It had been found.

But it was empty.

The cargo was gone.

The transport office was notified immediately.

A military officer arrived in Alder's Crossing that afternoon.

He inspected the wagon.

The vehicle had been left beside the road after its cargo was unloaded.

The cargo manifest listed winter blankets, preserved food, and replacement clothing.

The officer found no sign of damage to the wagon.

The driver was missing.

The transport office issued a new notice.

The missing wagon had been located.

Its cargo had not.

Seraphina read the notice.

"How much cargo?"

Cedric checked the manifest.

"Forty crates."

"Forty?"

"Yes."

"What was the destination?"

"The northern staging post."

"Was the cargo supposed to be unloaded in Alder's Crossing?"

"No."

Seraphina put the report down.

This was no longer a question of a delayed wagon.

The cargo had been removed somewhere along the route.

There might be an ordinary explanation.

The driver could have been ordered to unload it.

The cargo might have been transferred to another vehicle.

The manifest might have been changed.

But none of those possibilities had been recorded.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Do not assume theft."

"No."

"Do not assume an authorized transfer."

"No."

"Ask the military office for the original loading record and the driver's instructions."

Cedric nodded.

"And ask whether any transfer order was issued."

"Yes."

She paused.

"Also ask whether the wagon was inspected before it left the depot."

Cedric wrote the instruction.

Seraphina leaned back.

The winter had apparently decided that the northern roads were not complicated enough.

The military office responded within two days.

The original loading record confirmed forty crates.

The cargo had been loaded at the western supply depot.

The manifest had been signed by the depot's receiving clerk.

The driver had been assigned to wagon 14.

No transfer order had been issued.

No authorized unloading had been recorded.

The driver had not submitted a report.

The transport office had also checked the other two missing wagons.

Wagon 17 had arrived at the northern staging post.

The innkeeper's report had helped confirm its route.

Wagon 19 remained unaccounted for.

The military office asked House Veyr to provide the location of wagon 14 and the innkeeper's statement.

Seraphina sent both.

She also requested a copy of the investigation's findings once the cargo had been located.

Cedric looked at her.

"Do you think someone took it?"

"I think forty crates were loaded, and the wagon was found empty."

"That is suspicious."

"Yes."

She considered the report.

"But suspicion is not a finding."

Cedric nodded.

Seraphina had learned that distinction the hard way.

She did not intend to abandon it because the missing cargo belonged to the military.

The first witness was the innkeeper.

His name was **Gareth Mill**.

He arrived at the estate with the regional officer.

He was a small, nervous man with a weathered face and a habit of holding his hat in both hands.

Seraphina met him in the estate's receiving room.

Cedric sat beside her.

The regional officer explained that Gareth had been asked to clarify the wagon's arrival.

Seraphina nodded.

"Please tell us what you remember."

Gareth described the evening.

The wagon had arrived shortly after sunset.

The driver had complained about a broken wheel.

The innkeeper had allowed him to use the stable yard.

A local wheelwright had repaired the wheel.

The driver had paid for the repair with a military voucher.

Gareth had not seen the cargo.

The wagon had remained covered.

The driver had left before dawn.

Seraphina asked one question.

"Did the driver say where he was going?"

"To the northern staging post."

"Did he say why he was traveling alone?"

"No, my lady."

"Did he ask you to unload anything?"

"No."

Gareth looked at her.

"I did not see any crates."

The regional officer asked whether the wagon had been full.

Gareth shook his head.

"I could not tell. The cover was tied down."

Seraphina nodded.

"Thank you."

Gareth looked relieved.

The interview had lasted less than twenty minutes.

There was no reason to make it longer.

The wheelwright was interviewed next.

He confirmed that the wagon's wheel had been damaged.

The repair had been ordinary.

He had replaced two spokes and tightened the hub.

The driver had paid with a military voucher.

The wheelwright had recorded the voucher number.

It belonged to wagon 17.

Seraphina looked at the regional officer.

"That is not wagon 14."

"No."

The wheelwright had repaired wagon 17.

The innkeeper had seen wagon 17.

Wagon 17 had reached the staging post.

The military office had initially reported it missing because its arrival record had been delayed.

The wagon had not been lost.

The first missing wagon had been found empty.

The second had arrived.

The third remained unaccounted for.

The investigation was becoming less dramatic.

That was good.

It was also inconvenient.

Seraphina had hoped the reports would be easier to understand.

Instead, the first two had become a mixture of mistaken identification and delayed reporting.

The third was still a genuine problem.

The military officer returned to the estate after examining wagon 14.

He brought a new report.

The wagon had been found on the old western road.

The cargo had been removed.

The driver had not been located.

The officer had also found a second set of wheel tracks near the wagon.

They led toward a disused quarry road.

The tracks were not clear enough to identify the vehicle.

The officer believed that the crates might have been transferred to another wagon.

Seraphina read the report.

"Did the quarry road lead anywhere?"

"To an abandoned loading yard."

"Was the yard inspected?"

"Yes."

"What was found?"

"Several old timber piles. No crates."

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Could the cargo have been moved to the northern road?"

"Possibly."

"Could it have been taken elsewhere?"

"Possibly."

She turned to the officer.

"Do you have a list of the cargo markings?"

"Yes."

"Then distribute it to the villages along the route."

The officer nodded.

Seraphina continued.

"Ask them to report any crates matching the markings. Do not ask them to seize anything."

The officer agreed.

The search would continue.

The next day, the northern staging post sent a message.

A wagon had arrived late the previous evening.

It was number 19.

The driver had been delayed by a broken axle.

He had repaired it with help from a roadside carpenter.

The cargo was intact.

The military office had not received the report because the driver had delivered it to the wrong clerk.

The third missing wagon had been found.

Seraphina read the message.

"That is two."

Cedric nodded.

"Wagon 17 and wagon 19."

"And wagon 14?"

"Still empty."

The military office now knew where all three wagons were.

Two had arrived.

One had lost its cargo.

The missing cargo remained the only unresolved issue.

Seraphina looked at the map.

The wagon had been abandoned near the quarry road.

The cargo had been removed.

The driver had disappeared.

There was no authorized transfer.

There was no record of unloading.

There was no explanation.

The officer asked whether House Veyr could assist with the search.

Seraphina answered carefully.

"The estate can provide the route map and the reports from the villages. The military office should direct the investigation."

The officer accepted.

The distinction mattered.

House Veyr could help establish what had happened along its roads.

It could not investigate a military supply loss on its own authority.

The search continued for four days.

The cargo markings were distributed.

Village elders checked storage yards.

Merchants examined their receipts.

Road supervisors inspected abandoned buildings.

No crates were found.

The military office began reviewing the depot's loading records.

The first discrepancy appeared in the dispatch register.

Wagon 14 had been assigned forty crates.

The depot's loading sheet recorded forty.

The receiving clerk's copy, however, recorded thirty-eight.

Two crates had been marked as damaged before departure.

The military office had not included that note in the original manifest.

The discrepancy did not explain the missing cargo.

But it changed the question.

The wagon had not necessarily left with forty crates.

It might have left with thirty-eight.

The depot's records were being examined.

Seraphina received the report.

She placed it beside the earlier documents.

"How many crates are actually missing?"

Cedric checked the latest note.

"Thirty-eight, according to the receiving copy."

"Has the depot confirmed that?"

"Not yet."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the quantity remains unverified."

Cedric smiled.

"You are becoming very consistent."

"I am becoming very tired."

The final report arrived the following week.

The military office had located the driver.

He was recovering at a small infirmary near the western road.

He had suffered a fall after leaving the wagon.

He had been unable to return to the staging post.

The driver explained that he had stopped wagon 14 near the quarry road because the wheel had become unstable.

He had left the vehicle and gone to seek assistance.

When he returned, the cargo had been removed.

He had not seen who took it.

The driver had then continued toward the nearest settlement on foot.

He had not reported the incident because he believed the wagon had been recovered by the transport office.

The office had not.

The driver had been found.

The wagon had been found.

The cargo had not.

The military office confirmed that thirty-eight crates had been loaded.

Two had been damaged and removed before departure.

The final quantity was now established.

The investigation into the missing cargo would continue separately.

Seraphina read the report.

It was a strange conclusion.

The caravan had not been lost.

The drivers had not all disappeared.

The road had not swallowed the wagons.

One wagon had been delayed by a wheel.

Another had been delayed by an axle.

The third had been abandoned after its cargo was removed.

The original report had described all three as missing.

The later investigation had separated them.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"At least the wagons are accounted for."

"Yes."

"And the cargo?"

"Still missing."

She closed the report.

"Then the military office can continue."

Cedric nodded.

The estate had provided the route information.

The village reports had helped locate the wagons.

The innkeeper and wheelwright had clarified the mistaken identification.

The military office had established the actual quantity of missing cargo.

The investigation was now in the hands of the people responsible for it.

Seraphina considered that a reasonable ending.

That evening, Evelyne asked about the caravan.

Seraphina explained the findings.

Evelyne listened.

"Do you believe the cargo was stolen?"

"I believe it was removed without an authorized record."

"That sounds like theft."

"It may be."

Evelyne smiled faintly.

"You have become very careful with your words."

Seraphina looked at her.

"I have become very familiar with people using words carelessly."

Her mother laughed.

Alaric entered the room.

"Has the military office completed its investigation?"

"No. The wagons are accounted for. The cargo is still missing."

Alaric nodded.

"Then the office will continue."

"Yes."

He looked at her.

"Did the estate need to intervene?"

"Only to provide information about the roads and villages."

"Good."

Seraphina picked up her tea.

The western pass had reopened.

The grain deliveries had been completed.

The mills were working.

The military wagons had been found.

The missing cargo remained unresolved.

It was not a satisfying conclusion.

But it was an accurate one.

She drank her tea.

Then Mira entered with another letter.

Seraphina looked at it.

"Please tell me that is not about a road."

Mira checked the seal.

"It is from the Royal Academy."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

The winter had not yet reached its middle.

And the kingdom had apparently remembered that it possessed magical infrastructure.
