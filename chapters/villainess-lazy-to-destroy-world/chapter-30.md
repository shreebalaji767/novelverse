# Chapter 30 — The Station That Was Not on the Map

Cedric arrived with a map.

Seraphina had expected him to bring a notebook, perhaps a copy of the Academy's letter, and a mildly concerned expression.

She had not expected the map.

It was large enough to cover most of her desk.

"Where did you get that?" she asked.

"The regional archives."

"When?"

"This morning."

Seraphina looked at him.

"You received the letter this morning?"

"No. I received it yesterday afternoon. I sent a request for the older maps immediately."

She examined the document.

It was an old northern district map, yellowed at the edges and covered in faded ink. Several roads had been redrawn over the original lines. Rivers had shifted slightly, or perhaps the old surveyors had simply disagreed about their positions.

Cedric placed another document beside it.

"This is the engineering catalogue entry."

Seraphina read the passage.

The Academy's discovery concerned a maintenance station designated **Northern Relay Station N-4**.

It had been constructed to support the western mana conduit and two smaller regional circuits.

The catalogue described it as a temporary maintenance facility.

Its final recorded status was *decommissioning pending*.

There was no closure date.

There was no record of demolition.

There was no clear transfer of responsibility.

Seraphina looked at the map.

"Where is it?"

Cedric pointed to a small mark near the old northern road.

"Here."

She leaned forward.

The mark was faint.

It appeared to be a small square beside a narrow route running between two wooded slopes.

"Is that still estate land?"

"Yes."

"Does the current estate map show it?"

"No."

"Does the current maintenance map show it?"

"No."

"Does the land register?"

Cedric hesitated.

"The land register identifies the surrounding parcel. It does not mention a station."

Seraphina sat back.

"Then we have an old building that may or may not still exist."

"That is one way of describing it."

"Is there another?"

"We have an undocumented structure on estate land that may have once been part of the regional magical infrastructure."

Seraphina considered the difference.

"Your version is less comforting."

Cedric smiled faintly.

The first person they consulted was the estate maintenance superintendent, Master Harlan.

He had worked on the northern roads for more than twenty years and knew the district better than most officials.

When Cedric showed him the map, Harlan studied it for a long time.

"I know this road."

"Do you know the station?" Cedric asked.

"Not by that name."

"Was there a building here?"

Harlan traced the route with one finger.

"There used to be an old stone shelter near the northern slope. It was already abandoned when I began working here."

"Do you know who maintained it?"

"No."

"Was it demolished?"

Harlan frowned.

"I don't believe so."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why?"

"The last time I passed that road, the stone walls were still standing."

"When was that?"

"Seven or eight years ago."

"Did anyone use it?"

"Not that I saw."

Harlan folded the map.

"There were rumours that it had been used by the old road surveyors. But nobody had a reason to go there."

Seraphina considered this.

A structure had existed.

It had been abandoned.

It had not appeared in recent maintenance records.

And the land remained part of the estate.

That was all they knew.

"Could you arrange a visit?" Cedric asked.

Harlan nodded.

"Certainly. The road is passable, though not suitable for a large carriage."

Seraphina looked up.

"Who needs to go?"

Cedric answered.

"The Academy should inspect it. We should not send estate workers to examine a magical facility without technical supervision."

"Agreed."

She turned to Harlan.

"Please arrange access, but do not disturb the structure."

"Understood."

"And take someone who knows the northern route."

Harlan nodded again.

The meeting ended.

The Academy responded two days later.

Lucien's letter explained that the station was not believed to be an active mana-regulation facility.

It had apparently served as a secondary maintenance point, allowing workers to inspect a section of the conduit without travelling back to the main junction.

The Academy wanted to determine whether any original components remained.

It also wanted to establish whether the station had been formally disconnected.

Seraphina read the request twice.

The second point interested her.

"Formally disconnected," she murmured.

Cedric looked up.

"Yes."

"Wouldn't decommissioning mean it had been disconnected?"

"Not necessarily."

"Explain."

Cedric examined the letter.

"A facility can be marked for decommissioning before the work is completed. The circuit may be isolated, the equipment removed, or the building simply abandoned. Those are different actions."

Seraphina nodded.

"So the catalogue's status does not prove the station was disconnected."

"Correct."

"Could it still contain active components?"

"That is a question for the Academy."

Seraphina returned to the letter.

She approved the inspection under the same conditions as the western junction examination.

The Academy would provide the technical specialists.

The estate would provide access and local assistance.

No excavation, repairs or alterations would be permitted without separate authorization.

She also requested that the Academy provide a written account of any remaining equipment.

Cedric prepared the response.

This time, Seraphina did not rewrite it.

The inspection party departed three days later.

Lucien arrived with Master Fenwick and two specialists.

Harlan led them along the northern road.

Cedric accompanied the party as the estate representative.

Seraphina remained at the residence.

She had considered attending, but the journey would take several hours, and there was little reason for her to stand beside a building while trained specialists examined it.

Instead, she spent the morning reviewing the estate's land records.

The parcel had been registered under the same general description for decades.

The station itself did not appear.

There was a reference to a boundary survey conducted thirty-one years earlier.

The surveyor had noted an old stone structure but described it as an abandoned road shelter.

No mention of magical equipment.

Seraphina made a note.

The structure had been visible to the surveyor.

Someone had decided it did not require a separate entry.

That might have been reasonable.

A small abandoned shelter was not necessarily a valuable estate asset.

But the omission meant that later administrators had no obvious reason to investigate it.

She set the document aside.

Perhaps the station had simply been forgotten.

That was an ordinary explanation.

It was also becoming an increasingly expensive habit.

At the northern slope, the inspection party found the building.

It was smaller than the main western station.

The stone walls remained intact, though the roof had partially collapsed on one side.

A narrow entrance faced the old road.

The surrounding area was overgrown with shrubs and grass.

Harlan stopped at a safe distance.

"That is the place."

Lucien examined the structure.

"Has anyone entered recently?"

"Not to my knowledge."

"Any signs of animals?"

"Possibly. There are tracks near the western wall."

One of the specialists approached the entrance.

"Should we inspect the interior?"

Lucien nodded.

"After the external reading."

The team began with the mana instruments.

The readings were weak.

There was a faint residual pattern around the building, but it did not resemble an active circuit.

Lucien examined the measurements.

"Nothing significant."

Master Fenwick looked at the structure.

"Could be a residual trace from the old conduit."

"Possibly."

They moved to the entrance.

The door was missing.

The wooden frame had deteriorated, leaving a gap large enough for a person to enter.

Lucien inspected the threshold.

"No visible activation marks."

The specialists entered carefully.

Inside, the room was almost empty.

A stone platform stood against the northern wall.

There were rusted metal fittings near the floor.

A narrow shelf had collapsed.

The roof damage allowed daylight to enter through a gap.

Master Fenwick examined the platform.

"Equipment mounting?"

"Likely," Lucien replied.

He crouched to inspect the fittings.

"These are older than the western station's replacement brackets."

One specialist measured the spacing.

"It may have held a small stabilizing unit."

"Or a monitoring device," Lucien said.

They continued examining the room.

There was no active mana circuit.

No visible catalyst.

No sign of recent work.

But on the eastern wall, partly hidden beneath a layer of dirt, was a metal plate.

Lucien carefully removed the loose dirt with a brush.

The inscription was faint.

He read it aloud.

"Regional Circuit Maintenance. Northern Relay Station N-4."

Master Fenwick leaned closer.

"That confirms the designation."

"Yes."

Below the inscription were several smaller lines.

Most were illegible.

One date remained visible.

The station had been inspected twenty-four years earlier.

Lucien copied the date into his notebook.

Then he noticed something else.

A second line had been scratched away.

Not naturally worn.

Scratched.

He examined it more closely.

"Do not touch that section."

The specialist withdrew his hand.

"Is it damaged?"

"Yes. But we cannot tell whether the damage was deliberate or caused by later deterioration."

Master Fenwick examined the surface.

"The surrounding metal has corroded. The scratched section is cleaner."

Lucien nodded.

"Record it. Do not attempt to restore the inscription."

Back at the estate, Seraphina was reviewing a letter from the Academy when Cedric's report arrived.

The inspection had found no active magical equipment.

The station appeared to have been disconnected at some point, though the Academy could not determine when.

The mounting platform remained.

A metal identification plate confirmed its designation.

One part of the inscription had been scratched away.

No equipment had been recovered.

No immediate danger had been identified.

Seraphina read the report carefully.

"Was the station officially closed?" she asked.

Cedric shook his head.

"Not according to the documents Lucien has."

"Was it disconnected?"

"The Academy believes it was, based on the readings and the absence of an active circuit."

"Believes?"

"They cannot identify the exact disconnection procedure."

"Then we should not call it confirmed."

Cedric smiled.

"I thought you might say that."

Seraphina looked at the final page.

There was another detail.

The station's identification plate listed a maintenance office.

Not the estate.

Not the Royal Academy.

A regional engineering office that had been dissolved twenty-two years earlier.

Seraphina read the name.

"Who replaced this office?"

Cedric checked his notes.

"The Regional Infrastructure Board."

"Does that board still exist?"

"Not under that name."

"Then who is responsible now?"

Cedric was silent for a moment.

"I will have to find out."

Seraphina closed the report.

There it was again.

A building.

A circuit.

A maintenance office.

A dissolved board.

And no clear statement of who had inherited responsibility.

She looked at Cedric.

"Do not spend a week searching every government office in the kingdom."

"I was not planning to."

"Good."

"I intended to begin with the Academy's transfer records."

"Even better."

The next morning, Lucien sent a separate letter.

He had examined the scratched inscription more closely.

The surviving letters suggested that the missing line might have contained a reference to a transfer order.

He could not identify the full wording.

However, the date beside the damaged section was approximately the same as the year in which the station had been marked for decommissioning.

The Academy had found no corresponding transfer order in its current archives.

Lucien believed the original document might have been held by the former Regional Infrastructure Board.

Seraphina placed the letter beside the previous report.

"That is inconvenient."

Mira looked at the papers.

"Is it important?"

"Potentially."

"Does it mean the station was deliberately abandoned?"

"No."

Mira waited.

Seraphina sighed.

"It means we do not know who was responsible for closing it."

"Ah."

"That is different."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina picked up her pen.

She wrote to Lucien, asking him to preserve the original inspection notes and send a copy of the metal plate's inscription.

She also asked whether the Academy could identify the office that had absorbed the old board's records.

Lucien replied the following day.

The records had been transferred to a central engineering archive.

The archive was still operating.

Unfortunately, it had been reorganized twice.

Seraphina stared at the letter.

"Twice?"

Mira looked at her.

"Is that bad?"

"It means the records may be in three places."

The central engineering archive was located in Aurelion.

It was not a particularly impressive building.

Unlike the Royal Academy, it had no grand entrance, no ceremonial courtyard and no decorative statues of celebrated scholars.

It was a narrow stone structure beside an administrative road.

Its windows were small.

Its corridors were crowded.

Its shelves were full.

A clerk named Edwin Clarke received the Academy's inquiry and searched the current index.

He found the old Regional Infrastructure Board listed under a dissolved administrative category.

The transfer reference was present.

The physical record location was not.

Edwin searched again.

Then he searched the previous index.

Then the index before that.

Finally, he found a note written in the margin.

*Northern regional records transferred to temporary storage during archive reorganization.*

There was no location.

Edwin frowned.

He called another clerk.

The second clerk examined the entry.

"Temporary storage?"

"Yes."

"Which temporary storage?"

"That is what I am trying to establish."

The second clerk considered the matter.

"Perhaps the old municipal archive."

Edwin checked.

No.

The municipal archive had received property records, not engineering records.

"Then the old provincial office?"

"Possibly."

Edwin looked at the transfer reference.

"Do we have a receipt?"

The second clerk searched another register.

He found an acknowledgment of receipt.

It was signed.

The date was twenty-one years old.

The destination was listed as **Central Storage Annex, Building C**.

Edwin looked up.

"Do we still have a Building C?"

The second clerk thought about it.

"Not under that name."

Edwin slowly closed the register.

"Of course not."

Three days later, Seraphina received a short note from Cedric.

The archive had located the transfer receipt.

The records had been moved to a storage annex.

The annex had since been renamed.

The current building was used for inactive government documents.

The archive staff were arranging access.

Seraphina read the note and set it aside.

At least the records had not disappeared.

They had merely moved.

Several times.

She found this marginally reassuring.

That evening, Evelyne joined her in the sitting room.

"You seem occupied."

"I am."

"With the Academy?"

"Partly."

"And the Church?"

"That is progressing without me."

Evelyne smiled.

"Good."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Why do you sound pleased?"

"Because you have begun to understand that being informed of a problem does not make it yours."

Seraphina considered this.

"I knew that already."

"Knowing and behaving accordingly are different things."

Seraphina closed her book.

Her mother had a habit of saying things that were irritating precisely because they were true.

"Are you going to attend the gathering at the neighbouring estate?" Evelyne asked.

"I have not decided."

"You need not."

"Will you?"

"Yes."

"Then I may stay here."

Evelyne nodded.

"That is perfectly reasonable."

Seraphina looked surprised.

Her mother smiled.

"Did you expect me to insist?"

"A little."

"I am not trying to turn you into a permanent representative of House Veyr."

"That is comforting."

"I am trying to ensure that you understand the responsibilities you may one day inherit."

Seraphina looked toward the window.

Outside, the estate grounds were quiet.

She thought of the old maintenance station.

The forgotten building.

The dissolved board.

The transfer receipt.

There were many people who had inherited responsibilities without knowing they had done so.

Perhaps that was part of the problem.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"Do you think people can be responsible for something they do not know exists?"

Evelyne considered the question.

"Legally, sometimes. Morally, that depends on what they could reasonably have known."

Seraphina nodded slowly.

"That seems fair."

"It is also why records matter."

Seraphina smiled.

Her mother had understood the question without needing the documents.

The next report arrived from the central engineering archive.

The old Regional Infrastructure Board's records had been located in the storage annex.

However, the boxes had not yet been examined.

The archive staff had identified three relevant catalogue numbers.

One concerned the northern relay stations.

Another concerned the western junction.

The third was a general regional maintenance register.

Cedric requested copies.

The archive estimated that the search would take several weeks.

Seraphina read the estimate.

She placed it on her desk.

Several weeks.

Not several hours.

Not several days.

Several weeks.

She could live with that.

The old station was not active.

The Academy had found no immediate danger.

The records were preserved.

And the people responsible for the archive had finally located them.

For now, that was enough.

She turned to Mira.

"Please tell Cedric that he may continue with the archive request."

"Yes, my lady."

"And tell him not to send me daily updates unless something changes."

Mira smiled.

"I will."

Seraphina returned to her book.

She had just reached the next chapter when a second letter arrived.

This one bore the seal of the Royal Academy.

She opened it.

Lucien's handwriting was unmistakable.

The Academy had compared the dates from the northern relay station with the old western junction modification.

The two projects had been recorded under the same regional maintenance programme.

They had also been supervised by the same engineering office.

The final sentence was brief.

*We have found a third station listed under the same programme. Its location is in the western marches.*

Seraphina stared at the page.

Then she placed it face down.

"No."

Mira looked over.

"My lady?"

Seraphina took a slow breath.

"Nothing."

She picked up her book again.

There were three stations.

Not one.

Not two.

Three.

And the third was not even in the northern duchy.

She read the same paragraph twice without understanding a word.

Finally, she closed the book.

"Please bring me some tea."

Mira bowed.

"Of course."

Seraphina looked at the letter once more.

The kingdom was apparently full of old magical facilities.

And someone, somewhere, had once decided that keeping track of them was optional.

She was beginning to understand why the original villainess had destroyed the world.

Not that she intended to follow that particular career path.

But she was beginning to understand the temptation.

**End of Chapter 30**
