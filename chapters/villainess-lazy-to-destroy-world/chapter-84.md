# **Chapter 84 — The Interval That Had Disappeared**

Lucien Arkwright arrived at the palace carrying three books, two rolled diagrams and the expression of a man who had recently discovered that his work was going to become considerably more complicated.

Seraphina was in the breakfast room.

She looked at the books.

Then at Lucien.

Then at the books again.

"Is someone moving into the library?"

"No."

"Then why are you carrying half of it?"

"Because the Academy's records are incomplete."

"That is unfortunate."

"Lady Seraphina, I need to speak with you."

She put down her spoon.

"About the register?"

"About the interval."

Seraphina looked at the books again.

"Does the interval require all three volumes?"

"Possibly."

"Then I suggest you explain it before I lose interest."

Lucien placed the books on the table.

Alaric, who had been reading a letter beside the window, looked up.

Evelyne quietly moved her teacup away from the documents.

Lucien opened the first volume.

"The scheduled balancing interval was part of the original operating arrangement for the capital's modern protective network. It specified a recurring period during which the reserve zone would temporarily adjust its contribution to compensate for differences between the eastern and western distribution circuits."

"That sounds like the procedure in the maintenance handbook."

"It is."

"Then what disappeared?"

"The interval was removed from the central operating model during the second modernization."

Seraphina waited.

Lucien turned a page.

"The operating directive was issued eleven months before the model's final register amendment. It retained the balancing interval. The amendment subsequently classified the reserve zone as transitional and removed the interval from the central model."

"Did the amendment also cancel the procedure?"

"No."

"Did it replace the procedure?"

"Not according to the records we have found."

"Did anyone tell the maintenance staff to stop following it?"

"No."

Seraphina took another spoonful of porridge.

"So the instructions continued, but the central description changed."

"Yes."

"That is a very long way of saying the register was incomplete."

Lucien looked offended.

"It is a very long way of establishing why."

Alaric closed his letter.

"Is the network safe?"

"The network is currently operating within its established limits. The Academy has suspended further reduction tests until we understand the discrepancy."

"Can the interval itself be dangerous?"

"We don't know."

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"That answer is becoming familiar."

"It is an honest answer."

"I did not say otherwise."

He opened the second volume.

"This is the original operating directive. It specifies a balancing interval every fourteen days under ordinary conditions, with additional adjustments when the eastern or western circuit approaches its upper operating range."

"Every fourteen days?"

"Yes."

"Does the current maintenance team still follow that schedule?"

"They follow a modified version. The regular interval is still observed, but the timing has become dependent on recorded load conditions."

"Who modified it?"

"We are trying to establish that."

Seraphina looked toward her father.

"How many people are involved in this investigation?"

"At present?" Alaric asked.

"Yes."

"Too many for a breakfast table."

"Then I am leaving."

Evelyne smiled.

"You have not finished your tea."

Seraphina sat back down.

She was beginning to suspect that her family had developed a deliberate policy of preventing her from escaping conversations by using beverages.

The second book contained a series of operating diagrams.

Lucien unrolled one of them.

The eastern and western circuits were shown as two large branches connected to a central distribution structure. The reserve zone sat between them, with smaller connections leading to both sides.

Seraphina recognized the general layout from the previous briefing.

"What does the scheduled interval actually accomplish?"

"It allows the reserve zone to redistribute a portion of the load between the two circuits."

"Why not leave it in that position permanently?"

"Because the reserve contribution is not intended to remain fixed. The distribution requirements change with demand, environmental conditions and the condition of the circuits."

"Then why not adjust it continuously?"

"Because the system was designed to operate within defined balancing periods. Continuous adjustment would create unnecessary changes in the control assembly and complicate monitoring."

Seraphina studied the diagram.

"So the interval is not simply a maintenance break."

"No. It is an operating procedure."

"That distinction should be written somewhere."

"It is written in the original directive."

"Not in the central model."

Lucien looked at her.

"Precisely."

Evelyne examined the diagram from her side of the table.

"Does this mean the reserve zone is essential?"

Lucien hesitated.

"It means the network was designed to use it under certain conditions. Whether the current circuits still require the same arrangement is what we need to determine."

"And if they do?"

"Then the central model must be corrected."

"And if they do not?"

"Then the operating procedure must be revised, tested and formally withdrawn."

Alaric leaned back.

"Can either change be made immediately?"

"No. Not without technical review, controlled testing and formal approval."

Seraphina nodded.

"Good."

Lucien gathered the diagram's corners.

"Why is that good?"

"Because I would prefer not to hear that someone has decided to change the capital's protective network before lunch."

Alaric returned to his letter.

"That makes two of us."

The Royal Infrastructure Department had arranged a review meeting for the following afternoon.

Seraphina was invited only for the administrative portion.

She accepted on that condition.

The meeting took place in a smaller chamber adjoining the Department's main records office. The participants included Lucien, Master Fenwick, Director Adrian Holt, Master Harlan, two Academy specialists, a Treasury observer and Theodore.

A separate table held the historical operating records.

Seraphina sat beside the door.

She had deliberately chosen a chair that would make it difficult for anyone to place a stack of documents in front of her.

It did not work.

Director Holt placed a folder on the table.

"Lady Seraphina, the Department has reviewed the register amendment you identified. We would appreciate your assistance in confirming the administrative sequence."

"I can confirm dates and references. I cannot determine technical validity."

"Understood."

He opened the folder.

The sequence appeared simple at first.

Twelve years earlier, the Department had commissioned an updated central operating model.

Eleven months before the model's final register amendment, the Academy had issued the directive retaining the scheduled balancing interval.

The maintenance authority had received the directive.

The central records office had received the updated model.

The Directorate had reviewed the new designation.

The reserve zone had been classified as transitional.

The old balancing interval had disappeared from the central model.

No formal cancellation of the operating directive appeared in the records.

No replacement instruction had been issued.

Seraphina examined the dates.

"Who prepared the register amendment?"

"That is not yet confirmed," Holt said.

"Who approved it?"

"The Directorate's former deputy standards officer."

"Did that officer have authority to alter the technical model?"

"To approve the administrative description, yes. The technical model itself required separate Academy validation."

"Was that validation completed?"

Lucien answered.

"The file contains a validation reference, but the supporting technical schedule has not yet been located."

Seraphina looked at him.

"So the amendment was approved administratively, but the technical basis is still being checked."

"Correct."

Master Fenwick placed his hands on the table.

"We should be careful. The amendment may have been based on a valid technical decision that was documented elsewhere."

"Then we need that document," Seraphina said.

"Yes."

Theodore turned toward Holt.

"What has the Department found about the maintenance staff's continued use of the interval?"

Harlan answered.

"The current staff inherited the procedure from their predecessors. The schedule was included in the handbook and in the local operating instructions. It was never removed from the maintenance team's working documents."

"Did anyone question the difference between the handbook and the central model?"

"Not that we can establish."

"Why not?"

Harlan considered the question.

"The maintenance team was responsible for keeping the network within operating limits. The central model was used for planning and reporting. We did not have reason to assume the two documents were inconsistent."

Seraphina understood that.

A person responsible for maintaining a machine might follow the instructions that had worked for years without examining whether a central office had changed a description.

That did not make the arrangement correct.

But it did not make the operators responsible for the missing amendment either.

She looked at the folder.

"Do the records distinguish the date the new model was received from the date the maintenance handbook was revised?"

Holt checked.

"No."

"Then that should be a separate entry."

"Why?"

"Because receiving a new model does not establish that the operating staff received revised instructions. And revising a handbook does not establish that the old procedure was formally withdrawn."

Lucien nodded.

"That is important."

Seraphina continued.

"The record should also distinguish the person who approved the designation from the person who validated the technical model."

Master Fenwick made a note.

Theodore looked at the folder.

"Then the review should establish four things: what was approved, what was technically validated, what the maintenance staff were instructed to do, and what they actually did."

"Yes," Seraphina said.

"And whether the reserve zone's current operation is consistent with the intended design."

"That is for the Academy."

Lucien gave a brief nod.

"Agreed."

For the next hour, the meeting proceeded without anyone asking Seraphina to solve a magical problem.

She considered that progress.

The first new piece of information arrived from the capital maintenance archive two days later.

It was not the missing technical schedule.

It was a collection of staff training records.

Master Harlan had requested them after the review meeting.

The records covered three generations of maintenance personnel.

Each group had received training on the reserve zone's balancing procedure.

The earliest training sheet was dated fourteen years earlier.

It described the scheduled interval as a normal operating requirement.

The next sheet, issued twelve years earlier, retained the procedure but referred to the reserve zone as a transitional component.

The most recent sheet, issued six years earlier, used the same language.

There was no instruction to discontinue the interval.

There was no explanation for the transitional designation.

There was also no record of the staff receiving the updated central operating model.

Lucien examined the training sheets in the Academy chamber.

"This is significant."

Seraphina, who had accompanied him only to confirm the archive references, looked at the papers.

"Significant how?"

"The maintenance team has consistently followed the procedure they were trained to follow."

"That is generally what training is for."

"Yes, but it means the continued use of the interval was not an informal decision made by individual operators."

Master Fenwick examined the dates.

"It also means the transitional designation entered the training material without the procedure being withdrawn."

Harlan looked uncomfortable.

"I can confirm that the staff have followed these instructions."

"No one is questioning that," Master Fenwick said.

Harlan seemed relieved.

Lucien turned to the latest training sheet.

"There is another problem."

Seraphina sighed.

"Of course there is."

"The six-year-old sheet references a revised operating schedule."

"Is that the same as the central model?"

"No. It is a local schedule prepared by the maintenance authority."

"Where is it?"

Harlan checked the reference.

"Regional operating archive. Section Four."

Lucien looked at Master Fenwick.

"Then we need that."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"Request it."

Seraphina stood.

"My part here is finished."

Lucien looked up.

"You have not seen the schedule."

"I do not need to see every document in the kingdom."

"That is not what I meant."

"Then you should be more careful with your wording."

She left the chamber.

Behind her, Lucien was already asking Harlan to arrange access to Section Four.

The regional operating schedule arrived the following morning.

It was a thick, practical document.

Unlike the central model, it contained instructions for the actual maintenance team.

The scheduled balancing interval appeared in a table on page seventeen.

Every fourteen days, the reserve zone was to be adjusted according to the difference between eastern and western distribution readings.

If the difference remained below the ordinary threshold, the adjustment was limited.

If the difference exceeded the threshold, the operator was to use the extended balancing procedure and record the duration.

The schedule also included a warning.

**The reserve zone must not be isolated solely on the basis of its transitional classification. Any reduction test requires confirmation that both primary distribution circuits are operating within the approved independent range.**

Lucien read the sentence twice.

Then he looked at Master Fenwick.

"This is the missing condition."

"Possibly," Fenwick replied.

"It explains why the test produced an unexpected relationship."

"It may explain part of it."

Theodore, who had arrived to review the findings, leaned over the table.

"Why was this condition not in the central model?"

"That remains unknown," Lucien said.

Seraphina was not present.

She had deliberately declined the invitation to attend another technical meeting.

Instead, she was at the palace reviewing a revised estate transport account with Cedric.

The message arrived while she was examining a carriage maintenance expense.

Mira brought it in.

"From the Academy, my lady."

Seraphina looked at the seal.

"Does it require a reply?"

"I do not believe so."

"Then it can wait."

Mira placed it beside the account book.

Seraphina continued reading.

The carriage account contained a charge for replacement harness fittings.

The invoice listed twelve.

The storehouse receipt listed ten.

The workshop record listed eight installed and two returned.

The remaining two had apparently been included in the invoice but not delivered.

Seraphina closed the book.

"Why does every organization insist on making quantities interesting?"

Mira glanced at the letter.

"Would you like me to open it?"

"No. I am going to finish this first."

She checked the receipt again, marked the discrepancy and sent the account to Cedric for confirmation.

Only then did she open the Academy's message.

It contained a summary of the regional schedule and the new condition.

Seraphina read it carefully.

The test had not necessarily been performed incorrectly.

The initial reduction had remained within the approved safety limits, but the regional schedule suggested that the test conditions had not fully accounted for the relationship between the two primary circuits.

She wrote a brief reply.

She did not offer a technical interpretation.

She asked only that the test record identify the regional schedule as a separate source and distinguish the conditions specified in that schedule from the assumptions used in the central model.

Then she returned to the carriage account.

The next Academy meeting produced a more difficult question.

The regional schedule was valid.

The maintenance team had followed it.

The central model had omitted the balancing interval.

But the schedule also contained a note indicating that the reserve zone's contribution should be adjusted differently during periods of high eastern demand.

The note referred to a previous test.

The test had taken place six years earlier.

Its purpose had been to compare the eastern and western circuits under a revised load arrangement.

The results were summarized in a single line:

**Extended balancing interval retained pending final assessment of eastern distribution response.**

There was no final assessment attached.

Lucien placed the summary beside the original test record.

"That means the current operating procedure may have been modified because of an unresolved result."

Master Fenwick frowned.

"Or because the final assessment was filed separately."

"Then where is it?"

"That is what we must establish."

Theodore looked at the date.

"Six years is a long time for a pending assessment."

"Yes," Lucien said.

"Was the network operating outside its limits?"

"No. The routine readings remained within approved ranges."

"Then why was the assessment necessary?"

"Because the eastern circuit's response differed from the earlier model during the test."

Theodore looked toward the seven-zone diagram.

"Is that the same difference we observed?"

"Not necessarily. The earlier test used a different load arrangement. We cannot assume the results are comparable."

Master Fenwick tapped the table.

"We need the original readings, the test conditions and the final review, if one exists. Until then, no further isolation tests."

The decision was recorded.

The seventh zone would remain in its normal configuration.

The maintenance team would continue its established balancing procedure.

The Academy would compare the six-year-old test with the current operating model before recommending any changes.

There was no emergency.

There was no immediate danger identified.

But the technical review had uncovered another unresolved stage in the network's history.

That evening, Theodore visited the palace.

Seraphina was in the small family sitting room, attempting to read a book without being interrupted.

She failed.

"Lady Seraphina."

She looked up.

"Your Highness."

"I wanted to update you on the review."

"Is the network still functioning?"

"Yes."

"Then I am pleased."

"The Academy found a previous test from six years ago."

"That sounds less pleasing."

"It was conducted to assess the eastern circuit's response under a revised load arrangement."

"And?"

"The result was marked pending final assessment."

Seraphina closed her book.

"Was the assessment completed?"

"We have not found it."

"Was the test successful?"

"The summary does not say."

"Was it unsuccessful?"

"It does not say that either."

She looked at him.

"Then the summary is very committed to saying nothing."

Theodore sat opposite her.

"The Academy believes the reserve zone may have retained its current role because the earlier test did not establish whether the eastern circuit could operate independently under certain conditions."

"That would explain why the reserve zone continued to be used."

"It would."

"Does it explain why the central model omitted the balancing interval?"

"Not yet."

Seraphina considered the sequence.

The reserve zone had been classified as transitional.

The operating interval had been removed from the central model.

The maintenance team had continued following the regional schedule.

A test six years earlier had produced a result that required further assessment.

And that assessment was missing.

"Have they found the original test records?" she asked.

"Only the summary."

"Then they should not change anything."

"They have not."

"Good."

Theodore watched her for a moment.

"You have become very cautious."

"I have become tired of hearing that a missing document proves whatever the speaker wants it to prove."

He nodded.

"That is understandable."

She reopened her book.

"Is there anything else?"

"Yes. The King has asked the Academy to provide a full technical assessment before the winter council session."

"That seems reasonable."

"He also wants the Department to establish whether the current maintenance arrangements are adequately documented."

"That also seems reasonable."

"And he has asked whether you would review the final administrative register."

Seraphina slowly lowered her book.

"Why?"

"Because you identified the original discrepancy."

"I identified that two documents disagreed. I did not become the kingdom's official register inspector."

"I told him you had not accepted such a role."

"Good."

Theodore smiled faintly.

"He has not asked you to."

"Then I may review one final draft, provided the technical findings are already approved and my role remains limited to the accuracy of the administrative entries."

"I will convey that."

"Thank you."

Theodore rose.

At the door, he paused.

"There is one other thing."

Seraphina looked at him.

"What?"

"The Academy has located the reference to the six-year-old test's equipment log."

"Where?"

"In the Central Protective Network's original commissioning archive."

"That sounds promising."

"It is not there."

She waited.

"The reference indicates that the equipment log was transferred to the Royal Maintenance Office after the test."

"Then ask the Royal Maintenance Office."

"They have no record of receiving it."

Seraphina stared at him.

"Of course they don't."

Theodore gave a quiet laugh.

"I thought you might say that."

She reopened her book.

"Good night, Your Highness."

"Good night, Lady Seraphina."

The following morning, Lucien sent the Academy's formal request for the equipment log.

The Royal Maintenance Office acknowledged the request.

It also confirmed that the six-year-old test had been conducted using a temporary monitoring assembly installed between the eastern distribution circuit and the reserve zone.

The assembly had been removed after the test.

Its instruments had been returned to the Academy.

Its connecting fittings had been transferred to Central Stores.

But the equipment log itself had been listed separately.

The transfer record contained a destination code:

**RMO/CPN/TEST-06 — FINAL REVIEW ATTACHMENT**

No receiving signature appeared.

No rejection was recorded.

No return entry existed.

The Academy had the instrument readings.

The Maintenance Office had the summary.

The Department had the revised operating schedule.

The final review attachment remained unaccounted for.

Lucien placed the transfer record beside the current network diagram.

He looked at the seventh zone.

Then at the eastern circuit.

The reserve was not simply an unused spare.

It had been part of an operating procedure for years, and the last known technical assessment of its role had never been formally completed.

He wrote to Master Fenwick.

No further tests were to be scheduled until the original conditions and results could be reconstructed.

For the first time since the review began, the Academy's concern was not merely that the central model was incomplete.

It was that the network's current operating assumptions might have been built around a technical question that had never received a final answer.

And somewhere in the capital's records, a six-year-old test had ended without its conclusion.
