# Chapter 43 — The Building That Had Been Renamed Twice

The report on A-2 was delivered to the Crown Prince before Seraphina had finished unpacking.

Theodore's reply arrived the following morning.

It contained three requests.

First, the committee was to update the national infrastructure register to show A-2 as a confirmed physical location.

Second, the Royal Infrastructure Department was to investigate the reclassification of the building.

Third, the Central Stores Division was to establish whether the reserved regulating casing had ever been released.

Seraphina read the instructions.

Then she read them again.

"They are reasonable," she said.

Mira, who was arranging her writing materials, looked at the papers.

"That sounds like good news."

"It means I cannot complain about them."

"Is that unusual?"

"Extremely."

Mira smiled and continued arranging the desk.

Seraphina picked up her pen.

The committee had work to do.

The first difficulty was the building's name.

A-2 had been constructed as a western relay facility.

It had later been reclassified as a roadside maintenance shelter.

But the regional property register contained another change.

Four years after the reclassification, the building had been renamed **Western Road Service Depot Fourteen**.

There was no reference to the original relay programme.

The property office had treated the structure as an ordinary road-maintenance building.

The infrastructure department had continued to list A-2 as an unfinished facility.

The two departments had therefore maintained separate descriptions of the same property.

Seraphina placed the documents before Adrian Holt.

"Does the property office know these refer to the same building?"

"It appears not."

"Has anyone compared the land references?"

"No."

She pointed to the survey coordinates.

"The land parcel is identical."

Holt checked the property number.

"So it is."

"Then the departments have been maintaining separate records for one building."

"Yes."

Seraphina looked at him.

"That should be corrected."

Holt made a note.

The committee met that afternoon.

Minister Garrick examined the two registers.

"This is not unusual. Properties are sometimes reclassified when their original use changes."

"Agreed," Seraphina replied.

"Then why is it a problem?"

"Because the infrastructure department still has an unfinished project associated with the property, while the property office believes the building is an ordinary maintenance depot."

Garrick frowned.

"Does that affect the accounts?"

"It may affect the equipment inventory, maintenance responsibility and the authority to approve future work."

Lucien nodded.

"If the building is treated as an ordinary shelter, its magical infrastructure records may not be included in routine inspections."

"Was it ever connected?" Theodore asked.

"No," Lucien replied. "But the foundation and equipment chamber were built for that purpose."

Theodore looked at Holt.

"Can we establish who authorized the reclassification?"

"The surviving register identifies the property office's regional director. The original authorization has not been located."

"Who held that position?"

Holt checked his papers.

"Mr Edmund Price."

"Can we contact him?"

"He died eleven years ago."

Theodore looked toward Seraphina.

"His successor?"

"The regional office has been asked to provide the transfer records."

Seraphina nodded.

"Until then, we should not assume the reclassification was intended to conceal the facility."

Garrick looked at the documents again.

"Could it have been a simple administrative change?"

"Yes."

"Then perhaps we are spending too much time on a name."

Seraphina considered the building.

"Names determine which office receives the maintenance request."

Garrick did not answer immediately.

She continued.

"If a roof leaks, the property office may respond. If a magical foundation deteriorates, the infrastructure authority may need to inspect it. If both departments believe the other is responsible, neither may act."

Lucien looked at her.

"That is exactly what could happen."

Theodore closed the file.

"Then the property description must be corrected."

The regional property office responded three days later.

Its records showed that Western Service Shelter 14 had been classified as a roadside maintenance building because the original construction contract had been submitted under the regional road-maintenance budget.

The infrastructure chamber had appeared in an attached technical schedule.

However, the schedule had not been included in the property transfer file.

The building was therefore registered according to its primary administrative purpose.

Seraphina read the explanation.

It was plausible.

The road authority had paid for the building.

The infrastructure authority had paid for the separate equipment allocation.

The property office had recorded the structure under the road authority's ownership.

The two departments had never completed a joint registration.

There was no evidence that the name change had been designed to hide A-2.

It appeared to have been an administrative consequence of divided funding.

Seraphina added the explanation to the report.

Then she wrote a recommendation.

*The property should retain its existing roadside designation while the infrastructure chamber is separately recorded as an unfinished component of A-2. Maintenance responsibilities should be assigned in writing.*

She sent it to Holt.

The second question concerned the equipment.

Central Stores had located a release register for Group Seven.

The register covered equipment issued between twenty and eighteen years earlier.

It contained several entries for regulating assemblies.

One entry referred to RC-17-051.

The serviceable casing reserved for A-2.

Seraphina examined the entry.

It had been issued eighteen years earlier.

The destination was listed as:

**Western Infrastructure Department — Technical Evaluation Unit.**

The receiving signature belonged to a clerk named Samuel Dyer.

There was no installation certificate.

No return entry.

No later inspection record.

But the casing had not remained in Central Stores.

It had been released.

Lucien examined the technical documents.

"Technical Evaluation Unit," he repeated.

"Was that a separate office?" Seraphina asked.

"Yes. It was responsible for testing equipment before installation."

"Would it have been able to modify the casing?"

"Possibly, but it would not have been authorized to install it at A-2 without a separate commissioning instruction."

"Then the release was not necessarily the final equipment allocation."

"Correct."

Seraphina looked at the register.

"Can we locate Samuel Dyer?"

Holt checked the personnel records.

"He worked in the Technical Evaluation Unit for eleven years. He retired seventeen years ago."

"Is he alive?"

"Yes. He lives in Ashenford."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we should ask him about the release."

Samuel Dyer arrived at the regional office the following week.

He was a short, elderly man with a neatly trimmed beard and a habit of examining documents before answering questions.

When Holt showed him the register, he nodded.

"I remember this."

"Do you remember RC-17-051?" Seraphina asked.

"Not the number. But I remember the casing."

"Was it delivered to your unit?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because it had been reserved for the western relay programme. We were asked to inspect it before installation."

"Did you inspect it?"

"Yes."

"Was it serviceable?"

Dyer paused.

"That depends on what you mean by serviceable."

Seraphina waited.

"It was in good condition. No cracks, no external damage. The fittings were compatible with the planned A-2 assembly."

Lucien leaned forward.

"Was its internal alignment within tolerance?"

Dyer looked at him.

"Not initially."

He opened his satchel and produced a folded document.

"I kept the inspection sheet."

The inspection sheet was dated eighteen years earlier.

It contained a list of measurements.

The casing had been found slightly outside the permitted alignment range.

The correction required was minor.

The final note stated:

*Adjustment completed. Component suitable for installation subject to final calibration.*

Lucien examined it carefully.

"This is important."

"Why?" Seraphina asked.

"Because the component was not rejected. It was repaired and declared suitable for installation."

Dyer nodded.

"That was my conclusion."

"Was the calibration completed?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"The installation team was never assigned."

Seraphina looked at the paper.

"Did you send a report to the infrastructure department?"

"Yes."

"Do you have proof of receipt?"

Dyer produced another sheet.

The report had been received.

It was assigned reference **TE-88/4**.

Seraphina copied the reference.

"Was the equipment returned to stores?"

"No. It remained with our unit until we received further instructions."

"Did you eventually receive instructions?"

Dyer hesitated.

"Yes."

"What were they?"

"We were told to transfer the casing to a different project."

The room became quiet.

Lucien looked at Seraphina.

She looked at the document.

"Which project?"

Dyer searched through his papers.

"I do not remember the name. But I believe it was a southern facility."

Seraphina wrote the answer down.

"Do you remember the station number?"

Dyer shook his head.

"No."

The committee's next meeting was longer than usual.

Theodore examined the inspection sheet.

"So the casing reserved for A-2 was repaired and declared suitable."

"Yes," Seraphina said.

"But it was never installed there."

"Correct."

"It was transferred to another project."

"According to Dyer's recollection."

Minister Garrick looked at the receiving report.

"Then the equipment was not lost."

"It may have been used elsewhere," Seraphina replied.

"That sounds reasonable."

"It does. But we need the transfer record."

Theodore looked at Holt.

"Can you locate the Technical Evaluation Unit's outgoing register?"

"The original unit was dissolved seventeen years ago. Its records were divided between the Academy and the Western Infrastructure Department."

"Then request both."

Holt made a note.

Lucien examined the inspection sheet again.

"If the casing was transferred to a southern facility, it may be related to one of the other entries in the six-facility programme."

Seraphina shook her head slightly.

"Possibly. But we should not assume that."

"Agreed."

Theodore looked at the register.

"Which facility in the south?"

"We do not know."

"Then we have another question."

Seraphina looked at the growing pile of documents.

"Yes."

The southern facility was listed as S-3.

Unlike A-2, it had no confirmed physical location.

Its only references appeared in old infrastructure funding records and a regional technical catalogue.

The catalogue described it as a **Southern Circuit Storage Facility**.

Seraphina frowned.

"Storage facility?"

Lucien examined the description.

"That could mean several things. It might have stored replacement components, catalysts or calibration equipment. It does not necessarily mean it was a relay station."

"Was it part of the same six-facility programme?"

"The funding summary suggests that it was."

"Then why does the catalogue describe it differently?"

"We need the original technical register."

Holt checked the committee's archive list.

"The reference is held by the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office."

"Can we request it?"

"Yes."

Seraphina nodded.

"Please do."

Liora's letter arrived the following day.

She had heard that the committee was reviewing the southern infrastructure records and wanted to know whether the work would affect the Church's medical supply programme.

Seraphina wrote back promptly.

*The current investigation concerns historical infrastructure equipment and funding. There is no evidence connecting it to the Church's medical deliveries. The committee has not requested access to Church stores or records.*

She added that she would inform Liora if any relevant connection emerged.

The letter was sent.

Seraphina returned to the A-2 documents.

The equipment had been reserved for one station, inspected and repaired, then transferred elsewhere.

The building had been constructed but never connected.

The station's official name had been replaced by a road-maintenance designation.

And the southern catalogue contained a facility whose purpose was still unclear.

The evidence suggested that the original programme had changed considerably over time.

But the reasons were not yet known.

The outgoing register was found a week later.

The Technical Evaluation Unit had transferred the casing to the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office.

The receiving officer was listed as **Master Thomas Wren**.

Seraphina stared at the name.

For a moment, she wondered whether it was the same Thomas Wren who served as steward at Veyr Estate.

She checked the personnel details.

The southern officer had worked in the capital's technical unit and later transferred to the southern region.

There was no evidence connecting him to the estate steward.

She placed a note beside the entry.

*Same name as Veyr Estate steward. Identity not established. Do not conflate.*

Then she read the destination.

The casing had been sent to the southern regional office for temporary storage.

Not directly to S-3.

The receiving entry was signed.

The equipment had arrived.

But the final destination was still missing.

A second document accompanied the register.

It was a transfer instruction.

The casing was to remain in the southern office until a technical installation team had been assigned.

The instruction carried a reference:

**S-3/INSTALL/12.**

Seraphina looked at Holt.

"Does that establish that S-3 was a physical facility?"

"Not by itself."

"But it establishes that someone planned to install the casing under that project reference."

"Yes."

Lucien examined the document.

"The equipment was intended for an installation, not merely storage."

"Can we locate the installation team?"

"The Southern Regional Office may have a personnel assignment register."

Seraphina wrote the request.

Then she paused.

"Do we know whether the casing was ever installed?"

"No."

"Then we need the receiving and installation records."

Holt nodded.

That evening, Seraphina sat at her desk with the committee's revised register.

The six facilities now had different levels of confirmation.

- N-4: confirmed location, disconnected, equipment history incomplete.
- W-2: confirmed location, structural work complete, commissioning incomplete.
- E-1: catalogue reference only, physical location unconfirmed.
- S-3: project reference confirmed, southern installation records incomplete.
- A-2: confirmed location, construction completed, circuit never installed.
- Sixth reserve facility: funding reference only, location unknown.

She examined the entries.

The original programme had not simply failed in one place.

Its facilities had followed different paths.

Some had been built but never commissioned.

Some had received equipment that was later transferred.

Some existed only in records.

Others had been renamed, reorganized or removed from the active register.

It was possible that the programme had been gradually abandoned.

It was also possible that individual projects had been delayed for different reasons.

The committee needed to distinguish those possibilities.

Seraphina closed the register.

Mira entered with dinner.

"My lady, your mother asked whether you would be attending tomorrow's family breakfast."

"Why?"

"She said your father has returned from the northern estate inspection."

Seraphina looked at the clock.

"Then yes."

Mira placed the tray on the desk.

"Would you like the documents moved?"

"No. I will finish this page first."

Mira glanced at the six-facility register.

"Are these all the stations?"

"Apparently."

"How many have you found?"

"Four in some form."

"That is good."

"Four are not necessarily complete."

Mira considered this.

"Then you have found four incomplete things."

Seraphina looked at her.

"That is one way of describing it."

Mira smiled.

"At least there are only two left."

Seraphina returned to the register.

She had learned not to trust statements like that.

The next morning, the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office sent an acknowledgment.

It confirmed that the S-3 installation instruction had been received.

The office was searching its historical files.

But the acknowledgment included one additional detail.

The installation project had been temporarily suspended after a disagreement over land access.

The landowner had refused permission for the technical team to enter the proposed site.

The regional office had requested legal assistance.

No resolution had been recorded.

Seraphina read the message twice.

A project had received equipment.

An installation team had been planned.

But the landowner had refused access.

She placed the acknowledgment beside the transfer instruction.

The casing might have been waiting for a site that the authorities could not enter.

That would explain why the equipment had been transferred to the southern office rather than installed.

But it raised another question.

Who owned the land?

And why had the landowner refused access?

Seraphina wrote to Holt.

*Please obtain the original land-access agreement, the refusal notice and the legal correspondence connected to S-3/INSTALL/12. Establish whether the project was suspended, relocated or formally cancelled.*

She sealed the letter.

Then she looked at the calendar.

Her three-month advisory appointment was approaching its final month.

She had expected to review historical records.

She had not expected the records to reveal an entire programme of unfinished construction, transferred equipment and disputed land access.

Still, the committee had made progress.

A-2 had been found.

The casing reserved for it had been traced to the southern region.

And S-3 was beginning to acquire a history of its own.

Seraphina picked up her tea.

There was a certain comfort in knowing that the next mystery involved land ownership.

At least land did not usually move between departments.
