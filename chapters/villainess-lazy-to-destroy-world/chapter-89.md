# **Chapter 89 — The Supervisor Who Had Followed the Drawing**

Master Dorian Pell arrived at the Royal Academy carrying a leather case.

He was seventy-one years old, broad-shouldered, and considerably less impressed by the reception arrangements than the officials had expected him to be.

The receptionist had offered to take his case.

He had refused.

The attendant had offered to escort him to the conference room.

He had asked whether the conference room was the one listed in the letter.

When told that it was, he had gone there without assistance.

By the time Lucien arrived, Master Pell was examining a framed drawing on the wall.

"That is an eastern distribution assembly," Lucien said.

Pell glanced at him.

"Yes. An old one."

"Do you recognize the design?"

"I recognize the mistake."

Lucien stopped.

Pell pointed to the drawing.

"The access panel is on the wrong side. Whoever mounted that arrangement would have had to reach across the conduit to inspect the lower housing."

Lucien looked more closely.

The drawing was an Academy training illustration, not a construction plan.

He had never noticed the access problem.

"Was that a common issue?"

"It was a common issue when people designed chambers without asking the maintenance workers how they were supposed to maintain them."

Pell closed his case.

"Are we discussing the eastern circuit?"

"Yes."

"Then I assume somebody has finally opened the housing."

Lucien did not answer immediately.

Master Pell looked at him.

"Ah. So you have."

The meeting included Lucien, Master Fenwick, Master Harlan and two representatives from the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Theodore attended for the first half.

Seraphina was not invited.

She received the administrative summary later.

Master Pell examined the original site survey, the installation drawing and the current photographs of the support bracket.

He spent several minutes comparing them.

Then he placed the documents side by side.

"That is the wrong drawing."

Master Fenwick looked at him.

"Which one?"

"The installation drawing."

Pell tapped the upper corner.

"It is the approved arrangement before the site adjustment."

Lucien pointed to the handwritten note.

"Your initials are here. The note requests confirmation of the revised support position."

"Yes."

"Do you remember writing it?"

"I remember the chamber."

"Do you remember the request?"

Pell leaned back.

"I remember that the conduit would not fit where the drawing said it should."

"Why?"

"The eastern wall was not where the survey had placed it."

Master Fenwick examined the site measurements.

"The wall was further inward?"

"By approximately two handspans. The original survey was taken before the final masonry lining."

"Was the lining thickness not included?"

"It was included in the structural estimate. Not in the conduit clearance drawing."

Lucien looked at the installation plan.

"Then the original route was physically impossible."

"Not impossible. Unnecessarily difficult. We could have used a narrower support assembly, but that would have required a different bracket."

"Which is what you proposed?"

"Yes."

Pell opened his case and removed a small notebook.

The pages were worn and yellowed.

He turned several pages.

"I wrote the dimensions here."

Lucien leaned forward.

The notebook contained site measurements, short remarks and several sketches.

One sketch showed the eastern control housing.

A line marked the original conduit route.

Another showed the adjusted route passing close to the support bracket.

Beside it, Pell had written:

*Clearance insufficient after lining. Request revised support approval.*

Below that was a second note.

*Temporary arrangement acceptable for installation. Final alignment to be checked after conduit placement.*

Master Fenwick read it twice.

"Was the final alignment checked?"

Pell looked at the note.

"I don't remember."

"Do you have a record?"

"No."

Lucien examined the date.

It was the same date as the handwritten request on the site survey.

"What happened after you submitted this?"

"I was reassigned to the western maintenance project."

"Before the eastern installation was completed?"

"Yes."

"Who took over?"

Pell thought for a moment.

"An engineer from the central installation office. I believe his name was Harland."

Master Harlan looked up.

"Not related to me."

Pell gave him a brief glance.

"I did not suggest you were."

Harlan lowered his eyes.

Lucien continued.

"Did you receive a response to the request?"

"No."

"Did you ask again?"

"I sent one reminder."

"Do you have a copy?"

Pell opened the notebook again.

There was a short entry recording the reminder.

No response was recorded.

Master Fenwick asked, "Did you authorize the altered bracket?"

"No."

"Did you approve its installation?"

"No."

"Did you object to it?"

Pell looked mildly irritated.

"I was no longer on the project."

The room became quiet.

It was not an accusation.

It was a distinction.

Pell had proposed an adjustment.

He had requested confirmation.

He had been transferred.

The installation had continued under another supervisor.

The documents showed no formal approval from him.

They also showed no recorded objection.

Lucien placed the notebook beside the official installation drawing.

"Then we need to identify who approved the final arrangement."

Pell nodded.

"That would be sensible."

The Department reviewed the installation records.

The final construction entry had been signed by the central installation supervisor.

His name was Master Gideon Rusk.

He had retired eleven years earlier and now lived in the eastern district.

The personnel office confirmed that he was available for an interview.

Before contacting him, the Department examined his authority.

His appointment had permitted him to supervise installation work within the approved design.

It had not permitted him to approve structural alterations to a control assembly.

Such alterations required confirmation from the technical design office.

The distinction had been included in the project instructions.

The Department found a copy of those instructions.

It also found the installation supervisor's completion certificate.

The certificate stated:

*The eastern distribution assembly has been installed in accordance with the approved technical arrangement, subject to routine calibration and operational testing.*

Master Fenwick read the sentence.

"That is not accurate."

Lucien looked at him.

"Not necessarily intentionally."

"No. But the installation was not identical to the approved arrangement."

Theodore, who had returned for the afternoon review, examined the certificate.

"Was the difference recorded anywhere?"

"Only in the site adjustment drawing," the Department representative replied.

"Was that drawing formally accepted?"

"We have not found an acceptance record."

"Was the circuit tested?"

"Yes."

"Did the test identify the altered bracket?"

"The commissioning test recorded the circuit's initial response and operating range. It did not include a detailed measurement of the support bracket."

Theodore put the certificate down.

"Then the installation was tested, but the altered arrangement was not separately approved."

"That is the current interpretation."

Theodore looked toward Lucien.

"Can the bracket be corrected without replacing the entire control assembly?"

"Possibly. We need to measure the chamber clearance and prepare a revised support design."

"Will the eastern circuit remain isolated until then?"

"Yes."

"Then proceed carefully."

The meeting ended.

Seraphina received the updated register on Thursday.

She was in the estate library, trying to determine why three separate suppliers had submitted invoices for the same drainage repair.

Cedric had already confirmed that the work had been completed.

The dispute concerned the distribution of costs between the northern pasture, the lower orchard and a section of road used by both.

The invoices were not identical.

One covered stone.

One covered labour.

One covered transport.

They had simply been submitted under the same project reference.

Seraphina had been reading the second invoice for the third time when Mira placed the Academy's report beside her.

"Another capital document?"

"Apparently."

"Would you like me to move the invoices?"

"No. They are currently preventing the Academy report from occupying the entire desk."

Mira looked at the papers.

"That is a very small victory."

"I accept small victories."

Seraphina opened the report.

The summary was straightforward.

The eastern circuit's support bracket had been altered during installation to accommodate a conduit route that differed from the original drawing.

Master Dorian Pell had requested technical confirmation but had been transferred before the work was completed.

Master Gideon Rusk had supervised the installation.

His completion certificate described the installation as being in accordance with the approved arrangement.

No formal approval of the bracket alteration had been located.

The initial commissioning tests had been completed.

The eastern circuit had operated within its established limits.

The report recommended a revised engineering assessment before the control assembly was returned to service.

Seraphina turned to the register.

There was a new entry:

*Installation variation identified. Formal technical acceptance not located. Operational testing completed under initial commissioning procedure. Current condition requires corrective assessment.*

She read it twice.

Then she added a note.

*Distinguish installation completion from technical acceptance of the variation.*

She sent the page back.

Master Gideon Rusk was interviewed the following Monday.

Unlike Pell, he brought no notebook.

He brought a folder containing several old letters and a small collection of personal work records.

He remembered the eastern installation.

He remembered the clearance problem.

He remembered the altered bracket.

He did not remember receiving a formal approval from the design office.

The interview proceeded carefully.

"Did you consider the alteration safe?" Lucien asked.

"At the time, yes."

"On what basis?"

"The bracket was thicker than the original. The support points were unchanged. The conduit cleared the housing. We completed the installation test."

"Did you measure the internal guide alignment?"

"No."

"Was that part of your responsibility?"

"Not under the installation instructions."

"Who was responsible?"

"The design office."

"Did they inspect the completed arrangement?"

Rusk hesitated.

"They inspected the installation."

"Did they inspect the bracket?"

"I cannot say."

"Was there a separate technical acceptance?"

"I don't remember one."

Master Fenwick examined the letters in Rusk's folder.

One was a request for an extension to the installation schedule.

Another concerned the delivery of replacement conduit sections.

The third was a note from the central installation office acknowledging receipt of the site adjustment drawing.

It did not approve the alteration.

It merely confirmed that the drawing had been received.

Lucien placed it beside the completion certificate.

"Did you understand the acknowledgment as permission to proceed?"

Rusk looked at the letter.

"I understood that the office had received the drawing. The installation was already underway."

"Did you ask for further confirmation?"

"No."

"Why not?"

Rusk rubbed his forehead.

"Because the circuit had been delayed twice. We had an approved installation schedule, a chamber that had already been opened, and a conduit that could not fit the original route. The revised bracket solved the physical problem."

"Did you believe the technical approval had been granted?"

"I believed the design office would object if the change was unacceptable."

The room remained silent.

Rusk looked at the documents again.

"I understand now that this was not the same as receiving approval."

"No," Lucien said. "It was not."

The interview did not establish deliberate misconduct.

Rusk had followed the installation schedule.

Pell had requested confirmation.

The central office had acknowledged receipt of the revised drawing.

The design office had not issued a formal approval that could be found.

The installation proceeded.

The completion certificate used wording that did not distinguish between the approved original arrangement and the site-adjusted version.

The initial commissioning tests passed.

The circuit remained in service.

No one had produced evidence that the bracket had been deliberately weakened or that the conduit route had been altered to conceal anything.

The problem was more ordinary.

Several people had completed their individual responsibilities without ensuring that the change between them had been formally accepted.

The Department recorded this conclusion.

Master Fenwick asked that the review avoid assigning responsibility beyond what the records established.

Theodore agreed.

"An incomplete process is not automatically proof of individual misconduct," he said. "We should correct the process and preserve the evidence."

Lucien nodded.

The next step was technical.

The engineers needed to determine whether the support bracket could be corrected while preserving the existing conduit route.

The revised assessment took four days.

The Academy's engineers measured the chamber.

They examined the masonry lining, the conduit clearance and the control housing.

They prepared three possible arrangements.

The first retained the existing bracket.

It required no construction work, but the internal guide would remain at an angle that could produce uneven wear.

The second replaced the bracket with a narrower support assembly.

It preserved the conduit route but reduced the available maintenance clearance.

The third adjusted the conduit route and restored the original bracket arrangement.

It required additional work but preserved the original support geometry.

Lucien preferred the third option.

Master Harlan was less enthusiastic.

"Moving the conduit will require opening part of the chamber wall."

"Only the outer section," Lucien replied.

"That still means additional masonry work."

"Yes."

"And additional inspection."

"Yes."

Harlan examined the second arrangement.

"This one avoids disturbing the conduit."

"It also makes future inspection more difficult."

"Not impossible."

"No. But difficult."

Master Fenwick considered both plans.

"What is the difference in long-term maintenance?"

Lucien pointed to the drawings.

"The original arrangement allows the guide to be removed without disturbing the conduit. The second arrangement requires partial access around the support assembly. Every internal inspection would take longer."

"How much longer?"

"We cannot give a reliable estimate until the access arrangement is built."

Harlan looked at the third drawing.

"And the conduit adjustment?"

"One-time construction work, followed by inspection and a new operating assessment."

Theodore examined the plans.

"Then we should not choose the cheapest immediate option without considering the next twenty years."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"The third arrangement appears preferable, subject to a cost and structural review."

The Department prepared the estimate.

It was not large by royal infrastructure standards.

But the work required a separate maintenance allocation.

The Treasury would have to approve the expenditure.

The eastern circuit would remain isolated until the correction was complete.

The reserve zone would continue to support the temporary arrangement within its approved limits.

The plan was submitted.

The Treasury's response arrived two days later.

The expenditure could be approved under the existing maintenance allocation.

However, the Department needed to identify whether the work was a repair, a design correction or a completion of the original installation.

The distinction affected the accounting classification.

Lucien read the request.

He looked at Seraphina's earlier register note.

Then he sent the question to the Department.

Seraphina was not involved in the technical classification.

She was involved in the wording.

The Department's first draft described the work as a *repair to the eastern control assembly*.

She rejected it.

The proposed work would not simply repair the existing assembly.

It would correct an installation arrangement that had never received a documented technical acceptance.

She suggested:

*Corrective modification to the eastern control assembly and adjacent distribution conduit, following identification of an undocumented installation variation.*

The Treasury accepted the description.

The Department submitted the revised expenditure request.

The funds were approved.

The work began the following week.

The eastern conduit was temporarily supported.

A section of the outer chamber wall was opened.

The engineers adjusted the conduit route to restore the required clearance.

The original support geometry was reinstated with a new bracket manufactured to the approved specification.

The replacement internal guide was installed.

Its alignment was measured.

The control housing was inspected.

The work passed its structural and technical checks.

The Academy then conducted a series of controlled operating tests.

The first test measured ordinary response.

The second measured elevated demand.

The third measured the eastern circuit's response while the reserve zone maintained its ordinary balancing contribution.

The results were consistent.

The response delay had decreased.

The internal guide operated within the original design tolerance.

The reserve zone continued to compensate as expected.

The eastern circuit was returned to service.

The six other zones remained stable throughout the restoration.

The capital's protective network continued operating.

No interruption was reported.

The final assessment was issued on a Friday.

Lucien brought it to the review room himself.

Master Fenwick read the technical conclusion.

"The eastern circuit is operating within the approved range. The corrected support arrangement has passed the required tests. The replacement guide is aligned within tolerance."

Theodore looked at the final page.

"And the original installation?"

"Recorded as completed, but the site adjustment lacked formal technical acceptance."

"Will the record be corrected?"

"Yes. The installation history will retain the original documents, the site adjustment request, the acknowledgment of receipt, the completion certificate and the current corrective assessment."

"Good."

Master Harlan looked tired.

"The maintenance team will also receive a revised inspection form."

Lucien nodded.

"It will require actual alignment measurements rather than a general visual category."

"That will take longer."

"Yes."

"Considerably longer."

"Only during internal inspection."

Harlan sighed.

"I suppose we shall survive."

Master Fenwick closed the report.

"That is the purpose of the maintenance procedure."

The corrected register was distributed to the Council the following week.

The eastern circuit's status had changed from *under investigation* to *corrective work completed; operating assessment passed*.

The installation history remained open as a documentary matter.

The Department would continue searching for the original design office's technical response.

But the physical issue had been addressed.

The reserve zone's role was also clarified.

It was not compensating for a failing eastern circuit.

It was performing its intended balancing function while the eastern assembly responded within its approved range.

Lucien explained this distinction during the Council briefing.

The King listened without interruption.

When the report ended, he asked one question.

"How many other installations have site adjustments that were acknowledged but not formally accepted?"

The room became quiet.

Master Fenwick answered carefully.

"We do not know."

"Can we find out?"

"Yes. The Directorate can review projects where installation drawings differ from approved designs."

"How long would that take?"

"Several months for the capital network alone."

The King looked toward Theodore.

"Then begin with the capital network."

Theodore nodded.

The King turned back to Fenwick.

"Do not turn this into an inspection of every bridge and water channel in the kingdom. We are reviewing the protective infrastructure."

"Understood, Your Majesty."

The King looked at Lucien.

"Are there any immediate safety concerns?"

"None identified in the current assessment."

"Then continue the review without interrupting functioning systems unnecessarily."

The meeting ended.

Seraphina learned about the Council's decision from a letter Theodore sent to the Veyr estate.

The letter was short.

The capital network remained stable.

The eastern circuit had returned to service.

The review of installation variations would continue.

The Department had also agreed to revise its technical acceptance procedures.

At the bottom, Theodore had added a separate paragraph.

*Your distinction between an installation being completed and a variation being technically accepted was useful. The revised register will preserve that distinction.*

Seraphina read the sentence.

Then she placed the letter beside the estate's drainage invoices.

Mira glanced at it.

"Is the capital problem finished?"

"The eastern circuit problem is."

"That sounds different."

"It is."

"Will there be another one?"

Seraphina looked at the letter.

"Almost certainly."

Mira sighed.

"Then I hope it is somewhere far away."

Seraphina returned to the invoices.

"So do I."

The following morning, Cedric arrived with a report from the northern estate.

The old storage building mentioned in the earlier correspondence had been inspected from the outside.

The building was structurally sound, although the western roof required repair.

Its ownership was confirmed.

The inspection had not found an underground chamber.

However, the surveyor had identified an old stone drainage passage beneath the northern wall.

It was not shown on the current estate map.

The passage appeared on a forty-seven-year-old agricultural survey.

Cedric placed the survey beside the report.

"The surveyor thinks it may have been built to drain the old storage yard."

"Does it connect to the river?"

"Possibly. The outlet is blocked."

"Has anyone entered it?"

"No. The surveyor recommended against entering until the passage is cleared and assessed."

"Good."

Cedric looked at her.

"Would you like the Academy to inspect it?"

"No."

He blinked.

"No?"

"Why would the Academy inspect an old drainage passage?"

"Because the map has a reference to a stone structure beneath the building."

"That does not make it magical."

Cedric looked at the survey again.

"It might not be."

"It might also be an ordinary drain. Have the estate engineer assess it first. If there is anything unusual beyond the drainage structure, we can consider the appropriate specialist."

Cedric nodded.

He was about to leave when Seraphina noticed a second page attached to the report.

It contained an old maintenance note.

The passage had been cleared once, thirty-one years earlier.

The work order described a blocked eastern outlet and a damaged stone cover.

The repair had been completed.

The final inspection box was blank.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Do we have the original work order?"

"Only this copy."

"Then ask the archive to locate the original."

Cedric nodded.

Seraphina returned to her desk.

She had no intention of turning a drainage passage into another royal investigation.

But she had learned something from the capital network.

A completed repair and an inspected repair were not necessarily the same thing.

And sometimes the most useful question was not what a structure had been built to do.

It was whether anyone had ever confirmed that it still did it.
