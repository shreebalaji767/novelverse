# **Chapter 82 — The Pipe That Had Been Removed on Paper**

The first draft of the capital infrastructure register arrived in a leather folder.

It contained 146 entries.

Seraphina counted them because the cover page claimed there were 145.

She counted again.

There were still 146.

She placed a small mark beside the total and continued.

The register was divided into six sections:

1. Original construction records.
2. Modernization and replacement work.
3. Physical inspections.
4. Equipment and component inventories.
5. Disconnection and decommissioning records.
6. Outstanding technical questions.

It was considerably better organized than the old documents.

Every entry had a source reference.

Unconfirmed information was marked separately.

Missing documents were identified without being described as evidence of unfinished work.

Seraphina approved of the general structure.

She did not approve of the number on the cover.

Mira brought tea.

"Is the document satisfactory?"

"Mostly."

"What is wrong with it?"

"It has 146 entries and says 145."

Mira looked at the cover.

"That seems like a small mistake."

"It is."

"Will you correct it?"

"Yes."

Mira placed the tea beside her.

"Then perhaps it is a good document."

Seraphina considered that.

"Perhaps."

She continued reading.

The first section contained the original construction plan for the capital's central protective network.

The second contained the modernization records.

The third listed the current inspection findings.

The fourth identified equipment that had been replaced or transferred.

The fifth contained the disconnection records.

The sixth listed unresolved matters.

One entry concerned the old C-2 conduit.

Its status was recorded as:

**Removal authorized — physical removal unconfirmed.**

Seraphina read the supporting note.

The second modernization plan had authorized removal of the conduit.

The work order had been issued.

The contractor's completion report was missing.

The final inspection certificate was also missing.

The later maintenance register described C-2 as removed.

But that entry cited the work order, not the completion report.

Seraphina put down her pen.

There was the problem.

The document did not prove that the pipe had been removed.

It proved that someone had authorized its removal.

She wrote a note in the margin.

*Distinguish authorized work from completed work. The later maintenance entry cites the work order, not a completion certificate.*

Then she continued.

The next entry concerned the C-1 conduit.

Its modern isolation plate had been inspected.

The plate was intact.

The conduit was classified as disconnected, pending a final internal survey.

The two entries were not interchangeable.

One had a modern isolation plate.

The other had an old closure.

Seraphina marked the difference.

She was beginning to dislike the letter C.

The register's remaining sections were mostly accurate.

There were several missing references.

One equipment transfer had no receiving signature.

Two inspection reports were available only as summaries.

A replacement regulator had been recorded as delivered, but its installation certificate had not been located.

None of these discrepancies established a technical failure.

They established that the record was incomplete.

Seraphina wrote a short list of corrections.

She did not attempt to determine the physical status of the conduits.

She did not recommend opening the chamber.

She did not suggest replacing equipment.

She corrected the administrative language and returned the folder to Lucien.

Her covering note contained three paragraphs.

The first listed the corrections.

The second explained that the register should distinguish between authorization, completion, inspection, and acceptance.

The third reminded the Department that her review concerned the register's structure, not the technical accuracy of its findings.

She signed it.

Then she went to lunch.

Lucien replied the next day.

He accepted the corrections.

The Directorate had also agreed that the C-2 entry should remain unresolved until the physical inspection was completed.

However, the inspection team had encountered a problem.

The original contractor's records were incomplete.

The second modernization had been carried out by a company called **Crownline Engineering and Works**.

The company had ceased trading twenty-four years earlier.

Its remaining technical records had been transferred to a private archive.

The archive's catalogue identified several boxes from the project.

One was labelled:

**Capital Protective Network — Inner Distribution Modernization.**

Another was labelled:

**Crownline Engineering — General Accounts and Contracts.**

A third contained equipment drawings.

The Department had requested access.

The archive had agreed.

Lucien's letter ended with a sentence that made Seraphina pause.

*The company retained a separate completion register, which may establish whether C-2 was removed.*

She read it again.

A completion register.

That was precisely what they needed.

She sent a brief reply.

*Please obtain the original register, if available. Do not rely on a later summary.*

The archive was located in the old commercial district.

It belonged to a private records company that specialized in preserving the papers of dissolved businesses.

The company was managed by a retired solicitor named Percival Dunn.

Seraphina knew the name from the earlier Veylan Works investigation.

She wondered whether every old company in the kingdom had eventually entrusted its records to the same three solicitors.

Lucien visited the archive with Master Fenwick and a representative from the Department.

The first box contained general contracts.

The second contained equipment drawings.

The third contained the completion register.

It was not a single book.

It was a collection of bound sheets and signed work reports.

The first section listed the work authorized during the second modernization.

It included the replacement of several control channels, the installation of modern isolation plates, and the removal of obsolete conduits.

C-2 appeared on the list.

The instruction was clear.

**Remove obsolete inner conduit C-2 from the central chamber to the eastern junction. Retain outer masonry sleeve if structurally sound.**

The work had been assigned to a subcontractor.

The subcontractor's name was **Merrick and Sons**.

The completion register contained an entry dated twelve years after the original modernization plan.

It stated:

*Conduit C-2 removed from central chamber to eastern junction. Remaining sleeve inspected and retained for structural support.*

The entry was signed by the subcontractor's site supervisor.

Beneath it was a second signature.

The Department's supervising engineer had initialed the work.

Lucien examined the page.

"This is promising."

Master Fenwick nodded.

"It appears to establish removal."

"Does it establish final inspection?"

"Not yet."

The register contained a separate inspection section.

The relevant page was missing.

The page before it described the inspection of C-1.

The page after it concerned the replacement regulator.

The C-2 inspection page had been removed.

There was no note explaining why.

Lucien examined the binding.

The page had been detached cleanly.

It might have been separated for copying.

It might have been transferred to another file.

It might have been removed accidentally during later preservation.

There was no evidence of deliberate destruction.

The archive's handling records showed that the register had been rebound after the company closed.

The missing page had not been present when the archive received it.

Lucien sent the discovery to the Department.

The Department replied that it would search the original inspection files.

The completion register had answered one question.

The contractor reported that C-2 had been removed.

But the final inspection remained unconfirmed.

Three days later, the Department found the inspection report.

It had been filed under the contractor's project number rather than the conduit designation.

The report described the removal of C-2.

The physical inspection had confirmed that the old conduit was no longer present between the central chamber and the eastern junction.

The remaining masonry sleeve had been sealed.

The closure was inspected.

The work was accepted.

The report was signed by the supervising engineer.

There was one qualification.

The engineer had recommended a separate survey of the eastern junction to confirm that the old branch had been isolated at its far end.

That survey had been assigned to the regional maintenance team.

The completion report for that survey was not attached.

Lucien sent the document to Seraphina.

She read it carefully.

The central conduit had been removed.

The sleeve had been sealed.

The chamber side had been inspected.

The eastern junction still required confirmation.

The status was not simply "removed."

It was more precise:

**Central section removed and sealed. Eastern junction isolation not independently confirmed.**

Seraphina sent the correction to Lucien.

He replied that the register would be updated.

The eastern junction was located beneath a service corridor near the old administrative district.

The site was not part of the royal palace.

It was maintained by the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The junction had been covered during the construction of a newer service passage.

The maintenance team had a plan showing its approximate position.

The plan was thirty-two years old.

A newer map showed the service corridor but omitted the old junction.

The Department arranged a surface survey.

No excavation was authorized.

The team used a technical locator to identify the buried structure.

It found a stone access chamber beneath the paving.

The chamber's upper cover was intact.

The maintenance team opened it under supervision.

Inside, the old masonry sleeve ended at a sealed wall.

The seal was intact.

There was no visible conduit beyond it.

A technical examination confirmed that the eastern branch had been closed.

The old conduit was not connected to the modern protective network.

The inspection did not require opening the sealed masonry.

The Department updated its records.

C-2 was now classified as removed from the central chamber, with the eastern branch sealed and confirmed inactive.

The old chamber had two known historical conduits.

One had a modern isolation plate.

The other had been removed and sealed.

Neither was active.

The uncertainty had been resolved.

Seraphina received the final report on a Friday.

She read it at breakfast.

Mira was arranging the dishes.

"Is something wrong?"

"No."

"Then why have you read that page three times?"

"Because the report says the conduit was removed."

"Wasn't that what they wanted?"

"Yes."

Mira looked puzzled.

"Then why are you checking it?"

"Because the first report said it was removed on paper."

"And now?"

"Now they have inspected the remaining connection."

Mira nodded.

"That sounds better."

"It is."

Seraphina folded the report.

The capital's old chamber was still sealed.

The two conduits had been accounted for.

The modern protective network remained stable.

The historical register had been corrected.

The missing inspection page had been found in another file.

The work had been completed.

It was a satisfying result.

She almost enjoyed it.

Then Lucien arrived.

He had brought another document.

Seraphina looked at it.

"Is this related?"

"Yes."

"Is it another conduit?"

"No."

"Good."

He placed the document on the table.

It was an old equipment inventory from the second modernization.

The report identified several control components removed during the project.

One item had been classified as a **primary balancing assembly**.

Its removal had been authorized.

Its completion record stated that it had been transferred to the technical stores.

But the receiving register showed only the outer casing.

The internal control ring was not listed.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Was it removed?"

"The contractor's completion report says the assembly was removed."

"Was it received?"

"The stores register records the casing."

"And the internal ring?"

"Not separately recorded."

She looked at the report again.

"Could it have been attached to the casing?"

"Possibly."

"Could it have been retained in the chamber?"

"Possibly."

"Could it have been transferred with another assembly?"

"Also possible."

Seraphina closed the document.

"I thought we had finished the old chamber."

"We have resolved the conduit question."

"And now?"

"The Academy wants to identify the original control assembly."

"Why?"

"Because the modernization report states that the assembly was removed and replaced. The new regulator's installation record refers to the original control ring as a retained reference component."

Seraphina stared at him.

"Retained where?"

"That is what we are trying to establish."

She looked at the ceiling.

The kingdom had spent eighty years building a magical infrastructure system.

It had spent thirty years replacing it.

And now, apparently, it had spent another thirty years forgetting where some of the old parts had gone.

"Is the current regulator functioning?"

"Yes."

"Is the capital protected?"

"Yes."

"Is the old chamber stable?"

"Yes."

"Then find the inventory."

Lucien smiled.

"We intend to."

The following week, the Academy located a technical reference in the original modernization manual.

The primary balancing assembly had been removed from the chamber.

Its outer casing had been transferred to technical stores.

The internal control ring had been retained for comparative testing.

The reference was signed by the project's lead engineer.

It also contained a storage location.

**Technical Reference Room — Central Academy Annex.**

Lucien visited the annex.

The room was still in use.

It contained old measuring instruments, disassembled regulators, damaged catalysts, and several cabinets of historical components.

The Academy's inventory identified the balancing assembly's outer casing.

It did not identify the internal ring.

The room's older paper register did.

The ring had been placed in a wooden case with other control components.

The case was marked:

**Capital Network — Modernization Reference Materials.**

It had remained in the room for thirty years.

Lucien found it.

The ring was intact.

It had been kept as a technical reference.

It had not been installed in any system.

It had not been used to regulate mana.

Its condition was stable.

The Academy's technical team inspected it.

The component was suitable for historical comparison but not for installation.

The inventory was corrected.

The missing item had never been missing.

It had been recorded under a general storage category.

The report was updated.

By the end of the month, the capital review had established several important facts.

The old control chamber had remained sealed.

The two historical conduits were inactive.

The C-2 conduit had been removed and its remaining branch sealed.

The original balancing assembly had been removed.

Its outer casing was in technical stores.

Its internal ring had been retained by the Academy as a reference component.

The modern protective network was operating independently.

The missing commissioning record for the second modernization remained unresolved.

That final issue was more complicated.

The surviving records showed that the replacement regulator had been installed.

The technical inspection report confirmed that it had passed its initial tests.

The financial records showed that the contractor had received payment for the installation stage.

But the final commissioning certificate had not been found.

The Academy believed the system had been accepted for use.

The Department's maintenance records showed that it had been operating for thirty years.

The Treasury had no outstanding payment.

The missing certificate did not prove that commissioning had never occurred.

It meant that the formal evidence was incomplete.

The Directorate recommended a controlled review of the modern system's present condition.

No changes would be made until that review was complete.

Seraphina read the final summary.

It was a sensible conclusion.

The capital was not in immediate danger.

The old network was not secretly connected to the modern system.

The missing equipment had been accounted for.

But the final commissioning record still had not been located.

She placed the report in its folder.

Then she wrote one sentence on the cover.

**Historical questions resolved; modern commissioning status pending verification.**

It was not dramatic.

It was accurate.

That evening, Theodore sent a personal note.

He thanked Seraphina for reviewing the register and said the corrected structure had helped the departments distinguish between authorization, completion, inspection, and acceptance.

He also informed her that the Council would receive a preliminary report the following week.

The note ended with a request.

The King wanted her to attend the presentation.

Seraphina stared at it.

She had already reviewed the register.

The technical work was complete enough for the next stage.

The departments had their responsibilities.

There was no need for her to attend.

She wrote back politely.

She would not attend the presentation.

Theodore replied the next day.

He accepted her decision.

He also sent the presentation summary.

Seraphina read it.

The Council had approved the controlled review of the modern protective network.

The Academy would conduct the technical tests.

The Directorate would oversee standards compliance.

The Department would coordinate access and maintenance.

The Treasury would review any required expenditure.

The royal property office would preserve the chamber's access restrictions until the review was complete.

Everything was proceeding properly.

Seraphina was pleased.

She put the report away.

Then she noticed a final attachment.

It was a list of the modern network's operating zones.

There were seven.

The first six were familiar.

The seventh was marked:

**Central Protective Reserve — Transitional Operation.**

Seraphina frowned.

The description stated that the reserve zone had been retained during the second modernization to provide continuity while the inner distribution network was replaced.

It was still listed as part of the current capital system.

Its maintenance responsibility belonged to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Its technical supervision belonged to the Academy.

Its original commissioning record was not attached.

Seraphina read the note beneath it.

*Operational continuity confirmed by historical maintenance entries. Independent performance assessment pending.*

She looked at the date of the most recent entry.

Two years earlier.

She put the paper down.

The reserve zone was not the old chamber.

It was a modern part of the capital's protective system.

It had been maintained.

It had been operating.

But its independent performance had not been formally assessed.

Seraphina sat quietly for a moment.

Then she picked up the letter and wrote a note to Lucien.

*Please confirm whether the Central Protective Reserve is included in the current technical review. Its status appears to be based on maintenance records rather than an independent performance assessment.*

Lucien's reply arrived the following morning.

*It is included. The Academy has scheduled a controlled test.*

Seraphina read the message.

Then she put it aside.

That was the correct answer.

The test would be conducted by the people responsible for it.

The capital's protective system continued to operate.

The old chamber remained sealed.

The winter roads were open.

And Seraphina had no further reason to examine the capital's magical infrastructure.

At least, that was what she hoped.

Because the controlled test was scheduled for the following week.

And for the first time, the Academy would be examining not the old system that had been retired, but the modern reserve zone that had been quietly operating in its place.
