# Chapter 20 — The Dinner Where Nobody Discussed Dinner

Seraphina had prepared herself for a discussion about winter transport.

She had not prepared herself for six military commanders, three regional administrators, two estate officials, one enormous map, and a disagreement about whether a bridge could be considered passable if a horse could cross it.

The dinner was held in the western dining room of Veyr Estate.

Unlike the grand reception hall, the western room was designed for practical gatherings. Its walls were decorated with maps rather than portraits, its chairs were sturdy rather than ornamental, and the table was wide enough to accommodate several rolled documents without forcing everyone to move their plates.

Seraphina approved of it immediately.

She also approved of the food.

There was roasted lamb, vegetable soup, fresh bread, potatoes, and a dish of baked apples.

For once, the arrangements seemed sensible.

Then General Cassian placed a map beside her plate.

Seraphina looked at it.

"Is that the northern road network?"

"Yes, my lady."

"Should I move my soup?"

"That would be helpful."

She moved it.

General Cassian, a broad-shouldered man with greying hair and a face that seemed permanently unimpressed by weather, unfolded the map.

"His Grace thought you should see the transport concerns before winter."

Duke Alaric sat at the head of the table.

"I thought it would be useful for you to understand how the estate's agricultural and military routes overlap."

Seraphina looked at the map.

Several roads had been marked in red.

Others were blue.

A number of small black symbols appeared beside bridges, storage buildings, and mountain passes.

She leaned forward.

"Which marks are current?"

"Red indicates routes requiring attention," Cassian explained. "Blue indicates routes currently suitable for military transport."

"And black?"

"Sites requiring inspection."

Seraphina studied the map.

There were more black marks than she expected.

"How old is this map?"

Cassian glanced at the duke.

"Three years."

Seraphina looked at her father.

Alaric's expression remained neutral.

"Some sections have been updated."

"Which sections?"

"The eastern road and the southern pass."

"On the map?"

"No."

Seraphina slowly sat back.

She looked at the commanders.

Then at the administrators.

Then at the map again.

"I see."

General Cassian folded his arms.

"Is there a problem?"

"Not yet. I am simply trying to determine whether this map describes the roads or the intentions of the people who last examined them."

One of the commanders coughed into his hand.

Alaric gave his daughter a warning glance.

Seraphina picked up her spoon.

She had promised to listen.

She had not promised to pretend that an outdated map was useful.

The first discussion concerned the northern mountain pass.

The military wanted the road cleared before the first heavy snowfall.

The agricultural administration wanted the same road kept open for grain transport.

The local landowners wanted compensation for damage to the road bordering their properties.

The transport office wanted a larger maintenance budget.

The treasury wanted estimates.

Nobody wanted to pay for the work before receiving those estimates.

Seraphina listened.

She learned that the road had been repaired twice in the last five years.

The first repair had failed after a severe winter.

The second had been delayed because the contractor could not obtain enough stone.

The military had subsequently paid for temporary repairs, but the work had not been entered into the regional maintenance register.

"Why not?" she asked.

The transport administrator, a thin man named Lord Hadrien, adjusted his collar.

"The military classified the work as an emergency measure."

"Was it paid from the military budget?"

"Partly."

"Then why is it absent from the maintenance register?"

"Because the work was not commissioned through the transport office."

Seraphina nodded.

"Was the condition of the road inspected afterward?"

"By the military."

"Was the report sent to the transport office?"

Lord Hadrien hesitated.

"I believe so."

General Cassian leaned forward.

"It was sent."

"Then the report should be available."

"It should."

Seraphina looked at the map.

"How much of the road remains damaged?"

"Approximately two miles."

"Approximately?"

Cassian pointed to the section.

"The worst damage is here, where the hillside slopes toward the road."

"Has the slope been examined?"

"Not recently."

"Then how do you know the damage is limited to two miles?"

The general looked at her.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then he gave a short laugh.

"That is a fair question."

Seraphina looked relieved.

She had not wanted to challenge a military commander over road measurements.

She had merely wanted to know whether the measurement meant anything.

Alaric took a sip of wine.

"Arrange an inspection."

Lord Hadrien nodded.

"Of course, Your Grace."

"And obtain the military report," Cassian added.

Seraphina picked up her spoon again.

One problem had acquired two responsible offices.

That was better than having none.

The second discussion concerned grain transport.

This one was less straightforward.

The agricultural administrator, Harold Fenwick, explained that several villages had requested permission to use military wagons during the winter months.

The military objected because its wagons were required for supply movements.

The merchants objected because the arrangement would undercut their transport contracts.

The villages objected because the merchants charged more during periods of poor road access.

Everyone appeared to have a valid complaint.

Seraphina listened until the discussion reached a familiar point.

"The merchants have been asked to provide fixed winter rates," Fenwick said.

"And they refused?" Seraphina asked.

"Some did."

"Why?"

"Because the roads become unpredictable. They cannot guarantee delivery dates."

"Did they refuse to provide prices, or did they refuse to guarantee dates?"

Fenwick paused.

"I believe both."

"Those are different things."

General Cassian looked toward her.

Seraphina continued.

"If a merchant cannot promise that a wagon will arrive on a particular day, that does not mean the merchant cannot provide a price for transporting a certain quantity over a particular route."

"Unless the route becomes impassable," Lord Hadrien said.

"Then the contract should state what happens when it becomes impassable."

The administrator frowned.

"That would complicate the agreement."

Seraphina looked at him.

"It is already complicated. The road does not become passable because the contract refuses to mention snow."

A commander at the far end of the table laughed.

Fenwick looked mildly offended.

Seraphina continued before the discussion could become personal.

"Could the merchants provide separate rates for normal conditions and difficult conditions? We could also identify alternative storage points before winter rather than paying for emergency transport after the roads close."

Cassian nodded slowly.

"That would help us."

"Would it?"

"Yes. We could use smaller supply points along the main route. The military would not need to move every shipment through the same road."

Seraphina glanced at the map.

"Are those storage points already available?"

"Some are."

"How many?"

Cassian looked at Fenwick.

Fenwick looked at Lord Hadrien.

Lord Hadrien looked at the map.

Seraphina sighed.

"Perhaps we should begin by identifying which ones actually exist."

Alaric's expression changed slightly.

He was not smiling.

But he looked amused.

By the time the main course had been cleared, the map had acquired several new annotations.

Three storage buildings required inspection.

Two roads needed updated surveys.

One bridge had been identified as unsuitable for heavy wagons.

The mountain pass required a separate engineering assessment.

The military and agricultural offices had agreed to share their transport reports.

Seraphina had not approved a single major expenditure.

This pleased her.

It also made her suspicious that someone might ask her to do so later.

General Cassian moved a small marker on the map.

"There is another concern."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Of course there is."

The general ignored her.

"During the last winter, a supply wagon was delayed near Greyford. The driver reported that the road was blocked by fallen timber."

"Was it cleared?"

"Eventually."

"How long was the delay?"

"Four days."

"Was the cargo damaged?"

"No. But the delay affected the next two deliveries."

Seraphina looked at the route.

"Was there an alternative road?"

"Yes, but it adds nearly a day's travel."

"Why wasn't it used?"

"The alternative road passes through private land."

"Was permission requested?"

"It was not considered necessary."

Seraphina blinked.

"Why?"

Cassian pointed to the map.

"The road is listed as a public access route."

"Then why is it private land?"

"Because the access agreement permits passage under certain conditions."

"What conditions?"

Cassian paused.

"I do not have the document."

Seraphina turned toward her father.

Alaric looked at Lord Hadrien.

Lord Hadrien looked at Fenwick.

Fenwick looked at the map.

It was becoming a familiar sequence.

"Find the agreement," Alaric said.

The administrator nodded.

Seraphina studied the route again.

"Could we speak to the landowner before winter?"

"Certainly," Lord Hadrien replied.

"Not merely to request permission. We should understand what the agreement actually allows. If the route is already available for public use, there may be no reason to negotiate a new arrangement."

Cassian nodded.

"That would save time."

"And possibly money."

"That too."

Seraphina took a piece of bread.

She was beginning to understand why her father had invited her.

The estate's military and agricultural systems had been developed separately, but they relied on many of the same roads, bridges, storage sites, and transport workers.

A problem in one department could easily become a problem in another.

The map was not merely outdated.

It was incomplete because each office maintained a different version of reality.

She did not particularly want to solve this.

But she could see why it mattered.

After dinner, Alaric asked Seraphina to remain.

The commanders and administrators departed with their documents, leaving the duke, his daughter, and General Cassian in the western room.

Cassian rolled up the map.

"Your daughter asks inconvenient questions."

Seraphina looked at him.

"I thought they were ordinary questions."

"They are ordinary. That is why they are inconvenient."

Alaric dismissed the general with a nod.

When the door closed, he turned to Seraphina.

"You handled the discussion well."

"Thank you."

"You did not attempt to dictate the answers."

"I did not know the answers."

"That has not always stopped people."

Seraphina considered this.

"That seems like a poor habit."

"It is."

Alaric walked to the window.

"Your mother has noticed that you are becoming more careful with your decisions."

Seraphina looked down at the map.

"Is that a problem?"

"No."

He paused.

"But I wonder whether you are being careful because you understand your responsibilities or because you are afraid of making mistakes."

The question caught her off guard.

She looked up.

Alaric was still facing the window.

"I do not expect you to know everything," he continued. "You are seventeen. You are learning how this estate works. You will make mistakes."

Seraphina folded her hands.

"I know."

"Do you?"

She did not answer immediately.

She had spent weeks trying to avoid the future she remembered.

The villainess in the novel had made reckless decisions because she believed her status protected her. She had treated other people as pieces on a board. She had ignored warnings and mistaken obedience for loyalty.

Seraphina wanted to be different.

But sometimes, she was so afraid of becoming that person that she hesitated to act at all.

"I do not want to hurt people because I misunderstood something," she said eventually.

Alaric turned.

"That is a reasonable concern."

"I also do not want to become responsible for everything simply because I noticed it first."

A faint smile crossed his face.

"That is also reasonable."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then why do people keep bringing me problems?"

"Because you have begun asking questions."

"Perhaps I should stop."

"Perhaps you should learn which questions are yours to ask."

She was quiet.

Alaric approached the table and placed one hand on the map.

"You are the heir to House Veyr. You cannot avoid every matter that affects the duchy. But you are not required to personally manage every road, warehouse, village, and account."

"That is reassuring."

"You may also discover that some problems cannot be solved quickly."

"I have noticed."

"And some people will dislike the changes you propose."

"I have noticed that too."

"Then you are learning."

Seraphina looked at the map.

The roads seemed less like lines on paper and more like a collection of obligations.

She could not repair every road.

She could not guarantee every delivery.

She could not make every office communicate properly.

But she could help ensure that the people responsible knew what was expected of them.

Perhaps that was enough.

For now.

The following morning, Seraphina received a letter from the Northern Agricultural Administration.

It concerned the five destinations listed in the partial distribution ledger.

The office had located an old village register from Westmere.

The register confirmed that forty measures of grain had arrived during the winter emergency.

The entry was signed by the village elder at the time.

It also included a note explaining that part of the delivery had been distributed to households whose stores had been damaged by flooding.

The quantity was not broken down by household.

But the total matched the working ledger.

Seraphina read the entry carefully.

"That confirms Westmere."

Roland nodded.

"It does."

"What about Northbridge and Greyford?"

"Still under examination."

"And Millhaven?"

"The office has sent a request to its current local administrator."

Seraphina looked at the letter again.

The Westmere entry included another note.

**Additional grain to be held for families unable to travel to the distribution point.**

That suggested the original distribution process had included provisions for people who could not collect their own supplies.

It also meant that the village might have maintained separate household lists.

"Ask whether the old household register survived," she said.

Roland made a note.

"And the 105 measures?"

"Still unresolved."

He nodded.

Seraphina looked at him.

"You do not sound disappointed."

"I am not. One confirmed delivery is more useful than five guesses."

She smiled slightly.

"That is a very accountant-like answer."

"I shall take it as a compliment."

"It was intended as one."

Roland returned to the documents.

There was a knock at the door.

Mira entered carrying a letter.

"My lady, this arrived from the Royal Academy."

Seraphina took it.

The letter bore Lucien Arkwright's name.

She opened it.

*Lady Seraphina,*

*Our inspection team will arrive at the western conduit access point as scheduled. However, after reviewing the local survey, we believe the second access point near the old mill road may be more useful for the initial examination.*

*The conduit appears to pass beneath the irrigation channel. We will not conduct repairs or discharge mana during the inspection.*

*I would also like to clarify that the Academy's purpose is to determine the condition of the conduit, not to request immediate intervention from House Veyr.*

*Respectfully,*

*Lucien Arkwright*

Seraphina read the final paragraph again.

Then she looked at Mira.

"Has Master Arkwright written something unusual?"

Mira considered the letter.

"He seems very concerned that you will refuse to let him inspect the conduit."

"That is because he remembers the reception."

"Perhaps."

Seraphina folded the letter.

"At least he understands the conditions."

She turned toward Roland.

"Please send confirmation that the inspection may proceed through the old mill road, provided the maintenance supervisor is present and the Academy marks the area before anyone begins work."

"Yes, my lady."

"And tell them the irrigation repairs retain priority."

"Understood."

Mira took the letter away to be copied.

Seraphina returned to the grain records.

The old emergency investigation had finally produced one confirmed delivery.

One village.

Forty measures.

A signature.

A surviving register.

It was not enough to close the account.

But it was enough to establish that at least part of the grain had reached the people it was meant to help.

She wrote the confirmation into the investigation ledger.

Then she turned to the next entry.

Northbridge.

Sixty measures.

No confirmation yet.

The search continued.

Two days later, the Academy inspection team arrived at the western boundary.

Seraphina did not accompany them.

This was deliberate.

Cedric had arranged for the local maintenance supervisor to meet the engineers. The surveyor had marked the irrigation channel, the old mill road, and the two known conduit access points.

Seraphina received a short report that evening.

The conduit was intact along the inspected section.

Its mana flow was irregular but stable.

No immediate danger had been identified.

The engineers recommended a more detailed examination of the junction beneath the old mill road.

They had also discovered that an old maintenance marker was missing.

That was all.

No explosion.

No magical catastrophe.

No ancient seal awakening beneath the earth.

Seraphina read the report twice.

Then she placed it beside the grain ledger.

Two separate investigations.

One concerned old grain supplies and missing allocation records.

The other concerned an ageing magical conduit that required proper maintenance.

They were both important.

They were also being handled by people with the relevant responsibilities.

She was not required to personally stand beside every road, warehouse, or underground conduit.

For the first time in several days, she felt reasonably satisfied with the arrangement.

Then Mira entered.

"My lady, the Duchess has requested your presence."

Seraphina looked up.

"Is something wrong?"

"I do not believe so."

"That is not a very reassuring answer."

"Her Grace said she would like to discuss your next visit to the capital."

Seraphina stared at her.

The capital.

She had been hoping the matter would remain distant.

"Why?"

Mira handed her a second letter.

It was an invitation from the Royal Academy.

The Academy was holding a formal review of regional infrastructure and magical maintenance records. House Veyr had been asked to send a representative familiar with the western conduit inspection.

Seraphina read the invitation.

Then she read it again.

The Academy had not requested that she fund the work.

It had not asked her to supervise the engineers.

It had simply requested that House Veyr provide a representative to discuss the findings.

That was reasonable.

Unfortunately, the review would be held in Aurelion.

The royal capital.

The place where the crown prince lived.

The place where the original novel's political plot had begun to accelerate.

Seraphina slowly lowered the letter.

"How many days?"

Mira glanced at the invitation.

"The review is scheduled for the second week of next month."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

She had attended one reception.

She had spoken to the crown prince once.

She had met the saintess and the royal magician.

And now the Academy wanted her in the capital again.

Perhaps the world was not actively trying to make her a villainess.

Perhaps it was simply very committed to inconvenient scheduling.

She opened her eyes.

"Tell my mother I will come."

Mira smiled.

"Of course, my lady."

"On one condition."

"What condition?"

"I am not attending another reception."

Mira considered the invitation.

"It does not mention a reception."

"Good."

Seraphina returned to her desk.

She added a new line to her notebook.

**Capital visit: Academy review. Attend the meeting. Avoid unnecessary gatherings.**

Then, after a moment, she added:

**Do not assume that avoiding a reception means avoiding politics.**

She looked at the sentence.

It was unpleasantly sensible.

She disliked it.

But she left it there.

The next month would bring her to Aurelion.

And although she still had no intention of becoming involved in royal affairs, she was beginning to understand that the kingdom's problems did not remain neatly inside the offices responsible for them.

Sometimes they travelled.

Sometimes they crossed regions.

And sometimes, apparently, they sent invitations.

**End of Chapter 20**
