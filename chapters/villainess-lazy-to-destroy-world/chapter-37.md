# Chapter 37 — The Clerk Who Had Kept the Wrong Copy

Henry Cole was not difficult to find.

That was the first surprise.

The second was that he had been living in the same town for eleven years, within walking distance of the Royal Archives.

The third was that he had already written to the Treasury twice about an old box of documents.

Neither letter had received a reply.

Seraphina read the correspondence in disbelief.

"Two letters?"

Roland nodded.

"One was sent six years ago. The other was sent four years later."

"And both concerned the Central Project Review Office?"

"Yes."

"What did he ask for?"

"Confirmation that a collection of personal working papers could be returned to him."

Seraphina looked at the file.

"Personal working papers?"

"That is how he described them."

"Did he identify the contents?"

"Only generally. He mentioned copies of correspondence, indexes and transfer schedules."

Seraphina placed the letters on the desk.

"Why did the Treasury not respond?"

Roland checked the correspondence register.

"The first letter was forwarded to the Royal Infrastructure Department. The second was recorded as received but has no response entry."

"Was the first letter ever delivered?"

"That is unclear."

Seraphina leaned back.

A retired clerk had spent years trying to recover papers from an office that no longer existed.

The papers might be relevant to W-2.

They might also be ordinary personal copies with no connection to the missing file.

But the fact that Henry Cole had retained copies of the old transfer schedules was worth investigating.

"Can we arrange a meeting?"

"I have already requested one."

"Good."

Roland hesitated.

"There is something else."

Seraphina looked up.

"The Treasury has located the box."

She stared at him.

"Where?"

"In its secondary archive."

"And?"

Roland paused.

"The box has been marked as personal correspondence. It has not been opened."

Seraphina closed her eyes briefly.

"Of course it hasn't."

Henry Cole arrived at the estate two days later.

He was a thin, elderly man with carefully combed white hair and a walking stick.

His clothes were neat, though old-fashioned.

He carried a small leather folder under one arm.

Seraphina received him in the library.

"Mr Cole. Thank you for coming."

He bowed.

"Lady Seraphina."

They sat opposite each other.

Roland remained in the room as a witness and note-taker.

Seraphina began with the simplest question.

"Did you work for the Central Project Review Office?"

"Yes. I was its records administrator."

"For how long?"

"From its establishment until its dissolution."

"Did you handle the W-2 file?"

Cole looked at her.

"Yes."

The answer was immediate.

Seraphina did not move.

"Do you remember what happened to it?"

"I remember transferring it."

"To the Treasury?"

"Not directly."

"Where?"

Cole opened his leather folder.

He removed a folded sheet of paper.

"This is a copy of the original transfer schedule."

Seraphina accepted it.

The schedule listed several files.

W-2 technical completion papers.

W-2 settlement correspondence.

W-2 equipment inventory.

W-2 commissioning review.

Each had a separate destination.

She examined the entries.

The technical completion papers had been sent to the Royal Academy.

The settlement correspondence had been sent to the Treasury.

The equipment inventory had been sent to the Royal Logistics Department.

The commissioning review had been transferred to a regional maintenance authority.

Seraphina looked up.

"Four separate destinations?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"That was the procedure."

"Who authorized the division?"

"The office director."

"Was there a reason for sending the commissioning review to the regional authority?"

Cole nodded.

"The regional authority was responsible for corrective work. The review was intended to accompany the work order."

"Was the work order issued?"

"I believe so."

"Do you have a copy?"

"No."

"Do you remember who received the commissioning review?"

Cole tapped the schedule.

"The receiving officer's name is written there."

Seraphina looked down.

The name was faint.

She tilted the paper toward the window.

**Peter Alden.**

She looked up.

"The maintenance supervisor?"

"Yes. He was the designated recipient."

"Did he receive the document?"

"I sent it."

"Do you have a signed receipt?"

Cole shook his head.

"No."

Seraphina placed the schedule on the table.

"Then we know the document was assigned to him, but not that he received it."

Cole nodded.

"That is correct."

The interview continued.

Cole explained that the Central Project Review Office had been created to process outstanding infrastructure files.

The office had received hundreds of records from regional authorities.

Some files were complete.

Others were missing supporting documents.

Some contained financial disputes.

Others involved projects that had been physically completed but never formally commissioned.

The W-2 file belonged to the last category.

"Was that unusual?" Seraphina asked.

"Not especially."

"How many projects were in that condition?"

"Several."

"Do you remember how many?"

"Not exactly. Perhaps twelve or fifteen."

Roland wrote down the number with a note that it was an estimate.

Seraphina continued.

"Was there a deadline for resolving them?"

"Yes. The office was expected to review all outstanding projects within eighteen months."

"Was that achieved?"

Cole gave a tired smile.

"No."

"What happened?"

"The office was dissolved before all the cases were resolved."

"Why?"

"Budget reduction. Administrative consolidation. There were also disagreements about which department should retain responsibility."

"Was the decision to dissolve the office made after the unresolved cases were identified?"

"Yes."

"Who was responsible for transferring those cases?"

"The office director."

"Did you receive instructions?"

"Yes."

Cole opened his folder again.

He removed another document.

"This is the instruction I received."

Seraphina examined it.

The document ordered the records administrator to divide the remaining files according to subject.

Technical documents were to be sent to the Academy.

Financial documents were to be sent to the Treasury.

Equipment records were to be sent to the Royal Logistics Department.

Outstanding maintenance cases were to be returned to the relevant regional authorities.

It was a sensible arrangement.

But the instruction contained a problem.

There was no requirement for the receiving departments to confirm that each file had been complete.

There was no unified index linking the divided records.

There was no central register of unresolved projects.

The office had been dissolved.

The records had been distributed.

And the responsibility for following up the remaining cases had not been clearly assigned.

Seraphina read the instruction again.

"Was this considered a completed transfer?"

"Yes."

"Even if the receiving departments did not confirm the contents?"

Cole nodded.

"That was the procedure."

She placed the document down.

"Who approved it?"

Cole pointed to the signature.

Lord Pembroke.

The former director of the Central Project Review Office.

Seraphina made a note.

She was not surprised.

But she was increasingly concerned.

"Why did you keep these copies?" she asked.

Cole looked at his folder.

"Because I did not think the transfer was complete."

"Why?"

"The office was closing. We had too many files and too little time. I asked for an extension."

"Was it granted?"

"No."

"Did you object?"

"I submitted a memorandum."

"Do you have it?"

Cole nodded.

He handed her another sheet.

The memorandum was brief.

It stated that the proposed transfer procedure did not provide sufficient confirmation of receipt for unresolved project files.

It recommended that a master register be retained by the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The recommendation had not been adopted.

Seraphina read the final paragraph.

*Without a consolidated register, the receiving departments may not be able to identify the outstanding responsibilities attached to individual projects.*

She set the paper down.

Cole watched her.

"I was told the receiving offices would maintain their own records."

"And did they?"

"Some did. Others did not."

"Did you know that at the time?"

"I suspected it."

"Why did you not refuse to transfer the files?"

Cole looked at her quietly.

"I was an administrator, Lady Seraphina. I could recommend a procedure. I could record an objection. I could ask for more time."

He rested his hands on his walking stick.

"I could not prevent the office from closing."

Seraphina did not answer immediately.

It was an uncomfortable distinction.

The clerk had been responsible for handling the records.

He had also been working under an instruction that he did not control.

His warning had been recorded.

It had simply not changed the decision.

Before the interview ended, Cole mentioned the box.

"Do you still have the original index?" Seraphina asked.

"No. The original went with the office records."

"Then what is in the box?"

"Copies I made for my own reference. I thought they might help if questions arose later."

"Did you keep the W-2 commissioning report?"

"I do not remember."

"Could we examine the box?"

Cole considered this.

"I would prefer to be present."

"That is reasonable."

Seraphina looked at Roland.

"Can the Treasury arrange an inspection with Mr Cole present?"

"Yes."

Cole nodded.

"Thank you."

He stood slowly.

Seraphina thanked him for his time.

As he reached the door, he turned back.

"There is one thing I should mention."

She waited.

"The transfer schedule may not be complete."

"Why?"

"Because I remember preparing a separate list of files that had not received confirmation from the regional offices."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Do you have a copy?"

"I believe so."

"Where?"

Cole tapped the leather folder.

"I have a handwritten note of its reference number. The original should be in the box."

Seraphina looked at Roland.

The box had become considerably more interesting.

The Treasury inspection was arranged for the following week.

Seraphina did not attend.

The box was an administrative record, not a personal investigation.

Cole went with two Treasury archivists and an officer from the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The inspection took several hours.

The box contained old correspondence, duplicate indexes, working notes and several copies of transfer schedules.

There were also documents concerning projects unrelated to W-2.

One folder concerned a bridge repair in Merrow.

Another contained a dispute over road maintenance payments.

A third concerned the replacement of a damaged water conduit.

The W-2 documents were scattered across three smaller bundles.

The first contained a copy of the technical review.

The second contained the original equipment transfer schedule.

The third contained a handwritten list of files whose receipt had not been confirmed.

The W-2 commissioning review appeared on that list.

Beside it was a note.

*Destination: Western Regional Maintenance Authority.*

*Receiving confirmation: not returned.*

The Treasury archivist examined the entry.

"Then the file may never have reached Peter Alden."

Cole looked at the note.

"I told you I could not confirm that it did."

The archivist continued.

"There is another entry."

It referred to a later correspondence from the regional authority.

The note was brief.

*Request for missing commissioning review forwarded to central administration.*

No date of receipt was recorded.

No response was attached.

Cole examined the page.

"I remember that request."

"Who sent it?"

"The regional maintenance office."

"Do you remember who handled it?"

Cole thought.

"An administrative clerk. I cannot remember his name."

The archivist made a note.

The commissioning report had not merely been lost during the original transfer.

The regional authority had later requested it.

Someone had known it was missing.

And the request had been forwarded to central administration.

Holt delivered the inspection report to the committee.

Theodore read it in silence.

Minister Garrick looked increasingly frustrated.

"Then the regional authority never received the commissioning review."

"We cannot confirm receipt," Holt replied.

"Is that not the same thing?"

"No, Minister. The transfer list identifies the intended destination, but no receiving confirmation survives."

Garrick rubbed his forehead.

"Must we distinguish every possible stage?"

Seraphina answered.

"Yes."

He looked at her.

She continued.

"If we say the regional authority received the file, we may be assigning responsibility to an office that never had it. If we say the file was lost, we may be overlooking a later transfer. We need to establish what happened."

Theodore turned to Holt.

"Was the regional request for the missing report preserved?"

"Yes. It was included in Mr Cole's box."

"Where was it sent?"

"To central administration."

"Which office?"

"The surviving copy does not identify the recipient."

Seraphina examined the note.

"Was there a central administration registry at the time?"

"Yes," Holt replied.

"Then we should search its incoming correspondence register."

Mr Bellamy looked at the date.

"That registry was later transferred to the Treasury."

"Then the Treasury can search it."

Garrick sighed.

"Naturally."

The meeting continued.

Lucien presented a technical note concerning the original commissioning requirements.

He explained that W-2 required a final calibration, a circuit stability test and a documented inspection before it could be placed into normal service.

The three regulating assemblies were necessary for the final configuration.

Without them, the circuit could not be considered complete.

"Could the station have been used without the final configuration?" Seraphina asked.

"Not as intended."

"Could it have provided limited service?"

"Possibly, depending on the circuit arrangement. But there is no evidence that it did."

Professor Vale added that the original programme had allowed temporary testing under controlled conditions.

Those tests did not constitute commissioning.

Seraphina wrote that down.

Theodore watched her.

"You are still maintaining that the station may have been physically complete but not operational."

"Yes."

"And that the equipment was delivered but not necessarily installed."

"Yes."

"And the commissioning review was transferred but not confirmed as received."

"Yes."

He glanced at the papers.

"Then our report should say exactly that."

Seraphina nodded.

It was the first time the committee had managed to describe W-2's status without collapsing several separate questions into one conclusion.

After the meeting, Holt approached her.

"Lady Seraphina, the Department would like your assistance with the proposed records procedure."

Seraphina looked at him.

"What procedure?"

"A consolidated register for all unresolved infrastructure projects."

She hesitated.

"Is that part of my appointment?"

"No. It would be a separate request."

"Then please send it formally."

Holt nodded.

"I expected you to say that."

"Good. It means my reputation is becoming predictable."

He smiled.

"The Crown Prince believes the committee would benefit from your advice."

Seraphina gathered her notes.

"The committee would benefit more from a permanent records officer."

"We are considering one."

"Then appoint one."

"We will."

She looked at him.

"Someone who understands that a transfer is not complete merely because a clerk writes the word transferred."

Holt nodded.

"I will include that in the proposal."

At the estate, Cedric was waiting with the winter road expenditure report.

He looked at Seraphina's expression.

"Another missing file?"

"Several."

"Did you find them?"

"Some."

"Did they explain the problem?"

"Partly."

Cedric sighed.

"That sounds like progress."

"It is."

She reviewed the road report.

The contractor had submitted an estimate for repairing a damaged section near the northern market road.

The work had been divided into three stages.

The first concerned drainage.

The second concerned the road surface.

The third concerned the replacement of a small bridge support.

Seraphina examined the figures.

"Why is the bridge support listed under the road-surface payment?"

Cedric checked.

"That is how the contractor submitted it."

"Does the contract allow that?"

"I will check."

"Please do."

He looked at her.

"You see a discrepancy everywhere now."

"Not everywhere."

She pointed to another line.

"Only where the numbers are wrong."

Cedric laughed.

He took the report back.

For a moment, the ordinary work of the estate felt almost restful.

The Treasury's search of the central correspondence register produced a result three days later.

The regional authority's request for the missing W-2 commissioning review had been received.

It had been assigned a reference number.

It had also been forwarded to a different department.

The receiving department was the **Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office**.

Seraphina read the result.

The Allocation Office had been responsible for coordinating project funding and monitoring financial milestones.

It had not been responsible for technical commissioning.

Why had the request been sent there?

The surviving register did not say.

She checked the date.

The request had been received four months before the reduced Veylan settlement was approved.

Four months.

The office had known that the commissioning review was missing.

The technical completion status remained unresolved.

And yet the financial settlement had proceeded.

Seraphina looked at the attached reference.

The Allocation Office's response was not included.

She placed the document on her desk.

The next search would have to concern the office's correspondence.

But there was something else.

The request had been assigned to a reviewing officer.

The name was clearly written.

**Margaret Ellison.**

Seraphina stopped.

It was a familiar name.

Not the head housekeeper at Veyr.

A different woman, according to the register's accompanying personnel details.

The Allocation Office's Margaret Ellison had worked in the capital.

And she had been the reviewing officer responsible for the correspondence concerning W-2.

Seraphina read the entry again.

The old file had passed through the hands of several people.

The engineer had deferred commissioning.

The records administrator had divided the file.

The regional authority had requested the missing report.

The Allocation Office had received that request.

And a reviewing officer named Margaret Ellison had been assigned to it.

Seraphina placed the paper beside the earlier documents.

She did not yet know what Margaret Ellison had done.

She did not know whether she had answered the request, forwarded it, or simply recorded it.

But she now had a specific office, a specific reference and a specific person.

The investigation had reached the financial administration that had approved the settlement.

For the first time, the question was no longer simply where the missing report had gone.

It was what the Treasury and the Allocation Office had known when they decided the project could be paid.
