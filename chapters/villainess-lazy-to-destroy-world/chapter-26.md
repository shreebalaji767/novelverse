# Chapter 26 — The Schedule That Was Approved Before It Was Ready

The royal reception ended without anyone declaring an emergency.

Seraphina considered this a success.

She had greeted the king, spoken to the queen, endured several conversations about the weather, listened to the Academy's presentation and avoided agreeing to anything that might require her to spend the next six months travelling between magical conduits.

She had also discovered that a public announcement could become a problem before the people responsible for carrying it out had finished discussing it.

That last achievement was less satisfying.

The following morning, she was still thinking about it.

"Your breakfast is getting cold, my lady."

Seraphina looked down.

Her eggs had indeed reached an unfortunate temperature.

"Is there any reason the Academy could not have waited until its locations were confirmed?"

Mira poured her tea.

"Perhaps they wanted to announce the programme."

"That is not a reason."

"It may be a reason to them."

Seraphina cut a piece of toast.

"People should not announce things merely because they want to announce them."

Mira considered this.

"Would that apply to the royal reception?"

Seraphina stopped.

"That is different."

"Why?"

"Because the reception was already scheduled."

"So the announcement was expected?"

"Yes."

"And the Academy's programme?"

"Was not ready."

Mira nodded thoughtfully.

"Then perhaps the Academy should have announced that it was still preparing the programme."

Seraphina looked at her.

"That is irritatingly sensible."

"Thank you, my lady."

"I was not complimenting you."

"I know."

The first official letter arrived before noon.

It was addressed to Duke Alaric, with copies to the regional administration and the Royal Academy.

The Crown's administrative office requested confirmation of House Veyr's cooperation with the proposed northern inspection schedule.

Alaric read it in his study.

Seraphina sat across from him, while Cedric stood near the window with a separate copy.

"They want confirmation within ten days," Alaric said.

"Of what?" Seraphina asked.

"Access to the western conduit, local coordination, and assistance in identifying the remaining maintenance records."

"Those are three different things."

"Yes."

"Are they asking us to confirm all three?"

Alaric handed her the letter.

Seraphina read it carefully.

The wording was broad. It referred to regional cooperation and preliminary arrangements but did not clearly distinguish between the responsibilities of the Academy and those of the estate.

She looked at Cedric.

"Can the estate grant access to the conduit?"

"To the section on Veyr land, yes. The access chamber is maintained by our regional maintenance office."

"Does that mean we own the conduit?"

"No. The estate maintains the access structure and surrounding land. The original conduit ownership is not clearly recorded."

"Then we can authorize access to the land, not claim ownership of the structure."

"Correct."

Seraphina looked back at the letter.

"And the local coordination?"

Cedric replied, "We can assign a guide, provide existing maintenance records and notify the relevant village offices."

"Can we provide technical staff?"

"No. We have maintenance workers, but none qualified to inspect the mana circuits."

"Then that should be stated."

Alaric nodded.

"What about the remaining records?"

Cedric glanced at his folder.

"The regional archives are being searched. We have not yet established where the older maintenance office transferred its documents."

Seraphina placed the letter down.

"Then we can confirm the things we can actually provide."

Alaric looked at her.

"Draft a response."

She hesitated.

"Me?"

"You have identified the distinctions."

"That does not mean I should write the official reply."

Cedric stepped forward.

"I can prepare the response, Your Grace. Lady Seraphina can review the wording."

Seraphina looked relieved.

"That would be better."

Alaric nodded.

"Do that."

Cedric opened his folder.

"I will separate land access, local coordination, technical responsibility and archive assistance."

"Good," Seraphina said. "And please make clear that cooperation with an inspection does not authorize repairs or changes to the conduit."

Cedric wrote it down.

Alaric watched them.

"You are both learning to use the same words."

Seraphina glanced at him.

"Is that a problem?"

"No. It is progress."

The second letter arrived from Lucien.

Unlike the royal request, it was brief.

It stated that the Academy had identified the source of the discrepancy in the public schedule.

The schedule had been prepared by the Academy's regional coordination office using an earlier planning document. The latest technical qualifications had been included in a separate attachment but were not carried into the public summary.

The omission had not been deliberate.

At least, no evidence suggested that it had been.

Lucien also explained that the Academy intended to issue a corrected schedule.

Seraphina read the letter twice.

Then she looked at Mira.

"Do you think the first schedule was approved?"

Mira considered the wording.

"It was announced."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

"Do you think people will understand the difference?"

"Some will."

Seraphina sighed.

"That is not reassuring."

In Aurelion, Lucien was having a considerably less pleasant morning.

The regional coordination office occupied three rooms on the Academy's lower administrative floor.

It was not a particularly impressive place.

The walls were crowded with maps, shelves held old technical reports, and a long table was covered with folders.

Lucien stood at the centre of the room while Master Fenwick examined the public schedule.

Professor Vale sat near the window.

A junior clerk named Edwin Clarke was checking the correspondence register.

"Who prepared this version?" Fenwick asked.

Edwin looked at the document.

"I did, sir."

"Who gave you the location list?"

"Mr. Halden."

"Which list?"

"The regional planning summary."

"Did it include the provisional status of the western sites?"

Edwin checked his notes.

"It included a separate column."

"Where is that column?"

"It was removed during formatting."

Fenwick stared at him.

"Why?"

Edwin looked uncomfortable.

"The summary was required to fit on one page."

Lucien closed his eyes.

Professor Vale looked toward the ceiling.

Fenwick placed the document on the table.

"Who requested a one-page summary?"

"The communications office."

"Who approved the final wording?"

Edwin checked the register again.

"The regional director."

"Did the director receive the technical attachment?"

"Yes."

"Did he review it?"

"I don't know."

Lucien approached the table.

"Can we establish whether he received the attachment?"

Edwin nodded.

"The distribution register should show that."

"Then check it."

Edwin began searching.

Fenwick turned toward Lucien.

"This is not a technical failure."

"No," Lucien said. "It is an administrative one."

"It is still our responsibility."

Lucien did not disagree.

He had spent years researching magical systems and developing methods for examining them.

He had not spent much time considering what happened when his findings passed through several offices before reaching the people expected to act on them.

Now he was learning.

The measurements could be accurate.

The report could be accurate.

And the public document could still be misleading.

By afternoon, the distribution register had been found.

The technical attachment had been delivered to the regional director's office.

However, the public schedule had been prepared by the communications office using an earlier summary. The updated attachment had not been included in the final formatting instructions.

There was no evidence that anyone had intentionally removed the qualifications.

There was also no evidence that the final schedule had been checked against the latest technical version.

Professor Vale read the register.

"So the problem is a broken review process."

"Yes," Lucien replied.

"Not simply a careless clerk."

"No."

Edwin looked relieved.

Fenwick did not.

"Then we need a review step before anything technical is published."

Lucien nodded.

"And the person approving the summary should confirm that it matches the underlying report."

Professor Vale smiled faintly.

"That is a rather ordinary solution."

"It should have been ordinary before."

"Many useful procedures are obvious after they have failed."

Lucien looked at the schedule.

He had begun to dislike it.

Not because the information was entirely wrong, but because it had been made to appear more certain than it was.

He took a clean sheet of paper.

"We will prepare a corrected version."

The revised schedule arrived at the Veyr estate two days later.

This time, it separated locations into three categories.

**Confirmed for preliminary inspection.**

**Awaiting access confirmation.**

**Awaiting ownership or maintenance clarification.**

The western conduit appeared in the first category, subject to the estate's limited land-access authorization.

The two disputed sites were listed in the third.

Seraphina examined the document.

It was better.

Not perfect, but better.

She handed it to Cedric.

"Please keep this with the original."

"Yes, my lady."

"Do we have confirmation that the first version has been withdrawn?"

Cedric checked the accompanying letter.

"The Academy has instructed its offices to replace the earlier summary."

"Have they notified the regional representatives?"

"Yes."

"Good."

She returned to her desk.

For the rest of the afternoon, she intended to do something completely unrelated to the Academy.

Unfortunately, the estate had other plans.

A message arrived from the northern maintenance office.

The old stone foundation near the western road junction had been examined by the surveyors.

It was not a road shelter.

It was the remains of a small maintenance building.

The foundation measured approximately twenty feet by twelve, with a narrow entrance facing the former conduit route.

The surveyors had also found several pieces of metal beneath the collapsed western wall.

One appeared to be part of an old sign bracket.

Seraphina read the report.

"Does it mention a marker?"

Cedric checked.

"No. Only a bracket."

"Has the metal been moved?"

"The surveyors collected it and placed it in the regional office."

"Did they photograph it first?"

"Yes."

"Good. Ask them to preserve it."

Cedric looked at the report again.

"There is another detail."

"What?"

"The foundation appears on an old estate map, but not on the later maintenance map."

"How old is the estate map?"

"Forty-one years."

"And the maintenance map?"

"Twenty-two years."

Seraphina looked at him.

"So the building existed when the conduit was first constructed, but disappeared from the later records."

"That seems to be the case."

"Do we know when it stopped being used?"

"No."

She leaned back.

"Then we have another question."

Cedric nodded.

"I will ask the regional office to search the property records."

Seraphina considered the map.

The building could have been demolished after the conduit modification.

It could have been abandoned.

It could have been replaced by the newer access shelter.

There were ordinary explanations.

She did not intend to choose one without evidence.

Three days later, Lucien sent another message.

Master Whitlock had reviewed the old maintenance register and confirmed that the modification had involved a change to the junction's regulating circuit.

The work had been ordered because the original junction produced irregular mana distribution during periods of high demand.

The modification was intended to improve stability.

The final inspection had been deferred because the contractor's completion certificate had not been received.

Master Whitlock remembered that the contractor had completed the work.

He did not remember the company's name.

He also remembered that the old maintenance building had been used to store tools and replacement materials.

Seraphina read the message slowly.

The building was not merely a shelter.

It had been part of the maintenance arrangement.

She placed the letter beside the survey report.

"That explains why it appears on the older map," she said.

Mira looked at the papers.

"Does it explain the missing marker?"

"No."

"Does it explain the missing completion certificate?"

"No."

"Does it explain why the building disappeared from the later map?"

"Not yet."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina looked at her.

"You're becoming very good at this."

"At what?"

"Not assuming things."

"I learned from you."

Seraphina sighed.

"That is becoming a problem."

The following week, Seraphina travelled to the western maintenance office with Cedric.

The office was a modest stone building near the regional transport yard. It contained several rooms, a small archive and a workshop.

The superintendent, Mr. Thomas Wren, had arranged for the surveyors to bring the recovered metal pieces.

They were laid out on a wooden table.

One was a bent iron bracket.

Another was a narrow metal plate, heavily corroded.

Seraphina examined the plate.

It bore several faint marks.

"Can you read them?" she asked.

The superintendent shook his head.

"Not yet. We have not cleaned it."

"Do not clean it until someone has examined it properly."

Cedric looked at the superintendent.

"Can we arrange a technical examination?"

"Yes. The Academy can send someone."

Seraphina pointed to the bracket.

"Does it fit the old foundation?"

The superintendent nodded.

"The surveyors believe so."

"Then record its location and condition before moving it again."

He made a note.

Seraphina turned to the archive shelves.

"Have you found any records relating to the building?"

"Only a few."

He brought out a thin folder.

Inside were several maintenance entries.

The first was dated twenty-two years earlier.

It recorded the delivery of replacement materials to the western junction.

The second listed a temporary storage arrangement.

The third mentioned the removal of unused tools.

There was no demolition order.

No final inspection record.

No transfer of responsibility.

Seraphina read the entries.

"Who signed these?"

"The regional maintenance supervisor at the time."

"Is his name recorded elsewhere?"

The superintendent pointed to the bottom of the page.

"Here."

**Hugh Mercer.**

Seraphina looked up.

"Is he still employed by the estate?"

"No. He retired many years ago."

"Do we know where he lives?"

The superintendent checked a personnel register.

"He moved to a village near Greyford."

Cedric looked at Seraphina.

"That may be worth following up."

"Yes."

She closed the folder.

"Please make a copy of these records and keep the originals here."

The superintendent nodded.

As they prepared to leave, he added something.

"There is another old record, my lady."

Seraphina stopped.

"What is it?"

"A maintenance inventory from the same period. It lists a metal marker issued to the western inspection station."

"Does it say what happened to it?"

"No."

"Does it list a return?"

The superintendent shook his head.

"Not in the surviving copy."

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

The marker might have belonged to the old building rather than the current access chamber.

That would explain why the Academy had found its mounting position empty.

It would not explain where the marker had gone.

But it gave them a more precise object to search for.

That evening, Seraphina sat in the family sitting room with her parents.

Alaric had returned from a meeting with the regional agricultural administrators.

Evelyne was reading correspondence from the capital.

Cedric had already submitted the maintenance office report.

Seraphina explained what they had found.

When she finished, Alaric leaned back.

"So the old building was a maintenance station."

"Yes."

"The junction was modified there."

"Yes."

"The final inspection was never recorded."

"Correct."

"And the marker is missing."

"Yes."

Alaric looked at her.

"Do you believe there was misconduct?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"I believe the records are incomplete. I believe the maintenance station was removed or abandoned without a surviving record. I believe the modification was not formally closed in the register."

"And the marker?"

"We know one was issued. We don't know whether it was installed, transferred or discarded."

Alaric nodded.

"Then that is what we know."

"Exactly."

Evelyne put down her letter.

"What about the Academy?"

"They are preparing a corrected schedule. The western inspection remains planned, but the other two sites are provisional."

"Good."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

"Do you think the royal reception helped?"

Evelyne smiled.

"It gave you an opportunity to ask a necessary question."

"It also gave the Academy an opportunity to announce something before it was ready."

"Both can be true."

Seraphina sighed.

"I dislike situations where everyone is partly correct."

Alaric laughed.

"Then you will find public administration difficult."

"I have already discovered that."

The next morning, Seraphina received a letter from Master Whitlock.

It was written in a careful, slightly uneven hand.

The retired engineer explained that the western junction modification had been performed by a contractor selected through the regional maintenance office.

He remembered that the contractor had arrived with its own workers and tools.

He remembered signing the preliminary work inspection.

He remembered the final inspection being postponed.

But he could not remember why.

There was one detail he considered important.

The contractor had been instructed to leave a set of spare regulating components at the maintenance station.

He did not know whether those components had been collected later.

The letter ended with a suggestion.

*If the old maintenance inventory survives, it may indicate whether the components were returned to the regional stores.*

Seraphina placed the letter on her desk.

The missing contractor's name remained unknown.

The completion certificate remained missing.

The final inspection had not been confirmed.

And the spare components might have been left at the old station.

She looked at the pile of documents.

There was now enough information to justify a more thorough archive search.

Not enough to accuse anyone.

Not enough to declare the conduit unsafe.

Not enough to claim that the missing components had been stolen.

But enough to continue.

That afternoon, she visited the infirmary.

Eleanor was reviewing the new supply ledger with Master Rowan.

The entries were clear.

Delivered quantities were recorded separately from usable stock, and every adjustment had a reason attached.

Seraphina examined the latest page.

"How is the new system working?"

"Better," Eleanor said.

"Any difficulties?"

"The purchasing office still forgets to include the batch numbers."

"Have you told them?"

"Twice."

"Have they corrected it?"

"Not yet."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we will send the instruction again."

Eleanor looked at her.

"Will that help?"

"Perhaps."

"Perhaps?"

"I am learning not to promise things I cannot guarantee."

Eleanor smiled.

"That is progress."

Seraphina looked at the ledger once more.

A delivery had been received.

The items had been inspected.

Some were ready for use.

Others were awaiting preparation.

Every figure had a meaning.

It was not a remarkable achievement.

Nobody would announce it at a royal reception.

But it meant that a physician could now look at the records and know what was actually available.

Seraphina found that more satisfying than she expected.

A week later, a message arrived from the capital.

The Academy had issued a formal correction to the regional inspection schedule.

The Crown's administrative office had acknowledged the revised version.

The two disputed sites would not proceed until ownership and access arrangements were confirmed.

The western conduit inspection would continue under the limited authorization granted by House Veyr.

There was also a short note from Lucien.

*Master Whitlock has remembered the contractor's first name.*

Seraphina stopped reading.

She turned the page.

*He believes the company was called Veylan Works. He is not certain. He remembers the name because the company's representative complained that the Academy's measurement instruments were too delicate for field conditions.*

Seraphina read the sentence again.

Veylan Works.

It was a name.

Not yet a confirmed name.

But it was something they could search for.

She took out a fresh sheet of paper and wrote to Roland.

She asked him to check the regional procurement registers for a contractor by that name, or a similar one, around the year of the modification.

She instructed him not to assume that a matching name identified the same company without supporting records.

Then she folded the letter and placed it in the outgoing tray.

The Academy's programme had been corrected.

The infirmary's records were improving.

The old maintenance station had been located.

And the search for a contractor had finally acquired a direction.

Seraphina looked out of the window.

The estate was quiet.

For a brief moment, she allowed herself to enjoy it.

Then Mira entered.

"My lady, a messenger has arrived from the capital."

Seraphina turned.

"Another letter?"

"Yes."

"From whom?"

"The Royal Academy."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

"What do they want now?"

Mira held out the envelope.

"They are requesting your presence at a meeting."

Seraphina stared at it.

"Why?"

Mira checked the accompanying note.

"It concerns the regional inspection programme."

Seraphina took the letter.

The meeting was scheduled for the following month.

The invitation specified that House Veyr could send a representative.

It did not specify that the representative had to be Seraphina.

She looked at the final paragraph.

The Academy had suggested her attendance because of her previous involvement in clarifying the scope of the western inspection.

Seraphina placed the letter on her desk.

Then she wrote a reply.

She would not attend.

She would send Cedric, accompanied by the estate's regional maintenance superintendent.

The Academy needed a representative who understood the estate's authority, the local maintenance arrangements and the limits of its technical capacity.

That was not Seraphina.

It was Cedric.

She signed the letter and handed it to Mira.

"Send this."

Mira accepted it.

"Yes, my lady."

Seraphina returned to her chair.

For once, she had not merely refused a responsibility.

She had identified the person better qualified to perform it.

That, she decided, was an improvement worth keeping.

**End of Chapter 26**
