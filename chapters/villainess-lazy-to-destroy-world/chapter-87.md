# **Chapter 87 — The Signature That Had Been Approved Without a Name**

The unfamiliar signature was not particularly elegant.



It leaned slightly to the right, contained an unnecessarily elaborate capital letter and ended in a short line that might have been intended as a flourish.



Seraphina disliked it immediately.



Not because it looked suspicious.



Because she could not read it.



"Is that a name?" she asked.



Lucien examined the approval sheet.



"It appears to be one."



"That is unfortunate."



"Why?"



"Because I cannot tell what it says."



Lucien placed the paper beneath a reading lamp.



The document had been brought to the palace for the administrative review. It was an original approval sheet from the design review conducted before the eastern circuit's final modification.



The first signature belonged to the former deputy standards officer.



The second belonged to an unnamed temporary senior examiner.



The personnel index contained no matching permanent appointment.



The approval sheet itself identified the examiner only by title.



Seraphina looked at the date.



"Four months."



"What?"



"The temporary appointment lasted four months, according to the surviving personnel reference. Was this approval signed during that period?"



Lucien checked the record.



"Yes."



"Then the appointment existed."



"That is established."



"Was the examiner authorized to approve the design?"



"That is what we need to establish."



Seraphina leaned back.



"Then perhaps we should begin with the appointment."



Lucien looked at her.



"That is what the Directorate intends to do."



"Good. I would hate to have invented an entirely different investigation."



He ignored the remark.



The original approval sheet was returned to its protective folder.



For the first time, the review group had a document showing that the eastern circuit's revised design had been approved by two people.



One was identifiable.



The other was not.



And the technical authority of the second signature remained uncertain.

The Technical Standards Directorate began with its appointment records.



The temporary examiner had been appointed under a special provision allowing the Board to recruit external specialists when a project required expertise unavailable among its permanent staff.



The appointment was valid for four months.



The examiner was authorized to review technical designs and submit recommendations.



However, the appointment did not automatically grant authority to approve changes to the capital's protective network.



Such approval required either the Board's collective authorization or a specific delegation from its chair.



The surviving record contained neither.



The Directorate's legal officer, Master Edwin Sorrell, explained the distinction during the next review meeting.



"The examiner could review the design, identify technical concerns and recommend approval. That does not establish that the examiner could approve the design independently."



Theodore looked at the document.



"Was the second signature intended as a recommendation?"



"Possibly."



"Then why does the approval sheet contain two signatures?"



"Because the first signature may have represented administrative authorization and the second technical review."



Master Fenwick considered this.



"Or the second signature may have been the technical approval, with the first confirming procedural compliance."



"Both interpretations are possible," Sorrell replied.



Seraphina sat near the end of the table.



"Does the document have a signature legend?"



Sorrell checked.



"No."



"Does the original approval form have instructions?"



"It has a printed heading, but no explanation of the signature fields."



"Then we do not know what either signature means."



"Not entirely."



She looked at the paper.



"That should be stated in the register."



Master Fenwick nodded.



"Agreed."



Lucien looked mildly frustrated.



"We are discussing the authority of a design approval, not merely the wording of a register."



"Then the technical and legal review should establish the authority. The register should record what they establish."



Seraphina paused.



"And until they establish it, the register should not pretend that the question has been answered."



Theodore glanced at Master Fenwick.



"That seems straightforward."



"It is," Fenwick replied.



Seraphina returned to her notes.



She had learned that straightforward things often required several meetings.

The former Board's chair was found in the capital.



Lord Matthew Orlan had retired twelve years earlier and now lived outside the administrative district.



He was seventy-one, physically healthy and considerably less patient than the officials who had been searching for him.



He agreed to an interview after receiving a written explanation of the inquiry.



The meeting took place in his residence.



Lucien, Master Fenwick and the Directorate's legal officer attended.



Seraphina was not invited.



This was probably for the best.



Lord Orlan remembered the design review.



He remembered the temporary examiner.



He even remembered the eastern circuit's proposed modification.



But he could not remember the examiner's name.



"Was the appointment made because the Board lacked the necessary expertise?" Lucien asked.



"Yes. We needed someone familiar with distribution systems operating under variable seasonal loads."



"Was the examiner an engineer?"



"Certainly."



"From the Academy?"



"No."



"The Royal Infrastructure Department?"



"Not directly."



"Then where?"



Orlan thought for a moment.



"I believe the examiner had previously worked with a private engineering consultancy."



"Do you remember its name?"



"No."



"Was the examiner recommended by the Directorate?"



"I believe so."



"Did the Board approve the appointment?"



"Yes."



"Was the design review completed during the appointment?"



"Partially."



Lucien leaned forward.



"What do you mean by partially?"



"The examiner reviewed the proposed changes and identified several conditions that needed to be met before installation. The Board discussed those conditions."



"Was the design approved?"



Orlan looked at him.



"The design was approved subject to technical confirmation after installation."



"Was that confirmation completed?"



"I cannot say."



"Do you remember the examiner's concerns?"



Orlan considered the question.



"The eastern circuit was expected to carry a greater share of the distribution load after the modification. The reserve zone was supposed to compensate during periods of increased demand. The examiner wanted confirmation that the revised control assembly could respond without relying on assumptions from the previous design."



Lucien exchanged a glance with Master Fenwick.



"Was that concern included in the final recommendation?"



"I do not remember a final recommendation."



"Do you remember the approval sheet?"



Orlan examined a copy.



He studied the two signatures.



Then he pointed to the unfamiliar one.



"That is the examiner's signature."



Lucien sat straighter.



"Are you certain?"



"I remember the handwriting."



"Do you remember the name?"



"No."



"Did the examiner sign the document as an independent technical reviewer?"



"That would have been the purpose of the appointment."



"Was the Board's approval also required?"



"Yes."



"Then why is the Board's collective approval not recorded?"



Orlan looked at the sheet again.



"I cannot explain that."



The interview established one important point.



The unfamiliar signature belonged to the temporary examiner.



It did not establish whether the examiner had been authorized to approve the design independently.



It also did not establish whether the Board had completed its own approval procedure.



Lord Orlan signed his interview statement.



He did not attempt to fill the gaps with recollection.



Lucien found that refreshing.

The Academy searched its external examiner records.



It found three names associated with temporary appointments during the relevant period.



One had been a specialist in agricultural circuits.



One had reviewed transport infrastructure.



The third had worked on distribution systems.



The third name was Master Adrian Voss.



He had been employed by a private engineering consultancy called Eastbridge Technical Associates.



The company had since dissolved.



Its surviving commercial registration showed that it had worked on regional power distribution, industrial mana regulation and large-scale protective systems.



Master Voss had been appointed as an external examiner for four months.



His contract specified that he would review the eastern circuit's revised design and provide a technical recommendation.



It also stated that he would not approve installation, commissioning or operational changes.



Lucien read the contract twice.



"Then the signature was a technical recommendation."



"That is the likely interpretation," Master Fenwick said.



"Why was it placed on the approval sheet?"



"Perhaps the form was used for both recommendation and approval."



"That would be poor procedure."



"Yes."



Seraphina, who had been invited to review the administrative sequence, examined the contract.



"Does the contract say where the recommendation should be submitted?"



"To the Board's technical secretary," Sorrell replied.



"Is there a receipt?"



"No."



"Then we know what the examiner was supposed to do, but not whether the recommendation reached the correct office."



Lucien frowned.



"We have the signed sheet."



"Yes. But the contract says it should have been submitted to the technical secretary. Do we know that this sheet was received by the secretary?"



Sorrell looked at the transfer record.



"The sheet was found in the final design file."



"That establishes where it ended up. Not how it arrived."



Master Fenwick made a note.



"Then we need the Board's correspondence register."



The Directorate requested it.

The correspondence register was found in a box transferred during the Board's reorganization.



It contained a reference to Master Voss's recommendation.



The entry was dated three days after the approval sheet.



**External technical review received. Forwarded to Board Secretariat for consideration.**



There was no recipient's signature.



There was no decision recorded beside the entry.



However, a later page contained a second reference.



**Eastern Circuit Design — Board Discussion Deferred Pending Installation Conditions.**



The date was two weeks later.



The Board had not rejected the design.



It had not fully approved it either.



It had deferred the final decision until the installation conditions were confirmed.



Seraphina read the entry.



"That is clearer."



Lucien looked at her.



"How?"



"Because it establishes that the Board did not consider the examiner's signature sufficient by itself."



Master Fenwick nodded.



"The design was conditionally accepted for implementation, with final technical confirmation required afterward."



"Was the installation authorized under that condition?"



Sorrell checked the construction file.



"Yes. The authorization permitted installation, subject to the post-installation assessment."



"Then the installation itself was not necessarily improper."



"No."



"And the missing assessment remains the problem."



"Correct."



Seraphina put down the register.



The distinction mattered.



The design had been reviewed.



The Board had discussed it.



Installation had been authorized conditionally.



The eastern circuit had been modified.



But the final technical confirmation had not been found.



The system had continued operating, and the reserve zone had remained active.



Nothing in the surviving records established that the original design had been invalid.



The problem was that the final stage of approval had never been documented.

The next question concerned Master Adrian Voss.



The Academy found him living in the eastern territories.



He was seventy-four and had retired from engineering work.



His health was poor, but he agreed to answer written questions.



The first letter asked whether he remembered the eastern circuit review.



He replied that he did.



He remembered the design.



He remembered the conditions attached to installation.



He also remembered that he had recommended a second assessment after the control assembly was installed.



His original recommendation had not been an unconditional approval.



It had been a technical opinion that the design could proceed to installation, provided the final measurements confirmed the expected distribution response.



He had signed the sheet in the space reserved for the external examiner.



The Board had discussed the recommendation afterward.



He did not know why the final assessment had never been issued.



The second letter asked whether he had been transferred or removed from the project.



His answer was brief.



He had completed the four-month contract.



The Board had not renewed it.



He had not been asked to continue the assessment.



He had assumed the permanent examiners would complete the work.



The third letter asked whether he believed his recommendation had been misrepresented.



He replied:



*I cannot determine how later officers interpreted the document. My recommendation was conditional. I did not certify the completed installation or authorize the reserve zone to be reduced.*



Lucien placed the reply beside the approval sheet.



Master Fenwick read it.



"That settles the meaning of the second signature."



"Yes," Lucien replied.



"It does not settle the final approval."



"No."



Theodore looked toward Sorrell.



"Then the Board's discussion record is the strongest evidence we have."



"Correct."



"And the final assessment remains missing."



"Yes."



Theodore leaned back.



"Then we should record the design as conditionally accepted for installation, with final technical confirmation unverified."



Seraphina nodded.



"That is accurate."



Lucien looked at the documents.



"At least we now know who signed the sheet."



Seraphina looked at him.



"That is progress."



"It feels rather small."



"It is considerably larger than not knowing."

The revised administrative register was updated the following week.



The eastern circuit's design history now contained four distinct entries.



The original design review had been conducted by the Board.



The external examiner's recommendation had been conditional.



The Board had permitted installation subject to later technical confirmation.



The final assessment had not been located.



The reserve zone remained active under the existing maintenance procedure.



The register also identified the two separate approvals that had previously been confused.



The technical recommendation had been signed by Master Adrian Voss.



The Board's conditional decision had been recorded in the correspondence register.



Neither document constituted final commissioning.



The new wording was submitted to the King.



He approved the administrative correction.



The technical review continued.

Seraphina received the revised register at the palace on a Thursday afternoon.



She was in the library with Mira.



Mira was sorting a collection of household receipts.



Seraphina was attempting to finish a chapter of her book.



The register arrived in a sealed envelope.



She opened it.



"Is it complete?" Mira asked.



"Administratively, this section is."



"Does that mean the investigation is finished?"



"No."



Mira looked disappointed.



"Why?"



"Because the engineers still need to test the eastern circuit under the correct conditions."



"Then why did they send you the register?"



"Because someone has to make sure the records describe what is actually known."



Mira considered that.



"That sounds like a reasonable task."



"It is."



Seraphina read the final page.



The current operating arrangement was unchanged.



The next assessment would use a controlled load profile and the reserve zone would remain active during the initial measurements.



The technical team would determine whether the eastern circuit's revised control assembly behaved as expected under sustained demand.



If the readings remained within the established range, the team would proceed to a limited reduction test.



If the readings deviated, the test would be stopped and the reserve configuration restored.



The document also stated that no further modification to the capital protective network could be authorized until the assessment was complete.



Seraphina approved the administrative wording.



She returned the document to its envelope.



"Finished?" Mira asked.



"For now."



Mira gathered the receipts.



"Then perhaps you can finish your book."



Seraphina opened it.



She read two paragraphs.



Then the door opened.



Cedric entered carrying a different folder.



"Seraphina, I need your opinion."



She closed her eyes.



"About what?"



"The estate's northern storage building."



"Does it have a roof?"



"Yes."



"Then I am already relieved."



Cedric placed the folder on the table.



"The regional authority has asked whether House Veyr would permit a temporary inspection of the old ventilation shaft."



Seraphina looked at him.



"Why?"



"They believe the building may have been included in an earlier infrastructure survey."



"Does it contain a magical circuit?"



"That is not established."



"Then why does the authority want to inspect it?"



Cedric opened the folder.



"The request refers to a historical map from the northern winter network."



Seraphina stared at him.



For several seconds, she said nothing.



Then she placed her book face down.



"How old is the map?"



"Forty-seven years."



"Does it identify the building?"



"Only as a storage structure with a possible underground connection."



"Possible?"



"Yes."



"Does the map show an active circuit?"



"No."



"Does the regional authority have a technical assessment?"



"Not yet."



Seraphina leaned back.



"Then we will not allow anyone to open the floor or enter the shaft until the authority provides a proper inspection scope."



Cedric nodded.



"I expected that."



"And we are not providing engineers, equipment or transport."



"I know."



"Good."



Cedric glanced at the palace register.



"Is this related to the capital investigation?"



"Not directly."



"Are you certain?"



"No. But there is no evidence connecting them."



Cedric closed the folder.



"Then I will ask the authority to clarify the request."



Seraphina reopened her book.



She had reached the part where the protagonist was about to appoint herself governor of a province.



She was beginning to understand why the original villainess had become so unpleasant.



She herself was developing a strong dislike of people who volunteered for things.

That evening, Lucien sent a final message.



The Academy had completed the preliminary comparison of the eastern circuit's original design, installation records and surviving measurements.



The system's current operating configuration remained within its established limits.



The reserve zone continued to perform the balancing function described in the original design.



The eastern circuit's independent capacity under sustained high demand remained unverified.



The new controlled assessment would begin in twelve days.



Lucien added one final sentence.



*The technical team has also requested the original seasonal load records from the eastern district.*



Seraphina read it.



Then she placed the message on her desk.



The eastern district's seasonal records were maintained by the regional authority.



They had not been part of the original capital network review.



But if the old assessment had been incomplete because it used average demand rather than the actual seasonal profile, those records would matter.



She made a note to ensure that the Academy received the correct documents.



Then she stopped.



She was not responsible for collecting them.



The Department could request them.



The regional authority could provide them.



The Academy could examine them.



She crossed out her note.



It was an unusually satisfying act.

Twelve days later, the Academy began the new assessment.



The first stage used the reserve zone in its ordinary configuration.



The eastern circuit's readings were stable.



The second stage increased the simulated demand to reflect the seasonal profile.



The reserve zone responded as expected.



The third stage involved a small reduction in reserve contribution.



The eastern distribution pressure rose.



The reserve zone compensated.



The test was stopped before the readings approached the upper operating threshold.



The network remained stable.



Lucien studied the results.



The Academy had established that the reserve zone was not redundant.



It continued to perform a necessary balancing function under elevated eastern demand.



But the test also revealed something else.



The eastern circuit's control assembly was responding more slowly than the original design predicted.



Not dangerously slowly.



Not outside the current operating range.



But consistently slower than expected.



The difference was small.



The readings had remained safe.



The reserve zone had compensated.



Lucien checked the instruments again.



The delay appeared in all three measurement sets.



Master Fenwick ordered the test stopped for the day.



The current configuration was restored.



The technical team recorded the result.



The next stage would require a comparison of the eastern control assembly's actual response time with the original installation specifications.



The network continued operating.



No alarm sounded.



No district lost protection.



But the Academy's report now contained a new line:



**Eastern control assembly: response delay observed under elevated demand. Within current operating limits. Cause not established. Further assessment required.**



The seventh zone had been understood at last.



It was a working part of the modern system, not an obsolete temporary arrangement.



But it had been compensating for a difference in the eastern circuit that the central model did not fully describe.



And the engineers still did not know why the eastern circuit was responding more slowly than its original design predicted.
