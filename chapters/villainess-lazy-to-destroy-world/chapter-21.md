# Chapter 21 — The Royal Academy's Very Reasonable Request

Seraphina had three conditions for her visit to the capital.

First, she would attend the Academy's infrastructure review and nothing else.

Second, she would not accept responsibility for projects outside House Veyr's authority.

Third, she would return home before anyone could invent another reason for her to remain.

Her mother listened to these conditions over breakfast.

Then Duchess Evelyne Veyr put down her teacup.

"Those are sensible conditions."

Seraphina felt encouraged.

"Thank you."

"However, you should understand that the capital does not always respect the boundaries people establish for themselves."

Seraphina's encouragement disappeared.

"That sounds like a warning."

"It is."

Across the table, Duke Alaric unfolded his morning correspondence.

"Your mother is right. The Academy has requested a representative from House Veyr. You should expect questions about the western conduit, but you should not assume that every question will concern the conduit."

"Why would they ask about anything else?"

"Because people in the capital are curious."

Seraphina looked at him.

"About the conduit?"

"About you."

She lowered her spoon.

That was considerably worse.

Evelyne smiled faintly.

"Your appearance at the spring reception attracted attention. Your questions about regional maintenance attracted more. Your father's decision to allow an Academy inspection attracted still more."

"I thought the inspection was a technical matter."

"It is. But technical matters rarely remain technical when they involve the property, money, or authority of several powerful institutions."

Seraphina considered this.

"Could we send Cedric instead?"

"Your cousin is responsible for the estate's administrative records," Alaric said. "He is not familiar with the Academy's discussions."

"Could we send Lucien?"

"He is the Academy's representative."

"That seems unfairly convenient for him."

Evelyne laughed.

"You are going because you are the person who authorized the inspection and understands the conditions under which it was permitted."

Seraphina looked at her breakfast.

She had wanted a quiet life.

Apparently, a quiet life required an increasingly detailed understanding of public infrastructure.

"How long will the review last?"

"Two days," Evelyne replied. "Your father has arranged accommodation at the Veyr residence in Aurelion."

"Two days."

"Possibly three."

"Mother."

"Travel arrangements sometimes change."

Seraphina sighed.

She had not yet left the duchy, and her return journey was already becoming uncertain.

The preparation for the journey was more complicated than Seraphina expected.

Not because she needed many clothes.

Her mother had already decided which dresses were appropriate.

Not because she needed documents.

Cedric had prepared the relevant inspection reports, correspondence, and authorization records.

The difficulty was that every person involved seemed to have a different idea of what a representative of House Veyr ought to carry.

Margaret Ellison wanted her to bring additional formal clothing.

Mira wanted her to bring comfortable shoes.

The steward wanted to know whether the capital residence required extra household supplies.

Cedric wanted her to carry the original inspection authorization.

Roland wanted her to carry a copy of the regional maintenance register.

Her father wanted her to bring a letter confirming that she was not authorized to approve any expenditure on behalf of the duchy without further consultation.

Seraphina looked at the growing stack of documents.

"Am I attending a review or establishing a second estate?"

Mira folded another dress.

"Would you like me to remove anything?"

Seraphina looked at the documents.

"No. I have learned that removing documents before travelling is how people discover they needed them."

Mira nodded.

"That sounds wise."

"It is a very unpleasant lesson."

The final packet contained:

- The Academy's formal inspection request.
- The Veyr authorization for access to the western conduit.
- The local maintenance supervisor's report.
- The Academy's preliminary inspection findings.
- The relevant regional road and irrigation maps.
- A copy of the current maintenance register.
- A statement limiting Seraphina's authority to matters already approved by the duke.

She checked the list twice.

Then she placed the packet inside a leather case.

It was heavier than she wanted.

But it was better than arriving in the capital and discovering that somebody had decided a missing page represented consent.

They departed on the morning of the fifth day of Rainmelt.

The journey to Aurelion required two nights on the road.

The first day passed through familiar northern farmland.

The second brought them into increasingly populated territory, where roads were wider, inns were more frequent, and merchants travelled in larger groups.

Seraphina spent much of the journey reading.

She reviewed the Academy's preliminary findings.

The western conduit had been constructed nearly forty years earlier as part of a regional mana-distribution network. It was intended to regulate small amounts of ambient mana flowing through the northern agricultural district.

The conduit did not produce magic.

It directed existing mana through a prepared circuit.

The system supplied several minor functions, including signal lamps, small protective barriers around storage buildings, and controlled energy flow to a handful of agricultural facilities.

It was not a weapon.

It was not a source of unlimited power.

It was infrastructure.

And like any infrastructure, it required maintenance.

The Academy's report identified three concerns.

First, the mana flow had become irregular near the old mill road.

Second, a maintenance marker was missing from the junction.

Third, the surviving maintenance records did not contain a complete account of the last major inspection.

Seraphina read the third concern again.

Incomplete records.

She looked out of the carriage window.

There was something deeply irritating about discovering that even magical infrastructure suffered from the same problems as ordinary warehouses.

"Do you think the conduit is dangerous?" Mira asked.

Seraphina looked up.

"The Academy says there is no immediate danger."

"Then why are they reviewing it?"

"Because something can be safe today and still require attention."

Mira considered that.

"Like a roof."

"Exactly."

"Or a wheel."

"Yes."

"Or the old east stair."

Seraphina glanced at her.

"The east stair is still damaged?"

"The lower step is uneven."

Seraphina stared at her.

She had been investigating regional infrastructure for weeks.

She had forgotten about a staircase inside her own residence.

"Please tell Margaret."

"I already did."

Seraphina leaned back.

"Good."

Mira smiled.

"She has already arranged for it to be repaired."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

She was surrounded by competent people.

It was a wonderful thing.

It was also making it increasingly difficult to justify her complaints.

Aurelion was larger than Seraphina remembered.

Her previous-life memories of the city were incomplete, shaped by descriptions from the novel rather than actual experience. She remembered the royal palace, the Academy towers, the grand avenue, and the aristocratic quarter.

She had not remembered the noise.

Carriages moved through the main streets in long lines. Merchants called from their stalls. Apprentices carried boxes between shops. Street vendors sold warm pastries, fruit, and roasted chestnuts.

The city smelled of horses, bread, wet stone, and smoke.

It was not an elegant painting.

It was a living place.

Seraphina watched from the carriage as they passed a public fountain.

A group of children were arguing beside it.

One had dropped a wooden toy into the water.

Another was trying to retrieve it with a stick.

A woman stood nearby, apparently deciding whether to intervene.

Seraphina watched until the carriage turned the corner.

"Is something wrong, my lady?" Mira asked.

"No."

"You were watching them."

"They reminded me that people live in the capital."

Mira looked puzzled.

Seraphina turned toward her.

"I know that sounds obvious."

"It does."

"I suppose I expected the city to be full of people attending meetings."

Mira smiled.

"It is also full of people who have lost their toys."

That was a reasonable observation.

The Veyr residence stood in the northern aristocratic quarter.

It was smaller than the main estate but still substantial, with a stone courtyard, a central garden, and three floors of guest and family rooms.

The household staff welcomed them.

A housekeeper named Mrs. Lillian Harcourt presented herself at the entrance.

"Welcome to Aurelion, Lady Seraphina."

"Thank you, Mrs. Harcourt."

"Your rooms have been prepared. The Duchess's instructions have been followed."

Seraphina looked at Mira.

Mira looked pleased.

Her mother had evidently managed to arrange the residence without requiring Seraphina's involvement.

Another victory for competent people.

The next morning, Seraphina arrived at the Royal Academy.

The Academy occupied an extensive complex of stone buildings, enclosed by iron gates and surrounded by carefully maintained gardens.

Its main tower rose above the other structures.

Several smaller towers stood behind it, each carrying a different crest or insignia.

Students crossed the courtyard in groups.

Some wore ordinary academic robes.

Others carried books, instruments, or small containers marked with protective symbols.

A pair of apprentices hurried past carrying a wooden frame.

One of them nearly collided with a porter.

The porter shouted something unkind.

The apprentices apologized and continued running.

Seraphina watched them disappear.

"Is that normal?" she asked.

Mira glanced toward the porter.

"At the Academy?"

"I suppose."

"Then perhaps."

Seraphina decided not to investigate.

A young woman in a dark blue robe approached them.

"Lady Seraphina Veyr?"

"Yes."

"I am Assistant Registrar Helena Ward. Professor Vale has asked me to escort you to the review chamber."

"Thank you."

Helena led them through the main building.

The Academy's interior was quieter than the courtyard.

The walls were lined with portraits of former scholars. Display cases held old instruments, fragments of magical circuits, and carefully preserved catalysts.

Seraphina stopped briefly before one case.

Inside lay a small metal disc with several engraved lines.

"What is that?"

Helena glanced back.

"An early mana regulator. It was used in the first generation of regional conduits."

"Does it still work?"

"No. The catalyst has been removed."

"Why?"

"Because the regulator is no longer considered safe for practical use."

Seraphina looked at the disc.

"Then why keep it?"

Helena smiled.

"Because it is historically important."

Seraphina nodded.

She understood.

Some things were preserved because they were useful.

Others were preserved because they reminded people not to repeat mistakes.

She continued walking.

The review chamber was already occupied.

Professor Edmund Vale stood beside a long table covered in documents.

Lucien Arkwright was speaking with Master Alistair Fenwick.

Liora Everwyn sat near the windows, reading a report.

Several other Academy officials were present.

Seraphina recognized none of them.

Professor Vale approached.

"Lady Seraphina. Welcome."

"Professor."

"Thank you for coming."

"Thank you for inviting House Veyr."

Lucien turned.

"Lady Veyr."

"Master Arkwright."

He glanced at the leather case she carried.

"You brought the regional records?"

"Yes."

"Excellent. We have been trying to obtain the original maintenance schedule."

Seraphina placed the case on the table.

"These are copies. The originals remain in the Veyr archives."

Lucien nodded.

"Of course."

Liora approached.

"It is good to see you again."

Seraphina smiled.

"You too."

Liora looked toward the documents.

"Have you had a chance to review the inspection report?"

"Yes. I understand that the conduit is stable but requires further examination."

"That is correct."

"Is the second access point still under consideration?"

"Yes," Lucien said. "The first junction can be examined from the western boundary, but the old mill road gives us a better view of the section where the mana flow becomes irregular."

Seraphina opened the leather case.

"Then I would like to clarify the local work schedule before we begin."

Lucien looked mildly surprised.

"Of course."

She placed the regional map on the table.

"The irrigation channel is scheduled for repair in this area. The workers will be replacing the damaged supports and clearing the drainage route."

Master Fenwick leaned over the map.

"That is close to the conduit."

"Exactly. I do not want the inspection team and the maintenance workers operating in the same area without coordination."

Lucien examined the markings.

"That should be manageable."

"Good. The Academy may inspect the conduit. Any repairs requiring excavation or mana discharge will need a separate assessment."

One of the officials frowned.

"Surely the Academy can determine whether a repair is necessary."

"Of course. But the Academy cannot authorize work on land belonging to House Veyr without the appropriate permission."

The official looked toward Professor Vale.

Professor Vale gave a small nod.

"Lady Seraphina is correct. The inspection authorization does not constitute permission for repairs."

Seraphina was grateful.

She had not wanted to argue about it.

Lucien moved a marker on the map.

"We can arrange the examination without interfering with the irrigation work."

"Thank you."

The discussion proceeded.

For the next hour, the Academy presented its preliminary findings.

The conduit had been constructed in stages.

Its original design used a series of regulated mana circuits connected through underground channels.

The junction near the mill road had been modified twice.

The first modification had been made after the original system experienced excessive flow during a period of unusually high ambient mana.

The second had been recorded as a routine maintenance adjustment.

However, the surviving documents did not clearly identify which components had been replaced.

Seraphina listened carefully.

"Could the irregularity be caused by the second modification?" she asked.

Lucien nodded.

"It could be."

"Do you know who performed it?"

"We have a partial record."

"Partial?"

"The name of the supervising engineer is present. The contractor's name is missing."

Seraphina looked at the document.

The entry was dated twenty-two years earlier.

She recognized the format.

A short description of the work.

A quantity of materials.

A signature.

No attached inspection certificate.

It reminded her of the old maintenance authorization records at Veyr Estate.

Not the same documents.

Not necessarily the same problem.

But familiar enough to make her uncomfortable.

"Was the work inspected afterward?" she asked.

"According to the register, yes."

"Is the inspection report available?"

Lucien hesitated.

"Not yet."

Seraphina looked at Professor Vale.

"Then perhaps the Academy should locate it before deciding whether the modification was responsible."

Professor Vale smiled.

"An entirely reasonable suggestion."

Lucien did not look pleased.

But he nodded.

"Agreed."

During the midday break, Liora joined Seraphina in the Academy garden.

The garden was quieter than the courtyard.

A narrow stone path wound between trimmed hedges and small flowering trees.

A fountain stood near the centre.

Liora carried two cups of tea.

"Would you like one?"

"Thank you."

Seraphina accepted it.

They sat on a stone bench.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Liora looked toward the main building.

"Do you find the Academy overwhelming?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"I find the number of people who want to discuss the same problem from different angles overwhelming."

Liora laughed.

"That is a very accurate description."

"Does it happen often?"

"More often than I would like."

Liora took a sip of tea.

"People assume that because I can heal injuries, I can also solve every problem connected to illness."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Can you?"

"No."

Liora's answer was immediate.

"I can treat certain injuries. I can help stabilize patients. I can reduce pain and support recovery. But I cannot replace trained physicians, clean water, proper nutrition, or medicine."

"Do people understand that?"

"Some do. Others prefer to believe that a miracle can solve everything."

Seraphina nodded.

It was a familiar kind of misunderstanding.

People wanted a single answer to complicated problems.

A saintess.

A powerful spell.

A generous noble.

A clever administrator.

Someone who could take responsibility for everything and make the difficulties disappear.

It was a comforting idea.

It was also dangerous.

"Do you ever refuse?" Seraphina asked.

"Yes."

"Does that cause problems?"

"Frequently."

Liora looked down at her cup.

"But refusing a request is sometimes more responsible than pretending I can fulfill it."

Seraphina thought about the old grain records.

The 105 measures.

The missing allocation schedule.

The unanswered question of where the grain had gone.

She had been tempted to make the numbers fit.

To find a document that would settle everything.

But she could not create evidence simply because uncertainty was uncomfortable.

"That sounds difficult."

"It is."

Liora smiled.

"But I have learned that being useful is not the same as being available for everything."

Seraphina looked at her.

Then she laughed.

"What?"

"Nothing. I think I needed to hear that."

Liora smiled.

"Perhaps we both did."

The afternoon session concerned the Academy's wider regional infrastructure programme.

This was where Seraphina's first condition began to fail.

The discussion started with the western conduit.

It moved to the northern storage facilities.

Then to the maintenance records of several older mana channels.

Then to the cost of replacing damaged regulators.

Finally, an official named Master Roderick Hale turned toward Seraphina.

"House Veyr has considerable experience maintaining regional infrastructure. Would the duchy be willing to participate in a broader inspection programme?"

Seraphina paused.

"What would participation involve?"

"Providing access to relevant sites and records."

"Would the Academy fund the inspections?"

"That would depend on the scope."

"Would House Veyr be expected to fund repairs?"

"That would also depend on the scope."

"Would the Academy provide a written estimate before requesting any expenditure?"

Master Hale looked uncomfortable.

"We would need to examine the individual circumstances."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then House Veyr can consider individual requests when they are submitted."

Master Hale frowned.

"Would that not delay the programme?"

"Possibly. But agreeing to a programme without knowing its costs would not make the costs disappear."

Professor Vale intervened.

"Perhaps we should distinguish between inspections and repairs."

"That would be helpful," Seraphina said.

Lucien looked toward her.

"We are not asking House Veyr to finance the entire programme."

"I understand. I am asking what is being requested."

There was a short silence.

Then Professor Vale nodded.

"An inspection partnership. No financial commitment at this stage."

"Then I can take the proposal back to my father."

Master Hale looked dissatisfied.

"Would it not be possible to provide a preliminary commitment today?"

Seraphina folded her hands.

"No."

The answer was quiet.

Not hostile.

Not apologetic.

Simply final.

She had learned something important from the previous months.

A request did not become reasonable merely because it was presented politely.

And a proposal did not become harmless because nobody had yet written down its cost.

Professor Vale closed the discussion.

"We shall prepare a formal proposal for House Veyr's consideration."

Seraphina inclined her head.

"Thank you."

The meeting moved on.

She had not saved the kingdom.

She had not destroyed the Academy's plans.

She had simply refused to sign an agreement she did not understand.

It was a satisfying afternoon.

As the review ended, Lucien approached her.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Master Arkwright."

"I wanted to thank you for the conditions you placed on the inspection."

Seraphina looked at him.

"You are thanking me for limiting your authority?"

"I am thanking you for making the responsibilities clear."

He glanced toward the map.

"Some landowners agree to access without considering the consequences for their workers. Others refuse access entirely because they fear losing control of their property."

"And House Veyr?"

"House Veyr has allowed an inspection without surrendering control of its land."

Seraphina considered that.

"That sounds like a very elaborate way of saying everyone has agreed to behave sensibly."

Lucien gave a tired smile.

"That is not always easy."

She believed him.

"Have you found the missing maintenance report?"

"Not yet."

"Then I hope you do."

"We may need to examine the Academy's older records."

"Good luck."

Lucien looked at her.

"You sound as though you expect difficulty."

"I have spent the last several weeks searching for records that were transferred between offices. I have become familiar with the concept."

He laughed.

"Then you may understand why I prefer experiments."

"Do your experiments have missing receipts?"

"Occasionally."

"Then I suggest you begin keeping them."

Lucien looked offended.

"I keep receipts."

"All of them?"

He paused.

Seraphina nodded.

"I thought so."

Liora approached before Lucien could answer.

"The review is finished?"

"For today," Seraphina replied.

"Then perhaps we can walk back together."

Seraphina agreed.

They left the chamber and crossed the courtyard.

Students were gathering near the fountain.

A group of apprentices had arranged several small metal objects on a bench.

One object emitted a faint blue glow.

Another made a soft clicking sound.

A third appeared to be doing nothing at all.

Seraphina watched it.

"What is that one?"

Liora glanced over.

"I believe it is supposed to measure mana density."

"It appears to be measuring nothing."

"Perhaps it is measuring very quietly."

Seraphina considered this.

Then she laughed.

It was the first time that day she had laughed without thinking about the consequences of doing so.

That evening, back at the Veyr residence, Seraphina received two letters.

The first was from Cedric.

The Agricultural Administration had located a second portion of the old distribution ledger.

Northbridge's receiving register had been found.

It confirmed that sixty measures had arrived during the emergency.

The delivery had been signed by the village administrator and witnessed by two local officials.

The grain had been distributed to households affected by crop failure.

The second letter concerned the remaining 105 measures.

A Treasury records clerk had located a reference to a separate reserve transfer.

The document did not state the destination.

It merely noted that the remaining grain had been retained pending further distribution instructions.

The final line was written in a different hand.

**Transfer authority to be confirmed by central committee.**

Seraphina placed the two letters beside each other.

Westmere: forty confirmed.

Northbridge: sixty confirmed.

Greyford: thirty recorded, confirmation pending.

Millhaven: twenty-five recorded, signature missing.

Alder's Crossing: quantity uncertain.

Remaining balance: 105 measures.

She added the new information to her ledger.

The investigation was progressing.

Slowly.

But it was progressing.

She looked at the second letter again.

The phrase *central committee* appeared in several of the old documents.

It was becoming clear that the temporary depot had not been the only office involved.

The original emergency committee had authorized the transfer.

The temporary depot had received the grain.

The Northern Provincial Supply Office had inherited reconciliation.

The Agricultural Administration had collected village confirmations.

The central reconciliation office had later been reorganized.

The Treasury had retained some records.

Each office held a portion of the story.

No single document contained the complete account.

Seraphina closed the ledger.

She had come to Aurelion to discuss a magical conduit.

She had spent the afternoon refusing an undefined financial commitment.

And now the old grain investigation had advanced by another sixty confirmed measures.

It was not the sort of progress that made for an impressive speech.

But it was progress.

Mira entered with a fresh cup of tea.

"Your mother has sent a message. She asks whether the review went well."

Seraphina took the cup.

"Tell her it was productive."

"Anything else?"

"Tell her the Academy wants to discuss a wider inspection programme."

Mira looked concerned.

"Does that mean you will be staying longer?"

"No."

Seraphina took a sip.

"At least, not unless someone gives me a very good reason."

Mira smiled.

"I shall tell Her Grace."

Seraphina looked toward the darkened window.

The capital was full of institutions.

Each had its own rules, responsibilities, records, and ambitions.

The Academy had invited her to a technical review.

She had discovered a wider programme.

She had refused to commit House Veyr to something undefined.

And she had made a few useful acquaintances.

That was enough for one visit.

She still had two days left before returning north.

Two days.

She could manage that.

Then, from somewhere in the residence, a servant called that a messenger had arrived from the royal palace.

Seraphina stopped drinking.

Mira looked toward the door.

The messenger was announced moments later.

He carried a sealed envelope bearing the royal crest.

Seraphina stared at it.

She had not met the Crown Prince since the spring reception.

She had not requested an audience.

She had not written to the palace.

She looked at the seal.

Then at Mira.

"Do not say anything."

Mira closed her mouth.

Seraphina broke the seal.

The letter was brief.

The Crown Prince requested her presence at a private administrative discussion the following morning.

The subject was the Academy's regional infrastructure programme.

Seraphina read the final line.

*Your observations concerning the distinction between inspection authority and financial responsibility would be valuable.*

She lowered the letter.

There went her second condition.

And possibly her third.

**End of Chapter 21**
