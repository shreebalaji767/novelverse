# Chapter 40 — The Spare Part That Had Found Another Home

The equipment transfer register arrived on a Tuesday.

Seraphina had begun to distrust Tuesdays.

Not because anything particularly terrible happened on them, but because the Royal Infrastructure Department seemed to have developed a habit of delivering inconvenient discoveries at the beginning of the working week.

The register was accompanied by three certified extracts, a technical inventory and a letter from Adrian Holt.

The letter contained one sentence that immediately attracted her attention.

*The transfer to N-4 appears to have been authorized under the regional emergency maintenance procedure.*

Seraphina read it twice.

Then she turned to the first extract.

The casing had been transferred from the western infrastructure reserve to Northern Relay Station N-4 twenty years earlier.

The receiving officer was listed as Alden Mercer.

She stopped.

Alden Mercer.

The former regional maintenance supervisor who had previously confirmed receiving equipment at W-2.

Seraphina looked at the date again.

The transfer had taken place before his later testimony about the W-2 equipment.

It was possible that the two records referred to different responsibilities.

She made a note not to assume otherwise.

Holt arrived that afternoon with the original transfer authorization.

"The regional authority found the document in a separate emergency maintenance file," he explained.

Seraphina examined it.

The authorization was brief.

It stated that N-4 required a replacement regulating casing after an inspection had identified damage to an existing assembly.

The western reserve was instructed to release one compatible spare.

The transfer was approved by the regional maintenance superintendent.

The receiving officer was Alden Mercer.

The document also contained a technical reference.

**Component RC-17.**

Seraphina turned to the inventory.

W-2's damaged casing had been recorded under the same component classification.

She looked up.

"Does that establish compatibility?"

"Not completely," Holt replied. "The classification covers several versions of the casing. The dimensions and internal fittings still need to be compared."

"Then we should obtain the original specifications."

"Lucien has already requested them."

Seraphina nodded.

"Good."

Holt placed another document on the desk.

"This is the N-4 receiving report."

She examined it.

The casing had been received in good condition.

It had been stored temporarily while the station's existing assembly was inspected.

There was no installation certificate.

No commissioning record.

No subsequent transfer entry.

Seraphina looked at the last page.

"Was it ever installed?"

"We don't know."

"Was it ever returned to the reserve?"

"No return has been recorded."

"Then we know it arrived."

"Yes."

"We know it was stored."

"Yes."

"We do not know what happened afterward."

Holt nodded.

"That is correct."

Seraphina closed the folder.

It was becoming a familiar pattern.

An object arrived somewhere.

A clerk recorded its arrival.

Then the object disappeared into a gap between administrative responsibilities.

Theodore convened a meeting two days later.

Lucien presented the technical findings.

"The RC-17 classification includes three casing designs. The W-2 component and the N-4 component belong to the same general series, but the fittings are different."

"Could the N-4 casing have been modified for W-2?" Theodore asked.

"Not without additional machining. It would have required an engineering approval."

"Could it have been done?"

"Technically, yes. But there is no evidence that it was."

Seraphina examined the technical drawings.

"Would the modification have been safe?"

"Only after testing."

"And would that testing have been recorded?"

"It should have been."

She nodded.

"Then we cannot treat the N-4 transfer as the missing W-2 component."

Lucien agreed.

Minister Garrick leaned back.

"Then why are we discussing it?"

"Because the transfer may explain why the regional reserve no longer had a suitable replacement for W-2," Seraphina said.

"Was it the only spare?"

"The inventory lists two."

"One went to N-4."

"And the other was transferred elsewhere."

"Where?"

"We do not know yet."

Garrick frowned.

"Could the second casing have been used at W-2?"

"Possibly. But the transfer record does not identify its destination."

Theodore looked at Holt.

"Find it."

Holt nodded.

The second transfer had a different authorization reference.

It was recorded as an ordinary regional equipment movement rather than an emergency allocation.

The receiving destination was abbreviated.

**W.R.S. — Central Maintenance Annex.**

Seraphina looked at the letters.

"Western Regional Stores?"

Holt checked his reference list.

"Possibly. The regional stores office used that abbreviation."

"Can we confirm it?"

"The original annex register is being located."

Seraphina placed the paper down.

"Then we should wait for the register."

Garrick looked impatient.

"We cannot wait indefinitely for every old ledger."

"No," she replied. "But we can wait long enough to avoid assigning a component to the wrong destination."

Theodore concealed a smile.

Garrick did not.

The committee's attention then turned to the original emergency procedure.

The procedure had been established to ensure that damaged magical infrastructure could receive replacement components without waiting for the full annual procurement cycle.

It allowed regional maintenance authorities to transfer compatible equipment between stations.

However, the procedure required three records.

First, a written request from the receiving station.

Second, approval from the regional maintenance superintendent.

Third, confirmation from the receiving officer that the component had arrived.

The N-4 transfer had all three.

The request came from the station's maintenance team.

The superintendent approved the movement.

Alden Mercer signed the receipt.

There was nothing obviously improper about it.

But the procedure also required a later installation or return report.

That report had not been found.

Lucien examined the technical specification.

"Even if the casing was never installed, it should have been returned to stores."

"Unless it was retained for future maintenance," Holt said.

"That required an extension."

"Was one issued?"

"Not in the documents we have."

Seraphina wrote it down.

The question was no longer simply where the casing had gone.

It was whether N-4 had properly accounted for it.

A letter from Alden Mercer arrived the following week.

He had been informed that the committee was examining the old transfer.

He remembered the delivery.

He also remembered why the casing had been requested.

N-4 had suffered damage during a maintenance inspection.

The station's original regulating assembly was not considered safe to use until the casing had been replaced.

The replacement component was delivered and stored.

Mercer recalled that it had remained in the station's maintenance cabinet.

He could not confirm whether it had ever been installed.

He also remembered that the station had been temporarily closed to further technical work because the required calibration equipment was unavailable.

Seraphina read the letter carefully.

"Does he identify the cabinet?"

Holt checked.

"Yes. The eastern maintenance room."

"Is it still there?"

"The station is still standing. The cabinet may have been removed."

"Who held the keys after Mercer transferred?"

Holt consulted the register.

"Peter Alden."

Seraphina looked up.

"Peter Alden?"

"Yes. He was appointed incoming maintenance supervisor."

She recognized the name from the earlier W-2 investigation.

Not Peter Hales.

Not Peter Hales, the old grain depot recipient.

Not Peter Hales, whose name appeared in a separate historical authorization.

Peter Alden was the successor to Mercer at the western maintenance authority.

The distinction was important.

"Is he still alive?"

"The personnel office is checking."

Seraphina nodded.

"Please ask him whether he remembers the cabinet."

The reply arrived sooner than expected.

Peter Alden was living in a town near the western regional road.

He had retired twelve years earlier.

He remembered N-4.

He remembered the maintenance cabinet.

And he remembered that it had been emptied.

The cabinet's contents had been transferred during a building reorganization.

The receiving location was listed as the Western Regional Stores Annex.

Seraphina read the letter.

The second casing had also been transferred to the same annex.

There was now a possible explanation for both movements.

The first casing had arrived at N-4 and remained in storage.

The second casing had been sent to the regional annex.

Both might have ended up in the same general inventory system.

But neither had yet been accounted for after the reorganization.

She called Holt.

"Do we have the annex inventory?"

"We are still searching."

"Please prioritize it."

"Yes."

That evening, Seraphina received a message from her mother.

Evelyne wanted her to attend a small gathering at the estate.

Several northern landowners would be present, along with two representatives from the merchant guild.

Seraphina read the invitation.

Then she read it again.

She had already attended two gatherings that month.

"Do I have to go?" she asked Mira.

Mira examined the invitation.

"Your mother has written that your presence would be appreciated."

"That is not the same as required."

"No, my lady."

"Then I shall ask her."

She wrote a short reply.

Evelyne's answer arrived before dinner.

*You may decline, but I would prefer you to attend. The merchant representatives have been asking about the regional infrastructure committee, and I would rather they hear an accurate explanation from someone involved than invent their own.*

Seraphina looked at the letter.

That was reasonable.

Unfortunately.

The gathering was held in the smaller reception room.

There were no elaborate decorations, no orchestra and no formal speeches.

Evelyne had deliberately kept the event modest.

Seraphina arrived just as a merchant representative was explaining the difficulties of transporting replacement equipment through the western roads.

The man was called Dorian Pell.

He had a broad face, a polished waistcoat and the confidence of someone accustomed to being listened to.

"Your Ladyship," he said, bowing. "We have heard that the Crown is reviewing the old relay stations."

"We are reviewing their records."

"Of course. A necessary distinction."

He smiled.

"I have long believed the kingdom should invest more heavily in regional magical infrastructure. The western stations could support trade, improve transport and reduce losses."

"That depends on their condition."

"Certainly. But the principle is sound."

Seraphina nodded.

Pell continued.

"My guild has several suppliers who could provide replacement components. We could arrange a regional procurement agreement."

"Have you examined the technical specifications?"

"Not yet."

"Then you cannot know whether your suppliers can provide compatible components."

His smile faltered.

"Naturally, the specifications would be reviewed."

"By the Academy?"

"That could be arranged."

"Before or after the agreement?"

Pell hesitated.

"Before, I should think."

"Good."

Evelyne watched the exchange with quiet interest.

Pell changed the subject.

"May I ask whether the committee expects to restore the stations?"

"No decision has been made."

"But surely the intention is to bring them back into service."

"The intention is to establish their actual condition and determine what remains necessary."

"That sounds rather cautious."

"It is."

Seraphina accepted a glass of water.

"An incomplete station is not improved by pretending it is complete."

Pell studied her.

"An interesting philosophy."

"It is also a useful accounting principle."

Later, Evelyne joined her daughter near the windows.

"You handled that well."

"He wanted a procurement agreement."

"He wanted an opportunity to sell equipment."

"Those are not necessarily the same thing."

"No," Evelyne agreed. "But they often arrive together."

Seraphina looked across the room.

Pell was speaking with one of the merchant guild representatives.

"Do you know whether his suppliers have experience with magical infrastructure?"

"Some of them, perhaps. The guild has several firms involved in metalwork and specialized fittings."

"Would they be suitable?"

"That depends on the specifications and the quality of their work."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the committee should not accept offers until the technical requirements are established."

Evelyne smiled.

"Your father would approve."

"That is unfortunate. I was hoping to avoid another committee."

"You may be disappointed."

The next morning, Holt sent an urgent message.

The Western Regional Stores Annex had been located.

Its records were incomplete, but the office had recovered an inventory book from the reorganization period.

The book listed a collection of equipment transferred from N-4.

Among the entries was a regulating casing.

The description was brief.

*RC-17, one unit, stored pending inspection.*

The entry was signed by the receiving clerk.

But the inventory number was different from the number assigned to the casing transferred from the western reserve.

Seraphina examined the two records.

One casing had gone to N-4.

Another had been transferred to the regional annex.

Both had been recorded under the same general component classification.

Only one had a confirmed receiving entry in the annex.

She looked at Holt.

"Can we establish which casing this was?"

"Not yet. The original equipment numbers were written in a separate index."

"Where is the index?"

"Missing."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

For a moment, she considered asking whether the kingdom had ever possessed a complete record of anything.

Instead, she opened them.

"Then we need the reorganization schedule."

Holt placed another paper on the desk.

"We found it."

The schedule showed that the annex had been reorganized under a new central stores system twenty years earlier.

Several equipment categories had been transferred to the Royal Logistics Department.

One of them was regulating assemblies.

The receiving department had acknowledged the transfer.

But the individual items were not listed.

Seraphina read the destination.

**Central Stores Division — Equipment Group Seven.**

She looked at the date.

The transfer had occurred eleven months after the N-4 casing was received.

It might have been among the items moved.

It might not.

But the records had finally provided another office to question.

The committee met the following day.

Theodore listened as Holt presented the findings.

"One casing was transferred to N-4 and stored pending inspection. A second was transferred to the Western Regional Stores Annex. During the reorganization, regulating assemblies were moved to the Central Stores Division."

"Can we confirm that both casings were included?" Theodore asked.

"No."

"Can we confirm that either was installed?"

"No."

"Can we confirm that either was returned to the reserve?"

"No."

Minister Garrick looked at the ceiling.

"How many times can the same object be transferred without anyone knowing where it is?"

Seraphina answered calmly.

"Apparently several."

Lucien examined the technical records.

"At least the N-4 entry establishes that a casing was received there."

"Yes," Seraphina said.

"And Mercer remembered it in the cabinet."

"Yes."

"Then the first casing existed at N-4."

"Correct."

Theodore looked toward Holt.

"Contact the Central Stores Division. Request the original equipment-group inventory, the reorganization schedule and any later issue or disposal records."

Holt wrote the instruction.

Seraphina added one more point.

"Also request the technical inspection history. If the casing was stored pending inspection, there should have been a reason for that condition to change."

Theodore nodded.

"Include it."

That night, Seraphina reviewed the updated W-2 summary.

The station had not been commissioned because its regulating assembly was incomplete.

A replacement component had been requested.

The regional reserve had recorded two spare casings.

One had been sent to N-4 under an emergency maintenance procedure.

Another had been transferred to a regional stores annex.

The N-4 casing had been stored pending inspection.

The annex had later undergone reorganization.

Regulating assemblies were transferred to Central Stores Division.

The individual equipment records were incomplete.

Seraphina put down her pen.

The investigation had moved beyond a missing report.

It was now tracing the history of equipment that had passed through several offices over two decades.

She glanced at the committee's list of six facilities.

Only three had been physically confirmed.

The records for the others were still scattered.

She had agreed to help with historical administration for three months.

The agreement had not included locating every missing magical component in the kingdom.

She checked the date.

Two months had already passed.

Seraphina stared at the calendar.

Then she reached for another biscuit.

There was no reason to panic.

After all, the committee had not yet asked her to find the equipment personally.

That was a small mercy.

A very small one.
