# Chapter 46 — The Approval That Had Never Been Granted

The Royal Academy had received the calibration reference.

It had not received the calibration report.

This distinction occupied the first paragraph of its reply.

Seraphina read it while eating breakfast.

She stopped halfway through her toast.

"Why do they keep sending us references to documents they don't have?"

Mira poured her tea.

"Perhaps they are trying to be helpful."

"That is a generous interpretation."

"The alternative is that they are not."

"That is the interpretation I am beginning to favor."

Mira placed the teapot down.

"Will you be leaving for the capital again?"

"Probably."

"Then I shall prepare your travelling case."

Seraphina looked at her.

"How did you reach that conclusion?"

"The letter is addressed to the committee, and you have not finished your breakfast."

Seraphina glanced at the toast.

Mira was right.

She disliked how often that happened.

The reference CTEB/EAST/44 belonged to the former Central Technical Evaluation Board.

The board had been responsible for examining equipment before regional installation teams were permitted to connect it to active magical infrastructure.

Its successor, the Technical Standards Directorate, retained the technical standards.

The Royal Academy retained research and calibration records.

The Infrastructure Department retained installation authorizations.

Three offices.

Three different responsibilities.

And one missing decision.

Lucien reviewed the Academy's response.

"The reference confirms that the equipment was submitted for calibration review."

"Does it confirm that calibration began?" Seraphina asked.

"No."

"Does it confirm that the equipment passed?"

"No."

"Does it confirm that the equipment was returned?"

Lucien shook his head.

"Not by itself."

Seraphina put down the letter.

"Then we need the original submission register."

Holt opened his notebook.

"The Academy says it may have been transferred to the Technical Standards Directorate."

"Then ask them."

He wrote down the request.

The Technical Standards Directorate was housed in a narrow stone building behind the Royal Academy.

It was not impressive.

Its corridors were lined with shelves, its windows were too small, and the reception desk had a permanent stack of forms.

Seraphina found the building strangely reassuring.

At least it looked like a place where someone might actually keep a record.

The director, Master Alistair Fenwick, received them in a room filled with old equipment catalogues.

He was a thin, serious man who spoke carefully.

"The former board's records were divided according to function," he explained. "We received technical standards, equipment specifications and inspection procedures. The Academy received research material and calibration references."

"Did you receive the E-1 equipment file?" Lucien asked.

Fenwick consulted a register.

"Not the complete file."

"What did you receive?"

"A technical specification and a preliminary inspection sheet."

"Was the equipment inspected?"

"Externally, yes."

"Was it calibrated?"

Fenwick turned a page.

"The record does not say."

Seraphina leaned forward.

"Does the Directorate have authority to approve calibration?"

"Not in the old system. The board conducted the review. We now certify compliance with technical standards, but calibration itself is performed by authorized specialists."

"Who performed it under the former board?"

"That depended on the equipment."

Fenwick took another document from a drawer.

"For regulating assemblies, the review usually required a technical examiner, a calibration specialist and a representative of the installation authority."

"Three people?"

"At minimum."

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

He nodded.

"That would be appropriate for a component intended to regulate a regional conduit."

Fenwick continued.

"The equipment had to be examined for internal alignment, pressure tolerance, mana response and compatibility with the intended installation."

"Could it have been installed before calibration?"

"Not safely."

Seraphina looked at the register again.

"Then the absence of approval is important."

"It is."

Fenwick found the technical specification.

It described the E-1 regulating assembly as a medium-capacity unit designed for a regional distribution junction.

The equipment had three principal components:

- An outer stabilizing casing.
- An internal alignment assembly.
- A regulating core.

The casing could be inspected separately.

The alignment assembly required specialized testing.

The regulating core had to be calibrated against the intended mana channel.

Lucien examined the specification.

"The equipment was not interchangeable with every station."

"Meaning?" Seraphina asked.

"It could be moved physically, but the calibration would have to be repeated for a different conduit."

"Would that explain why the casing sent to S-3 was not simply installed at another site?"

"Partly. The casing itself might be serviceable, but the complete assembly would still require technical approval."

Seraphina looked at Fenwick.

"Did the old board maintain records of equipment that failed calibration?"

"Yes."

"And equipment that passed?"

"Yes."

"Then the absence of a result does not tell us whether it failed or whether the review never happened."

Fenwick considered that.

"Correct."

Seraphina nodded.

That was the first useful answer of the morning.

The former board's submission register had been found in the Academy's archive.

It was delivered the following afternoon.

The entry for E-1 contained a date, an equipment number and a receiving signature.

The final column read:

*Assigned to Calibration Group Two.*

There was no result.

Lucien examined the handwriting.

"Group Two was responsible for medium-capacity regulating assemblies."

"Can we identify its members?" Theodore asked.

"Possibly. The personnel register should list them."

Holt requested the register.

The reply arrived the next day.

Group Two had consisted of four people.

One had died.

One had retired abroad.

One had transferred to the Western Technical Institute.

The fourth was still employed by the Royal Academy.

His name was Master Julian Harcourt.

Master Harcourt was not pleased to be summoned.

He arrived carrying a leather case and wearing an expression that suggested he had already decided the meeting would be unnecessarily long.

"I worked with the former board for six years," he said. "But I cannot remember every piece of equipment that passed through our rooms."

Seraphina appreciated the honesty.

"We are not asking you to remember everything."

Lucien placed the E-1 register before him.

Harcourt examined the reference.

"Yes. Group Two."

"Do you remember the assembly?" Lucien asked.

Harcourt adjusted his spectacles.

"Not from the number alone."

"Could you check your personal working records?"

"I retained some."

"Would you be willing to?"

Harcourt looked at the committee members.

"Provided I am given time."

Seraphina nodded.

"Of course."

He placed the register back on the table.

Then he hesitated.

"May I ask something?"

"Certainly," Theodore replied.

"Why are you examining this now?"

Theodore explained the committee's wider review.

Harcourt listened.

When the prince finished, the former examiner looked down at the document.

"I see."

He did not sound surprised.

He sounded tired.

Two days later, Harcourt returned with a small notebook.

It was not an official register.

The pages contained handwritten observations, equipment references and reminders.

He found E-1 after several minutes.

"Here."

Lucien leaned over.

Harcourt read aloud.

"Assembly received. Outer casing inspected. Alignment discrepancy noted. Calibration postponed pending replacement of internal guide."

Seraphina looked at the entry.

"Was a replacement requested?"

Harcourt turned the page.

"Yes. There is a request number."

He pointed to the margin.

**CTEB-RG-118.**

"Was the replacement supplied?" Lucien asked.

Harcourt checked the next line.

"Not according to my notes."

"Was the equipment returned to the site?"

"I don't know."

Seraphina examined the wording.

"Did the assembly fail calibration?"

Harcourt shook his head.

"No. It never reached calibration."

"Because of the internal guide?"

"Correct. The alignment discrepancy had to be corrected before we could test it."

Lucien looked at the notebook.

"Was the guide damaged?"

"Not necessarily. It was outside the permitted tolerance."

"Could it have been adjusted?"

"Possibly. But we did not have authorization to modify it."

"Why not?"

"Because the adjustment would have altered the assembly's operating characteristics. We needed approval from the manufacturer or the responsible engineering authority."

Seraphina nodded.

So the component had reached the board.

It had been inspected.

A defect had been identified.

The board had requested a replacement or authorization.

And then the trail stopped.

Holt located the replacement request.

It had been sent to the Eastern Regional Infrastructure Office.

The office had forwarded it to the Central Stores Authority.

The stores authority had acknowledged receipt.

But no issue record followed.

There was, however, a separate note.

*Replacement guide unavailable in standard stock. Special procurement required.*

Seraphina examined the note.

"Was a special order placed?"

Holt searched the procurement register.

There was no matching purchase order.

"Was the request cancelled?"

"No cancellation record."

"Was the equipment returned to the technical group?"

"Not in the available register."

Lucien looked at the original submission.

"Then it may have remained in the calibration workshop."

"Or been transferred somewhere else," Seraphina said.

Holt nodded.

"We should check the workshop inventory."

The former calibration workshop had been closed nine years earlier.

Its equipment had been transferred to the Royal Academy's eastern laboratory.

The laboratory's current director agreed to search the historical inventory.

The search took several days.

When the result arrived, it contained an unexpected entry.

**E-1 / ER-22-091 — Assembly retained pending replacement guide.**

The date was ten years after the original submission.

Seraphina read it carefully.

"Ten years?"

Lucien checked the date.

"Yes."

"Was the assembly still there?"

"The entry says retained."

"That is not confirmation of its current location."

"No."

The following line listed a transfer to the Eastern Technical Institute.

The reason was:

*Long-term storage and preservation.*

The transfer had been approved by the laboratory director.

The receiving signature belonged to Master Julian Harcourt.

Seraphina looked at the name.

"Was he still working there?"

Holt checked the personnel record.

"Yes. He was appointed temporary equipment custodian during the workshop closure."

Harcourt had not mentioned this.

But he had not been asked.

The committee requested the Eastern Technical Institute's storage records.

The institute replied that it still held the equipment.

It was stored in a sealed cabinet within the old calibration laboratory.

The cabinet had not been opened for several years.

Seraphina read the report twice.

"At least we know where it is."

Lucien looked relieved.

"That is useful."

"Can we inspect it?"

"Yes, but not immediately. We need to confirm the cabinet conditions and arrange a technical team."

"Could the assembly have deteriorated?"

"Possibly. It has been stored for years. We should not assume it remains serviceable."

Theodore examined the transfer record.

"Could it still be used at E-1?"

Lucien considered the question.

"Only after a complete inspection, correction of the alignment issue and calibration against the intended conduit."

"How long would that take?"

"I cannot estimate before seeing it."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then no one should promise that it can be returned to service."

Theodore agreed.

The visit to the Eastern Technical Institute was arranged for the following week.

The institute stood beyond the eastern walls of the capital, near a cluster of research buildings.

It was quieter than the Royal Academy.

Its grounds were clean, its corridors well maintained, and its laboratories were arranged around a central courtyard.

Master Harcourt accompanied the committee.

He appeared increasingly uncomfortable as they approached the old calibration building.

"Have you been here since the transfer?" Seraphina asked.

"Several times."

"Did you inspect the assembly?"

"No. I was responsible for the transfer, not the technical condition."

"Did you confirm the cabinet was sealed?"

"Yes."

"Was the cabinet inspected afterward?"

"Not regularly."

Lucien looked at him.

"Why not?"

"The institute had no active project involving the equipment. It was stored as an unresolved historical component."

Seraphina stopped walking.

"Who was responsible for checking its condition?"

Harcourt hesitated.

"The institute's storage department."

"Did it have technical staff?"

"Not assigned to that cabinet."

Seraphina looked at the door ahead.

"Then the equipment may have been stored without technical supervision."

"That is possible."

The cabinet was in a restricted room.

Two institute employees checked the seal before opening it.

The equipment was wrapped in protective cloth and secured to a wooden frame.

The outer casing appeared intact.

The internal alignment assembly was visible through a removable inspection panel.

Lucien examined the identification plate.

"ER-22-091."

He checked the original specification.

The number matched.

The assembly had not been replaced.

A small metal guide was missing from its designated position.

Harcourt examined the interior.

"That is the guide mentioned in my notebook."

Lucien nodded.

"Yes."

Seraphina looked at the empty fitting.

"Could it have been removed during inspection?"

Harcourt checked the maintenance label.

"It may never have been installed."

Lucien examined the surrounding mechanism.

"The alignment issue remains unresolved."

"Can you repair it?" Theodore asked.

"Not here. We would need to assess the component properly and determine whether the original guide can be manufactured or replaced with an approved equivalent."

Seraphina looked at the equipment.

"So it was never calibrated."

"Correct."

"Never installed."

"Correct."

"And never returned to Central Stores."

"Correct."

She considered the long chain of records.

The assembly had not disappeared.

It had simply remained in storage, waiting for a technical decision that had never been completed.

The institute's storage record contained one additional document.

It was a handwritten instruction from the former workshop director.

*Retain until responsible regional authority confirms whether installation remains required.*

There was no response.

No cancellation.

No return instruction.

No disposal authorization.

Seraphina looked at the date.

It had been written nine years earlier.

"Does the Eastern Regional Infrastructure Office still exist?" she asked Holt.

"Yes."

"Then we need to ask whether E-1 remains part of its current infrastructure plan."

Theodore nodded.

"And whether the equipment is still required."

Lucien looked at the assembly.

"That decision should be made only after a technical assessment."

"Agreed," Seraphina said.

She turned to the institute director.

"Please keep the equipment secured. No one should attempt to repair, move or install it without written authorization from the responsible technical authority."

The director nodded.

On the journey back to the capital, Theodore examined the updated register.

"E-1 is now better understood than the other facilities."

"Better understood is not the same as resolved."

"Of course."

"Construction was completed. Equipment was delivered. Calibration was postponed because a component was outside tolerance. The replacement was never supplied. The assembly was transferred to long-term storage."

Theodore looked at the page.

"Could the project be revived?"

"That is a technical and financial decision."

"You would not recommend it?"

"I would recommend assessing whether the facility is still needed before spending more money on it."

He nodded.

"That is reasonable."

Seraphina looked out of the carriage window.

The eastern countryside passed in quiet stretches of fields and low hills.

She had once imagined that avoiding a villainess's fate would involve staying away from royal politics, refusing invitations and never interfering with magical research.

Instead, she had spent months examining the consequences of decisions made by people who had retired, died or transferred to other departments.

It was not what she had planned.

But at least nobody had tried to make her fund a magical experiment.

Yet.

The next committee meeting began with a disagreement.

Minister Garrick wanted the E-1 equipment written off as obsolete.

Lucien objected.

"We have not completed a technical assessment."

"It has been stored for years."

"That does not establish that it is unusable."

"It has no approved installation plan."

"That does not establish that it cannot be used."

Seraphina listened.

Then she looked at the Treasury representative.

"How much would a full technical assessment cost?"

Mr Bellamy checked the preliminary estimate.

"Between one hundred and one hundred and fifty crowns, depending on whether replacement parts are required."

"And a new assembly?"

"Several thousand."

Garrick folded his arms.

"Then the assessment seems worthwhile."

Lucien nodded.

"Provided the equipment is not altered before inspection."

Seraphina looked at the register.

"Add a note that the equipment is in secure storage, uncalibrated, and not approved for installation."

Holt recorded it.

"Should we recommend a new technical assessment?"

"Yes. But the decision to fund it belongs to the responsible department."

Theodore looked at her.

"You are careful to avoid making recommendations beyond your appointment."

"I am trying to finish my appointment with the same number of responsibilities I started with."

Garrick gave a brief laugh.

"An admirable ambition."

After the meeting, Holt approached Seraphina.

"There is another matter."

She looked at him.

"Is it another building?"

"No."

"Good."

"It concerns your advisory appointment."

Seraphina sighed.

Holt handed her a letter.

The Crown Prince had requested a review of the committee's structure and proposed a limited extension of the historical records advisory arrangement.

The letter did not ask her to supervise the technical programme.

It proposed that she remain available for two additional months, with the same restrictions as before.

Seraphina read it.

"No implementation authority?"

"None."

"No financial authority?"

"None."

"No responsibility for technical decisions?"

"None."

"Maximum two meetings per month?"

"Yes."

She looked at Holt.

"Then why does the letter say 'expanded coordination support'?"

Holt paused.

"That wording was included in the first draft."

"Who wrote it?"

"I believe the Crown Prince's secretary."

Seraphina took out her pen.

She crossed out the phrase.

In its place, she wrote:

*Limited historical records advisory service, subject to the original restrictions.*

Holt looked at the correction.

"I can return this."

"Please do."

That evening, Seraphina received a letter from Liora.

It was not about the committee.

It was a personal note describing a visit to a small southern infirmary where the staff had finally received a proper storage cabinet for medical instruments.

The cabinet was not impressive.

It had two shelves, a lockable compartment and a separate section for sterilized equipment.

But the infirmary staff had been pleased.

Liora had written that the head nurse insisted on showing her every shelf.

Seraphina smiled.

She read the letter again.

Then she placed it on her desk.

The kingdom's problems were enormous.

Some involved old magical stations, missing equipment and decades of administrative confusion.

Others involved a small room, a broken cupboard and instruments that needed somewhere clean to be kept.

Neither kind of problem became less important simply because it was ordinary.

Seraphina folded the letter and placed it beside her books.

For the moment, she was content to leave the committee's documents closed.

Unfortunately, a messenger arrived before she could retire.

The letter was from the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Its subject line read:

**Historical Programme — Sixth Reserve Allocation.**

The Treasury had located the two original payment vouchers.

One was for Central Stores.

The other was for an engineering consultancy.

The receiving account belonged to a company that had been dissolved twenty years earlier.

Its name was:

**Veylan Works.**
