# **Chapter 74 — The Depot That Had Received Nothing It Could Confirm**

The letter from the Royal Military Council arrived on a Thursday.

Seraphina was in the middle of deciding whether the estate's winter curtains required replacement.

The answer, according to Margaret Ellison, was yes.

The answer, according to Seraphina, was that the curtains had survived three winters and were therefore demonstrating a degree of loyalty that should not be punished.

The letter interrupted this discussion.

Mira placed it beside the fabric samples.

"From the Royal Military Council, my lady."

Seraphina looked at the seal.

"That sounds expensive."

"It is an official letter."

"Those are frequently expensive."

She opened it.

The military office had completed its preliminary investigation into the missing cargo from the northern caravan.

The three wagons had been accounted for.

The cargo had not.

Thirty-eight crates had been recorded as loaded onto Wagon Fourteen.

The wagon had reached Alder's Crossing without its cargo.

The driver had reported that the crates were removed during a stop near the quarry road.

The military office had now identified a possible destination.

A depot in the western marches had received a shipment matching the general description of the missing goods.

The depot's receiving register, however, did not contain the caravan's number.

The military office requested confirmation from House Veyr because the northern route had crossed the duchy's roads.

Seraphina read the final paragraph.

*The Council would appreciate any information concerning the movement of military cargo through the northern region during the relevant period.*

She placed the letter down.

"That is a reasonable request."

Cedric, who had entered to discuss the estate's winter transport schedule, nodded.

"Do we have anything?"

"Not yet."

"Would you like me to ask the road office?"

"Yes. Only the records already held by the estate."

Cedric paused.

"Should we send someone to the western depot?"

"No."

"Even if the cargo might be there?"

"The military office has jurisdiction over its own depot."

Cedric smiled faintly.

"I thought you might say that."

Seraphina returned to the curtain samples.

"Please tell Margaret that the curtains may remain."

The northern road office replied the following afternoon.

Its records confirmed that the caravan had passed through Veyr territory on the scheduled date.

The caravan had used the eastern route after the western pass closed.

The road office had recorded the number of wagons at the northern checkpoint, but it had not inspected their cargo.

The military escort had been responsible for the load.

The office had also recorded a temporary halt near the quarry road.

The halt had lasted approximately forty minutes.

No road obstruction had been reported.

No accident had been recorded.

The road office had no authority to inspect military cargo unless the escort requested assistance.

Seraphina read the report.

"That is all we have."

Cedric nodded.

"We can send it to the Council."

"Yes."

"Would you like to include the names of the road officers?"

"Only those who signed the relevant entries."

Cedric made a note.

The report was sent.

The military office acknowledged receipt.

For several days, nothing happened.

Seraphina began to hope that the matter would remain where it belonged.

Then a second letter arrived.

The western depot had found a discrepancy.

Its receiving register showed an entry for thirty-eight crates of winter equipment.

The shipment had arrived on the same day as the missing caravan.

The crates had been delivered by a private transport company.

The receiving clerk had recorded them under a general supply designation.

The military office had therefore identified a possible match.

The depot's inventory, however, showed only thirty-four crates.

Four had been transferred to a field workshop.

The workshop's receiving record showed thirty-four crates.

The transfer document described the original shipment as thirty-eight crates.

The discrepancy was not between the two registers.

It was between the shipment's description and the depot's actual count.

Seraphina read the letter twice.

Then she put it down.

"Four crates."

Cedric nodded.

"Possibly."

"Possibly?"

"The military office has not confirmed that the shipment was the same one."

"Then why are they asking us?"

"They want to know whether the northern road records can establish the transport company's route."

Seraphina sighed.

The letter included the transport company's name.

It was **Harrow and Sons**, a regional carrier based in the western marches.

The company had provided a delivery receipt.

The receipt contained a wagon number.

It was not Wagon Fourteen.

It was Wagon Twenty-Six.

The military office wanted to know whether Wagon Twenty-Six had been part of the northern caravan.

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Was it?"

"The caravan's loading register lists twenty-one wagons."

"Wagon Twenty-Six was not among them."

"Correct."

"Then it was not part of the caravan."

Cedric nodded.

The carrier might have collected cargo elsewhere.

It might have delivered goods to the same depot.

It might have used the same road.

None of those facts established that it had transported the missing crates.

Seraphina wrote a brief reply.

The estate's records did not list Wagon Twenty-Six as part of the military caravan.

The road office had recorded no such wagon among the caravan's twenty-one vehicles.

The estate could not confirm whether the private carrier had transported any of the missing cargo.

She signed the letter.

"Send it."

The military office's next request was more specific.

It asked whether House Veyr could provide access to the quarry road's maintenance records.

The road passed through a narrow stretch of estate land before reaching the western boundary.

The estate maintained the surface.

The regional authority maintained the drainage structures.

The military office had used the road during several winter movements.

Seraphina agreed to provide the maintenance records.

She did not agree to conduct a new investigation.

The records showed that the quarry road had been inspected six weeks before the caravan passed.

The surface was stable.

A drainage ditch near the western bend had been cleared.

A retaining wall had been repaired.

No work had been performed on the relevant day.

The road supervisor, Martin Cole, had recorded a temporary obstruction near the quarry entrance.

It consisted of a fallen branch and loose stones.

The obstruction had been removed by the road crew.

The report did not mention military cargo.

Seraphina sent the records.

The military office acknowledged them.

Then it requested a statement from the road supervisor.

Seraphina declined to prepare the statement herself.

Martin Cole had inspected the road.

He could describe what he had seen.

He could not be expected to describe what he had not seen.

The military office accepted this distinction.

Martin submitted a short account.

He remembered the caravan passing.

He remembered the temporary obstruction.

He did not remember seeing any crates removed.

He had been working near the drainage ditch, several hundred yards from the stopping point.

His statement was signed and dated.

It was sent to the Council.

The matter might have ended there.

Instead, the military office sent an inspector to the estate.

He arrived on Monday morning.

His name was Captain Roland Ashford.

He was a narrow-faced man with a neat uniform, a careful manner, and the expression of someone who had been given a complicated assignment without being told which part of it was supposed to be complicated.

Seraphina received him in the estate's administrative room.

Cedric sat beside her.

Captain Ashford placed a folder on the table.

"Lady Seraphina, thank you for agreeing to meet."

"You requested the meeting."

"Indeed."

He opened the folder.

The missing cargo consisted of winter field equipment.

The crates contained replacement fittings, insulated signal components, and several sets of protective coverings.

The military office had initially assumed the cargo was intended for the western depot.

The depot's records suggested that a similar shipment had arrived.

But the transport company had not been part of the caravan.

The wagon number did not match.

The receiving clerk had used a general designation.

The inventory count differed.

The four missing crates had been transferred to a field workshop.

The workshop's records did not identify the original shipment.

Captain Ashford looked at Seraphina.

"We are attempting to establish whether these entries refer to the same goods."

Seraphina nodded.

"That is sensible."

"We have not concluded that they do."

"Also sensible."

Captain Ashford appeared relieved.

He had probably expected a noblewoman to be offended by the investigation.

Seraphina was not offended.

She was simply unwilling to become responsible for it.

The captain continued.

"The northern caravan's escort reported that the cargo was removed near the quarry road."

"Yes."

"The road records show a halt."

"Yes."

"The depot received a shipment matching the description."

"Possibly."

He paused.

"Do you believe the shipment was connected?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"I believe the records are insufficient to establish that."

Captain Ashford nodded.

"That is the difficulty."

He turned a page.

The military office had found another document.

A private carrier's dispatch note.

It listed a delivery to the western depot on the day after the caravan passed.

The delivery had been arranged by a local merchant.

The merchant had purchased winter equipment from a regional supplier.

The supplier's name appeared on the military caravan's procurement list.

The same supplier had also sold goods to private customers.

Seraphina examined the note.

"Was the private shipment inspected?"

"At the depot."

"Was it compared with the missing cargo?"

"Not at the time."

"Then the depot may have received unrelated goods."

Captain Ashford nodded.

"That is one possibility."

Seraphina placed the note down.

The situation was not mysterious.

It was merely incomplete.

The military office had a missing shipment.

The depot had a similar shipment.

The carrier was different.

The supplier was the same.

The crate descriptions were broad.

The inventory counts were inconsistent.

There was no direct evidence that the shipments were connected.

Captain Ashford closed the folder.

"We will continue the investigation."

"That seems appropriate."

He stood.

"Thank you for your cooperation."

Seraphina returned the courtesy.

Then she watched him leave.

Cedric remained seated.

"Do you think the cargo will be found?"

"I hope so."

"Do you think it was stolen?"

Seraphina looked at the closed folder.

"I think the military office has not yet established what happened."

Cedric nodded.

That was not a particularly dramatic answer.

It was, however, the only answer supported by the records.

The following week, the military office sent a short notice.

The western depot had completed its inventory.

The thirty-four crates transferred to the field workshop had been inspected.

They contained winter signal equipment.

The workshop had used four crates for repairs.

The remaining thirty had been returned to the depot.

The shipment had arrived through Harrow and Sons.

The supplier confirmed that the goods had been purchased by the western depot.

The military office had therefore ruled out that shipment as the missing caravan cargo.

The four crates had not been stolen.

They had been used for authorized repairs.

The missing cargo remained missing.

Seraphina read the notice.

"Good."

Cedric looked at her.

"Good?"

"The depot has accounted for its goods."

"But the caravan's cargo is still missing."

"That is unfortunate."

She folded the notice.

"But it is not our cargo."

Cedric smiled.

"That distinction may be the most useful thing you have said all week."

Seraphina looked at him.

"I have said several useful things."

"Of course."

She returned to the estate's accounts.

The military office had not found the missing crates.

The western depot had not received them.

The private carrier had not transported them.

The road records did not establish where they had gone.

The investigation would continue under the military office.

House Veyr had provided the records it possessed.

It had not searched the western marches.

It had not inspected military wagons.

It had not accused the carrier.

It had not declared the depot responsible.

The matter was still unresolved.

But the estate's role was finished.

For the moment.

That evening, Seraphina went to the northern courtyard.

The snow had begun to melt.

The road crews were repairing the last damaged sections.

A wagon carrying flour passed through the gate.

Another carried timber.

A third brought a load of lamp oil from the regional supplier.

The winter had not ended.

It had simply become less inconvenient.

Seraphina watched the wagons pass.

Mira stood beside her.

"Will the military office send another letter?"

"Probably."

"Will you answer it?"

"If it asks for information we possess."

Mira nodded.

A fourth wagon entered the courtyard.

Its driver stopped to speak with the gatekeeper.

The load consisted of sacks of oats.

The gatekeeper checked the receipt.

The driver checked the destination.

The storehouse clerk checked the number of sacks.

Three people confirmed the same delivery.

Seraphina watched the process.

It was ordinary.

It was dull.

It was exactly what she wanted.

Mira glanced at her.

"Are you pleased?"

"Yes."

"Because the delivery was correct?"

"Because nobody has asked me to inspect an underground chamber, investigate a military depot, or determine the fate of thirty-eight crates."

Mira smiled.

Seraphina turned toward the house.

The winter had not ended.

It had simply become less inconvenient.

The roads were open.

The grain stores were replenished.

The old chamber remained closed.

The military investigation continued elsewhere.

And the estate's ordinary work had returned to its proper scale.

Seraphina considered that a successful week.

Then the gatekeeper approached with another letter.

She stopped walking.

Mira looked at the envelope.

The seal belonged to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Seraphina stared at it.

"No."

The gatekeeper bowed.

"It was marked urgent, my lady."

Seraphina took the letter.

The first sentence read:

*The Department requests confirmation of House Veyr's availability for a regional inspection of winter mana regulation facilities.*

She closed her eyes.

The kingdom had apparently discovered that winter was not only cold.

It was also an excellent excuse to inspect everything that had been left underground for forty years.
