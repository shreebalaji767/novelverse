# Chapter 56 — The Storage Building That Had Become a Question

The storage building was not falling down.

This was the first thing Cedric reported.

The second was that it had become difficult to determine what the building was for.

Seraphina considered both statements.

"Is it still a storage building?"

"Technically."

"That sounds ominous."

"It contains storage."

"Then the technical definition is satisfied."

Cedric placed a small inventory book on her desk.

The building stood near the western pasture, between the Veyr road and the Bellmont boundary. It had been constructed forty-two years earlier as a shared storehouse for agricultural tools, fencing materials, spare cart parts, and emergency supplies.

The original agreement divided ownership equally.

House Veyr maintained the roof and exterior walls.

House Bellmont maintained the doors, interior fittings, and storage racks.

Both estates had access.

The arrangement had been sensible when both estates used the building.

Now, House Veyr used it almost exclusively.

Bellmont had moved most of its tools to a newer storehouse near the southern pasture. Its remaining property consisted of two wooden ladders, a set of pruning hooks, several iron brackets, and a box of nails that had apparently been forgotten during the move.

The building itself contained a great deal more.

Cedric opened the inventory.

"Fencing materials. Spare wheel parts. Three sacks of nails. Two damaged tools. Four wooden handles. A broken wheelbarrow. One old grain measure. Several unidentified iron fittings."

Seraphina looked at the list.

"Why are the iron fittings unidentified?"

"Because no one remembers what they belong to."

"Have they been examined?"

"Not yet."

"Then they are not unidentified. They are unexamined."

Cedric paused.

"That is a distinction."

"It is a useful one."

He wrote it in the margin.

Seraphina looked at the inventory again.

"Who uses the building?"

"Veyr's pasture workers. Occasionally the Bellmont steward sends someone to retrieve an item."

"Does Bellmont want continued access?"

"Yes."

"Does Bellmont want to maintain the interior?"

Cedric hesitated.

"That is less certain."

Seraphina leaned back.

The building was not in danger.

It was not empty.

It was not entirely neglected.

It had simply become a shared property in which one owner had acquired most of the practical responsibility while the other retained the legal obligation.

That was not necessarily a problem.

It was, however, an arrangement that deserved to be understood.

"Arrange a meeting with Bellmont," she said. "And have the inventory checked before anyone decides what to do with the building."

Cedric nodded.

"Would you like to attend?"

"No."

He looked unsurprised.

The meeting took place in the western storehouse.

Arthur Bell arrived with Master Gideon Shaw, the Bellmont steward, and a young clerk who carried three copies of the original agreement.

Cedric brought the inventory.

Seraphina attended only because Evelyne had suggested that a shared-property decision might benefit from the heir hearing both sides.

She sat at the end of the table.

The building's condition was discussed first.

The roof was sound.

The eastern wall required minor repair.

The door hinges were worn.

The interior racks were old but usable.

The floor had been damaged by repeated movement of heavy tools.

No urgent structural work was required.

Bell then explained Bellmont's position.

"We do not need the building for ordinary storage," he said. "Our southern storehouse is more convenient."

"Would you prefer to relinquish access?" Cedric asked.

"No."

"Why?"

Bell considered the question.

"Because the western pasture is still shared. During fencing repairs, the building is useful. If a cart breaks on the road, spare parts are close at hand. We also use it during the autumn harvest when the southern storehouse is too far away."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the building still has a purpose."

"Yes."

"Does Bellmont want to sell its share?"

"No."

"Does Bellmont want to lease its share to Veyr?"

Bell looked toward Shaw.

The steward shook his head.

"We have no reason to surrender ownership. The building serves both estates, even if Veyr uses it more often."

Cedric examined the agreement.

"Then the maintenance division remains?"

Bell frowned.

"Not exactly."

"Why?"

"The roof and walls are Veyr's responsibility. That has been manageable. But the interior fittings are no longer used equally. Bellmont's old racks are still there, but most of the current storage equipment belongs to Veyr."

Seraphina looked at the inventory.

"Then we need to distinguish the building from its contents."

Cedric turned a page.

"The agreement does not."

"It should."

The room fell quiet.

Seraphina continued.

"The building is jointly owned. The tools are not necessarily jointly owned. The racks may belong to one estate or the other. The maintenance obligations should apply to the structure and shared fittings, not automatically to every item placed inside."

Bell nodded.

"That would prevent future confusion."

Shaw looked relieved.

"We have already had one disagreement over a cart wheel."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Was it a shared cart wheel?"

"No. It belonged to Bellmont."

"Then why was there a disagreement?"

"Because it was stored in the Veyr building."

Seraphina closed her eyes briefly.

The kingdom was not going to collapse.

It was simply going to require an alarming number of people to understand that storage did not transfer ownership.

The inventory examination took place the following day.

The building was opened.

The workers removed the tools from the racks and placed them in separate groups.

Veyr property.

Bellmont property.

Shared equipment.

Unclaimed items.

The first three categories were easy.

The fourth was not.

The old grain measure had a mark from the Veyr estate but no recorded purchase.

The iron fittings had no identifying marks.

The broken wheelbarrow had been repaired twice, once by Veyr workers and once by Bellmont.

The wooden handles belonged to tools that no longer existed.

The sacks of nails had been purchased from a merchant whose name appeared in both estates' accounts.

Cedric examined the records.

"Some of these items may have been shared."

"May have been?" Seraphina asked.

"We cannot establish ownership from the records."

"Then what do we do?"

Cedric thought.

"Retain them as shared supplies?"

"That seems reasonable."

"Unless one estate claims them."

Seraphina looked at the broken wheelbarrow.

"Who wants it?"

No one answered.

"Then the wheelbarrow is not a source of conflict."

Master Hobb, who had been called to inspect the building's interior fittings, lifted the wheelbarrow's frame.

"It could be repaired."

Seraphina stared at it.

"Would anyone use it?"

"Possibly."

"Would repairing it cost more than replacing it?"

Master Hobb considered the question.

"Probably."

"Then it may remain broken."

The workers laughed.

The inventory was completed by afternoon.

The building's contents were divided into four categories:

1. Individually owned tools and materials.
2. Shared equipment used by both estates.
3. Unclaimed items retained temporarily.
4. Damaged items awaiting disposal.

The old grain measure was placed in the third category.

The iron fittings were examined and identified as spare brackets for the pasture gate.

The broken wheelbarrow was marked for disposal.

The wooden handles were retained as firewood.

The sacks of nails were divided according to the purchase records.

The building itself remained jointly owned.

Its purpose had not disappeared.

It had merely become smaller and more specific.

The revised agreement was brief.

The building remained shared property.

Both estates retained access.

Veyr would maintain the roof, exterior walls, and drainage.

Bellmont would maintain the doors and interior fittings, except where an estate installed or owned its own equipment.

Shared equipment would be listed separately.

Each estate could store individually owned tools, provided they were marked and recorded.

The building would be inspected annually.

The agreement would be reviewed every five years or whenever the shared use changed substantially.

The old agreement would remain in the archive.

The new schedule would be attached to it.

Seraphina read the final draft.

"Does this resolve the cart wheel?"

Cedric looked up.

"Yes."

"Does Bellmont agree?"

"Yes."

"Does Veyr agree?"

"Yes."

"Then I approve it."

She signed.

The building's ownership had not changed.

The maintenance responsibilities had not changed dramatically.

The difference was that the arrangement now described how the estates actually used the property.

Seraphina found this preferable to inventing a new dispute simply because an old document had become inconvenient.

That evening, Cedric brought the completed shared-property register.

It had grown to thirty-one pages.

Seraphina looked at the cover.

"Why?"

"Because the storage building required an inventory."

"Why did the inventory require thirty-one pages?"

"It did not. The register contains all six properties."

She opened it.

The orchard's watercourse had a temporary maintenance arrangement.

The mill had a revised operating schedule.

The bridge had a structural inspection plan.

The pasture road had a restored boundary marker.

The drainage channel had been cleared and assigned maintenance.

The storage building had a new inventory and shared-use schedule.

Each entry contained the original agreement, current conditions, responsible parties, and the next review date.

The register did not claim that every problem had been permanently solved.

It recorded what had been done.

It recorded what remained.

It recorded who was responsible.

Seraphina turned to the last page.

There was a summary.

Six shared properties.

Six agreements.

Four repairs completed or scheduled.

Two arrangements still under review.

No unresolved ownership dispute.

No immediate safety concern.

No outstanding emergency expenditure.

She closed the register.

"Good."

Cedric smiled.

"That is the first time you have approved the entire document without finding something to correct."

"I found several things."

"You did not mention them."

"They were minor."

Cedric looked suspicious.

"Such as?"

"The heading on page twelve is inconsistent."

He sighed.

Seraphina handed the register back.

"Correct it before the next copy."

The next morning, Alaric summoned Cedric and Seraphina to the estate council room.

The Duke had reviewed the register.

He had also received a request from the regional agricultural office asking whether House Veyr intended to conduct a broader review of shared roads, watercourses, and storage facilities.

Alaric placed the letter on the table.

"They have noticed our work."

Seraphina looked at the letter.

"Why?"

"Because the bridge inspection was recorded in the regional road register. The pasture road survey was also submitted."

"That seems reasonable."

"They would like to know whether we are willing to share the inspection format."

Cedric looked interested.

"It could help neighboring estates."

Seraphina considered the request.

The format was not particularly complicated.

It separated ownership, access, maintenance, current use, condition, and outstanding obligations.

It did not require a royal committee.

It did not require a new office.

It did not require Seraphina to attend meetings.

"Send them a copy," she said.

Alaric nodded.

"Would you like to explain the process?"

"No."

Cedric smiled.

"I can."

"Good."

The Duke looked at his daughter.

"You understand that this may encourage other estates to review their arrangements."

Seraphina considered that.

"It may."

"Some will discover problems."

"Then they should inspect their properties."

"Some may ask for assistance."

"Then they should ask Cedric."

Alaric leaned back.

"Your name may become associated with the review."

Seraphina stared at him.

"That is unfortunate."

Evelyne, who had been listening from the doorway, laughed.

Alaric smiled.

The letter was answered that afternoon.

House Veyr would share the inspection format.

It would not supervise other estates.

It would not assume responsibility for their agreements.

It would not establish a permanent advisory office.

The regional agricultural office was free to adapt the format.

Seraphina approved the wording.

Then she returned to her study.

A week later, the first neighboring estate requested a copy.

The second requested a meeting.

The third sent a letter asking whether the format could be used for a shared drainage system.

Cedric handled all three.

He explained the register.

He sent the forms.

He advised the estates to begin with properties they already knew were in need of attention.

Seraphina did not attend.

She was occupied with a much smaller matter.

The kitchen had discovered that the estate's winter apple stores were lower than expected.

The missing quantity was not large.

It was enough to require a revised menu.

Seraphina examined the storage record.

The apples had been counted at harvest.

They had been counted again after the first month.

The difference was explained by spoilage, kitchen use, and a delivery to the infirmary.

The infirmary delivery had been recorded as a gift.

The kitchen had recorded it as a transfer.

The storehouse had recorded it as a loss.

Seraphina looked at the three entries.

Then she called for Thomas Hale.

"How many apples were sent to the infirmary?"

"Forty baskets."

"How many were used?"

"Thirty-six."

"How many were spoiled?"

"Four."

"Then why does the storehouse record show a loss?"

Hale looked at the book.

"Because the transfer was not entered in the storehouse ledger."

Seraphina closed it.

"Correct the record."

Hale nodded.

The apples had not vanished.

They had simply been counted by three departments that used three different words for the same event.

Seraphina looked toward the window.

The estate was becoming more orderly.

Not because she had discovered a secret.

Not because she had defeated an enemy.

Because people were finally recording what they did.

She found this deeply satisfying.

And, as usual, deeply inconvenient.

There were still more records to correct.
