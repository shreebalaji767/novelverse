# **Chapter 73 — The Furnace That Had Been Warm for Forty Years**

The Royal Academy's letter contained a request for access to an abandoned magical facility.

Seraphina read it over breakfast.

Then she read it again.

The facility was located beneath the northern road, approximately six miles from Westmere.

It had been constructed during an earlier period of regional infrastructure development.

The Academy believed it might contain a dormant mana-regulation chamber.

The chamber had reportedly been used to stabilize local magical circuits during severe winter conditions.

The Academy wished to inspect it.

Seraphina placed the letter beside her plate.

"No."

Mira blinked.

"Should I prepare a reply?"

"Yes."

"What should it say?"

"That House Veyr has not authorized access to the site."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina continued.

"Ask them to identify the legal basis for the request, the responsible authority, the purpose of the inspection, and whether the facility is still connected to any active circuit."

Mira wrote the instructions.

Seraphina took another bite of breakfast.

The letter had arrived at an inconvenient time.

The northern roads were still recovering from the winter disruption.

The military office was investigating missing cargo.

The grain stores needed replenishment.

And now the Academy wanted to inspect something underground.

She looked at the request again.

The facility's name was **Northern Thermal Regulation Chamber, NTR-4**.

The Academy had described it as a possible source of historical information about winter mana distribution.

It had not confirmed whether the chamber was active.

It had not confirmed whether the chamber was safe.

It had not confirmed whether the chamber belonged to House Veyr.

Seraphina disliked the word *possible* when it appeared beside the word *underground*.

She returned the letter to Mira.

"Send it today."

The Academy replied three days later.

It apologized for the incomplete request.

The chamber was believed to be inactive.

Its original purpose had been to regulate mana flow through a network of small agricultural and transport circuits.

The system had been discontinued decades earlier.

The Academy had discovered a reference to the chamber while reviewing old winter maintenance records.

It wanted to determine whether the facility could provide information about the kingdom's earlier methods of mana regulation.

The request was not for immediate restoration.

It was for a preliminary inspection.

The Academy also acknowledged that ownership and access rights had not been established.

Seraphina read the reply.

"That is better."

Cedric, seated across from her, examined the attached map.

"The chamber appears to be beneath the old northern road."

"Does the map show an entrance?"

"Two."

"Are they still open?"

"The Academy does not know."

Seraphina looked at him.

"How did it discover a facility without knowing whether the entrances exist?"

Cedric considered this.

"Through an old maintenance register."

"Of course."

The register listed the chamber as NTR-4.

It also mentioned a nearby surface structure called the **Winter Service House**.

The service house had been removed from the regional property map forty years earlier.

The chamber itself had remained in the infrastructure index.

Seraphina examined the map.

"Who owns the land?"

"House Veyr."

"Who owns the chamber?"

"Unknown."

"Who maintained it?"

"Unknown."

"Who disconnected it?"

"Unknown."

She looked at Cedric.

"Then the Academy is correct to ask before entering."

Cedric nodded.

The request would need to be reviewed.

But the matter was no longer simply a question of refusing access.

The facility might be important.

The Academy had discovered it while studying winter infrastructure.

If it had been designed to regulate mana flow during cold weather, its records could help explain why certain northern circuits became unstable during severe winters.

That was a legitimate question.

Seraphina did not want to become responsible for answering it.

She did, however, want the Academy to answer its own questions properly.

"Ask Lucien to provide the technical description."

Cedric nodded.

"And ask the regional land office to identify the ownership records."

"Yes."

Seraphina paused.

"Do not ask anyone to open the chamber."

Cedric smiled.

"I will make that explicit."

Lucien Arkwright arrived at the estate the following week.

He carried a leather case, three rolled diagrams, and the expression of a man who had recently discovered an interesting problem.

Seraphina recognized the expression.

It was the same one he had worn when he found the old western conduit.

She disliked it.

Lucien bowed.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Master Arkwright."

He placed the diagrams on the table.

"The chamber may be more important than the Academy initially believed."

Seraphina looked at him.

"That is not an encouraging opening."

Lucien smiled.

"I assure you, I am not asking to activate it."

"Good."

He unrolled the first diagram.

The Northern Thermal Regulation Chamber had been designed as a distribution point.

It did not generate mana.

It did not create heat independently.

It redirected and stabilized mana supplied by a network of local circuits.

The chamber had contained three regulating assemblies, a central control ring, and a set of pressure-balancing conduits.

Seraphina examined the diagram.

"Pressure?"

Lucien nodded.

"Not physical pressure in the ordinary sense. Mana pressure. The term was used to describe the difference in flow between connected circuits."

"Could the chamber become dangerous if the flow were unstable?"

"Yes."

"Could it remain dangerous after being disconnected?"

"Possibly."

Seraphina placed the diagram down.

"Then we are not opening it."

Lucien nodded.

"Agreed."

He unrolled the second diagram.

The chamber had been connected to four smaller circuits.

One served an agricultural district.

One served a transport station.

One supplied a maintenance workshop.

The fourth was described as a reserve circuit.

The Academy had no record of the reserve circuit's final destination.

Seraphina looked at the diagram.

"How old is this?"

"Approximately fifty years."

"Was it inspected?"

"Several times."

"Was it maintained?"

"According to the register, yes."

"Was it disconnected?"

Lucien hesitated.

"The register says it was placed in a dormant state."

Seraphina looked at him.

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

He opened his case.

"The final maintenance entry is forty years old."

Seraphina leaned back.

"Then the chamber has been dormant for forty years."

"Presumably."

"Presumably."

Lucien smiled.

"You are very difficult to reassure."

"I am very easy to reassure. You simply need to know what happened."

He laughed.

Then he became serious.

"The Academy believes the chamber may explain a pattern of winter instability in the northern circuits."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Which circuits?"

"Several agricultural and transport systems."

"Are they currently active?"

"Some."

"Are they connected to this chamber?"

"Not according to the current maps."

"Then why is the chamber relevant?"

Lucien considered the question.

"Because the old system may have influenced the design of the newer circuits."

Seraphina nodded slowly.

That was a reasonable possibility.

It was not a reason to open an abandoned chamber.

The land office replied two days later.

The chamber stood on Veyr land.

The surface service house had been removed from the property register after a road reconstruction.

The underground facility had not been transferred.

Its ownership was therefore unclear.

The original construction agreement assigned the chamber's structural maintenance to the regional infrastructure authority.

The Academy had been responsible for technical inspection.

The local maintenance office had been responsible for routine monitoring.

The final record showed that the chamber had been placed in a dormant state after the reserve circuit failed.

It did not record who had disconnected the remaining connections.

Seraphina read the document.

"Three authorities."

Cedric nodded.

"At least."

"One owns the land."

"Yes."

"One maintained the structure."

"Yes."

"One inspected the equipment."

"Yes."

She looked at the final page.

"And none of them recorded who disconnected it."

Cedric sighed.

"It appears so."

Seraphina placed the document down.

The chamber had been inactive for forty years.

The land belonged to House Veyr.

The structure belonged to an unclear administrative arrangement.

The technical records were held by the Academy.

The maintenance records were held by the regional authority.

The disconnection record was missing.

It was a familiar problem.

But this time, the facility was underground.

That made the missing information considerably less amusing.

The Academy requested a joint inspection.

Seraphina agreed to a preliminary surface survey.

She did not agree to opening the chamber.

The inspection would include:

- Confirmation of the physical location.
- Examination of the entrances.
- Assessment of the surrounding ground.
- Identification of any visible mana conduits.
- Verification of land and maintenance responsibilities.

No equipment would be activated.

No internal chamber would be entered without a separate safety assessment.

Lucien accepted the conditions.

The regional infrastructure authority appointed an engineer.

The land office sent a surveyor.

House Veyr sent Master Armitage.

Cedric attended as the estate's representative.

Seraphina did not go.

She had learned that an inspection could proceed without her standing beside the people conducting it.

The first report was encouraging.

The chamber's location had been confirmed.

The northern entrance was blocked by collapsed masonry.

The southern entrance was partially visible beneath a slope.

The surface structure no longer existed.

The surrounding ground showed no signs of recent movement.

No active mana flow had been detected at the surface.

The surveyor found the original boundary markers.

The chamber lay entirely within Veyr land.

The engineer also found a small maintenance shaft.

It had been sealed with stone and mortar.

The Academy's diagrams showed that the shaft had once connected to the central control ring.

Lucien examined the photographs.

"The seal appears to be original."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Can you confirm that?"

"Not without closer inspection."

"Then do not."

Lucien nodded.

The report recommended that the entrances remain closed until the chamber's internal condition could be assessed.

The regional authority agreed.

House Veyr agreed.

The Academy agreed.

For once, three institutions had reached the same conclusion without requiring a committee to explain why.

Seraphina was almost pleased.

The second report was less encouraging.

The Academy's historical records showed that NTR-4 had been connected to a larger winter regulation network.

The network had included five facilities.

Three were confirmed.

One had been dismantled.

The fifth was listed only in a maintenance schedule.

The chamber had not operated alone.

It had been part of a system.

The original network was designed to stabilize mana flow during severe cold, when certain circuits became less predictable.

The system had been discontinued after several components failed.

The Academy had assumed that all five facilities were inactive.

The regional authority's records, however, showed that two of the smaller circuits had been transferred to later agricultural infrastructure.

Their current status was uncertain.

Seraphina read the report.

"Transferred?"

Lucien nodded.

"Possibly."

"Where?"

"The records identify the districts, but not the exact installations."

"Are those circuits active?"

"We do not know."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then the Academy should identify them before investigating the old chamber."

Lucien agreed.

The new inquiry would begin with the two surviving circuit records.

The chamber itself would remain closed.

The first surviving circuit was located near Westmere.

It supplied a small agricultural mana network used to regulate water distribution during the winter.

The network was still active.

It had been maintained by the regional agricultural office.

The old chamber's records showed that the circuit had once been connected to NTR-4.

The current network had been redesigned twenty-six years earlier.

Its connection to the chamber had been removed.

The second circuit was located near the northern transport station.

It had been used to stabilize a signal relay during winter.

The relay had been replaced by a newer system.

The old circuit was inactive.

Its equipment had been removed.

The Academy confirmed that neither surviving circuit depended on NTR-4.

The chamber was isolated.

The question of whether it could be safely inspected remained.

But the immediate concern was resolved.

Seraphina read the confirmation.

"Good."

Cedric looked at her.

"Are you relieved?"

"Yes."

"Because the chamber is inactive?"

"Because we know which circuits are not connected to it."

He smiled.

"That is a distinction."

"It is a useful one."

The Academy then discovered something unexpected.

The chamber's original maintenance register contained a reference to a winter emergency procedure.

It described the use of a reserve regulating assembly when one of the smaller circuits failed.

The procedure required the assembly to be installed by a qualified engineer.

It also required the chamber to be inspected before activation.

The reserve assembly had been stored in a regional maintenance depot.

The register recorded that it had been issued forty years earlier.

No return entry existed.

Lucien brought the record to Seraphina.

She looked at it.

"Do we know where the assembly went?"

"No."

"Was it installed?"

"Unknown."

"Was it returned?"

"Unknown."

She placed the register down.

"Then we have another missing object."

Lucien sighed.

"I was afraid you would say that."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because you are going to ask for the inventory."

"Yes."

He smiled.

The Academy had already requested it.

The regional authority was searching its stores.

The assembly might have been transferred to another facility.

It might have been dismantled.

It might still be in a warehouse.

There was no reason to assume it had been stolen.

But the missing return entry mattered.

The chamber had been placed in a dormant state.

The reserve assembly had been issued.

The final disconnection record was missing.

The assembly's location was unknown.

The winter network had been discontinued.

Seraphina looked at the documents.

The pattern was familiar.

Not because it proved wrongdoing.

Because it showed how easily a technical system could outlive the records that explained it.

The regional authority found the assembly three days later.

It was stored in a sealed equipment room at the northern maintenance depot.

The inventory number matched.

The outer casing was intact.

The internal components had not been inspected recently.

The assembly had been transferred to the depot after the winter network was discontinued.

The receiving register had recorded it as reserve equipment.

The final maintenance register had not been updated.

The object had not disappeared.

It had simply been recorded in one place and forgotten in another.

Lucien examined the inventory report.

"The assembly is intact."

"Can it be used?"

"That requires a technical inspection."

"Will it be used?"

"No."

Seraphina nodded.

The Academy had no intention of restoring the old network.

The assembly would remain in storage until its technical status was established.

The chamber would remain closed.

The surviving circuits would continue under their current maintenance arrangements.

The historical records would be corrected.

The inquiry had not discovered a hidden magical catastrophe.

It had discovered an abandoned system, several incomplete records, and a piece of equipment that had been properly stored but incorrectly indexed.

Seraphina considered that a reasonable result.

The final report was issued at the end of the month.

It confirmed:

NTR-4 existed.

The chamber was located on Veyr land.

Its structure had been maintained by the regional authority.

Its technical records belonged to the Academy's historical archive.

The chamber had been placed in a dormant state approximately forty years earlier.

Its active connections had been removed or redesigned.

The surviving agricultural and transport circuits no longer depended on it.

The reserve regulating assembly had been located in the northern maintenance depot.

The assembly had not been installed.

The chamber's internal condition remained unverified.

No activation was recommended.

No immediate restoration was proposed.

The report also recommended that the chamber's ownership, maintenance responsibility, and closure status be formally recorded.

Seraphina read the conclusion.

"That is acceptable."

Cedric looked at her.

"Would you like to sign the estate's acknowledgment?"

"Yes."

She signed.

The Academy thanked House Veyr for allowing the surface survey.

The regional authority agreed to update its maintenance register.

The land office agreed to record the chamber as an inactive underground infrastructure site.

The reserve assembly would remain in the depot.

The matter was closed for the present.

That evening, Lucien sent Seraphina a final note.

It was short.

The Academy had identified two additional historical references to winter regulation facilities.

One appeared to concern a southern agricultural circuit.

The other was located near the western marches.

Neither had been investigated.

Lucien wrote that the Academy intended to review the records before making any further requests.

Seraphina read the note.

Then she placed it in the archive.

Mira entered.

"Another letter, my lady?"

"No."

Mira looked at the note.

"Is the chamber safe?"

"Unknown."

"Will it be opened?"

"Not unless the Academy can establish that it is safe."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina leaned back.

The chamber had been dormant for forty years.

The reserve assembly had been found.

The active circuits were independent.

The records were being corrected.

There was no need to open an underground facility simply because it existed.

She considered this a satisfactory outcome.

Then Cedric entered with a new message.

Seraphina looked at him.

"Please tell me it is not another facility."

Cedric unfolded the letter.

"It concerns the western border."

She stared at him.

The letter was from the Royal Military Council.

The northern supply caravan had been accounted for.

The missing cargo had not.

The investigation had now identified a possible destination for the crates.

A military depot near the western marches.

Seraphina closed her eyes.

The kingdom had apparently decided that winter would be an excellent time to connect every unresolved problem to every other unresolved problem.

She opened them again.

"Is the depot ours?"

Cedric checked the letter.

"No."

"Good."

She returned to her chair.

"Then the military office can investigate."

Cedric smiled.

Seraphina picked up her tea.

The chamber beneath the northern road would remain closed.

The winter circuits would remain inactive.

The reserve assembly would remain in storage.

And she would remain exactly where she wanted to be.

At home.

With a cup of tea.

And no reason to enter a tunnel.
