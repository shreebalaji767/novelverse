# **Chapter 33 — The Engineer Whose Name Was Crossed Out**

The first thing Seraphina learned about Darius Veylan was that nobody could agree on how to spell his name.

The second thing she learned was that this was not unusual.

Roland's initial search produced three references:

- Darius Veylan, listed as a supervising engineer in the Royal Infrastructure Programme.
- D. Veylan, listed in a procurement correspondence concerning the western junction.
- Darius Veyland, mentioned in an old regional engineering register.

The final letter differed.

Seraphina examined the three documents.

"Are these the same person?"

Roland stood across the desk.

"We cannot confirm that yet."

"Do the dates match?"

"Two of them do. The third is dated four years later."

"Could the spelling difference be an error?"

"Certainly. But we should not assume that."

Seraphina nodded.

"Do we have a personal record?"

"Not yet."

"An employment record?"

"Only the programme assignment."

"Then we have a name, an assignment and two possible references."

Roland smiled faintly.

"That is correct."

Seraphina placed the documents together.

"At least we have something to search."

The Veylan Works procurement records were kept in the regional estate archive.

Roland had requested access to the remaining files, including the original contractor agreements.

The first agreement concerned the western junction modification.

It identified Veylan Works as the contracted supplier.

The work involved supplying regulating components, replacing damaged mounting equipment and adjusting the distribution circuit.

The document listed Darius Veylan as the technical representative.

The second agreement was incomplete.

It concerned the construction of a maintenance station.

The location was missing from the surviving copy.

The third document was a payment authorization.

It referred to the same contractor but contained no individual name.

Seraphina examined the papers.

"Does the construction agreement have a project reference?"

Roland checked.

"Yes. W-2/24."

"Western Relay Station?"

"Possibly."

"Is there a completion certificate?"

"No."

"An inspection certificate?"

"No."

"An equipment receipt?"

"Not in this file."

Seraphina looked at the second agreement again.

"Then we cannot yet establish whether Veylan Works completed the station."

Roland nodded.

"Correct."

She placed the document beside the first agreement.

"Do we know whether the company was still operating after the programme was reorganized?"

Roland hesitated.

"That is what I am trying to establish."

The next person they consulted was Master Oren Whitlock.

He arrived at the estate two days later.

He was older than when Seraphina had first met him, but his memory for engineering arrangements remained sharp.

He examined the name.

"Darius Veylan."

"Do you remember him?" Seraphina asked.

Whitlock took his time answering.

"I remember the family name."

"Not the man?"

"I may have met him. I cannot say that I remember him clearly."

Seraphina waited.

Whitlock examined the procurement reference.

"Veylan Works supplied components for the western junction. I remember that much."

"Was Darius Veylan responsible for the work?"

"He was listed as a technical representative."

"Was he the supervising engineer?"

Whitlock shook his head.

"No. The supervising engineer would have been appointed by the regional office. The contractor's representative was responsible for the supplier's work."

Seraphina nodded.

"Do you remember the engineer?"

Whitlock looked at the document again.

"Possibly. But I would rather not guess."

"That is fair."

He leaned back.

"There were several engineers involved in the programme. Some worked for the regional office. Others were assigned temporarily by the Academy. A few were contracted privately."

"Was Darius Veylan one of the private engineers?"

"I cannot confirm that."

Seraphina thanked him.

It was not much.

But it was an important distinction.

The name appeared in the contractor records.

That did not make the person the supervising engineer.

The Academy's surviving personnel register arrived the following week.

Lucien sent a copy with a note explaining that the original had been damaged.

The document contained the names of engineers assigned to the six facilities.

Northern Relay Station N-4 was assigned to Master Oren Whitlock.

Western Relay Station W-2 was assigned to an engineer named **Darius Veylan**.

Eastern Road Maintenance Station E-1 was assigned to an Academy engineer.

Southern Circuit Storehouse S-3 had two names listed.

Ashenford Relay Station A-2 had one.

The sixth facility had no confirmed assignment.

Seraphina examined the entry.

The same name appeared.

This time, the spelling was clear.

Darius Veylan.

She turned to the next page.

The register contained a note beside his assignment.

*Temporary transfer pending final inspection.*

There was no date of return.

No final inspection entry.

No formal closure.

She read the line again.

"Temporary transfer," she murmured.

Mira looked over.

"What does that mean?"

"It means he was assigned elsewhere."

"Where?"

"That is what we do not know."

Seraphina placed the register beside the procurement agreement.

The documents now established that Darius Veylan had been assigned to Western Relay Station W-2.

They also established that Veylan Works had supplied components for the western junction.

Whether the engineer and the contractor were connected remained uncertain.

They might have been related.

They might have been the same person.

They might have been separate people with the same name.

She would not decide without evidence.

Lucien's next letter explained that the Academy had located a personnel transfer reference.

It was a short entry in a regional engineering register.

Darius Veylan had been temporarily assigned to a project in the western marches.

The transfer date was recorded.

The destination was not.

The register contained a second note:

*Assignment extended pending completion of regional inspection.*

No further entry followed.

Seraphina read the letter.

"How many years ago?" she asked.

Mira checked the date.

"Twenty-two."

Seraphina leaned back.

Twenty-two years.

The same period in which the original engineering office had been dissolved.

The same period in which the maintenance programme had been reorganized.

The same period in which several facilities had disappeared from current records.

It was a pattern.

But a pattern was not proof of a connection.

She wrote to Lucien.

*Please confirm whether the transfer was authorized by the regional engineering office or the Academy. Also establish whether any completion report was received. We should not treat the absence of a return entry as evidence that the engineer remained assigned to the project indefinitely.*

She sealed the letter.

The next discovery came from Roland.

He had found a company registration document.

Veylan Works had been established thirty-eight years earlier.

Its original owner was **Edmund Veylan**.

The company had specialized in metal fittings, regulating components and industrial equipment.

Darius Veylan was listed as a family member and later as a technical representative.

The company had ceased trading twenty-one years earlier.

No formal bankruptcy record had been found.

Its final registration entry stated:

*Operations suspended pending settlement of outstanding contracts.*

Seraphina examined the document.

"Does the record say why?"

"No."

"Was the business sold?"

"No record of a sale."

"Was it dissolved?"

"Not formally."

Roland placed another paper on the desk.

"This is a tax assessment from the following year."

The company had no recorded operating income.

Its remaining assets were listed as equipment, tools and unfinished inventory.

There was a note indicating that some assets had been transferred to a regional storage authority.

Seraphina looked up.

"Which authority?"

"Regional Stores Office."

She stared at him.

"Do we have the receiving record?"

"Not yet."

"Does the inventory list the equipment?"

"Only in general categories."

"Could it include the regulating components from the western junction?"

"Possibly."

"Can we establish that?"

"Not from this document."

Seraphina nodded.

The company had stopped trading.

Its assets had been transferred, at least according to the tax record.

But the final destination remained unclear.

The same old problem had appeared again.

A transfer had been recorded.

The receiving confirmation was missing.

That afternoon, Cedric joined Seraphina in the archive room.

He examined the company record.

"Do you think the company and the engineer were connected?"

"It seems likely that they were."

"Then perhaps Darius Veylan was the owner's son."

"Perhaps."

Cedric looked at her.

"You do not want to make that assumption?"

"Not until we find a family or employment record."

"Why?"

Seraphina closed the file.

"Because the company could have employed several people with the same name. And even if he was related to the owner, that does not establish that he controlled the company's decisions."

Cedric nodded.

"Fair."

She looked at the transfer note.

"More importantly, we need to know where the company's equipment went."

"Regional Stores Office."

"Which may have been reorganized."

Cedric sighed.

"Of course."

Seraphina smiled.

"At least we know where to begin."

In Aurelion, the Royal Infrastructure Department was facing a different difficulty.

The committee coordinator, Adrian Holt, had received the first consolidated inventory from the central engineering archive.

The document was incomplete.

Several files had been marked as transferred to regional stores.

Others had been sent to the Treasury.

A few had no destination listed.

Holt had begun comparing the entries with the surviving financial records.

He discovered that the regional stores office had been reorganized twice.

Its first successor was the Provincial Materials Authority.

That authority had later been absorbed into the Royal Logistics Department.

The Royal Logistics Department still existed.

But its older records were held in a separate warehouse.

Holt wrote to the department.

He requested the original receiving registers.

The reply arrived three days later.

The warehouse contained the registers.

They had not yet been indexed.

Holt read the message.

Then he placed it on his desk.

He was beginning to understand why the Crown Prince had wanted an external adviser.

The old programme had left behind an administrative trail.

The trail had not disappeared.

It had simply become difficult to follow.

The next committee meeting was held in Aurelion.

Seraphina attended with Cedric.

Theodore was present, along with Minister Garrick, Lucien, Professor Vale, Holt and the Treasury representatives.

Holt opened the meeting.

"The central engineering archive has completed its first inventory. We have identified several transfer references that may be relevant to the missing maintenance records."

He distributed the list.

Seraphina examined it.

There were eight references.

Three concerned the northern region.

Two concerned the western marches.

One concerned the southern storehouse.

Two were general equipment transfers.

She looked at the southern entry.

"Does this refer to Southern Circuit Storehouse S-3?"

Holt checked.

"Possibly. The reference is incomplete."

"Then it should be marked as unconfirmed."

Holt nodded.

"Agreed."

Lucien examined another page.

"These two western references may concern the same equipment."

Minister Garrick looked at him.

"Why?"

"The dates are close, and the quantities are similar."

"Could they be separate transfers?"

"Yes."

"Then we should not combine them."

Seraphina nodded.

"Each should remain separate until the receiving records establish whether they concern the same shipment."

Theodore looked toward Holt.

"Record that."

Holt made a note.

The discussion moved to the transfer of Veylan Works equipment.

Seraphina presented the company's final registration entry.

She explained that the company had suspended operations twenty-one years earlier and that its remaining assets were listed as transferred to the regional stores office.

Minister Garrick examined the document.

"Does this establish that the equipment was received?"

"No. It establishes that the company recorded a transfer."

"Then we need the receiving register."

"Yes."

Holt looked at the reference.

"Do we know which regional office received it?"

"The document says Regional Stores Office. It does not identify a location."

Lucien examined the date.

"That may correspond with the reorganization of the northern maintenance programme."

"May," Seraphina repeated.

Lucien smiled.

"Yes. May."

Theodore turned toward Holt.

"Can the Royal Logistics Department search its older receiving registers?"

Holt nodded.

"I have already requested access."

"Good."

The committee agreed to keep the equipment transfer as a separate line of inquiry.

It would not be merged with the western junction payment records.

It would not be treated as proof that the contractor had failed to complete its work.

It would remain an unresolved transfer until the receiving evidence was found.

Seraphina was satisfied.

After the meeting, Theodore asked her to review the committee's progress report.

The document was shorter than the previous one.

It listed the six facilities, their current status and the records still missing.

Seraphina examined it.

The first five entries were reasonable.

The sixth, however, was described as a facility with an unknown location.

She looked at Holt.

"Do we know that it was constructed?"

"No."

"Then it should not be called a facility."

Holt frowned.

"What would you call it?"

"An unverified project allocation."

Theodore nodded.

"That is more accurate."

Holt amended the report.

Seraphina returned to the first page.

The report now distinguished between confirmed buildings, unverified locations and projects known only through financial references.

It was not particularly elegant.

It was honest.

She approved it.

On the journey back to Veyr, Cedric reviewed the meeting notes.

"Do you think we will find the receiving register?"

"Probably."

"That sounded optimistic."

"It is in a warehouse that still exists. That is better than searching for a building that may never have been constructed."

Cedric laughed.

Seraphina looked out of the carriage window.

The countryside was quiet.

Farmers were preparing their fields.

A group of children were walking along the road with baskets.

A merchant's cart passed in the opposite direction.

The ordinary world continued.

It did not care about the old maintenance programme.

It did not care about the Crown Prince's committee.

It did not care whether a contractor had received a final payment twenty-two years earlier.

Seraphina found that reassuring.

The next morning, Roland brought her a new document.

It was a copy of an old company letter.

The letter had been written by Edmund Veylan, the owner of Veylan Works.

It was addressed to the Regional Engineering Office.

The subject was the company's suspended operations.

The letter stated that Veylan Works had completed its assigned work on the western junction but had been unable to collect the remaining equipment from the regional maintenance station.

The company requested permission to retrieve the equipment or transfer it to the regional stores authority.

There was no reply attached.

Seraphina read the letter carefully.

"Does it have a date?"

"Yes. Twenty-one years ago."

"Does it identify the equipment?"

"Not precisely. It refers to the remaining regulating components and workshop tools."

"Does it identify the station?"

"The western junction."

"Does it say why the equipment was left there?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at Roland.

"Do we know whether the company was paid?"

"The letter refers to an outstanding settlement."

"Then the company believed it was still owed money."

"Yes."

"Does that establish that the payment was not made?"

"No. It establishes that the company made the claim."

She nodded.

"Good."

She placed the letter beside the payment authorization.

There was a possible explanation for the company's suspension.

It might have been waiting for payment.

It might have been waiting for permission to collect equipment.

It might have been involved in a dispute with the regional office.

But none of those possibilities was confirmed.

She wrote a note to Holt requesting that the committee obtain the original correspondence file.

Then she turned to Roland.

"Can you find out whether Edmund Veylan is still alive?"

Roland hesitated.

"May I ask why?"

"Because the letter is twenty-one years old. He may remember the circumstances."

Roland nodded.

"I will make inquiries."

Three days later, Roland returned with an answer.

Edmund Veylan had died twelve years earlier.

Darius Veylan, however, was still alive.

He lived in a small town in the western region.

His name appeared in a current property register.

He had not been associated with Veylan Works for many years.

Seraphina read the report.

"Does he know about the company's old contracts?"

"We do not know."

"Has anyone contacted him?"

"No."

"Then we should not send an official inquiry without establishing the proper procedure."

Roland nodded.

"I agree."

She considered the matter.

Darius Veylan might be able to explain the old company records.

He might also know nothing.

He had been an engineer assigned to the western station.

The company had been owned by Edmund Veylan.

The relationship between them was still uncertain.

Seraphina wrote a short note to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

She suggested that the committee consider a formal request for information from Darius Veylan, limited to his recorded assignment and any documents he might still possess.

She did not ask the estate to contact him privately.

The investigation belonged to the committee.

It should remain there.

That evening, Mira found Seraphina sitting beside the fire.

She had three open folders on the table.

"Would you like tea, my lady?"

"Yes, please."

Mira placed the cup beside her.

"Are you working on the old station again?"

"Yes."

"Have they found the missing documents?"

"Some."

"Is that good?"

"Yes."

Mira looked at the folders.

"How many more are missing?"

Seraphina considered the question.

"Enough."

Mira smiled.

Seraphina closed the first folder.

The second contained the contractor's correspondence.

The third contained the Academy's personnel register.

Three separate records.

Three separate questions.

The company had claimed that work was completed.

The engineer had been temporarily transferred.

The equipment had been recorded as moved to regional stores.

None of these facts proved the others.

But together, they suggested that the final years of the programme had been more complicated than the surviving summaries indicated.

Seraphina took a sip of tea.

She had a feeling the next discovery would not be a magical secret.

It would probably be another letter.

And she was beginning to suspect that the kingdom's entire history could be reconstructed from the things its officials had failed to file correctly.

She put the folders away.

There was no need to reconstruct the entire kingdom tonight.

The following afternoon, a messenger arrived from Aurelion.

The Crown Prince had approved the committee's request to contact Darius Veylan.

The Royal Infrastructure Department would send the inquiry.

It would ask whether he retained any personal records concerning the western station, the original maintenance programme or the transfer of equipment.

No accusations would be made.

No conclusions would be drawn from his response.

Seraphina read the letter.

She placed it in the committee file.

Then she noticed a handwritten note at the bottom.

It was from Theodore.

*The Treasury has also located a reference to an outstanding settlement involving Veylan Works. The amount does not match the final payment authorization. We are checking the original accounts.*

Seraphina read the note twice.

The contractor had claimed it was owed money.

The Treasury had a payment authorization.

The amount did not match.

There was still no proof of wrongdoing.

But now there was a specific financial discrepancy to examine.

She closed the file.

The next committee meeting would be interesting.

She did not particularly want it to be.

And that, she thought, was probably the most reliable sign that she was becoming involved in something important.

**End of Chapter 33**
