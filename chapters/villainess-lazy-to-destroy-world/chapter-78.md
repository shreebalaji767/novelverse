# **Chapter 78 — The Appendix That Had Been Attached to the Wrong Report**

The missing appendix was not found in the Academy's archive.

It was not found in the Royal Infrastructure Department's technical records.

It was not found in the Northern Regional Maintenance Authority's files.

By the end of the first week, Lucien had also searched the Academy's old correspondence register, its technical review index, and the personal papers of two retired researchers.

The result was consistent.

The **Regional Winter Network Dependency Schedule** had been mentioned three times.

It had been attached to none of the surviving copies.

Seraphina received this information in a letter.

She read it while Mira was arranging the morning tea service.

"Three references," Seraphina said.

Mira looked up.

"To the same document?"

"Yes."

"That seems promising."

"It would be, if anyone had actually seen it."

Mira placed the teapot down.

"Perhaps it was never completed."

Seraphina looked at her.

That was possible.

The appendix might have been drafted but never finalized.

It might have been discussed without being completed.

It might have been removed before the report was distributed.

Or it might have been attached to another document.

The last possibility was particularly irritating.

Seraphina folded the letter.

"Send this to Cedric."

"Shall I ask Master Arkwright for another copy?"

"No. Ask him for the exact wording of the three references."

Mira nodded.

Seraphina took her tea.

She had no intention of searching fifty-year-old archives herself.

She did, however, want to know whether the document had ever existed in a usable form.

There was a difference between a missing document and a document that had never been completed.

Unfortunately, both could produce very similar correspondence.

Lucien arrived the following afternoon.

He brought the minutes of the technical review group's final meeting.

The document was considerably less impressive than its title suggested.

It contained four pages of discussion, two pages of attendance records, and a list of decisions.

The final meeting had taken place thirty-four years earlier.

The group had discussed the retirement of the old winter network.

It had agreed that the surviving facilities should be assessed individually.

It had recommended replacing the old general operating threshold with separate limits for normal and reduced-load conditions.

It had also recommended preparing a dependency schedule showing which circuits had been disconnected, which had been replaced, and which still required technical review.

The schedule was to be attached to the final report.

Seraphina examined the minutes.

"Was it completed?"

Lucien opened another page.

"The secretary recorded that it was."

"Did anyone approve it?"

"The chairman initialed the attachment list."

"Did anyone receive it?"

"The report's distribution register says the final report was issued with three appendices."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then it was completed."

"Presumably."

"Please stop using that word."

Lucien smiled.

"I cannot confirm the attachment's final condition."

"Was the schedule numbered?"

"Yes."

He pointed to the attachment list.

**Appendix III — Regional Winter Network Dependency Schedule.**

The list identified the appendix as a separate document.

It had been assigned the reference **WMR/TECH/18-4/III**.

Seraphina looked at the reference.

"Could it have been filed under the appendix number?"

"That was our first assumption."

"And?"

"No matching entry."

"Could it have been filed under the main report?"

"We checked."

"Could it have been attached to a regional transfer schedule?"

"That is now being investigated."

Seraphina nodded.

"Good."

Lucien looked at her.

"You sound disappointed."

"I was hoping the answer would be in the first place we looked."

"That would have been convenient."

"Yes."

He rolled up the minutes.

"There is another possibility."

Seraphina sighed.

"Go on."

"The original review group included a regional maintenance inspector named Master Oren Whitlock."

She looked up.

The name was familiar.

Oren Whitlock had inspected the old northern facilities many years earlier.

He had also advised on the suitability of the reserve equipment found in the central stores.

"Is he still alive?"

"Yes. He lives in the northern region."

Seraphina looked at the minutes again.

"Then ask him."

Lucien smiled.

"I already have."

Master Oren Whitlock arrived at the estate three days later.

He was older than Seraphina remembered from the Academy's description.

His hair had turned almost completely white.

He walked with a cane, but his eyes remained sharp.

He greeted Seraphina politely.

"Your Ladyship."

"Master Whitlock."

He sat opposite Lucien.

Cedric joined them with the old review minutes.

Oren examined the document.

"I remember this."

"Do you remember the appendix?" Lucien asked.

Oren frowned.

"The dependency schedule?"

"Yes."

"I remember preparing some of the information."

Seraphina leaned forward.

"Did you complete it?"

Oren considered the question.

"I completed the regional inspection portion. I cannot say whether the final schedule was completed by the secretary."

"Did you sign it?"

"No."

"Did you send it?"

"No."

Lucien looked disappointed.

Oren noticed.

"That does not mean it was never finished."

"What happened?"

Oren adjusted his glasses.

"The review group was responsible for examining the old winter network. We had technical records, maintenance records, and regional site reports. The problem was that the records did not use the same facility references."

Seraphina nodded.

"Different names for the same locations?"

"Sometimes. Other times, one facility had several numbers. Some facilities had been renamed after road construction or regional reorganization."

Lucien nodded.

Oren continued.

"We were trying to produce one schedule that could be used by all the departments. I prepared the technical status of the facilities I had inspected. The surveyor prepared the physical locations. The Academy provided circuit information. The records officer collected the references."

"Henry Cole?" Cedric asked.

Oren nodded.

"Yes. He was responsible for the records."

Seraphina remembered Henry Cole's name from the earlier infrastructure investigation.

He had retained copies of several transfer schedules.

"Did he prepare the final appendix?" she asked.

Oren shook his head.

"He assembled the material. I do not know who compiled the final version."

"Was it approved at the meeting?"

"The review group agreed that it should be attached to the report. We did not approve every entry individually."

Seraphina considered this.

"Then the appendix may have contained information that had not been verified."

Oren looked at her.

"Yes. It was meant to distinguish confirmed facilities from unresolved references."

Lucien leaned forward.

"Do you remember what happened to your section?"

Oren thought for a moment.

"I submitted it to the secretary. I also kept a working copy."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Do you still have it?"

Oren smiled faintly.

"I believe so."

Oren returned the next morning with a bundle of old papers.

The pages had been folded and tied with string.

He placed them carefully on the table.

The first was a technical inspection summary.

The second was a list of facilities.

The third contained handwritten notes.

The fourth was a map.

Lucien examined the papers.

"These are your original working documents?"

"Yes."

Oren pointed to the first page.

"This was the technical section I prepared for the review group."

The document listed six facilities.

NTR-4.

The Westmere agricultural regulation station.

The northern transport signal facility.

A western marches relay.

A southern agricultural regulator.

And an unidentified reserve connection.

The first three had been inspected.

The western relay had been inspected once.

The southern regulator had been reported as dismantled.

The reserve connection had no confirmed physical location.

Seraphina looked at the list.

"Does this match the current Academy records?"

Lucien compared the entries.

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

"The western relay has a different reference number."

Oren nodded.

"It was renumbered after the road authority changed the district boundary."

Lucien examined the map.

"That explains the discrepancy."

Seraphina looked at the final entry.

"What is the reserve connection?"

Oren shook his head.

"We never found it."

"Was it part of the original network?"

"According to the funding schedule."

"Was it ever constructed?"

"Unknown."

"Was it included in the review?"

"Only as an unresolved reference."

Seraphina nodded.

The schedule had not been a list of six confirmed facilities.

It had been a list of six references, with different levels of certainty.

That distinction mattered.

She looked at the map.

"Does this show the connection routes?"

Oren nodded.

"Some of them."

The map contained several lines.

The old central regulation network had once connected the northern chamber to the Westmere station and the transport signal facility.

The western relay appeared to have been connected through a separate branch.

The southern regulator had been connected to a different system.

The reserve connection was represented by a dotted line.

There was no confirmed destination.

Lucien examined the map carefully.

"This is useful."

Oren smiled.

"I thought it might be."

Seraphina looked at the handwritten notes.

One line had been underlined.

*Final connection status to be confirmed after regional disconnection reports received.*

She pointed to it.

"Did those reports arrive?"

Oren shook his head.

"Not to me."

"Did you follow up?"

"I sent a request to the regional authority. I received an acknowledgment, but no final report."

Lucien looked up.

"Do you remember the reference?"

Oren turned over the page.

There it was.

**NWR/DIS/27.**

The same reference appeared beside the western relay and the northern transport facility.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"That may be where the appendix went."

He nodded.

"Or where the missing confirmation was supposed to come from."

Oren gathered his papers.

"I cannot tell you more than that."

Seraphina thanked him.

He had provided something useful.

Not the missing appendix.

But the original material from which it might have been prepared.

Lucien took the papers back to the Academy.

He compared them with the review group's minutes.

The reference NWR/DIS/27 appeared in the Department's correspondence register.

It referred to a regional disconnection report.

The report had been prepared by the Northern Regional Maintenance Authority.

It had been sent to the Royal Infrastructure Department.

The Department had acknowledged receipt.

But the archive contained no copy of the report itself.

There was another entry.

The report had been transferred to the Central Technical Standards Directorate for inclusion in the final review.

The Directorate's receiving register confirmed that it had arrived.

The file had been assigned a new number.

**CTS/REG/44.**

Lucien sent the reference to Seraphina.

She read it.

"At least we know where to look."

Cedric nodded.

"The Directorate?"

"Yes."

"Should we request the file?"

"Lucien can do that."

Cedric smiled.

"Of course."

The Central Technical Standards Directorate replied after six days.

The file existed.

It was not missing.

It had been placed under a different subject heading.

The original title was:

**Northern Regional Winter Network — Disconnection and Transition Records.**

The Directorate had filed it under:

**Regional Maintenance Closure Reports.**

The file contained several documents.

The first was a report confirming that the northern transport signal circuit had been disconnected from the old winter network.

The second confirmed that the Westmere agricultural station had been rebuilt with an independent regulator.

The third described the closure of NTR-4.

The fourth was a request for clarification concerning the western relay.

The fifth was a note about the unidentified reserve connection.

Seraphina read the summary.

The first three findings were consistent with the current records.

The western relay had been isolated, but the final technical confirmation had not been completed.

The reserve connection remained unverified.

There was also a note stating that the dependency schedule had been prepared using the regional closure reports.

However, the schedule itself was not attached.

Lucien sent another letter.

The Directorate searched the remaining contents of the file.

They found a reference to an outgoing transfer.

The appendix had been sent to the Royal Infrastructure Department's Regional Transition Office.

The receiving clerk had signed for it.

The transfer register identified the document as:

**WMR/TECH/18-4/III — Original Attachment.**

Seraphina read the entry.

"That is it."

Cedric nodded.

"Where is the Regional Transition Office?"

"It was dissolved thirty years ago."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Of course."

The Regional Transition Office had been merged into the Central Infrastructure Records Division.

Its records had been transferred twice.

The first transfer was to the Central Records Receiving Bay.

The second was to the Royal Infrastructure Department's archive.

The Department's archive contained the transfer list.

It did not contain the appendix.

The transfer list showed that the appendix had been placed in a package containing twelve other technical documents.

The package had been received.

The receiving clerk had signed.

But the package inventory listed only eleven documents.

The twelfth entry had been crossed out.

The name of the crossed-out document was not legible.

Seraphina examined the copy.

"Could it have been removed before transfer?"

Lucien shook his head.

"The original transfer list says twelve documents. The receiving inventory says eleven."

"Then one document was missing when it arrived."

"Possibly."

Seraphina gave him a look.

Lucien corrected himself.

"One document was not recorded in the receiving inventory."

"Better."

Cedric studied the crossed-out entry.

"Could the appendix have been filed separately?"

"Yes," Lucien said. "The transfer may have included an attachment that was not counted as an independent document."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we should search the related files before concluding that it was lost."

Lucien smiled.

"That is what the Department intends to do."

The search produced another lead.

The Regional Transition Office had prepared a separate technical summary for the Treasury.

It was titled:

**Winter Network Closure — Equipment and Financial Consequences.**

The summary listed the facilities and the costs associated with their closure.

It also referred to a technical dependency schedule.

The schedule was described as an attachment.

The Treasury's copy contained the summary.

It did not contain the attachment.

However, the summary included a handwritten note:

*Technical schedule retained by Standards Directorate pending confirmation of western branch.*

Lucien examined the note.

"That suggests the schedule was not lost at the transition office."

Seraphina looked at him.

"It suggests someone believed the Directorate had retained it."

"Yes."

"Have they checked?"

"They are checking."

The Directorate's archive had already confirmed receipt of the original disconnection reports.

It had not confirmed receipt of the final schedule.

The Treasury note might have referred to the working papers rather than the completed appendix.

Or the schedule might have been sent separately.

Or it might have been retained by a technical officer.

The evidence did not establish which.

Seraphina leaned back.

"At least we have narrowed the search."

Lucien nodded.

He looked more pleased than she thought appropriate.

She had learned that researchers could become cheerful after discovering that a document was missing in a more specific way.

A week later, the Directorate sent its final response.

The schedule had been located.

It was stored in the personal technical papers of a retired standards officer.

The officer had kept several working documents after the Regional Transition Office was dissolved.

The papers had been transferred to the Directorate after his death.

The schedule was found inside a folder titled:

**Northern Winter Network — Final Technical Reconciliation.**

It had never been formally added to the central archive index.

The document was thirty-one pages long.

It contained the original dependency schedule.

Lucien sent Seraphina a copy.

She read the summary.

The schedule classified the six historical references.

NTR-4 was confirmed and dormant.

The Westmere agricultural station had been replaced by an independent system.

The northern transport signal facility had been disconnected.

The western relay had been isolated, but its final technical confirmation remained incomplete.

The southern regulator had been dismantled.

The reserve connection had never been physically identified.

The schedule also contained a list of the circuits that had been affected by the original winter network.

Three were confirmed to have been disconnected or replaced.

One required further verification.

Two had never been established as physical facilities.

Seraphina put down the report.

"That is useful."

Cedric nodded.

"Does it answer the question?"

"Most of it."

Lucien, seated across from her, opened the final section.

"There is one unresolved item."

Seraphina looked at him.

"The western relay?"

"Yes."

The schedule listed the relay as isolated.

But its final technical confirmation had never been attached.

The modern circuit near the western marches was likely independent.

However, the old relay's connection point had not been physically examined.

The recent inspection had found that its balancing assembly was worn.

The new replacement component had been deemed unsuitable.

The Academy now wanted to inspect the old connection point before approving the replacement design.

Seraphina looked at Lucien.

"Is the old connection active?"

"We do not know."

"Has the modern circuit been tested?"

"Yes. It is stable."

"Then why inspect the old connection?"

"To confirm that the isolation was completed as recorded."

She considered this.

That was reasonable.

It was not an emergency.

But it was a legitimate technical question.

"Who owns the land?"

"The western landowner, Lady Helena Ashcombe."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the Academy should ask her."

Lucien smiled.

"Already done."

The final inspection was scheduled for the following month.

Lady Helena Ashcombe granted access to the site.

The Academy would examine the old connection point.

The regional authority would verify the current circuit's independence.

The Technical Standards Directorate would review the replacement component's specifications.

No activation would take place.

No old equipment would be installed.

No changes would be made to the modern circuit until the inspection was complete.

Seraphina read the arrangement.

"Good."

Cedric smiled.

"You seem pleased."

"I am pleased that the correct people are doing the work."

Lucien rolled up the schedule.

"The missing appendix has been found."

"Yes."

"And the network's historical status is much clearer."

"Yes."

"The Academy can now complete the review."

"Excellent."

He paused.

"There may be another issue."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

Lucien stopped speaking.

She opened them.

"What?"

"The final technical reconciliation schedule contains a reference to a separate reserve equipment list."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Is that the component we already found?"

"No. That was the reserve regulating assembly from NTR-4."

"Then what is this?"

Lucien turned the page.

The schedule referred to a list of equipment reserved for the transition away from the old winter network.

It included replacement regulators, internal guides, connecting assemblies, and several types of calibration instruments.

The list had been prepared by the Regional Transition Office.

Its purpose was to identify equipment that could be reused, modified, or retired.

The schedule stated that the list had been sent to the Central Stores Authority.

The Central Stores Authority had acknowledged receipt.

But the current stores register contained no corresponding inventory.

Seraphina looked at the reference.

**TRANS/RES/31-B.**

"How much equipment?"

Lucien checked the report.

"The schedule does not provide a total."

"Then how do we know it was received?"

"The transfer register records the package."

"Does it list the contents?"

"Only by category."

Seraphina stared at the document.

The missing appendix had finally been found.

It had clarified the old network.

It had confirmed which facilities were active, dormant, disconnected, dismantled, or never physically identified.

It had also revealed that a separate reserve equipment list had been transferred to central stores.

The equipment itself might have been reused.

It might have been dismantled.

It might have been included in later inventories under different descriptions.

There was no reason to assume anything improper.

But there was another record to locate.

Seraphina pushed the report toward Lucien.

"Please tell me the Academy is searching for this."

"It is."

"Good."

She stood.

"I am going to have lunch."

Lucien nodded.

"Of course."

Seraphina left the room.

Behind her, Cedric looked at the schedule.

Then he looked at Lucien.

"Do you think the equipment list will be found?"

Lucien considered it.

"I hope so."

Cedric sighed.

Seraphina's voice came from the corridor.

"Do not hope. Search."

Lucien smiled.

"Yes, Your Ladyship."

And for the first time in several weeks, Seraphina was pleased to hear that someone else had been assigned the work.
