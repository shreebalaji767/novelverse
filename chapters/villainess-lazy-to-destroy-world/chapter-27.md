# Chapter 27 — The Representative Who Was Not the Villainess

Seraphina discovered that delegating a responsibility was considerably easier than watching someone else carry it out.

The difficulty began when Cedric entered her sitting room with three folders, two maps and an expression that suggested he had questions.

"Lady Seraphina."

"Good morning."

"I have received your reply to the Academy."

"Excellent."

"You have declined their invitation."

"Correct."

"And nominated me."

"Also correct."

Cedric placed the folders on the table.

"May I ask why?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"Because you are the estate administrator."

"I am aware of my position."

"And you understand the regional maintenance arrangements."

"Yes."

"And you are authorized to discuss the estate's administrative responsibilities."

"Yes."

"Then I believe the reasoning is quite clear."

Cedric remained standing.

"Do you know what the Academy intends to discuss?"

"No."

"Do you know whether they intend to request additional regional cooperation?"

"No."

"Do you know whether the meeting will involve funding arrangements?"

"No."

"Then why did you decline before receiving the agenda?"

Seraphina considered this.

"Because I am not the appropriate representative."

Cedric opened one of the folders.

"Perhaps. But they may still ask questions about the western conduit inspection."

"Then you can answer the questions concerning the estate."

"And if they ask about your previous observations?"

"I will write them down for you."

Cedric looked at her for a moment.

Then he sat.

"Very well."

Seraphina smiled.

"Thank you."

"I have one condition."

She became suspicious.

"What condition?"

"You will review the documents with me before I leave."

Seraphina's smile disappeared.

"I see."

"You cannot reasonably expect me to attend without knowing what has already been discussed."

"That is fair."

"And I will need the original access authorization."

"Of course."

"And the latest maintenance report."

"Naturally."

"And the old inspection map."

Seraphina looked toward the ceiling.

"Perhaps I should attend after all."

Cedric almost smiled.

"No, my lady. You should not."

The Academy meeting was scheduled for the fourth day of the following month.

Until then, Cedric would need to review the estate's position and prepare a clear account of what House Veyr could provide.

Seraphina spent the morning helping him assemble the necessary records.

They began with the western conduit.

The estate could provide access to the land surrounding the current inspection point, subject to ordinary safety arrangements. It could provide an assigned guide and copies of surviving maintenance records. It could also arrange for local workers to assist with opening and securing the access chamber.

It could not certify the conduit's structural or magical condition.

It could not authorize modifications to the circuit.

It could not guarantee that the original construction records were complete.

It could not accept responsibility for sections whose ownership remained unclear.

Cedric wrote these points into a draft.

Seraphina read it.

"Good."

"Anything missing?"

"One thing."

"What?"

"Make clear that the estate's workers are not Academy assistants. They are responsible for the site and its ordinary maintenance, not for conducting technical tests."

Cedric added the sentence.

"Anything else?"

"No."

He looked surprised.

"That is all?"

"Yes."

Cedric closed the folder.

"You have become remarkably difficult to persuade into doing unnecessary work."

"I consider that a personal achievement."

He shook his head.

Two days later, Cedric received confirmation from the Academy.

The meeting agenda had been attached.

It included five subjects:

1. Regional access arrangements.
2. Technical responsibilities.
3. Existing maintenance records.
4. Emergency procedures during inspections.
5. Preliminary estimates for future work.

Cedric read the last item twice.

"Preliminary estimates," he said.

Seraphina glanced at the page.

"Have they asked for funding?"

"Not directly."

"Then what do they want?"

"An indication of the cost of providing local support."

"That is different."

"Yes."

Cedric looked at her.

"The estate can estimate the cost of assigning workers, providing transport and preparing the access site."

"Can it estimate the cost of repairing the conduit?"

"No."

"Then don't."

Cedric nodded.

"That is what I intend to say."

He made another note.

Seraphina returned to her book.

She had finally found a quiet afternoon.

She intended to protect it.

The next morning, the northern maintenance superintendent arrived with an update.

The recovered metal bracket had been examined by the estate's metalworker.

It was old, but not as old as the original conduit construction.

The metalworker believed it had been manufactured between twenty and thirty years earlier.

There were faint stamped markings on the underside.

The first two letters appeared to be V and W.

The remaining marks were too corroded to read.

Seraphina looked at the report.

"V and W?"

"Yes, my lady," the superintendent said.

"Could they stand for Veylan Works?"

"Possibly."

"Could they stand for something else?"

"Certainly."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we should not identify it yet."

The superintendent placed a drawing on the table.

"The bracket also has two mounting holes. They match the dimensions recorded in the old maintenance inventory."

Seraphina examined the drawing.

"Does that establish that it belonged to the maintenance station?"

"It supports that possibility."

"Good. Keep the bracket preserved. Ask the Academy to examine the markings, but make sure they record its present condition before attempting to clean it."

The superintendent bowed.

"Yes, my lady."

After he left, Seraphina sent another message to Lucien.

She informed him about the markings and requested that the Academy's instrument specialist examine the object.

She also asked whether Master Whitlock could be shown a drawing before the examination.

The old engineer might recognize it.

Or he might not.

Either answer would be useful.

Meanwhile, Cedric began preparing for his journey to Aurelion.

He requested copies of the original land-access agreement, the current maintenance schedule and the regional office's surviving archive register.

Seraphina reviewed the documents once.

Then she left him to work.

It was difficult.

She had become accustomed to examining every detail herself. The idea of someone else attending the meeting, answering questions and making decisions without her supervision produced an uncomfortable feeling.

Not because she distrusted Cedric.

Quite the opposite.

She trusted him.

The discomfort came from knowing she would not be present to correct anything she disliked.

That, she realized, was precisely why she needed to let him go.

On the evening before Cedric's departure, he came to her sitting room.

He carried a small notebook.

"I have a question."

"That sounds dangerous."

"About the old maintenance station."

Seraphina set aside her book.

"What about it?"

"The regional office's records indicate that the building was still listed as operational four months after the junction modification."

"That is useful."

"But the maintenance inventory records the return of several tools two months later."

"To the regional stores?"

"Yes."

"Does it identify the person who returned them?"

Cedric opened the notebook.

"Not clearly. The entry is signed with initials."

"Which initials?"

"J.H."

Seraphina considered this.

"Could be anyone."

"Yes."

"Does the inventory list the missing marker?"

"No. It lists a metal bracket, two measuring rods, a set of tools and several regulating components."

"Are the components accounted for?"

"Some are. Others are marked as transferred."

"Transferred where?"

"That is unclear."

Seraphina leaned forward.

"Does the entry include a destination?"

Cedric showed her the page.

There was a faint handwritten note beside the transfer column.

*Regional stores — pending confirmation.*

Seraphina stared at it.

"Again."

Cedric nodded.

"Again."

They were encountering the same phrase in different records.

Pending confirmation.

It did not prove that the items had never arrived.

It did not prove that someone had taken them.

It meant only that the surviving record did not show the transfer being completed.

"Can we find the regional stores register?" Seraphina asked.

"That is what I intend to request at the meeting."

"Good."

Cedric closed the notebook.

"I will send you a copy of anything they provide."

"Thank you."

He stood.

"One more thing."

Seraphina looked up.

"Yes?"

"You were right to nominate me."

She blinked.

Cedric continued.

"The meeting concerns responsibilities that belong to the estate's administration. You should not have to attend simply because you were present during the earlier discussions."

Seraphina smiled.

"I appreciate that."

Cedric bowed and left.

She returned to her book.

For the first time in several days, she felt that something had been handled correctly without requiring her to remain involved.

It was a pleasant sensation.

She hoped it would last.

Cedric departed for Aurelion the following morning.

The estate continued its usual work.

The northern road survey was completed. Two drainage repairs were approved. The Westmere irrigation channel was functioning properly, and the agricultural office had confirmed the next grain delivery under the revised receiving procedure.

The village elders had also submitted their winter storage requirements.

Seraphina reviewed the summary.

The quantities were separated into seed grain, household reserves and emergency stock.

It was a considerable improvement over the earlier reports.

However, one village had requested more storage space than its recorded population appeared to require.

Seraphina marked the discrepancy.

She did not immediately assume that the request was inflated.

There could be several reasons.

Perhaps the village supported nearby settlements.

Perhaps its previous storage had been damaged.

Perhaps the population figures were outdated.

She sent a question to the agricultural office.

That was all.

In Aurelion, Cedric's meeting began at the appointed hour.

The Academy had chosen a conference room on its administrative floor.

Lucien was present, along with Master Fenwick, Professor Vale and two representatives from the regional coordination office.

A Treasury official had also been invited.

Cedric introduced himself and presented the Veyr estate's written position.

The discussion began with land access.

There were no major disagreements.

House Veyr would permit the Academy's technical team to enter the specified area, examine the access chamber and take measurements.

The estate would provide a guide and arrange for the site to be opened.

Any additional excavation or alteration would require separate approval.

The Treasury official made a note.

"Does the estate intend to provide financial support for the inspection?"

Cedric replied calmly.

"The estate will cover its ordinary local coordination costs. It has not agreed to fund Academy personnel or technical equipment."

"Would the estate consider a contribution toward future repairs?"

"That would depend on the nature of the repair, the responsible authority and the cost estimate."

The official nodded.

"Reasonable."

Lucien turned to the next subject.

"The maintenance records."

Cedric opened his folder.

"We have several documents relating to the original junction modification. They are incomplete."

He presented the register, the inventory and the survey report.

Master Fenwick examined them.

"This entry says the maintenance station was still operational after the modification."

"Yes."

"And this one records the transfer of tools."

"Yes."

"Where were they transferred?"

"We do not know. The surviving copy says regional stores, pending confirmation."

Professor Vale leaned forward.

"Do we have the receiving register?"

"Not yet."

Lucien looked at Cedric.

"Could the estate continue searching?"

"We can search our surviving archives. However, if the records were transferred to a central office, the Academy or Treasury will need to help identify the receiving location."

The Treasury official looked at the documents.

"Which year?"

Cedric gave him the date.

The official frowned.

"That was before the last regional reorganization."

"Yes."

"Some of those records may be in the old provincial accounts archive."

Lucien looked up.

"Is that the same archive that received the emergency supply records?"

The official considered it.

"Possibly. But I would not assume the records were transferred together."

Cedric made a note.

The meeting continued.

They discussed technical responsibilities, local emergency procedures and the cost of providing access.

At no point did Cedric claim that House Veyr owned the conduit.

At no point did he promise to fund repairs.

And when asked whether the estate could provide a technical assessment, he declined.

The Academy would need to provide its own qualified personnel.

By the end of the meeting, the parties had agreed on a limited inspection arrangement.

It was not a national partnership.

It was not a repair authorization.

It was a practical agreement for one site.

Cedric considered it a successful outcome.

Before leaving the Academy, Lucien approached him.

"Mr. Veyr."

"Master Arkwright."

"Thank you for the documents."

"You are welcome."

Lucien held a small sheet of paper.

"We have located a possible reference to the regional stores register."

Cedric examined it.

The reference was **RS-684/19**.

"What does it identify?"

"A transfer ledger from the period. The archive office has not yet confirmed whether it survives."

"Where is it held?"

"Possibly in the old provincial accounts archive."

Cedric looked at him.

"Possibly?"

Lucien gave a small, apologetic smile.

"The records are not well organized."

Cedric folded the paper.

"Then we should confirm the location before requesting the ledger."

"Agreed."

Lucien hesitated.

"Lady Seraphina has been helpful in keeping the estate's position clear."

Cedric looked at him.

"She is trying to ensure that the estate does not accept responsibilities it cannot fulfill."

"That is useful."

"It is also necessary."

Lucien nodded.

"I hope she will attend the next technical review."

Cedric shook his head.

"I expect she will prefer to remain at home."

Lucien looked disappointed.

Cedric added, "If you need an answer from her, write to her. If you need an estate representative, write to me."

"Understood."

They parted.

When Cedric returned north, he brought three things.

The first was a written record of the meeting.

The second was a limited access agreement for the western conduit inspection.

The third was the reference number of a possible archive ledger.

Seraphina examined all three.

"You did well."

Cedric sat opposite her.

"Thank you."

"The agreement is clear."

"I insisted on the separate authorization clause."

"Good."

"The Academy has also agreed to provide its own technical staff."

"Excellent."

"And the Treasury official believes the old transfer ledger may be in the provincial accounts archive."

Seraphina looked at the reference.

"Have they confirmed it?"

"No."

"Then we still need to locate it."

"Yes."

She placed the papers together.

For several moments, neither spoke.

Then Cedric said, "I understand why you did not attend."

Seraphina smiled.

"Do you?"

"Yes. The meeting required someone who could speak for the estate without confusing personal observations with official commitments."

"That sounds important."

"It is."

He looked at her.

"However, you should not assume that every responsibility can be delegated."

Seraphina's smile faded.

"I know."

"Some decisions require the heir's approval."

"I know."

"And some matters will eventually require your direct involvement."

"I know."

Cedric leaned back.

"I am not suggesting you avoid everything."

"That is unfortunate. I was hoping to become very good at it."

He laughed.

Seraphina smiled.

Then she returned to the archive reference.

The next day, a message arrived from Lucien.

The Academy had examined the recovered metal bracket.

The two visible letters were indeed part of a longer stamped mark.

After careful cleaning and comparison with surviving manufacturer records, the specialist believed the initials stood for **Veylan Works**.

The identification was not yet certain.

The company name appeared in a procurement register from the same period, but the Academy had not located the original contract.

Lucien had also asked Master Whitlock to review the photograph.

The retired engineer recognized the bracket.

He remembered seeing it attached to the maintenance station.

He did not remember who had installed it.

The marker itself remained missing.

Seraphina read the message.

Veylan Works.

The contractor's name might finally have been identified.

But a name alone was not enough.

She sent the information to Roland and requested a search of the old procurement registers.

She also asked him to check whether the company had received payment for the modification.

Not because payment would prove the work was completed.

Not because the absence of payment would prove anything improper.

Because the contract, invoice, completion certificate and final inspection record needed to be compared.

She was learning to look for a complete sequence rather than a single convenient answer.

That evening, Seraphina sat with her mother.

Evelyne was reviewing a list of guests for an upcoming charitable gathering.

Seraphina looked at the list.

"How many people are attending?"

"Forty-two."

"How many have confirmed?"

"Thirty-six."

"How many are expected to bring assistants?"

"Most of them."

Seraphina considered the crowded room she was imagining.

"Will there be enough chairs?"

Evelyne looked up.

"Yes."

"Have you checked?"

"I have."

Seraphina nodded.

"Good."

Her mother smiled.

"You are not asking whether you must attend?"

"I was about to."

"Then ask."

"Do I have to attend?"

"Yes."

Seraphina sighed.

Evelyne laughed.

"You cannot avoid every gathering."

"I can try."

"You have already declined the Academy meeting."

"That was a professional decision."

"And this is a family obligation."

"An unfortunate distinction."

Evelyne set down her papers.

"You did well in Aurelion."

Seraphina looked at her.

"How would you know? I wasn't there."

"Your father told me Cedric handled the meeting successfully."

"That was Cedric's work."

"Yes."

Seraphina waited.

Evelyne continued.

"And you allowed him to do it."

Seraphina looked down at her hands.

She had not thought of it as an accomplishment.

It had simply been the correct decision.

But perhaps that was the point.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"Do you ever feel that people expect you to be responsible for things simply because you are capable of handling them?"

Evelyne considered the question.

"Frequently."

"What do you do?"

"I decide whether the responsibility is mine."

"And if it isn't?"

"I find the person whose responsibility it is."

Seraphina smiled.

"That sounds familiar."

"It should."

Evelyne returned to her correspondence.

Seraphina looked out of the window.

The estate was quiet.

The Academy meeting had been completed without her.

The western inspection had a clear agreement.

The old maintenance records were being examined by people qualified to understand them.

And the recovered bracket had finally provided a possible contractor name.

She felt, for once, that the work was moving forward without her having to stand in the middle of it.

It was a good feeling.

The following morning, Roland sent his first findings.

Veylan Works had existed during the relevant period.

The company had been registered in the eastern territories and had undertaken several infrastructure contracts across Elarion.

One of those contracts involved a regulating junction in the northern region.

The reference number matched the number on the Academy's maintenance register.

However, the surviving procurement ledger showed only the initial payment.

There was no final settlement entry.

There was also no surviving completion certificate.

Roland included a note.

*The absence of a final settlement entry does not establish that the contractor was unpaid. The relevant payment ledger may have been transferred to another office.*

Seraphina read the note twice.

Then she looked at the reference.

**VW-22/06.**

She placed the document beside the old maintenance register.

The pieces were beginning to align.

The contractor existed.

The contract reference matched.

The initial payment was recorded.

The final settlement was not.

The completion certificate was missing.

The inspection had been deferred.

And the spare components had been transferred to an unidentified regional store.

It was no longer simply a missing marker.

There was an entire chain of records that had never been brought together.

Seraphina wrote to Roland.

She asked him to locate the payment ledger and any surviving correspondence with Veylan Works.

Then she added one final instruction.

*Please do not assume that the missing final payment means the work was incomplete. We need the receiving and inspection records before reaching that conclusion.*

She signed the letter.

For a moment, she considered adding another line.

Instead, she closed the folder.

The investigation could continue without her staring at it every hour.

She stood, stretched and walked toward the window.

Outside, the northern fields were beginning to change colour with the approaching season.

The next inspection was scheduled.

The estate had agreed to provide limited access.

The Academy had agreed to provide technical staff.

Cedric had handled the administrative meeting.

And Seraphina had successfully spent several days without attending a single committee.

She considered this a very promising development.

Then Mira entered with another letter.

Seraphina looked at it.

"Please tell me that isn't from the Academy."

"It is from the Church, my lady."

Seraphina relaxed.

Mira placed it on the table.

"The Saintess has written."

Seraphina opened the envelope.

Liora's letter was brief.

The Church had completed its review of the medical provisions for regional inspection teams.

The revised recommendations were ready.

There was one additional concern.

A southern charitable infirmary had reported that several medical supply deliveries were marked as received in its central records, but the local infirmary had never acknowledged them.

Liora had asked the Church's regional office to investigate.

She had not asked Seraphina to intervene.

She had simply mentioned that the affected deliveries came from a distribution network that also supplied some of the northern charitable stores.

Seraphina read the letter again.

Then she placed it beside the Veylan Works documents.

Two unrelated investigations.

One concerned an old magical junction.

The other concerned medical supplies.

There was no evidence that they were connected.

There was no reason to assume they were.

Seraphina looked at the growing collection of folders on her desk.

She took out a fresh sheet of paper and wrote:

*Southern medical deliveries — separate inquiry.*

Then she added:

*Do not connect unrelated problems without evidence.*

She placed the note on top of the letter.

At least this time, she had learned the lesson before making the mistake.

Unfortunately, she suspected the kingdom would provide plenty of opportunities to practise it.

**End of Chapter 27**
