# **Chapter 96 — The Reserve Depot Had Been Closed for Four Years**

The Central Reserve Materials Depot had three addresses.

The first appeared in the original procurement records.

The second appeared in the Royal Infrastructure Department's property register.

The third appeared in a closure notice issued four years earlier.

None of them referred to the same entrance.

This was discovered at eight fifteen in the morning, when a departmental carriage arrived at the address recorded in the procurement ledger and found a furniture warehouse.

The warehouse manager was understandably confused.

"We sell chairs," he explained.

The Royal Infrastructure Department's representative examined his document.

"We are looking for the Central Reserve Materials Depot."

"Then you have the wrong building."

"The address is correct."

"The address is correct for this building."

The representative looked at the paper again.

The warehouse manager pointed toward the street.

"That depot closed years ago. The old entrance was further east."

"How far?"

"Two streets."

"Can you show us?"

The manager considered the group.

Then he looked at the Crown Prince's carriage.

"I suppose I can."

Seraphina had arrived ten minutes earlier.

She was standing beside the carriage, holding the procurement ledger and wondering how many more buildings the kingdom could misplace without actually moving them.

Lucien was examining the old property register.

Theodore had already spoken with the Infrastructure Department and obtained temporary access authorization.

Master Colin Reeve, the regional surveyor, was accompanying them to verify the building's structural condition.

Nobody had brought a large technical team.

They did not yet know whether there was anything worth inspecting.

"Why does the procurement record use this address?" Seraphina asked.

Reeve examined the entry.

"The depot originally occupied the eastern building. The furniture warehouse is the former western annex."

"Then why does the property register list the western annex as the depot?"

"The buildings were renumbered when the road was widened."

"Was the register updated?"

"It appears so."

"Was the procurement record updated?"

Reeve looked at the document.

"No."

Seraphina handed it back.

"That explains the three addresses."

"Not entirely."

"Of course not."

The warehouse manager led them along the street.

The former depot stood behind a low stone wall. Its entrance was narrower than the other buildings, and a faded sign remained above the gate.

**CENTRAL RESERVE MATERIALS DEPOT**

The sign had been painted over once.

The old letters were still visible beneath the newer layer.

Reeve examined the outer walls.

"The structure is sound. The roof was repaired last year."

"Who owns it now?" Theodore asked.

"The Royal Infrastructure Department. The building was retained for storage."

"Then why was the depot closed?"

"Because the materials were supposedly transferred elsewhere."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Supposedly?"

Reeve opened the closure notice.

"The notice says the reserve materials were transferred to the Central Technical Stores."

Lucien glanced at the document.

"That is not the same as the central stores we inspected yesterday."

"No," Reeve replied. "It was a separate facility."

Seraphina looked toward the building.

"How many central stores does the kingdom have?"

Lucien answered without looking up.

"More than we need."

"That is the first encouraging thing you've said this morning."

The depot's records office was locked.

The key was held by the Department's property division, which had to be contacted before the inspection could proceed.

Theodore's coordinator sent a messenger.

While they waited, Seraphina reviewed the closure notice.

The depot had held reserve materials for several major infrastructure programmes. The capital protective network was listed among them, alongside regional maintenance supplies and replacement components for older transport circuits.

The reserve allocation CAP/PROT/12-6 appeared in the appendix.

It was listed as a quantity of eighteen sealed containers.

The material description was abbreviated.

**C-4 STABILIZING COMPOUND — RESERVE GRADE.**

Lucien read the entry.

"Eighteen."

"Is that enough?" Theodore asked.

"For the central assembly, potentially. But the reserve grade may differ from the ordinary C-4 formulation."

"Can it be used?"

"I need the original technical specification."

Seraphina turned the page.

The transfer record contained a destination.

Central Technical Stores, Building Four.

Receiving officer: pending.

There was no receiving signature.

"Another transfer without confirmation," she said.

Lucien looked at the page.

"Perhaps the materials were delivered but the signature was never entered."

"Perhaps."

"Or they remained here."

"Perhaps."

He gave her a tired look.

"Do you ever find an answer satisfying?"

"Yes. When it is supported by something."

The messenger returned with the key.

The property division had also sent a maintenance officer.

His name was Master Harold Finch, and he had been responsible for the building's exterior upkeep since its closure.

He unlocked the door.

The depot contained six storage rooms.

The first held empty crates.

The second contained damaged shelving.

The third held several pieces of obsolete measuring equipment.

The fourth had been cleared.

The fifth contained sealed boxes of ordinary maintenance materials.

The sixth was locked separately.

Harold Finch checked the inventory.

"Room Six was retained for restricted materials."

Lucien looked at him.

"Does the inventory identify what is inside?"

"Not the current inventory."

"What about the original?"

"I would have to check."

Seraphina looked at the door.

"Then let's check."

The second key was kept in a sealed envelope inside the property office.

The seal was intact.

Finch broke it under Reeve's supervision and opened the room.

Inside were several metal storage racks.

Most were empty.

A few held wooden boxes, each marked with a project reference.

Lucien walked toward the nearest rack.

"CAP/PROT/12-6."

There were no containers.

Only a narrow wooden label attached to the shelf.

Seraphina examined the shelf.

"Was anything removed recently?"

Finch checked the dust.

"I cannot say."

"Was the room inspected when the depot closed?"

"Yes."

"Is there an inspection report?"

"I believe so."

"Where?"

"The property office."

Lucien turned toward the old register.

"Then we need the closure inspection."

Finch left to retrieve it.

Theodore looked around the room.

"If the materials were transferred, we need to find the receiving facility."

Seraphina nodded.

"If they were not, we need to establish who removed them and under what authority."

Lucien examined the empty shelf.

"And if they were used?"

"Then there should be an issue record."

He looked at her.

"You're becoming very good at describing the three possible outcomes."

"I am becoming very good at recognizing that we have not established any of them."

The closure inspection arrived at eleven.

It contained a detailed account of the depot's condition.

The building had been cleared of most materials. Equipment had been transferred to the Central Technical Stores. Restricted materials had been placed in sealed transport containers.

Room Six had been emptied.

The report listed eighteen containers of C-4 stabilizing compound.

Each had been inspected and sealed.

The transport team had departed on the afternoon of the fourth day of the closure operation.

The destination was Building Four.

The report also included a transport manifest.

There were eighteen containers.

There were two transport carts.

There were four accompanying staff members.

The receiving authority was listed as the Central Technical Stores.

But the final receipt was missing.

Lucien read the report.

"At least we know they left this building."

Seraphina examined the transport manifest.

"Yes."

Theodore looked at the final line.

"Can we identify the carts?"

"The transport office should have the vehicle records," Reeve replied.

"Then obtain them."

Theodore turned to the coordinator.

The coordinator departed.

Seraphina studied the staff names.

One was a stores clerk.

One was a driver.

One was a departmental inspector.

The fourth was a technical assistant from the Academy.

She recognized none of them.

"Are any of these people still employed?"

Reeve checked the personnel references.

"Two retired. One transferred to the western maintenance office. The driver left departmental service."

Lucien looked at the assistant's name.

"Can we find him?"

"His name is Martin Hale."

Seraphina glanced at the page.

There were several people in the kingdom named Martin Hale.

"Do we have his personnel number?"

Reeve checked.

"Yes."

"Then use that."

Theodore nodded.

"Find the records and contact the retired staff. No assumptions."

The transport office confirmed that both carts had reached Building Four.

The first cart had delivered nine containers.

The second had delivered nine.

The transport manifest had been signed by the receiving guard.

But the guard's signature did not confirm the contents.

The receiving register listed eighteen sealed containers.

It did not identify their project reference.

The materials had been classified under a general heading:

**Stabilizing Compound — Reserve Stock.**

The quantity was correct.

The batch reference was missing.

The receiving clerk had initialled the entry.

Theodore examined the record.

"That confirms eighteen containers arrived at Building Four."

"Yes," Seraphina said.

"Does it confirm they were the eighteen from the depot?"

"Not independently."

Lucien sighed.

"We are going to need another register."

"Probably."

The receiving clerk's name was identified as Robert Weller.

He had retired three years earlier and lived in a small house outside the capital.

The Department sent a formal request for an interview.

The reply arrived before lunch.

He agreed to meet them.

Robert Weller was a thin, elderly man who walked with a cane.

He received the group in his sitting room and examined the documents carefully.

"I remember the depot closure," he said.

"Do you remember the eighteen containers?" Lucien asked.

"I remember receiving a shipment of stabilizing compound."

"Do you remember its project reference?"

"No."

"Do you remember where it was stored?"

"Building Four. Lower storage."

"Was it inspected?"

"Not by me. I was a receiving clerk."

Seraphina leaned forward.

"What did your signature confirm?"

"That eighteen sealed containers were delivered."

"Did you check the contents?"

"No."

"Did you verify the batch?"

"No. The containers were sealed."

"Did anyone else?"

Weller thought for a moment.

"The technical assistant might have."

"Martin Hale?"

"Yes. He came with the transport."

"Did he remain at Building Four?"

"For a while."

"How long?"

"Not long. He helped identify the storage section."

Lucien looked at Theodore.

"Then we need to speak with him."

Seraphina continued.

"Do you remember whether the containers were moved afterward?"

Weller frowned.

"There was a reorganization."

"When?"

"Perhaps a year later."

"Where were they moved?"

"I don't remember."

"Was there a record?"

"There should have been."

Theodore thanked him.

They left with a copy of the receiving clerk's statement.

It was useful.

It was not the answer.

Martin Hale was easier to locate.

He had retired from the Academy's technical stores division and now worked part-time maintaining a small private workshop.

He agreed to meet them that afternoon.

Unlike Weller, he remembered the eighteen containers.

"They were not ordinary maintenance stock," he said.

Lucien sat forward.

"Why?"

"Because the reserve grade had been prepared for the capital's second modernization. It was not meant to be mixed with ordinary C-4."

"Was it suitable for the current central assembly?"

"It was designed for that assembly's original replacement components."

"Do you know whether the specification changed?"

Hale considered the question.

"Some components changed. The compound itself was not necessarily obsolete."

"Was it tested?"

"Yes."

"Do you remember the results?"

"Not in detail."

Seraphina placed the old technical reference beside him.

"Do you recognize this?"

Hale examined the number.

"Yes. That was the reserve allocation."

"Do you remember what happened to it after Building Four?"

He nodded slowly.

"There was a transfer to the Academy's technical materials division."

"All eighteen containers?"

"I believe so."

"Do you have a record?"

"Not with me."

"Where would it be?"

"In the old project file. The transfer was arranged under the central modernization account."

Lucien looked at him.

"Why was it transferred?"

"The stores building was being reorganized. The materials were placed with the technical division because they required controlled conditions."

"Did you inspect them?"

"Yes."

"Condition?"

"Sealed. No damage."

"Did you verify the quantity?"

"All eighteen."

Seraphina watched him.

"Did you see the receiving office sign for them?"

Hale hesitated.

"I remember the containers being moved. I do not remember who signed the receipt."

"Then we should not record the transfer as confirmed."

Hale nodded.

"That is fair."

Lucien looked frustrated.

But he did not argue.

The old project file was located at the Academy's technical archives.

It contained a transfer instruction dated four years after the depot's closure.

The eighteen containers were to be moved from Building Four to the Academy's technical materials division.

The reason was clear.

Building Four was being converted into a general equipment store, and the reserve-grade compound required controlled storage.

The instruction carried the signature of the project director.

A dispatch record listed eighteen containers.

The receiving section was left blank.

However, a separate inventory from the Academy's technical materials division, dated three days later, listed eighteen containers of C-4 reserve compound.

The batch number was recorded.

The project reference was correct.

The quantity matched.

The inventory had been signed by the technical stores supervisor.

Lucien looked at the entry.

"That is stronger."

"Yes," Seraphina replied.

"It establishes that the material reached the Academy."

"It establishes that eighteen containers of the correct reserve grade were recorded there three days later."

"You're going to make that distinction every time?"

"Until we have a receipt that says exactly what was received."

He looked at the inventory again.

Then he nodded.

"Fine."

The next question was where the containers were now.

The technical materials division had been reorganized two years earlier.

Its remaining stock had been distributed among three locations.

The first was the Academy's chemical reference store.

The second was the central catalyst laboratory.

The third was the Royal Infrastructure Department's controlled materials store.

The eighteen containers had been divided.

Six were sent to the chemical reference store.

Eight were sent to the catalyst laboratory.

Four were transferred to the Department.

The transfer records were complete.

For the first time that day, the figures added up.

The chemical reference store had six containers.

All six were present.

The catalyst laboratory had eight.

Six were present.

One had been used for a comparison test and recorded as consumed.

The eighth had been transferred to a different research section.

The Department's controlled materials store had four.

All four were present.

The total was eighteen.

Six plus six, one consumed, one transferred, and four.

The accounting was correct.

The containers had not vanished.

The material had been dispersed through several facilities because the original reserve stock had been divided among technical departments.

Lucien read the final inventory.

"Fourteen containers remain physically present."

"Two have been accounted for through documented use and transfer," Seraphina said.

"One was consumed during testing."

"And one was transferred."

"Yes."

"Then where is the transferred container?"

"The research section has its own inventory."

Theodore looked at the stores representative.

"Can we inspect the four containers held by the Department first?"

"Yes."

"Then the six at the chemical reference store."

Lucien nodded.

"We need to verify the technical specification before using any of them."

Theodore looked at Seraphina.

"Your record comparison has helped."

"It has established where the containers were recorded."

"That is what we needed."

She gathered the papers.

"Then I would like to return to the less exciting work of making sure nobody moves them again without a receipt."

At five in the afternoon, the technical examination began.

Their seals were intact.

The labels matched the original reserve allocation.

The Academy confirmed that the compound remained within its storage period.

The technical specification was compatible with the central assembly's original replacement design.

However, the current regulator had been modified during the second modernization.

The engineers needed to verify whether the compound was suitable for the modified configuration.

The six containers at the chemical reference store were examined next.

Their seals were intact, but their storage conditions differed slightly from those required for immediate installation.

They could still be used after additional testing.

The six containers at the catalyst laboratory were in better condition.

Their specifications matched the current central assembly's required formulation.

Lucien reviewed the report.

"Six containers are immediately suitable for controlled use."

"How much do you need?" Theodore asked.

Fenwick shook his head.
