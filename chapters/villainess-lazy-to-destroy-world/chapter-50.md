# Chapter 50 — The Balance That Had Changed Its Name

The amount was wrong.

Seraphina was certain of that much.

Not because she knew what the correct amount should have been, but because she had already read the original contract, the payment schedule, the equipment deduction and the Treasury's settlement summary.

The outstanding amount under the W-2 contract had been recorded as 320 crowns.

Now a document dated one year after Veylan Works ceased trading listed 1,160 crowns.

She placed the two schedules side by side.

"That is a difference of 840 crowns," she said.

Cedric leaned over the table.

"Could it include additional work?"

"Possibly."

"Storage fees?"

"Possibly."

"Then we should not assume the amount is wrong."

"I haven't."

She tapped the newer document.

"But we should establish what it represents."

Cedric pulled a chair closer.

The document was titled *Outstanding Contractual Obligations: Western Regional Infrastructure Works.*

It contained six entries.

W-2 was listed under Veylan Works.

The original contract reference appeared beside it.

The amount was 1,160 crowns.

There was no explanation.

Cedric read the line twice.

"Could this be the amount originally requested?"

"That is one possibility."

"Or a revised claim."

"Another."

"Or an accounting error."

"An especially convenient possibility, since it would allow everyone to stop thinking about it."

Cedric smiled slightly.

Seraphina did not.

She was beginning to dislike the number.

The original contract had established three payment stages.

The first covered structural completion.

The second covered equipment delivery and installation.

The third covered commissioning and final acceptance.

The first two stages had been assessed at 1,840 crowns.

The Treasury had approved 1,520 crowns after deducting 320 crowns for equipment that had not been returned.

The final commissioning payment had never been approved.

The company had ceased trading before the remaining technical requirements were fulfilled.

The 1,160 crowns could not simply be added to the 320.

It might represent a different calculation altogether.

Seraphina wrote the figures on a clean sheet.

**W-2 contract figures**

| Category | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Original assessed value | 1,840 crowns |
| Treasury-approved payment | 1,520 crowns |
| Equipment deduction | 320 crowns |
| Newly discovered outstanding amount | **1,160 crowns** |

The figures belonged to different records and had not yet been reconciled.

Cedric studied the page.

"The new amount is larger than the deduction but smaller than the original contract value."

"Yes."

"Could it be the unpaid portion of the contract?"

"It could. But then we need to know why the earlier schedule recorded only 320 crowns."

"Perhaps the earlier schedule covered the equipment deduction alone."

"Perhaps."

Seraphina set down her pen.

"Find out which office prepared the new schedule."

Holt arrived that afternoon with the answer.

The schedule had been prepared by the former Royal Infrastructure Allocation Office during its reorganization.

It was not a Treasury payment authorization.

It was a transfer statement.

The amount had been copied from a document titled *Provisional Outstanding Obligations Register.*

"Where is the original register?" Seraphina asked.

"At the Treasury's historical accounts archive."

"And the person who prepared it?"

"An accounts clerk named Edwin Marsh."

Seraphina paused.

"Is this the same Edwin Marsh who managed the Western Storage Annex?"

"No. Different person. The accounts clerk retired from the Allocation Office more than twenty years ago."

"Good. I would prefer not to acquire another mysterious connection through a shared name."

Holt made a note.

"The archive also has a reference to an attached calculation sheet."

"Then we need that."

The register was retrieved two days later.

It was a thick volume containing outstanding obligations from several regional projects.

Most entries were ordinary.

A delayed transport invoice.

A disputed land survey fee.

An unpaid contractor's balance.

A request for additional materials.

W-2 appeared on page 47.

The entry read:

*Veylan Works — Western Relay Station W-2 — provisional outstanding obligation: 1,160 crowns.*

A handwritten note followed.

*Subject to reconciliation of equipment deductions and final commissioning assessment.*

Seraphina read the sentence again.

"That is not a confirmed debt."

"No," Holt agreed.

"It is an amount awaiting reconciliation."

"Yes."

The next line contained a reference.

**CALCULATION SHEET: W-2/FIN/14.**

The sheet was not attached.

The Treasury archive had no copy.

The Allocation Office's successor archive had no copy.

The Central Project Review Office had a register entry showing that the sheet had been included in the original handover.

But the receiving inventory did not list individual calculation sheets.

It listed only the volume and the number of pages.

Seraphina stared at the transfer inventory.

"So we know the sheet existed."

"Yes."

"We know it was included in a handover."

"According to the outgoing register."

"We do not know whether it arrived."

"Correct."

She leaned back.

"Another document that was transferred without confirmation of its contents."

Holt looked uncomfortable.

"That appears to be the case."

Seraphina sighed.

"At least this time we know what we are looking for."

The next lead came from a retired Treasury accountant.

His name was Samuel Northvale.

He had worked on regional infrastructure accounts during the reorganization and remembered the W-2 settlement.

He agreed to meet Seraphina and Cedric at the Treasury's former regional office.

Northvale was a thin, elderly man who spoke slowly and disliked being interrupted.

He examined the register reference.

"W-2," he said. "Yes. I remember the project."

"Do you remember the 1,160-crown figure?" Seraphina asked.

"I remember that there was a disagreement about the amount."

"What was the disagreement?"

"The contractor's account included equipment and installation costs. The Treasury's account treated the unreturned equipment as a deduction. The technical office had not accepted commissioning."

"So the amounts were not measuring the same thing."

Northvale nodded.

"Exactly."

He examined the newer schedule.

"This is a provisional balance. It was never a final debt assessment."

"Was it intended to be paid?"

"No."

"Was it intended to be recovered?"

Northvale shook his head.

"Not without further review."

Seraphina waited.

"Do you remember the calculation?"

Northvale rubbed his forehead.

"Not completely. But I remember that the 1,160 crowns included a disputed equipment valuation."

Cedric leaned forward.

"What equipment?"

"Several regulating assemblies and their associated fittings."

"W-2 only?"

"That was the difficulty. The contractor's claim included materials intended for the wider regional reserve programme."

Seraphina exchanged a glance with Cedric.

The reserve programme had been separate from the W-2 construction contract.

"Why were reserve materials included in the W-2 account?" she asked.

Northvale frowned.

"They were not supposed to be. That was why the calculation needed reconciliation."

Northvale explained that Veylan Works had submitted an account covering several categories.

Some materials had been supplied specifically for W-2.

Others had been purchased for the regional reserve programme.

A third category involved fittings that could be used at more than one site, depending on technical approval.

The original payment review had separated the structural and equipment milestones from the reserve account.

But the provisional outstanding obligations register had been prepared from a combined summary.

It had included amounts that were still disputed.

"Was this an attempt to claim the same money twice?" Cedric asked.

Northvale looked at him.

"I cannot tell you that."

"Why not?"

"Because the combined account did not establish whether the materials were claimed twice, transferred between projects or simply listed under the wrong heading."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the correct answer is that the records were not sufficiently reconciled."

"Yes."

Northvale closed the folder.

"I remember recommending that the accounts be separated before any final decision was made."

"Was that recommendation accepted?"

"I do not know."

"Did you prepare the calculation sheet?"

"No. I reviewed it."

"Who prepared it?"

Northvale thought for a moment.

"An accounts officer in the Allocation Office. I believe her name was Eleanor Price."

Holt wrote down the name.

"Do you know where she went?"

"She transferred to the regional expenditure division after the reorganization."

Seraphina thanked him.

It was not a solution.

But it was the first explanation that connected the figures without requiring anyone to invent missing money.

Eleanor Price was still alive.

She had retired several years earlier and lived in a small town outside the capital.

Her response arrived within a week.

She remembered preparing the provisional register.

She also remembered that W-2 had been difficult to reconcile.

Her letter included a note:

*The 1,160-crown figure was a consolidated provisional balance, not an approved contractor payment or an assessed debt. It included disputed equipment valuation, materials attributed to the regional reserve programme and a portion of the contractor's claimed installation costs. I recommended that the categories be separated before any final accounting decision.*

Seraphina read the letter twice.

Then she looked at Cedric.

"That explains the difference."

"Does it?"

"It explains where the number came from. It does not establish the correct final balance."

Cedric nodded.

"Do we have her calculation?"

"She says she retained a working copy."

Eleanor Price's working copy arrived in a sealed envelope.

It was not an official document.

The paper was yellowed, the handwriting faded, and several figures had been crossed out.

But the calculation was clear enough.

**Provisional W-2 account — Price working copy**

| Category | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Contractor's claimed installation costs | 520 crowns |
| Equipment valuation under dispute | 420 crowns |
| Reserve materials awaiting allocation | 220 crowns |
| **Combined provisional total** | **1,160 crowns** |

The breakdown was from a retained working copy, not an approved final settlement.

The three categories had been combined into one figure.

The 520 crowns related to claimed installation costs.

The 420 crowns related to equipment valuation.

The 220 crowns related to reserve materials.

None of the three had been finally approved.

Seraphina placed the working copy beside the original contract.

"The amount was not invented."

"No," Cedric said.

"It was assembled from claims and unresolved valuations."

"Yes."

"And then transferred into a register where it looked like a single outstanding obligation."

Cedric looked at the handwritten note.

"That could easily be mistaken for a debt."

"Especially if someone reads only the summary."

Seraphina placed a mark beside the entry.

*Provisional consolidated balance. Not approved. Not payable without reconciliation.*

The committee reviewed the discovery at its next meeting.

Minister Garrick was particularly interested.

"Could the Treasury have paid the 1,160 crowns?"

"No," Seraphina said. "The register did not authorize payment."

"Could Veylan Works have demanded it?"

"It could have submitted a claim. Whether that claim would have been accepted is another question."

Theodore looked at the calculation.

"And the 320-crown deduction?"

"That remains separate. It was recorded against equipment that had not been returned under the original contract's terms."

Lucien examined the list.

"Does the 420-crown valuation include the equipment assemblies we have found?"

"Not necessarily. The calculation does not identify individual serial numbers."

"Then we cannot connect it to RC-17-063."

"Correct."

Garrick looked dissatisfied.

"Can we establish the final balance?"

Seraphina answered carefully.

"Not from these records alone."

She placed three documents on the table.

"The original contract establishes the payment stages. The Treasury schedule establishes the approved payment and deduction. The provisional register establishes a disputed consolidated balance. The working copy explains how that balance was assembled."

She paused.

"What we do not have is a final reconciliation separating the equipment, installation and reserve materials."

Theodore nodded.

"Then that is the finding."

The committee agreed to amend the W-2 account.

The 1,160 crowns would not be recorded as a confirmed debt.

The 320-crown deduction would remain subject to equipment reconciliation.

The original payment of 1,520 crowns would remain recognized as the amount approved under the first two contractual milestones.

The final commissioning stage would remain incomplete.

The reserve materials would be accounted for separately under the reserve programme.

No payment would be recommended without supporting documentation.

No recovery would be recommended without a confirmed liability.

The decision was not dramatic.

It was, however, the first time the four figures had been placed in their proper categories.

Seraphina signed the review note.

Then she handed it to Holt.

"Make sure the Treasury receives the calculation copy."

"Yes."

"And the Central Stores office."

"Why Central Stores?"

"Because some of the materials may have been transferred there."

Holt made another note.

Seraphina looked at the growing stack of documents.

"Please include a clear warning that the 1,160 crowns is not an approved amount."

"I will."

That evening, Seraphina received a letter from Darius Veylan.

The letter had been forwarded through the western regional office after the committee's request for clarification.

Darius had read the summary of the provisional balance.

He was concerned that the combined figure might be mistaken for a final contractor claim.

He confirmed that Veylan Works had submitted installation costs and equipment valuations, but he could not verify the 220 crowns attributed to reserve materials.

Those materials had been managed through a separate purchasing account.

He also clarified that the equipment left in storage had not been released for installation because the intended site requirements had never been confirmed.

His letter ended with a short statement.

*I cannot confirm the final financial balance. My responsibility was limited to technical inspection and the condition of equipment assigned to the project. I advised against release where installation requirements were incomplete. I do not have authority to determine contractual payment.*

Seraphina set the letter down.

It was consistent with everything else they had found.

Darius had been a technical engineer.

He had not been the contractor's financial officer, the Treasury's decision-maker or the person responsible for the Allocation Office's records.

His knowledge was valuable.

It was also limited.

She wrote a reply thanking him for the clarification.

The next morning, Holt brought a document from the Central Stores archive.

It was a list of reserve materials transferred during the reorganization.

The list included several fitting sets and protective containers originally purchased under the regional reserve programme.

One entry referred to 220 crowns' worth of materials.

The amount matched the third category in Eleanor Price's working copy.

Seraphina looked up.

"Where were they sent?"

"Central Stores, Bay Four."

"Are they still there?"

"No. The receiving register shows they were transferred to the Western Regional Stores Annex."

"Which annex?"

"Annex Four."

Seraphina paused.

That was where RC-17-063 had been held.

"Do we have a detailed inventory?"

"Not yet."

"Then request it."

Holt looked at the document.

"This could resolve the reserve materials portion."

"It could identify what happened to the materials. It won't automatically establish whether the contractor's claim was valid."

"Of course."

Seraphina pointed to the transfer entry.

"Also, do not assume the 220 crowns represents the exact value of the items that arrived. We need the original purchasing schedule and the receiving record."

Holt nodded.

The detailed inventory arrived three days later.

It showed that the reserve materials had been received in two batches.

The first contained protective containers and standard fittings.

The second contained specialized components intended for regional evaluation.

Both batches had been entered into Central Stores.

The receiving officer had signed for the quantities.

The materials were later transferred to the Western Regional Stores Annex after the central warehouse was reorganized.

There was no evidence that the materials had been lost.

There was also no evidence that the 220-crown amount represented a separate payment due to Veylan Works.

The amount appeared to be an estimated value used in the provisional account.

Seraphina added the inventory to the report.

The reserve materials were now accounted for physically.

Their financial treatment remained subject to the original purchasing records.

At the end of the week, the committee met to review its progress.

The register now contained a clearer account of the six facilities, the reserve programme, the old review group and the incomplete financial settlements.

Theodore looked through the latest version.

"We have made considerable progress."

"We have made the records more accurate," Seraphina replied.

"That is progress."

"Yes."

He closed the register.

"Your appointment ends in twelve days."

"I am aware."

"The Department has asked whether you would consider an extension."

Seraphina looked at him.

"For how long?"

"Three months."

"No."

The answer came quickly enough that Garrick looked up.

Theodore remained calm.

"Would you hear the proposed terms first?"

"I know what they will involve."

"Perhaps they are narrower than you expect."

"Perhaps. But the work has already expanded beyond the original historical review."

Theodore nodded.

"We would need your assistance in preparing the final handover."

"That is reasonable."

"And in reviewing the department's new register."

"That is also reasonable."

"But not a continuing appointment?"

"No."

He studied her for a moment.

"Then what would you accept?"

Seraphina considered it.

"A written handover review. One meeting to explain the register and its limitations. After that, the responsible departments should manage their own work."

Garrick looked at her.

"That seems rather limited."

"It is supposed to be."

Lucien quietly covered a smile with his hand.

Theodore nodded.

"Very well. We will prepare the handover terms."

That night, Seraphina returned to the estate's northern wing.

The investigation had taken months.

It had uncovered missing records, misclassified payments, unfinished construction, uncalibrated equipment, incomplete transfers and a committee that had never met again.

It had not uncovered a single mastermind behind the entire mess.

There were mistakes.

There were poor decisions.

There were responsibilities that had never been assigned.

There were also people who had acted carefully within the limits of their authority, even when the larger system failed to make use of their work.

Seraphina found that distinction important.

She was not interested in accusing someone simply because their name appeared repeatedly in an old file.

Nor did she want the committee to declare everything harmless because no single act of wrongdoing had been proven.

The records needed to say what they actually established.

Nothing more.

Nothing less.

Mira entered with a tray.

"My lady, your mother asked whether you would attend breakfast with the family tomorrow."

"Was there a reason?"

"She said it would be pleasant to have everyone together."

Seraphina looked at the tray.

"That sounds suspicious."

Mira placed the tea down.

"Your mother also said that you have spent too many evenings working."

"That sounds less suspicious."

"Your father agrees with her."

Seraphina sighed.

"Then it is an organized campaign."

Mira adjusted the tray.

"Would you like me to prepare your blue dress?"

"Why?"

"For breakfast."

"Is breakfast now a formal occasion?"

"I believe your mother intends to make it one."

Seraphina looked toward the window.

She had spent months trying to prevent a royal infrastructure programme from remaining unfinished forever.

Now she was apparently expected to attend a family breakfast without being permitted to read during it.

The kingdom was full of unreasonable demands.

Some were simply closer to home.

She picked up her tea.

"Prepare the blue dress."

Mira smiled.

"Yes, my lady."

Seraphina looked at the papers still spread across her desk.

Twelve days remained in her appointment.

For the first time, the end was close enough to be counted.

And she intended to reach it.

Preferably before someone discovered another old register.
