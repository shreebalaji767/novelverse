# Chapter 59 — The Water That Had Been Sent Somewhere Else

The southern drainage channel had been cleared to prevent flooding.

It had succeeded.

The water was now reaching a property that had not experienced flooding in several years.

Seraphina stood beside the lower channel and watched a narrow stream run through the newly cleared bed.

The water was not moving particularly quickly.

It was not overflowing.

It was not even especially deep.

The downstream landowner, however, had sent a letter describing it as an unacceptable increase in water discharge.

Seraphina had read the letter twice.

The phrase *unacceptable increase* had appeared four times.

The phrase *measurable increase* had appeared once.

She suspected the distinction was important.

Cedric stood beside her, holding the inspection report.

"The lower channel was originally designed to carry water from the western fields toward the southern stream."

"Is it still doing that?"

"Yes."

"Then what changed?"

"The upper section had accumulated silt and vegetation. Clearing it restored the original flow."

Seraphina looked toward the downstream fields.

"And the lower section?"

"Partly obstructed."

"By what?"

"Sediment, fallen branches, and an old timber barrier."

She turned to him.

"A barrier?"

Cedric nodded.

"It appears to have been placed across the channel years ago."

"By whom?"

"We don't know."

Seraphina looked at the water again.

The channel was wider than the narrow stream currently passing through it.

Its banks were uneven.

On the far side, the ground sloped gently toward a cultivated field.

There was no sign of immediate danger.

There was, however, a clear indication that someone had altered the watercourse.

"Let's inspect the barrier before deciding what to do."

The barrier was found half a mile downstream.

It consisted of three old timber beams and several stones placed across the channel.

The timber had rotted.

The stones had settled.

Water passed through the gaps, but the structure slowed the flow and caused sediment to accumulate upstream.

Master Colin Armitage examined it.

"This is not part of the original drainage design."

"Can you tell when it was built?" Seraphina asked.

"Not precisely. The timber is old, but that is not enough to establish a date."

Cedric looked at the structure.

"Could it have been installed during the road works?"

"Possibly," Armitage replied. "There are references to temporary drainage adjustments in the old survey."

Seraphina examined the map.

The channel ran from the upper western fields through Veyr land before entering property belonging to the Bellmont estate.

Beyond Bellmont, it crossed a narrow strip of land owned by the Ashcombe family.

The southern stream lay beyond that.

Three estates.

One watercourse.

She looked at Cedric.

"Does the agreement cover all three?"

"No. The original agreement is between Veyr and Bellmont."

"What about Ashcombe?"

"There is a separate drainage access document, but it only grants permission for the channel to cross their land."

"Does it assign maintenance?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at the barrier again.

"Then we have three different questions. Who owns the land, who maintains the channel, and who is affected by the water."

Armitage nodded.

"Yes."

"Good. We shall not confuse them."

The downstream landowner was Lord Benedict Marlowe.

He was not the same person as Arthur Bell, the Marlowe estate manager, who had been involved in the bridge and mill arrangements.

The two Marlowe households were related through an older branch of the family, but the property affected by the drainage channel was managed separately.

Lord Benedict arrived the following morning.

He was accompanied by his steward and a local farmer named Tomas Reed.

Reed cultivated the lower fields and had noticed the increased flow.

"The channel used to carry less water," he explained.

"Before it was cleared?" Seraphina asked.

"Yes."

"Did the fields flood?"

"Not for several years."

"Did the channel overflow before it was cleared?"

"Sometimes, during heavy rain."

Seraphina looked at the ground.

"Where did the water go?"

"Into the lower field."

"Was that a problem?"

"It damaged the eastern crop."

Lord Benedict joined the conversation.

"The timber barrier was installed to reduce the flow into our land."

"Who installed it?" Cedric asked.

"We believe it was done by a former estate manager."

"Was permission obtained from House Veyr?"

Lord Benedict hesitated.

"I cannot find a record."

Seraphina looked at the barrier.

"Was the structure maintained?"

"No."

"Was it inspected?"

"No."

"Then it is possible that the channel was gradually blocked without anyone understanding how much water it was diverting."

Lord Benedict looked toward the fields.

"That is what I suspect."

Seraphina turned to Armitage.

"Can you determine the original water capacity?"

"Approximately. We have old survey measurements and can compare the channel's current dimensions."

"Can you determine how much water the barrier diverted?"

"Not exactly. We would need to inspect the land around it."

"Then do that."

The survey took three days.

Armitage examined the channel from the upper fields to the southern stream.

He measured the width and depth at several points.

He recorded the slope, the condition of the banks, and the location of the old timber barrier.

He also found two smaller obstructions.

One was a fallen tree.

The other was a stone-lined opening that had been partially covered by soil.

The opening led to an old overflow ditch.

It had once carried excess water away from the main channel during heavy rain.

The ditch was now almost entirely blocked.

The original drainage system had therefore contained three elements:

- A main channel carrying ordinary flow.
- An overflow ditch for periods of heavy rain.
- A timber control structure that had reduced the amount of water reaching the lower fields.

The timber barrier had been intended as a temporary measure.

It had never been removed.

The overflow ditch had not been maintained.

The main channel had gradually accumulated sediment.

The system had become less efficient.

Clearing the upper section had restored part of the original flow without restoring the lower overflow arrangements.

Seraphina studied the survey.

"So the upper channel is working properly, but the lower system cannot handle the water."

Armitage nodded.

"That is the likely explanation."

"Can we reopen the overflow ditch?"

"Yes, but we must inspect where it discharges. It currently appears to lead toward the Ashcombe boundary."

"Then we need their agreement."

"Correct."

Lord Benedict examined the report.

"If the overflow ditch is restored, the water should be distributed more safely."

"Provided the lower banks are reinforced," Armitage said.

"And the timber barrier?" Cedric asked.

"It should be removed, but not before the overflow ditch is restored."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we do the work in that order."

Lord Benedict looked relieved.

"Would House Veyr cover the entire cost?"

"No," Seraphina replied.

He looked at her.

She pointed to the map.

"Veyr owns the upper section. Marlowe owns the lower fields. Ashcombe owns the land crossed by the overflow ditch. The work benefits all three properties, but that does not mean every cost should be divided equally."

Cedric opened his notebook.

"How do you propose we divide it?"

"First, establish the cost of maintaining the main channel. Then establish the cost of restoring the overflow ditch. Then identify which parts are necessary for the protection of each property."

Armitage nodded.

"That is the correct approach."

Seraphina looked at Lord Benedict.

"We should not negotiate the cost until we know what the work actually involves."

He agreed.

The Ashcombe estate responded cautiously.

Its steward, Master Philip Grant, was willing to permit a survey of the overflow ditch but did not want to accept responsibility for a watercourse that had not been formally recorded in the estate's property documents.

Seraphina understood the concern.

The ditch crossed Ashcombe land.

If it were restored without a clear agreement, the estate might inherit an ongoing maintenance obligation.

Cedric arranged a meeting.

Grant arrived with a copy of the old access document.

It granted House Veyr permission to maintain a drainage channel across the Ashcombe boundary.

It did not mention the overflow ditch.

It also did not identify the discharge point.

Grant placed the document on the table.

"We cannot accept responsibility for an unrecorded channel."

Seraphina nodded.

"That is reasonable."

Grant looked surprised.

"I beg your pardon?"

"You should not be expected to maintain a structure that your estate never agreed to maintain."

He relaxed slightly.

"Then we are in agreement."

"Not entirely. The ditch may still be necessary to prevent water damage to all three properties."

"That is possible."

"Then we need to establish its route, condition, and purpose before deciding whether access should be granted."

Grant considered this.

"Would House Veyr maintain it?"

"If the agreement requires us to do so."

"And Marlowe?"

"That depends on the benefits and obligations established by the survey."

Grant nodded.

"I can agree to a survey."

Seraphina looked at Cedric.

"Arrange it."

The meeting ended without a new agreement.

That was acceptable.

A survey did not require anyone to promise more than they understood.

The overflow ditch was restored in stages.

First, the route was cleared.

Then the banks were examined.

The old discharge point was found beneath a patch of dense vegetation.

It led into a shallow natural depression that eventually drained toward the southern stream.

The depression had not been cultivated.

It was covered in grass and low shrubs.

The water could pass through without damaging fields or buildings.

Master Hobb inspected the ditch and recommended repairing two sections of collapsed stone lining.

He also advised against rebuilding the old timber barrier.

Instead, he proposed a simple stone overflow lip that would allow excess water to enter the ditch when the main channel reached a certain level.

The arrangement would require less maintenance.

It would also avoid creating another obstruction.

Seraphina approved the design after Armitage confirmed that the measurements were suitable.

The work began.

The main channel was cleared carefully.

The old barrier was removed.

The overflow ditch was reopened.

The two damaged sections were repaired.

The discharge point was protected with stone to prevent erosion.

The water began to move through the system as intended.

It was not a dramatic transformation.

There was no sudden surge.

No miraculous restoration.

The channel simply carried water more efficiently.

During ordinary flow, most of the water remained in the main channel.

During heavier flow, excess water could enter the overflow ditch.

The lower fields were protected.

The water reached the southern stream without flooding the cultivated land.

The three estates had found a practical solution.

The agreement took another two weeks.

House Veyr would maintain the upper main channel.

The Marlowe estate would maintain the lower main channel and its banks.

The Ashcombe estate would grant access to the overflow ditch and permit necessary maintenance.

Veyr and Marlowe would share the cost of the overflow structure.

The Ashcombe estate would not be responsible for maintaining it, but it would receive notice before major repairs.

The agreement also required inspections after significant floods.

The original access documents remained valid where they did not conflict with the new schedule.

The old timber barrier was recorded as a removed temporary structure.

The overflow ditch was added to the estate maps.

The new discharge point was marked.

The three parties signed.

Seraphina approved House Veyr's obligations.

She did not claim ownership of the entire watercourse.

She did not promise that flooding would never occur.

She simply ensured that the people responsible for maintaining each section understood what the system was supposed to do.

A month later, the southern channel survived its first heavy rain.

The rainfall was not extraordinary, but it was enough to test the repairs.

The main channel filled.

The overflow lip began carrying excess water into the ditch.

The lower fields remained dry.

The southern stream received the additional flow without overflowing its banks.

Tomas Reed sent a short report.

*The drainage system performed as intended. No damage to the eastern crop.*

Seraphina read the note.

Then she handed it to Cedric.

"Good."

Cedric smiled.

"That is all?"

"What else would you like?"

"Perhaps a little celebration."

"For a drainage channel?"

"Some estates would consider it an achievement."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Then those estates should celebrate."

Cedric laughed.

That evening, Evelyne found Seraphina reviewing the updated map.

"You have finally finished the drainage channel."

"For now."

"Is there another problem?"

"Not with this one."

Evelyne sat beside her.

"That sounds almost like satisfaction."

"It is satisfaction."

"Then why do you look concerned?"

Seraphina turned the map around.

The channel crossed three estates.

The main road crossed two.

The old orchard watercourse crossed the Marlowe boundary.

The mill's supply channel had its own agreement.

The bridge carried a road used by several villages.

The estate's responsibilities extended far beyond its own fences.

"I am beginning to understand why the old arrangements were so complicated," Seraphina said.

Evelyne looked at the map.

"Because land is rarely as separate as the documents make it appear."

"Water certainly isn't."

"No."

Seraphina tapped the southern channel.

"One estate clears a ditch. Another estate receives more water. A third estate owns the land where the overflow passes."

"And if they do not speak to one another?"

"Someone's fields flood."

Evelyne smiled.

"That is why agreements exist."

"Then they should be reviewed more often."

"Agreed."

Seraphina returned to the map.

The work had taken weeks.

It had required surveyors, masons, farmers, estate stewards, and the cooperation of three families.

No single person could have solved it alone.

She found that reassuring.

She also found it exhausting.

Evelyne watched her for a moment.

"Your father has received another letter from the regional office."

Seraphina slowly lowered her pen.

"About what?"

"Several estates have asked for copies of the shared-property register."

"Send them the format."

"They have."

"Then what do they want?"

"They would like to establish a district inspection schedule."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

"That sounds like a regional responsibility."

"It is."

"Then why are you telling me?"

"Because they have suggested that House Veyr host the first meeting."

Seraphina stared at the map.

The southern channel was finally working.

The bridge was scheduled for repair.

The pasture road had its boundary marker.

The storage building had a proper agreement.

And now the regional office wanted to bring more people to her estate.

She put down her pen.

"Tell them Cedric may attend."

Evelyne smiled.

"That is what your father suggested."

"Excellent."

"However, they also asked whether you would give the opening address."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

"No."

Evelyne laughed.

Outside, the rain began again.

The southern channel carried the water away.

For the first time in weeks, Seraphina could hear rain without wondering which estate would receive it.

She decided to enjoy the sound while it lasted.
