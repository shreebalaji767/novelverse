# Chapter 45 — The Warehouse That Might Have Been a Relay Station

The eastern office's reply arrived on a Tuesday.

It contained four pages, two maps, a property extract and a note from the regional records administrator.

The note read:

*The property identified in reference E-1 is currently registered as Warehouse 27, Eastern Trade Road. The office cannot confirm whether this is the same structure described in the historical infrastructure schedule.*

Seraphina read the sentence again.

Then she placed the letter on the table.

"At least they haven't claimed it is definitely a warehouse."

Holt looked at her.

"Would that have been preferable?"

"Not particularly. It would only have created another problem."

He opened the property extract.

Warehouse 27 belonged to the Eastern Regional Transport Authority. It stood beside an old freight road, near a junction where two commercial routes met.

The building was described as a storage facility for road-maintenance materials.

There was no mention of magical equipment.

There was, however, an unusual entry in the historical property description.

*Subterranean chamber retained as a restricted structural feature.*

Seraphina tapped the line.

"What does that mean?"

Holt checked the attached map.

"It could refer to a cellar."

Lucien, who had been examining the eastern schedule, looked up.

"Or an equipment chamber."

Seraphina sighed.

"Naturally."

The original E-1 schedule was less helpful.

It described an eastern relay station intended to regulate mana flow along the commercial road network.

The proposed installation included:

- A regulating chamber.
- Two inspection galleries.
- A maintenance access route.
- A reinforced foundation.
- A connection to the regional mana conduit.

Unlike S-3, E-1 had a construction authorization.

Unlike A-2, it also had an equipment allocation.

The document identified the supervising engineer as Master Colin Reeve.

Seraphina recognized the name.

Reeve had appeared in the records of an earlier regional survey.

She checked the reference.

It was the same man.

"Does anyone know whether he is still alive?" she asked.

Holt consulted the personnel index.

"Retired. He lives near the eastern capital."

"Can we speak with him?"

"Yes. The eastern office has already located his address."

Lucien studied the schedule.

"The most important question is whether the facility was ever connected to the conduit."

"Can the building itself tell us?"

"Not without an inspection."

"Then we need permission."

Holt nodded.

"I'll request it."

The eastern office's reply came three days later.

The property could be inspected, but only with the permission of the Transport Authority's regional director.

The warehouse was still in use.

Its underground chamber had been sealed for safety reasons.

No one was permitted to enter without a structural assessment.

Seraphina read the response.

"That seems sensible."

Lucien nodded.

"An old underground chamber should not be opened casually."

"Can the assessment establish whether it was originally built for E-1?"

"Possibly. We would need the original plans or enough surviving structural features to compare."

Seraphina turned to Holt.

"Ask for the plans before requesting an inspection."

"Understood."

"And make sure the Transport Authority knows we are not proposing to reopen the chamber."

Holt smiled faintly.

"You have become very particular about that."

"I have learned that people hear the word 'inspection' and imagine someone arriving with a shovel."

Master Colin Reeve arrived in the capital the following week.

He was an elderly man with a careful manner and an uncomfortable habit of correcting dates before answering questions.

He examined the E-1 schedule first.

"Yes," he said. "I remember this project."

"Did you supervise its construction?" Theodore asked.

"Not entirely. I supervised the site assessment and the first stage of structural work."

"What happened afterward?"

Reeve adjusted his spectacles.

"The project was transferred to another engineering office."

"Why?"

"The original design was altered."

Lucien leaned forward.

"Altered how?"

"The location of the regulating chamber changed."

Seraphina looked at the map.

"From where to where?"

"Approximately forty yards east. The first position had unsuitable ground conditions."

"Was the new position approved?"

"Yes. After additional assessment."

"Was the foundation constructed there?"

Reeve nodded.

"That was the second stage."

Theodore examined the authorization.

"Was the equipment installed?"

Reeve paused.

"I did not supervise that stage."

"Who did?"

"I believe the installation was assigned to the Eastern Technical Works Office."

Holt wrote down the name.

"Do you remember the supervising engineer?"

"Master Dorian Pell, perhaps. I would not swear to it."

Seraphina looked at the document.

The schedule listed Reeve as supervising engineer.

It did not identify Pell.

"Was there a handover report?"

"There should have been."

"Did you prepare one?"

"I prepared a site completion statement."

"Was it accepted?"

Reeve considered the question.

"I submitted it. Whether it was formally accepted is another matter."

The site completion statement was found in the eastern archive two weeks later.

It confirmed that the foundation and outer structure had been completed.

It also recorded a change in the design.

The regulating chamber had been moved to the eastern side of the property because the original ground was unsuitable.

The inspection galleries had been shortened.

The maintenance access route had been redirected.

A handwritten note appeared at the bottom.

*Final chamber access to remain sealed until equipment installation and calibration are complete.*

Seraphina examined the note.

"Who wrote this?"

The signature was difficult to read.

Reeve studied it.

"That looks like mine."

"Do you remember writing it?"

"I remember the instruction. I cannot remember the exact wording."

Lucien examined the statement.

"This is important. It means the chamber was not intended to be opened for ordinary storage."

"Was it safe to leave sealed?" Theodore asked.

"Yes, provided the structure remained stable and no equipment was operating inside."

"Could the Transport Authority have converted the building into a warehouse without knowing what was underneath?"

Reeve nodded.

"That is possible. The building's upper structure was designed to resemble a maintenance depot. It was intended to house tools and replacement components."

Seraphina looked at the property description.

The warehouse might have been built as part of the relay station from the beginning.

Its later use was not necessarily a reclassification.

It might simply have been the ordinary function of the building's upper floors.

The eastern office sent another document.

It was a transport authority property transfer from fourteen years earlier.

The building had been transferred from the Royal Infrastructure Department to the Eastern Regional Transport Authority.

The transfer description listed:

*Roadside maintenance warehouse, including restricted underground structural chamber.*

The property had not been renamed.

The transfer had simply omitted the E-1 designation.

The equipment chamber had been retained as a restricted feature.

Seraphina read the document carefully.

"Why was the property transferred?"

Holt checked the accompanying memorandum.

"The infrastructure department had reorganized its eastern maintenance facilities. The transport authority took responsibility for the road network."

"Did it also take responsibility for the underground chamber?"

"The transfer says it did not assume responsibility for any active magical installation."

"Was the chamber active?"

"We don't know."

Lucien looked at the technical schedule.

"It should not have been active without equipment installation and calibration."

"Should not have been," Seraphina repeated.

"Correct."

"That is not the same as confirming it wasn't."

Lucien nodded.

"No. We need the installation record."

The Eastern Technical Works Office had been dissolved eleven years earlier.

Its remaining records had been transferred to the Eastern Regional Infrastructure Archive.

The archive's index listed an installation file under E-1.

The file itself was missing.

However, a separate equipment register contained an entry for a regulating assembly.

It had been dispatched to the site.

The receiving signature belonged to Master Dorian Pell.

The equipment number was **ER-22-091**.

Lucien compared it with the committee's earlier records.

It did not match the casing transferred from Central Stores to S-3.

It was a different component.

Seraphina looked at the register.

"Then E-1 had its own equipment allocation."

"Yes," Lucien replied.

"Was the equipment installed?"

"The receiving entry proves it reached the site. It does not prove installation."

Holt found another line.

*Equipment held in chamber pending final calibration.*

Seraphina leaned back.

"That sounds familiar."

"It does," Lucien said.

"How many times has this happened?"

"No one knows."

The committee requested the remaining installation papers.

The eastern archive located a maintenance inspection from twelve years earlier.

It was short.

The inspector had entered the upper warehouse but had not entered the underground chamber.

The report stated:

*Restricted chamber remains sealed. No external signs of structural failure. Equipment status not verified.*

The inspection had been conducted by Master Samuel Dyer.

Seraphina looked at the name.

"Is this the same Samuel Dyer who examined the casing later transferred to S-3?"

Holt checked.

"Yes. The Technical Evaluation Unit inspector."

Lucien frowned.

"He may have been assigned to multiple regional inspections."

"Does the report say why he visited?"

"Routine property inspection," Holt replied.

"Not a technical inspection of the magical installation?"

"No."

Seraphina looked at the wording again.

The report had confirmed that the building was structurally intact.

It had not confirmed the condition of the equipment.

The difference mattered.

Master Dyer was contacted.

He remembered the visit.

"The Transport Authority wanted confirmation that the warehouse was safe for continued use," he explained.

"Did you inspect the underground chamber?" Theodore asked.

"No. I was not authorized to open it."

"Did you know there was equipment inside?"

"I knew there was supposed to be."

"Did you confirm it was there?"

"No."

Seraphina examined his report.

"Why did you write that the equipment status was not verified?"

"Because I had not verified it."

"Did anyone ask you to change the wording?"

Dyer shook his head.

"No. It was an ordinary inspection."

"Did you consider the chamber safe?"

"The structure appeared stable from the outside. That is all I could say."

Lucien asked whether he had seen any signs of mana activity.

Dyer shook his head again.

"I did not have instruments with me. The visit was not an operational assessment."

Seraphina nodded.

The report was accurate.

It was simply being used for a purpose it had never been intended to serve.

The committee meeting took place after Dyer's statement.

Theodore reviewed the findings.

"E-1 was constructed. The foundation and upper structure were completed. Equipment was delivered to the site, but installation and calibration remain unconfirmed."

"Correct," Seraphina said.

"The chamber remains sealed."

"Yes."

"And the property is currently used as a road-maintenance warehouse."

"Yes."

Garrick examined the property transfer.

"Then the Transport Authority may have been operating the building without knowing its original purpose."

"Or it may have known about the chamber but not the equipment," Holt said.

Lucien folded his hands.

"The chamber should not be opened until the technical team establishes what was installed and whether any components remain connected."

Theodore nodded.

"Then we should arrange a controlled inspection."

Seraphina looked at him.

"Only after the Transport Authority approves it and the structural assessment is complete."

"Agreed."

"And the committee should not describe E-1 as operational."

"Agreed."

Garrick looked at the register.

"We now have three facilities with incomplete histories."

"Four," Seraphina said.

He looked up.

"Four?"

"Depending on how we classify the sixth reserve facility."

The room became quiet.

Holt opened the final historical funding summary.

The sixth facility had appeared only as a reserve allocation.

There was no location.

No property reference.

No construction authorization.

No supervising engineer.

The amount assigned was considerably smaller than the amounts allocated to N-4, W-2 and A-2.

A handwritten note appeared beside it.

*Reserve installation — subject to regional necessity.*

Seraphina examined the line.

"Could this have been a contingency fund rather than a facility?"

"Possibly," Holt replied.

"Then why was it listed alongside the five named facilities?"

"We don't know."

Garrick studied the amount.

"Was the money ever spent?"

Holt checked the Treasury reference.

"There are two payments associated with the reserve allocation."

"To whom?"

"One to the Central Stores Authority. The other to an engineering consultancy."

"Which consultancy?"

"That is the problem. The payment summary lists only the receiving account."

Seraphina looked at the amount.

"Then we need the underlying vouchers."

Garrick nodded.

"I will request them from Treasury."

Later that afternoon, Seraphina visited Liora.

The saintess had returned from the southern provinces and was sorting through correspondence at the small office assigned to her charitable work.

Several boxes stood beside her desk.

Nessa was attempting to organize them.

Seraphina looked at the boxes.

"How many letters do you receive?"

"Too many," Liora replied.

"That sounds familiar."

Liora smiled.

"Your committee has been busy."

"Unfortunately."

"Did you find the southern facility?"

"Not exactly. We found a proposed project that never obtained permission to proceed."

"Was it on private land?"

"Yes. The owner wanted detailed plans before granting access."

Liora nodded.

"That seems reasonable."

"It was. The plans were never supplied."

Liora looked at her.

"Then perhaps the owner was not the problem."

"That is what I concluded."

Nessa placed another box on the table.

"Lady Liora, these are the letters from the southern infirmaries."

Liora turned toward her.

"Thank you."

Seraphina noticed the labels.

The letters concerned the delivery of medical instruments, transport delays and shortages of clean bandages.

They were unrelated to the infrastructure committee.

She did not ask to examine them.

Instead, she looked at Liora.

"How are things in the southern provinces?"

"Better in some places. Worse in others."

"That is not a very encouraging answer."

"It is an honest one."

Liora explained that the revised delivery forms had reduced confusion between regional stores and local infirmaries. However, several smaller clinics still struggled to arrange transportation.

The Church could provide supplies, but it could not always provide the vehicles or staff needed to deliver them.

"We are trying to coordinate with local authorities," Liora said. "But every district has different arrangements."

"Can the regional council help?"

"Some councils can. Others have no available transport budget."

Seraphina considered that.

"Then perhaps the first step is identifying which clinics can receive supplies reliably and which cannot."

Liora nodded.

"We have started doing that."

Seraphina smiled faintly.

"Good."

Liora looked at her.

"You sound relieved."

"I am. I prefer problems that people have already started solving."

The conversation was interrupted by a knock.

A young assistant entered carrying a sealed letter.

"Lady Liora, this came from the Bellford infirmary."

Liora opened it.

Her expression changed.

Seraphina noticed immediately.

"What happened?"

"The infirmary has received the instruments from the regional storehouse."

"All of them?"

"Yes. But the delivery was delayed again."

"Why?"

Liora read the letter more carefully.

"The transport authority changed the route because a bridge is under repair."

Seraphina looked at the map on Liora's desk.

"Which bridge?"

"The eastern road bridge."

Seraphina glanced at the letter.

"Is that near Warehouse 27?"

Liora checked.

"No. The infirmary is much farther south."

"Then it is probably unrelated."

"Probably," Liora agreed.

They returned to the letter.

The instruments had arrived, but the infirmary still needed replacement parts for two damaged devices.

Liora began writing a reply.

Seraphina watched her for a moment.

"You should ask the regional storehouse to record the replacement parts separately."

Liora looked up.

"Why?"

"Because if they are included in the same delivery entry, someone may assume the entire order was complete."

Liora considered it.

"You're right."

She added a note to the reply.

Seraphina did not offer to take over.

The work belonged to Liora and the people responsible for the infirmary.

She had only noticed a small possibility of confusion.

That was enough.

When Seraphina returned to the committee office, Holt had received another message from the eastern archive.

The original E-1 equipment installation register had been found.

It contained three entries.

The first recorded delivery.

The second recorded inspection of the external casing.

The third was marked:

*Installation deferred pending calibration authority.*

There was no later entry.

The equipment had reached the building.

It had been inspected.

It had not been confirmed as installed.

Seraphina read the final line.

"Who had authority to approve calibration?"

Holt checked the accompanying index.

"The Central Technical Evaluation Board."

Lucien looked at the reference.

"That board was reorganized."

"When?"

"Eleven years ago."

Seraphina closed the register.

"Then we need to find out where its records went."

Theodore, standing near the window, turned around.

"How much longer do you expect the review to take?"

Seraphina considered the question.

She could have said a month.

Or two.

But the truth was less convenient.

"We cannot estimate until we know whether the records survived the reorganization."

Theodore nodded.

"Your appointment ends soon."

"I am aware."

"Would you consider an extension?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"Not before the committee has established what work remains and who will be responsible for it."

He accepted the answer.

"Then we shall discuss it after the next review."

Seraphina gathered the documents.

She had no intention of becoming a permanent member of a committee whose responsibilities seemed to multiply every time someone found an old filing cabinet.

That evening, she sat with her mother in the garden.

Evelyne was reading a letter from a northern merchant association.

Seraphina had brought no documents.

This was deliberate.

"You look tired," Evelyne said.

"I am."

"Is the committee still investigating the old stations?"

"Yes."

"Have you found anything?"

"Several buildings, several pieces of equipment, and a considerable number of people who believed another office was responsible."

Evelyne lowered her letter.

"That sounds exhausting."

"It is."

"Then why continue?"

Seraphina considered the question.

Because the records were incomplete.

Because equipment might still be sitting in storage.

Because people had spent money on projects whose actual status nobody could explain.

Because the kingdom's infrastructure was not a harmless collection of old documents.

But none of those answers explained why she had agreed to help in the first place.

"I thought it would take three months," she said.

Evelyne smiled.

"An optimistic estimate."

"An extremely optimistic estimate."

"Your father will be pleased to hear that you have discovered the limitations of royal administration."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Did he already know?"

"He has governed a duchy for more than twenty years."

"That explains his expression whenever I mention a committee."

Evelyne returned to her letter.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Seraphina noticed something.

Her mother had placed the merchant association's letter beside another document.

It concerned a proposed road-maintenance contribution from several northern estates.

The same kind of contribution that had appeared in the old infrastructure records.

Seraphina glanced at it.

"Is that a new arrangement?"

"Yes. The merchants want the estates to contribute toward road repairs."

"Have they provided a maintenance schedule?"

Evelyne looked at her.

"Not yet."

Seraphina leaned back.

"Then don't sign anything."

Evelyne laughed softly.

"I was not planning to."

Seraphina returned her attention to the garden.

For once, she had no intention of examining another document.

The next morning, the Central Technical Evaluation Board's successor office sent its response.

The board's records had been divided during reorganization.

Calibration approvals had gone to the Royal Academy.

Equipment inspection reports had gone to the Technical Standards Directorate.

Regional installation authorizations had been transferred to the Infrastructure Department.

The original register was not among them.

However, the response identified a surviving reference:

**CTEB/EAST/44 — Calibration Review Pending.**

Seraphina read the reference.

It was the first document to suggest that the equipment at E-1 had reached the calibration stage.

Not installation.

Not operation.

Only review.

She placed the letter in the committee folder.

Then she wrote the next request.

The old stations were no longer merely missing from maps.

They were missing from the administrative memory of the kingdom.

And somewhere between the offices that had once been responsible for them, a decision had been made—or had never been made at all.

Seraphina wanted to know which.

She also wanted the committee to stop calling every incomplete building a facility.

That seemed a reasonable request.

Unfortunately, she had begun to suspect that reasonableness was not a recognized category in royal administration.
