# The Villainess Is Too Lazy to Destroy the World

## Chapter 1 — An Extremely Inconvenient Awakening

The first thing Seraphina noticed was that her bed was excessively comfortable.

The second thing she noticed was that her ceiling was unfamiliar.

The third thing was that there was a chandelier hanging directly above her.

A very expensive chandelier.

Seraphina stared at it.

For several seconds, she didn't move.

Then she slowly pulled the blanket over her head.

"...No."

The blanket was soft.

Unreasonably soft.

It smelled faintly of lavender and something expensive.

She pulled it down again.

The ceiling remained.

The chandelier remained.

The unfamiliar room remained.

Seraphina closed her eyes.

Perhaps she was dreaming.

That was the most reasonable explanation.

She had gone to sleep in her small apartment, after spending an exhausting evening finishing a report that her supervisor had insisted was urgently required.

She remembered sitting at her desk.

She remembered drinking an excessively sweet cup of instant coffee.

She remembered complaining about the office printer.

She even remembered setting an alarm for the following morning.

And now...

This.

She opened her eyes again.

The chandelier was still there.

"...What happened to my apartment?"

Her voice sounded different.

Younger.

Softer.

She raised one hand from beneath the blanket.

The fingers were slender, pale and unfamiliar. There were no small scars from years of ordinary accidents. No faint calluses from typing on a keyboard every day.

She turned her hand over.

Then she looked at her other hand.

Both belonged to someone else.

Seraphina sat upright.

The blanket slid down to her waist.

Long, dark hair spilled over her shoulders.

She stared at it.

Then she touched her face.

Her cheeks.

Her nose.

Her chin.

"...Excuse me?"

The bedroom door opened.

A young woman entered carrying a silver tray.

She wore a black-and-white uniform, with her brown hair neatly tied behind her head.

"My lady?"

Seraphina froze.

The maid stopped walking.

For a moment, the two stared at each other.

Then the maid's expression changed.

"My lady!"

She hurried toward the bed.

"You're awake!"

Seraphina looked at her.

The maid placed the tray on a nearby table and immediately approached.

"How are you feeling? Should I call the physician? Your fever was quite high last night."

Seraphina opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

The maid became increasingly worried.

"My lady?"

"...Where am I?"

The maid blinked.

"In your bedroom, my lady."

Seraphina looked around.

The large canopy bed.

The enormous windows.

The embroidered curtains.

The polished furniture.

The fireplace.

The chandelier.

Yes.

That was fairly obvious.

She took a breath.

"I mean... what is this place?"

The maid's expression became concerned.

"Lady Seraphina, this is your residence. You are in the Veyr estate."

Seraphina stopped breathing for a moment.

Veyr.

That name.

She knew that name.

No.

That couldn't be right.

She slowly turned toward the maid.

"What did you call me?"

"My lady?"

"My name."

The maid looked confused.

"Lady Seraphina Veyr."

Silence.

A very long silence.

Seraphina stared at her.

The maid stared back.

Then Seraphina whispered:

"...I'm going to need a moment."

### 1. The Most Unfortunate Name in the Kingdom

The maid introduced herself as Mira Bell.

She was twenty years old, had worked in the Veyr household for several years and appeared genuinely worried about her employer's condition.

She also seemed completely unaware that the young woman sitting in front of her was experiencing a rather significant existential crisis.

Mira poured a glass of water.

"Please drink this, my lady."

Seraphina accepted it.

She took a small sip.

Then another.

The water tasted normal.

That was reassuring.

Unfortunately, nothing else was.

She glanced toward the mirror across the room.

"Could you..."

She hesitated.

"Bring me a mirror?"

Mira looked surprised.

"Of course."

She retrieved a smaller hand mirror from the dressing table and handed it over.

Seraphina looked into it.

A beautiful girl stared back.

Long black hair.

Pale skin.

Fine features.

Deep crimson eyes.

She looked seventeen.

Perhaps eighteen.

Definitely not the face she remembered having.

The girl in the mirror tilted her head.

Seraphina tilted hers.

The reflection did the same.

She closed her eyes.

Then opened them.

Still there.

She placed the mirror down carefully.

"...Right."

Mira folded her hands.

"Would you like me to call the physician?"

"No."

"Are you certain?"

"Yes."

"Perhaps some breakfast would help?"

Seraphina considered that.

Breakfast.

Food.

An ordinary activity.

Something that existed in every possible world.

"That sounds reasonable."

Mira smiled with relief.

"I'll have the kitchen prepare your usual breakfast."

She turned toward the door.

"Wait."

Mira stopped.

"Yes, my lady?"

"What is my usual breakfast?"

The maid looked confused.

"Your usual breakfast?"

"Yes."

"Eggs, toasted bread, fruit, cream pastries and tea."

Seraphina stared at her.

"...That's a lot."

Mira hesitated.

"My lady?"

"Nothing. Please continue."

Mira left.

Seraphina sat quietly.

Then she looked toward the window.

Beyond the glass was an enormous garden.

There were neatly trimmed hedges, fountains, stone pathways and several gardeners working near a row of flowering trees.

Beyond the garden stood the outer buildings of an enormous estate.

It was not a house.

It was practically a small village.

She pressed her fingers against her forehead.

Veyr.

Seraphina Veyr.

There was only one reason that name felt so familiar.

She had read about it.

Not in a history book.

Not in a newspaper.

In a fantasy novel.

A particularly dramatic fantasy novel, actually.

She remembered reading it several months ago.

It had been a popular story about a kingdom, a saintess, a crown prince, a magical catastrophe and a noblewoman who eventually became the main antagonist.

That noblewoman had been named Seraphina Veyr.

The villainess.

The girl who was eventually executed after attempting to manipulate the royal family and destroy the kingdom's magical foundation.

Seraphina's expression became blank.

She looked at the mirror again.

"...No."

She picked it up.

Looked at herself.

Put it down.

Picked it up again.

"No, no, no."

She remembered the character description.

Black hair.

Crimson eyes.

Daughter of the Duke of Veyr.

Seventeen years old at the beginning of the original storyline.

She remembered the family.

The kingdom.

The saintess.

The crown prince.

The academy.

Everything was beginning to fit.

Seraphina slowly lowered the mirror.

"...I'm the villainess."

She stared at the ceiling.

Then she lay down again.

There was only one appropriate response.

She pulled the blanket over her head.

"I don't want this job."

### 2. A Future Best Avoided

An hour later, Seraphina had reached a preliminary conclusion.

She was almost certainly living inside the world of the novel.

The circumstances surrounding her awakening remained unclear.

She had no explanation for how she had arrived here.

She had no explanation for what had happened to the original Seraphina.

And she had absolutely no intention of wasting the entire morning investigating questions she couldn't answer.

There were more immediate concerns.

For example, her future execution.

She sat at the dressing table while Mira carefully brushed her hair.

"Is something troubling you, my lady?"

"Several things."

Mira paused.

"Would you like to speak about them?"

"No."

"Very well."

Seraphina looked at herself in the mirror.

The original Seraphina Veyr had been a difficult person.

That was the polite description.

In the novel, she was proud, temperamental and deeply concerned with maintaining her social position.

She had a reputation for humiliating servants, insulting other nobles and treating anyone below her station as an inconvenience.

Her relationship with her parents had also been complicated.

Her father, Duke Alaric Veyr, was strict and politically influential.

Her mother, Duchess Evelyne Veyr, was elegant, intelligent and deeply involved in aristocratic society.

Neither parent had been particularly successful at correcting their daughter's behavior.

The original Seraphina had grown up surrounded by wealth, privilege and people who rarely challenged her.

Then came the saintess.

Liora Everwyn.

The beloved young woman whose magical abilities and charitable work earned her admiration throughout Elarion.

In the original story, Seraphina became increasingly jealous of Liora.

The crown prince, Theodore Aurelian, also played an important role in the original conflict.

Seraphina had wanted political influence and recognition.

She had made increasingly reckless decisions.

Her actions eventually led to a series of events involving the royal court, the academy and a dangerous magical project.

The kingdom had suffered.

Seraphina had been blamed.

And the villainess had died.

Seraphina remembered the broad outline.

Unfortunately, she did not remember every detail.

She had not read the novel carefully enough to memorize the exact sequence of events.

She remembered major characters and dramatic developments.

Some locations.

Some political conflicts.

A few particularly memorable scenes.

But not everything.

She couldn't simply walk into the royal palace and announce that she knew the future.

Especially when she didn't.

She considered the situation.

Then she spoke.

"Mira."

"Yes, my lady?"

"How old am I?"

Mira stopped brushing.

"Seventeen, my lady."

Seraphina closed her eyes.

Seventeen.

The same age as the character at the beginning of the novel.

"When is my birthday?"

"Your birthday is in the spring, my lady. It is still several months away."

"Understood."

Mira resumed brushing.

Seraphina stared at her reflection.

There was another question.

"What day is it?"

"Today is the twenty-third day of the Frostfall month."

"Year?"

"Year 684 of the Royal Calendar."

Seraphina nodded slowly.

She tried to recall the timeline.

The original novel began shortly before the Royal Academy's new academic year.

The saintess had not yet entered the academy.

The crown prince had not yet begun his investigation into the magical infrastructure.

And the major political conflicts were still several years away.

That was good.

Probably.

She had time.

A considerable amount of time, actually.

She could avoid the major events.

She could stay away from the academy's most troublesome social circles.

She could avoid the saintess.

She could avoid the crown prince.

She could refuse invitations to political gatherings.

She could refrain from funding questionable magical experiments.

Most importantly, she could stop behaving like the original villainess.

Simple.

Very simple.

Seraphina leaned back slightly.

"From today onward, I shall live quietly."

Mira nodded.

"Of course, my lady."

"I will avoid unnecessary attention."

"Certainly."

"I will not interfere with royal politics."

"Understood."

"And I will not participate in any elaborate schemes."

Mira smiled politely.

"Naturally, my lady."

Seraphina nodded.

Excellent.

A peaceful life was within reach.

Then Mira added:

"Would you like me to inform the duke that you have recovered sufficiently to attend breakfast?"

Seraphina's shoulders stiffened.

"...Breakfast?"

"Yes, my lady. His Grace requested that you join the family this morning."

Seraphina stared at her.

"Why?"

Mira looked surprised.

"Because His Grace and Her Grace are concerned about your health."

That was reasonable.

Annoyingly reasonable.

Seraphina sighed.

"Fine."

She stood.

Mira immediately helped adjust her dress.

It was a dark burgundy gown with delicate black embroidery.

The original Seraphina's wardrobe was apparently larger than Seraphina's previous apartment.

She looked at the dress.

Then at the mirror.

Then at the maid.

"Do I really need to wear this?"

Mira blinked.

"My lady?"

"It seems rather elaborate."

"It is one of your simpler morning dresses."

Seraphina slowly looked down at herself.

"...I see."

Her new life was going to be expensive.

### 3. Breakfast With the Duke

The Veyr estate was even more impressive outside her bedroom.

The corridor stretched across the upper floor, lined with paintings and decorative furniture.

Servants moved quietly through the residence.

Some carried trays.

Others exchanged instructions.

A pair of attendants discussed the afternoon's household arrangements.

Everything seemed organized.

Almost unnaturally so.

Seraphina followed Mira down a grand staircase.

At the bottom, she found a large entrance hall with polished marble flooring.

There were portraits of previous generations of the Veyr family.

She recognized the family crest.

A silver hawk against a dark crimson shield.

House Veyr.

One of the oldest ducal houses in Elarion.

Seraphina had remembered that much from the novel.

The family controlled extensive northern territories, including agricultural estates, mining operations and several strategically important transportation routes.

Their influence was considerable.

Their wealth was even greater.

She stopped in front of a portrait.

A stern-looking man with silver-black hair stared down from the frame.

Mira noticed.

"That is the first Duke of Veyr, my lady."

Seraphina looked at the portrait.

"He looks unhappy."

Mira hesitated.

"He was apparently a very serious man."

"I can see that."

"His Grace often says that the first duke built the foundation of the family's prosperity through discipline."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then I hope he also built a comfortable chair."

Mira looked at her.

Seraphina continued walking.

They reached the dining room.

The doors opened.

Inside, a long table was arranged with polished silverware, fine porcelain and an assortment of breakfast dishes.

Two people were already seated.

A middle-aged man with dark silver hair and sharp eyes.

And a beautiful woman with auburn hair.

Seraphina recognized them immediately.

Duke Alaric Veyr.

Duchess Evelyne Veyr.

Her parents.

Alaric looked up first.

"Seraphina."

His voice was deep and measured.

"You are awake."

Seraphina stopped near the entrance.

"Good morning, Father."

The words came naturally.

That surprised her.

She had not consciously decided to use them.

Perhaps the body's memories were helping.

Evelyne smiled.

"Come, darling. Sit down."

Seraphina approached the table.

She took the seat indicated by her mother.

A servant pulled out the chair.

"Thank you."

The servant looked up briefly.

Then lowered his head.

"Of course, my lady."

Seraphina noticed the slight hesitation.

Interesting.

Had the original Seraphina been particularly unpleasant toward the servants?

Probably.

She made a mental note.

The less she resembled the original villainess, the better.

A servant placed a plate in front of her.

Eggs.

Bread.

Fruit.

Cream pastries.

Tea.

Exactly as Mira had promised.

Seraphina looked at the food.

Then at the two people across from her.

Alaric was watching her.

Evelyne was watching her.

Neither had begun eating.

"...Is something wrong?"

Alaric placed his fork down.

"How are you feeling?"

"Better."

"Your physician said the fever was severe."

"I don't remember much of last night."

"That is understandable."

Evelyne reached for her teacup.

"You frightened us."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

There was genuine concern in her expression.

The original novel had not spent much time discussing Seraphina's family life.

It had focused primarily on her political mistakes and eventual downfall.

Her parents had been described as influential figures who were devastated by their daughter's actions.

But the story had never explored their ordinary family interactions in great detail.

Now Seraphina was sitting across from them.

They were real people.

Not names in a fictional description.

She lowered her eyes.

"I'm sorry."

Alaric paused.

Evelyne's hand stopped near her teacup.

A brief silence followed.

Then Alaric spoke.

"You need not apologize for becoming ill."

Seraphina nodded.

"Still."

Her father studied her.

"You are behaving strangely."

There it was.

Seraphina almost sighed.

"Am I?"

"Yes."

Evelyne gave her husband a mildly reproachful look.

"Alaric."

"What?"

"She has only just recovered."

"I am asking a simple question."

Seraphina picked up her fork.

Perhaps eating would make this conversation easier.

She cut a small piece of egg.

Chewed.

Swallowed.

Then answered.

"I was thinking."

"About what?"

"How much I have been sleeping."

Alaric stared at her.

Evelyne blinked.

Seraphina continued eating.

The silence became increasingly uncomfortable.

Finally, Evelyne laughed softly.

"Well, rest is important."

"Exactly."

Alaric looked unconvinced.

He resumed eating.

For the next several minutes, breakfast proceeded peacefully.

Seraphina began to relax.

The food was excellent.

The bread was fresh.

The tea was fragrant.

The fruit had been carefully prepared.

She was beginning to appreciate her circumstances.

Perhaps being reincarnated into a wealthy noble family wasn't entirely unfortunate.

Then her father spoke.

"Your mother tells me you have been asking unusual questions."

Seraphina stopped chewing.

She swallowed.

"Such as?"

"Questions about your household expenses."

She blinked.

"...I have?"

"Apparently."

Evelyne smiled.

"You asked Mira about the cost of maintaining the gardens yesterday."

Seraphina stared at her mother.

That was not something she remembered doing.

Perhaps the original Seraphina had asked before her fever.

Or perhaps the servants were simply discussing her recent behavior.

Alaric folded his hands.

"Why are you suddenly interested in household expenditure?"

Seraphina considered her answer.

Because I don't want to die.

That was not appropriate.

Because I want to avoid becoming a villainess.

Even less appropriate.

Because I suspect the entire kingdom is eventually going to experience a magical catastrophe.

Definitely not.

She chose the simplest explanation.

"I was curious."

Alaric looked at her.

"Curious?"

"Yes."

"About the household accounts?"

"Yes."

Her father remained silent.

Evelyne placed her teacup down.

"Perhaps she would like to learn more about managing the estate."

Seraphina looked at her mother.

Then at her father.

Then back at her mother.

"...Perhaps."

Alaric nodded slowly.

"If that is what you want, I can arrange for Roland to explain the basic accounts to you."

Seraphina nearly dropped her fork.

Roland.

The accountant.

No.

Absolutely not.

She had not asked for a new occupation.

"I don't think that will be necessary."

Alaric raised an eyebrow.

"You were curious about the accounts."

"I was."

"Then?"

Seraphina paused.

"...I have reconsidered."

Evelyne laughed.

Alaric shook his head.

For the first time that morning, his expression softened.

"Very well. We shall discuss it another time."

Seraphina quietly returned to her breakfast.

She had survived the first family conversation.

That was enough.

### 4. The Problem With Being Rich

After breakfast, Seraphina returned to her bedroom.

She immediately dismissed the idea of exploring the estate.

There would be time for that later.

She wanted to understand her circumstances first.

Mira brought a collection of clothing and placed it inside the wardrobe.

Seraphina watched her work.

"Mira."

"Yes, my lady?"

"Could I see the household schedule?"

Mira turned.

"The household schedule?"

"Yes. The daily arrangements."

"Of course. Would you like the general schedule or the private schedule for your residence?"

"Both, if possible."

Mira nodded.

"I shall bring them."

She left.

Seraphina sat beside the window.

She began organizing what she knew.

First, she was Seraphina Veyr.

Second, she was seventeen.

Third, her father was the Duke of Veyr.

Fourth, her family was extremely wealthy.

Fifth, the original Seraphina was destined to become a villainess.

Sixth, she had no intention of following that storyline.

She counted the points on her fingers.

Then she stopped.

There was another important matter.

Her family's finances.

The original novel had mentioned that House Veyr possessed enormous wealth.

However, Seraphina vaguely remembered something about the family's financial resources becoming strained during the later political conflicts.

She couldn't remember exactly why.

It might have been related to military expenditure.

Or transportation projects.

Or a failed magical investment.

The details were unclear.

She frowned.

This was precisely why relying on memories of a novel was dangerous.

A fictional narrative often omitted the boring details.

Real life did not.

Real life contained contracts, taxes, property rights, inheritance rules and the occasional person who forgot to submit an invoice.

She knew that much from her previous life.

She rubbed her forehead.

"I should avoid making decisions based on incomplete information."

That sounded sensible.

She would observe first.

Understand the household.

Learn the local customs.

Then quietly arrange her life so she would not become involved in the events that had destroyed the original Seraphina.

A peaceful residence.

A comfortable allowance.

Perhaps a small library.

A garden.

No royal court.

No political games.

No dramatic confrontations.

She could spend her days reading, eating and occasionally taking walks.

It was a perfectly respectable life.

Mira returned with several documents.

"My lady, I brought the schedules."

Seraphina accepted them.

"Thank you."

The first document contained the household's general daily timetable.

The second listed the arrangements for the ducal residence.

The third detailed the responsibilities of the household staff.

Seraphina began reading.

Her eyes moved down the page.

Then stopped.

She read the line again.

And again.

"...Mira?"

"Yes, my lady?"

"How many people work in this estate?"

Mira considered the question.

"Approximately two hundred and forty, including the groundskeepers and kitchen staff."

Seraphina slowly lowered the document.

Two hundred and forty.

For one family.

She looked at the schedule again.

The gardeners had assigned shifts.

The kitchen staff had separate responsibilities for breakfast, lunch and dinner.

The stable staff managed the horses.

The laundry staff operated from a separate building.

There were also maintenance workers, security personnel, administrative assistants, attendants and several other categories she hadn't yet understood.

She stared at the paper.

"...That's a lot of people."

Mira nodded.

"Yes, my lady."

"Do they all work here?"

"Some are stationed at the estate permanently. Others work in the surrounding properties."

Seraphina nodded slowly.

She turned another page.

Then another.

A list of household expenses followed.

Food supplies.

Clothing.

Maintenance.

Heating.

Transportation.

Gardening.

Security.

Staff salaries.

Various household purchases.

Seraphina's eyes narrowed.

She wasn't an accountant.

But she had spent enough time working with financial reports in her previous life to recognize something strange.

There were several overlapping entries.

A maintenance payment appeared twice.

One gardening contract listed a different amount from the supporting invoice.

A furniture purchase was recorded under two separate departments.

She flipped back.

Then forward.

Then back again.

Her expression became increasingly serious.

"...This is odd."

Mira leaned slightly forward.

"What is, my lady?"

Seraphina pointed at a line.

"Why is the same maintenance service listed twice?"

Mira examined the document.

"I am not certain."

"And why is this furniture purchase recorded under both the guest residence and the main household?"

"I couldn't say."

Seraphina looked at the next page.

There was another discrepancy.

Not necessarily fraud.

It could be a recording error.

It could be an old contract.

It could be a difference between payment dates and invoice dates.

She did not know.

But she recognized the pattern.

Someone needed to reconcile these accounts.

She leaned back.

For several seconds, she stared at the ceiling.

Then she spoke.

"Mira."

"Yes, my lady?"

"Do we have a quiet place where I can read?"

Mira blinked.

"Of course. The eastern library is usually quiet in the mornings."

"Good."

Seraphina stood.

"Let's go there."

Mira collected the documents.

"Would you like me to inform the librarian?"

"No need."

Seraphina paused.

"Actually, could you bring these papers as well?"

Mira nodded.

"Certainly."

Seraphina walked toward the door.

Then stopped.

She looked back at the documents.

A terrible realization was beginning to form.

She had intended to spend the morning doing absolutely nothing.

Instead, she was about to investigate household expenditure.

This was how it began.

In the original novel, the villainess had become obsessed with controlling everything around her.

Seraphina looked at the papers.

Then at Mira.

"...If I ask you something, will you answer honestly?"

"Of course, my lady."

"Am I difficult to work for?"

Mira froze.

Her expression changed slightly.

It was subtle.

But Seraphina noticed.

Mira lowered her eyes.

"My lady?"

"Just answer."

The maid hesitated.

Then spoke carefully.

"You can be... rather demanding."

Seraphina nodded.

"Do I treat the staff badly?"

Mira did not answer immediately.

That was answer enough.

Seraphina looked down.

"I see."

Mira quickly added:

"But you have never been unreasonable about everything, my lady. And you have always ensured that the household staff receive their wages."

Seraphina appreciated the attempt to soften the truth.

"Still, I should improve."

Mira looked up.

"My lady?"

Seraphina met her eyes.

"I don't want people to be afraid of speaking to me."

The maid stared.

For a moment, she appeared unsure how to respond.

Then she bowed.

"Understood, my lady."

Seraphina nodded.

"Let's go to the library."

### 5. A Quiet Afternoon That Never Happened

The eastern library was enormous.

Tall bookshelves lined the walls, reaching nearly to the ceiling.

Large windows allowed sunlight to enter the room.

There were several reading tables, comfortable chairs and a fireplace.

The air smelled of paper, leather and polished wood.

Seraphina stopped just inside the entrance.

"...This is nice."

Mira smiled.

"This is one of your favorite rooms, my lady."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Really?"

"Yes. You used to spend quite a lot of time here."

That was useful information.

Perhaps the original Seraphina had possessed at least one peaceful hobby.

Seraphina walked toward a large reading table.

She placed the documents down.

Then looked at the shelves.

"Do we have books about the kingdom's history?"

"Certainly."

"Economics?"

Mira blinked.

"Economics?"

"Trade, taxation, agriculture, estate management. Anything related to administration."

"I believe those books are on the western shelves."

"Good."

Mira looked at the documents.

"Would you like me to ask someone to bring them?"

"No. I'll look myself."

Seraphina approached the shelves.

She found several books.

Some were old.

Some had elaborate covers.

One contained a detailed account of the kingdom's tax system.

Another discussed agricultural production in the northern territories.

A third described the responsibilities of noble estate administrators.

She carried them to the table.

Then sat down.

She opened the first book.

The writing was difficult, but understandable.

The kingdom's economic system was not particularly different from the systems she knew.

There were taxes.

There were tariffs.

There were public expenditures.

There were local administrative budgets.

There were also several outdated laws.

She turned a page.

Then another.

She was beginning to enjoy herself.

Not because the subject was particularly exciting.

But because understanding how things worked was satisfying.

An hour passed.

Then another.

Mira occasionally brought tea.

Seraphina read.

She made notes.

She compared the household schedules with the financial records.

And gradually, the initial confusion gave way to something more concerning.

The household's accounting structure was unnecessarily complicated.

Several departments appeared to use separate reporting methods.

Some expenses were recorded according to the date of purchase.

Others were recorded according to the date of payment.

There was no single summary document that made the overall expenditure easy to understand.

Again, this did not prove wrongdoing.

But it made mistakes difficult to identify.

Seraphina rubbed her eyes.

She looked at the clock.

It was already afternoon.

"...I was supposed to be resting."

Mira placed a fresh cup of tea beside her.

"You have been reading for quite some time, my lady."

"I noticed."

"Would you like lunch?"

Seraphina looked at the clock again.

Then at the documents.

Then at the books.

"...Yes."

Mira smiled.

"I'll inform the kitchen."

Seraphina closed the book.

She leaned back in her chair.

Perhaps she should stop.

She had already done more than enough for one day.

There was no reason to investigate every minor discrepancy.

She was not responsible for the household accounts.

She was not the duchess.

She was not the duke.

She was simply a seventeen-year-old girl who had woken up in an unfamiliar world.

She should behave accordingly.

She closed the documents.

Then she looked at the final page.

One entry caught her attention.

A payment to a regional maintenance contractor.

The amount was unusually large.

The description was vague.

And the supporting document was missing.

Seraphina frowned.

She turned the page over.

Nothing.

She checked the attached list.

Nothing.

She examined the date.

It was several months old.

She placed the document down.

"...That's strange."

Mira returned.

"Is something wrong, my lady?"

Seraphina looked at the paper.

Then at the maid.

"Do you know who handles the household accounts?"

"The estate accountant is Mr. Roland Ashford."

"Can I speak with him?"

Mira nodded.

"Certainly. Would you like me to arrange an appointment?"

Seraphina considered it.

Then sighed.

"Tomorrow."

Mira nodded.

"Very well."

Seraphina stood.

She gathered the books and documents.

"Today, I want to do something else."

"Of course, my lady."

"Something that doesn't involve paperwork."

Mira smiled.

"Would you like to take a walk in the garden?"

Seraphina considered the suggestion.

A garden.

Fresh air.

Flowers.

No financial documents.

No political problems.

No complicated conversations.

"...Yes."

For the first time that day, she felt genuinely pleased.

### 6. The Villainess Makes Her First Decision

The western garden was beautiful.

The pathways were lined with flowering shrubs.

A small fountain stood near the center.

Beyond it was a greenhouse surrounded by carefully maintained flowerbeds.

Seraphina walked slowly.

Mira followed a few steps behind.

Several gardeners bowed as they passed.

Seraphina returned their greetings.

Some looked surprised.

She pretended not to notice.

They reached a bench beneath a large tree.

Seraphina sat down.

The afternoon sunlight was warm.

A light breeze moved through the branches.

She closed her eyes.

This was better.

Much better.

She could become accustomed to this life.

There would be difficulties, certainly.

She still needed to understand the circumstances of her arrival.

She needed to learn the kingdom's laws.

She needed to understand the original Seraphina's relationships and responsibilities.

But none of that had to happen immediately.

She had time.

The original novel's major events were still years away.

If she avoided the people and situations that had led to Seraphina's downfall, perhaps she could live peacefully.

She didn't need to become a saint.

She didn't need to become a political genius.

She didn't need to change the kingdom.

She just needed to avoid becoming the villainess.

Seraphina opened her eyes.

"Mira."

"Yes, my lady?"

"I have decided something."

Mira stood beside the bench.

"What is it?"

"From now on, I intend to live quietly."

Mira nodded.

"That sounds lovely, my lady."

"I will avoid unnecessary social gatherings."

"Of course."

"I will not involve myself in royal politics."

"Certainly."

"I will not compete with the saintess."

Mira looked slightly confused.

But she nodded.

"And I will not become involved in any dangerous magical projects."

Mira smiled.

"I understand."

Seraphina leaned back.

Excellent.

Everything was settled.

She would live quietly.

She would maintain good relationships with her family and household staff.

She would enjoy her wealth responsibly.

She would avoid the events that had destroyed the original Seraphina.

And, if possible, she would never again look at an accounting document.

A peaceful future.

A comfortable future.

A future without unnecessary complications.

She closed her eyes.

For a brief moment, she imagined it.

Then Mira spoke.

"My lady?"

"Hm?"

"His Grace has requested that you attend a meeting tomorrow morning."

Seraphina opened one eye.

"...What meeting?"

"An estate administration meeting."

She stared at Mira.

"Why?"

"His Grace wishes to discuss the northern agricultural reports."

Seraphina slowly sat upright.

The northern agricultural reports.

Of course.

Why wouldn't there be agricultural reports?

She had barely been in this world for one day.

She had already discovered accounting discrepancies.

Now there were agricultural reports.

She looked at the sky.

Then at the garden.

Then at Mira.

"...Can I refuse?"

Mira hesitated.

"I believe His Grace would prefer that you attend."

Seraphina stared into the distance.

The peaceful life she had imagined was already beginning to look rather difficult.

She sighed.

"Fine."

Mira smiled.

"I shall prepare your schedule."

Seraphina immediately regretted agreeing.

That evening, Seraphina returned to her bedroom.

She dismissed the attendants and sat beside the window.

The estate was quiet.

Lights illuminated the pathways below.

Somewhere in the distance, she could hear the sound of servants closing the garden buildings for the night.

She opened a small notebook.

It had been placed on her desk earlier that afternoon.

The pages were blank.

Seraphina picked up a pen.

She considered what she needed to do.

Then began writing.

**Personal Rules for a Peaceful Life**

1. Do not interfere with royal politics.
2. Do not antagonize the saintess.
3. Do not become involved with the crown prince.
4. Do not participate in questionable magical experiments.
5. Avoid unnecessary social gatherings.
6. Do not make enemies among powerful nobles.
7. Maintain a good relationship with the family.
8. Treat household staff properly.
9. Do not become obsessed with controlling everything.
10. Avoid unnecessary paperwork.

She looked at the list.

Then added another sentence.

**Most importantly, do not become the villainess.**

She put the pen down.

There.

A reasonable plan.

She closed the notebook.

Outside, the wind moved gently through the trees.

Seraphina looked toward the darkening sky.

She did not know why she had been given another life.

She did not know whether the original Seraphina's memories remained somewhere inside her.

She did not know how much of the original story would still happen.

But she knew what she wanted.

A quiet life.

Nothing more.

She turned off the bedside lamp.

Then climbed into bed.

For the first time since waking up in this unfamiliar world, she felt somewhat at ease.

Unfortunately, she had forgotten one small detail.

The original Seraphina Veyr was not merely the daughter of a wealthy duke.

She was also the legal heir to one of the largest and most complicated estates in the northern kingdom.

And tomorrow morning, someone would be bringing her the agricultural reports.

**End of Chapter 1**
