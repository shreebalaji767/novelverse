# **Chapter 97 — The Report That Declared the Emergency Over**

The capital's protective network was restored on a Thursday.

By Friday morning, the Royal Infrastructure Department had issued a notice declaring that the emergency interruption had been resolved.

By Friday afternoon, the Academy had requested that the notice be withdrawn.

The disagreement concerned one word.

*Restored.*

The Department believed the word referred to the return of the two isolated outer sections to service.

The Academy believed it could be understood as confirmation that the entire network had returned to its original operating condition.

It had not.

The central assembly was functioning again, but its response time remained slower than the original specification. The western and southern replacement components still required detailed inspection. The reserve was active, but its operating margin had not yet been fully reassessed.

The notice was technically defensible.

It was also misleading.

Seraphina discovered this when the amended version arrived on her desk.

She read the first paragraph.

Then the second.

Then the heading.

"Who wrote this?"

Cedric, who had travelled to the capital to deliver House Veyr's signed loan documents, looked up from his own papers.

"The Infrastructure Department, I believe."

"Did they ask the Academy to review it?"

"I don't know."

Seraphina pushed the notice toward him.

"Read the heading."

Cedric did.

**CAPITAL PROTECTIVE NETWORK — FULL RESTORATION CONFIRMED**

He looked at the final paragraph.

"That seems premature."

"It is."

"Perhaps they mean the interrupted sections have been restored."

"Then they should say that."

Cedric placed the notice down.

"Will you tell them?"

"I will tell the prince."

"Why not the Department?"

"Because the Department issued it, and the prince is coordinating the response. I would prefer not to create two competing corrections."

Cedric nodded.

"Reasonable."

Seraphina looked at him.

"You sound surprised."

"I have learned not to underestimate the number of people who can become offended by a correction."

"That is unfortunate."

"It is also true."

Theodore received the notice at ten.

He read it, then summoned the Department's coordinator.

The coordinator arrived with a second document.

"The Department believed the Academy had approved the wording," he explained.

Theodore looked toward Lucien.

Lucien had not approved it.

Fenwick had not approved it.

Neither had the Academy's technical review office.

"Who authorized the statement?" Theodore asked.

The coordinator checked his records.

"The communications office prepared the notice using the Department's restoration summary."

"Who provided the summary?"

"The emergency coordination office."

"Who approved the summary?"

The coordinator hesitated.

"The summary was circulated for comment."

"Was it approved?"

"No, Your Highness."

Theodore placed the paper on the table.

"Then why was it issued as a confirmed technical statement?"

The coordinator had no satisfactory answer.

Seraphina sat quietly beside the window.

She was not particularly interested in assigning blame for an inaccurate heading.

The more important question was whether the people who relied on the notice would understand that the network still required monitoring.

Theodore turned to her.

"What wording would you use?"

She considered the document.

"Say that the two interrupted sections have returned to service. State that the central assembly has passed its initial post-repair tests. Then say that the wider technical review remains ongoing."

"Would you mention the reserve?"

"Yes. It is still an active part of the system."

Lucien nodded.

"That is accurate."

Theodore looked at the coordinator.

"Prepare the correction. Have the Academy confirm the technical wording before publication."

The coordinator bowed.

"And the original notice?"

"Withdraw it."

The man left.

Seraphina returned to her papers.

"That was relatively painless."

Theodore looked at her.

"Give it an hour."

The correction was issued shortly after noon.

It was shorter than the original notice.

**CAPITAL PROTECTIVE NETWORK — OPERATIONAL UPDATE**

*The two outer sections isolated during the recent interruption have returned to service following approved maintenance procedures.*

*The central assembly has passed its initial post-repair tests. All seven zones are currently operating under the approved configuration, with the reserve system active.*

*The Academy's technical assessment and the Royal Infrastructure Department's maintenance review remain ongoing. Additional inspections will be conducted before the network is declared fully cleared.*

Seraphina read it once.

"Better."

Lucien, standing beside her, looked relieved.

"At least nobody has called it a complete restoration."

"That is progress."

He glanced toward the observation board.

"Not all the readings are normal."

"How far outside the original range?"

"None. But the central response remains slower than the original specification."

"Then the report is correct."

"Yes."

Seraphina folded the notice.

"That is all anyone can reasonably ask of a report."

Lucien looked at her.

"Would you believe that if the readings were worse?"

"No."

He nodded.

"Fair enough."

The first detailed post-repair inspection began that afternoon.

The central assembly had passed its initial tests, but the Academy wanted to observe it under a full evening demand cycle.

The engineers also began examining the western and southern components.

The western assembly had a replacement guide installed during the second modernization.

Its supplier's maintenance note recommended inspection at shorter intervals in areas with elevated mineral content.

The note had been included in the original equipment package.

It had not been carried into the central maintenance schedule.

The southern assembly had a different replacement lining.

Its supplier had recommended a periodic examination of the contact surface after prolonged operation.

That recommendation had also been omitted.

Neither component had failed.

Neither had been found damaged.

But both had been operating under maintenance schedules that did not fully reflect the requirements of their installed materials.

Fenwick reviewed the findings.

"We need a complete component register."

Lucien nodded.

"Every replacement part, its original specification, supplier requirements, installation date and maintenance history."

"And the current condition."

"Yes."

Theodore looked at the Department's representative.

"Can the Infrastructure Department provide the installation records?"

"We can provide the records held by our office."

"Can the Academy provide the technical specifications?"

"Yes."

"Then combine them."

The representative nodded.

Seraphina glanced at the draft register.

"Who will maintain it after the review?"

Theodore looked at the coordinator.

The coordinator consulted his notes.

"That has not yet been assigned."

Seraphina put down her pen.

"Then you are preparing another document that will become outdated as soon as everyone leaves the room."

Theodore exhaled.

"Who should maintain it?"

"The Department should maintain the administrative record. The Academy should confirm technical changes. Each regional maintenance authority should submit inspection results."

Lucien nodded.

"That is workable."

"Provided the Department does not alter technical specifications without Academy confirmation."

Fenwick added, "And the Academy must not assume that an installation has been completed merely because the Department records a completed expenditure."

Theodore looked between them.

"Then establish those responsibilities in writing."

Seraphina returned to the register.

She had not intended to spend another day explaining the difference between a record and the thing it described.

But apparently the kingdom required repeated instruction.

At three in the afternoon, the Treasury received the first formal request for emergency expenditure.

The request covered replacement materials, transport, technical labour and the inspection of all seven zones.

Minister Garrick examined the figures.

The total was larger than the preliminary estimate.

It was also considerably smaller than the amount that would have been required if the entire central assembly had needed replacement.

He approved the first stage.

The second stage remained conditional on the Academy's inspection results.

The Treasury also accepted House Veyr's emergency loan offer.

The funds would be transferred through the established government account.

The repayment schedule would be reviewed after the technical work was complete.

Evelyne's letter included one additional condition.

The Treasury was to provide a quarterly expenditure statement showing how the loan had been used.

Garrick read the condition.

Then he looked at Theodore.

"Your Duchess of Veyr is thorough."

"She is."

"She also appears to distrust the Treasury."

Theodore looked at the signed agreement.

"She has requested a statement, Minister. I would not call that distrust."

Garrick gave him a dry look.

"Of course not."

The first payment was transferred the following morning.

House Veyr's reserve was not exhausted.

The northern winter programme continued.

Two nonessential estate projects were postponed, and Cedric had already sent the village council an explanation of the delayed bridge approach work.

The council had responded with a short letter.

It expressed disappointment, acknowledged the reason and requested a revised schedule.

Cedric placed the letter before Seraphina.

"They were more understanding than I expected."

"Why?"

"Because they wanted the work completed before winter."

"Then they should have said so."

"They did."

Seraphina looked at the letter.

The council had also offered to contribute local labour if the estate could provide materials later.

She read the proposal.

"That is useful."

"Yes."

"Can we accept?"

"Not without reviewing the costs and the workers' arrangements."

"Then review them."

Cedric smiled faintly.

"You could have simply told me that."

"I just did."

He gathered the papers.

For a moment, he remained beside the desk.

"Your mother asked whether you were eating properly."

Seraphina looked at the untouched plate beside her.

"Yes."

Cedric followed her gaze.

"That plate is empty."

"It was empty when I received it."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the answer available to you."

Cedric sighed.

He had become considerably more difficult to dismiss since taking over the estate's administrative work.

Seraphina considered this one of the less convenient consequences of his growing confidence.

The Academy's evening assessment produced encouraging results.

The central assembly responded more quickly than it had before the repair.

The reserve contribution remained within the approved range.

The western and southern zones showed no further interruptions.

The northern zone remained stable.

However, the central assembly's response still differed from the original design specification.

The difference was small.

It was consistent.

Fenwick did not approve the system for unrestricted operation beyond the existing configuration.

Lucien explained the results to Theodore.

"The repair has improved the regulator's response. We have not restored the original performance margin."

"Can it be restored?"

"Possibly. We need to inspect the remaining contact surfaces and confirm the replacement lining's condition."

"How long?"

"Several days."

Theodore nodded.

"Continue."

Seraphina was reviewing the component register nearby.

"Is the reserve still necessary?"

Lucien looked at her.

"Yes."

"Even after the repair?"

"Especially after the repair. The network's current configuration relies on the reserve during demand fluctuations. We cannot remove it simply because the central assembly is responding better."

"Good."

He looked mildly surprised.

"You agree?"

"I have no desire to watch the capital lose its lights again."

The following day, the first district reports arrived.

The western district requested that the temporary street barriers be removed.

The city watch confirmed that the affected streets were safe for ordinary passage.

The southern quarter requested an additional inspection of two public buildings whose outer lamps had flickered during the interruption.

The Academy approved the request.

The infirmaries reported that their ordinary lighting reserves had been replenished.

The Church submitted a list of emergency supplies distributed during the incident.

Liora had organized the list into three categories:

- supplies used;
- supplies returned;
- supplies still held by the receiving institutions.

Seraphina examined it.

"That is clear."

Liora, who had come to deliver the report personally, looked pleased.

"I thought you might approve."

"I do."

"Then I will tell Nessa."

"Please don't. She may become unbearable."

Liora laughed.

"She already thinks she is indispensable."

"She probably is."

Liora sat down.

"The southern infirmary has asked whether the Church should maintain a larger emergency lighting reserve."

"Can it afford to?"

"Not without reducing some other supplies."

"Then don't decide based only on this incident. Review the actual usage."

"I thought you would say that."

Seraphina looked at her.

"Have I become predictable?"

"Only about certain things."

Liora placed another paper on the desk.

"This is the Church's proposed arrangement with the city shelters. They want to keep two rooms available during winter, with a small supply of ordinary lamps and blankets."

Seraphina read it.

The arrangement was modest.

It did not depend on the magical network.

It did not require the Church to take responsibility for city infrastructure.

It simply provided a place for people who might need shelter during an interruption.

"That seems sensible."

"Then I'll recommend it."

Liora gathered her papers.

At the door, she paused.

"Seraphina?"

"Yes?"

"You have been working since the emergency began."

"That is an accusation."

"It is an observation."

"An unnecessary one."

Liora smiled.

"Go home tonight."

Seraphina considered the request.

"Are you going home?"

"After I finish the infirmary reports."

"Then I will consider it after I finish mine."

"That is not what I asked."

"It is the answer I have."

Liora shook her head and left.

On the fourth day after the interruption, the Academy completed its comparison of the seven zones.

The findings were not uniform.

The central assembly had suffered from residue accumulation and uneven catalyst distribution.

The western component had maintenance requirements that had not been fully incorporated into the operating schedule.

The southern component had a different replacement lining with a separate inspection recommendation.

The northern component had a complete maintenance history and had remained stable.

The eastern circuit had already received corrective work during the earlier assessment.

The two remaining zones had no significant findings.

The reserve system had operated within its permitted limits, although it had been required to compensate more frequently than intended.

The report did not identify one single cause.

It identified several conditions that had accumulated over time.

The central regulator's delayed response had reduced the system's ability to stabilize demand.

The western and southern components had maintenance gaps.

The reserve had been carrying an increasingly heavy load.

The network's records had not provided a sufficiently clear picture of the combined condition.

Fenwick read the report aloud at the technical review meeting.

When he finished, the room remained quiet.

Theodore looked at the final page.

"Is the system safe?"

Fenwick answered.

"Under the current configuration and monitoring schedule, yes. I recommend continued operation with the reserve active, reduced demand restrictions lifted gradually, and the remaining inspections completed before the network is declared fully cleared."

"Can we restore ordinary demand?"

"Gradually."

Theodore nodded.

"Then proceed."

The technical report was followed by an administrative inquiry.

It was not a criminal investigation.

It was an examination of how maintenance requirements, technical recommendations and material records had become disconnected.

The inquiry would examine:

- the second modernization's final technical acceptance;
- the transfer of supplier maintenance instructions;
- the central assembly's replacement lining;
- the reserve material inventory;
- the incomplete receipt records;
- the authority responsible for maintaining the current component register.

No official was accused of deliberately causing the interruption.

No evidence of sabotage had been found.

The inquiry's purpose was to establish how the system had been allowed to operate with incomplete maintenance information.

The King appointed a review panel consisting of representatives from the Academy, the Royal Infrastructure Department, the Treasury and the regional maintenance authorities.

Theodore was appointed to coordinate the government's response.

Seraphina received another letter.

It requested her participation in the administrative review for thirty days.

This time, the scope was clear.

She would examine records, compare transfers and help establish responsibility for maintaining the administrative register.

She would not direct technical work.

She would not authorize expenditure.

She would not supervise the regional authorities.

She would not be responsible for the network's operation.

Seraphina read the letter.

Then she placed it beside the previous request.

The two documents were nearly identical.

Except the new one contained a sentence that she particularly appreciated.

**The appointment shall expire automatically upon completion of the specified review period unless renewed by mutual written agreement.**

She looked at Theodore.

"Who wrote this?"

"I did."

"Good."

"Will you accept?"

She considered the request.

Thirty days.

A defined scope.

A clear end date.

A named coordinator.

No permanent office.

No technical authority.

It was not unreasonable.

"I will accept the thirty days."

Theodore nodded.

"Thank you."

"Do not interpret that as an invitation to extend it."

"I won't."

"Good."

He handed her the final copy.

She signed.

That evening, the royal family held a small meeting in the palace.

There were no celebrations.

The capital's protective network was functioning again, but the review was incomplete.

The King thanked the Academy's technicians, the city watch, the Church, the Department and the regional authorities.

He also acknowledged House Veyr's emergency loan.

Alaric and Evelyne attended.

Evelyne listened as the Treasury minister described the expenditure arrangement.

She asked two questions about repayment and one about the final accounting statement.

Garrick answered all three.

The King looked at her.

"Your conditions are reasonable, Duchess."

"I am pleased to hear it, Your Majesty."

Alaric sat beside her, silent.

He had not expected his wife to become involved in a royal infrastructure emergency.

He had also not expected her to negotiate the loan terms more efficiently than several Treasury officials.

Evelyne noticed his expression.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"Then stop looking at me."

Alaric returned his attention to the King.

Seraphina watched the exchange.

It was one of the few things about the evening that seemed entirely normal.

When the meeting ended, the King asked Seraphina to remain.

Theodore stood nearby.

"I understand you have accepted the thirty-day review appointment," the King said.

"Yes, Your Majesty."

"Will you consider remaining afterward?"

Seraphina looked at him.

"In what capacity?"

"The Crown may require an administrative adviser on infrastructure matters. Your work on the records has been useful."

She waited.

The King continued.

"Your experience in the previous review and the present emergency suggests that you understand the importance of distinguishing technical decisions from administrative responsibility."

Seraphina considered her answer.

She could have refused immediately.

She could have explained that she had never wanted the position.

Instead, she asked a question.

"Would the adviser have authority to direct the departments?"

"Not necessarily."

"Would the adviser be expected to attend every emergency meeting?"

"Only when required."

"Would the adviser be responsible if a department failed to maintain its records?"

The King paused.

"No."

"Would the adviser be expected to resolve disputes between the Academy, Treasury and Infrastructure Department?"

"To assist with coordination."

Seraphina looked at Theodore.

He did not intervene.

She turned back to the King.

"Then I believe the kingdom needs clearer departmental responsibilities more than it needs another adviser."

The King studied her.

"Explain."

"The recent interruption was not caused by the absence of one person willing to take responsibility. It developed because several institutions held different parts of the information and did not maintain a reliable process for combining them."

She spoke carefully.

"The Academy knew the technical requirements. The Department held installation records. The Treasury held expenditure records. The stores offices held the materials. The maintenance teams knew what they were observing locally."

She paused.

"None of those responsibilities required a permanent adviser to replace them."

The King leaned back.

"And what would you recommend?"

"That the departments maintain their own records, confirm transfers and assign responsibility for reviewing changes. If they need assistance coordinating, they should use a defined committee with a limited purpose and a named coordinator."

Theodore looked at the King.

"That is already being prepared."

"Then it should be sufficient."

The King was silent for a moment.

"You do not wish to remain in royal service."

"No, Your Majesty."

"Even in a limited capacity?"

"I would prefer to return to my estate."

The King studied her expression.

Then he nodded.

"Very well. Complete the thirty-day review. After that, your appointment will end as agreed."

Seraphina bowed.

"Thank you, Your Majesty."

The next morning, the capital's protective lamps remained lit.

The city had begun removing the temporary barriers.

The hospitals had returned to their ordinary schedules.

The Church's emergency shelters remained available for the winter.

The Academy continued its monitoring.

The western and southern components were scheduled for their final inspections.

The central assembly's response had improved, though it had not yet returned to its original specification.

The reserve remained active.

And the administrative inquiry had begun.

At the Veyr residence, Seraphina received a letter from Cedric.

It contained the revised northern maintenance schedule, the village council's response concerning the delayed bridge approach and an update on the household accounts.

She read the first page.

Then the second.

Then she reached the final paragraph.

*The eastern garden wall repairs have been postponed. The household has accepted the decision. Margaret has requested that you stop sending reports after midnight because she considers it an unreasonable use of correspondence.*

Seraphina stared at the sentence.

Mira, standing beside her, looked curious.

"What is it, my lady?"

"Margaret is becoming involved in matters beyond her authority."

"Which matters?"

"My correspondence."

Mira considered this.

"Perhaps she is correct."

Seraphina lowered the letter.

"You are becoming dangerously confident."

"I have had a good teacher."

"That is unfortunate."

Mira smiled.

Outside, the household was preparing for an ordinary day.

There were deliveries to inspect, accounts to review and several estate decisions waiting for Seraphina's attention.

For once, none of them involved the capital's magical infrastructure.

She picked up her tea.

Then another letter arrived.

It bore the seal of the Royal Infrastructure Department.

Seraphina looked at it.

Mira looked at her.

Neither spoke.

At last, Seraphina set down her cup.

"Perhaps I should have stayed in bed."

Mira opened the letter.

It was not an emergency.

It was the official notice confirming the start of the thirty-day review.

Seraphina took it from her.

"At least they have learned to use the correct address."

Mira smiled.

"That is progress, my lady."

Seraphina looked at the document.

Then she looked at the untouched breakfast.

Thirty days.

She could survive thirty days.

Probably.

Provided nobody invented another central storage facility.
