# Chapter 44 — The Landowner Who Had Refused the Surveyors

The southern office's reply arrived with an attachment.

It was a copy of the original land-access refusal.

The document was brief.

The landowner, Lord Benedict Arwell, had refused permission for the proposed S-3 installation team to enter his property.

His stated reason was that the regional authority had not provided a complete description of the intended construction.

The refusal had been submitted eighteen years earlier.

Seraphina read it twice.

Then she placed it beside the equipment transfer instruction.

"Does the land register still identify Lord Arwell as the owner?" she asked Holt.

"Yes. The property remains within the Arwell estate."

"Has the estate changed hands?"

"No. Lord Benedict died nine years ago. His daughter, Lady Marianne Arwell, inherited the property."

"Is she still there?"

"She resides in the southern provinces."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then we should ask whether the family retains any records of the proposed installation."

Holt wrote down the request.

The land-access agreement was more complicated than the refusal.

It had been drafted by the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office and sent to the Arwell estate for approval.

The document described the proposed work as:

*Construction of a regional magical storage and regulation facility, including underground equipment chambers, a maintenance access road and associated protective structures.*

There were no detailed drawings attached.

No construction timetable.

No explanation of the facility's operating purpose.

The landowner had been asked to grant access for an indefinite period.

Seraphina examined the final clause.

The agreement also allowed the regional authority to expand the facility if future technical requirements demanded it.

She looked at Holt.

"I would have refused this too."

Holt examined the document.

"It was a standard agreement."

"That does not make it reasonable."

"The regional authority may have expected the details to be provided later."

"Then the agreement should have said so."

Lucien examined the technical description.

"The proposed structure would have required substantial excavation."

"Would it have affected the surrounding land?"

"Potentially. The underground chambers would have required stable foundations and a protected mana channel."

Seraphina looked at the refusal notice.

"Then Lord Arwell was not necessarily obstructing the project."

"No," Lucien said. "He may simply have wanted to know what was being built on his land."

Lady Marianne Arwell replied within a week.

She had received the committee's letter and was willing to provide access to the family's old estate records.

She also wanted clarification.

*Is the Crown intending to revive the original S-3 proposal?*

Seraphina drafted the answer herself.

*No decision has been made to revive the project. The committee is reviewing historical records only. No construction, excavation or land access is currently proposed.*

She added that the family was under no obligation to grant access to the property.

The letter was sent through Holt.

The committee meeting was held two days later.

Minister Garrick examined the proposed agreement.

"Why did the regional authority draft an agreement without providing technical plans?"

"Perhaps the plans were being prepared separately," Seraphina replied.

"Then why were they not attached?"

"We don't know."

Garrick turned to Lucien.

"Could the proposed facility have been built safely?"

"Not without a site assessment."

"Was one completed?"

"Apparently not."

Theodore examined the refusal notice.

"Then the project may have been suspended before construction began."

"That is the most likely explanation," Holt said.

Seraphina looked at the documents.

"Possibly. But we should distinguish between a refusal to grant access and a formal cancellation."

Theodore nodded.

"Agreed. We need the legal correspondence."

Lady Marianne's records arrived in two boxes.

The first contained estate correspondence.

The second contained maps, survey notes and a copy of the proposed land agreement.

Seraphina examined the correspondence first.

The original refusal had been written by Lord Benedict Arwell.

It was not an angry letter.

It was a formal request for further information.

He had asked the regional authority to provide:

- The exact location of the proposed facility.
- The dimensions of the underground chambers.
- The intended use of the regulating equipment.
- The expected duration of construction.
- The maintenance obligations after completion.
- The authority responsible for damage to surrounding land.

The regional office had replied with a general explanation.

It stated that the facility would support regional infrastructure and improve the reliability of magical distribution.

It did not answer the specific questions.

Lord Arwell had refused access.

A second letter had followed.

This time, the estate had offered to reconsider if the Crown supplied a detailed plan and accepted responsibility for any structural damage.

The regional office had not responded.

Seraphina put the letters down.

"How long did they wait?"

Holt checked the dates.

"Five months."

"And then?"

"The legal office requested an extension."

"Why?"

"The project was under review."

Seraphina looked at the correspondence.

"Was the extension granted?"

"Yes."

"Did the office ever provide the plans?"

"No."

The maps revealed another detail.

The proposed S-3 facility was not intended to stand near the main estate residence.

It was planned for a low-lying section of land beside an old watercourse.

The area had previously been used for agricultural storage.

A road passed nearby, but it was narrow and unsuitable for heavy construction vehicles.

Lucien examined the map.

"The site may have been selected because of its proximity to a natural mana channel."

"Would that have made it suitable?"

"Potentially. But the soil conditions would have needed testing."

"Was there a survey?"

"Northvale Cartographic Services may have conducted one."

Seraphina looked up.

"Northvale?"

"The same firm that surveyed the western corridor for A-2."

Holt checked the payment records.

There was a separate payment to Northvale Cartographic Services for a southern site assessment.

The reference was **S-3/14**.

Seraphina looked at the date.

The survey had been commissioned three months before the land-access agreement was sent to Lord Arwell.

She placed the payment certificate beside the maps.

The survey had been paid for.

The landowner had been asked for permission.

But the survey report was missing.

"Did Northvale have permission to enter the land?" she asked.

Holt checked the correspondence.

"No."

"Then how did they conduct the survey?"

Lucien examined the map.

"They may have used public roads and existing geographical information."

"Could they have assessed the soil?"

"Not properly."

Seraphina looked at the survey certificate.

"Then what exactly did the Crown pay for?"

The committee requested the original survey report.

Northvale Cartographic Services had closed, but Samuel Northvale had already agreed to provide any surviving records.

Holt wrote to him again.

This time, the reply came with an envelope containing several folded sheets.

Northvale had found the commercial record for S-3/14.

It listed the work completed:

- Review of existing regional maps.
- Preliminary route assessment.
- Identification of three possible facility sites.
- Review of publicly available land records.
- Preparation of a preliminary access plan.

The final line was particularly important.

*Physical survey and soil assessment not undertaken due to absence of landowner authorization.*

Seraphina examined the record.

The firm had not entered the property.

It had not examined the soil.

It had not confirmed the site's suitability.

The payment had covered preliminary research, not a completed engineering survey.

That was reasonable.

But the project records had not preserved the distinction.

Lucien examined the proposed location.

"If the soil had been unsuitable, the facility might have required expensive reinforcement."

"Could the natural mana channel have justified that cost?"

"Perhaps. But we cannot determine that from a map."

Seraphina nodded.

"Then the original project was never technically ready for construction."

"Correct."

"Was the equipment transferred before the land issue was resolved?"

Holt checked the dates.

"Yes."

"How long before?"

"Approximately two months."

Seraphina looked at the equipment transfer record.

The casing had been moved from the Technical Evaluation Unit to the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office before the land-access agreement was completed.

It had been placed in storage while the authority waited for permission.

The equipment had arrived before the site was ready.

The project had then stalled.

That explained the transfer.

It did not explain why the original programme had continued to list S-3 as an active facility.

Theodore examined the findings.

"So the landowner refused access because the authority did not provide sufficient information."

"Yes," Seraphina replied.

"The survey had not been completed."

"Correct."

"The equipment was transferred before the site was approved."

"Yes."

"And the project was suspended."

"At least in practical terms."

Theodore looked at Holt.

"Was it formally cancelled?"

"We have not found a cancellation order."

"Then it remained administratively open."

Holt nodded.

Seraphina looked at the old funding summary.

S-3 had continued to appear as a planned facility.

Its equipment had been stored.

Its land access had never been secured.

Its survey had never been completed.

And no formal cancellation had been recorded.

She wrote a recommendation.

*The committee should classify S-3 as an uncompleted proposed facility, with land access unresolved and technical suitability unconfirmed. It should not be listed as an existing or operational installation.*

Theodore read it.

"Agreed."

There was one remaining question.

The equipment.

The casing had been transferred to the Southern Regional Infrastructure Office.

The receiving officer had signed for it.

The installation project had been suspended.

But where was the casing now?

The southern office's stores ledger provided an initial answer.

It had been placed in **Storehouse S-12**.

A later inventory listed it as *held pending project authorization*.

The next inventory did not mention it.

Seraphina looked at the dates.

"Was S-12 closed?"

"Yes," Holt replied.

"When?"

"Sixteen years ago."

"Where did its contents go?"

"The regional office says they were transferred to a central southern depot."

"Which depot?"

"That is not specified."

Seraphina stared at the ledger.

"Of course."

The committee had one more task before it could close the S-3 entry.

It needed to identify the receiving depot.

Holt requested the storehouse closure schedule.

The document arrived the following week.

It listed the transfer of equipment, furniture and administrative records from S-12 to three separate locations.

The regulating casing was included in the equipment category.

Its destination was listed as **Southern Depot C**.

A receiving signature was present.

The clerk was identified as **Thomas Wren**.

Seraphina stared at the name.

Again.

She took the document to her desk and compared the personnel details with the Veyr Estate steward's records.

The southern clerk had been employed by the Regional Stores Authority twenty years earlier.

The Veyr steward had worked in household administration throughout that period.

They were different men.

She wrote a clear note in the committee file.

*The receiving clerk named Thomas Wren is not the Veyr Estate steward. No relationship is established.*

Then she sent the reference to Holt.

The southern depot had not been closed.

It was still operating under a different name.

The committee requested access to its historical inventory.

The response was delayed.

The depot manager explained that the old records had been moved to a separate administrative building and were awaiting classification.

Seraphina read the letter.

"At least they know where the records are."

Mira, who was preparing her afternoon tea, looked at her.

"Is that good?"

"It is an improvement."

"Will you have to visit?"

"Possibly."

Mira placed the cup on the desk.

"Then I shall prepare your travel clothes."

Seraphina looked at her.

"I have not agreed to travel."

"No, my lady."

"Why are you preparing them?"

"Because the records are in the south."

Seraphina stared at the cup.

Mira smiled politely.

The depot manager sent another letter.

The historical inventory had been located.

The casing was still listed.

Its equipment number matched the original allocation.

But the condition column contained a new note.

*Transferred to Regional Technical Storage, pending inspection.*

The entry was dated fifteen years earlier.

Seraphina examined it.

The casing had not been installed at S-3.

It had not been returned to Central Stores.

It had been transferred again.

She placed the document beside the earlier records.

The equipment's path was now clear up to a point:

It had been reserved for A-2.

It had been released for technical evaluation.

It had been repaired and declared suitable for installation.

It had been transferred to the southern region.

It had been stored for S-3.

The project had been suspended because land access and technical surveys were unresolved.

It had then been transferred to Southern Depot C.

And finally, it had been moved to Regional Technical Storage.

The destination was identifiable.

The contents of the storage facility were not yet known.

Seraphina wrote the updated report.

S-3 was not a completed facility.

It was a proposed project that had received equipment and preliminary funding but had never obtained the land access or technical approval necessary for construction.

The equipment reserved for A-2 had been redirected to S-3.

That explained why A-2 had never received its reserved casing.

It did not establish that the transfer was improper.

The equipment had been suitable for A-2, but the project had not been ready for installation.

The Crown had apparently reassigned it to another proposed facility.

That facility had also failed to progress.

Seraphina looked at the report.

The programme had not simply suffered from missing paperwork.

Its projects had become disconnected from one another.

Equipment had been moved to places that were not ready.

Facilities had remained in funding summaries long after construction had stopped.

Different departments had maintained different versions of the same history.

And no single office had been responsible for confirming that the entire programme had reached completion or been formally closed.

She placed the report in the committee folder.

That evening, Theodore sent a brief message.

*The committee will review the updated S-3 classification tomorrow. Please attend.*

Seraphina read it.

Then she looked at the calendar.

Her advisory appointment would end in three weeks.

Three weeks.

She had begun with a simple task: reviewing historical records.

Now she had traced equipment across three regions, confirmed an abandoned facility and discovered that the southern project had never received the land access necessary to begin construction.

She put down the letter.

At least the next meeting was in the capital.

She could sleep in her own bed.

That was something.

The following morning, the committee accepted the revised classification.

S-3 was recorded as an uncompleted proposed facility.

Its physical location remained unconfirmed.

Its land access had never been secured.

Its technical survey was incomplete.

Its equipment had been transferred to Regional Technical Storage.

No construction or commissioning certificate existed.

Theodore signed the revised register.

"That leaves E-1 and the sixth reserve facility."

Seraphina nodded.

"Yes."

"Do we have any new information?"

Holt opened another folder.

"We have a response from the Eastern Regional Infrastructure Office."

Seraphina looked at the heading.

**E-1 — Eastern Relay Station.**

The response contained a map reference and a land record.

The facility might have been constructed near the eastern trade road.

But the property register described the site as a private warehouse.

Seraphina slowly closed her eyes.

"Another building with a different name?"

Holt hesitated.

"Possibly."

Theodore looked at the map.

"Then we should establish whether it is the same property."

Seraphina opened her eyes.

She looked at the location.

The eastern road was far from the western facilities.

The committee had not yet examined the records from that region.

She picked up her pen.

"Please request the property file, construction authorization and original technical schedule."

Holt nodded.

The next investigation had begun.

And Seraphina was beginning to suspect that the kingdom had built several magical facilities, given them ordinary names, transferred their equipment elsewhere and then forgotten to tell the offices responsible for maintaining them.

She hoped the eastern warehouse was actually a warehouse.

It would be a refreshing change.
