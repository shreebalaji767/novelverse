# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 52 — The Man Who Approved It Without Reading**

**Thursday — 6:12 p.m.**

The first person to leave Greyhaven House was Aria.

The second was Victoria.

The third was Felix, who had been ordered not to follow them with a folder containing seventeen newly discovered documents.

He obeyed.

He carried only sixteen.

Maximilian noticed.

“Felix.”

“What?”

“Put it back.”

Felix looked down at the folder.

“How did you know?”

“Because you are holding it like a man concealing stolen property.”

“I am holding it like a man protecting historical evidence.”

“Put it back.”

Felix returned the folder to the table.

Across the room, Octavian had not moved.

He was staring at the protective authority document.

“Someone approved the arrangement without understanding its purpose,” he repeated.

Blackwood stood beside him.

“That's what Thomas says Elias believed.”

“Elias would not have made that distinction without a reason.”

“No. But we need the register before we decide what it means.”

Lucian turned to Catherine.

“Who had access to the administrative council's approval system?”

“Four people at the time.”

“Names.”

“Elias Vesper. Margaret Ellison. The council secretary. And the executive custodian.”

Octavian looked up.

“Who was the executive custodian?”

Catherine hesitated.

“Your brother.”

The room fell silent.

“Elias?” Lucian asked.

“No. The executive custodian was appointed by the council. Elias was the trust's legal custodian.”

Octavian's expression darkened.

“Then who?”

Catherine looked towards the shelves.

“I don't remember the name.”

Felix immediately turned around.

“You don't remember?”

“I remember the position. I don't remember who held it during that period.”

“Convenient.”

“Felix,” Helena warned.

Catherine didn't react.

“He may have been replaced shortly before the reversal. The appointment records were part of the register Elias removed.”

Nathaniel folded his arms.

“Then we need the original council minutes, the appointment record, and the register.”

Blackwood nodded.

“And we need to establish whether the approval was genuine.”

Octavian finally stepped away from the table.

“Find them.”

**6:38 p.m.**

At Vesper Estate, the family gathered in the smaller conference room.

Aria had declined to attend.

Victoria had declined more emphatically.

Neither refusal had been challenged.

Marcus Vale placed two glasses of water on the table.

“Miss Aria requested that no one contact her about the trust tonight.”

“Reasonable,” Helena said.

“Miss Victoria requested that no one contact her at all.”

Cassian raised an eyebrow.

“An efficient distinction.”

Marcus continued.

“She also requested that any further documents concerning her identity be sent through her solicitor rather than delivered personally.”

“Good,” Maximilian said.

“Miss Aria made the same request.”

Lucian nodded.

“Arrange it.”

Marcus made a note.

Then he looked at Octavian.

“There is another matter. The Sterling family has requested a copy of the protective allocation record.”

Richard Sterling had made the request through the legal representatives.

Octavian's expression remained cold.

“Why?”

“They claim they need to understand the legal basis on which their estate was used.”

“They have a right to understand that.”

Lucian looked at his father.

“Richard authorised the estate's temporary use. He should see the complete record.”

“Not the entire trust ledger,” Blackwood said. “Only the documents relating to his authorisation and the infant's temporary custody.”

Marcus wrote it down.

“Understood.”

Cassian leaned back.

“So we are now sending historical documents to the family that expelled Aria.”

“We are sending documents to the person whose property was used,” Maximilian replied.

“I know. I was merely observing the irony.”

“Observe quietly.”

Cassian smiled.

“Your leadership style remains inspiring.”

**7:03 p.m.**

Felix had taken over one end of the conference table.

Three screens displayed the old Vesper administrative structure.

The fourth displayed a list of former council members.

He enlarged the appointment records.

“Here.”

Nathaniel approached.

“What have you found?”

“The council secretary changed twice during the relevant period. The first appointment ended eleven months before the allocation reversal. The second secretary served for only seven months.”

“Name?”

“Dorian Hale.”

Lucian frowned.

“I remember him.”

“Who was he?” Maximilian asked.

“Administrative secretary. He handled council correspondence and maintained approval records. He wasn't a voting member.”

Felix searched the archived personnel index.

“Dorian Hale resigned twenty-two years ago.”

“Why?” Octavian asked.

“Officially, retirement.”

“Age?”

“Forty-one.”

Cassian looked at the screen.

“An ambitious retirement.”

Felix opened the attached resignation note.

It contained three sentences.

The final one read:

*I request that all outstanding authority records be transferred to the incoming secretary.*

Felix stared at it.

“That's interesting.”

Nathaniel leaned closer.

“Why?”

“Because the incoming secretary was never appointed.”

He opened the appointment history.

The position had remained vacant for five months.

Yet approval records continued to appear during that period.

Lucian's expression hardened.

“Who processed them?”

“That's what I'm checking.”

Felix opened the approval log.

There were eleven entries.

Ten had complete authorisation records.

The eleventh had a reference number but no recorded approving officer.

**PA-03-771.**

The protective allocation approval.

**7:16 p.m.**

Octavian read the number.

“Can you identify the terminal?”

Felix checked.

“Old administrative terminal at Ellison House.”

Margaret Ellison, who had joined the meeting by secure call, immediately spoke.

“That building had been closed by then.”

“Not entirely,” Felix replied. “The records room remained operational.”

“Who had access?”

“According to the official list, only authorised archive personnel.”

Margaret's expression tightened.

“Which included me.”

“And Thomas Reed,” Felix added.

Thomas, seated beside her, shook his head.

“I was a courier. I had access to the dispatch area, not the approval terminal.”

Felix enlarged the access log.

“Someone used the terminal at 11:42 p.m. The following morning, the approval reference was entered into the allocation record.”

“Whose credentials?” Nathaniel asked.

Felix hesitated.

“Dorian Hale's.”

Lucian looked at Octavian.

“His credentials were still active?”

“Apparently.”

“Twenty-two years after his resignation?”

“Yes.”

Blackwood studied the record.

“Was the approval entered by Hale himself?”

Felix shook his head.

“The system only identifies the credentials. It doesn't identify the person physically operating the terminal.”

Margaret spoke again.

“I remember that evening.”

Everyone turned towards the call.

“You remember it?” Catherine asked.

“I remember being told that the council approval had arrived. I was instructed to proceed with the allocation amendment.”

“By whom?” Octavian demanded.

Margaret looked down.

“I received a telephone instruction.”

“From whom?”

“I don't know.”

“You don't know?”

“The caller used the council's internal verification phrase. I assumed the instruction was legitimate.”

Catherine's expression sharpened.

“What phrase?”

Margaret gave it to them.

Felix typed it into the archive search.

A result appeared.

Then another.

Then a third.

All three documents carried the same verification phrase.

But the phrase had been retired six months before the reversal.

Nathaniel's voice became quiet.

“Someone used an obsolete verification procedure.”

Margaret nodded slowly.

“I didn't know it had been retired.”

Catherine looked at the screen.

“Neither did I.”

**7:29 p.m.**

Aria was sitting in her apartment when her phone rang.

She glanced at the screen.

Unknown number.

She answered.

“Hello?”

“Miss Aria Vesper?”

“Depends who's asking.”

“This is Dorian Hale.”

Aria sat upright.

“Who?”

“The former Vesper administrative secretary.”

She was silent for a moment.

Then she looked towards the untouched dinner on her table.

“I'm guessing this isn't about a delayed appointment.”

“No.”

“Then speak.”

“I understand that you have discovered the original protective allocation.”

Aria's expression changed.

“Who told you?”

“I have been following the recent inquiries into the trust.”

“That's not an answer.”

“No. It isn't.”

She stood and walked towards the window.

“Why are you calling me?”

“Because the approval recorded under my credentials was not mine.”

Aria said nothing.

Hale continued.

“I did not approve the allocation reversal. I did not authorise the use of my credentials. And I did not submit any instruction concerning either child.”

“Then why did your name appear?”

“I don't know.”

“You were the council secretary.”

“I was the former secretary. I had resigned before the approval was entered.”

“Your credentials were still active.”

“Yes. That was an administrative failure.”

Aria gave a short laugh.

“An administrative failure separated two babies.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

There was a pause.

“I have spent twenty-two years knowing that something happened under my name.”

Aria's voice cooled.

“Then why didn't you come forward?”

“Because the first time I questioned the record, I was warned that the inquiry would expose the children.”

“Who warned you?”

“I don't know.”

“You seem to know very little.”

“I know one thing that may help you.”

Aria waited.

“The approval was not entered by someone using my old credentials remotely.”

“What does that mean?”

“The original terminal required a physical security key. My key was returned when I resigned.”

“Who received it?”

“The executive custodian.”

Aria's grip tightened around the phone.

“What's the name?”

“I don't remember the name.”

She closed her eyes.

“Another person who remembers everything except the one thing that matters.”

“I remember the office. The appointment register will identify the holder.”

“Where is it?”

“Ellison House.”

Aria looked towards the darkening window.

“That's where the original records were kept.”

“Yes.”

“Why are you telling me this now?”

“Because someone contacted me yesterday.”

“Who?”

“A woman.”

“What did she want?”

“She asked whether I still had the original key receipt.”

Aria went still.

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

“Who was the woman?”

“I couldn't identify her.”

“Did she leave a name?”

“No.”

“Then why did you call me instead of the Vespers?”

Hale's answer came after a pause.

“Because the Vespers have spent twenty-two years searching for the person who approved the reversal. I believe the person who physically carried out the approval is still alive.”

Aria looked at the clock.

“Where are you?”

“I would rather not say over the telephone.”

“Then don't call me again until you're ready to give me a place and a time.”

She ended the call.

**7:43 p.m.**

Aria immediately called Marcus.

He answered on the first ring.

“Miss Aria?”

“I've just spoken to Dorian Hale.”

There was a brief silence.

“Are you certain?”

“He identified himself as the former administrative secretary.”

“What did he say?”

“He claims someone used his credentials to approve the protective allocation. He says the original security key was handed to the executive custodian when he resigned.”

Marcus's voice became more alert.

“Did he identify the custodian?”

“No. He says the appointment register at Ellison House contains the name.”

“I'll inform Mr. Vesper.”

“Don't.”

Marcus stopped.

“Miss Aria?”

“I said don't. Not yet.”

“May I ask why?”

“Because he contacted me, not the family. I want to know whether he has something real before twelve people descend on him.”

Marcus considered that.

“Understood.”

“And Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“Don't send a security team to my apartment.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

“Good.”

“I was planning to send one to Hale.”

Aria sighed.

“Of course you were.”

**8:02 p.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Marcus entered the conference room.

“Mr. Hale contacted Miss Aria.”

Octavian looked up.

“What did he say?”

Marcus gave the account.

Felix immediately opened the appointment records.

“The executive custodian's appointment register is at Ellison House.”

Catherine stood.

“I'll go.”

“No,” Octavian said.

She turned.

“I know the building.”

“And you have been missing for nineteen years. You are not walking into an unsecured archive.”

“I can still read a security plan.”

“Then read one from here.”

Catherine looked ready to argue.

Blackwood intervened.

“Octavian is right. If someone has been monitoring the records, Ellison House is the obvious place to intercept us.”

Nathaniel nodded.

“We should establish whether the appointment register still exists before anyone enters.”

Felix brought up the archived inventory.

“There are two copies.”

“Where?” Maximilian asked.

“One at Ellison House. The second was transferred to the old council records vault.”

Lucian frowned.

“That vault was closed.”

“Not destroyed,” Felix replied.

“Who has access?”

Felix opened the access register.

His expression changed.

“Someone entered it three weeks ago.”

“Name?” Nathaniel asked.

Felix enlarged the entry.

The access was recorded under an old internal authority number.

**EXECUTIVE CUSTODIAN — EC-01.**

No name.

No signature.

No physical identification.

Only a verification mark.

Catherine stared at the screen.

“That authority should have expired when the council was dissolved.”

Blackwood leaned forward.

“Then someone has been using it.”

Octavian looked at the screen for a long moment.

“Find out who.”

**8:17 p.m.**

In a small office far from the Vesper estate, Dorian Hale opened a locked drawer.

Inside was a yellowed receipt.

He unfolded it carefully.

The paper recorded the return of his security key twenty-two years earlier.

At the bottom was the receiving officer's signature.

Hale stared at it.

He had spent years trying to remember the name.

Now he had it.

He picked up his phone and dialled a number.

The call connected.

“It's Hale,” he said.

A man's voice answered.

“You shouldn't be calling.”

“I know who received the key.”

Silence.

Hale looked at the signature again.

“It's someone I never expected to see in that office.”

“Destroy the receipt.”

“No.”

“You don't understand what you're doing.”

“I understand perfectly.”

Hale lowered his voice.

“The person who approved the reversal wasn't the person who signed the allocation instruction.”

Another silence.

Then the man spoke.

“Who did you tell?”

Hale glanced at the phone.

“Aria Vesper.”

The line went dead.

Hale remained still.

Then someone knocked on his office door.

Three times.

He looked towards it.

“Who is it?”

A woman's voice answered.

“I'm here for the receipt.”

Hale slowly folded the paper.

He had not told her where he was.

And he had not told her he still possessed it.

**The door handle began to turn.**
