# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 45 — The Woman Who Was Supposed to Be Dead**

**Thursday — 8:20 a.m.**

The first person to object to Catherine Vesper's request was Maximilian.

“She cannot simply demand that we bring Aria and Victoria to her.”

Edmund Voss sat opposite him in the Vesper Estate's private conference room.

“She didn't ask you.”

“She sent the instruction through you.”

“She sent it through someone else. I was merely informed.”

Cassian leaned back in his chair.

“That distinction is doing an extraordinary amount of work.”

Voss ignored him.

Octavian stood at the window, studying the gardens.

“My sister has been dead for nineteen years.”

“Officially,” Voss replied.

“And you allowed the family to believe it.”

“I was told she was dead before I discovered otherwise.”

“By whom?”

“Elias.”

The room went quiet.

Lucian folded his arms.

“My uncle knew Catherine was alive?”

“He suspected it. He had evidence she survived the incident, but he couldn't establish where she was or who was protecting her.”

Octavian turned.

“What incident?”

Voss placed a thin folder on the table.

“An attempted removal of the legacy trust's controlling records. Nineteen years ago. The same year Catherine disappeared.”

Felix immediately opened his laptop.

“Do you have the incident number?”

“No.”

“Date?”

“Approximately three weeks before the official death report.”

“Location?”

“An administrative residence belonging to the Vesper family.”

Felix stopped typing.

“That's not an answer.”

“It's the only one I can give you.”

Damian watched Voss carefully.

“You were her protective adviser.”

“Yes.”

“Then why didn't you protect her?”

Voss looked at him.

“Because I was protecting the children.”

That answer did not satisfy anyone.

Octavian returned to his chair.

“Explain.”

Voss opened the folder.

Inside were three documents.

The first was a security assignment dated twenty-four years earlier.

The second was a personnel transfer.

The third was a photograph of two women standing beside a black vehicle.

One was Catherine.

The other was Margaret Ellison.

**8:47 a.m.**

“Catherine appointed you,” Lucian said.

“Yes.”

“Before the children were born?”

“Before the allocation was reversed. The assignment was originally intended to protect two expected descendants from interference.”

Maximilian examined the document.

“Why would Catherine arrange protection for children who had not yet been born?”

“Because she believed someone intended to use the legacy trust to control the next generation.”

“Who?”

“She never named the person.”

Cassian studied the photograph.

“Catherine and Margaret were working together?”

“Initially.”

“Initially?”

“Margaret was the administrative custodian. Catherine was the person who gave me the assignment. Elias was the legal custodian of the trust.”

Felix looked up.

“Three separate authorities.”

“Three separate responsibilities,” Voss corrected. “That was deliberate.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Then why did my sister disappear?”

“Because the three responsibilities stopped being separate.”

Voss pointed to the second document.

“Catherine requested that the trust's original allocation schedule be sealed. Elias refused. Margaret then accessed the legacy authority system and submitted the reversal.”

“On whose instructions?” Lucian asked.

“I don't know.”

“Did Catherine instruct her?”

“No.”

“You expect us to accept that?”

“No. I expect you to investigate it.”

Octavian stared at him.

“You were her adviser. You were present when these events unfolded. You vanished for more than twenty years. Now you return with documents and instructions from a supposedly dead woman.”

Voss did not look away.

“I understand why you don't trust me.”

“Good.”

“I wouldn't trust me either.”

Cassian sighed.

“Finally, a meeting where everyone is being reasonable.”

Nobody acknowledged him.

Felix turned the photograph over.

There was handwriting on the back.

**Catherine — Margaret — E. Voss.**

Below it was a date.

He compared it with the date on the assignment.

“Wait.”

Everyone looked at him.

“This photograph was taken eleven days after the protective assignment was issued.”

Voss nodded.

“And?”

Felix enlarged the image.

“Margaret was still working with Catherine after you received your instructions.”

“Yes.”

“Then why does the personnel record say Margaret was dismissed two days later?”

Voss leaned forward.

“Show me.”

Felix rotated the laptop.

The dismissal record was signed by the family administrative office.

Voss studied it for several seconds.

“That document is false.”

“How can you tell?” Maximilian asked.

“Margaret was transferred, not dismissed.”

“Where?”

“Ellison House.”

Felix searched the archived records.

His expression changed.

“That's where the legacy authority was accessed.”

Octavian slowly sat back.

The room had acquired another problem.

**9:16 a.m.**

Aria was informed about the discovery at breakfast.

She listened while Helena explained the situation.

Then she looked at the photograph.

“That's Catherine?”

“Yes,” Helena replied.

“And Margaret Ellison?”

“Yes.”

Aria examined the dates.

“So Catherine knew about the children before we were born.”

“She appears to have,” Lucian said.

“Did she know I would end up with the Sterlings?”

“We don't know.”

“Did she know Victoria would be there?”

“We don't know.”

“Did she order the switch?”

“We don't know.”

Aria lowered the photograph.

“That's a lot of not knowing for one breakfast.”

Victoria, seated opposite her, picked up the second document.

“Catherine could have arranged everything.”

“Possibly,” Maximilian said.

“Or she could have been trying to stop someone else.”

“Also possible.”

Victoria looked irritated.

“I dislike investigations where every answer creates three additional questions.”

Felix raised one finger.

“Technically, this one has created seven.”

“Felix.”

“I'm only counting.”

Aria looked at Lucian.

“Why does she want us?”

“We don't know.”

“Does she want to meet us?”

“Apparently.”

“Does she expect us to go?”

“No.”

Aria nodded.

“Good. Because I haven't agreed to be collected by anyone.”

Victoria placed the document down.

“Neither have I.”

Helena looked between them.

“Whatever happens, neither of you will be forced into a meeting.”

Octavian entered the room.

He had heard the last sentence.

“My sister will not be allowed to dictate terms to this family.”

Aria glanced at him.

“Then don't let her.”

Octavian paused.

It was a remarkably simple answer.

Unfortunately, he had spent most of his life dealing with people who made simple answers complicated.

**10:02 a.m.**

The first attempt to contact Catherine came through a secure channel.

Marcus received the message.

He read it twice.

Then he brought it to Maximilian.

“Her representative has responded.”

“Who?”

“A woman identifying herself as Elena Voss.”

Voss looked up sharply.

“That's impossible.”

Marcus placed the message on the table.

**Catherine Vesper acknowledges receipt of the family's inquiry. She will not attend the estate. She requests that Aria and Victoria meet her at the old observatory on Elysium Island. No family representatives. No security personnel within the building.**

Damian immediately shook his head.

“Absolutely not.”

Victoria read it again.

“No family representatives?”

“Correct,” Marcus said.

“No security?”

“Within the building.”

“That is an absurd condition.”

Cassian tilted his head.

“It's also a rather obvious attempt to isolate them.”

Voss remained silent.

Octavian looked at him.

“Do you know this Elena?”

“Yes.”

“Who is she?”

“Catherine's daughter.”

Silence.

Lucian stared at him.

“Catherine has a daughter?”

Voss nodded.

“Her name is Elena.”

Octavian's face changed.

“My sister had a child?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Twenty-two years ago.”

Felix slowly closed his laptop.

“Catherine has a daughter older than the two of us?”

“By three years,” Cassian said.

“Thank you. I can subtract.”

Maximilian ignored them.

“Why was her existence never recorded?”

“Because Catherine believed the family would use her daughter as leverage.”

Octavian stood.

“Did Elias know?”

“Yes.”

“Did I?”

“No.”

Octavian's expression became unreadable.

For nineteen years, he had believed his sister dead.

For twenty-two years, she had apparently raised a daughter outside the family.

And now that daughter was arranging a meeting with the two young women whose lives had been altered by the legacy trust.

“Where is Elena?” he asked.

Voss hesitated.

“She is the person who delivered the original assignment to me.”

“Meaning?”

“Catherine didn't personally recruit me. Elena did.”

**11:30 a.m.**

Aria and Victoria were alone in the sitting room when the new information arrived.

Victoria read the message.

Then she read it again.

“Catherine has a daughter.”

“Apparently.”

“And she is arranging our meeting.”

“Yes.”

“Through a woman who was supposedly dead.”

“That's a different woman.”

Victoria looked at her.

“I know.”

Aria put down her cup.

“Do you want to meet her?”

Victoria considered the question.

“I want answers.”

“That wasn't what I asked.”

Victoria looked toward the window.

For once, she did not immediately answer.

“I want to know why my life was arranged by people who never bothered to tell me what they were doing.”

Aria said nothing.

Victoria continued.

“And I want to know whether Jonathan really saved me from something, or whether he simply interrupted another arrangement.”

“He might have done both.”

Victoria gave a small, humourless laugh.

“That's irritatingly possible.”

Aria looked at the message again.

“I'll meet her if we can establish some basic conditions.”

Victoria raised an eyebrow.

“Such as?”

“We choose the location. We control who attends. We leave whenever we want. Nobody takes our phones. Nobody separates us.”

“And if she refuses?”

“Then she can keep waiting.”

Victoria stared at her.

Then she nodded.

“Agreed.”

**12:04 p.m.**

At the other end of the estate, Octavian was studying an old family photograph.

It showed him, Catherine and Elias as young adults.

Catherine stood between her brothers, smiling at something outside the frame.

He had not seen that smile in nineteen years.

“Why didn't you tell me?” he murmured.

Voss stood behind him.

“Elias believed telling you would expose her location.”

“To whom?”

Voss did not answer.

Octavian turned.

“Was Catherine protecting the trust, or was she trying to take control of it?”

“I don't know.”

“Was she protecting the children?”

“I believe she was.”

“Then why did she allow the reversal?”

Voss looked at the photograph.

“Perhaps she didn't.”

Octavian's eyes narrowed.

“What does that mean?”

Voss took a folded document from his coat.

“I found this in Elias's private archive after his death. It was never included in the trust records.”

Octavian unfolded it.

The document was a handwritten instruction.

**If the original allocation is reversed, do not attempt to restore it until both descendants are beyond the reach of the person who ordered it.**

There was no signature.

Only a date.

Twenty-three years earlier.

Octavian read it again.

Then he looked at Voss.

“Elias knew the allocation would be reversed.”

“He feared it.”

“And Catherine?”

Voss's answer was quiet.

“She may have known who would do it.”

Octavian looked down at the instruction.

For the first time since Catherine's disappearance, he considered that his sister might not have been the person who abandoned the family.

She might have been the person who had been trying to keep it from destroying itself.

And somewhere, after nineteen years of silence, she had decided that the time for hiding was over.

**12:31 p.m.**

On Elysium Island, Elena Voss entered a private room.

Catherine stood beside the window.

She was older than the photographs, her hair silver at the temples. Her posture remained straight, her expression composed.

A second woman sat at the table.

Margaret Ellison.

The former family custodian secretary.

The woman whose credentials had been used to access the legacy authority.

The woman the Vespers believed had died.

Elena placed a message on the table.

“They've agreed to consider a meeting.”

Catherine read it.

“Both?”

“Yes.”

“Together?”

“Yes.”

Margaret looked uneasy.

“You shouldn't meet them.”

Catherine folded the message.

“I know.”

“Then why invite them?”

“Because they deserve to know what happened.”

Margaret lowered her eyes.

“And if they ask who reversed the allocation?”

Catherine looked at her.

“Then we tell them the truth.”

Margaret's fingers tightened around the edge of the table.

Catherine noticed.

“You still haven't told me what you did with the original instruction.”

“I kept it.”

“Where?”

Margaret did not answer.

Catherine's expression hardened.

“Margaret.”

The older woman finally looked up.

“Where it was supposed to be.”

Catherine went still.

For the first time that morning, her composure cracked.

“Then someone has already found it.”

She turned toward Elena.

“Cancel the observatory.”

Elena frowned.

“Why?”

“Because if the original instruction is no longer where Margaret left it, then the person who ordered the reversal knows we're preparing to speak.”

Margaret stood abruptly.

“No. That's impossible.”

Catherine looked at her.

“Is it?”

A telephone rang.

Elena checked the screen.

Her expression changed.

“It's from the old Vesper administrative office.”

Catherine answered.

A man's voice came through.

“Madam, the archive has been opened.”

“By whom?”

A pause.

Then:

“Your brother.”

Catherine closed her eyes.

Octavian had found the archive.

And the document inside it was not the one she had expected him to discover.

**End of Chapter 45**
