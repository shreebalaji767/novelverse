# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 17 — The Instruction Nobody Was Allowed to Read**

**Tuesday — 8:40 a.m.**

Aria had a new responsibility at work.

Peter Collins had assigned her to help prepare monthly accounts for a small manufacturing client.

It sounded straightforward.

It was not.

The client had three bank accounts, two accounting systems and a filing method that appeared to have been invented by someone who disliked folders.

Aria stared at the screen.

“Peter?”

He appeared at her desk.

“Yes?”

“Did the client explain why they have two separate records for the same transactions?”

Peter looked at the screen.

“No.”

“Did they explain why one system uses invoice dates and the other uses payment dates?”

“No.”

“Did they explain why half the supporting documents are named 'final'?”

Peter sighed.

“Welcome to the client.”

Aria nodded.

“I see.”

“Do you think you can organise it?”

“Yes. But I'll need clarification on which system is authoritative.”

“Good. Ask before changing anything.”

Peter walked away.

Aria opened a fresh spreadsheet.

She was beginning to understand that a good accountant spent half the day asking people what they had actually done.

**9:30 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

The archive company had agreed to release the original instruction sheet under a formal preservation agreement.

Marcus reviewed the paperwork with the legal department.

The document was held in a secure storage facility, separate from the acknowledgement recovered the previous day.

A representative would deliver it to Vesper Tower that afternoon.

“Is the chain of custody documented?” Maximilian asked.

“Yes,” Marcus replied. “Every transfer since the archive was acquired is recorded.”

“And the original company?”

“Northstar Document Custody dissolved years ago. The surviving records were transferred legally to its successor.”

Octavian looked at the report.

“Any reason the instruction sheet was stored separately?”

“The classification was changed after the original collection. We don't know why.”

Damian crossed his arms.

“Convenient.”

“Possibly,” Marcus said. “But we don't yet know whether the separation was deliberate.”

Octavian closed the report.

“Then we wait until we have the document.”

**12:25 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria sat across from Eleanor.

Her mother had been sorting correspondence when Victoria entered.

“Do you remember Crestline Administrative Partners?”

Eleanor looked up.

“Why?”

“Because I found a payment made to them twelve years ago.”

Eleanor placed the letter in her hand down.

“Your father handled the family office.”

“Did you know about the payment?”

“I remember the name.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“What did they do?”

“Administrative work. Records, correspondence, that sort of thing.”

“Were they connected to the hospital?”

Eleanor's expression became guarded.

“I don't know.”

“Did you ever meet anyone from the company?”

“No.”

“Then why do you look worried?”

Eleanor stared at her daughter.

“Because you are asking questions about a period when our family went through something very difficult.”

Victoria frowned.

“What difficult period?”

Eleanor hesitated.

“Your father was under considerable pressure. There were legal matters, business problems and family concerns.”

“That doesn't explain the hospital records.”

“No.”

“Mother, do you know something?”

Eleanor looked away.

“I know that your father did not want those old matters discussed.”

Victoria's frustration rose.

“Everyone keeps telling me what Father wants.”

“Perhaps because he has reasons.”

“Or perhaps because nobody wants to tell me the truth.”

Eleanor said nothing.

Victoria stood.

“I'm going to find out.”

**2:15 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The original instruction sheet arrived.

Marcus received it personally.

The archive representative confirmed the seal was intact and handed over the custody documentation.

Marcus signed for the package.

He then brought it directly to Octavian's office.

Maximilian, Damian, Cassian and Felix were already there.

Octavian examined the sealed envelope.

“Open it.”

Marcus carefully removed the contents.

One sheet of paper.

Two pages of supporting notes.

The first page contained the instruction.

NORTHSTAR DOCUMENT CUSTODY — RESTRICTED CLIENT INSTRUCTION

**Reference:** PRIV-07 / SECOND TRANSFER

**Date:** Twelve years earlier

**Instruction:**

1. The first collection must be completed through the designated courier.
2. The second collection must be handled separately.
3. No family names are to appear on the external delivery documents.
4. Both acknowledgements must be retained independently.
5. No recipient is authorised to disclose the final destination without written approval from the client.

**Authorised by:** Client Representative

**Verification:** Two signatures required.

The room was silent.

Damian spoke first.

“Two collections.”

Marcus nodded.

“The first was the hospital package. The second was collected from Greybridge.”

“Why separate them?” Maximilian asked.

“Possibly to prevent either courier from knowing the complete route,” Cassian replied.

Octavian studied the second page.

It contained a handwritten note.

*The two records must remain separate until the second transfer is confirmed.*

Beneath it was a signature.

**E. Shaw.**

Miriam Shaw.

Marcus placed the earlier acknowledgement beside it.

“The signatures appear similar.”

“Appear?” Damian asked.

“We need authentication.”

Octavian looked at the documents.

“Arrange it.”

**3:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had been examining the instruction's reference numbers.

He found something unusual.

“The document refers to a client approval code that isn't included in the archive.”

“Which code?” Marcus asked.

“CA-17.”

“Can you trace it?”

“Possibly. The reference format resembles the internal codes used by Crestline Administrative Partners.”

Cassian looked at him.

“So the Sterling family's company appears again.”

“It's not proof that the Sterlings authorised this instruction,” Felix replied. “The code may have been reused or copied.”

Octavian nodded.

“Keep the possibilities separate.”

Felix opened another file.

“There is also a note indicating that the second transfer was not considered complete when the document was signed.”

Maximilian frowned.

“What was still pending?”

“The instruction doesn't say.”

Damian looked at the handwritten line.

“Then the second transfer may not have been a delivery to the final destination. It may have been another stage.”

Marcus nodded slowly.

“That would explain why the destination field was withheld.”

Octavian looked at the room.

“Find the final acknowledgement. We need to know whether the second transfer was completed.”

**5:18 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria was reading a message from Nina when Marcus called.

“Good evening, Miss Aria.”

“Hello. Have you found something?”

“Yes. We've recovered an original instruction sheet connected to the two deliveries.”

“What does it say?”

Marcus explained the separate collections, the withheld family names and the two required acknowledgements.

Aria listened without interrupting.

“Does it identify who ordered it?”

“Not directly. It contains an approval reference that may connect to Crestline Administrative Partners.”

“May?”

“Yes. We are still verifying it.”

“And Miriam Shaw's signature?”

“We are arranging authentication.”

Aria was quiet for a moment.

“Did she know what the documents were for?”

“We don't know.”

“Then don't assume she did.”

Marcus made a note.

“Understood.”

“Keep going. And tell me if you find something that changes what we know.”

“I will.”

After the call, Aria returned to Nina's message.

*Peter says the client has finally sent the missing documents.*

Aria replied:

*Miracles happen.*

**7:45 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria found Richard in his study.

This time, she did not bring photocopies.

She brought a question.

“Did you authorise Crestline to handle the hospital records?”

Richard looked up.

His face changed.

“Where did you hear that?”

“So you did.”

“I didn't say that.”

“Then answer me.”

Richard stood.

“Victoria, you are interfering in something you don't understand.”

“Then explain it.”

He looked at her for a long moment.

Finally, he said, “The Sterling family office had an administrative arrangement with Crestline. It was not unusual.”

“Was the hospital part of that arrangement?”

“I don't remember.”

“Did you approve a payment?”

Richard did not answer.

Victoria's voice became quieter.

“Was I part of it?”

He looked away.

She stared at him.

“Was I?”

Richard walked to the window.

“I will not discuss this without reviewing the original records.”

Victoria gave a bitter laugh.

“You have had twelve years.”

“Enough.”

“No. Not enough.”

She left.

Richard remained by the window.

On his desk, beneath a closed folder, lay a letter dated the same week as the births.

He did not open it.

**9:12 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman read a report from her associate.

“The Vespers have recovered the original instruction.”

She lowered the page.

“Did they authenticate Shaw's signature?”

“Not yet.”

“What about the final acknowledgement?”

“They are searching for it.”

The woman stood.

“Then find it before they do.”

“We don't know where it is.”

“Check the old custody records. The second transfer may have generated another archive entry.”

The associate hesitated.

“And Victoria Sterling?”

“She has found the Crestline payment.”

“Should we stop her?”

The woman considered this.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because she is looking for an answer that concerns her. Let her keep looking.”

She opened the locked cabinet.

Two newborn photographs lay inside.

Beside them was a document with a handwritten note.

*The children must remain separate until the second transfer is complete.*

The woman stared at it.

Then she closed the cabinet.

**Wednesday — 8:55 a.m.**

Miriam Shaw arrived at Vesper Tower with her legal representative.

She looked tired.

Marcus greeted her and escorted her into the meeting room.

The original instruction sheet was placed on the table.

Miriam examined the signature.

Her face went pale.

“I signed this.”

Marcus remained still.

“You recognise it?”

“Yes.”

“Do you remember signing it?”

Miriam nodded slowly.

“I remember being told to sign a receipt for the second collection. I thought it was an administrative acknowledgement.”

“Who asked you to sign?”

“A woman.”

“Was she the same woman you previously described?”

Miriam looked at the paper again.

“I believe so.”

“Did you know what was being transferred?”

“No.”

“Did you know why the records were being kept separate?”

“No.”

“Did you know the final destination?”

Miriam shook her head.

“I was told not to ask.”

Damian's expression hardened.

“Why did you agree?”

Miriam looked down.

“Because I was frightened.”

The room fell silent.

She continued.

“The woman knew things about the hospital that she shouldn't have known. She knew where the records were stored, which staff members had access and which procedures could be bypassed.”

“Did she threaten you?” Marcus asked.

“Not directly. She said that if I refused, the hospital would suffer consequences. She said I would be held responsible for the missing records.”

Octavian watched her carefully.

“Did you understand that two children had been switched?”

Miriam looked up sharply.

“No.”

“Did you know the identities of the children?”

“No. I swear I didn't.”

“Then what did you believe the second transfer involved?”

Miriam hesitated.

“Records. I thought it was a records transfer.”

She looked at the document.

“But there was something else.”

Marcus leaned forward.

“What?”

“The woman told me that the second collection was not supposed to be delivered to the same place as the first.”

**9:28 a.m.**

Marcus exchanged a glance with Octavian.

“Did she explain why?”

Miriam shook her head.

“No. She only said the two collections had to remain separate until she gave further instructions.”

“Did she tell you when that would happen?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

Miriam looked at the paper.

“She said after the second transfer was complete.”

Octavian's expression became very still.

“Did she ever contact you again?”

Miriam hesitated.

Then she nodded.

“Once.”

“When?”

“Several weeks later.”

“What did she say?”

Miriam swallowed.

“She asked whether the children were still where they were supposed to be.”

No one moved.

Marcus spoke carefully.

“Did she identify which children?”

“No.”

“Did you answer?”

“I told her I didn't know what she meant.”

Miriam looked at Octavian.

“She became angry. She said I had been paid to make sure the records remained separate.”

Octavian's voice was quiet.

“Did you know then that something had happened to the children?”

“I suspected something was wrong. But I didn't know what.”

“Why didn't you report it?”

Miriam's eyes filled with tears.

“Because I thought I had already become involved in something illegal. I was afraid they would blame me.”

Octavian said nothing for several seconds.

Then he turned to Marcus.

“Record everything. Have her statement reviewed by her legal representative. No pressure.”

Miriam looked relieved.

**10:02 a.m.**

After Miriam left, the room remained silent.

Maximilian finally spoke.

“She knew there were two collections, but not what they contained.”

“Correct,” Marcus said.

Damian looked at the instruction sheet.

“She was involved in the process.”

“But we cannot yet establish that she knew the purpose,” Octavian replied.

Cassian folded his arms.

“And the woman clearly knew more than she admitted.”

Octavian nodded.

“Find the final acknowledgement.”

Marcus picked up the documents.

“We will.”

On the table, the handwritten instruction remained visible.

*The two records must remain separate until the second transfer is confirmed.*

For the first time, they had a witness who remembered the woman giving that instruction.

But the most important question remained unanswered.

What exactly had the woman been waiting to confirm?

And why had she needed two children kept separate?
