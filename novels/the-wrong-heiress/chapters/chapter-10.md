# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 10 — Two Families, One Missing File**

**Wednesday — 7:15 a.m.**

Aria had a new problem.

Her first salary was still two weeks away.

Her electricity bill was due in five days.

And the washing machine in her apartment building had apparently developed a personal disagreement with her clothes.

She stood in the laundry room, holding a damp shirt.

The machine displayed an error code.

Aria pressed the button.

Nothing happened.

She pressed it again.

The machine beeped.

"Wonderful. Even the washing machine has boundaries."

Her neighbour, an elderly woman carrying a basket, glanced at the display.

"Did you put too much detergent?"

Aria looked at the shirt.

"Possibly."

"These new machines are sensitive."

Aria nodded.

"I've noticed."

The neighbour helped her reset the machine.

Five minutes later, it started working.

Aria thanked her and returned upstairs.

It was a small victory.

She intended to enjoy it.

**Vesper Tower — 8:30 a.m.**

Damian had not slept well.

The investigation team had obtained permission to examine the hospital's old security archive.

The relevant recordings were not available as continuous footage. The system had preserved only selected still images from security incidents.

There were hundreds of them.

Most were useless.

Some were damaged.

A few showed hospital staff entering and leaving the east entrance.

Felix sat in front of two monitors.

"Found something."

Damian moved closer.

"What?"

Felix enlarged an image.

A woman stood near the hospital entrance.

Her face was partially visible.

The clothing matched the description Miriam had given.

"Can you identify her?" Damian asked.

"Not yet. The image quality is poor."

"Date?"

"Two days before the births."

Damian looked at the screen.

"Any other images?"

Felix opened another file.

The same woman appeared near the administrative entrance.

This time, she was carrying a folder.

Damian studied the photograph.

"Look at the person behind her."

Felix enlarged the background.

A man was standing beside a parked vehicle.

His face was not visible.

But the vehicle's registration plate was partially readable.

Felix began searching the archived parking records.

"That might help."

Damian nodded.

"Find the registration."

**Vesper Estate — 9:10 a.m.**

Octavian received the initial report.

"Two images?" he asked.

"Two confirmed images of the woman," Damian replied over the secure call. "One additional image shows a man near the parking area."

"Can you identify him?"

"Not yet."

"Then don't speculate."

Damian acknowledged.

Octavian turned to Marcus.

"Has Aria been told about Miriam?"

"Yes. She knows Miriam has provided a statement."

"Good."

"She has not asked for further details."

"Then let her work."

Marcus hesitated.

"She starts at nine every morning."

Octavian looked at him.

"Why are you telling me this?"

"Because you asked about her."

Octavian considered that.

"Fair enough."

**Sterling Mansion — 10:05 a.m.**

Victoria had made an important discovery.

She had located an old medical document in the Sterling family's records.

It was not a birth certificate.

It was a hospital transfer summary.

She placed it beside the document she had found earlier.

The two papers shared the same reference number.

One listed a transfer from Central Women's Hospital.

The other listed Westbridge Administrative Services as the receiving office.

Victoria stared at them.

Why would her family have a document connected to an administrative transfer?

She called Richard.

"Father, I need to ask you something."

Richard was already irritated.

"What now?"

"Why do we have records from Westbridge?"

A pause.

"Where did you find those?"

"In the family documents."

"Put them away."

"Why?"

"Because they are old administrative papers."

"Then why are you worried?"

Richard's voice hardened.

"Victoria, I said put them away."

She ended the call.

For several seconds, she stood still.

Then she returned to her desk.

She photographed both documents.

She had no intention of handing them over.

Not yet.

**Vesper Tower — 11:40 a.m.**

Felix finally identified the vehicle.

It had been registered to a transport company that had operated in the city twenty years earlier.

The company had since been dissolved.

Damian examined the registration history.

"Who owned it?"

"An individual named Thomas Reed."

"Can you find him?"

Felix checked the records.

"He's deceased."

"Family?"

"One daughter. Her name is Clara Reed."

Damian looked at him.

"Where is she?"

"Currently living outside the city."

"Contact her through legal representatives. Ask whether she remembers her father's work."

Felix nodded.

Then he hesitated.

"There's something else."

Damian waited.

"The vehicle was registered to Thomas Reed, but the insurance was paid by a different company."

"Which company?"

Felix displayed the record.

**Northbridge Data Services.**

Damian's expression changed.

The same company that had employed Daniel Mercer.

The same company that had handled the hospital's archived records.

And now, a vehicle connected to the unidentified woman.

**Aria's Workplace — 12:20 p.m.**

Aria was checking invoices when Peter Collins approached.

"Miss Sterling, could you come into my office?"

She followed him.

He closed the door.

"How are you settling in?"

"Quite well, thank you."

"Your work has been accurate. Rachel says you've been helping with the older accounts too."

Aria smiled.

"I've been trying to understand the system."

"Good. I wanted to discuss something."

He placed a folder on the desk.

"We have a backlog of supplier records. I would like you to take responsibility for organising them."

Aria looked through the folder.

"All of them?"

"Over the next few weeks."

"That's manageable."

"Excellent."

He smiled.

"I think you've been a good addition to the team."

Aria returned to her desk feeling considerably better.

She had not inherited a company.

She had not been appointed to a board.

She had simply been trusted with a little more responsibility.

That was enough.

**Vesper Estate — 2:00 p.m.**

Miriam Shaw returned for a second interview.

This time, she brought another document.

A handwritten note.

Damian examined it.

"Where did you get this?"

"It was given to me by the woman who arranged the transfer."

"Why did you keep it?"

"I didn't know what to do with it."

The note contained only a few lines.

**The records must be kept separate. No copies are to be sent to the hospital. All further communication will be handled through Northbridge.**

Maximilian read it.

"Did she give you any other instructions?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"She told me that if anyone asked about the transfer, I should say it was a routine records-management procedure."

Damian looked at her.

"Did you?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Miriam lowered her head.

"Because I was afraid of losing my job. I had a family to support. I convinced myself it was an ordinary administrative matter."

"Did you know the children had been switched?"

"No."

"Did you know which child belonged to which family?"

Miriam hesitated.

Then she answered.

"I knew there were two families involved. I didn't know their identities."

Maximilian placed the note on the table.

"Why did you come forward now?"

Miriam looked at him.

"Because I saw the news about the missing Vesper heiress."

Damian's expression sharpened.

"You saw what?"

"A report about the Vesper family searching for their granddaughter. I recognised the hospital and the dates."

"Why didn't you contact us immediately?"

"I was frightened."

"Of whom?"

Miriam looked towards the window.

"The woman who gave me the instructions."

**Vesper Estate — 3:15 p.m.**

Octavian read the second statement.

He remained silent for a long time.

Then he spoke.

"She knows more than she has told us."

Damian nodded.

"Possibly."

"Do you believe she arranged the switch?"

"No. I believe she helped conceal the records. Whether she knew the purpose is still unclear."

Lucian looked at the handwritten note.

"Northbridge was involved in more than one part of the operation."

"Yes," Maximilian said.

"Then its records are essential."

Damian nodded.

"We're searching for the original archive and the people who handled it."

Octavian looked at the photograph again.

"Find the woman."

**Sterling Mansion — 5:45 p.m.**

Victoria received a message from the unknown number.

**Unknown:** Did you find the transfer documents?

She replied immediately.

**Victoria:** Yes.

**Unknown:** Keep them.

Victoria frowned.

**Victoria:** Why?

**Unknown:** They may be useful.

**Victoria:** What do you want from me?

A pause.

Then:

**Unknown:** I want to know whether you can be trusted.

Victoria laughed bitterly.

**Victoria:** You contacted me. You tell me what you want.

The response arrived.

**Unknown:** The Vespers are looking for the person who arranged the records transfer. They will eventually discover the reason.

Victoria stared at the screen.

**Victoria:** And you know the reason?

**Unknown:** Yes.

Her fingers moved quickly.

**Victoria:** Tell me.

No answer.

She called the number.

It was unavailable.

Victoria threw her phone onto the bed.

"Whoever you are, you're becoming very irritating."

She picked it up again.

Then she looked at the photographs of the documents she had taken.

A thought occurred to her.

If the Vespers wanted the original records, perhaps the Sterling family had something they didn't.

She opened the family document cabinet.

And began searching.

**Vesper Estate — 7:20 p.m.**

Cassian found Octavian in the library.

"Grandfather."

"What?"

"Have you spoken to Aria today?"

"No."

"Neither have I."

Octavian turned a page.

"Then why are you telling me?"

Cassian smiled.

"I thought I would ask whether I could invite her to lunch sometime."

Octavian looked at him.

"Ask her."

"I intended to."

"Then do it."

Cassian nodded.

He took out his phone.

He wrote a message.

**Cassian:** Hello, Aria. I hope your week is going well. Would you be interested in having lunch with me sometime? No pressure if you're busy.

He read it twice.

Then sent it.

**Aria's Apartment — 8:05 p.m.**

Aria had just finished dinner when the message arrived.

She read it.

Cassian.

She remembered him from Saturday.

He had been surprisingly normal.

She typed a reply.

**Aria:** Hello. Thank you for asking. Saturday afternoon would work, if you're free.

The reply came almost immediately.

**Cassian:** Perfect. I'll leave the choice of place to you.

Aria thought for a moment.

**Aria:** Somewhere ordinary, please.

**Cassian:** I can manage ordinary.

She smiled.

**Aria:** I'll believe that when I see it.

**Unknown Location — 11:30 p.m.**

A man placed a file on the desk.

The woman opened it.

Inside were several pages of financial records.

One payment had been made to Northbridge Data Services.

The date was three months before the births.

Another payment had been made six months afterwards.

The recipient was not Daniel Mercer.

It was a private account belonging to a person whose name had been concealed behind a corporate trust.

The woman studied the figures.

"Who found this?"

"An old accountant."

"Where is he?"

"Dead."

She closed the file.

"Then find his records."

The man hesitated.

"The Vespers are getting closer."

"I know."

"Should we move?"

The woman looked at him.

"Not yet."

She stood and walked towards the cabinet.

Inside was a photograph of two newborn babies.

One had a small hospital identification band.

The other had a different band.

She touched the edge of the photograph.

"Twenty years," she whispered.

Then she turned to the man.

"Find out whether Aria has been told anything about the woman who arranged the transfer."

"Why?"

The woman looked at the photograph again.

"Because if she remembers her, everything changes."

She closed the cabinet.

And locked it.
