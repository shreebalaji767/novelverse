# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 13 — The Man Who Remembered the Wrong Thing**

**Monday — 8:15 a.m.**

Aria had developed a new appreciation for Mondays.

Not because she particularly liked them.

She simply preferred having somewhere to go, something to do, and a reason to get out of bed that didn't involve staring at the ceiling.

At Peter Collins's accounting firm, the morning began with a stack of supplier invoices and a message from her colleague, Nina.

*Peter wants the payment reconciliation before lunch. Apparently, the client has discovered that numbers are supposed to match.*

Aria typed back.

*Ambitious of them.*

Nina sent three laughing emojis.

Aria put her phone away and opened the accounts software.

She had work to do.

**9:32 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus stood in front of a conference table.

A photograph of the demolished commercial building was displayed on the screen.

“Edmund Hale has confirmed the building was his,” he began. “He remembers leasing a second-floor office to Greybridge Records & Dispatch. He also remembers the company requested a separate entrance key and refused to provide a permanent contact number.”

Octavian studied the photograph.

“Did he know what they did?”

“He believed they handled document storage and private deliveries. The lease described the activity that way.”

“Believed?”

“He never inspected the office after the keys were handed over.”

Damian leaned forward.

“Did he meet the tenant?”

“Once. A man collected the keys and paid the initial deposit.”

“Description?”

“Middle-aged. Average height. Dark hair. He wore glasses. Mr. Hale cannot remember his name.”

Cassian examined the lease.

“Convenient.”

“Not necessarily,” Marcus replied. “The building had several short-term tenants. Mr. Hale was managing multiple properties.”

Maximilian looked at the payment records.

“Who paid the rent?”

“Northbridge Data Services. The payments came through a commercial property management account.”

Felix raised his hand slightly.

Everyone looked at him.

“Why are you raising your hand?” Cassian asked.

“Because I have information.”

“Just speak.”

Felix ignored him.

“The trading certificate for Greybridge was issued using an administrative address that belonged to a company called Bellweather Corporate Services.”

Marcus nodded.

“We found that yesterday.”

“But Bellweather's records show it received correspondence from another company. That company was dissolved six months later.”

Octavian's gaze sharpened.

“Name?”

“Crestline Administrative Partners.”

Marcus added it to the board.

Another company.

Another intermediary.

Another layer between the hospital and whoever had arranged the transfer.

Damian folded his arms.

“How many companies are we going to find?”

“Hopefully fewer than the number of people who have asked me that question,” Marcus said.

Cassian smiled.

“That's the spirit.”

**11:48 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria placed the completed reconciliation report on Peter's desk.

He checked the figures.

Then he checked the supporting entries.

“Everything matches.”

“Yes.”

“Did you find the discrepancy from Friday?”

“The supplier had entered the same invoice twice under slightly different reference numbers. The descriptions were identical, but one had an extra space in the reference field.”

Peter looked at the report.

“That's irritating.”

“It's also expensive if nobody notices.”

He nodded.

“Good work. Send the corrected version to Nina and keep the original records attached.”

“Already done.”

Peter gave her a brief smile.

“You're settling in.”

“I'm trying.”

“Keep doing that.”

Aria returned to her desk.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Marcus.

*The investigation has identified the former owner of the commercial building connected to the courier's notebook. We are meeting him tomorrow. I will update you when we have something reliable.*

Aria replied:

*All right. Please don't turn a meeting into an interrogation.*

Marcus answered almost immediately.

*I will make that clear.*

She put the phone away.

Then she returned to her invoices.

**Tuesday — 10:05 a.m.**

Edmund Hale arrived at Vesper Tower carrying a brown leather folder.

He was an elderly man with neatly combed white hair and a walking stick he used more for balance than support.

Marcus greeted him personally.

“Mr. Hale. Thank you for coming.”

“I was curious when your people contacted me. I haven't thought about that old building in years.”

“We appreciate your time.”

Edmund glanced at the security personnel near the lift.

“Am I in trouble?”

“No.”

“Good. I'm too old to be in trouble.”

Marcus smiled politely.

“We only want to understand a historical business arrangement.”

Edmund followed him into the meeting room.

Octavian, Maximilian and Damian were already there.

No one stood over him. No one raised their voice.

A chair had been placed at the table, with water and tea nearby.

Edmund looked at Octavian.

“Mr. Vesper?”

“Yes. Thank you for coming.”

Edmund sat.

“I assume this has something to do with Greybridge.”

“It does,” Marcus said.

He placed the lease copy on the table.

“Do you recognise this?”

Edmund adjusted his glasses.

“Yes. That's my old building.”

“And this company?”

“Greybridge Records & Dispatch. They rented the second-floor office.”

“Do you remember the person who signed the lease?”

Edmund frowned.

“Not his name.”

“Anything else?”

“He had a briefcase. Dark coat. He spoke very little.”

“Did he work alone?”

“I only met him once. I couldn't tell you.”

Damian leaned forward.

“Did you ever see anyone else entering the office?”

Edmund considered the question.

“Yes. A woman.”

Marcus looked up.

“Can you describe her?”

“Not very well. It was a long time ago.”

“Anything you remember would help.”

“She came in once while I was checking the building's electrical supply. She was carrying a large envelope. She asked me whether the office had a rear entrance.”

“Did you tell her?”

“Yes. There was a service staircase.”

“Did she say why she needed it?”

“No. I thought she was an employee.”

Edmund tapped his walking stick lightly against the floor.

“Actually, I remember something else.”

Everyone waited.

“She didn't have a key.”

Marcus's expression changed slightly.

“What do you mean?”

“She asked me to open the office. I told her the tenant had been given keys. She said she had forgotten hers.”

“Did you let her in?”

“Yes.”

“Did you see anyone inside?”

“No. She went in alone.”

“How long did she stay?”

“Ten minutes. Perhaps fifteen.”

Marcus wrote it down.

“Do you remember the date?”

Edmund shook his head.

“No. But it was before the building's annual safety inspection. I still have those records.”

Octavian spoke for the first time.

“Would you be willing to let us examine them?”

“Copies only.”

“Of course.”

Edmund looked relieved.

“I don't want to be dragged into something I don't understand.”

“You won't be,” Octavian said.

**11:16 a.m.**

The old safety inspection records were stored in Edmund's personal archive.

His assistant had brought the relevant folder to the meeting.

Marcus opened it carefully.

A handwritten entry appeared beside the inspection date.

*Second-floor tenant requested access to rear staircase. Office occupied. No electrical faults reported.*

The entry was dated eleven days after the Greybridge lease began.

Another note appeared on the next page.

*Additional key issued to authorised representative.*

Marcus looked at Edmund.

“Do you remember issuing another key?”

Edmund studied the handwriting.

“I remember the request. I don't remember the person.”

“Was the key issued to the woman?”

“I think so. She was the one who came to me.”

“Was identification checked?”

Edmund looked uncomfortable.

“Probably not. I was managing the building myself. We weren't as formal about these things then.”

“Do you have a record of the key number?”

Edmund searched the folder.

He found a small metal-key register.

The entry listed two keys.

One issued to the original tenant.

One issued to an authorised representative.

The second key had been returned four months later.

The name column was blank.

Octavian examined the register.

“Was the key returned by the same person?”

“I can't remember.”

Marcus made a note.

The woman had entered the office.

She had access to the rear staircase.

And she had been authorised to possess a key.

It was not proof that she arranged the hospital transfer.

But it was evidence that she had access to the company operating the office connected to the courier.

“Mr. Hale,” Marcus said, “would you allow us to make copies of these records?”

“Yes. As long as I keep the originals.”

“Agreed.”

**12:40 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

After Edmund left, the room remained quiet.

Maximilian reviewed the notes.

“The woman could have been an employee.”

“Or a representative,” Damian said.

“Or someone using the office,” Cassian added.

Octavian looked at Marcus.

“Do we know whether the key was returned before or after the courier delivery?”

“Not yet. The surviving records don't provide a precise date.”

Felix entered the room carrying a laptop.

“I found something.”

Cassian sighed.

“Do you ever arrive without a dramatic announcement?”

“No.”

Felix connected his laptop to the display.

A scanned municipal record appeared.

“Greybridge's business certificate included a postal forwarding instruction. It was submitted under the name of the tenant's authorised representative.”

Marcus studied the document.

“Can you read the name?”

Felix enlarged the image.

The signature was faint, but the printed name was legible.

**Mara Voss.**

Damian looked at the screen.

“Is that the woman?”

“We don't know,” Felix said. “It could be the woman, the man who rented the office, or another authorised representative.”

“Can you trace her?”

“I can trace the registration records. That doesn't mean the name belongs to a real person.”

Octavian nodded.

“Verify it before drawing conclusions.”

Felix looked pleased.

“I already started.”

Cassian leaned toward Maximilian.

“Do you think he sleeps?”

“No,” Maximilian replied.

“I heard that,” Felix said.

“Good.”

**3:21 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria sat at her desk, examining the photocopies she had taken.

The Greybridge name had bothered her all morning.

She had searched through the Sterling family documents again, looking for anything that might connect it to Westbridge.

She found an old envelope tucked behind a folder of property records.

Inside was a payment summary from a private administrative account.

The date was close to the births.

The amount was modest compared with the sums usually associated with Sterling business.

But the recipient was listed as:

**Crestline Administrative Partners.**

Victoria stared at it.

She read the name again.

Then she searched the other documents.

The same company appeared in a correspondence summary, but without an explanation.

Her phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

*You found the payment.*

Victoria immediately typed:

*Who are you?*

The reply came after several seconds.

*Someone who knows why the records were separated.*

Victoria's fingers tightened around the phone.

*Then tell me.*

*Not yet.*

*Why contact me if you won't explain?*

*Because you have access to documents other people would prefer to remain buried.*

Victoria looked at the payment summary.

*What does Crestline have to do with my birth?*

The response was brief.

*Ask your father why the Sterling family paid an administrative company connected to the hospital records.*

Victoria stood up.

For the first time, the anonymous messages had given her something she could verify.

And she intended to use it.

**5:07 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria returned home, put her handbag down and checked her messages.

There was one from Helena.

*I hope work went well. Felix has promised not to send you any equipment without asking.*

Aria smiled.

She replied:

*Please tell him I appreciate the restraint.*

A second message arrived from Marcus.

*Mr. Hale confirmed that Greybridge Records & Dispatch rented the office connected to Thomas Reed's delivery. A woman had access to the premises under the name Mara Voss. We are verifying whether that identity is genuine.*

Aria read it twice.

Then she replied:

*That's useful. Please keep checking. Don't assume the name is real until you have proof.*

Marcus answered:

*Agreed.*

Aria put her phone down.

She had spent most of the day looking at invoices.

Somewhere, people were investigating a chain of companies, old records and a mysterious woman who had entered an office twelve years ago.

Her own life remained considerably simpler.

She heated dinner, changed into comfortable clothes and opened a book.

**9:38 p.m. — Unknown Location**

A woman sat at a desk in a dimly lit room.

An associate stood nearby.

“They found the building owner,” he reported.

“I know.”

“They have the key register.”

She turned a page in the folder before her.

“Did they find the forwarding instruction?”

“Yes.”

“And the name?”

“Mara Voss.”

The woman closed the folder.

“That name was never meant to survive.”

“Should we be concerned?”

She looked toward a locked cabinet.

Inside were two old photographs of newborn babies.

“No,” she said. “Not yet.”

The associate hesitated.

“What about Daniel Mercer?”

“Keep looking.”

“And the girl?”

The woman's expression changed.

“Has Aria been told about the woman who arranged the transfer?”

“Not that we can confirm.”

“Find out.”

She stood and walked to the cabinet.

For a moment, her hand rested against the lock.

Then she turned away.

“Someone has started connecting the records,” she said quietly. “We need to know how much they have found before they reach the next name.”

On her desk lay a single sheet of paper.

At the top was an old hospital reference number.

Beneath it, written in faded ink, were two words:

**Second transfer.**
