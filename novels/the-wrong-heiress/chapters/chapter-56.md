# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 56 — The Woman Who Appeared Twice**

**Friday — 1:23 a.m.**

The man stared at the photograph.

Catherine Vesper stood outside the hospital entrance, wearing a dark coat. Her hair was shorter than in the family portraits. One hand rested against the strap of a leather bag.

There was no visible sign of distress.

No security personnel.

No one accompanying her.

Just Catherine, standing beneath the hospital sign.

“You said she was dead,” Vane repeated.

The man looked up.

“She was declared dead.”

“That's different.”

“Her death was registered. Her estate was settled. Her identity was closed.”

“Yet here she is.”

The man snatched the photograph.

“Where did you get this?”

“Elias gave it to me.”

“When?”

“Before he died.”

“Why didn't he show it to the family?”

“Because he believed the photograph would expose someone who was still inside the administration.”

The man examined the date.

Then he looked at Vane.

“This photograph was taken nineteen years ago.”

“Yes.”

“Three years after the allocation reversal.”

“Correct.”

“Then Catherine was alive long after the children were separated.”

Vane nodded.

“And she was still investigating the arrangement.”

The man looked at the photograph again.

“Why was she at the hospital?”

“I don't know.”

“You must have an idea.”

“I have several. None of them is evidence.”

The man put the photograph down.

“Where is the original?”

“Not here.”

“Where?”

Vane looked towards the empty cabinet.

“Elias gave it to someone he trusted.”

“Who?”

“That's what I came to discover.”

**1:31 a.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Catherine was still awake.

The photograph had reached her through Nathaniel.

She stared at it for several seconds.

Then she spoke.

“I remember this day.”

Octavian looked up.

“You remember the photograph?”

“I remember the hospital.”

Lucian moved closer.

“Why were you there?”

Catherine did not answer immediately.

“I was looking for Mara.”

Octavian's expression changed.

“Mara Voss?”

“Yes.”

“Was she there?”

“I don't know. I arrived after receiving a message that she had been taken to the hospital. The message was anonymous.”

Nathaniel examined the photograph.

“Do you remember who sent it?”

“No.”

“Did you find Mara?”

“No. The hospital had no record of her admission.”

Felix looked up from his computer.

“Then why didn't you investigate?”

“I did. The hospital's private admission records had been accessed and partially altered.”

“By whom?”

“I never found out.”

Blackwood studied the photograph's background.

“Can you identify the building?”

Catherine nodded.

“Central Women's Hospital.”

Felix immediately searched its archived records.

“That's where the original allocation began.”

Lucian looked at the photograph again.

“Were you investigating the children?”

“I was investigating Mara. At the time, I believed she had information about the person who arranged the reversal.”

Octavian's voice was low.

“Did you know the girls were still in danger?”

“Yes.”

“Then why did you allow the arrangement to continue?”

Catherine looked directly at him.

“Because I still couldn't identify who was using the trust.”

**1:39 a.m.**

Felix found the hospital's old visitor register.

The relevant page had been scanned years earlier.

He enlarged it.

“Catherine's name isn't listed.”

“Was it a restricted visit?” Nathaniel asked.

“Possibly. There's a separate entry for private visitors.”

Felix opened the second register.

One entry had been marked confidential.

**VISITOR: C. VOSS**

**PURPOSE: PRIVATE CONSULTATION**

**AUTHORISED BY: M. VOSS**

Catherine leaned closer to the screen.

“C. Voss?”

Blackwood frowned.

“Your daughter?”

Catherine nodded slowly.

“Could be.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“Why would Elena use that name?”

“She used Voss as her surname. It was the name she had been raised with.”

“Was she with you?”

“No. I didn't see her that day.”

Nathaniel examined the record.

“The visit was authorised by Mara Voss.”

Catherine's expression changed.

“Mara was alive.”

“Or someone was using her credentials,” Nathaniel replied.

Felix checked the time.

The visit had taken place at 3:18 p.m.

Catherine's photograph had been taken at 3:42 p.m.

Twenty-four minutes apart.

“Someone arranged for both of you to be recorded at the same hospital,” Maximilian said.

Catherine shook her head.

“Not necessarily. I received a message. I went there. Someone else may have used the same location for an entirely different purpose.”

“Then we need the original visitor record,” Nathaniel said.

Felix nodded.

“I'll request it.”

**1:47 a.m.**

At the old archive, Vane had found another document.

It was a hospital payment authorisation.

The reference number matched the visitor register.

**CW-118-PRIV.**

The payer was listed as a private trust.

The authorising signature belonged to Mara Voss.

The recipient was not Catherine.

It was a hospital records officer named **Dr. Simon Weller**.

Hale examined the document.

“Who was he?”

“Records administrator,” Vane replied.

“Was he involved in the infant transfers?”

“Not directly.”

“Then why was he paid?”

“To preserve a confidential admission record.”

Hale frowned.

“Whose admission?”

Vane pointed to the date.

The payment had been processed the morning after Catherine's visit.

The record did not identify a patient.

Only a reference:

**SUBJECT: M.V.**

Hale looked up.

“Mara Voss?”

“Possibly.”

“Was she admitted?”

“I don't know.”

“Then why preserve the record?”

“Because someone wanted proof that a person had been there.”

Hale stared at the paper.

“Someone wanted to prove Mara was alive.”

Vane nodded.

“Or wanted someone else to believe she was.”

**1:54 a.m.**

The second man finally spoke.

“You're telling me Elias knew Catherine was alive, suspected Mara was alive, and still didn't tell Octavian?”

“Elias believed the two women were connected to the same investigation.”

“Connected how?”

“I don't know.”

“Then you know nothing.”

Vane looked at him.

“I know that Elias refused to restore the trust until he could establish who had separated the children and why.”

“And Catherine?”

“She signed the reversal.”

“Yes.”

“She also spent years trying to find Mara.”

“Yes.”

The man looked towards the photograph.

“Then perhaps she wasn't protecting the children from the person who arranged the reversal.”

Vane nodded.

“Perhaps.”

“Perhaps she was protecting them from the person who knew why it happened.”

“That is possible too.”

The man picked up the photograph.

“Who was the person who knew?”

Vane's expression hardened.

“Elias never told me.”

**2:02 a.m.**

Aria's phone rang.

She answered without looking at the screen.

“If this is another historical document, I'm blocking everyone.”

“It's Victoria.”

Aria sat up.

“What happened?”

“I received the hospital photograph.”

“Which photograph?”

“The one of Catherine outside Central Women's Hospital.”

Aria rubbed her forehead.

“Why are you calling me about it?”

“Because the visitor register has another name. C. Voss.”

Aria became more alert.

“Catherine's daughter?”

“Possibly. The record says the visit was authorised by Mara Voss.”

“Did Catherine know?”

“I asked her. She says she went there looking for Mara.”

Aria paused.

“Did she find her?”

“No.”

“Then why is there a payment to preserve a private admission record?”

Victoria went quiet.

“You've seen that too?”

“Yes.”

“Do you think Mara was there?”

“I think somebody wanted a record to exist.”

“That isn't the same thing.”

“No. It isn't.”

Victoria sighed.

“I hate these people.”

“Get some sleep.”

“I'm not tired.”

“Neither am I. That doesn't make us useful at two in the morning.”

Victoria gave a reluctant laugh.

Then she said, “There's something else.”

“What?”

“The hospital register has a second reference. M.V. It might mean Mara Voss.”

Aria looked at the screen.

“Or someone with those initials.”

“Exactly.”

“Send it to me.”

“Already did.”

**2:11 a.m.**

At Greyhaven House, Octavian received a message from Felix.

The hospital record had been recovered.

The confidential visitor entry contained a second page.

It listed a temporary authorisation number.

**CW-118-PRIV-02.**

The authorising officer was Dr. Simon Weller.

The record had been sealed under a special instruction.

*Do not disclose visitor identity without independent custodial approval.*

Catherine read the instruction.

“That isn't a normal hospital procedure.”

“No,” Felix said. “It was a private arrangement.”

“Who paid for it?”

“The trust.”

Octavian looked at the page.

“Then the trust was funding hospital records nineteen years after the children were born.”

“Correct.”

“Who authorised the payment?”

Felix enlarged the final field.

The name was partly obscured by a scanning error.

He ran the image through another enhancement.

The name became readable.

**ALISTAIR VANE.**

Nobody spoke.

Catherine slowly looked at the photograph.

“He was involved.”

Blackwood's expression hardened.

“He was funding the hospital record.”

Nathaniel shook his head.

“Not necessarily. The authorisation could have been made using his office.”

Felix checked the signature.

“This one is handwritten.”

Octavian looked at the date.

“Then Vane was alive and personally authorising payments.”

Catherine stared at the screen.

“Why would he pay to preserve Mara's hospital record?”

**2:18 a.m.**

Vane placed the hospital authorisation on the cabinet.

Hale stared at it.

“You paid for this?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“To preserve evidence.”

“Evidence of what?”

Vane looked towards the door.

“An encounter that never appeared in the official hospital register.”

“Between whom?”

Vane did not answer.

Hale's voice sharpened.

“Was Catherine there?”

“Yes.”

“Was Mara there?”

“I don't know.”

“Then what exactly did you preserve?”

Vane reached into his coat and removed a final document.

It was a hospital security incident report.

The report stated that a visitor had entered the private records corridor at 3:26 p.m.

The visitor had used a temporary access card issued under the name **C. VOSS**.

At 3:31 p.m., the visitor had removed one sealed patient file.

At 3:38 p.m., the file had been returned.

No staff member had reported seeing the visitor's face.

Hale read the final note.

*Patient identity not verified. File contents not altered.*

He looked up.

“Who took the file?”

Vane shook his head.

“I don't know.”

“Was it Catherine?”

“I don't know.”

“Was it Elena?”

“I don't know.”

Hale dropped the report onto the cabinet.

“Then why keep it?”

Vane looked at him.

“Because the file was not supposed to exist.”

**2:26 a.m.**

A car stopped outside the archive.

Both men turned.

A moment later, the building's entrance buzzer sounded.

Thomas Reed's voice came through the intercom.

“It's me.”

Vane approached the door.

“Who is with you?”

“Margaret.”

Vane unlocked it.

Margaret entered first.

She looked at the documents on the cabinet.

Then she saw the photograph of Catherine.

Her face tightened.

“You found the hospital record.”

Vane nodded.

Margaret looked at Hale.

“Did you read the incident report?”

“Yes.”

“Then you should know the person who removed the file was not Catherine.”

Hale stared.

“How do you know?”

“Because Catherine was with me.”

Vane became very still.

“Where?”

“Outside the hospital.”

“Why?”

Margaret placed a small envelope on the cabinet.

“Because we were waiting for Mara.”

Hale looked between them.

“You saw Mara?”

Margaret shook her head.

“No.”

“Then who took the file?”

Margaret opened the envelope.

Inside was a photograph of the hospital corridor.

A person stood near the records room.

The face was visible.

Hale recognised it immediately.

So did Vane.

Margaret's voice was barely audible.

“That was the person who took the file.”

She turned the photograph over.

On the back was a handwritten name.

**DORIAN HALE.**

Hale stared at his own photograph.

“I wasn't there.”

Margaret looked at him.

“Then someone had your face, your credentials, and access to the hospital.”

Vane picked up the photograph.

For the first time that night, he looked genuinely unsettled.

“Someone was impersonating Hale nineteen years ago.”

Thomas looked towards the locked archive door.

“And now someone is trying to recover the same records.”

Margaret nodded.

“Which means whoever took that file may still be alive.”

**The hospital had preserved the wrong person's identity.**
