# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 54 — The Man Who Was Declared Dead**

**Thursday — 10:14 p.m.**

Dorian Hale stared at the photograph.

The man stood between Elias Vesper and Gideon Rook, one hand resting on a walking stick.

He wore a dark suit. His expression was composed, almost bored.

Hale recognised him.

Not from a photograph.

From a signature.

“That's impossible,” he repeated.

Margaret Ellison placed the folder on the table.

“Apparently, you have developed a habit of saying that.”

Hale pointed at the man.

“Where did you get this?”

“From Elias.”

“When?”

“Six months after the man's recorded death.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“Who is he?”

Margaret looked at Hale.

“Tell him.”

Hale swallowed.

“Lord Alistair Vane.”

Thomas frowned.

“The former executive custodian?”

“Yes.”

“And he was dead?”

“According to the official records, he died twenty-two years ago.”

Margaret corrected him.

“Twenty-one years and seven months ago.”

Hale looked at her.

“You remember the exact date?”

“I remember the date because I was instructed to remove his name from the active authority register.”

Thomas examined the photograph again.

“Why would a dead man be standing with Elias?”

“That,” Margaret said, “is what I have been trying to establish.”

**10:21 p.m.**

The folder contained four documents.

The first was the photograph.

The second was a copy of Vane's death registration.

The third was an appointment record.

The fourth was a handwritten note.

Hale read the appointment record.

**ALISTAIR VANE — EXECUTIVE CUSTODIAN**

Appointment date: twenty-four years earlier.

Authority: EC-01.

Termination: pending formal succession.

He looked at Margaret.

“His appointment was never terminated.”

“No.”

“But his death was recorded.”

“Yes.”

“Then who took over?”

Margaret pointed to the final line.

**Temporary authority transferred to the legal custodian pending replacement appointment.**

“Elias,” Thomas said.

Margaret nodded.

“Elias became the temporary executive custodian.”

Hale looked again at the photograph.

“So Rook delivered my key to Elias.”

“Yes.”

“But Elias was only temporary.”

“Yes.”

“And the approval under my credentials was entered while he held both positions.”

Margaret nodded.

“That is why the old records are important. The person who authorised the approval may have been acting under Vane's original appointment.”

Thomas frowned.

“Wouldn't that make Elias responsible?”

“Not necessarily,” Margaret replied. “The executive custodian could authorise the use of the terminal, but the approval still required a separate verification.”

Hale turned the photograph over.

There was writing on the back.

*Elias — do not let them replace the register.*

Below it was a single initial.

**A. V.**

**10:29 p.m.**

Thomas examined the photograph's date.

“Six months after Vane's death.”

Margaret nodded.

“That's when I received it.”

“From Elias?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“He wanted me to understand that Vane's death had been used to justify a change in the authority structure.”

Hale looked up.

“Did you tell anyone?”

“I asked Elias what he intended to do.”

“And?”

“He told me not to investigate the death registration.”

“Why?”

“Because the registration was not the important part.”

Margaret tapped the appointment record.

“The important part was that someone had continued using Vane's authority after his supposed death.”

Thomas looked at the photograph.

“Then he may have been alive.”

“Possibly,” Margaret said.

“Or someone used his name.”

“Also possible.”

Hale frowned.

“You've had this photograph for twenty years.”

“Yes.”

“And you never told the family?”

“I tried.”

“Who stopped you?”

Margaret's expression became guarded.

“I was instructed to keep the matter confidential by someone who had authority to issue that instruction.”

“Who?”

She looked at him.

“Alistair Vane.”

**10:37 p.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Felix received a new document from Thomas.

He enlarged the photograph on his screen.

“That's him.”

Octavian approached.

“Who?”

“Alistair Vane.”

Octavian's face changed.

Lucian looked over.

“You knew him?”

“I met him twice.”

“Was he the executive custodian?”

“Yes.”

“Did you know he had died?”

“I was informed of his death.”

Octavian studied the photograph.

“When?”

“Twenty-one years ago.”

Felix looked up.

“The photograph was received six months after that.”

Octavian said nothing.

Catherine had joined the meeting by secure call.

When she saw the photograph, she leaned closer to the screen.

“Where did you find this?”

“Margaret kept it,” Felix replied.

Catherine stared at Vane.

“That's him.”

“You knew him?” Nathaniel asked.

“He was responsible for the trust's administrative authority before Elias took over.”

“Did you know he was alive?”

“No.”

“Did you know he had been involved in the protective arrangement?”

Catherine hesitated.

“He was present during the original discussions.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“You said the three custodians were you, Elias, and Evelyn.”

“They were the three people responsible for the trust's protective arrangement. Vane was responsible for the administrative authority.”

“Then why wasn't he included?”

“Because the arrangement was designed to prevent any single administrator from controlling the descendants or the trust.”

Blackwood examined the photograph.

“Yet he was present when it was discussed.”

“Yes.”

Nathaniel's expression darkened.

“Which means he knew about the children.”

Catherine nodded.

“He knew that two descendants were expected. He knew the trust's safeguards. And he knew that a protective allocation was being considered.”

**10:46 p.m.**

Felix opened the original authority register.

The entry for Vane had a termination date.

But the signature confirming his death was missing.

Instead, there was a notation:

*Authority suspended pending independent confirmation.*

Felix enlarged the entry.

“That's strange.”

“What?” Maximilian asked.

“His death was recorded in the personnel system. But the executive authority register never formally terminated his appointment.”

Blackwood leaned closer.

“So the authority remained legally suspended.”

“Until someone confirmed his death and appointed a replacement.”

“Who confirmed it?”

Felix searched.

A document appeared.

**Death Verification — A. Vane**

The verification had been signed by a medical officer and countersigned by a family representative.

The family representative's name was obscured in the scanned copy.

“Can you recover it?” Lucian asked.

“Possibly.”

Felix ran the document through the archive's image enhancement system.

The obscured signature gradually became clearer.

He stopped.

“That's not possible.”

Maximilian looked at him.

“Try saying something else.”

Felix ignored him.

The signature read:

**Elias Vesper.**

Octavian stared at the screen.

“My brother certified Vane's death?”

“Yes.”

“Then why did he later photograph him alive?”

Nobody answered.

**10:54 p.m.**

At the private office, Hale was still studying the documents.

“There is another problem,” he said.

Margaret looked at him.

“What?”

“The death verification was countersigned by Elias.”

“Yes.”

“Then Elias knew Vane had supposedly died.”

“Yes.”

“And six months later, he received a photograph showing Vane alive.”

“Yes.”

Hale placed the documents together.

“Why didn't he report it?”

Margaret's voice lowered.

“Because he believed the person who had arranged the false death was still inside the family administration.”

Thomas looked at her.

“Did he know who?”

“No.”

“Did he suspect Vane himself?”

Margaret shook her head.

“He suspected someone was using Vane's authority. He didn't know whether Vane was alive, whether someone had impersonated him, or whether the photograph had been deliberately staged.”

Hale rubbed his forehead.

“So Elias kept the photograph and did nothing.”

“He did something,” Margaret said.

She opened the folder's inner pocket.

A narrow strip of paper was attached to the lining.

It contained a reference number.

**REGISTER VA-09.**

Thomas looked at it.

“What is that?”

“The original appointment register.”

“Where?”

Margaret took a breath.

“Not at Ellison House.”

Hale leaned forward.

“Where did Elias hide it?”

Margaret looked towards the window.

“At the old Vesper family archive.”

**11:02 p.m.**

Octavian received the same information five minutes later.

He stood at the window of the conference room.

“The old family archive was sealed after Elias died.”

Lucian nodded.

“Only the original custodial keys could open it.”

“Who has them?”

“Two were recorded as destroyed. The third was assigned to Elias.”

Felix checked the key inventory.

“There's no record of its return.”

Catherine spoke through the call.

“Elias never returned it.”

Octavian turned.

“Do you know where he kept it?”

“No.”

“Did he tell you?”

“No. He told me that if the protective arrangement failed, the original records should remain inaccessible until both descendants could examine them independently.”

Blackwood folded his arms.

“That sounds like the same principle in Anna's trust.”

“It was,” Catherine replied.

Nathaniel looked at the screen.

“Then the archive may contain the original authority register, the appointment records, and the identity of whoever approved the allocation.”

Felix was already opening the old archive index.

“I've found the building's inventory.”

“Can you access it?” Maximilian asked.

“Not remotely.”

“Why?”

“The archive's system was physically disconnected from the network after Elias's death.”

Octavian looked at Lucian.

“Then we go there.”

**11:11 p.m.**

Aria received another call.

This time, the number was familiar.

Marcus.

“Miss Aria, we have located the original appointment register.”

“Where?”

“The old Vesper family archive.”

“Is it accessible?”

“Not yet.”

“Who has the key?”

“According to the records, Elias Vesper.”

Aria looked at her phone.

“Then it's been missing for twenty-two years.”

“Yes.”

She sighed.

“Of course it has.”

Marcus paused.

“There is another development.”

“Go on.”

“The man in the photograph, Alistair Vane, was officially declared dead. However, the authority associated with his appointment was never formally terminated.”

“Meaning?”

“Someone may have continued using his office after his death.”

“Or he wasn't dead.”

“Correct.”

Aria walked towards the kitchen.

“Have you told Victoria?”

“Not yet.”

“Tell her the facts. Don't send her a photograph with a dramatic message.”

“I wouldn't dream of it.”

“Marcus.”

“Yes?”

“I know you.”

He paused.

“I'll send the facts.”

“Thank you.”

She ended the call.

Then she looked at the dinner she had abandoned earlier.

It was cold.

She put it in the microwave.

For a few seconds, the entire Vesper mystery was less important than finding something to eat.

**11:19 p.m.**

At the old family archive, a security light flickered on.

The building had been closed for decades.

Its windows were covered. Its entrance was sealed. The access records showed no authorised visitor in twenty-two years.

A man stood outside the main door.

He removed a key from his coat.

It was old, heavy, and marked with a small engraved symbol.

**VA-09.**

He inserted it into the lock.

The mechanism turned.

Inside, the archive was dark.

He entered without switching on the lights.

He knew where he was going.

At the end of the corridor stood a locked cabinet.

He opened it.

There were three empty shelves.

The fourth contained a single envelope.

He picked it up.

On the front, in Elias Vesper's handwriting, were six words:

*For the person who finds the key.*

The man opened the envelope.

Inside was a note.

He read it once.

Then again.

His expression changed.

The note contained no explanation, no accusation, and no name.

Only one sentence:

**“If Vane is alive, do not let him reach the girls.”**

The man folded the note.

Behind him, the archive door closed.

Someone else had entered.

“Still looking for Elias's records?”

The man turned.

A familiar voice came from the darkness.

“Then perhaps you should start looking for Elias.”

**And somewhere in the sealed archive, a second key turned.**
