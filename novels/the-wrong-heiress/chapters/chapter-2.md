# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 2 — The Man Who Ordered the Entire Restaurant**

At 9:15 the following morning, Aria received a message.

**Unknown Number:** Good morning, Miss Sterling. This is Marcus Vale. We spoke yesterday. Your meeting has been arranged for 12:30 p.m. at the Grand Meridian Hotel. A table has been reserved under your name.

Aria read it twice.

Then she replied.

**Aria:** Is there food?

**Marcus:** Yes, Miss Sterling.

**Aria:** Actual food or those tiny expensive things served on enormous plates?

Marcus stared at his phone.

He typed a response.

Deleted it.

Typed another.

**Marcus:** The restaurant serves a full menu.

**Aria:** Acceptable. See you there.

Marcus placed the phone on his desk.

Across from him, a young security officer waited with a tablet.

"Sir?"

"What?"

"Should we inform the restaurant that the guest is arriving?"

Marcus looked at him.

"We have already informed them."

"Should we increase security?"

"No."

"Cancel the public reservations?"

"No."

"Clear the entire restaurant?"

Marcus slowly removed his glasses.

"Did I say anything about clearing the restaurant?"

"No, sir."

"Then why are you suggesting it?"

The officer straightened.

"Security protocol."

Marcus stared at him for a moment.

"Your understanding of security is apparently based on preventing people from having lunch."

The officer said nothing.

Marcus put his glasses back on.

"Two discreet security personnel. No visible weapons. No unnecessary interference. The restaurant remains open."

"Understood."

"And tell the manager not to make a spectacle of the meeting."

"Certainly."

Marcus paused.

"Also, no flowers."

The officer looked confused.

"No flowers, sir?"

"She did not request flowers."

"Understood."

The officer left.

Marcus looked at the appointment details again.

**Guest: Aria Sterling.**

He had spent most of the night reviewing her available records.

Twenty years of ordinary life.

School results. Medical records. Residential history. Travel records. Employment enquiries. A few photographs.

Nothing suggested she had any idea who she was.

He had also reviewed the Sterling family's treatment of her.

That particular file had made him reconsider several opinions about human intelligence.

He picked up his telephone.

"Connect me to Chairman Vesper."

At 11:40 a.m., the Vesper family gathered in the main residence.

This was not unusual.

What was unusual was that nobody had been told why.

Octavian Vesper sat at the head of the long table.

Beside him, Isolde Vesper calmly poured tea.

Lucian stood near the window, looking at his phone.

Helena sat with both hands clasped together.

Maximilian had brought a laptop.

Damian had brought security reports.

Cassian had brought coffee.

Felix had brought a tablet, two charging cables, and a small device that looked suspiciously like a dismantled router.

Octavian surveyed them.

"Where is Marcus?"

"On his way," Maximilian answered.

"Good."

Cassian leaned back.

"Grandfather, are we discussing a hostile acquisition?"

"No."

"Government interference?"

"No."

"An assassination attempt?"

"No."

Cassian considered this.

"Then why did you summon everyone before lunch?"

Octavian looked at him.

"Because I have something important to tell you."

Felix raised his hand.

"Is it about the missing successor?"

Everyone turned toward him.

Felix blinked.

"What?"

Octavian slowly put down his teacup.

"Why would you ask that?"

Felix pointed at the security report on Maximilian's laptop.

"Because the report was open on his screen."

Maximilian immediately closed the laptop.

Cassian grinned.

"Excellent security. Our secrets survived until breakfast."

Damian ignored him.

"Is the information confirmed?"

Octavian nodded.

"Three independent genetic laboratories. Original hospital records. A verified chain of custody."

Helena's hands tightened.

"She's alive?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Here. In the country."

Helena stood.

"Then bring her home."

Octavian looked at her.

"We will."

"Today?"

"Not yet."

"Why not?"

"Because she doesn't know us."

Helena stared at him.

"She's our daughter."

"To us, yes. To her, we're strangers."

The words settled over the room.

Lucian finally turned away from the window.

"What does she know?"

"She knows that the hospital made a mistake and that she was raised by the wrong family."

"And the Sterlings?"

"Her biological identity was confirmed yesterday. They have accepted Victoria Sterling into their household."

Lucian's expression became colder.

"They sent our daughter away?"

Octavian did not answer immediately.

"They did."

Damian placed a file on the table.

"I reviewed the circumstances of her departure. She left voluntarily after Richard Sterling informed her that she would no longer have a place in the household."

"Voluntarily?" Helena repeated.

"She was told to leave," Damian clarified. "She chose not to argue."

Cassian picked up the file.

"That's almost impressive. They managed to make a twenty-year-old leave her home without understanding what they had thrown away."

Maximilian looked at him.

"Don't turn this into a joke."

"I'm not. I'm wondering how someone can be so confident and so spectacularly wrong."

Isolde finally spoke.

"Enough about the Sterlings."

Everyone became quiet.

She looked at Octavian.

"When will we meet her?"

"Marcus has arranged a meeting for today."

Helena's eyes widened.

"Today?"

"At half past twelve."

"Where?"

"The Grand Meridian."

Helena looked toward Lucian.

"Why a hotel?"

"Because she requested somewhere public."

That silenced the room.

Octavian continued.

"She also requested food."

Cassian lowered his coffee.

"Food?"

"Yes."

"What kind?"

"I don't know."

Cassian looked delighted.

"At last, a family member with sensible priorities."

Maximilian shut his laptop.

"Who is attending?"

"Marcus will meet her."

"And us?"

"Not today."

Helena looked disappointed.

Octavian reached across the table and gently covered her hand.

"We have waited twenty years. We can wait another day if that is what she needs."

Helena lowered her eyes.

"I don't want her to think we don't want her."

"She won't."

"How can you be certain?"

Octavian looked at the photograph on the table.

"Because I will make certain she knows."

At 12:24 p.m., Aria arrived at the Grand Meridian Hotel.

She wore a simple blue shirt, black trousers, and comfortable shoes.

She had spent fifteen minutes deciding whether the occasion required something more formal.

Then she remembered that she was meeting a stranger for lunch.

A stranger who had refused to explain the purpose of the meeting over the telephone.

She decided the blue shirt was sufficient.

The hotel entrance was enormous.

A chandelier hung above the marble lobby. The floor was polished so thoroughly that Aria could see her reflection in it.

She looked down.

Her shoes were clean.

Good.

She approached the reception desk.

"Hello. I'm looking for a restaurant reservation under Aria Sterling."

The receptionist immediately straightened.

"Of course, Miss Sterling. Welcome."

Aria blinked.

"That was quick."

"Your guest is expecting you."

"Is he?"

"Yes, Miss."

The receptionist gestured toward a private dining area.

Aria followed.

A man in a dark suit stood beside the entrance.

He appeared to be in his forties, with neatly arranged hair and an expression that suggested he had never once been late for anything in his life.

He stepped forward.

"Miss Sterling?"

"Marcus?"

"Marcus Vale. It's a pleasure to meet you."

He offered his hand.

Aria shook it.

"Likewise. You look exactly like someone who schedules meetings for a living."

Marcus paused.

"I suppose that's accurate."

"Do you also schedule people's breathing?"

"Only when absolutely necessary."

Aria smiled.

"Good. I was worried."

Marcus gestured toward the table.

"Please."

The restaurant was elegant, quiet, and almost empty.

Aria looked around.

"Is it always this quiet?"

"At this hour, yes."

"Good. I dislike shouting over food."

Marcus pulled out her chair.

She sat.

A menu was placed in front of her.

Aria opened it.

Then stopped.

She looked at the prices.

Then looked at Marcus.

"Are these prices real?"

"Yes, Miss Sterling."

"Does the restaurant provide a small house with every meal?"

Marcus almost smiled.

"No."

"Disappointing."

She examined the menu more carefully.

Marcus waited.

After nearly a minute, she looked up.

"Do you have anything normal?"

"Normal?"

"Rice. Bread. Chicken. Something that doesn't require a financial consultation."

Marcus turned to the waiter.

"Please bring her the grilled chicken, rice, and vegetables."

Aria looked at him.

"I can order for myself."

"My apologies."

She turned toward the waiter.

"That would be fine, thank you."

The waiter left.

Marcus sat opposite her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Aria folded her hands.

"All right. Let's get to the reason I'm here."

"Of course."

"Who do you represent?"

Marcus considered his answer.

"The Vesper family."

Aria waited.

He waited.

She blinked.

"Should that mean something to me?"

Marcus studied her face.

There was no recognition.

Not even a flicker.

"Perhaps not."

"Are they the people involved in the hospital incident?"

"Yes."

"Are they my biological parents?"

Marcus nodded.

"Your biological family is the Vesper family."

Aria looked at him.

Then she looked at the menu.

Then back at him.

"Right."

Marcus waited for the next question.

It did not come.

"Miss Sterling?"

"Yes?"

"Do you have anything you'd like to ask?"

Aria thought for a moment.

"Are they alive?"

"Yes."

"Are they well?"

"Yes."

"Do they know about me?"

"They do."

She nodded.

"Good."

Marcus looked slightly surprised.

"That's all?"

"For now."

He hesitated.

"You don't seem particularly shocked."

"I was told yesterday that the people I grew up with weren't my biological family. Today you're telling me who is. I'm still processing yesterday."

Marcus inclined his head.

"Understandable."

Aria looked out of the window.

"Twenty years is a long time."

"Yes."

"Did they know I existed?"

Marcus took a breath.

"They have been searching for you for many years."

Aria turned back.

"Searching?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because you disappeared from their lives shortly after your birth."

Aria frowned.

"Disappeared?"

Marcus placed a thin folder on the table.

"The Vesper family has reason to believe that your removal from their care was deliberate."

Aria looked at the folder but did not touch it.

"Someone stole me?"

"We don't yet have the complete explanation."

"Do you know who did it?"

"Not conclusively."

"Then why are you telling me?"

"Because you deserve to know that the circumstances surrounding your birth were not simply an administrative mistake."

Aria leaned back.

For the first time that afternoon, her expression became serious.

"So someone switched two babies deliberately."

"That is our current understanding."

"And nobody knows why?"

"We are investigating."

Aria looked at him for several seconds.

Then she asked:

"Does this mean I'm in danger?"

Marcus answered without hesitation.

"We have no evidence of an immediate threat to you. However, we would like to ensure your safety."

"By following me?"

"By offering protection."

"That sounds suspiciously similar."

Marcus accepted the criticism.

"I understand."

The waiter arrived with their food.

Aria waited until the plates were placed on the table.

Then she picked up her fork.

"Can we continue after lunch?"

"Certainly."

"Excellent. I think better when I'm not hungry."

Marcus watched her take the first bite.

He had prepared for anger, disbelief, tears, demands, and possibly accusations.

He had not prepared for a lunch break.

Half an hour later, Aria put down her fork.

"That was good."

"I'm glad."

"Now, about the protection."

Marcus opened a second folder.

"We can arrange a security detail, provide a secure residence, and assist with any practical difficulties you may encounter."

Aria raised an eyebrow.

"Such as?"

"Accommodation, transportation, financial arrangements, legal representation."

"That's quite a list."

"We would like to make the transition as comfortable as possible."

Aria studied him.

"Why?"

Marcus paused.

"Because you are family."

Aria looked away.

It was a simple answer.

Perhaps too simple.

She had spent the previous evening being told that family was a matter of blood.

Now a stranger was telling her that people she had never met wanted to help her because of it.

She was not sure what to do with that.

"Can I ask something?"

"Anything."

"Are you expecting me to move in with them?"

"No. That decision is entirely yours."

"Good."

Marcus nodded.

"We would like you to meet them when you're ready."

"All of them?"

"Your parents, grandparents, and brothers."

Aria stopped.

"Brothers?"

"Four."

She stared at him.

"Four?"

"Yes."

"How many people are in this family?"

Marcus considered the question.

"That depends on how broadly you define family."

Aria sighed.

"I was afraid you'd say that."

He continued.

"The immediate family would be pleased to meet you."

Aria took a sip of water.

"Do they know what I look like?"

"Yes."

"Have they seen photographs?"

"Yes."

"Then they know I look like a person?"

Marcus blinked.

"Yes."

"Good. I was worried I'd have to introduce myself as a mysterious stranger."

Marcus finally laughed.

It was brief, but genuine.

Aria smiled.

"There's hope for you."

At 1:42 p.m., Marcus escorted Aria toward the hotel entrance.

A black car waited outside.

Aria stopped.

She looked at the car.

Then at Marcus.

"What's that?"

"Transportation."

"I have transportation."

"Your vehicle?"

"My feet."

Marcus looked toward the pavement.

"Would you prefer a taxi?"

"Yes."

"I can arrange one."

"Thank you."

A few minutes later, a taxi pulled up.

Aria opened the door.

Before getting in, she turned back.

"Marcus?"

"Yes?"

"One more thing."

"Of course."

"Please don't tell anyone that I agreed to join the family."

Marcus's expression became serious.

"You haven't agreed to anything."

"Exactly."

"I'll make that clear."

"Good."

She got into the taxi.

Marcus closed the door.

The vehicle pulled away.

He watched until it disappeared into traffic.

Then he took out his phone.

**Vesper Estate — 2:03 p.m.**

Octavian answered on the first ring.

"Marcus?"

"The meeting is complete, Chairman."

"How was she?"

Marcus considered his words.

"She's well."

"Did she ask about us?"

"She asked whether her biological parents were alive and whether they were safe."

Helena, standing beside Octavian, covered her mouth.

"And?"

"She knows the Vesper family has been searching for her."

Octavian nodded.

"Does she want to meet us?"

"She hasn't agreed yet."

Helena's shoulders dropped.

Octavian noticed.

"Did she refuse?"

"No. She simply wants time."

"Good."

Marcus continued.

"She also declined the security vehicle."

Octavian frowned.

"Why?"

"She preferred a taxi."

Silence.

Then Cassian's voice came from somewhere behind Octavian.

"She has excellent judgment."

Octavian ignored him.

"Was the meeting comfortable?"

"Yes, Chairman."

"Did she eat?"

"Yes."

"What did she order?"

Marcus glanced at his notes.

"Grilled chicken, rice, and vegetables."

Octavian nodded slowly.

"Good."

He looked at Helena.

"She ate."

Helena smiled faintly.

As if that small detail were enough to make the day bearable.

Then Marcus added:

"She also requested that we not announce her acceptance into the family."

Octavian's expression changed.

"Acceptance?"

"She has not accepted anything, Chairman."

Octavian leaned back.

"Then we will not announce anything."

"Understood."

"And Marcus?"

"Yes, Chairman?"

"Arrange another meeting."

"Certainly."

"Ask her when she would like to come."

"Yes."

Octavian paused.

"And tell her..."

He stopped.

For the first time in decades, the head of the Vesper Sovereign Dynasty struggled to find the right words.

"Tell her that her mother would very much like to see her."

Marcus looked toward the window.

"I will."

**2:19 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria Sterling was having a difficult morning.

Not because anything had gone wrong.

Quite the opposite.

Everything was going according to plan, except that people were not admiring her quite enough.

She sat in the drawing room with a cup of tea.

Eleanor was beside her.

"Would you like anything else, darling?"

"No, Mother."

"Are you comfortable in your room?"

"Mostly."

"Mostly?"

"The curtains are acceptable, but the furniture is rather old-fashioned."

Eleanor smiled politely.

"We can replace it."

Victoria nodded.

"Thank you."

She picked up her phone.

A photograph of the Sterling mansion's main entrance appeared on her social media account.

She added a short caption.

*Home at last.*

Within minutes, the first comments arrived.

Victoria smiled.

Then she noticed a message from an unfamiliar number.

**Unknown:** I heard the other girl has left.

Victoria's smile disappeared.

**Victoria:** Who is this?

**Unknown:** Someone who knows the situation. Be careful. The girl may not be as insignificant as you think.

Victoria stared at the message.

Then she laughed.

Eleanor looked over.

"What's funny?"

"Nothing, Mother."

Victoria deleted the message.

She placed her phone on the table.

"Some people really do enjoy making trouble."

She lifted her tea.

After all, the Sterling family had welcomed her home.

The mansion was hers.

The inheritance would be hers.

And the girl who had occupied her place for twenty years was gone.

What could possibly go wrong?

**2:36 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria returned home carrying a small takeaway box.

She placed it on the kitchen counter and checked her phone.

A new message had arrived.

**Marcus Vale:** Miss Sterling, thank you for meeting me today. Your family would be grateful for an opportunity to meet you whenever you feel comfortable. Your mother would particularly like to see you. There is no pressure to decide immediately.

Aria read the message twice.

Then she sat on the sofa.

Her mother.

The word felt strange.

She had one mother.

At least, she had believed she did.

Now there was another woman somewhere in the world who had given birth to her and spent twenty years not knowing where she was.

Aria opened the message again.

Her thumb hovered over the keyboard.

Finally, she typed:

**Aria:** I'll think about it.

She sent the reply.

Then she put the phone down.

A moment later, another notification appeared.

**Marcus Vale:** Thank you, Miss Sterling.

Aria stared at the ceiling.

"Four brothers."

She counted on her fingers.

"Four."

Then she looked toward the kitchen.

"At least I won't have to cook for all of them."

Her phone vibrated again.

This time, it was a message from Sebastian.

**Sebastian:** Are you seriously ignoring all of us?

Aria sighed.

She typed:

**Aria:** I'm busy.

**Sebastian:** Doing what?

Aria looked at the takeaway box.

**Aria:** Eating.

She sent it.

Then she switched off her phone.

For the first time in two days, she had something resembling a normal afternoon.

Unfortunately, somewhere across the city, four Vesper brothers were already arguing about who would get to meet their missing sister first.

And none of them intended to lose.

**End of Chapter 2**
