# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 35 — The Person Who Ordered the Switch**

**Thursday — 8:30 a.m.**

The Sterling mansion had never been particularly quiet.

But that morning, nobody seemed willing to speak.

Richard sat at the breakfast table with an untouched cup of coffee. Eleanor stood beside the window. Adrian, Alexander, and Sebastian had gathered after receiving Marcus's call.

Victoria had not come home.

She was at Vesper Estate.

And for the first time, the family understood that the girl they had welcomed back was also the child someone had deliberately removed from their custody.

Eleanor finally broke the silence.

“Is she really Lily?”

Richard looked at her.

“Yes.”

“Then Jonathan Vale raised her?”

“No. Margaret Vale raised her. Jonathan brought her to Margaret.”

Eleanor sat down.

“Why would anyone do that?”

Richard had no answer.

Adrian opened the documents Marcus had provided.

“Infant B was transferred to our estate. Then moved under Route C. Jonathan collected her and took her to Ashford Lane.”

Alexander looked at Richard.

“You approved the temporary custody arrangement.”

“I did.”

“And you never saw the child?”

“No.”

Sebastian leaned back.

“So we had our biological daughter in our estate for six weeks, and nobody told us.”

Richard's expression tightened.

“I didn't know it was Victoria.”

Eleanor looked at him.

“You never told me there was a child there.”

“I know.”

“You let me believe the estate was closed for maintenance.”

“I know.”

Eleanor stood abruptly.

“Stop saying that.”

Richard looked down.

There was nothing he could say that would make the truth less ugly.

**Vesper Estate — 9:20 a.m.**

Victoria had spent the night in the guest wing.

She had not asked for a room.

Helena had simply offered one.

Victoria had accepted after discovering that refusing would apparently lead to an extended discussion involving four people, three tea trays, and an alarming number of pillows.

She entered the breakfast room wearing yesterday's clothes.

Aria was already eating.

Victoria stopped.

“Do you always eat this early?”

“It's breakfast.”

“It's nine twenty.”

“Exactly.”

Victoria looked at the table.

Grilled chicken, toast, fruit, eggs, and several other dishes were arranged across it.

“Why is there so much food?”

Helena smiled.

“Everyone has different preferences.”

Victoria glanced at Aria.

“She eats grilled chicken for breakfast?”

“Not usually,” Aria replied. “But I wasn't going to complain.”

Victoria sat down.

For several minutes, they ate without speaking.

Then Victoria put down her fork.

“Did you know I was the other child?”

“No.”

“But you knew there was another child.”

“Yes.”

“And you never asked whether it was me?”

“I didn't know you were Lily.”

Victoria looked at her.

“You don't seem particularly surprised.”

“I've had a lot of time to get used to people hiding things.”

That silenced Victoria.

After a moment, Aria added, “I don't know what you're expecting me to say.”

“Nothing.”

“Good.”

Victoria looked down at her plate.

“I'm not going to apologise for being the child who was found.”

“I didn't ask you to.”

“And I'm not going to apologise for wanting my family.”

Aria put down her glass.

“Neither did I.”

For once, Victoria had no immediate response.

**10:10 a.m.**

The Vespers had assembled in the conference room.

Felix had enlarged the newly discovered instruction from Mara Voss's locked file. A photograph of the document had been obtained through an independent source who had accessed the records before Mara could remove them.

The wording was clear:

*Neither child is to be returned until the first placement has been accepted.*

Below it was a handwritten reference.

**Instruction Authority: L.V.**

Lucian studied it.

“Lydia Vale?”

“Possibly,” Felix said. “But we can't assume that. The initials could refer to someone else.”

Damian looked at the document.

“Why would someone require the first placement to be accepted before returning the second child?”

“Because the two placements were connected,” Maximilian replied.

Cassian tapped the table.

“Someone wanted one family to accept a child before the other family received theirs.”

Aria frowned.

“Why?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Octavian looked toward Richard.

“Mr. Sterling, did you ever hear that phrase?”

Richard examined the photograph.

“No.”

“Did Mara Voss ever tell you the first placement had been accepted?”

“Yes. She told me the first placement had become permanent.”

“Did she explain what that meant?”

“No.”

Aria looked at Marcus.

“Who was supposed to accept the first placement?”

Marcus brought up the Subject 02 record.

“According to the file, Aria's placement with the Sterlings was marked active. The record later described it as permanent.”

“And the second?”

“Suspended.”

Aria stared at the screen.

“So someone wanted the Sterlings to keep me before they decided what to do with Victoria.”

“That is what the wording suggests,” Felix said.

“Why would they need the Sterlings to accept me?”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“That is the question we must answer.”

**11:35 a.m.**

Lydia Vale arrived at Vesper Tower.

She had been asked to examine the new instruction because her initials appeared on the original hospital administration records.

She read the document carefully.

Then she shook her head.

“That's not my handwriting.”

Felix showed her the scanned hospital instruction signed L. Vale.

“Is this yours?”

“Yes.”

“And this?”

“No.”

Lydia pointed at the new document.

“I never issued an instruction to withhold either child until a placement was accepted.”

“Did you know such an instruction existed?” Maximilian asked.

“No.”

“Did Mara Voss discuss the two placements with you?”

“She discussed administrative separation. She told me the children had to remain under different references until the receiving arrangements were confirmed.”

“Did she say who ordered that?”

“No.”

Lydia examined the initials again.

“Whoever wrote this knew the process. They knew that the first placement had to be completed before the second could proceed.”

“Could the initials refer to you?” Damian asked.

“They could. But they don't prove it.”

She looked at Aria.

“I'm sorry.”

Aria shook her head.

“You've said that before.”

“I know.”

“You didn't make the decisions.”

Lydia looked down.

“I still handled the records.”

“And when you discovered the child hadn't arrived, you tried to find out what happened.”

Lydia nodded.

Aria gave a small shrug.

“Then help us find the person who actually made the decision.”

Lydia's expression changed.

“I will.”

**1:20 p.m.**

Margaret Vale arrived with a small envelope.

She had received the revised letter from Marcus and had spoken with Lily—Victoria—earlier that morning.

She placed the envelope on the table.

“This is the last photograph Jonathan sent me.”

Victoria looked at it.

It showed a little girl sitting on a wooden bench, holding a biscuit in both hands.

On the back, Jonathan had written:

*She's safe. Don't ask where we found her. Just let her grow up.*

Victoria touched the photograph.

“Did he ever tell you why he brought me to you?”

Margaret shook her head.

“He told me that someone had tried to move you somewhere you shouldn't have been. He didn't explain further.”

“Did he know I was a Sterling?”

“No.”

“Did you?”

“No, darling.”

Victoria looked up.

Margaret's voice softened.

“You were Lily to me. You were a little girl who needed a home. That was enough.”

Victoria looked away.

“Why didn't you tell me about the photograph?”

“I did. You were too young to remember.”

Margaret reached into her bag and removed a second envelope.

“This was found among Jonathan's papers. I thought it might belong to the investigation.”

Inside was a short handwritten note.

*The first child was already where they wanted her. The second was never going to be sent there.*

Victoria read it twice.

“Where did he get this?”

“I don't know.”

Felix examined the paper.

“There's a reference number on the back.”

He turned it over.

**CA-17 / ROUTE C**

Richard, who had joined the meeting, went pale.

“That reference was used in my family office.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Did you write this?”

“No.”

“Have you seen it before?”

Richard shook his head.

“But CA-17 was the account I authorised.”

Marcus took the note.

“Then we need to establish how Jonathan obtained it.”

**3:00 p.m.**

Victoria asked to speak with Margaret alone.

They sat in the small garden outside the conference wing.

For several minutes, Victoria said nothing.

Then she asked, “Do you still think of me as Lily?”

Margaret looked at her.

“I think of you as the girl I raised.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It is the only one I can give honestly.”

Victoria looked at the garden.

“Everyone keeps telling me who I was.”

Margaret took her hand.

“You don't have to choose between the names.”

Victoria's eyes filled, though she quickly looked away.

“I don't want to be treated like a missing document.”

“Then don't let them.”

Margaret squeezed her hand.

“You are not a file, Victoria.”

Victoria remained quiet.

For the first time since the investigation began, she did not feel the need to prove anything.

**4:45 p.m.**

Felix had traced the CA-17 reference on Jonathan's note.

It had appeared in a payment ledger maintained by Crestline Administrative Partners.

The entry was dated three weeks before the births.

The payment description was brief:

*Initial placement authorisation — two-family arrangement.*

The beneficiary field was blank.

But the approval signature was preserved.

Felix enlarged it.

A name appeared beneath the signature.

**E. Sterling.**

Richard stared at the screen.

“That's not my signature.”

Eleanor, who had arrived at the estate with Richard, stepped closer.

“It's mine.”

Everyone turned toward her.

Richard looked stunned.

“Eleanor?”

She examined the entry.

“I don't remember signing this.”

“You recognise the signature?”

“Yes. It's mine.”

Felix checked the document metadata.

“The signature was recorded on the original payment instruction. It hasn't been added to the digital copy.”

Eleanor looked at Richard.

“I never authorised a payment to arrange the transfer of children.”

Richard's face had lost its colour.

“Then someone used your signature.”

Octavian looked at the date.

“Three weeks before the births.”

Marcus placed the original Sterling account register beside the payment entry.

“Mr. Sterling, did you know your wife's signature had been used?”

“No.”

Eleanor looked at the document again.

Then she noticed something.

“Wait.”

She pointed to the bottom of the page.

“That's not my signature.”

Felix enlarged it further.

The name beneath the approval was not simply E. Sterling.

It was:

**E. Shaw — acting under Sterling estate authority.**

Richard stared.

“Eleanor Shaw?”

The property custodian.

The same woman who had retained the original birth registration.

**Unknown Location — 10:30 p.m.**

Mara Voss received a call.

She answered without greeting.

“They found the payment instruction,” the caller said.

Mara closed her eyes.

“Which one?”

“The one signed by Eleanor Shaw.”

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then she asked, “Did they identify the authorisation?”

“Yes.”

Mara looked at the locked drawer.

The original birth registration was still inside.

“So they know who approved the first payment.”

“They know who signed it.”

“That is not the same thing.”

The caller was silent.

Mara opened another file.

Inside was a document bearing the name of the person who had issued the original instruction.

She had kept it hidden for twenty years.

The caller spoke again.

“Should we move the original?”

Mara looked at the document.

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because they are finally asking the right question.”

She closed the file.

“The person who ordered the arrangement is not the person who carried it out.”

She paused.

“And Eleanor Shaw was never supposed to sign that instruction.”
