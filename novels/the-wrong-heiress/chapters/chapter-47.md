# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 47 — The Trustee Who Died Twice**

**Thursday — 4:38 p.m.**

Nobody moved.

Catherine was still staring at the security officer.

“Repeat the name.”

“Adrian Blackwood, ma'am.”

“Not possible.”

“He presented identification and a sealed trustee's commission.”

Catherine's fingers tightened around her walking stick.

“Did you see him?”

“Yes.”

“Describe him.”

“Late sixties. Grey hair. Walking with a slight limp. He arrived alone.”

Catherine closed her eyes.

“That is him.”

Victoria looked between them.

“Wasn't Adrian Blackwood supposed to be the third independent trustee?”

“He was,” Catherine replied.

“And you just said he died six years ago.”

“He did.”

Aria looked at the officer.

“Is he still at the estate?”

“Yes.”

“Then don't let anyone take him somewhere else.”

Maximilian nodded to the officer.

“Keep him in the main reception room. No private conversations. No one touches his documents.”

The officer left.

Victoria turned toward Catherine.

“Does everyone in your family have a habit of dying and returning?”

Catherine looked at her.

“Apparently, it has become fashionable.”

Marcus quietly looked at Aria.

“I believe that was a joke.”

“I heard it.”

“I wanted to make sure.”

**5:02 p.m.**

Adrian Blackwood sat in the Vesper Estate reception room.

He was an elderly man with a narrow face, silver hair and a walking stick resting beside his chair.

A glass of water stood untouched before him.

Octavian entered with Lucian, Maximilian and Cassian.

Blackwood rose.

“Octavian.”

Octavian did not offer his hand.

“You were declared dead.”

“I was aware.”

“Your death certificate was authenticated.”

“I was also aware of that.”

“Your burial was attended by representatives of three independent institutions.”

Blackwood glanced at the empty chair.

“I assume you have questions.”

“I have nineteen years of questions for Catherine and six years of questions for you.”

“Then we should avoid wasting time.”

Octavian sat opposite him.

“Why are you here?”

Blackwood placed a sealed envelope on the table.

“Because the trust's verification period has been triggered.”

Lucian examined the envelope.

“By whom?”

“By the surviving trustee.”

“Which surviving trustee?”

“Me.”

Cassian leaned forward.

“You were declared dead. Catherine is alive. Elias is dead. Who exactly has been administering the trust?”

Blackwood looked at him.

“An appointed interim administrator.”

“Name?”

“Gideon Rook.”

Felix, who had just entered, stopped.

“Gideon Rook?”

Blackwood nodded.

Felix opened his laptop.

“Former director of Vesper Fiduciary Services?”

“Yes.”

“Retired twenty-one years ago?”

“Officially.”

Felix looked at Maximilian.

“His credentials were used in several legacy trust transactions.”

Blackwood's expression changed.

“Then you have already found the administrator.”

“Not quite,” Felix said. “We've found his credentials. That doesn't prove he used them.”

“Good,” Blackwood replied. “At least one person in this family understands the difference.”

Cassian smiled faintly.

“He's going to fit in wonderfully.”

**5:19 p.m.**

Aria and Victoria arrived with Catherine.

Blackwood stood immediately.

For several seconds, he looked at them without speaking.

Then he addressed Aria.

“Miss Vesper.”

Aria did not correct him.

“You know who we are?”

“I know who you were intended to be.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“And what does that mean?”

Blackwood looked at her.

“It means your birth identities were never the problem. The problem was the authority attached to them.”

Aria took the chair beside Victoria.

“Can we start with something simpler?”

“Certainly.”

“Why are you alive?”

Blackwood blinked.

Cassian looked down.

Maximilian pressed his lips together.

Even Octavian seemed briefly caught off guard.

Blackwood finally answered.

“Because someone tried to kill me.”

“And the death certificate?”

“Was arranged to prevent a second attempt.”

“By whom?”

“I don't know.”

Aria considered him.

“That's becoming a familiar answer.”

Blackwood gave a tired smile.

“Unfortunately, it is also an honest one.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“You were one of three trustees. Why didn't you contact us?”

“Because the trust's conditions had not been satisfied.”

“Which conditions?”

“Both descendants had to be independently identified, their identities verified, and their consent obtained before any transfer or restoration of authority.”

“We haven't consented.”

“I know.”

“Then what are you doing here?”

Blackwood opened the envelope.

“Someone has attempted to transfer the trust's voting rights without your consent.”

**5:34 p.m.**

Felix took the document.

It was a formal notice of attempted transfer.

The transaction had been submitted through the interim administrator.

The recipient was a private holding company.

**Northstar Heritage Holdings.**

Maximilian read the name twice.

“Who owns it?”

Blackwood answered.

“Gideon Rook.”

Lucian's expression darkened.

“Then the administrator was attempting to transfer the trust's authority to himself.”

“Not directly,” Blackwood said. “The holding company has three registered directors.”

“Who are they?”

Blackwood placed another sheet on the table.

Felix read the names.

“Two are deceased.”

“And the third?” Damian asked.

Blackwood pointed to the last entry.

**Elias Vesper.**

The room became silent.

Octavian stared at the document.

“My brother has been dead for twenty-two years.”

“His signature was used,” Blackwood replied.

Lucian examined the record.

“That's impossible. Elias's authority was terminated when he died.”

“His personal authority was. The trust's legacy system was not properly closed.”

Felix was already typing.

“Someone retained his credentials.”

“More than that,” Blackwood said. “Someone retained the original signing key.”

Octavian's face hardened.

“Who?”

Blackwood looked at Catherine.

She had gone very still.

“Catherine?”

She slowly lowered herself into a chair.

“I know who had it.”

Everyone turned toward her.

“Who?” Lucian asked.

Catherine looked at Octavian.

“Margaret Ellison.”

**5:51 p.m.**

The old secretary had not accompanied Catherine to the estate.

She was still at the residence on Elysium Island.

Maximilian immediately ordered a security team to retrieve her.

Catherine objected.

“Don't frighten her.”

“She may possess evidence of a fraudulent transfer,” Maximilian replied.

“She also knows where the original declaration is.”

“Then we need to speak to her.”

Catherine shook her head.

“Not without me.”

Octavian looked at her.

“You have had nineteen years to explain yourself.”

“And you have had nineteen years to ask.”

The words struck the room.

Octavian's jaw tightened.

Catherine continued.

“Margaret was not the person who controlled the trust. She was the person who maintained its records. Someone used her credentials, her position and her loyalty to make it appear that she was responsible.”

“Who?” Aria asked.

Catherine looked at Blackwood.

He answered.

“Gideon Rook.”

Felix turned his laptop toward them.

“I found him.”

A personnel record appeared.

Gideon Rook had been appointed as an external trust auditor twenty-four years earlier.

He had access to financial statements, voting records and administrative correspondence.

His appointment had been renewed repeatedly.

The last renewal had been signed by Elias.

“Who approved his first appointment?” Lucian asked.

Felix opened the original record.

The signature at the bottom was clear.

**Octavian Vesper.**

Octavian stared at it.

“I don't remember appointing him.”

Blackwood leaned forward.

“Then someone used your authority.”

Octavian's expression became cold.

“Find him.”

**6:14 p.m.**

At Elysium Island, Margaret Ellison was packing a small suitcase.

She had not been told that the Vespers were coming.

She had received a message instead.

**The declaration has been found. Leave immediately.**

Margaret read it again.

Then she looked at the locked cabinet beside her bed.

Inside was a sealed envelope she had kept for more than two decades.

She opened it.

The document was not a trust declaration.

It was a handwritten letter from Elias Vesper.

She had never delivered it.

The first line read:

**Margaret, if Catherine has returned, do not give her the original allocation instruction.**

Margaret sat down.

Her hands trembled.

There was more.

**She must not know that the reversal was not ordered by Rook.**

Margaret looked toward the door.

For twenty-three years, she had carried the guilt of a decision she had not made.

Now she understood.

Rook had used her credentials to submit the instruction.

He had used Catherine's former authority to disguise its origin.

And he had allowed everyone to believe the reversal was an internal Vesper decision.

Margaret clutched the letter.

“Why?”

Rook opened his folder.

“Because the trust was never meant to remain under family control.”

He reached for the letter.

Margaret pulled it away.

“You're not taking this.”

Rook looked at her.

“Then I suppose we have a problem.”

**6:27 p.m.**

The Vesper security team arrived twelve minutes later.

The residence was empty.

Margaret was gone.

Her suitcase remained beside the bed.

The cabinet stood open.

And the letter from Elias was missing.

Maximilian received the report while Octavian was still speaking with Blackwood.

He read it silently.

Then he looked at Catherine.

“Margaret has disappeared.”

Catherine stood.

“Where?”

“We don't know.”

“Was she taken?”

“We don't know.”

Aria glanced at Victoria.

Victoria looked furious.

“Someone is still moving people around as though they belong to them.”

Aria nodded.

“Then we stop letting them do it.”

Blackwood opened his briefcase.

“There is one more thing you should know.”

He removed a final document.

It was a copy of the trust's original voting structure.

At the bottom was a clause that had never appeared in any of the records Felix had recovered.

**In the event of unlawful interference by the appointed administrator, the custodial authority shall revert to the original creator's designated representative.**

Octavian read the clause.

“Who was designated?”

Blackwood looked at Catherine.

She said nothing.

Then he turned the document around.

The name was written clearly.

**EVELYN VESPER.**

Octavian's mother.

Dead for thirty-one years.

And beneath her name was a second line.

**Alternate representative: Catherine Vesper.**

Victoria stared at the document.

“So Catherine can take control of the trust?”

“No,” Blackwood replied.

“Then what can she do?”

He looked at Aria and Victoria.

“She can prevent anyone else from controlling it.”

Catherine closed her eyes.

For nineteen years, she had avoided returning to the family.

Now the trust had been placed back within her reach.

And the man who had stolen its authority knew exactly where she was.

**End of Chapter 47**
