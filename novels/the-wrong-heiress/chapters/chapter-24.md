# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 24 — The Woman Who Asked About the Missing Child**

**Friday — 8:10 a.m.**

Lydia Vale received the invitation at breakfast.

It was a formal letter from Vesper Global's legal department.

The letter explained that an investigation into historical hospital records had uncovered documents bearing her name. It requested a voluntary meeting to clarify her involvement in an administrative transfer twelve years earlier.

At the bottom was a handwritten addition.

*You are under no obligation to attend. We would appreciate the opportunity to hear your account.*

Lydia read it twice.

Her tea went cold.

Then she called the number printed on the letter.

“This is Lydia Vale.”

“Good morning, Ms. Vale. How may I help you?”

“I received your invitation.”

“Yes.”

“I'll come.”

There was a brief pause.

“Thank you. We'll arrange a convenient time.”

“Today.”

“Certainly.”

“And I want to know whether you've found the child.”

The person on the other end did not answer immediately.

“We have recovered records concerning an infant whose destination was never confirmed.”

Lydia closed her eyes.

“So you haven't found the child.”

“Not yet.”

“Then I'll come today.”

She ended the call.

For several minutes, she remained at the table.

Then she opened a drawer and removed an old photograph.

Her brother Jonathan stood beside a delivery van, smiling at the camera.

On the back, in his handwriting, were four words:

*Some things aren't deliveries.*

Lydia put the photograph in her handbag.

**9:30 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus informed Octavian that Lydia had agreed to meet.

“Does she know what we found?” Octavian asked.

“She knows the hospital transfer was never confirmed.”

“Did she ask about the second infant?”

“Yes.”

Octavian nodded.

“Arrange the meeting.”

Marcus hesitated.

“She specifically requested that the discussion include the original hospital records.”

“Agreed.”

“Should Aria attend?”

Octavian considered it.

“Only if she wishes to.”

Marcus contacted Aria.

**10:05 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria was reviewing a monthly reconciliation when Marcus called.

“Good morning, Miss Aria.”

“Hi, Marcus.”

“We have located Lydia Vale. She has agreed to give a statement today.”

“Is she connected to the second child?”

“She worked in Vesper Life Sciences' confidential administration division. Her brother was the courier who collected the second package.”

Aria put down her pen.

“Does she know what happened?”

“We don't know yet.”

“Are you asking me to attend?”

“Only if you want to. The meeting is entirely your choice.”

Aria glanced at her work.

“What time?”

“Three o'clock.”

“I can come after work.”

“Thank you.”

She ended the call.

Peter looked over from his office.

“Everything all right?”

“Family matter. I'll need to leave on time today.”

“That's fine. Your work is already up to date.”

Aria nodded.

“Thank you.”

She returned to the reconciliation.

The numbers still needed checking.

**12:40 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Lydia arrived early.

She wore a plain grey coat and carried a handbag that looked older than most of the furniture in the reception area.

Marcus greeted her.

“Ms. Vale. Thank you for coming.”

She looked around.

“It's a large building.”

“Yes.”

“Does everyone here work for the same company?”

“Not exactly.”

Lydia considered that.

“That's probably complicated.”

Marcus offered a small smile.

“Occasionally.”

He escorted her to a private conference room.

Octavian was already waiting with Lucian and Damian.

Lydia stopped when she saw Octavian.

“You're Mr. Vesper.”

“I am.”

“I didn't expect you to attend personally.”

“This matter concerns my family.”

Lydia sat.

Marcus placed a recorder on the table.

“May we record your statement?”

“Yes. I want everything documented.”

Marcus switched it on.

Octavian folded his hands.

“Ms. Vale, what do you know about the infant transferred from Central Women's Hospital twelve years ago?”

Lydia looked at him.

“I know that the child was supposed to be brought to one of your medical facilities.”

“Were you responsible for the arrangement?”

“No. I processed the administrative admission.”

“Who instructed you?”

“A woman calling herself Mara Voss.”

Damian leaned forward.

“Did you meet her?”

“Twice.”

“Did she identify herself?”

“No.”

“Did she provide credentials?”

“She had a private authorisation code. I was told it had been approved by the central administration office.”

Lucian asked, “Who told you that?”

Lydia looked down.

“I was told by my supervisor.”

“Who was your supervisor?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

The room became still.

**1:05 p.m.**

Marcus opened the recovered admission record.

“Was this the admission you processed?”

Lydia examined it.

“Yes.”

“Why was it cancelled?”

“I didn't cancel it.”

“Who did?”

“Mara Voss.”

“Did she give a reason?”

Lydia shook her head.

“She said the receiving authority had changed.”

“Where was the child supposed to go?”

Lydia looked at Octavian.

“That was what I wanted to know.”

Octavian remained silent.

Lydia continued.

“The infant was expected at a Vesper Life Sciences facility. A medical team had been prepared. A room had been assigned. I received confirmation that the hospital had released the child.”

“Did the child arrive?” Lucian asked.

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Because I was responsible for closing the admission paperwork. The facility never signed the receiving acknowledgement.”

Marcus checked the record.

“The system says the admission was cancelled.”

“That's the administrative status. It doesn't mean the infant was safely transferred somewhere else.”

Damian's expression hardened.

“Did you report this?”

“Yes.”

“To whom?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

“And what did he say?”

Lydia's mouth tightened.

“He told me to stop asking questions.”

**1:25 p.m.**

Lydia reached into her handbag.

“I brought something.”

She placed a folded sheet on the table.

It was a photocopy of an old internal memorandum.

The heading read:

**CONFIDENTIAL INTAKE — VLS-PRIVATE**

Below it was a list of instructions.

1. Prepare an infant admission.
2. Keep receiving-family details restricted.
3. Obtain physical handover confirmation.
4. Do not close the case without verified receipt.

The final instruction had been crossed out.

Beside it was a handwritten note.

*Close under external instruction. No further contact.*

The signature was M. Voss.

Marcus looked at Lydia.

“Why did you keep this?”

“Because I knew something was wrong.”

“Why didn't you take it to the authorities?”

“I tried to get the infant's identity first. I thought if I could establish who the child was, someone would listen.”

“And your request was rejected.”

Lydia nodded.

“They said I wasn't a relative.”

“Were you?”

“No.”

She looked at the document.

“But I was the person who had been told to prepare the child's arrival.”

**2:00 p.m.**

Octavian studied the memorandum.

“Did you ever see the infant?”

Lydia hesitated.

Then she nodded.

“Once.”

The room went quiet.

“Where?” Lucian asked.

“At the hospital.”

“When?”

“On the morning of the handover.”

“Was the child alone?”

“No. A nurse was with the infant.”

“Did you know the child's identity?”

“No. The wristband only carried a temporary reference.”

“Infant B?”

“Yes.”

Damian asked, “Did you see who collected the child?”

Lydia looked uncomfortable.

“I saw a woman.”

“Mara Voss?”

“I believe so.”

“Can you identify her?”

“Not confidently. I only saw her briefly.”

“Did she take the infant?”

Lydia shook her head.

“No. She spoke to the nurse and showed her a document. The nurse then accompanied the infant through the administrative exit.”

“Where did they go?”

“I don't know.”

Octavian's expression remained controlled.

“Did you see a vehicle?”

“Yes.”

“What kind?”

“A dark medical transport vehicle. It had no hospital markings.”

Marcus made a note.

“Do you remember its registration?”

“No.”

**2:35 p.m.**

Lydia looked at the recovered hospital record.

“There is something else.”

Marcus waited.

“My brother collected a package from Greybridge Records & Dispatch.”

“Jonathan Vale?”

“Yes.”

“Was he working for Northbridge?”

“Occasionally. He was a private courier.”

“Did he tell you what the package contained?”

“No. He told me he had been paid to deliver sealed documents.”

“Did he mention Mara Voss?”

Lydia nodded.

“He said she was difficult.”

Cassian, who had joined the meeting, raised an eyebrow.

“Difficult how?”

“He said she refused to sign ordinary receipts, insisted on separate acknowledgements, and kept changing delivery instructions.”

Marcus glanced at the old courier receipt.

“Did your brother know about the infant?”

Lydia shook her head.

“Not from what he told me.”

“Did he ever say where the package went?”

“He said it was not delivered to the address printed on the outside.”

“Did he tell you the actual destination?”

“No.”

Lydia opened her handbag again.

This time, she removed a small envelope.

“My brother gave me this before he died.”

Inside was a handwritten note.

*If anyone asks about the second delivery, tell them to look for the receiving signature. I never saw one.*

Marcus looked up.

“The second delivery?”

Lydia nodded.

“He said the package had been collected, but the final recipient never signed for it.”

**3:00 p.m.**

Aria arrived.

Marcus met her outside the conference room.

“Miss Aria.”

“Hi. Am I late?”

“No. The meeting is still in progress.”

“Has she told you anything?”

“Some important details. We haven't established the infant's final destination.”

Aria entered.

Lydia looked at her.

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Then Lydia stood.

“You're Aria?”

“Yes.”

Lydia's eyes moved over her face.

“I've seen your photograph.”

Aria offered a polite smile.

“Hello.”

Lydia seemed uncertain what to say.

“I'm sorry.”

Aria looked at her.

“For what?”

“For not finding the child.”

Aria pulled out a chair.

“You didn't know where the child went.”

“No.”

“Then you don't have to apologise for that.”

Lydia sat again.

Octavian watched the exchange quietly.

**3:15 p.m.**

Aria read the hospital memorandum.

“So the infant was physically taken from the hospital, but the Vesper facility never received the child?”

“Correct,” Marcus replied.

“And nobody knows where the child went afterward?”

“Not yet.”

Aria looked at Lydia.

“Did you know the infant was connected to my family?”

Lydia shook her head.

“No. I only knew the child had been assigned a temporary reference and was expected at a Vesper facility.”

“Did you know about me?”

“No.”

Aria nodded.

“Thank you for telling us.”

Lydia looked down.

“I should have done more.”

Aria considered her words.

“Maybe. But you came here today.”

Lydia's expression softened.

**4:00 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The meeting ended after Marcus recorded Lydia's full statement.

The legal team would authenticate the memorandum and compare it with the original hospital documents.

Lydia agreed to provide the names of former colleagues and any remaining records she possessed.

Before leaving, she asked to speak privately with Octavian.

He agreed.

Aria remained with Helena in the adjoining room.

Lydia stood across from Octavian.

“I need to tell you something I didn't mention in the statement.”

Octavian waited.

“Three days after the infant was taken from the hospital, I received a telephone call.”

“From whom?”

“Mara Voss.”

“What did she say?”

“She asked whether the infant had arrived at your facility.”

Octavian's eyes narrowed.

“And what did you tell her?”

“I said no.”

“What did she say?”

Lydia swallowed.

“She said, 'Then the first placement remains the only completed placement.'”

Octavian was silent.

Lydia continued.

“She sounded angry. Not frightened. Angry.”

“Did she explain what the first placement meant?”

“No.”

“Did she mention the Sterling family?”

“No.”

Octavian looked toward the closed door.

“Why did you not include this earlier?”

“I wanted to see whether you already knew.”

Octavian studied her.

“And now?”

“Now I think you should know everything.”

**5:10 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Marcus briefed the family.

The second infant had been physically handed over at the hospital.

A woman believed to be Mara Voss had presented an instruction.

A nurse had accompanied the child through the administrative exit.

The intended Vesper Life Sciences admission had never been completed.

No receiving acknowledgement existed.

And three days later, Mara Voss had asked whether the child had arrived.

The family listened in silence.

Maximilian broke it.

“So the infant was not lost inside our medical system.”

“Correct,” Marcus replied.

“The infant was removed before reaching it.”

“Yes.”

Damian looked at Lucian.

“Can we identify the nurse?”

“We have the maternity unit staffing roster. The hospital's legal team is retrieving it.”

Felix spoke from his laptop.

“I can compare the roster against the handover timestamp.”

Cassian leaned back.

“And Jonathan's package?”

Marcus placed the courier note on the table.

“The final recipient never signed for it.”

Octavian looked at the note.

“Then the package may have contained information about the infant's destination.”

“Or another part of the arrangement,” Marcus said.

Octavian nodded.

“Find the second acknowledgement.”

**7:30 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard sat in his study.

The notebook lay open.

Victoria stood opposite him.

“You knew Lydia Vale.”

“I met her once.”

“You said you didn't know who signed the hospital instruction.”

“I said I didn't know who authorised the transfer.”

“That's not the same thing.”

Richard closed the notebook.

Victoria pointed at it.

“What else is in there?”

“Notes.”

“About the children?”

“About the arrangement.”

She stepped closer.

“Father, I want the truth.”

Richard looked at her.

“I am trying to understand it myself.”

Victoria laughed without humour.

“Twenty years in this family, and now everyone has a mystery they refuse to explain.”

Richard's expression softened slightly.

“Victoria, you need to be careful.”

“I'm tired of being told to be careful.”

She turned toward the door.

Then stopped.

“Did you ever wonder why Aria was placed with us?”

Richard did not answer.

Victoria looked back.

“Did you?”

He finally spoke.

“Yes.”

She waited.

“Then why didn't you ask?”

Richard looked at the notebook.

“Because I was told not to.”

Victoria stared at him.

Then she left.

**9:45 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a report.

*Lydia Vale gave a statement to the Vespers.*

She read it.

“What did she tell them?”

“Everything about the hospital handover.”

The woman closed her eyes.

“Did she mention the call?”

“Yes.”

For several seconds, the woman said nothing.

Then she asked, “Did she tell them what I said?”

“Yes.”

The woman looked toward the cabinet.

“Then the Vespers know the first placement was completed.”

“They may suspect the second child was never received.”

“They should.”

The associate hesitated.

“Should we move the remaining records?”

The woman turned sharply.

“No.”

“But—”

“If we move them now, they'll know exactly where to look.”

She picked up a telephone.

“Contact the nurse.”

“Which nurse?”

“The one who carried Infant B out of the hospital.”

**11:20 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had finished comparing the maternity roster with the handover timestamp.

He found the nurse.

**NURSE: ANNE PORTER**

Her employment record showed that she had resigned four months after the incident.

Her forwarding address was outdated.

But there was a note in the personnel file.

**Reference contact: Lydia Vale.**

Felix stared at the screen.

He called Marcus.

“I found the nurse who accompanied Infant B out of the hospital.”

Marcus answered immediately.

“Name?”

“Anne Porter.”

“Where is she?”

“I'm tracing her current address.”

Felix paused.

“There's something else.”

“What?”

“Her personnel file says she left the hospital four months after the handover. Her resignation was processed by Daniel Mercer.”

Marcus looked at the old records.

Another connection.

Another person who had left.

“Find her,” he said.

Felix nodded.

Then he noticed a new entry in the archive.

A request had been made to access Anne Porter's old personnel file.

The request had been submitted six minutes earlier.

It carried a familiar authorisation code.

**M. VOSS.**
