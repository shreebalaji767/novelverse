# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 51 — The Signature That Divided Two Lives**

**Thursday — 4:36 p.m.**

Nobody spoke.

Catherine Vesper stood beside the open ledger, her hands resting lightly against its wooden cover.

The original allocation record lay on the table between her and the two girls.

Her signature was visible beneath the instruction.

*Reverse allocation. Do not notify either family.*

Aria read it again.

Then she looked up.

“You signed it.”

“Yes.”

“You knew there were two babies.”

“Yes.”

“You knew one of them was me.”

“Yes.”

“And you decided where we would live.”

Catherine did not look away.

“I authorised the reversal.”

Aria's expression hardened.

“That's a very careful way of saying it.”

Victoria stepped forward.

“Don't answer her first. Answer me.”

Catherine turned.

Victoria's voice was controlled, but her hands were trembling.

“Why was I kept at the Sterling estate? Why was I treated like some parcel waiting for collection? You knew I was there.”

“I knew you were in temporary custody.”

“That isn't an answer.”

“No,” Catherine said quietly. “It isn't.”

Victoria gave a short, disbelieving laugh.

“You arranged for me to be separated from my biological parents, placed in a private estate, and transferred somewhere else. Then someone took me away, and I grew up believing I was Lily Shaw.”

“I did not arrange for Jonathan Vale to take you.”

“But you knew I was missing.”

“Yes.”

“And you did nothing?”

“I tried to find you.”

“Apparently not very well.”

Catherine accepted the accusation without defending herself.

Octavian moved closer to the table.

“Catherine. Explain everything. Start with the threat.”

Catherine looked at him.

“I will. But I will not ask either girl to excuse what I did.”

“Good,” Aria replied. “Because I wasn't planning to.”

**4:42 p.m.**

Catherine asked Margaret Ellison to open the original record.

Margaret hesitated.

“I would prefer that you do it.”

“Why?”

“Because I have spent twenty years being blamed for documents I was instructed to submit.”

Catherine nodded.

“Fair.”

She opened the record herself.

Inside were three sheets, each bearing an old reference number.

LA-01.

Schedule B.

And a document marked **Protective Allocation — Confidential**.

Felix leaned over the table.

“That's the document we couldn't locate.”

“It was deliberately excluded from the family archive,” Catherine said. “The original was held here, under Anna's authority.”

“By you?” Maximilian asked.

“By the three people entrusted with the temporary arrangement. Me, Elias, and Evelyn.”

Octavian's expression changed.

“Mother?”

“Evelyn knew about the threat. She approved the protective arrangement.”

“Why did she never tell me?”

“Because she believed the danger was inside the dynasty.”

The room went still.

Lucian spoke.

“Inside our family?”

“Inside the administrative structure that controlled the trust.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“No. But it was close enough to make ordinary family channels unsafe.”

Catherine placed the first sheet on the table.

It contained a sequence of dates, names, and financial references.

“The first warning came from Elias. He discovered that someone had been requesting information about the expected descendants before either child was born.”

Felix looked at the page.

“The inquiry from the Vesper administrative system?”

“Yes. The one you found in the incident report.”

“Who submitted it?”

“We never established that conclusively.”

Nathaniel Cross examined the document.

“Then what did you establish?”

“That the inquiry was not merely asking for names.”

Catherine pointed to a paragraph near the bottom.

“It requested the expected beneficiaries' biological identities, their intended households, and the procedures required to activate their rights under Anna's trust.”

Blackwood's face tightened.

“Someone was preparing to use the children.”

“Exactly.”

Aria folded her arms.

“Use us for what?”

“Control.”

Catherine turned to the ledger.

“Anna's trust protected assets and voting rights that no single branch of the family could command. Its original structure required two descendants to exercise custodial authority jointly. Neither could transfer the protected rights alone.”

Victoria frowned.

“So whoever controlled both of us could control the trust?”

“Not automatically. But whoever controlled your identities, legal status, and access to the trust could obstruct the intended safeguards. They could manufacture disputes, delay consent, or claim that one descendant was unavailable or incapable.”

Felix's eyes moved across the page.

“The later incapacity filing.”

“An example of the same method,” Catherine said. “The attempt to have both of you declared incapable would have prevented your independent consent.”

Aria looked at her.

“So you decided to make sure nobody could find us.”

“I decided to separate you from the households that would make your identities immediately useful.”

“You decided that without asking us.”

“You were infants.”

“That doesn't make it better.”

“No,” Catherine said. “It doesn't.”

**4:51 p.m.**

Catherine laid the protective allocation document flat.

“The original plan assigned Subject 01 to the Sterling household and Subject 02 to the Vesper household. The two children would have been raised separately, but their biological identities would have remained connected to their respective families.”

Lucian stared at the paper.

“Then you reversed it.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because the threat had already identified the intended arrangement.”

Catherine pointed to the amended instruction.

“The Vesper child was placed with the Sterlings. The Sterling child was held at the Sterling trust estate under temporary custody.”

Richard's name appeared on the estate authorisation.

He stared at it.

“I approved temporary use of the property.”

“You did,” Catherine said. “You were told that a confidential custodial matter required the estate. You were not told the child's identity.”

Richard's jaw tightened.

“And you used my household.”

“I used the estate's administrative separation from your household. I believed it would keep the second child outside the reach of anyone trying to identify the Sterling beneficiary.”

Victoria looked at Richard.

“So you really didn't know I was there.”

Richard shook his head.

“No.”

She turned back to Catherine.

“Why didn't you put me with the Vespers immediately?”

“Because the threat had already identified the Vesper household as a target. Sending you there would have exposed your identity and placed both intended beneficiaries within reach of the same people.”

“And Aria?”

“Aria was placed with the Sterlings because the person searching for the Vesper child would not expect to find her there.”

Aria's eyes narrowed.

“You thought a family would protect me simply because the records said I belonged there.”

“I believed the placement would be temporary. I expected the threat to be neutralised before either child could be returned to her intended household.”

“You expected.”

“Yes.”

“You didn't know.”

“No.”

Aria looked down at the document.

“And when it went wrong?”

Catherine's expression became harder.

“Everything went wrong.”

**4:58 p.m.**

Elias had been responsible for the trust's legal safeguards.

Catherine had authorised the reversal.

Margaret had been instructed to submit the administrative amendment using the legacy authority system.

Edmund Voss had been assigned to protect both children.

The arrangement depended on three separate lines of protection.

None had survived intact.

“Elias discovered that someone was accessing the trust's records through an internal administrative channel,” Catherine explained. “He tried to identify the person. Before he could finish, the original custodial structure was compromised.”

“Elias died,” Octavian said.

“Yes.”

“Was he murdered?”

“I don't know.”

Octavian's face became expressionless.

Catherine continued.

“I was being watched. Edmund's protection network was attacked. Several records disappeared. I was forced to abandon my identity and go underground.”

“You could have contacted us,” Lucian said.

“I could have contacted you and exposed the girls' locations.”

“You could have told us the danger.”

“I did not know whom I could trust.”

Lucian's voice sharpened.

“You trusted Margaret.”

“I trusted her with the filing. Not with every detail.”

Margaret spoke for the first time.

“She gave me a signed instruction and a sealed supplementary order. I submitted the amendment because the legacy authority required an administrative custodian to process it.”

Victoria turned towards her.

“Did you know what it meant?”

“I knew the allocation was being reversed. I knew both children were being separated from their intended households. I did not know who had threatened them, or who was seeking control of the trust.”

“You still did it.”

“Yes.”

Margaret did not look away.

“I believed the order came from someone authorised to protect the trust. I was wrong to accept that without demanding independent verification.”

Thomas Reed stood beside her, holding a narrow envelope.

“I carried the original record to the archive,” he said. “I was told the document must remain separate from the family copies. I wasn't told what was inside.”

Aria looked at him.

“And you never questioned it?”

“I did. I was told the order was signed by the custodian and verified through the legacy authority.”

“Who told you?”

“Elias Vesper.”

Octavian turned sharply.

“Elias?”

Thomas nodded.

“He gave me the key to this room. He said that if the arrangement failed, the original record must eventually be returned to the two descendants.”

Felix looked at the key in Margaret's hand.

“So the key wasn't just for the room.”

“No,” Thomas said. “It was for the evidence inside it.”

**5:07 p.m.**

Victoria remained standing.

“One thing.”

Catherine looked at her.

“Jonathan Vale.”

“He was not part of my original plan.”

“Did you know he took me?”

“I learned about it afterward.”

“How?”

“Margaret Vale's guardianship records were discovered during a separate search. I traced the placement through the old birth registration.”

“You contacted him.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“To confirm that you were alive and safe.”

Victoria's expression twisted.

“And you didn't tell me who I was.”

“I didn't approach you because I believed the people searching for the descendants might still be monitoring the records. I feared that revealing your identity would expose you.”

“So you let me grow up without knowing.”

“Yes.”

“Did you ever consider that I might have wanted to know?”

Catherine's answer came slowly.

“Not enough.”

Victoria looked away.

For once, she had no immediate retort.

Aria spoke instead.

“And me?”

Catherine turned to her.

“You were with the Sterlings. I knew the placement had succeeded in concealing your identity from the people searching for the Vesper descendant.”

“Did you know Richard would reject me?”

“No.”

“Did you know I would be thrown out?”

“No.”

“Did you check whether I was safe?”

Catherine paused.

“I received reports that you were alive and remained in the Sterling household. I did not know the details of your treatment.”

Aria's expression was cold.

“So you watched a file.”

“I watched the only information I could safely obtain.”

“That's not the same as watching a person.”

“No,” Catherine said. “It isn't.”

Helena had been silent until then.

She looked at Catherine with unmistakable pain.

“You knew my daughter was alive. You knew where she was. And you let me believe she was lost.”

Catherine lowered her eyes.

“I thought I was protecting her.”

“You were protecting a plan.”

The words struck harder than a raised voice.

Catherine did not answer.

**5:14 p.m.**

Blackwood examined the supplementary order.

“There is something missing.”

Felix leaned closer.

“The authorisation to restore the original allocation?”

“Precisely. This document says the reversal was temporary. It does not specify who was responsible for declaring the threat neutralised.”

Catherine nodded.

“That decision was meant to be made jointly by the three custodial representatives.”

“Evelyn, Elias, and you,” Octavian said.

“Yes.”

“Evelyn died.”

“Yes.”

“Elias died.”

“Yes.”

“And you disappeared.”

“Yes.”

Blackwood tapped the document.

“Then the arrangement had no functioning authority to end it.”

“Correct.”

Maximilian looked at the record.

“Which means the children remained in their altered placements because nobody could legally reverse the instruction.”

“Not exactly,” Catherine said. “The original authority still permitted a restoration if the threat was independently verified as neutralised. But the verification mechanism was compromised.”

Nathaniel's eyes narrowed.

“By whom?”

“We don't know.”

Rook's name seemed to hang over the table without anyone speaking it.

Blackwood finally broke the silence.

“Northstar Heritage Holdings continued attempting to obtain administrator authority. Someone kept the trust under pressure long after the original protective arrangement should have ended.”

Catherine nodded.

“Gideon Rook benefited from the delay. That does not prove he initiated the threat.”

“He admitted knowing the reversal would happen,” Felix said.

“And denied ordering it,” Catherine replied.

“Do you believe him?”

“No.”

The answer was immediate.

“But disbelief is not evidence.”

Felix looked almost offended by the restraint.

“That is an inconvenient standard.”

“It is also the one that prevents us from accusing the wrong person.”

**5:21 p.m.**

Aria pulled the allocation record towards herself.

She studied the signature.

Then the handwritten note beneath it.

*Temporary. Restore when external threat is neutralised.*

She looked at Catherine.

“Who decided the threat was neutralised?”

“Nobody.”

“Who was supposed to decide?”

“The three custodians.”

“And two were dead.”

“Yes.”

“And you were hiding.”

“Yes.”

Aria put the paper down.

“So you made a decision that affected two people's entire lives, then disappeared before you could undo it.”

Catherine did not dispute it.

“Yes.”

Victoria's voice was quiet.

“You keep saying you were protecting us.”

“I was trying to.”

“You don't get to decide that everything you did was protection just because you were frightened.”

Catherine looked at her.

“You are right.”

Victoria's eyes flashed.

“Stop agreeing with me as if that settles anything.”

“It doesn't.”

“Then what does?”

Catherine had no answer.

For several seconds, the room remained silent.

Then Aria pushed the record away.

“I don't want the trust.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Aria—”

“I don't want its money, its voting rights, its authority, or whatever else Anna left behind. I haven't even decided what being a Vesper means to me. I'm certainly not accepting an inheritance built around decisions other people made before I could speak.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“I agree. Nobody is going to use me as a signature on an agreement.”

Blackwood nodded.

“Neither of you is required to accept custodianship.”

“Good,” Aria said. “Because I would like to finish one conversation in this family without someone producing another historical document.”

Cassian quietly closed the folder in front of him.

Felix looked disappointed.

Maximilian gave him a warning glance.

Felix closed the second folder too.

**5:27 p.m.**

Catherine remained beside the table.

“There is one more thing.”

Aria looked tired.

“Of course there is.”

Catherine pointed to the final page.

“This was attached to the original instruction, but it was never included in the copies sent to the archive.”

Margaret leaned forward.

“I have never seen that page.”

Neither had Thomas.

Felix took it carefully.

At the top was a reference number:

**PROTECTIVE AUTHORITY — PA-03**

Below it was a list of three authorisation requirements.

One was Catherine's signature.

One was Elias's.

The third was a verification mark from the Vesper administrative council.

Felix read the final line aloud.

“*No protective allocation shall be considered valid without the approval of the designated internal authority.*”

Lucian stared at it.

“Who was the designated authority?”

Catherine shook her head.

“That name was recorded separately.”

“Where?”

“In the original authority register.”

Octavian looked towards the shelves.

“Is it here?”

“No. Elias removed it before the arrangement was implemented.”

“Why?”

“Because he believed the register had already been accessed.”

Nathaniel examined the page.

“Then the person who approved the reversal may not be the person who ordered it.”

“Correct,” Catherine said.

“And that approval could have come from inside the Vesper administrative council.”

“Yes.”

Aria looked between them.

“So we're still looking for the person who made this necessary.”

Catherine nodded.

“And the person who helped them.”

Victoria glanced at the portrait of Anna Vesper.

“Two different people?”

“Possibly.”

Thomas Reed stepped forward.

“There is something else Elias told me.”

Everyone turned.

“He said the person who authorised the protective arrangement was not necessarily the person who understood its purpose.”

Felix frowned.

“That distinction matters.”

Thomas nodded.

“He said someone had approved the instruction without knowing what it would eventually be used for.”

Catherine's expression changed.

“Elias never told me that.”

“Perhaps he didn't know who had done it,” Thomas replied.

Octavian looked at the page again.

For the first time since Catherine's confession, his anger had acquired a new direction.

“Then we find the register.”

Aria stood.

“Find it. Investigate it. Do whatever you need to do.”

She looked at Catherine.

“But don't mistake that for forgiveness.”

“I won't.”

Victoria moved beside Aria.

“And don't make another decision about either of us without asking.”

Catherine bowed her head.

“I won't.”

Aria picked up her coat.

“Good. Then we're finished for today.”

She walked towards the door.

Victoria followed.

Neither girl looked back.

Behind them, the original allocation record remained open on Anna Vesper's table.

Three signatures had created the arrangement.

One had authorised the reversal.

And somewhere in the missing authority register was the name of the person who had approved it.

**Someone had signed the order without understanding what it would become.**

Or so Elias had believed.
