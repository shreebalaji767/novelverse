# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 53 — The Receipt in the Locked Drawer**

**Thursday — 8:21 p.m.**

Dorian Hale did not move.

The door handle turned again.

“Who are you?” he called.

“Someone who would prefer not to make this unpleasant.”

Hale looked at the telephone.

The call had ended.

He placed the receiver down carefully and slipped the folded receipt into his jacket.

“I'm not opening the door.”

A pause.

Then the woman spoke again.

“You've already made one mistake by keeping it.”

“I've made several. You'll have to be more specific.”

“I need the original key receipt.”

“You're twenty-two years too late.”

“Not quite.”

Hale moved towards the desk.

His office had two exits. The main door opened onto the corridor. The second led into a records room connected to the building's service passage.

He had never used it.

Tonight seemed an appropriate occasion.

The woman knocked once.

“Mr. Hale, I know you spoke to Aria Vesper.”

Hale stopped.

“How?”

“Because you are not as difficult to find as you imagine.”

He reached for the second door.

It opened.

A man stood on the other side.

Hale froze.

The man wore a dark coat and held a small identification card.

“Mr. Hale?”

Hale recognised him.

Thomas Reed.

The former courier.

“What are you doing here?”

“Trying to prevent you from making a very poor decision.”

The woman outside knocked again.

Thomas glanced towards the main door.

“Is she alone?”

“I don't know.”

“Then we're leaving.”

**8:26 p.m.**

The woman entered the office three minutes later.

The room was empty.

She checked the desk, opened the drawer, and examined the locked cabinet.

Nothing.

Her eyes settled on the telephone.

She picked it up.

The last outgoing call was still displayed.

She read the number.

Then she smiled without amusement.

“Thomas.”

She took out her phone and sent a short message.

*The receipt is no longer there.*

A reply arrived almost immediately.

*Then stop looking for it.*

She read the message twice.

The second message followed.

*You have already attracted enough attention.*

Her expression changed.

She deleted both messages and left the office.

**8:41 p.m.**

Thomas brought Hale to a small private office at the edge of the city.

Hale was furious.

“You could have mentioned you were coming.”

“You would have refused.”

“Correct.”

“Then mentioning it would have been pointless.”

Hale sat down.

“You always were an irritating courier.”

“I've improved.”

Thomas placed a glass of water on the desk.

Hale ignored it.

“You said you were trying to prevent a poor decision. What decision?”

“Calling the person who warned you twenty-two years ago.”

Hale stared at him.

“You know about that?”

“I know someone threatened you after you questioned the approval.”

“Who?”

“I don't know.”

“Then how do you know?”

Thomas opened a small envelope.

Inside was a photocopy of an old dispatch receipt.

Hale recognised the reference number.

**PA-03-771.**

“This was attached to the original record,” Thomas said. “I kept a copy because the dispatch instructions were unusual.”

Hale took it.

The receipt listed the documents sent to Ellison House.

There were four items.

The protective allocation.

The administrative approval.

The security key return record.

And a sealed envelope addressed to the executive custodian.

Hale looked up.

“I never saw that envelope.”

“I know.”

“Who received it?”

“The dispatch record doesn't say.”

“Then why keep this?”

“Because the envelope was marked for personal delivery. Someone changed the instruction after it left the archive.”

Hale examined the handwriting.

A short note had been added in the margin.

*Deliver only after approval is confirmed.*

He looked at Thomas.

“Who wrote this?”

“I don't know.”

Hale leaned back.

“Everyone in this affair knows nothing.”

Thomas considered that.

“Not everyone.”

**9:03 p.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Felix had found another problem.

He had searched the council vault's access logs and identified the three occasions on which the executive custodian authority had been used after the council dissolved.

The first was twenty-two years ago.

The second was nineteen years ago.

The third was three weeks ago.

“Why three weeks ago?” Maximilian asked.

“To access the appointment register.”

“Could it have been a routine archive inspection?”

“No. The vault was sealed. The access was authorised through an obsolete executive credential.”

Nathaniel examined the dates.

“The second access occurred around the time Catherine disappeared.”

Catherine nodded.

“That was when the protective arrangement collapsed.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Did you access the vault?”

“No.”

“Did Elias?”

“I don't know.”

Blackwood studied the third entry.

“Someone is still maintaining the credential.”

Lucian looked at Felix.

“Can you trace the physical terminal?”

“Not from this record. The old system only recorded the authority number.”

“Can you disable it?”

Felix hesitated.

“Not without knowing whether it controls any remaining trust safeguards.”

“Then don't touch it,” Blackwood said.

Felix looked dissatisfied.

“I wasn't going to.”

Maximilian glanced at the keyboard.

“You were.”

“I was considering it.”

“Don't.”

Felix closed the access panel.

**9:17 p.m.**

Aria received a message.

Unknown number.

*Dorian Hale is safe. He has the original receipt.*

She read it.

Then another message arrived.

*Do not trust anyone who asks you to collect it alone.*

Aria stared at the screen.

She called Marcus.

He answered immediately.

“Miss Aria?”

“Did you send me a message?”

“No.”

“Did anyone from the Vespers?”

“Not to my knowledge.”

“Someone says Hale is safe.”

Marcus paused.

“Where did the message come from?”

“Unknown number.”

“I'll check.”

“No. First tell me whether anyone has contacted Hale.”

“Mr. Vesper's security office has attempted to reach him. There has been no response.”

“Then he's not safe.”

Marcus's voice sharpened.

“Why?”

“Because whoever sent that message wants me to believe he is.”

She ended the call.

A minute later, her phone rang again.

This time it was Victoria.

“Did you receive something strange?”

Aria sat down.

“Yes.”

“Someone sent me a message saying the Vespers had recovered the original authority register.”

Aria frowned.

“Did they?”

“Apparently not.”

“Then we're receiving different lies.”

Victoria was silent.

Then she said, “I hate this.”

“So do I.”

“They keep moving us around like we're not people.”

Aria looked at the old family photograph on her shelf.

“Not anymore.”

**9:31 p.m.**

At the private office, Hale finally unfolded the receipt.

The receiving signature was written in blue ink.

He had remembered the surname.

He had forgotten the first name.

Now he could see it clearly.

**G. Rook.**

Thomas watched him.

“You know him?”

Hale nodded slowly.

“Gideon Rook.”

“Was he the executive custodian?”

“No.”

“Then why did he receive your key?”

Hale turned the paper over.

A second line had been written on the back.

*Received on behalf of the executive custodian.*

The signature belonged to Rook.

Hale looked up.

“He wasn't the custodian.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“Then who was?”

Hale pointed to the first line.

The receiving officer had written the name of the person for whom Rook collected the key.

**Elias Vesper.**

Thomas stared at it.

“That doesn't make sense.”

“No,” Hale said. “It doesn't.”

“Elias was the legal custodian.”

“He was also acting executive custodian for six weeks after the previous appointment ended.”

Thomas sat back.

“So Elias received the key.”

“Through Rook.”

“Why would Rook collect it?”

“Because Elias was abroad.”

Hale looked at the date.

Then at the signature.

His face changed.

“Wait.”

Thomas leaned closer.

“What?”

“The key was returned to Elias six weeks before the allocation reversal.”

“And?”

“And the approval was entered using my credentials.”

Hale tapped the receipt.

“Someone had the key after it was returned.”

Thomas understood.

“Someone could have used the terminal.”

“Exactly.”

**9:44 p.m.**

Hale took out his phone.

“I need to speak to Aria.”

Thomas stopped him.

“Not yet.”

“Why?”

“Because we don't know who sent those messages.”

“I can explain the receipt.”

“You can explain it tomorrow, with the Vespers present.”

Hale shook his head.

“You don't understand. If Rook collected the key on behalf of Elias, then he may have had access to the terminal.”

“Or he may have simply delivered it.”

“Then why was his signature on the receipt?”

“Because he was the courier.”

Hale stared at him.

Thomas continued.

“Do not turn a delivery into a conspiracy without evidence.”

Hale looked annoyed.

“You sound like a lawyer.”

“I've had the misfortune of working with several.”

Thomas took the receipt and placed it inside the envelope.

“We need the original register. It will tell us who held the executive authority when the key was used.”

Hale nodded.

“Then we go to Ellison House.”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because the woman who came to your office knew you had spoken to Aria. Someone is watching you. Someone also knows about the receipt.”

Hale looked towards the door.

“Then where do we go?”

Thomas placed the envelope inside his coat.

“To the person who knows where Elias kept the register.”

**10:02 p.m.**

At Greyhaven House, Catherine received a call.

She listened without interrupting.

Then she looked towards Octavian.

“Thomas has found the receipt.”

Octavian stood.

“Where is he?”

“He says he is taking Hale to someone who knows where Elias kept the original register.”

“Who?”

Catherine hesitated.

“He didn't say.”

Nathaniel took the phone.

“Ask him one question.”

Catherine waited.

“Does the person know about the protective allocation?”

She repeated it.

Thomas answered.

“Yes.”

“Does the person know who approved it?”

A pause.

“Possibly.”

Nathaniel exchanged a glance with Blackwood.

“Who is it?”

Catherine asked.

Thomas's answer was quiet.

“Someone who was present when Elias made the decision.”

Catherine went completely still.

“That's impossible.”

“Why?”

“Because the only people present were Elias, Evelyn, and me.”

Thomas said nothing.

Catherine lowered the phone.

Octavian watched her.

“Who?”

She looked at him.

“Evelyn.”

“My mother?”

Catherine nodded.

“Evelyn Vesper died before the reversal.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Then Thomas is mistaken.”

Catherine looked down at the old ledger.

“No.”

She turned the page.

“Evelyn was present when the original trust was created. But she wasn't present when the reversal was approved.”

Octavian stared at her.

“Then who was?”

Catherine looked towards the portrait of Anna Vesper.

“Someone who had been dead for years.”

**10:09 p.m.**

Thomas and Hale arrived at an old residential building.

The lights on the upper floor were still on.

Thomas knocked.

A woman opened the door.

She was elderly, neatly dressed, and holding a walking stick.

Hale recognised her immediately.

He stepped back.

“Mrs. Ellison?”

Margaret Ellison's face appeared in the doorway.

She looked at him.

Then at Thomas.

“You brought him here?”

Thomas nodded.

Margaret stepped aside.

“Come in.”

Hale entered slowly.

“Why are you here?”

Margaret closed the door.

“Because I never trusted the official record.”

She walked towards a small table and placed a thin document folder on it.

Inside was a photograph.

Three people stood together outside Ellison House.

Elias Vesper.

Gideon Rook.

And a third person.

Hale stared.

He knew the face.

He had seen it on the appointment register twenty-two years earlier.

But the name attached to it had always been marked as deceased.

Margaret placed one finger on the photograph.

“The executive custodian.”

Hale swallowed.

“That's impossible.”

Margaret looked at him.

“Apparently, a great many impossible things happened around those children.”

**The photograph was dated six months after the official death of its third subject.**
