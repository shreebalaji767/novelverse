# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 21 — The File With Her Name on It**

**Tuesday — 7:05 a.m.**

The man at Asterion had spent the entire night looking at the same folder.

It was thin.

Only six pages.

Yet someone had considered it important enough to keep under a separate classification, inside a locked cabinet, behind records that were supposed to have been destroyed.

The cover carried one name.

**ARIA STERLING**

He opened it again.

The first page was a personnel placement summary.

The second was an authorisation sheet.

The third contained a list of dates.

The remaining pages were copies of correspondence.

He read the last page.

Then he closed the folder.

He needed to make one decision.

Whether to follow the instruction he'd received twelve years earlier—or protect himself.

He picked up his telephone.

**8:30 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria stood beside the meeting table.

Across from her sat a client who looked deeply suspicious of spreadsheets.

“This is the reconciliation,” she explained. “The bank payments match the original invoices. The discrepancy comes from duplicated entries in your second statement.”

The client frowned.

“So you're saying my accounts are wrong?”

“I'm saying the statement you submitted contains repeated entries.”

“Who prepared it?”

“I don't know.”

Peter, seated beside her, coughed into his hand.

Aria continued.

“If you remove the duplicates, the figures agree with the bank records.”

The client looked at the spreadsheet.

Then at Peter.

“Is she certain?”

Peter nodded.

“I checked her reconciliation. It's correct.”

The client leaned back.

“Well. That's inconvenient.”

Aria blinked.

“Would you prefer it to be incorrect?”

Peter looked down at his notes.

The client didn't answer.

**9:50 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received an urgent call.

The caller was the former Asterion records officer.

“I have information concerning your investigation.”

Marcus straightened.

“What information?”

“A file connected to the old Northbridge arrangement.”

“Which file?”

“The placement register.”

Marcus glanced at Damian.

“Does it mention Daniel Mercer?”

“Yes.”

“Miriam Shaw?”

“Yes.”

“Who else?”

There was a pause.

“Aria Sterling.”

Marcus went completely still.

“Repeat that.”

“The file is labelled Aria Sterling.”

“Do not alter or remove anything. Our legal representative will contact you immediately.”

“I understand.”

Marcus ended the call.

Damian stood.

“What did he say?”

Marcus looked at him.

“He found a file carrying Aria's name.”

**10:05 a.m.**

Octavian was informed.

He listened without interrupting.

When Marcus finished, Octavian asked one question.

“Does the file concern her birth?”

“We don't know yet.”

“Then we will not assume.”

He turned to Damian.

“Secure the original through the proper legal process. Preserve the chain of custody.”

Damian nodded.

“And Aria?”

“Tell her.”

**10:40 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria was reviewing another client's records when her phone rang.

“Hello?”

“Miss Aria. It's Marcus.”

“Hi. Is something wrong?”

“We have located an additional document connected to the hospital investigation.”

“What does it say?”

“We haven't examined the original yet. But the file is labelled with your former name.”

Aria stopped typing.

“My name?”

“Aria Sterling.”

She was quiet for a moment.

“Why would a company have a file with my name?”

“We don't know yet.”

“Does it contain my personal information?”

“We believe it may.”

Aria looked at the papers on her desk.

“Can I see it?”

“Once the document has been secured and reviewed, yes.”

“Please don't keep things from me.”

“We won't.”

She ended the call.

Peter looked over.

“Everything all right?”

“Family investigation.”

He nodded politely.

“Do you need to leave?”

Aria glanced at the clock.

“No. I have work.”

Peter returned to his desk.

He didn't ask anything else.

She appreciated that.

**12:15 p.m. — Asterion Records Facility**

The Vesper legal representative arrived with a preservation order and an independent records examiner.

The former records officer, Leonard Price, led them to the archive.

“I haven't touched the file,” he said.

The examiner photographed the cabinet, the lock, and the folder before removing it.

The file was sealed in a transparent evidence sleeve.

Marcus joined by secure video.

“Can you read the cover?”

The examiner held it up.

**ARIA STERLING — SP-09 PLACEMENT RECORD**

Marcus looked at the label.

“Open it.”

The examiner photographed each page before turning it.

The first page was a placement summary.

The second was a personnel transfer authorisation.

The third was a payment schedule.

The fourth contained a handwritten instruction.

The examiner paused.

“There's a reference to a hospital.”

Marcus leaned closer to the screen.

“Read it.”

The examiner adjusted the page.

“'Subject's original identity must remain separate from receiving-family documentation until final placement confirmation.'”

Nobody spoke.

Then Damian asked, “What does 'subject' refer to?”

The examiner turned to the next page.

There was a code.

**SUBJECT 02 — FEMALE INFANT**

Marcus's expression hardened.

The examiner continued.

“'Receiving family: Sterling. Placement status: provisional.'”

Damian stood abruptly.

“Provisional?”

The examiner checked the following line.

“'Final confirmation pending second transfer.'”

Marcus looked at the document.

The second transfer.

The same transfer Richard Sterling had been told was never completed.

**12:45 p.m.**

The examiner continued reading.

The fifth page contained an instruction dated three days after the births.

*Do not amend the receiving-family record until both placements have been confirmed.*

Below it was a signature.

**M. VOSS**

The final page was a handwritten note.

*The first placement remains active. The second placement is suspended. Do not notify either family.*

Marcus read it twice.

“Is there any explanation of what the second placement involved?”

“No,” the examiner replied.

“Any identity?”

“No.”

“Any destination?”

“No.”

Marcus looked at Damian.

They had found something important.

But not the answer.

The document established that Aria's placement with the Sterlings had been recorded as provisional.

It did not establish who authorised the arrangement, why it had been made, or what the second placement was supposed to accomplish.

And it raised another question.

If the first placement was provisional, why had Aria remained with the Sterlings for twenty years?

**2:20 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria was having lunch when Richard entered.

He looked as though he had aged several years in a single morning.

“What happened?” she asked.

“The Vespers found another document.”

“What document?”

“A placement record.”

Victoria put down her fork.

“Whose?”

Richard hesitated.

“Aria's.”

She stared at him.

“Why would there be a placement record for her?”

“I don't know.”

“Father, stop saying that.”

Richard looked at her.

“What would you like me to say?”

“The truth.”

“I have told you what I know.”

“No. You've told me what you remember.”

Richard's expression hardened.

“Victoria.”

She leaned forward.

“Was Aria deliberately placed with us?”

Richard didn't answer.

Victoria stood.

“Was she?”

“I don't know.”

Victoria laughed bitterly.

“So the girl you threw out of this house might have been placed here by someone who had already decided she belonged somewhere else?”

Richard said nothing.

Victoria picked up her glass.

For once, she had no clever reply.

**4:00 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Octavian read the placement record.

Lucian stood beside the desk.

Maximilian, Damian, Cassian and Felix were also present.

Felix had enlarged the scanned pages on a wall display.

“There's a technical detail,” he said.

Octavian looked at him.

“The file references two placement codes. Subject 02 is Aria. Subject 01 has a separate record number.”

“Can you identify Subject 01?”

“Not yet. The number links to a different archive system.”

Cassian folded his arms.

“So the second child?”

“Possibly,” Felix said. “But we shouldn't assume the numbering reflects birth order or family assignment.”

Maximilian examined the page.

“The first placement was marked active. The second was suspended.”

Lucian's voice was quiet.

“Someone was deciding where two newborn children would live.”

Isolde, who had been listening silently, looked at the signature.

“Mara Voss.”

Octavian nodded.

“The name appears on every important document.”

Damian asked, “Do we know whether Mara Voss was a real person?”

“No,” Marcus replied. “We have a name, a signature and several records. We still have no verified identity.”

Felix pointed to the bottom of the final page.

“There's one more thing.”

He enlarged a small handwritten reference.

**RETURN TO ORIGIN — NOT AUTHORISED**

Maximilian frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“I don't know.”

Octavian studied it.

“Find out.”

**5:35 p.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria finished her work and packed her bag.

Peter approached her desk.

“How did the client meeting go after I left?”

“Better than expected.”

“Did they accept the reconciliation?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

He handed her a small envelope.

“Your revised employment confirmation. We updated your responsibilities after your probation review.”

Aria opened it.

Her role had been formally extended.

She read the letter twice.

“Thank you.”

“You earned it.”

She smiled.

“That's nice to hear.”

Peter returned to his office.

Aria put the letter in her bag.

Her phone vibrated.

Marcus.

*The document has been secured. It contains a record of your placement with the Sterling family. We would like to explain it in person tomorrow.*

She read the message.

Then replied:

*All right. Please send me the details.*

She stood, switched off her desk lamp, and left.

**8:10 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a photograph.

It showed the placement record.

She stared at the page.

Then she looked at the person standing across from her.

“They found SP-09.”

“Yes.”

“Did they find the other file?”

“No.”

She exhaled.

“Good.”

The associate hesitated.

“Why was Aria's placement marked provisional?”

The woman looked at him.

“Because it was.”

“But the Sterlings raised her for twenty years.”

“That was the first arrangement.”

“And the second?”

The woman closed the folder.

“The second arrangement was supposed to correct the first.”

“Then why wasn't it completed?”

Her expression changed.

“Because someone intervened.”

“Who?”

The woman walked to the cabinet and unlocked it.

Inside were several folders.

She selected one marked **SUBJECT 01**.

“Find out who stopped the second transfer,” she said.

The associate looked at the folder.

“And if the Vespers find it first?”

The woman closed the cabinet.

“Then we will finally learn whether they understand what they have been protecting.”

**11:25 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix was still working.

He had connected the Subject 01 reference to an older Northbridge index.

The result was incomplete.

One field remained readable.

**SUBJECT 01 — RECEIVING FAMILY: NOT ASSIGNED**

Below it was a status entry.

**IDENTITY VERIFICATION: WITHHELD**

Felix leaned closer.

There was a second notation, added several months later.

**PLACEMENT CLOSED — AUTHORITY: M. VOSS**

He called Marcus.

Marcus answered on the second ring.

“Tell me.”

Felix looked at the screen.

“Subject 01 wasn't assigned to a receiving family.”

“Was the placement cancelled?”

“No. The record says it was closed.”

“By whom?”

Felix enlarged the signature.

“Mara Voss.”

Marcus was silent.

Felix continued.

“And there's a date.”

“What date?”

Felix read it aloud.

“Three months after Aria was placed with the Sterlings.”

He looked at the screen again.

“Someone closed the second placement long after the first one had already been completed.”

And for the first time, the investigation had established something beyond a failed transfer.

**The second child had never been assigned to a family at all.**
