# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 34 — The Girl Who Was Called Lily**

**Thursday — 8:12 a.m.**

Victoria had received three calls from Richard.

She had ignored all three.

The address from the unknown woman led to a modest private office on the outskirts of the city. No company sign. No reception desk. No visible security.

She had spent the previous evening considering whether to go.

Curiosity had eventually defeated caution.

She arrived at 8:12.

A woman was waiting inside.

She was in her late forties, dressed in a dark coat, with neatly arranged hair and an expression that gave away nothing.

Victoria stopped at the doorway.

“You contacted me.”

“Yes.”

“Who are you?”

“Mara Voss.”

Victoria's stomach tightened.

She knew the name.

She had seen it in Richard's documents, the payment records, and the instructions concerning the two children.

“You're the woman who arranged everything.”

“I arranged certain transfers.”

“Where is Lily Shaw?”

Mara studied her.

“You're looking at her.”

Victoria stared.

For several seconds, she couldn't speak.

Then she laughed once.

It wasn't amusement.

“That's impossible.”

“Is it?”

“Lily Shaw was a girl who lived with Margaret Vale. I am Victoria Sterling.”

“You were Lily Shaw.”

Victoria stepped closer.

“Prove it.”

Mara opened a folder.

Inside were copies of old documents.

The first was a school admission record.

The second was a guardianship document.

The third was a photograph of a little girl standing beside Jonathan Vale.

Victoria recognised herself.

Not because the photograph was particularly clear.

Because she recognised the scar above the left eyebrow.

She touched her own eyebrow.

“Where did you get these?”

“From the records I retained.”

“Why was I called Lily?”

“Because Jonathan Vale brought you to Margaret under that name.”

“Why?”

“Because he believed the people who arranged your original transfer could not be trusted to keep you safe.”

Victoria looked at the photograph again.

“Why did he take me?”

Mara's expression remained composed.

“He was instructed to deliver you to a temporary location. Instead, he took you somewhere else.”

“Who instructed him?”

“I did not.”

“Then who?”

Mara said nothing.

Victoria slammed the folder onto the desk.

“You brought me here to tell me half a story?”

“I brought you here because you deserve to know that your childhood identity was deliberately concealed.”

“I'm a Sterling.”

“Yes.”

“Richard and Eleanor are my biological parents.”

“Yes.”

“Then what exactly are you trying to tell me?”

Mara looked directly at her.

“That you were the child recorded as Infant B.”

Victoria went pale.

**8:35 a.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Felix had just received the authentication report for the collection receipt.

He read it twice.

Then he called Maximilian.

“The signature is not Victoria's.”

Maximilian turned from his desk.

“Are you certain?”

“The handwriting comparison is inconclusive on its own, but the authorisation record identifies the actual collector. It wasn't Victoria.”

“Who was it?”

“An agent acting under a power of attorney issued by the legal office. The printed name was the account holder's name, not the collector's.”

Maximilian exhaled.

“So Victoria never collected the document.”

“Correct. Someone used her name as the beneficiary reference.”

Marcus entered.

“Have you located Victoria?”

“No,” Maximilian replied.

Marcus immediately called her.

The phone rang.

No answer.

He called again.

Still nothing.

“Contact Richard,” he said.

**8:42 a.m.**

Victoria stood opposite Mara.

“Infant B was the child taken from the Sterling estate.”

“Yes.”

“And Jonathan took me.”

“Yes.”

“Then why did the Vespers think the child was missing?”

“Because they were told you were supposed to be delivered to them.”

Victoria frowned.

“Why would they want me?”

Mara opened another document.

“Your original birth registration.”

She placed it on the desk.

Victoria stared at the paper.

The name printed on it was unfamiliar to her.

The date matched her birthday.

The hospital matched the one mentioned in the Vesper investigation.

And the mother's name was Eleanor Sterling.

Victoria slowly looked up.

“Why is this being kept from me?”

“Because the registration was never supposed to be seen by either family.”

“Why?”

“Because the arrangement depended on two children remaining separate.”

Victoria looked at the document again.

“Two children.”

“Yes.”

“Aria and me.”

Mara nodded.

Victoria's expression changed.

“Then Aria really is—”

“Aria Vesper is the biological granddaughter of Octavian Vesper. Her identity has been independently confirmed.”

Victoria looked away.

The confirmation hurt more than she expected.

For months, she had believed the Vespers were withholding something from her.

Now she understood that the missing child had been her.

“Why did Jonathan take me to Margaret?”

“He believed you were safer there.”

“Did he know who I was?”

“He knew you were the child he had been instructed to move. He did not know the complete arrangement.”

“Did Margaret know?”

“No.”

“Did she know I was a Sterling?”

“No.”

Victoria looked at the photograph again.

“Then why did the Sterlings find me?”

Mara closed the folder.

“Because your identity was eventually verified through the records that survived your original transfer.”

“When?”

Mara didn't answer.

Victoria's voice sharpened.

“When?”

“After you left Margaret's care.”

“Who found me?”

“I don't know who first located you.”

“You expect me to believe that?”

“I expect you to understand that I was not involved in every decision made after Jonathan took you.”

Victoria stared at her.

“You arranged the transfers.”

“I arranged the records.”

“You paid the people who moved children.”

“I paid intermediaries for administrative work.”

“You knew what was happening.”

Mara's expression hardened.

“I knew enough to understand that the arrangement was dangerous. I did not know everything.”

Victoria gave a bitter laugh.

“That's a convenient distinction.”

**9:05 a.m.**

Richard finally answered Marcus's call.

“Where is Victoria?”

“We're trying to locate her. She hasn't answered.”

Richard stood.

“She may have contacted Mara Voss.”

Marcus went still.

“Why do you think that?”

“She asked me about Lily Shaw yesterday. She knows the name now.”

“Do you have any idea where Mara might meet her?”

“No.”

Marcus immediately informed Octavian.

Damian was already arranging a search through lawful location records and known contacts.

Aria entered the conference room.

“What happened?”

“Victoria may have met Mara Voss.”

Aria stopped.

“Alone?”

“We don't know.”

Aria picked up her phone.

“Has anyone contacted Margaret?”

“Not yet.”

“Do that. If Victoria has gone to see Mara, Margaret might know where she was planning to go.”

Marcus nodded.

Aria looked at Octavian.

“We need to make sure she gets home safely.”

Octavian's expression was grave.

“Agreed.”

Cassian glanced at Damian.

“Find her before she decides to negotiate with the woman who organised twenty years of administrative disasters.”

Damian was already leaving.

**9:18 a.m.**

Victoria had reached the final page of the file.

It contained a copy of the temporary custody instruction.

The document confirmed that Infant B had been transferred from the hospital to the Sterling estate.

Six weeks later, a new instruction had been issued.

The child was to be moved under Route C.

The receiving authority was not named.

Jonathan Vale had collected the child.

A handwritten note appeared beneath the entry.

*Child to remain under protected identity until further instruction.*

Victoria read it again.

“Who wrote this?”

“Mara Voss.”

“Why?”

“To keep your identity concealed.”

“From whom?”

“Both families.”

Victoria looked up.

“Richard knew I was at the estate.”

“He knew an infant was there. He didn't know it was you.”

“And Eleanor?”

“She didn't know there was a child there at all.”

Victoria felt suddenly cold.

Her entire childhood had been built on a chain of decisions made by people who had never bothered to tell her what they were doing.

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I was waiting for the original registration to be recovered.”

“You had it.”

Mara's eyes shifted toward the document.

“I had an extract.”

“Where is the original?”

“Not with me.”

Victoria stood.

“I'm finished.”

Mara did not stop her.

But as Victoria reached the door, she spoke again.

“There is one more thing.”

Victoria turned.

“What?”

“The person who arranged your original transfer was not the person who decided to keep you from the Sterlings.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“Who made that decision?”

Mara looked at her.

“Jonathan Vale.”

**9:34 a.m.**

Victoria stepped outside.

Her phone was vibrating.

Richard.

She answered.

“Where are you?”

“Somewhere I should have gone years ago.”

“Victoria, listen to me—”

“You knew there was a child at the estate.”

“I knew an infant was there.”

“You knew the records were hidden.”

“I knew some records were confidential.”

“You never asked whether it was me.”

Richard fell silent.

Victoria's voice broke slightly.

“You never even wondered?”

“I didn't know who the child was.”

“Neither did I.”

She ended the call.

A black car was parked across the road.

A man stepped out.

“Miss Sterling?”

Victoria turned.

“Who are you?”

“I'm here to take you home.”

“Who sent you?”

“Mr. Vesper.”

She stared at him.

“I don't need an escort.”

“I'm aware.”

“Then leave.”

The man hesitated.

“Mr. Vesper asked me to make sure you weren't followed.”

Victoria looked back toward the building.

The office door was already closed.

She turned away.

“Fine. But I'm not getting into a limousine.”

The man looked at the vehicle.

“It's a regular sedan.”

“Good.”

**Vesper Estate — 11:20 a.m.**

Victoria arrived with a document folder.

Richard was already there.

So were Octavian, Lucian, and Marcus.

Aria stood near the window.

Victoria looked at her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Victoria placed the folder on the table.

“Mara Voss confirmed that I was Infant B.”

Felix immediately opened the documents.

“Did she provide the original registration?”

“An extract.”

“Did she identify who collected the original?”

“No.”

“Did she explain why Jonathan took you?”

“She said he believed I wasn't safe at the Sterling estate.”

Richard looked at her.

“Did she say anything about the person who ordered the original transfer?”

“She said she didn't know everything.”

Aria folded her arms.

“Do you believe her?”

Victoria looked at her.

“I don't know.”

That was perhaps the most honest answer she had given in weeks.

Octavian studied the documents.

“Then we will verify every statement independently.”

Victoria nodded.

She looked at Aria again.

“You knew I was the child they were searching for?”

“No.”

“You never suspected?”

“I suspected there was another child. I didn't know it was you.”

Victoria looked down.

For once, she had no accusation ready.

Aria continued.

“I'm glad you're safe.”

Victoria looked at her sharply.

“That's all?”

“What else should I say?”

Victoria didn't answer.

**12:05 p.m.**

Felix placed the original registration extract under the document scanner.

The hospital reference matched Infant B.

The date matched the custody records.

The mother's name matched Eleanor Sterling.

The father's name matched Richard Sterling.

There was no doubt.

Victoria was the child who had been taken from the Sterling estate by Jonathan Vale.

She was also the child who had lived under the name Lily Shaw.

Felix looked at Octavian.

“The records are consistent.”

Richard sat down.

Eleanor had not yet been informed.

Neither had Margaret.

Victoria stared at the document.

“Twenty years.”

No one interrupted.

“Twenty years, and nobody told me my own name.”

Aria quietly pulled out a chair.

“Sit down.”

Victoria looked at her.

“Don't tell me what to do.”

“Then stand. You look like you're about to fall over.”

Victoria stared at her for a moment.

Then she sat.

Cassian, unusually quiet, moved the untouched glass of water closer.

Victoria took it.

**Unknown Location — 2:40 p.m.**

Mara Voss received a message.

**Associate:** Victoria has spoken with the Vespers.

She read it.

**Associate:** They have confirmed Infant B was Victoria Sterling.

Mara placed her phone down.

Another message arrived.

**Associate:** Should we proceed with the next step?

She looked at the locked drawer.

Inside it was the original birth registration.

But beneath it lay another document.

An old instruction bearing a different signature.

The person who had issued it had never been identified by the Vespers.

Mara opened the document.

At the bottom was a line written in black ink:

*Neither child is to be returned until the first placement has been accepted.*

She folded it carefully.

Then she sent one reply.

**Mara:** No. The families have only discovered what happened to the children.

She looked toward the window.

“They still don't know who ordered it.”
