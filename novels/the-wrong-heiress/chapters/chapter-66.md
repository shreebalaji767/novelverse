# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 66 — The Declaration That Required Two Signatures**

**Tuesday — 11:24 a.m.**

“Close that record.”

Octavian's instruction made Felix look up.

“Grandfather?”

“Do not open anything else under Anna-02 until we understand the access conditions.”

Felix stopped.

“I haven't opened the declaration.”

“Good.”

Aria looked at the screen.

“Is there a reason we're treating a reference number like a bomb?”

“Because the last time someone opened an old custodial record,” Cassian said, “we discovered that half the people involved had been officially dead for twenty years.”

Maximilian looked at him.

“That's not a reason to be careless.”

“I'm not being careless. I'm developing a healthy respect for paperwork.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“Can we get back to the record?”

Felix enlarged the surviving entry.

**ANNA-02**

**The second descendant shall receive the original custodian's final declaration.**

**To be completed only in the presence of both descendants.**

Aria read it again.

“Why does it say the second descendant?”

Margaret answered.

“Because the original trust arrangement distinguished between the first beneficiary record and the second receiving record.”

“Which means somebody decided that one of us should receive the declaration.”

“Not necessarily,” Catherine said. “The wording may refer to the order in which the records were created, not the order in which you were born.”

Victoria frowned.

“Then how do we know which one of us is the second descendant?”

“We don't,” Felix replied.

Aria looked at him.

“Can you find out?”

“I'm trying.”

**11:46 a.m. — Vesper Estate, Archive Office**

Felix accessed the original trust catalogue.

The first record was straightforward.

**ANNA-01 — Original Beneficiary Declaration**

The second was restricted.

**ANNA-02 — Receiving Descendant Declaration**

Both records had been created on the same date.

But their registration numbers were different.

“There's a gap,” Felix said.

Alistair leaned over his shoulder.

“Between the two registrations?”

“Yes. ANNA-01 was registered at 9:14 a.m. ANNA-02 at 4:42 p.m.”

“Seven hours?”

“Twenty-eight minutes.”

Alistair examined the catalogue.

“That is unusual. The declarations should have been registered together.”

“Unless the second declaration was added later.”

Margaret shook her head.

“The original trust register indicates both were created by Anna Vesper.”

Felix opened the historical audit.

The second record had been amended twice.

The first amendment was made by Evelyn Vesper.

The second by Elias Vesper.

Octavian stared at the screen.

“My mother amended it?”

“According to the record.”

“And Elias?”

“His amendment was entered several years later.”

Catherine stepped closer.

“What did he change?”

Felix opened the surviving amendment description.

**Receiving descendant shall not be identified by household, family branch, or placement designation. Identity must be independently verified in the presence of both descendants.**

Aria looked at Victoria.

“So Elias removed the possibility of identifying us through the families that raised us.”

Catherine nodded slowly.

“He knew someone could manipulate the household records.”

“Or he discovered it had already happened,” Lucian said.

Felix opened the next field.

There was no explanation.

Only a reference to a sealed attachment.

**ATTACHMENT E-17.**

**12:19 p.m.**

Alistair searched the physical archive catalogue.

He found the attachment's storage location.

“It's in Room 17.”

Thomas looked at the key on the table.

“The room we just opened?”

“Yes.”

“Then why didn't we find it?”

Alistair checked the inventory.

“Because the attachment was never catalogued as a document.”

Felix frowned.

“What was it catalogued as?”

Alistair read the entry.

“A specimen envelope.”

Catherine looked up sharply.

“Medical?”

“Possibly.”

Felix opened the original inventory image.

The description was brief.

**E-17 — Identity verification reference material. Two sealed samples. Custodian's instruction attached.**

Victoria stared at the screen.

“Samples?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Aria looked at Catherine.

“Does this have anything to do with our birth records?”

“I don't know.”

“Could it be medical evidence?”

“Yes.”

“Could it be something else?”

“Yes.”

Aria nodded.

“Then let's find it.”

**12:43 p.m. — Original Vesper Family Archive**

Room 17 was searched again.

This time, the preservation team examined the desk, cabinets and storage compartments.

The specimen envelope was found inside a narrow compartment beneath the desk.

It was sealed with an old wax impression.

The mark showed a simple tree surrounded by seven small circles.

Alistair recognised it.

“Anna Vesper's personal seal.”

Octavian looked at the envelope.

“My mother used that seal only for documents she considered private.”

Felix photographed it.

The envelope contained two small sealed packets and a folded instruction.

The packets were labelled:

**REFERENCE A**

**REFERENCE B**

Neither had a name.

The instruction was written in Anna's hand.

Margaret read it aloud.

“'These references are not to be used to determine which descendant is more entitled to the trust. They exist only to verify that the two persons presenting themselves are the descendants for whom this arrangement was created.'”

Victoria looked at the packets.

“So they're identification samples?”

“Possibly,” Felix said. “But we shouldn't assume they're biological samples until they're examined.”

Catherine unfolded the rest of the instruction.

Her expression changed.

“What?” Octavian asked.

She handed him the paper.

A second paragraph had been written beneath the first.

**The custodian shall not disclose the contents of either reference to one descendant without the presence and consent of the other. Neither reference shall be used to establish authority over the other person.**

Aria looked at the words.

“That part seems important.”

“It is,” Catherine said.

“Then nobody opens them without both of us agreeing.”

Victoria nodded.

“Agreed.”

Octavian looked at the two girls.

“Of course.”

Aria gave him a brief look.

“I wanted to hear you say it.”

**1:28 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

The two packets were placed in separate evidence containers.

Felix examined the seals.

“Both appear original. There is no obvious indication of tampering.”

“Can you verify that?” Maximilian asked.

“Not without a forensic examination.”

“Arrange one.”

Victoria looked at the containers.

“What exactly are we supposed to learn from them?”

Felix considered his answer.

“Possibly whether the original records correspond to the two descendants. The instruction suggests they were intended as independent identity references.”

“Then they could establish that we're the two children.”

“Potentially.”

Aria looked at him.

“Would that change anything?”

Felix paused.

“No.”

“Would it make one of us more entitled to the trust?”

“No.”

“Would it make us responsible for accepting the custodianship?”

“No.”

“Then why are we doing this?”

Octavian answered.

“Because someone created a system to protect your identities and choices. Someone else interfered with it. We need to know what happened.”

Aria considered that.

“That's a better reason.”

Victoria looked towards the packets.

“And if they reveal something we don't want to know?”

Catherine spoke gently.

“Then you will decide what to do with that information.”

Victoria nodded.

She did not look reassured.

But she did not object.

**2:16 p.m.**

The forensic examiner arrived with a small team.

After documenting the containers, she examined the seals and packaging.

The results were preliminary.

“Both packets are consistent with the original archive materials,” she explained. “The seals show no visible signs of reopening. We cannot establish their complete history from physical inspection alone.”

Felix asked, “Can you identify what's inside?”

“We can inspect the contents, but the instruction requires both descendants' consent.”

Aria looked at Victoria.

Victoria looked back.

“I'm willing,” she said.

“So am I.”

The examiner placed the two packets on the table.

Both girls signed the consent record.

The first packet contained a small glass slide with a dried biological sample and a reference number.

The second contained another slide.

Neither carried a personal name.

The examiner checked the labels.

“These appear to be preserved biological reference samples.”

Felix looked at the numbers.

“Can they be compared with current samples?”

“Yes. But the results will depend on the preservation quality and the amount of usable material.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“Then test them.”

Aria nodded.

“Together.”

The examiner collected the packets.

Before leaving, she placed the instruction back in its evidence sleeve.

“There's another item.”

She pointed to the inside of the second packet.

A thin strip of paper had been folded beneath the slide holder.

Felix carefully removed it.

It contained a handwritten notation.

**A-01 — First reference: biological verification only.**

**B-02 — Second reference: biological verification only.**

And beneath them:

**No succession inference permitted.**

Octavian read the final line.

His expression grew solemn.

Anna Vesper had anticipated the possibility that someone might turn proof of identity into a claim of ownership.

She had explicitly forbidden it.

**4:03 p.m. — Vesper Estate, Private Study**

The family had barely begun discussing the samples when Felix received another message.

He opened it.

His expression changed.

“There's a result from the archive's access history.”

“What result?” Lucian asked.

“Room 17 was accessed before we opened it yesterday.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“By whom?”

Felix displayed the record.

**Access date: Eleven years ago.**

**Access authority: E.P.12**

**Entry purpose: Custodial inventory inspection.**

Margaret looked at the screen.

“Evelyn Price.”

Felix nodded.

“She entered Room 17 eleven years ago.”

“Why didn't the old access register show it?” Damian asked.

“Because the room's access system was disconnected. This is a manual authority record.”

Thomas looked at Margaret.

“She had access to the cabinet at Greyhaven House and the original archive.”

“Yes,” Margaret replied.

“And she left a statement saying she retained the original register.”

Felix opened the attachment.

“There's more.”

The entry contained a handwritten remark.

**E-17 inspected. Both references present. No substitution detected.**

Catherine stared at the words.

“Evelyn Price knew about the samples.”

“And confirmed they were still there eleven years ago,” Felix said.

Aria looked at the date.

“Then why didn't she tell anyone?”

Margaret examined the entry.

“Perhaps she wasn't permitted to.”

“Or perhaps she didn't trust the people who were asking.”

Thomas pointed to the access authority.

“Who authorised her entry?”

Felix enlarged the signature.

The name was clear.

**MARA VOSS.**

Catherine slowly sat down.

“That is not possible.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Because Mara was dead?”

“Because this was eleven years ago. Her credentials had been suspended long before then.”

Felix opened the approval metadata.

The authorisation had not been issued through the hospital.

It had been submitted through a private legal office.

The office's name appeared at the bottom.

**VOSS & ELLISON — LEGACY LEGAL SERVICES.**

Margaret looked at the screen.

“I've never heard of that firm.”

Catherine's expression tightened.

“I have.”

Everyone turned.

“It was Mara's private practice. She closed it before she disappeared.”

“Who reopened it?” Lucian asked.

Catherine shook her head.

“I don't know.”

Felix checked the registration record.

The firm had been reactivated eleven years ago.

Its sole authorised representative was listed as:

**ELENA VOSS.**

**4:21 p.m.**

Catherine stood.

“My daughter.”

Octavian looked at the record.

“Did Elena tell you she had reopened Mara's practice?”

“No.”

“Did she have access to Mara's legal credentials?”

“I don't know.”

Margaret examined the registration date.

“Eleven years ago, Elena would have been eleven.”

Catherine went still.

“An eleven-year-old child could not have independently reactivated a legal practice.”

“Then someone used her name,” Thomas said.

Felix opened the company registration file.

The representative's signature was electronically verified.

But the verification certificate had been issued by a private legal authority.

Its original application was missing.

Aria looked at Catherine.

“Do you know where Elena was living then?”

Catherine hesitated.

“With me.”

“Was she attending school?”

“Yes.”

“Could someone have taken her documents?”

Catherine's face tightened.

“I don't know.”

Octavian watched her carefully.

“Catherine, did you ever give Elena access to Mara's records?”

“No.”

“Did Mara?”

Catherine did not answer immediately.

Then she said:

“Mara loved Elena. She was her godmother. She may have left something with her.”

Felix looked at the registration record again.

“There's a correspondence address.”

He enlarged it.

**VOSS PRIVATE OFFICE — GREYHAVEN DISTRICT.**

Thomas recognised it.

“That's the address on the old message Elena sent us.”

Catherine stared at the screen.

“She arranged the meetings.”

Aria looked at the address.

“Then perhaps she knows why Mara's name remained active.”

Catherine picked up her phone.

She called Elena.

The call rang.

Once.

Twice.

Then a recorded message answered.

Catherine ended the call.

A new message arrived almost immediately.

It was from Elena.

**Mother, I know you've found Room 17.**

Catherine read it aloud.

A second message appeared.

**Do not open the original declaration without me.**

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Why?”

Catherine looked at the screen.

A final message arrived.

**Because Mara left something in it that was never meant for the Vespers.**

**4:29 p.m.**

Nobody spoke.

Aria read the message again.

“Something that wasn't meant for the Vespers?”

Victoria looked at Catherine.

“What does that mean?”

Catherine's expression was troubled.

“I don't know.”

Octavian looked at the phone.

“Ask her to explain.”

Catherine typed a response.

**Where are you?**

The reply came quickly.

**At the old legal office.**

Thomas looked at Felix.

“Can we locate it?”

Felix opened the company address.

“Yes.”

Damian stood.

“I'll arrange a team.”

Aria shook her head.

“Not yet.”

Damian looked at her.

“She's alone.”

“We don't know that.”

Catherine's phone rang.

Elena was calling.

She answered.

“Where are you?”

Elena's voice came through, quiet and strained.

“Mother, listen to me. Don't let them open the declaration.”

“Why?”

“Because the declaration contains two instructions. One is for the descendants.”

“And the other?”

A pause.

“The other is for the person who arranged their separation.”

Catherine's face drained of colour.

Octavian stepped closer.

“Who arranged it?”

Elena did not answer.

Instead, she said:

“Grandfather, you need to understand something.”

Octavian took the phone.

“What?”

“The original declaration was never missing.”

His grip tightened.

“Then where is it?”

Elena's answer was almost a whisper.

“Someone has had it for twenty-two years.”

The call ended.

On the table, the two sealed reference samples remained untouched.

Aria looked at Victoria.

Then at Octavian.

“Who had it?”

Nobody answered.

Because the only person who could have held the original declaration from the beginning was the person who had created the trust.

**Anna Vesper.**
