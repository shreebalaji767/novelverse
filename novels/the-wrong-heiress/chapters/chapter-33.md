# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 33 — The Registration Nobody Wanted Found**

**Wednesday — 8:35 a.m.**

Victoria had not slept.

She had spent the night searching for every document connected to the name Lily Shaw.

The results had been infuriating.

There were school records, scholarship records, guardianship records, and a photograph of a little girl standing beside Jonathan Vale.

There was no birth certificate.

No original registration.

No explanation for the name change.

And no confirmation that Lily Shaw and Victoria Sterling were the same person.

Richard had refused to answer her questions.

That was becoming a habit.

At breakfast, Eleanor noticed her daughter's expression.

“Are you ill?”

“No.”

“You look exhausted.”

“I'm investigating something.”

Eleanor put down her cup.

“Again?”

Victoria turned her laptop around.

“Do you recognise this girl?”

Eleanor studied the photograph.

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then she looked at Victoria.

“Where did you find this?”

“School records. Lily Shaw.”

Eleanor's fingers tightened around her napkin.

“Richard needs to see this.”

“He already knows.”

“Then you should stop.”

Victoria laughed without humour.

“Everyone keeps telling me to stop. Nobody tells me why.”

Eleanor stood.

“Because some things are better left alone.”

Victoria looked up.

“That's what people say when they know exactly what happened.”

**Vesper Estate — 10:00 a.m.**

Peter Shaw arrived carrying a metal document case.

He was in his late sixties, neatly dressed, and visibly uncomfortable with the number of security personnel waiting outside the conference room.

Marcus greeted him.

“Thank you for coming.”

“I hope this won't take long.”

“We'll do our best.”

Peter placed the case on the table.

“My sister kept this register after leaving the records facility. She told me it was important, but she never explained why.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Did she tell you who collected the original birth registration?”

“She remembered the authorisation. She didn't know the woman personally.”

“Did she describe her?”

Peter opened the case.

“Tall. Dark hair. Always wore gloves. She was apparently very particular about signatures.”

The room became still.

Marcus exchanged a glance with Maximilian.

It matched the descriptions of Mara Voss.

Peter removed a thick ledger.

“Eleanor wrote down every collection because the facility had a habit of losing paperwork.”

Cassian looked at Felix.

“Finally, someone with sensible administrative habits.”

Felix ignored him.

He examined the register.

“Here.”

A collection entry had been marked with the reference MV-04.

The date matched the Sterling estate's release record.

The recipient field was blank.

But the signature column contained a handwritten notation:

*Collected under authority of M. Voss. Original transferred to private custody.*

Beneath it was another entry.

*Secondary instruction: destroy duplicate index after verification.*

Felix looked up.

“That's important.”

Peter frowned.

“Why?”

“Because the original was deliberately separated from the duplicate record. Someone wanted one copy to survive while making the archive impossible to reconstruct.”

Peter looked disturbed.

“My sister said she didn't like the instruction.”

“Did she follow it?” Maximilian asked.

“She kept the register.”

That answer mattered.

Octavian closed the ledger.

“Mr. Shaw, you have helped us considerably.”

Peter nodded.

“I hope you find the truth.”

**11:25 a.m.**

The collection register provided another lead.

A handwritten reference directed the records facility to a private legal office.

The office had been dissolved, but its former managing partner was still alive.

His name was **Edward Hargreaves**.

Felix located the old office's correspondence archive.

A transfer note from twenty years earlier had survived.

It stated:

*Original registration received for restricted custody. Access permitted only upon written instruction from authorised representative.*

The authorised representative's name was missing.

But the final line contained a location.

**Ashford Lane — Charitable Custody Office.**

Aria looked at the screen.

“That's the same place Jonathan took Lily.”

“Yes,” Felix replied.

“So the birth registration went there?”

“Not necessarily. The legal office may have been instructed to retain it on behalf of the charity. We need Hargreaves to confirm.”

Damian folded his arms.

“Can we contact him today?”

Marcus nodded.

“His office has agreed to receive our legal representative this afternoon.”

Octavian looked toward Richard, who had joined the meeting remotely.

“Mr. Sterling, did you know the registration was connected to Ashford Lane?”

Richard shook his head.

“No.”

“Did Mara Voss mention the charity?”

“No. She told me the original registration would remain in restricted custody.”

Aria leaned back.

“Everyone seems to have been told just enough to do their part and not enough to understand what they were doing.”

Richard looked at her.

“That may be true.”

“It's a very efficient way to create a disaster.”

Nobody disagreed.

**Sterling Mansion — 12:40 p.m.**

Victoria found Richard in his study.

He was reviewing documents.

She closed the door.

“Tell me the truth.”

Richard looked up.

“About what?”

“Lily Shaw.”

He slowly removed his glasses.

“What about her?”

“Was I Lily?”

Richard didn't answer.

Victoria placed the school photograph on his desk.

“Look at it.”

He did.

His expression changed.

Victoria watched him carefully.

“Was I?”

“I don't know.”

“You're lying.”

“I am not.”

“Then why did you tell me we were looking for the wrong name?”

Richard looked exhausted.

“Because the original registration may contain a name that was used before you became Victoria.”

“May?”

“I have not seen it.”

“Then why are you so certain?”

“Because the records connect Lily Shaw to the child taken from the Sterling estate.”

Victoria went still.

“Jonathan Vale took me?”

Richard didn't answer immediately.

“I don't know.”

“You said the child wasn't identified.”

“It wasn't.”

“Then how do you know it was me?”

“I don't.”

Victoria's expression turned cold.

“Wonderful. Another answer that explains nothing.”

She picked up the photograph.

“I'm going to find Margaret Vale.”

“Victoria—”

“Don't.”

She left.

Richard remained seated.

He knew the danger was no longer confined to old documents.

Victoria was beginning to understand that her own history had been hidden from her.

And he still couldn't tell her what the truth was.

**3:15 p.m.**

Edward Hargreaves remembered the file.

He did not remember the woman who had authorised it.

His legal office had received the original birth registration under a restricted custody agreement. The document was not to be altered, copied, or released without written authorisation.

The file had been transferred to the Ashford Lane charitable office.

“Why?” Marcus asked.

Hargreaves adjusted his spectacles.

“I was told the child connected to the registration required protected identity arrangements.”

“Which child?”

“I wasn't told.”

“Was a name provided?”

“Yes.”

He opened an old index.

The entry was handwritten.

**Lily Shaw.**

Aria looked at the page.

“Was that the name on the original registration?”

“No,” Hargreaves replied. “It was the custody name. The original registration was sealed separately.”

“Who authorised the transfer?”

Hargreaves checked the accompanying letter.

“Mara Voss.”

“Did you meet her?”

“Once. She was very clear that the child was not to be contacted through the original registration details.”

Maximilian leaned forward.

“Was the registration ever returned?”

Hargreaves examined the final page.

“Yes.”

The room fell silent.

“When?”

“Six years ago.”

“Who collected it?”

Hargreaves turned the page.

A collection receipt had been attached.

The signature was difficult to read.

But the printed name beneath it was clear.

**Victoria Sterling.**

Nobody spoke.

Aria looked at the name.

Then at Marcus.

“That's not possible.”

Hargreaves checked the date.

“It was collected under this name.”

Felix immediately examined the receipt.

“The signature needs authentication. The printed name alone proves nothing.”

“Could someone have used her identity?” Damian asked.

“Yes,” Felix said. “Or the name could have been entered by the office. We need the original authorisation.”

Hargreaves looked through the file again.

“There is a copy.”

He removed a folded sheet.

At the bottom was a signature.

**V. Sterling.**

**4:05 p.m.**

Victoria received a call from an unknown number.

She answered.

“Who is this?”

A woman's voice replied.

“You have been asking about Lily Shaw.”

Victoria straightened.

“Who are you?”

“Someone who knows why you were given that name.”

“Then tell me.”

“Not over the telephone.”

“Where?”

“I will send you an address.”

Victoria hesitated.

“Why should I trust you?”

A quiet laugh came through the line.

“You shouldn't.”

The call ended.

A message arrived seconds later.

An address.

And one sentence:

*Bring no one.*

Victoria stared at it.

Then she forwarded the message to no one.

For once, she decided she would investigate without telling her family.

**Vesper Estate — 6:30 p.m.**

Marcus placed the collection receipt before Octavian.

“The signature is not yet authenticated. But the document suggests that someone collected Lily Shaw's original registration six years ago using Victoria Sterling's name.”

Richard, present in person, stared at the paper.

“Six years ago?”

“Yes.”

Richard shook his head.

“Victoria was sixteen.”

Aria looked at the receipt.

“That's the same age Lily left the charity.”

Felix nodded.

“Which means the timing is significant. It doesn't establish that Victoria personally collected the document.”

Maximilian studied the signature.

“Could the original registration identify the person who authorised the collection?”

“Possibly. The legal office retained the authorisation reference, but the original was removed with the registration.”

Cassian looked at Richard.

“Did you ever see Victoria sign documents under that name?”

“No.”

“Did she know she was called Lily?”

“I don't know.”

Aria stood.

“Then don't decide what happened before we find out.”

Octavian nodded.

“Agreed.”

He looked at Marcus.

“Find Victoria. Inform her that a document bearing her name has surfaced. Do not conceal it from her.”

Marcus nodded.

“I'll contact her.”

But his phone was already ringing.

Margaret Vale.

He answered.

“Mrs. Vale?”

Her voice was strained.

“Victoria Sterling just called me.”

Marcus stood.

“What did she want?”

“She asked whether Lily had ever been called Victoria.”

The room went silent.

Margaret continued.

“I told her I couldn't answer that question.”

“Did she say why she was asking?”

“Yes.”

Margaret hesitated.

“She said she had received a message from someone claiming to know the truth about Lily.”

Marcus looked at Octavian.

“Did she tell you where she was going?”

“No. But she sounded frightened.”

**Unknown Location — 9:45 p.m.**

The woman sat at her desk.

A message appeared.

**Associate:** Victoria has received the address.

She looked at the old registration extract.

Then at the photograph of Lily.

For years, she had kept the two identities apart.

Now Victoria was approaching the one place where the truth could no longer be controlled.

She picked up her phone.

“Has she arrived?”

A voice answered.

“Not yet.”

“Make sure she comes alone.”

The woman put the registration back into its sleeve.

Then she closed the drawer.

Tomorrow, Victoria Sterling would learn why someone had once called her Lily Shaw.

And the Vespers would discover that the original birth registration had not disappeared.

It had been waiting for her.
