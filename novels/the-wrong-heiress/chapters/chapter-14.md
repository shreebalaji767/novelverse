# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 14 — The Name That Wasn't on the List**

**Wednesday — 8:20 a.m.**

Aria had discovered that the accounting profession contained a surprising number of ways to write the same number incorrectly.

There were misplaced decimal points, duplicated invoices, missing references, inconsistent dates and people who apparently believed that rounding was a substitute for mathematics.

She was beginning to understand why Peter Collins drank so much tea.

“Aria?”

She looked up.

Nina stood beside her desk holding a file.

“Can you check these expense claims? The client says the totals don't match the supporting receipts.”

“Did they send all the receipts?”

Nina stared at her.

“Of course not.”

“Then we're making progress.”

Nina laughed and placed the file down.

“Peter wants them checked before tomorrow.”

“Fine.”

Aria opened the folder.

Her ordinary Wednesday had officially begun.

**9:15 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had been awake since five.

This was not unusual.

What was unusual was that he had asked Marcus to come to the technology division personally.

Marcus arrived carrying coffee.

“Tell me this is important.”

“It is.”

“Good.”

Felix turned his monitor around.

The name **Mara Voss** appeared on the screen.

“I checked the municipal registration, postal forwarding instruction and archived commercial records. The name was used by Greybridge's authorised representative.”

“Was?”

“The identity doesn't have a matching residential registration, tax record or employment history.”

“Could it be a false identity?”

“Possibly. But there's another problem.”

Felix opened a second document.

“The forwarding instruction was submitted using a reference number that belongs to a legitimate corporate mail-handling service. The service confirms that the reference was active, but its old customer records were destroyed under its retention policy.”

Marcus examined the document.

“So we have a real service, a valid reference and no surviving customer identity.”

“Exactly.”

“Can you recover anything?”

“I can ask the service's former records manager. She retired six years ago.”

Marcus looked at him.

“You've already contacted her?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because I wanted to establish whether she was the correct person first.”

Marcus took a sip of coffee.

“I'm proud of you.”

Felix frowned.

“Why?”

“Because you didn't immediately send someone to her house.”

“I was considering an email.”

“An excellent improvement.”

Felix looked back at the screen.

“There is one more thing.”

He opened the original forwarding instruction.

The destination address had been written by hand.

It was not the demolished building.

It was a post office box registered to a company called **Northstar Document Custody**.

Marcus set down his coffee.

“Northstar?”

“Yes.”

“Is that connected to Northbridge?”

“Not directly. But Northstar's registration address was a serviced office. Its directors were nominee officers, and the company was dissolved less than a year after Greybridge.”

Marcus looked at the screen for several seconds.

“Add it to the investigation.”

**11:30 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria entered Richard's study without knocking.

He looked up sharply.

“Have you forgotten how doors work?”

“No. I simply stopped believing you would answer if I knocked.”

“Victoria.”

She placed the payment summary on his desk.

“Crestline Administrative Partners.”

Richard's eyes moved to the page.

For a fraction of a second, his expression changed.

Victoria noticed.

“You recognise it.”

“Where did you get that?”

“From the family records.”

“I told you not to take documents from the archive.”

“That's not an answer.”

Richard stood.

“Put it back.”

“Why did our family pay them?”

“I don't know.”

“You looked at the name and knew exactly what it was.”

“I said I don't know why the payment was made.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“Then you know the company.”

Richard said nothing.

“Was it connected to the hospital?”

“Enough.”

“Did someone pay them to arrange the switch?”

Richard's face hardened.

“You have no proof that there was a switch arranged by anyone.”

“Two children were exchanged. Records were transferred. Money was paid. And you keep telling me not to ask questions.”

“Because you don't understand the consequences.”

“Then explain them!”

Richard's voice dropped.

“You are not going to involve yourself in matters that could damage this family.”

Victoria stared at him.

For a moment, she looked less angry than offended.

“You keep talking about protecting the family. What about protecting me?”

Richard didn't answer.

Victoria picked up the payment summary.

“Fine. I'll find out myself.”

She left.

Richard remained standing.

After a moment, he reached for his telephone.

He opened an old contact.

His thumb hovered over the call button.

Then he locked the screen.

**1:06 p.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria had found the problem.

Three expense claims had been entered twice, but the duplicates weren't exact copies. The dates differed by one day, and the reference numbers had been manually altered.

She compared the receipts against the bank statement.

The same payment had been recorded under two separate expense categories.

She marked both entries and brought the file to Peter.

“There's a duplicate payment entry.”

Peter examined the pages.

“Same transaction?”

“Yes. Same amount, same merchant, same bank reference. Different descriptions.”

“Why?”

“I don't know. It could be a clerical error or someone trying to make the expenses appear separate.”

Peter nodded.

“Don't assume intent. Record the discrepancy and ask the client for an explanation.”

Aria returned to her desk.

She liked that instruction.

Numbers were useful.

People were more complicated.

**2:40 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received a call from the former records manager.

Her name was **Helen Ward**.

She remembered the Northstar Document Custody account.

More importantly, she remembered why it had been unusual.

“Northstar didn't collect mail through ordinary forwarding,” Helen explained over the telephone. “It had a special collection arrangement. A representative would come to the office, present a collection card and receive sealed envelopes.”

“Was the representative always the same person?”

“No. There were two authorised collectors.”

“Do you remember their names?”

“Not their full names. One was registered as M. Voss. The other was registered as J. Vale.”

Marcus wrote both down.

“Do you remember either person?”

“I remember the woman.”

“Can you describe her?”

“Tall. Dark hair. Very composed. She wore gloves, even indoors.”

“Anything else?”

Helen paused.

“She never spoke to the receptionist. She would leave the collection card on the counter and wait.”

“Did you ever see the other collector?”

“Once. A man. He collected a package after she had already left.”

“Do you remember his name?”

“J. Vale. I remember because he complained that the envelope was too large.”

Marcus thanked her and ended the call.

He immediately forwarded the new information to Felix and Octavian.

Two authorised collectors.

Two identities.

One of them might have been the woman photographed outside the hospital.

But there was still no proof.

**4:18 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian read the new report in silence.

Isolde sat across from him.

“Two collectors,” she said.

“Yes.”

“And one of them may be the woman.”

“May be.”

She looked at him.

“You are being careful.”

“We have spent years searching for the truth. I will not let impatience turn a possibility into a fact.”

Isolde nodded.

“What about Aria?”

“Marcus will inform her when the findings are verified.”

“She should know what we know.”

“She will.”

Octavian closed the folder.

“Nothing important will be hidden from her.”

**6:12 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria was eating dinner when her phone rang.

Marcus.

“Hello?”

“Good evening, Miss Aria. We have another development.”

“Go ahead.”

“The company connected to Greybridge used a mail-handling service. Its records manager remembers two authorised collectors.”

Aria put down her fork.

“Do they have names?”

“Only initials and surnames. M. Voss and J. Vale.”

“Do they know who those people were?”

“Not yet. The former records manager remembers a woman who collected sealed envelopes. She also remembers a man collecting a package.”

“Are they sure these were the people connected to the hospital records?”

“No. We have established that they were authorised to collect mail for Northstar Document Custody. We haven't established what they collected.”

“Good. Keep those things separate.”

Marcus made a note.

“Of course.”

Aria thought for a moment.

“Have you checked whether the courier's delivery went to Northstar or Greybridge?”

“The surviving records confirm the Greybridge address in the courier's notebook. The forwarding instruction links Greybridge to Northstar, but we haven't found a document proving the package was forwarded.”

“So someone could have collected it directly.”

“Yes.”

“Or someone else could have.”

“Yes.”

Aria picked up her fork again.

“Keep looking.”

“I will.”

**9:03 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman stood beside a desk.

An associate handed her a printed report.

“They have identified the two collection registrations.”

She read the names.

“M. Voss and J. Vale.”

“They have also located the former records manager.”

The woman placed the report down.

“Did she remember anything else?”

“She remembered the collection procedure.”

“Of course she did.”

“Should we contact her?”

“No.”

The associate hesitated.

“What about the second collector?”

“Find him.”

“We don't know whether J. Vale is his real name.”

“Then find out.”

She opened the locked cabinet.

Inside, the two newborn photographs remained in their original envelopes.

She removed one.

The infant's hospital bracelet was visible.

She stared at it for a long moment.

Then she returned the photograph to its envelope.

“Someone has started looking at the collection records,” she said.

The associate nodded.

“And if they find the second collector?”

The woman closed the cabinet.

“Then we will know whether he remembers what he carried.”

**10:17 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix sent Marcus a final message before leaving.

*I found a possible connection between J. Vale and an old courier registration. It is not verified yet. The registration belongs to a person who used the same mail-handling service several years earlier.*

Marcus read it.

Then another message arrived.

*The name is Jonathan Vale.*

Marcus stared at the screen.

He opened the attachment.

A scanned courier registration form appeared.

The photograph was faded.

The address was old.

But the signature was clear.

**Jonathan Vale.**

Below it was a handwritten note.

*Former delivery contractor — Northbridge Data Services.*

Marcus slowly closed the file.

The second collector had worked for the same company that employed Thomas Reed.

And he might know what happened to the package after it left Greybridge.

For the first time, the investigation had a living name that could be followed.

Unfortunately, the name came with a problem.

Jonathan Vale had died eleven years ago.
