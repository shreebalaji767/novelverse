# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 30 — The Child in the Photograph**

**Monday — 7:48 a.m.**

Felix had a problem.

Actually, he had three.

The first was that the charitable organisation on Ashford Lane had closed eleven years ago.

The second was that its former director, a man named Arthur Bell, had apparently retired without leaving a forwarding address.

The third was that someone had sent Aria a message claiming Jonathan Vale had taken the wrong child.

Felix disliked all three.

He especially disliked the third.

“It's deliberately misleading,” he announced.

Cassian looked up from his tablet.

“You haven't established that.”

“It's obviously intended to confuse us.”

“That's not the same as being false.”

Felix frowned.

“Why do you always do that?”

“Do what?”

“Make everything more complicated.”

Cassian smiled.

“It's a professional skill.”

Maximilian entered the research room.

“Any progress?”

Felix turned towards him.

“I found the charity's original guardian register.”

Maximilian stopped.

“Where?”

“An archive maintained by the organisation's former legal trustees. They retained the register after the charity closed.”

“Does it identify MV-04?”

“Yes.”

“Who?”

Felix enlarged the document.

The guardian's name was not written.

Instead, the register contained a reference to another file.

**GUARDIAN MV-04 — PERSONAL IDENTITY SEALED.**

Maximilian stared at it.

“That's not helpful.”

“There's more.”

Felix opened the next page.

A handwritten entry appeared.

*Guardian assumed responsibility for the child on the third day following arrival.*

Below it:

*Child's identity to remain restricted until the guardian's written release.*

Cassian leaned closer.

“Who signed?”

Felix enlarged the signature.

**M. VOSS.**

Maximilian looked at him.

“So Mara Voss was the guardian?”

“Not necessarily,” Felix replied. “She authorised the restriction. The register doesn't establish that she personally cared for the child.”

Cassian nodded.

“Good distinction.”

Felix looked offended.

“I know how to distinguish things.”

“Of course.”

“Stop saying it like that.”

**8:32 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Aria arrived shortly after breakfast.

Marcus had already prepared the documents.

She read the latest guardian register.

“So Mara Voss restricted the child's identity.”

“Yes,” Marcus replied.

“But we still don't know who actually took care of the child.”

“Correct.”

“And we don't know whether this was the child originally intended for the Vespers.”

“Correct.”

Aria put the document down.

“Then we shouldn't call the charity's child the Vesper child.”

Marcus nodded.

“Agreed.”

Octavian watched her.

“What would you call the child?”

“Infant B until we establish an identity.”

A faint smile appeared on Isolde's face.

“Practical.”

Aria shrugged.

“Names are useful when they're correct.”

Felix turned his laptop towards her.

“There's another issue.”

He displayed the photograph from Jonathan's wooden box.

The woman standing beside Jonathan was visible.

Aria studied it.

“Is that Mara?”

“We can't confirm it,” Felix said.

“Can you identify the building?”

“Yes. It was the charity's former residence.”

Aria pointed at the cardboard box Jonathan was carrying.

“What's inside?”

Felix zoomed in.

“Not clear.”

“Can you compare it with the delivery records?”

“There's no corresponding inventory.”

“Then don't guess.”

Felix sighed.

“I wasn't guessing.”

Cassian glanced at the screen.

“You were about to.”

Felix closed the image.

“I dislike this family.”

“That's unfortunate,” Cassian replied. “We're rather fond of you.”

**9:17 a.m. — Former Ashford Lane Residence**

The building was smaller than the photographs suggested.

Its front windows had been replaced, and the original entrance had been converted into a reception area for a private medical practice.

The Vesper legal representative met the current property manager.

After reviewing the old charitable records and the relevant authorisation, the manager allowed the investigators to inspect the archive room.

Damian accompanied them.

The room contained several filing cabinets.

Most held ordinary administrative documents.

One cabinet contained the charity's confidential custody files.

The investigators found the relevant folder.

**MV-04 — TEMPORARY PROTECTION.**

Damian opened it.

Inside was a photograph.

The same child from the unknown woman's locked drawer.

This time, the child's face was visible.

A little girl, perhaps three years old, stood beside a woman in a garden.

The woman was turned away from the camera.

The girl wore a pale dress and held a small stuffed rabbit.

Damian looked at the photograph.

“There's no identity.”

The investigator examined the reverse.

A date had been written there.

Three years after the hospital handover.

Below it:

*Annual review — guardian present.*

Damian turned the page.

The review document contained a single sentence.

*Child remains under guardian's protection. No family contact authorised.*

He read it again.

“No family contact.”

The investigator nodded.

“That's what it says.”

Damian looked through the remaining papers.

There was no school registration.

No medical history.

No birth certificate.

No ordinary identity documentation.

The child had been protected from being found.

But the records did not establish why.

**10:04 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix received the photograph.

He immediately compared its date with the charity's financial records.

“That's interesting.”

Maximilian looked over his shoulder.

“What?”

“The anonymous trust continued making payments for two years after the charity's closure.”

“Who received them?”

“The guardian's maintenance account.”

“MV-04?”

“Yes.”

“Can you trace it?”

Felix opened the account reference.

“It was maintained through Crestline Administrative Partners.”

Cassian sighed.

“Of course it was.”

Marcus looked at the transaction history.

“Were the payments regular?”

“Monthly. For two years.”

“Then?”

“Stopped.”

“Why?”

Felix opened the final entry.

*Maintenance arrangement terminated following guardian withdrawal.*

Maximilian looked at the screen.

“Guardian withdrawal?”

“Yes.”

“Was there a date?”

“Eleven years ago.”

The same year Jonathan Vale died.

The room went quiet.

Cassian slowly folded his arms.

“That's not a coincidence we should ignore.”

**11:26 a.m. — Margaret Vale's Home**

Margaret was reading her brother's letter when Marcus called.

“Ms. Vale, we have found a photograph of the child at the charitable residence.”

“Did you find out who the child was?”

“Not yet.”

She was silent.

“Mr. Vale?”

“Yes?”

“I remembered something.”

Marcus straightened.

“What?”

“My brother visited me shortly before he died. He asked whether I would look after someone if he ever needed me to.”

“Someone?”

“A child.”

Marcus stopped writing.

“Did he identify the child?”

“No. He said he couldn't tell me anything. But he asked whether I would be willing to provide a home if necessary.”

“What did you say?”

“I told him yes.”

“Did he give you any documents?”

“No.”

“Did he mention the child's name?”

Margaret hesitated.

“He said the child had another name.”

Marcus frowned.

“What did he mean?”

“He said the name on the hospital papers wasn't the name the child would grow up with.”

Marcus looked towards the conference room.

“Did he tell you what name?”

“No.”

“Did you ever meet the child?”

Margaret's voice softened.

“No. He died before he could ask me again.”

Marcus thanked her and ended the call.

He immediately contacted Octavian.

**12:08 p.m. — Vesper Tower, Conference Room**

The family gathered around the table.

Marcus explained Margaret's statement.

Aria looked at the photograph.

“So Jonathan intended to give the child a different identity.”

“That's one possibility,” Marcus said.

“Or he had already done it,” Cassian added.

Felix shook his head.

“There's no identity record.”

“Then we need to find out how the child was registered,” Maximilian said.

Octavian looked at Marcus.

“Locate Arthur Bell.”

“The former director?”

“Yes. He may know who assumed guardianship.”

Marcus nodded.

“I'm already working on it.”

Lucian examined the charity records.

“Could the child have been adopted?”

“There's no adoption record in the archive,” Felix replied.

“Could the guardian have registered the child privately?”

“Possibly. But we need the identity documents.”

Aria looked at the photograph again.

“There's something else.”

Everyone turned towards her.

“The child was photographed three years after the handover. That means someone was still paying for her care.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“But the payments stopped when the guardian withdrew. So either the child was moved somewhere else, or someone took responsibility without using the same account.”

Cassian smiled slightly.

“Exactly.”

Aria looked at Marcus.

“Then don't just search for the child under Infant B or Subject 01. Search for the people who suddenly started paying for a child's expenses around that time.”

Felix stared at her.

Then he began typing.

**1:42 p.m. — Former Ashford Lane Residence**

Damian was examining the final pages of the custody folder.

One document had been folded and placed inside the back cover.

He carefully unfolded it.

It was a handwritten instruction.

*The guardian is to retain custody until the child can be transferred without exposing the original placement.*

Below it was a second sentence.

*If the original family requests the child, no transfer is to take place without independent verification.*

Damian looked at the investigator.

“Who wrote this?”

The investigator examined the handwriting.

“It resembles the other M. Voss annotations, but we need formal comparison.”

Damian read the final line.

*The child must never be returned to the household that first accepted the wrong placement.*

He stopped.

The investigator looked at him.

“Is that significant?”

Damian folded the paper carefully.

“Yes.”

He immediately called Marcus.

**2:03 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus listened to Damian's report.

Then he repeated the sentence aloud.

“The child must never be returned to the household that first accepted the wrong placement.”

Aria looked up.

“Which household?”

“We don't know,” Marcus replied.

“Could it mean the Sterlings?”

“Possibly.”

“Could it mean the Vespers?”

“Possibly.”

Cassian leaned forward.

“Or it could refer to a temporary household that has nothing to do with either family.”

Maximilian looked at the document.

“The wording suggests the original placement was considered wrong by whoever wrote the instruction.”

Lucian's expression darkened.

“Then someone knew the placement was wrong from the beginning.”

Octavian looked at the paper.

“And someone believed the child needed protection from being returned.”

Helena looked at Aria.

“Could the child have been Victoria?”

Aria didn't answer immediately.

The photograph showed a little girl.

Victoria was now twenty.

The timing was possible.

But the records did not establish identity.

“Maybe,” Aria said.

“Or it could have been someone else.”

Felix turned towards her.

“There were only two girls involved in the original switch.”

Aria looked at him.

“Then we need to prove which girl this was.”

**3:34 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria was in her room when her phone rang.

Unknown number.

She answered.

“Who is this?”

“You have been asking questions about the second child.”

Victoria sat down.

“Who are you?”

“Someone who knows the answer.”

“Then tell me.”

“You were not the child taken from the estate.”

Victoria froze.

“What?”

“The child Jonathan Vale removed was not you.”

“How do you know?”

“Because I know what happened.”

Victoria's voice sharpened.

“Who was it?”

The caller did not answer.

“Was it Aria?”

A pause.

“No.”

Victoria stood.

“Then who?”

“You should stop investigating.”

“I'm not stopping until I know.”

“You don't understand what you're asking.”

“Then explain it!”

The call ended.

Victoria stared at her phone.

For several seconds, she did not move.

Then she opened her messages and sent one to Richard.

*We need to talk. Now.*

**4:12 p.m. — Sterling Mansion, Study**

Richard read Victoria's message.

When she entered, he closed the notebook.

“Who called you?”

Victoria stopped.

“How do you know?”

“Because you look exactly like someone who has received information they don't understand.”

Victoria ignored the remark.

“Someone told me I wasn't the child taken from the estate.”

Richard's expression changed.

“Who?”

“I don't know.”

“What else did they say?”

“They said the child wasn't Aria either.”

Richard stared at her.

“Did they identify the child?”

“No.”

He sat down.

Victoria looked at him.

“Father, what if there was another child?”

Richard shook his head.

“The original hospital records identified two infants.”

“Then how can the child in the photograph be neither of us?”

“I don't know.”

“Maybe the records are wrong.”

Richard looked at the notebook.

“Or perhaps we have misunderstood what the records mean.”

Victoria stepped closer.

“What does that mean?”

Richard did not answer.

**6:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had found something.

He had searched the charity's financial records for payments made after the guardian arrangement ended.

One transaction stood out.

A private educational trust had begun paying school expenses for a child three months after the maintenance account closed.

The trust was registered under a different name.

**THE ELEANOR FOUNDATION.**

Cassian looked at the screen.

“Eleanor?”

Felix nodded.

“Not Eleanor Sterling. The foundation was established by a woman named Eleanor Shaw.”

Marcus leaned forward.

“Miriam Shaw?”

“No. The former estate custodian. Her full name was Eleanor Shaw.”

The room went quiet.

“She's dead,” Maximilian said.

“Yes. But the foundation continued operating for another four years under independent trustees.”

“Where?”

Felix displayed the registered address.

A private school.

The school had since merged with another institution.

Aria looked at the record.

“Can we find the student?”

Felix nodded.

“I'm trying.”

“Do it properly,” Maximilian said.

“I know.”

“Don't assume the child used the hospital name.”

Felix looked at Aria.

“Already accounted for.”

A moment later, the archive search returned a result.

A student file.

The child's name had been redacted in the public index.

But the birth year matched.

And the file contained a reference to the Eleanor Foundation.

Felix opened the attached document.

A school admission form appeared.

The child's photograph was attached.

A little girl with dark hair.

The same girl from the charity photograph.

Under the field marked *Previous Name*, someone had written:

**NOT TO BE DISCLOSED.**

Below it, the new name had been entered.

**LILY SHAW.**

**7:18 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Aria stared at the school photograph.

“Lily Shaw.”

Felix nodded.

“That's the name used in the school records.”

“Is she alive?”

“We don't know.”

“Where is she now?”

“Her school records end when she was seven. No later enrolment has been located.”

Cassian examined the photograph.

“She looks happy.”

Helena looked at him.

“Don't assume that from one photograph.”

Cassian nodded.

“Fair.”

Marcus opened the school file.

“There is an emergency contact.”

“Who?” Maximilian asked.

Felix enlarged the field.

**MARGARET VALE.**

The room went silent.

Aria looked at Marcus.

“Jonathan's sister?”

“Yes.”

“Then Margaret may have raised the child.”

Marcus immediately called her.

**7:34 p.m. — Margaret Vale's Home**

Margaret answered.

“Mr. Vale?”

“Ms. Vale, I need to ask you something important. Did Jonathan ever bring a child to you?”

Margaret was silent.

Then she said:

“Yes.”

Marcus stood.

“When?”

“Eleven years ago.”

“Was the child a little girl?”

“Yes.”

“Did Jonathan tell you where she came from?”

“No.”

“Did he give you a name?”

Margaret's voice softened.

“He called her Lily.”

Marcus looked at the school record.

“Was her previous name ever disclosed to you?”

“No. Jonathan said she needed a fresh start.”

“Did you adopt her?”

“I became her legal guardian. Jonathan had arranged the documents through a solicitor.”

“Where is she now?”

Margaret took a breath.

“She left when she was sixteen.”

“Where did she go?”

“She received a scholarship to study abroad. She wanted to become independent.”

“Have you spoken to her recently?”

“Yes. Last month.”

Marcus felt his pulse quicken.

“Is she safe?”

Margaret's answer was immediate.

“Yes.”

“Does she know about her birth?”

“No.”

Marcus closed his eyes briefly.

“Ms. Vale, we need to speak with her.”

Margaret's voice became guarded.

“Why?”

“Because we believe her identity may be connected to a long-standing investigation involving two families.”

There was a pause.

Then Margaret said:

“Please don't frighten her.”

Marcus looked at the school photograph.

“We won't.”

**9:02 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Octavian listened to Marcus's report.

The child from the photograph had been identified as Lily Shaw.

She had been placed under Margaret Vale's guardianship.

She was alive.

She had left the country for education.

And she knew nothing about the investigation.

Aria looked at the photograph again.

“So the child was not me.”

“No,” Marcus said.

“Was she Victoria?”

“We don't know yet.”

Felix displayed the school admission date.

“Lily was admitted under her new name at age four. The birth year matches the hospital records for Infant B.”

“Could the child have been born to the Sterlings?” Helena asked.

“Possible,” Felix replied.

“Could she have been born to the Vespers?”

“Also possible.”

Octavian looked at the photograph.

“Then we have found the missing child, but not her identity.”

Aria nodded.

“And we shouldn't contact her until we know how to explain this.”

Marcus agreed.

“Margaret has asked us not to frighten her.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Respect that.”

**10:26 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a message.

*The Vespers have identified Lily Shaw.*

She stood.

For the first time in many years, she looked genuinely alarmed.

“Who told them?”

“We don't know.”

“Have they contacted Margaret?”

“Yes.”

“And Lily?”

“Not yet.”

The woman walked to the locked drawer.

She opened it.

The photograph of the little girl lay inside.

She looked at it for a long time.

Then she removed another document.

A birth registration extract.

The name at the top was not Lily Shaw.

It was a name she had not seen written in years.

She folded the document and placed it inside her coat.

Then she called her associate.

“Find out whether Margaret has told Lily anything.”

“And if she has?”

The woman closed the drawer.

“Then we have to reach Lily before the Vespers do.”

**11:43 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria received one final message from Marcus.

*We have located the child from the photograph. She is alive and was raised under the name Lily Shaw by Margaret Vale. Her birth identity remains unverified.*

Aria read it carefully.

The missing child was alive.

That was something.

But the mystery had become more complicated.

Two babies had been switched.

One had been raised as Aria Sterling.

The other had been raised as Victoria Sterling.

And now a third identity appeared in the records.

Lily Shaw.

Aria looked at the message again.

There were only two possibilities.

Either Lily was one of the original two girls, and the records had concealed her identity.

Or the entire investigation had misunderstood what Subject 01 actually represented.

Her phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

**Lily was never supposed to be found.**

A second message followed.

**Ask Richard Sterling who signed her original birth registration.**

Aria stared at the screen.

Then she forwarded both messages to Marcus.

Across the city, Richard Sterling was opening his notebook again.

On its final page, beneath the entry concerning Jonathan Vale, he noticed a line he had overlooked.

A line written in different ink.

**Original birth registration retained by E. Shaw.**

Richard slowly closed the notebook.

He knew exactly where that document had been kept.

And he knew who had taken it.
