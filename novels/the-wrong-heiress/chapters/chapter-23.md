# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 23 — The Child Sent to the Wrong Address**

**Wednesday — 11:18 p.m.**

Octavian did not speak immediately.

Marcus waited.

“Read the document again,” Octavian said.

Marcus looked at the photograph.

“Receiving organisation: Vesper Life Sciences. Private Maternity Transfer.”

“Is the signature visible?”

“No. The receiving organisation is typed. The authorisation field contains the reference M.V.”

“Does it identify a facility?”

“No.”

Octavian turned toward Lucian.

“Did Vesper Life Sciences operate maternity facilities twelve years ago?”

Lucian nodded slowly.

“Several. Hospitals, specialist clinics and research facilities.”

“Could an infant have been admitted without your knowledge?”

“Not through a normal admission process.”

“Could an external transfer have been arranged?”

Lucian considered the question.

“Through the medical administration division, yes. But there would have been a patient record.”

Marcus added, “The hospital's document says no subsequent identity confirmation was received.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Then we have not established that the child ever arrived.”

“Correct.”

Octavian stood.

“Find every record connected to that transfer. Do not contact the media. Do not approach any child or family based on an incomplete record.”

Lucian nodded.

“I'll handle the medical division.”

**Thursday — 7:40 a.m.**

Felix had discovered a problem.

He had expected the Vesper Life Sciences archive to contain a clear record of the transfer.

Instead, the search returned seventeen results.

Fourteen were routine administrative references.

Two were incomplete billing entries.

One was a deleted patient number.

He stared at the screen.

“Why is everything deleted?”

A technician beside him replied, “Some of these systems were migrated.”

“Migration doesn't mean deletion.”

“It can.”

Felix turned.

“Then whoever designed the migration should be required to explain it to a committee.”

“Would you like me to arrange one?”

“No. Committees are where information goes to die.”

He opened the deleted patient number.

**VLS-PRIVATE-118**

The record had been created twelve years earlier.

Its status was marked:

**ADMISSION INITIATED — NOT COMPLETED**

Felix leaned forward.

There was a timestamp.

The entry had been created two days after Infant B's hospital handover.

Then, thirty-six hours later, someone had cancelled it.

The cancellation reason was blank.

Felix called Marcus.

“I found the Vesper Life Sciences admission record.”

“Was the infant admitted?”

“No. Admission was initiated, but never completed.”

“Who cancelled it?”

“That's what I'm trying to find.”

He opened the audit history.

A username appeared.

**ADMINISTRATIVE OVERRIDE — M.VOSS**

Felix went quiet.

“Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“The same name cancelled the admission.”

**8:25 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

The family gathered in Octavian's office.

Lucian had obtained the old medical division's operational records.

Maximilian stood beside the window, reading the admission summary.

Damian reviewed the security logs.

Cassian had stopped making jokes.

Felix displayed the record.

“Infant B was directed to Vesper Life Sciences. An admission was initiated. It was cancelled thirty-six hours later by an administrative override.”

Lucian examined the document.

“VLS-Private was a restricted intake category. It was used for confidential medical cases.”

“Who could authorise an override?” Maximilian asked.

“Only the director of medical administration or an approved external representative.”

“Was Mara Voss an approved representative?”

Lucian shook his head.

“Not according to the records we have.”

Damian pointed at the username.

“Then someone gave her access.”

“Or someone used her credentials,” Felix replied.

Marcus looked at the audit history.

“Can we identify the person who created the original admission?”

Felix enlarged the screen.

A second username appeared.

**L. VALE**

Cassian frowned.

“Another Vale?”

Marcus checked the record.

“We need to establish whether this is connected to Jonathan Vale.”

Felix shook his head.

“The username belongs to a different employee account. It could be a coincidence.”

Octavian looked at Lucian.

“Find the employee.”

**10:15 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria was reading through a client's financial statements when Peter approached.

“Do you have a moment?”

“Of course.”

He placed a document on her desk.

“I'd like you to take responsibility for the monthly reconciliation on this account.”

Aria looked at the file.

“Is this temporary?”

“For now. If it goes well, we'll make it a regular responsibility.”

She nodded.

“Thank you.”

Peter smiled.

“You've been doing good work.”

“I'll try not to disappoint you.”

“Just do the work carefully. That's all I ask.”

Aria opened the file.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Marcus.

*We have identified a connection between the second infant's handover and an old Vesper Life Sciences admission record. The admission was cancelled before completion. We are investigating who authorised it.*

Aria read it twice.

Then she replied.

*Was the child ever admitted?*

Marcus answered:

*No confirmed admission. We are checking whether the infant was physically received at any Vesper facility.*

Aria put the phone down.

She looked at the financial statement.

Then at the message again.

Someone had sent a newborn to a medical organisation that belonged to her biological family.

And nobody seemed to know whether the child had arrived.

**12:30 p.m. — Vesper Life Sciences Headquarters**

Lucian met the former director of medical administration, Dr. Samuel Reed.

Reed was retired and had been living outside the city.

He arrived carrying a leather folder.

“I was surprised by your request,” he said.

Lucian gestured toward a chair.

“We're reviewing a confidential transfer from twelve years ago.”

Reed opened his folder.

“Which one?”

“VLS-Private-118.”

The older man looked at him.

“I remember the reference.”

Lucian leaned forward.

“Do you remember the infant?”

“No.”

“Do you remember the transfer?”

Reed examined the reference.

“This was not a standard hospital admission.”

“What was it?”

“A reserved intake. The receiving facility was supposed to prepare a confidential room and assign a medical team before the patient arrived.”

“Was the patient ever received?”

Reed turned a page.

“According to the final report, no.”

Lucian looked at him.

“Who cancelled the admission?”

Reed hesitated.

“I didn't.”

“Who did?”

“I don't remember.”

“Your credentials were not used?”

“No. The system shows an external administrative override.”

“Did you investigate?”

Reed closed the folder.

“I tried.”

Lucian waited.

Reed continued.

“The medical team had already been informed to prepare for an infant. When the admission was cancelled, I asked for an explanation. I was told the transfer had been redirected.”

“By whom?”

“A private representative.”

“Name?”

Reed shook his head.

“I never met the person.”

Lucian's expression hardened.

“Did you ask where the infant was sent?”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“I was told the receiving authority had changed.”

“Did you verify that?”

“No.”

“Why not?”

Reed looked uncomfortable.

“The instruction came through the confidential administration channel. I believed the proper authorisation had been obtained.”

Lucian sat back.

“Who signed the cancellation?”

Reed opened the folder again.

“There was a printed copy in my old records.”

He removed a folded sheet.

At the bottom was a signature.

**M. VOSS**

**1:05 p.m.**

Lucian examined the document.

“Did you ever see this woman?”

“No.”

“Did you speak to her?”

“No.”

“Then why did you accept her instruction?”

Reed looked down.

“I didn't accept it personally. The instruction was entered by the central administration office. I was informed afterward.”

“Who worked there?”

Reed gave him a name.

“Lydia Vale.”

Lucian looked up.

“L. Vale?”

Reed nodded.

“She handled confidential admissions.”

“Where is she now?”

“I don't know. She left the organisation several years later.”

“Did she resign?”

“I believe she did.”

Lucian looked at the document again.

“Do you have her address?”

“No. But I may have an old employment file.”

“Please find it.”

Reed nodded.

**2:40 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had found Lydia Vale's employment file.

She had worked in Vesper Life Sciences' confidential administration division for six years.

Her final employment date was recorded as three months after the infant admission was cancelled.

Her departure reason:

**VOLUNTARY RESIGNATION**

The file included an emergency contact.

**Jonathan Vale — Brother**

Marcus read the entry.

“Jonathan Vale.”

Felix nodded.

“The courier who collected the second Northbridge package.”

Damian looked at the screen.

“His sister worked in our medical division?”

“Apparently.”

“Did she know about the infant transfer?”

“We don't know.”

Marcus looked at the employment dates.

“Was she still employed when the hospital records were being transferred?”

Felix checked.

“Yes.”

“Then we need to speak to her.”

**4:15 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard had not told Adrian about the notebook.

He had not told Eleanor.

And he had not told Victoria what was written inside it.

But Victoria had noticed that he had begun carrying it everywhere.

She found him in the study.

“What's in the notebook?”

Richard closed it.

“Nothing.”

“Then show me.”

“No.”

Victoria walked closer.

“Father, I know there was another child.”

Richard looked at her.

“Who told you?”

“Does it matter?”

“Yes.”

She crossed her arms.

“Why are you so frightened of this?”

“Because you don't understand what you're involving yourself in.”

“Then explain it.”

Richard looked down at the notebook.

“I authorised an account. I collected a payment acknowledgement. I received a document saying the second transfer had not been completed.”

“And?”

“And I was told not to interfere.”

“By Mara Voss?”

Richard shook his head.

“By someone else.”

Victoria went still.

“Who?”

Richard opened the notebook.

He turned it toward her.

A name was written beside the original hospital instruction.

**Lydia Vale.**

Victoria stared.

“Who is she?”

“I don't know.”

“Then why is her name here?”

“Because she was the person who contacted me about the second transfer.”

Victoria looked up.

“Did you meet her?”

“Once.”

“What did she tell you?”

Richard closed the notebook.

“That the second child was not going to be placed with the family originally intended.”

Victoria's face changed.

“Which family?”

Richard looked at her.

“She didn't say.”

**6:50 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria was having tea with Isolde.

She had finished work and decided to visit before going home.

Isolde poured tea into her cup.

“Your mother tells me you have been given more responsibility at work.”

“Yes. Monthly reconciliation.”

“Are you pleased?”

“Very. It means they trust me with something important.”

“Good.”

Aria took a sip.

“Although I suspect the client will eventually discover that their accounts are not magically repaired by handing me a folder.”

Isolde smiled.

“Perhaps you should send them a bill for the magic.”

“I'd have to charge extra.”

They sat quietly for a moment.

Then Aria asked, “Has the investigation found the second child?”

Isolde's expression softened.

“Not yet.”

“Do they know whether the child reached Vesper Life Sciences?”

“No. The admission was cancelled.”

Aria looked down at her cup.

“That's awful.”

“Yes.”

“Do you think the child is alive?”

Isolde did not offer false reassurance.

“We don't know.”

Aria nodded.

“Then I hope they find out.”

Isolde reached across the table and gently touched her hand.

“So do I.”

**9:30 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman stood beside a desk.

An associate entered.

“Lydia Vale has been located.”

The woman turned.

“Where?”

“She is living under her own name. She has been working as a private records consultant.”

“Does she know the Vespers are looking for her?”

“Not yet.”

The woman considered this.

“Contact her.”

“What should I tell her?”

“Tell her the old admission record has been recovered.”

The associate hesitated.

“And if she refuses to meet?”

The woman looked at him.

“She won't.”

“How can you be certain?”

“Because she has spent twelve years waiting for someone to ask what happened to that child.”

**11:20 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received a message from the legal team.

Lydia Vale's former employment file had been verified.

Her current address was confirmed.

Her employment history matched the old records.

And there was one more detail.

She had made a formal request to Vesper Life Sciences eleven years earlier.

The request concerned the missing infant admission.

It had been rejected.

The stated reason was:

**NO AUTHORISED NEXT-OF-KIN REQUEST ON FILE.**

Marcus read the message twice.

Then he called Octavian.

“Sir, we've located Lydia Vale.”

Octavian sat upright.

“Does she know about the investigation?”

“We don't know. But she tried to obtain information about the missing infant eleven years ago.”

“Why was her request rejected?”

“Because she wasn't listed as an authorised relative.”

Octavian looked toward the document on his desk.

“Arrange a meeting. No pressure. Let her decide whether she wishes to speak.”

Marcus nodded.

“There is another detail.”

“What?”

“Her brother was Jonathan Vale.”

Octavian was silent.

The courier who collected the second package.

The sister who worked in confidential medical administration.

The woman who had tried to find the missing infant.

For twelve years, the same family had appeared at different points in the investigation.

And they might finally hold the missing piece.
