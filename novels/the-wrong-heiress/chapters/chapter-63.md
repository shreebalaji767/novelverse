# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 63 — The Release That Had No Recipient**

**Sunday — 1:34 p.m.**

“Read the release record again.”

Octavian stood beside Felix's desk.

Felix enlarged the document.

“Original envelope received at Northstar Document Custody. Reference NC-2086 / PRIV-07. Stored under legacy authority LA-01. Released six weeks later under emergency executive authority EX-04.”

“And the recipient?”

“Not recorded.”

“Who authorised the release?”

“The authority code is recorded. No individual name.”

Octavian looked at the document.

“An archive cannot release a confidential original without recording who collected it.”

Margaret Ellison shook her head.

“Normally, no. But Northstar had an emergency release provision.”

Thomas turned towards her.

“What provision?”

“If the original custodian's authority was disputed or temporarily unavailable, the executive custodian could request immediate release to prevent loss or unauthorised seizure.”

“Was the recipient still required to identify themselves?”

“Yes. There had to be a signed receipt.”

Felix pointed to the bottom of the document.

“There isn't one.”

Margaret examined it.

“Then the release was incomplete.”

“Yet Northstar marked it completed,” Thomas said.

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Take us there.”

**2:10 p.m. — Northstar Document Custody**

The facility's current director received the Vesper delegation in a private conference room.

He was a careful man who had clearly spent considerable time learning how to avoid making promises.

“I understand you're investigating a historical release,” he said.

Thomas placed the record on the table.

“We need the original release ledger.”

The director studied the document.

“That's a restricted record.”

“It concerns a document removed under an emergency authority that may have been used fraudulently,” Margaret replied.

The director looked at Octavian.

“Mr. Vesper, I can arrange a supervised review. I cannot provide unrestricted access to the archive.”

Octavian nodded.

“Supervised access is acceptable.”

The director made a call.

Twenty minutes later, an employee brought in a large ledger and a sealed archival envelope.

The director opened the ledger.

“Here is the release entry.”

Felix leaned forward.

The original record was handwritten.

**NC-2086 / PRIV-07**

**Release authority: EX-04**

**Release date: Six weeks after initial deposit**

**Collection method: Secure dispatch**

**Recipient: Not entered**

**Receipt: Pending**

Felix frowned.

“Why was the receipt marked pending?”

The director turned the page.

“It was later closed.”

“By whom?”

The director pointed to the closing notation.

**Administrative exception approved.**

No name.

Only a code.

**P-01.**

Octavian's expression changed.

“My office code.”

The director nodded.

“That is what the register indicates.”

“Who accepted the exception?”

The director checked the adjacent field.

“An authorised senior officer.”

“Name?”

“Not recorded.”

Thomas stared at the ledger.

“So the archive released the envelope without a named recipient, then approved its own missing receipt under the authority of the Vesper patriarchal office.”

The director looked uncomfortable.

“That is what the surviving record indicates.”

Margaret turned another page.

“Where was the envelope sent?”

The director checked the dispatch field.

“Secure holding facility. The location is coded.”

Felix took a photograph of the entry.

“Can we identify the destination?”

“Possibly. The code belongs to an older Northstar facility.”

“Where?”

The director hesitated.

“Greyhaven Storage Annex.”

**3:02 p.m. — Northstar Document Custody**

The director brought up the old facility record.

Greyhaven Storage Annex had been a temporary archive for confidential legal and medical documents.

It had closed nineteen years ago.

Its contents had been transferred to other facilities.

Except for one storage unit.

**Unit 04 — Legacy Custodial Material.**

Thomas looked at Margaret.

“Is that the same Room 04 mentioned in the old trust records?”

“No. The numbering system was different.”

Felix examined the facility's transfer history.

“Unit 04 was never formally cleared.”

The director frowned.

“That cannot be right.”

He checked again.

The record showed that the unit had been placed under restricted access shortly before the annex closed.

Its contents were not transferred.

Instead, the storage status had been changed to:

**Retained under EX-04.**

Octavian looked at the screen.

“Who retained it?”

The director searched the record.

“An instruction from the executive custodian.”

“Elias?”

The director could not confirm.

“The record only identifies the authority.”

Catherine looked at the date.

“The annex closed nineteen years ago.”

“Correct.”

“Was Unit 04 inspected before closure?”

The director checked.

“No inspection report is attached.”

“Then we don't know whether the envelope is still there.”

The director looked at Octavian.

“If the unit remains sealed, access will require a legal review.”

Octavian nodded.

“Begin it.”

**4:26 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria was sitting in the garden when Victoria approached.

She had brought two cups of tea.

Aria accepted one.

“Is this an apology?”

“For what?”

“Nothing. I was checking whether there was a reason.”

Victoria sat opposite her.

“Jonathan remembered something else.”

Aria looked up.

“What?”

“He said the woman who received the envelope at Northstar wore a hospital identification badge.”

“That seems relevant.”

“She wasn't wearing a Northstar uniform.”

Aria considered that.

“Did he see the name?”

“No. He only remembers a photograph on the badge.”

“Was it her photograph?”

“He couldn't tell.”

Victoria took a sip.

“He also said she looked familiar.”

Aria waited.

“Not familiar because he'd met her. Familiar because he'd seen her somewhere before.”

“Where?”

“He doesn't know.”

Aria looked towards the estate.

“Did he describe her?”

“Middle-aged. Dark hair. Tall.”

“That describes quite a lot of people.”

“I know.”

Victoria put her cup down.

“I'm going to ask him again tomorrow.”

Aria nodded.

“Good.”

Victoria looked at her.

“You aren't going to tell me to leave it alone?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you have the right to ask him questions. Just don't decide what his answers mean before you hear them.”

Victoria gave a small nod.

For once, she seemed content with the advice.

**5:12 p.m. — Vesper Estate, Private Study**

Felix had received the Northstar dispatch index.

He was comparing it with the old courier records when a familiar reference appeared.

**TR-08.**

He opened the entry.

The dispatch had been assigned to Thomas Reed.

Felix immediately called him.

Thomas arrived a few minutes later.

“What have you found?”

Felix turned the screen towards him.

“Northstar recorded the secure dispatch under your courier number.”

Thomas stared at it.

“I never collected that envelope.”

“Did you receive an assignment?”

“No.”

“Could someone have used your credentials?”

“Yes.”

“Were they active?”

Thomas frowned.

“They shouldn't have been.”

Felix checked the date.

“Your courier credentials were deactivated eleven days before the release.”

Thomas leaned closer.

“Then someone used an inactive identity.”

Margaret entered the room.

“Again?”

Felix nodded.

“Northstar recorded the dispatch under Thomas's courier number, but the account had already been deactivated.”

Thomas examined the entry.

“Was the dispatch actually completed?”

Felix opened the status field.

“Marked delivered.”

“To whom?”

“Not recorded.”

Thomas let out a slow breath.

“So the envelope was released under EX-04, dispatched under my inactive courier identity, and delivered without a named recipient.”

Margaret looked at the screen.

“Someone has been very consistent.”

Aria, who had entered quietly behind them, studied the record.

“Consistent about what?”

Margaret answered.

“Making sure the paperwork records an event without identifying the person responsible.”

Aria looked at Thomas.

“Can the courier number tell us where it went?”

“Not by itself,” Felix said. “But the dispatch route might.”

He opened the route archive.

A destination code appeared.

**GSA-04.**

“Greyhaven Storage Annex,” Maximilian said.

Felix nodded.

“The same destination.”

**6:03 p.m. — Private Study**

Octavian read the report.

“Then we open Unit 04.”

Catherine looked at him.

“Not until we know whether the unit still contains the original envelope.”

“Why?”

“Because if someone has retained it for nineteen years, an unexpected visit may alert whoever has been monitoring the storage facility.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Someone may already know we are investigating.”

“Then we should not make their work easier.”

Damian stepped forward.

“I can arrange a discreet inspection.”

“No armed team,” Aria said.

Octavian turned towards her.

“We are not discussing your security.”

“We are discussing how to enter a storage facility without making it look like an invasion.”

Cassian gave a quiet laugh.

“She has a point.”

Maximilian looked at him.

“Don't encourage her.”

“I'm encouraging good logistics.”

Aria continued.

“Send two people who can inspect the unit and verify its contents. If it's empty, we leave. If something is there, document it before touching anything.”

Damian nodded.

“That is sensible.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Arrange it.”

**7:41 p.m. — Greyhaven Storage Annex**

The old facility stood behind a high perimeter wall.

Most of the building had been emptied years earlier. Its loading bays were sealed, and the remaining archive rooms were maintained by a small preservation team.

Damian arrived with Thomas and Margaret.

The facility manager led them to Unit 04.

The door was intact.

The seal was not.

Thomas crouched beside the locking mechanism.

“Someone opened this recently.”

Margaret looked at the seal.

“How recently?”

“Can't tell yet. The lock is old, but the seal was replaced.”

Damian examined the access panel.

“Any recorded entry?”

The facility manager checked his tablet.

“None.”

Thomas looked at him.

“Then someone entered without using the recorded access system.”

The manager looked alarmed.

“That's impossible.”

Thomas stood.

“It appears to have happened.”

Damian gave the instruction.

“Photograph everything. Then open it.”

The lock was released.

Inside were several metal cabinets and a long storage shelf.

Most of the cabinets were empty.

One remained sealed.

Its label read:

**NC-2086 / PRIV-07**

Thomas looked at Margaret.

“The original envelope.”

Margaret approached carefully.

The seal bore the same legacy reference.

Damian examined the outer casing.

“Do not open it here.”

Thomas photographed the label, the seal and the surrounding cabinet.

Then he noticed something.

A narrow strip of paper had been placed beneath the cabinet's locking bar.

He carefully removed it.

There were only six words.

**The second transfer was never authorised.**

Margaret stared at the message.

“Who left that?”

Thomas looked at the cabinet.

“Someone who knew what was inside.”

Damian checked the room again.

“Could they still be here?”

The facility manager shook his head.

“No one has been recorded entering.”

Thomas looked at the broken seal.

“Then someone has been using this facility without being recorded.”

**8:18 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

The report arrived.

Octavian read the six-word message.

Catherine sat beside him.

“The second transfer was never authorised,” she repeated.

Lucian looked at the old envelope reference.

“Then why was the envelope released?”

“To prevent the records from being reunited,” Margaret said.

Thomas shook his head.

“Not necessarily. The message could have been left by someone trying to stop the transfer.”

Aria looked at the paper.

“Or someone trying to make us believe it was never authorised.”

Victoria turned towards her.

“Why would they do that?”

“To keep us from asking who actually authorised it.”

Octavian looked at the sealed envelope.

“Whatever the explanation, we now have the original.”

Felix shook his head.

“We have a sealed cabinet. We haven't verified that the envelope is inside.”

Damian nodded.

“And the facility was accessed without a recorded entry.”

Maximilian looked at the report.

“Then we move it under controlled custody.”

Catherine stared at the handwritten message.

“No.”

Everyone turned.

“If someone left that note, they wanted it found. We need to establish whether the envelope has been opened, altered or replaced before moving it.”

Octavian considered her words.

Then he looked at Damian.

“Secure the unit. No one enters without a written record.”

Damian nodded.

Aria glanced at the message again.

“The second transfer was never authorised.”

She looked at Margaret.

“Who was supposed to authorise it?”

Margaret's expression changed.

“The two custodians.”

“Which two?”

“Evelyn Vesper and the independent family custodian.”

Catherine looked towards her.

“But Evelyn was the original custodian.”

“Yes.”

“And the second custodian?”

Margaret hesitated.

“Elias Vesper.”

The room fell silent.

The envelope had been released under his emergency authority.

The transfer had been recorded as completed.

Yet the message claimed it had never been authorised by the two custodians.

Octavian looked at the old reference.

“Then someone used Elias's authority to release something that Elias himself may never have approved.”

Catherine lowered her eyes.

“And we still don't know whether he was alive when it happened.”

For the first time, the sealed envelope at Greyhaven Storage Annex seemed less like a missing piece of evidence and more like a warning.

Someone had preserved it.

Someone had removed it.

Someone had returned it.

And someone had left a message insisting that the transfer should never have happened.

**The question was no longer where the envelope had gone.**

**It was why someone had brought it back.**
