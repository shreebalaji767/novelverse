# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 41 — The Father Who Signed the First Document**

**Wednesday — 7:05 a.m.**

Lucian Vesper was summoned to the library.

He arrived to find his father standing beside the desk, his mother seated in an armchair, and the original legacy authority document placed between them.

He looked at the signatures.

Then at Octavian.

“Where did you find this?”

“Private Archive 04.”

Lucian picked up the document.

His signature was unmistakable.

He read the date.

Twenty-four years earlier.

“I remember signing this.”

Octavian's expression tightened.

“Then explain it.”

Lucian sat down.

“Elias brought it to me. He said the family needed a contingency arrangement in case the succession became disputed.”

“Between whom?”

“He didn't say.”

“Why did you sign?”

Lucian looked at the document.

“Because I trusted him.”

Isolde spoke quietly.

“What did he tell you the authority could do?”

“Preserve an inheritance claim until the rightful beneficiaries were verified. Nothing more.”

Octavian tapped the second page.

“It allowed someone to separate two descendants and reverse their intended placements.”

Lucian looked genuinely surprised.

“That wasn't what I was told.”

“Did you ever use the authority?”

“No.”

“Did you give anyone permission to use your signature?”

“No.”

Octavian studied his son.

“Then we have another problem.”

Lucian nodded.

“We have to find Elias's original instructions.”

**8:30 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had found the original creation record.

The legacy authority had been established following a dispute over an inheritance trust belonging to the Vesper family.

The dispute involved a provision that had never been publicly disclosed.

He displayed the relevant clause.

**The inheritance shall pass to the first verified direct descendant eligible under the original succession instrument.**

Cassian read it.

“That's remarkably vague.”

“It's deliberately incomplete,” Felix replied. “The full succession instrument was stored separately.”

Maximilian looked at Octavian.

“Where?”

“The family archive.”

Alistair Monroe shook his head.

“The original instrument was removed twenty-three years ago.”

“By whom?” Damian asked.

Alistair checked the archive register.

“Elias Vesper.”

The room fell silent.

Aria looked at the document.

“So Elias created a system to protect an inheritance, then removed the document that explained the inheritance.”

Felix nodded.

“That is what the records suggest.”

“Excellent system.”

Cassian smiled faintly.

“Your great-uncle had a talent for creating administrative disasters.”

Octavian did not smile.

“Find the instrument.”

**10:15 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria was reviewing her own school records when Richard entered.

He placed a folder on the desk.

“What is this?” she asked.

“Documents concerning the Sterling estate.”

She opened it.

Inside were records from the period when she had lived under the name Lily Shaw.

One document caught her attention.

**Temporary Custody Review — Infant B.**

The date was six weeks after her delivery to the estate.

The form contained a handwritten note:

*Child remains under protective arrangement. No transfer until confirmation of second placement.*

Victoria looked up.

“Who wrote this?”

“Eleanor Shaw's office received it.”

“Did you ever see it?”

“No.”

“Then why are you giving it to me?”

Richard hesitated.

“Because I have been trying to understand what happened without admitting how little I knew.”

Victoria closed the folder.

“That isn't an explanation.”

“No.”

“It's a beginning.”

Richard accepted the criticism.

He placed another sheet on the table.

“This was attached to the review.”

It was a payment authorisation.

The recipient was a private trust.

The authorising initials were:

**L.V.**

Victoria stared at them.

“Again?”

Richard nodded.

“The same initials appearing in the Vesper records.”

She examined the payment date.

It was issued three days before Jonathan took her away.

“Someone was paying for my custody.”

“Yes.”

“And then someone paid for my transfer.”

“Yes.”

Victoria pushed the papers toward him.

“Find out who received the money.”

**12:40 p.m. — Vesper Archives**

Felix found the trust's beneficiary register.

The payment recipient was not a person.

It was a legal entity.

**Linden Vale Preservation Trust.**

Marcus checked the registration.

“Who established it?”

Felix opened the founding record.

“Margaret Ellison.”

Octavian looked at the screen.

“Elias's secretary.”

“Yes.”

“Who funded it?”

Felix scrolled down.

The original funding came from the Vesper family estate.

A later contribution came from a private account belonging to Elias.

But the final funding entry was different.

**Contributor: Lucian Vesper.**

Lucian looked at the screen.

“I never funded that trust.”

The transaction had been made eighteen months after he signed the legacy authority.

“Could it have been taken from the family account?” Maximilian asked.

“No. This was a personal account.”

Lucian examined the reference.

Then he frowned.

“I recognise this number.”

Octavian turned toward him.

“From where?”

Lucian stared at the transaction.

“My mother's estate.”

Isolde became very still.

**1:20 p.m.**

The original trust had received money from Isolde's private inheritance.

Lucian had been named as the account administrator.

He remembered signing routine estate documents after his mother's death.

He had never authorised a payment to Margaret Ellison.

“Someone used my administrative authority,” he said.

Isolde looked at him.

“Did you retain copies?”

“Yes.”

“Where?”

“In my private office.”

Marcus immediately arranged for the records to be retrieved.

Octavian looked toward his wife.

“You never mentioned this trust.”

“I had never heard of it.”

“Yet your inheritance funded it.”

“My inheritance funded several family trusts. I did not personally manage every transaction.”

Lucian looked uncomfortable.

“I was responsible for the administration.”

Isolde regarded him.

“Then you will help us find out who used your authority.”

**3:00 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The private office records arrived.

Felix compared the transaction number against the estate ledger.

He found the original payment.

It had been authorised by a power of attorney.

The document was signed by Lucian.

But the signature date was wrong.

It was dated six months before the payment.

Lucian studied it.

“That isn't my signature.”

Felix zoomed in.

The handwriting looked convincing.

The final stroke was different.

“Someone copied your signature,” Felix said.

Damian examined the document.

“Who witnessed it?”

Felix enlarged the witness field.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

Cassian exhaled.

“Margaret was remarkably busy.”

“Or someone was using her name,” Marcus said.

Octavian looked at the record.

“Find her original personnel file.”

Alistair nodded.

“I'll retrieve it.”

**5:45 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria found Victoria in the drawing room.

Victoria had been reading the custody review.

“You look busy,” Aria said.

“I am.”

“Anything useful?”

Victoria showed her the payment authorisation.

Aria read it.

“Linden Vale Preservation Trust.”

“Yes.”

“Have they found who controlled it?”

“Not yet.”

Aria handed the document back.

“Then don't assume the person who received the money was the person who arranged everything.”

Victoria looked at her.

“You think someone used the trust as a middleman?”

“It's possible.”

Victoria leaned back.

“You always manage to make everything sound irritatingly simple.”

“It isn't simple. It's just not useful to decide the answer before finding it.”

Victoria considered that.

Then she returned to the document.

“Fine. I'll wait for the evidence.”

Aria nodded.

“That's probably healthier.”

**8:10 p.m. — Vesper Archives**

Alistair returned with Margaret Ellison's personnel file.

It contained employment records, correspondence, and a final resignation letter.

The letter was dated eleven months before the births.

Margaret had resigned for personal reasons.

However, the personnel file included a handwritten note from Elias:

*Her departure must remain confidential. She will continue to assist with the legacy trust.*

Felix read it aloud.

“Margaret resigned publicly but continued working privately.”

Maximilian looked at the date.

“So she didn't die as the records suggest?”

Alistair shook his head.

“No. The death record belongs to another Margaret Ellison.”

Cassian stared.

“Someone deliberately used the identity of a dead woman.”

Damian checked the file.

“Where did the real Margaret go?”

A final address was listed.

An old coastal residence.

The property had been sold fourteen years ago.

But the buyer's name was still recorded.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

**11:30 p.m. — Coastal Residence**

An elderly woman opened the door.

She looked at the visitors without surprise.

“I wondered when the Vespers would come.”

Marcus stepped forward.

“Are you Margaret Ellison?”

“Yes.”

“Did you establish the Linden Vale Preservation Trust?”

Margaret looked at him.

“I did.”

“Did you arrange the allocation reversal?”

“No.”

“Did you submit the instruction under Isolde Vesper's authority?”

Margaret's expression changed.

“I submitted the instruction.”

Marcus waited.

“But I did not write it.”

“Who did?”

Margaret looked past him.

At the security vehicles.

Then at the family members standing behind him.

“Elias Vesper.”

Octavian stepped forward.

“Why did my brother order the reversal?”

Margaret hesitated.

“Because the original inheritance instrument contained a condition neither family was supposed to know about.”

“What condition?”

She looked directly at Octavian.

“The inheritance did not belong to the child born into the Vesper family.”

Octavian's face hardened.

“Explain.”

Margaret shook her head.

“I cannot explain it from memory. Elias gave me the original instrument. I kept a copy.”

“Where?”

Margaret turned toward the house.

“In a locked cabinet.”

She returned a moment later carrying a sealed envelope.

On its front was written:

**THE ORIGINAL SUCCESSION INSTRUMENT — DO NOT OPEN UNTIL BOTH DESCENDANTS ARE IDENTIFIED.**

Octavian took it.

The envelope was old.

The seal was intact.

And beneath the instruction, Elias had written one final sentence:

*The firstborn is not necessarily the rightful heir.*

Octavian looked at Margaret.

“Who was born first?”

Margaret answered immediately.

“Victoria.”

**11:58 p.m.**

Back at Vesper Estate, the family gathered around the sealed envelope.

Aria stood beside Helena.

Victoria sat opposite her.

Octavian placed the envelope on the table.

Nobody moved.

Then Aria spoke.

“Before anyone opens that, can we establish something?”

Octavian looked at her.

“What is it?”

“Whatever that document says, it doesn't decide what either of us wants.”

Victoria glanced at her.

Aria continued.

“I don't know what this inheritance is. I don't know why people have spent twenty years moving documents around. And I don't want anyone treating me like I agreed to something just because I was born into this family.”

Octavian nodded.

“You are right.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“And I don't intend to become part of a succession dispute simply because someone decided I was born first.”

Lucian looked at his daughters.

“Neither of you will be forced into anything.”

Octavian broke the seal.

Inside was a single legal instrument.

He read the first paragraph.

Then stopped.

His eyes moved to the second page.

For a long moment, he said nothing.

Finally, he looked at Aria and Victoria.

“The inheritance was never intended to pass automatically to either family.”

He placed the document on the table.

“It was intended to pass to whichever of you could satisfy a condition established before your births.”

Felix leaned forward.

“What condition?”

Octavian looked down at the final clause.

**The rightful successor must be chosen by both descendants, acting independently and without coercion.**

Victoria stared at the words.

Aria blinked.

Then she looked at Octavian.

“So someone separated us for an inheritance that neither of us has agreed to accept?”

Nobody answered.

Aria picked up her tea.

“That's a lot of trouble for something we might not even want.”

And for the first time in twenty years, the most powerful family in the world faced a problem that money, influence, and authority could not solve.

Neither girl had agreed to play.
