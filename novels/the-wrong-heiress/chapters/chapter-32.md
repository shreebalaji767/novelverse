# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 32 — The Girl Who Had Two Names**

**Tuesday — 8:15 a.m.**

Victoria Sterling had a new problem.

Her name was Lily Shaw.

She had spent most of the night searching every accessible record containing that name. School archives, charitable organisations, scholarship announcements, old photographs, guardianship notices.

The results were irritatingly ordinary.

Lily Shaw had attended a small school.

Lily Shaw had received a scholarship.

Lily Shaw had lived under the guardianship of Margaret Vale.

And Lily Shaw had apparently disappeared from public records at sixteen.

Victoria stared at the school photograph on her laptop.

A little girl with dark hair and a serious expression looked back at her.

There was something familiar about the face.

Not enough to identify.

Enough to irritate her.

She called Margaret Vale.

The call rang six times.

Then went to voicemail.

Victoria tried again.

This time, Margaret answered.

“Hello?”

“Mrs. Vale. My name is Victoria Sterling.”

“I know.”

Victoria frowned.

“You do?”

“Yes. I've seen your photograph.”

“Then you know why I'm calling.”

“I suspect I do.”

“I need to speak to Lily.”

“No.”

The answer came so quickly that Victoria momentarily forgot what she intended to say.

“Excuse me?”

“Lily has not agreed to speak with you.”

“I haven't explained why.”

“You don't need to. You called without an appointment and demanded access to someone you haven't spoken to in years.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“I'm trying to establish the truth.”

“Then begin by respecting her right not to be involved.”

The call ended.

Victoria stared at her phone.

She had been refused by a woman who had never even met her.

**Vesper Tower — 9:40 a.m.**

Felix had found the dissolved records company's successor.

Unfortunately, the successor had also dissolved.

Its archive had been transferred to a legal records custodian, which had merged with another company, whose records were now held by a third organisation.

Cassian examined the corporate history.

“Did anyone actually operate a business here, or did they spend eleven years transferring boxes between buildings?”

“Both,” Felix replied.

“Remarkable.”

Marcus entered.

“We have received a response from Margaret Vale.”

Octavian looked up.

“Has she agreed to provide contact details?”

“She has agreed to pass a letter to Lily. She will not disclose Lily's current address without permission.”

“Reasonable,” Lucian said.

Marcus placed the letter on the table.

Aria picked it up.

“Who wrote this?”

“Legal counsel prepared it,” Marcus said. “It explains the investigation, the possibility that Lily's childhood records may be connected, and the fact that no conclusion has been reached.”

Aria read the first paragraph.

Then the second.

Then she put it down.

“It's written like she's being summoned to court.”

Cassian leaned over.

“It's legally accurate.”

“It's also terrifying.”

Helena took the letter.

“She's right.”

Marcus considered it.

“Would you prefer a simpler version?”

“Yes,” Aria said. “Tell her what you know, what you don't know, and that she can refuse.”

Octavian nodded.

“Revise it.”

Marcus took the letter back.

Felix looked mildly disappointed.

“I thought it was quite clear.”

“Felix,” Aria said, “you once sent me a welcome presentation with a table of contents longer than the actual welcome.”

“It was comprehensive.”

“It was a hostage situation.”

Cassian laughed.

Felix looked betrayed.

**10:55 a.m.**

The legal custodian finally responded.

Felix read the message twice.

“They have located an archive index.”

Maximilian moved closer.

“What does it say?”

“The original registration was transferred to a private records facility under reference MV-04. The facility received it, but the physical file was later withdrawn.”

“By whom?” Damian asked.

“An authorised representative.”

“Name?”

Felix enlarged the entry.

There was no name.

Only a collection code.

**MV-04 / PERSONAL RETENTION**

Lucian's expression darkened.

“Can they identify the representative through the collection code?”

“They can identify the authorisation record. They cannot identify the person without the original collection register.”

“Where is that?”

“Separate archive.”

Cassian closed his eyes.

“Of course.”

Felix continued.

“There's something else. The collection register was never transferred to the legal custodian. It was retained by the original facility's compliance officer.”

“Who is now?” Maximilian asked.

“Unknown. But the company pension records identify the former compliance officer.”

He placed another document on the screen.

**Eleanor Shaw.**

Aria looked at the name.

“Again?”

“Yes.”

Richard's earlier statement had suddenly become much more important.

**Sterling Mansion — 11:30 a.m.**

Richard received the call while reviewing old family accounts.

Marcus spoke without ceremony.

“Mr. Sterling, we need to speak with you about Eleanor Shaw.”

Richard put down his pen.

“What happened?”

“She may have retained the original collection register.”

Richard closed his eyes.

“I remember her.”

“Where can we find her?”

“I don't know her current address. But she had a brother who handled her legal affairs. His name was Peter Shaw.”

“Do you have contact details?”

“Possibly.”

Richard opened a cabinet and searched through a stack of old correspondence.

He found a folded card.

**Peter Shaw — Legal and Estate Administration.**

A telephone number was printed beneath it.

Richard read it aloud.

Marcus wrote it down.

“Thank you.”

“Mr. Vale?”

“Yes?”

“If Eleanor Shaw kept the collection register, she may know exactly who took the original birth registration.”

“That's what we're hoping to establish.”

Richard hesitated.

“There is something else.”

Marcus waited.

“When she released the document, she looked frightened. I asked whether there was a problem.”

“What did she say?”

Richard stared at the card.

“She said, 'I was instructed to keep the children separate. I was never told what would happen if someone came back for the records.'”

Marcus's expression changed.

“Why didn't you mention that before?”

“Because I didn't understand it.”

“And now?”

Richard looked toward the window.

“Now I think she knew the arrangement was not finished.”

**Vesper Tower — 1:15 p.m.**

The revised letter was ready.

It was one page.

No legal terminology. No demands. No mention of inheritance, succession, or family obligations.

It explained that the Vespers were investigating the circumstances surrounding two infants' transfers twenty years earlier. It stated that records connected to Lily's childhood had surfaced, but that the family had not established what they meant.

It ended with one sentence:

*If you would like to speak with us, we will answer your questions honestly. If you do not, we will respect your decision.*

Aria read it.

“That's better.”

Marcus nodded.

“Margaret will deliver it.”

“Good.”

Helena sat beside Aria.

“Do you think Lily will agree?”

“I don't know.”

Aria looked at the letter.

“She might not want anything to do with us.”

“Would that upset you?”

“A little.”

Helena waited.

“But that's her choice,” Aria continued. “We can't spend all this time investigating whether someone had their life taken away and then start making decisions for them.”

Helena gently touched her hand.

“Your grandfather will agree.”

Across the room, Octavian had been listening.

“I do.”

Cassian looked at him.

“You agreed rather quickly.”

“Would you prefer I disagree?”

“No, Grandfather. I prefer remaining alive.”

Isolde smiled into her tea.

**3:40 p.m.**

Peter Shaw answered on the fourth attempt.

Marcus introduced himself and explained the reason for the call.

The older man listened without interruption.

When Marcus mentioned Eleanor's name, he became quiet.

“My sister kept many things,” Peter said eventually. “She was meticulous. Sometimes excessively so.”

“Do you know whether she retained a collection register from a private records facility?”

“Yes.”

Marcus sat straighter.

“Do you know where it is?”

“I have it.”

For the first time that day, the investigation had produced an answer without another company merger.

“Would you be willing to meet our representatives?”

Peter hesitated.

“Before I agree, I want to know something. Are you investigating a child who was placed in the Sterling estate?”

“Yes.”

“And the registration?”

“Yes.”

Peter exhaled.

“Then I believe I should bring the register myself.”

“Thank you.”

“One condition.”

“Please.”

“My sister was not responsible for whatever happened to those children. She followed instructions given to her. I don't want her name dragged through accusations because she kept a document.”

Marcus glanced toward Octavian.

“We will not make accusations without evidence.”

“Good. I will bring the register tomorrow.”

**Sterling Mansion — 6:20 p.m.**

Victoria had stopped searching school records.

She was now looking through old photographs.

One image showed Margaret Vale standing beside a young girl at a scholarship ceremony.

The girl was sixteen.

Victoria enlarged the photograph.

The girl's face was partially turned toward the camera.

She looked familiar.

Victoria zoomed in further.

Then she froze.

The girl had the same distinctive eyes.

The same small scar near the left eyebrow.

Victoria slowly opened a recent photograph of herself.

She placed the images side by side.

The resemblance was unmistakable.

Her phone rang.

Richard.

She answered.

“Where did you get that photograph?” he asked.

Victoria looked at the screen.

“You know Lily?”

“I know the name.”

“That's not what I asked.”

Richard's voice tightened.

“Victoria, listen to me. Do not contact Margaret again.”

“Why?”

“Because you may be involved in this.”

Victoria's fingers tightened around the phone.

“Involved how?”

Richard didn't answer immediately.

Then he said something that made her stop breathing for a moment.

“Before the Sterlings found you, what name were you registered under?”

Victoria stared at the photograph.

She remembered being told that her early records were incomplete.

She remembered the legal documents.

She remembered the day the Sterling family had confirmed her identity.

But she had never asked about the name used before then.

“Why are you asking me that?”

Richard spoke quietly.

“Because I think we may have been looking for you under the wrong name.”

The call ended.

Victoria remained motionless.

On her laptop, the old school photograph showed a sixteen-year-old girl.

Underneath it was a name.

**LILY SHAW.**

**Unknown Location — 11:47 p.m.**

The woman opened her locked drawer.

She removed the birth registration extract and placed it beneath the desk lamp.

Her phone vibrated.

**Associate:** Peter Shaw has agreed to meet the Vespers tomorrow.

She read the message.

Then another arrived.

**Associate:** Victoria has found the school photograph.

The woman closed her eyes.

For years, the records had remained separate.

The children had been given different names.

Different homes.

Different lives.

But one name had survived every attempt to bury it.

She opened a second drawer and removed an old photograph.

A little girl stood beside a man outside the Ashford Lane charity.

On the back, in Jonathan Vale's handwriting, were five words:

*She deserves her own life.*

The woman placed the photograph beside the registration extract.

Then she made a call.

“It's time,” she said.

The person on the other end asked a question.

The woman looked at the name on the document.

“No,” she replied. “Not to the Vespers.”

She paused.

“To Victoria.”
