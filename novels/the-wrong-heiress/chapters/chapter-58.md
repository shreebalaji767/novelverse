# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 58 — The Signature That Wasn't There**

**Friday — 4:18 p.m.**

The hospital's confidential admission register was not missing.

That was the first surprise.

The second was that nobody seemed particularly pleased to have found it.

Dr. Simon Weller stood in the administrative records room, facing Margaret Ellison, Thomas Reed, Dorian Hale and Alistair Vane.

“The register is restricted,” he said.

Margaret held up the hospital's own preservation authorisation.

“So was the patient file. That didn't prevent someone from removing it.”

Weller's expression tightened.

“The register is a different record.”

“Then it should be easier to establish who accessed it.”

“Not necessarily.”

Thomas placed a copy of the relevant payment entry on the table.

“Show us the release entry for M.V.”

Weller looked at the paper.

Then he looked at Vane.

“You authorised preservation. You didn't authorise this investigation.”

“I authorised the preservation of a record,” Vane replied. “I did not authorise anyone to conceal its release.”

Weller unlocked a metal cabinet.

He removed a thick register and placed it on the table.

Margaret turned to the relevant date.

There it was.

**M.V. — Confidential admission.**

Below it:

**Release authorised.**

No signature.

No receiving department.

No destination.

Just a date and a time.

Thomas leaned closer.

“Who entered this?”

“The records clerk,” Weller said.

“Name?”

“Staff identification number.”

“Who was the clerk?”

Weller checked the index.

“E. P. Twelve.”

Hale frowned.

“That isn't a name.”

“It is an identification code.”

“Then find the person attached to it.”

Weller hesitated.

Margaret closed the register.

“Dr. Weller, we have spent nineteen years looking at documents that appear to have been designed to answer every question except the one being asked. Please don't make this another one.”

Weller looked at her.

“I will check the staff archive.”

“Now,” Thomas said.

Weller picked up the register.

“Now.”

**5:02 p.m. — Hospital Records Office**

The staff archive produced a personnel record.

**E. P. 12 — Evelyn Price.**

Former confidential records clerk.

Employment terminated nineteen years ago.

Reason: voluntary resignation.

No forwarding address.

No disciplinary record.

No indication that she had been questioned about the admission register.

Hale read the personnel summary twice.

“She processed the release?”

“According to the register,” Weller said.

“Did she authorise it?”

“No. Her identification code was used to record it.”

“Those are different things.”

“Yes.”

“Did she have the authority to release confidential patient records?”

“Only with written approval from the designated custodian.”

Thomas pointed to the blank field.

“Which was never completed.”

Weller said nothing.

Margaret turned the page.

“Where is the approval?”

“There is no approval attached.”

“Then how was the release accepted?”

Weller looked uncomfortable.

“The hospital had an emergency administrative procedure.”

“What emergency?”

“A record could be released if the custodian's authority was independently verified.”

“By whom?”

“The hospital administrator.”

“And who was the administrator?”

Weller's face became guarded.

“I was.”

Silence.

Hale slowly put the personnel record down.

“So you approved the release.”

“I verified the request.”

“On what basis?”

“I cannot recall.”

“Convenient.”

Weller's voice sharpened.

“I have administered thousands of records. You expect me to remember one entry from nineteen years ago?”

“No,” Hale replied. “I expect you to remember why you authorised a release without a named custodian.”

Weller stared at him.

“I did not say I authorised it.”

“You said you verified it.”

“I verified the authority presented to me.”

“Then where is it?”

Weller had no answer.

**6:11 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Felix had found Evelyn Price.

Not her current address.

Not her telephone number.

Her old employment history.

“She's listed as having worked at the hospital for eleven months,” he announced.

Maximilian looked at the screen.

“Any other employment?”

“Two temporary administrative positions. Both ended within six months.”

“Why?”

“No reason given.”

Damian stood beside him.

“Could she have been working for someone else?”

“Possibly. Her employment history doesn't establish that.”

Cassian leaned against the desk.

“Perhaps she was paid to make the entry.”

“Perhaps,” Felix said. “But that would be a guess.”

Cassian smiled.

“Look at you. Resisting speculation.”

“I have been instructed to do that.”

“By Maximilian?”

“By several people.”

Maximilian ignored them.

“Find the original staff identification log. If E. P. 12 was used, the system should show whether it was her card.”

Felix opened another archive.

“The hospital's old system didn't retain digital access histories for that period. But it did keep a manual issue register.”

Damian looked at him.

“Where?”

“Hospital security archive.”

“Then request it.”

Felix nodded.

A moment later, his expression changed.

“What?”

“The card issued to E. P. 12 was returned.”

“When?”

“Three days before the M.V. release entry.”

Maximilian leaned forward.

“Who signed the return?”

Felix enlarged the scan.

The signature was faint.

But the name was legible.

**M. VOSS.**

Cassian stopped smiling.

**7:20 p.m. — Private Study**

Catherine Vesper read the scan in silence.

Octavian stood behind her.

Isolde sat beside the fireplace.

Lucian and Helena had joined them, along with the four brothers.

The room had become crowded with people who were increasingly tired of discovering that the past had left paperwork.

Catherine placed the document on the desk.

“I did not return that card.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Could it have been Elena?”

“I don't know.”

“Did she have access to your credentials?”

“She had access to my name. That is not the same thing.”

Felix spoke carefully.

“The card was issued to Evelyn Price. It was returned under M. Voss. That doesn't prove the person who returned it was you or Elena.”

“Correct,” Catherine said.

Lucian examined the date.

“Three days before the release.”

“And the photograph of the person in the corridor was taken on the day of the release,” Felix added.

Maximilian folded his arms.

“Someone may have returned the card, then used it later.”

Damian shook his head.

“Or the return was recorded falsely. The physical card may never have been returned.”

Isolde looked at the register.

“Who had access to the card issue records?”

“Hospital security,” Felix answered. “And the records administrator.”

“Weller.”

“Yes.”

Octavian's expression darkened.

“Bring him back.”

Catherine raised a hand.

“No.”

Everyone turned towards her.

“If Weller is involved, confronting him again will only make him cautious. If he is not involved, threatening him will make him less willing to help.”

Octavian regarded her.

“And what do you suggest?”

“Find Evelyn Price.”

Felix nodded.

“I can trace her employment records further.”

“Not just employment records,” Catherine said. “Find out who paid her, where she lived, and whether she had any connection to the people handling the trust.”

Cassian glanced at the document.

“And if she was simply a clerk who followed an instruction?”

“Then we will discover that.”

**8:03 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria was reading an email from her employer when her phone rang.

Marcus.

She answered.

“Please tell me nobody has discovered another secret register.”

“Unfortunately, we have.”

Aria closed her eyes.

“I was joking.”

“I know.”

“What does it say?”

“An old hospital access card issued to a records clerk was returned under the name M. Voss three days before the confidential file was released.”

“Catherine?”

“We cannot establish that.”

“Her daughter?”

“We cannot establish that either.”

“Someone pretending to be one of them?”

“Also possible.”

Aria considered the possibilities.

“So we have a clerk, a card, a missing file, a hospital administrator who can't remember approving its release, and a signature that may belong to someone who wasn't there.”

“Yes.”

“That's a lot of uncertainty for one small file.”

Marcus was quiet for a moment.

“Catherine wants to speak with you.”

“About the investigation?”

“Yes.”

“Tomorrow.”

“I'll arrange it.”

“And Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“No one comes to collect me.”

“I'll make sure of it.”

“Good.”

She ended the call.

Then she returned to her email.

The hospital's nineteenth-year-old administrative mystery could wait until tomorrow.

Her employer had asked her to correct a spreadsheet.

For once, the spreadsheet had a name attached to every entry.

**Saturday — 8:35 a.m.**

Evelyn Price's former address was an apartment building that had been demolished six years earlier.

Her last known telephone number belonged to a disconnected landline.

Her employment records offered nothing else.

Felix was about to close the search when a notification appeared.

A scanned document had been recovered from the hospital's off-site archive.

It was an old request for a change to the confidential records access list.

The request was dated four days before the M.V. release.

The form listed two changes.

**E. P. 12 — Access suspended.**

**Temporary replacement — M. VOSS.**

At the bottom was the authorising signature.

**S. WELLER.**

Felix stared at it.

He called Maximilian.

“Found something.”

**9:10 a.m. — Vesper Estate**

The document was placed before Octavian.

Weller's signature was clear.

His authorisation had temporarily replaced Evelyn Price's access with an account registered under M. Voss.

The change had been made four days before the confidential file was released.

Damian read the form.

“Weller claimed the M. Voss release was verified through an emergency procedure.”

“Yet he had already created the access account,” Maximilian said.

Cassian studied the wording.

“An account, not a person.”

“Exactly,” Felix replied.

Isolde looked at Catherine.

“Could you have requested it?”

“No.”

“Could Elena?”

“I don't know.”

Octavian turned towards Felix.

“Can we establish who used the account?”

“Not from this form. The access log identifies the account, not the individual.”

“Then find the person.”

Felix hesitated.

“There is one more thing.”

He enlarged the bottom section.

The replacement account had not been created by the hospital's standard records department.

It had been created through an external administrative terminal.

The terminal identifier was visible.

**ELLISON HOUSE — LA-01.**

Nobody spoke.

Catherine slowly looked at the document.

Margaret Ellison's old terminal.

The same terminal used to access the legacy authority.

The same reference that had appeared on the original allocation reversal.

And now it had been used to create a hospital access identity under the name M. Voss.

Octavian's voice was quiet.

“Who had access to Ellison House?”

Felix looked at the list.

“Margaret Ellison. The executive custodian. And anyone granted temporary access through the legacy authority.”

Margaret's name sat at the top.

But beside it was another entry.

**Temporary authority holder: ELIAS VESPER.**

Catherine stared at the screen.

“Elias.”

Lucian looked towards Octavian.

“His authority was active at the time.”

“Yes,” Octavian said.

“But that doesn't prove he created the account,” Maximilian added.

Catherine nodded.

“No. It proves the terminal was used under an authority that could have belonged to him.”

Octavian remained motionless.

For years, Elias had been the dead brother whose signature appeared in places it should not have been.

Now his authority had appeared in another place.

And this time, the document did not merely bear his name.

It showed where the instruction had originated.

**Ellison House.**

**LA-01.**

**Temporary authority holder: Elias Vesper.**
