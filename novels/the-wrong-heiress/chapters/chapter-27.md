# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 27 — The Man Who Had Been Paid to Disappear**

**Saturday — 7:35 a.m.**

Daniel Mercer had been missing for twelve years.

That was the first problem.

The second was that he had apparently been very good at it.

Felix stared at three monitors in the Vesper Tower research division.

One showed Mercer's hospital employment photograph.

Another showed a timeline of his known employment.

The third displayed a list of financial transactions that had been recovered from an old Northbridge archive.

“Nothing,” Felix muttered.

Marcus stood behind him.

“Nothing at all?”

“Not nothing. Just nothing useful.”

“That sounds reassuring.”

Felix pointed at the screen.

“Mercer disappeared from the hospital payroll, reappeared under Northbridge Special Projects, transferred to Asterion, and then vanished from every ordinary employment record. His salary payments stopped. His bank account was closed. His tax records became inactive.”

“Did he die?”

“No death certificate.”

“Change his identity?”

“Possibly.”

“Leave the country?”

“Possibly.”

Marcus considered the screen.

“Can we establish which possibility is most likely?”

Felix turned around.

“Yes.”

Marcus waited.

“Not yet.”

“Excellent.”

Felix frowned.

“You don't sound pleased.”

“I have spent my adult life learning not to celebrate the word possibly.”

**8:20 a.m. — Vesper Tower, Executive Floor**

The family gathered around the conference table.

Maximilian had reviewed the preliminary report.

Damian had reviewed the security implications.

Cassian had reviewed the financial trail.

Octavian had reviewed everything.

Aria arrived last, carrying a paper bag.

Helena looked at it.

“What's that?”

“Breakfast.”

Cassian brightened.

“For everyone?”

“For me.”

His expression fell.

Aria sat down.

“You can order your own.”

“I was hoping family affection had progressed further.”

“It has. You can now order without asking Marcus.”

Marcus closed his notebook.

“I would appreciate that.”

Octavian gestured towards the empty chair.

“Sit, Aria.”

She took out a sandwich.

“What's the latest?”

Felix projected the timeline.

“Mercer received Infant B at the hospital. Three days later, the temporary reference was closed. Eight days after the transfer, he was moved to Northbridge Special Projects. Eight months later, he was transferred to Asterion.”

“Who authorised those transfers?” Aria asked.

“Both records refer to M. Voss.”

Cassian leaned forward.

“Convenient.”

“Very,” Maximilian said.

“Too convenient?” Aria asked.

Cassian nodded.

“Possibly. We have no proof that M. Voss was the person making every decision. The name could have been used by several people.”

Octavian looked at Felix.

“Find the authorisation chain.”

“I am trying.”

“Try more effectively.”

Felix looked wounded.

“I am already trying at maximum effectiveness.”

Damian glanced at him.

“Then increase your maximum.”

“That's not how maximum works!”

Aria took a bite of her sandwich.

“Maybe let him finish before you break mathematics.”

Cassian smiled.

“She's been here long enough to understand our management style.”

Maximilian ignored him.

“There's another issue. The person who sent Aria those messages knows we're looking for Mercer.”

“Which means someone is monitoring the investigation,” Damian said.

Marcus nodded.

“We have confirmed attempts to access Anne Porter's old personnel file through an inactive administrative gateway. The same gateway was used to request Mercer's archived employment records.”

“Can we identify the user?” Lucian asked.

“No. The request was rejected before authentication completed. The originating credentials were no longer valid.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Then someone is still using the old system.”

Felix nodded.

“And I may have found where.”

**9:14 a.m. — Vesper Tower, Research Division**

Felix displayed a corporate registration record.

**Bellweather Corporate Services.**

“Bellweather again,” Cassian said.

“Yes. It handled administrative records for Greybridge, Northbridge, and several other companies connected to the hospital arrangement.”

Maximilian studied the document.

“Is it still operating?”

“No. It was dissolved eleven years ago.”

“Who owned it?”

Felix enlarged the registration page.

“Nominally, a man named Peter Hargrove. But the beneficial ownership declaration was filed through a corporate service provider. The original records were transferred to an archive company before dissolution.”

“Which company?” Marcus asked.

Felix changed the screen.

**Crestline Administrative Partners.**

The room became quiet.

Aria looked at the name.

“That's the company Richard used.”

“Correct,” Felix said. “Crestline appears in the Sterling payment records, the Bellweather ownership chain, and the Northbridge administrative arrangements.”

Cassian leaned back.

“So the same administrative company was connected to the hospital records, the courier arrangements, and the people who handled Mercer.”

“That's what the documents suggest,” Maximilian said.

“Can we identify the person who actually controlled it?”

Felix opened another file.

“Not yet. But Crestline used an external records custodian. That custodian received several boxes of corporate documents before the company dissolved.”

“Where are they?” Octavian asked.

“Halden Records Management.”

Damian stood.

“I'll arrange retrieval.”

“Through legal channels,” Lucian reminded him.

Damian looked at his father.

“I know.”

Cassian whispered to Aria, “He says that every time.”

Damian heard him.

“I can still hear you.”

“Excellent hearing is essential in security.”

**10:47 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria had returned to work.

The previous day's investigation had occupied most of her thoughts, but she had no intention of letting it interfere with her responsibilities.

Peter Collins placed a folder on her desk.

“Could you review these supplier agreements?”

“Certainly.”

“There's an inconsistency between the payment schedule and the contract dates.”

Aria opened the folder.

“How many?”

“Three.”

She looked up.

“Three?”

“Unfortunately.”

“Is this becoming a habit?”

Peter smiled.

“I was hoping you would say that.”

She began working.

For the next hour, she compared invoices, contract amendments, and payment records.

It was ordinary work.

Clear rules. Verifiable documents. Problems that could be solved by checking the right figures.

She preferred it to discovering that a missing infant had been transferred through a network of companies whose names sounded like expensive furniture.

At 12:05, she finished the first agreement.

At 12:31, she finished the second.

At 1:02, she found the inconsistency in the third.

Peter reviewed her notes.

“Good work.”

“Thank you.”

“You're getting faster.”

“I'm becoming familiar with the mistakes.”

“That's how accounting works.”

Aria returned to her desk.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Marcus.

*We have located the archive containing Bellweather's remaining records. Retrieval is being arranged.*

She replied:

*Please tell me if anything directly identifies who controlled the company.*

Then she put her phone away.

There was still a payment schedule to finish.

**2:16 p.m. — Halden Records Management**

The archive retrieval took place under formal legal authority.

A senior records officer presented the sealed inventory.

Maximilian, Marcus, and a legal representative reviewed the documentation before opening the boxes.

The first contained routine corporate filings.

The second contained payment registers.

The third contained correspondence.

Cassian examined the register.

“Crestline had more clients than we expected.”

“Most were ordinary administrative accounts,” Marcus said.

“Most?”

Cassian pointed to several entries marked with restricted client codes.

“Look at these.”

The entries included:

- PRIV-07
- CA-17
- SP-09
- ROUTE C
- SPECIAL CUSTODY

Marcus looked at the legal representative.

“Can we copy these records?”

“Yes. They are included in the authorised retrieval order.”

Cassian turned a page.

Then stopped.

“Interesting.”

Maximilian looked at him.

“What?”

“Crestline paid an individual for administrative consulting.”

“Who?”

Cassian placed the page on the table.

**MARA VOSS.**

The payment dates stretched across several months.

Some were made before the births.

Others were made afterward.

One entry was different.

It was marked:

**FINAL PROCESSING — SUBJECT 01**

The amount was substantially larger than the earlier payments.

Marcus read the description twice.

“Could this be the second transfer payment?”

“Possibly,” Cassian said.

Maximilian looked at the next page.

There was a handwritten annotation.

*Payment released only upon confirmation of receiving authority.*

And beneath it:

*Confirmation outstanding.*

Cassian's expression changed.

“That's important.”

Marcus photographed the document.

The payment had never been released.

**3:08 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix received the scanned document.

He immediately compared it with the existing financial records.

“It's consistent.”

Maximilian stood behind him.

“Explain.”

“Crestline was expected to release a final payment after receiving confirmation that Subject 01 had reached the designated receiving authority. That confirmation never arrived.”

“Which means?”

“The transfer was not completed. The payment remained outstanding.”

Cassian entered the room.

“Or someone prevented the confirmation from reaching Crestline.”

Felix nodded.

“Exactly.”

Marcus looked at the handwritten note.

“Who was supposed to receive the payment?”

Felix enlarged the document.

“There's a beneficiary reference, but the name field is blank.”

“Can it be recovered?”

“I'm checking the original ledger.”

A moment later, another field appeared.

**BENEFICIARY ACCOUNT: MV-04**

Cassian frowned.

“M. Voss?”

“Not necessarily,” Felix replied. “It is only an account reference. We need the original account register.”

Octavian entered.

“What have you found?”

Maximilian summarised the discovery.

Octavian looked at the document.

“Someone was paid to arrange the transfer.”

“Yes,” Cassian said.

“And the final payment depended on the child reaching the destination.”

“Yes.”

Octavian's eyes narrowed.

“Then the person who prevented the transfer also prevented the payment.”

No one answered.

It was the first explanation that connected the missing confirmation, the cancelled admission, and the unpaid account.

But it raised another question.

Who had stopped the transfer?

**4:52 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard finally opened the notebook.

He read the entries again.

The original instruction.

The two placements.

The warning that neither family should know the other child had been withheld.

Then he turned to the last page.

A name appeared beside a date.

**DANIEL MERCER — COLLECTION CONFIRMED.**

Richard stared at it.

He had written the entry himself twelve years earlier.

At the time, he had not understood what it meant.

Now he understood one thing.

Mercer had collected the child.

He picked up his phone and called Marcus.

The call connected after two rings.

“Mr. Sterling?”

“I have something else.”

Marcus sat upright.

“What is it?”

“My notebook contains a reference to Daniel Mercer collecting the infant.”

“Does it include a date?”

“Yes.”

“Can you send us a photograph?”

“No.”

A pause.

“Why not?”

“Because I don't trust electronic copies of this document anymore.”

Marcus understood.

“Would you be willing to bring the original to our legal office?”

Richard looked towards the study door.

“Yes.”

“Tomorrow?”

“Yes.”

“Mr. Sterling, I need to ask whether anyone has threatened you.”

Richard was silent.

Then he said, “Yes.”

Marcus's voice became colder.

“Who?”

“I don't know.”

“Did they identify themselves?”

“No.”

“Did they threaten your family?”

“They told me to keep the notebook hidden.”

Marcus glanced at the conference room.

“Please don't travel alone tomorrow. We can arrange a secure meeting.”

“I don't want your security personnel at my house.”

“Then we can arrange a neutral location.”

Richard looked down at the notebook.

“Fine.”

He ended the call.

For the first time in twelve years, he had decided to stop protecting the document.

**6:13 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a message.

*Richard Sterling has contacted Marcus Vale. He intends to surrender the notebook.*

She read it.

Then she called someone.

“Did you retrieve the original?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“He hasn't brought it out of the house.”

“Then retrieve it before tomorrow.”

A pause.

“And if he refuses?”

The woman looked at the old photograph on her desk.

It showed a hospital corridor.

A nurse.

A man standing near an administrative exit.

And a small bundle in his arms.

“Do not damage the notebook,” she said.

“Understood.”

“And do not approach the girl.”

The person on the other end hesitated.

“Which girl?”

The woman ended the call.

**8:36 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria had come for dinner.

Helena had prepared a simple meal, although the dining table suggested that the word simple had been interpreted generously.

Aria looked at the dishes.

“How many people are coming?”

“Only family,” Helena replied.

Aria counted the chairs.

“That's a suspicious answer.”

Cassian pulled out a chair.

“Sit down before she starts counting the spoons.”

Felix arrived carrying a laptop.

Maximilian looked at him.

“Not during dinner.”

“I have a new finding.”

“After dinner.”

“It may be important.”

“Is it urgent?”

Felix hesitated.

“Possibly.”

Aria sat down.

“Then it's probably not urgent.”

Felix looked personally betrayed.

Damian took the laptop from him.

“After dinner.”

Felix sat down.

Octavian watched the exchange.

“Good. We can eat without discussing missing documents for twenty minutes.”

Cassian smiled.

“Grandfather, your optimism is inspiring.”

The meal began.

For a while, the conversation remained ordinary.

Helena asked Aria about work.

Lucian asked whether Peter Collins was treating her fairly.

Maximilian reminded her that she could request help if her workload became unreasonable.

Aria assured him she was capable of asking for help herself.

Damian asked whether she had received any further threatening messages.

“No.”

“Good.”

Cassian looked around the table.

“Has anyone considered that we have turned dinner into a security review?”

“Only you,” Damian replied.

“I am the only one brave enough to say it.”

Aria smiled.

Then her phone vibrated.

A message from Marcus.

*Mr. Sterling has agreed to surrender the notebook tomorrow. We may finally establish what he knew about the original instruction.*

She read it and put the phone away.

Helena noticed.

“Something important?”

“Richard is giving them another document.”

Helena nodded.

“That's good.”

“Maybe.”

Aria looked at the food.

“Can we finish dinner before Felix announces another discovery?”

Felix raised his head.

“I haven't announced anything.”

“Yet.”

He closed his mouth.

Cassian looked impressed.

“Remarkable. She has achieved what three senior executives could not.”

**11:19 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard was asleep.

The notebook was no longer in the drawer.

He had placed it inside a locked briefcase beside his bed.

At 11:19, the telephone rang.

He woke immediately.

Unknown number.

He answered.

“You made a mistake.”

Richard sat up.

“I am giving them the notebook.”

“You don't understand what you're handing over.”

“I understand enough.”

“You will destroy your family.”

Richard looked at the briefcase.

“My family was already destroyed.”

The caller was silent.

Then:

“Tomorrow, no one will come to collect anything.”

Richard's expression changed.

“What does that mean?”

The call ended.

He immediately dialled Marcus.

No answer.

He tried again.

Then he sent a message.

*The meeting must be moved forward. Someone knows about the notebook.*

He sat on the edge of the bed, staring at the briefcase.

Outside, a car passed the mansion.

Richard stood and went to the window.

A dark vehicle was parked across the road.

Its headlights were off.

He watched it for several seconds.

Then it drove away.

**11:47 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received Richard's message.

He read it once.

Then again.

He called Damian.

“Richard Sterling has been threatened again. We need to secure the notebook before tomorrow.”

Damian was already standing.

“Where is he?”

“At Sterling Mansion.”

“I'm going.”

“Wait for legal clearance.”

Damian looked at him.

“Marcus.”

“I know.”

“Then don't waste time.”

Marcus called Octavian.

Within minutes, the Vesper security team had begun coordinating a lawful protective response.

But before the first vehicle could leave the tower, another message arrived.

It was from Felix.

**URGENT — OLD ARCHIVE MATCH FOUND.**

Marcus opened the attachment.

It contained a scanned transport record.

A vehicle registration.

A delivery authorisation.

And a destination.

Not Vesper Life Sciences.

Not Northstar Document Custody.

Not any hospital.

The destination field contained a private address.

The address had been registered to a company that no longer existed.

But the company's former director was still alive.

Marcus stared at the screen.

Then he called Octavian.

“Sir.”

“What is it?”

“We have found where Daniel Mercer was instructed to take Infant B.”

A pause.

“Where?”

Marcus looked at the address again.

“An estate outside the city. It was owned by a private family trust.”

Octavian's voice became very quiet.

“Whose trust?”

Marcus opened the final page.

The original beneficiary declaration was attached.

He read the name.

Then looked up.

“Richard Sterling's family trust.”

Silence.

“Sterling.”

**The child had not been taken to the Vespers.**

**The child had been taken to the Sterlings.**
