# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 6 — The Price of an Ordinary Life**

**Tuesday — 8:15 a.m.**

Aria Sterling had discovered three things about living alone.

First, plumbing was apparently held together by optimism.

Second, furniture manufacturers had a personal vendetta against people who assembled things without professional assistance.

Third, being unemployed was becoming expensive.

She sat at her small kitchen table, staring at her laptop.

Her bank balance was not alarming.

Yet.

But it was moving in a direction she disliked.

Aria opened a job-search website.

The first listing was for an administrative assistant.

The second was for a junior accounts clerk.

The third required five years of experience, three professional certifications, and the ability to work under pressure.

Aria stared at the screen.

"Five years of experience for an entry-level salary. Ambitious."

She clicked the next listing.

A small accounting firm near the city centre was looking for an assistant. The salary was modest, the hours were reasonable, and the requirements were surprisingly normal.

She submitted her application.

Then she made breakfast.

For the first time in several days, something in her life had happened without involving a billionaire, a mysterious family, or an unnecessary security arrangement.

She considered this progress.

**Vesper Estate — 9:00 a.m.**

Marcus Vale entered Octavian Vesper's private study carrying a folder.

The patriarch was reading a financial report.

"Progress?" Octavian asked.

"Some."

"Define some."

"We located Daniel Mercer's last confirmed employer."

Octavian closed the report.

"Go on."

"After leaving the hospital, Mercer worked for a private administrative consultancy. The company handled temporary staffing and records-management contracts for several medical institutions."

"Who owned it?"

"At the time, a holding company registered overseas. Its beneficial ownership records are incomplete."

"Convenient."

"Very."

Damian stood beside the window, reading a separate report on his tablet.

"The consultancy dissolved eleven months after Mercer's departure. Its registered director claims he never met Mercer."

"Do we believe him?" Lucian asked.

"No," Damian replied. "But we cannot prove he is lying."

Octavian looked at the folder.

"Money?"

"We found two payments to Mercer. Both came through separate intermediary accounts. One was a consultancy fee. The other was recorded as relocation assistance."

"Amounts?"

"Neither was extraordinary. Together, they were enough to make a temporary administrator comfortable leaving his job and moving elsewhere."

Maximilian leaned forward.

"Can we trace the accounts?"

"We can trace the first two transfers," Damian said. "The money passed through a legitimate payroll service before reaching Mercer. The original funding source is obscured by an investment firm's client account."

Cassian raised an eyebrow.

"Someone spent money to make a hospital administrator disappear into an ordinary employment history."

"Or someone paid for an unrelated service and Mercer happened to be involved," Maximilian said.

Damian nodded.

"That is why we're not making accusations."

Octavian tapped the folder once.

"Find the original funding source. Quietly. Through lawful channels where possible."

He paused.

"And Aria?"

Marcus answered immediately.

"She has not requested anything from us. She is managing her own affairs."

"Good."

"She also submitted a job application this morning."

The room went quiet.

Cassian blinked.

"A job application?"

"Yes."

"At what company?"

Marcus checked his notes.

"A small accounting firm."

Felix looked up from his tablet.

"Why?"

Marcus slowly turned towards him.

"Because she wants a job."

"I understand the words. I don't understand the decision."

"That," Cassian said, "is probably why she is more qualified to run a normal life than you are."

Felix frowned.

Octavian's expression softened.

"Let her."

Marcus nodded.

"Of course."

"And Marcus?"

"Yes, sir?"

"Do not purchase the accounting firm."

Marcus closed his eyes for one second.

"I wasn't planning to."

"Good. I wanted to make sure."

**Sterling Mansion — 10:40 a.m.**

Victoria Sterling was unhappy.

This was not unusual.

What made the situation unusual was that she had decided the entire household should be aware of it.

She stood in the breakfast room, scrolling through her phone.

Richard Sterling read the morning financial paper.

Eleanor was speaking to a household manager.

Adrian had joined a video conference.

Victoria sighed.

No one reacted.

She sighed again.

Richard turned a page.

"Is something wrong?"

"Several things."

"Such as?"

Victoria lowered her phone.

"I still haven't received a proper response from the Vesper family."

Richard put down his paper.

"Victoria, you met their secretary. She explained the situation."

"She explained nothing. She merely informed me that Aria had been verified as their granddaughter."

"That is the truth."

Victoria looked offended.

"You're taking her side?"

"I'm stating a fact."

Victoria folded her arms.

"She has no idea how to behave around people of their standing. She probably doesn't even understand what their family represents."

Eleanor looked at her daughter.

"Then perhaps you should stop worrying about her."

"I'm not worried."

"You're discussing her every morning."

Victoria opened her mouth.

Then closed it.

That was an inconvenient observation.

"I simply don't want people making assumptions."

"About what?" Adrian asked, ending his call.

"About who belongs in important circles."

Adrian studied her.

"Then earn your place in them."

Victoria's eyes narrowed.

"I intend to."

She picked up her phone and walked out.

Richard watched her leave.

Eleanor quietly returned to her conversation.

Neither of them noticed that Victoria had already opened the website of the Vesper Foundation.

She had found an upcoming public event.

The annual Vesper Foundation Global Medical Initiative would be held in three weeks.

Attendance was by invitation.

Victoria smiled.

An invitation was not impossible.

And she was beginning to understand that simply waiting for important people to notice her was an inefficient strategy.

**Aria's Apartment — 1:12 p.m.**

Aria was eating lunch when her phone rang.

Marcus.

She answered.

"Hello?"

"Good afternoon, Miss Aria."

"Is something on fire?"

"No."

"Has another relative appeared?"

"No."

"Then this is already a better call than the last few."

Marcus paused.

"I wanted to inform you that the investigation has progressed."

Aria put down her fork.

"What did you find?"

"A hospital administrator named Daniel Mercer had temporary access to the hospital's records system shortly before your birth. We have identified two payments he received after leaving the hospital."

"Do you know who paid him?"

"Not yet."

"Do you know why?"

"Not yet."

"Do you know where he is?"

"We are still establishing his current whereabouts."

Aria leaned back.

"So you have discovered that a man existed, worked somewhere, and received money."

"That is a rather severe summary."

"Is it inaccurate?"

Marcus considered this.

"No."

"Then keep looking."

"We will."

Aria was quiet for a moment.

"Marcus?"

"Yes?"

"Don't tell me things just to make me feel included. Tell me when there's something reliable."

"I understand."

"And don't keep anything important from me because you think it might upset me."

Marcus's expression changed.

"I will not."

"Good. I appreciate the update."

She ended the call and returned to lunch.

For several seconds, she stared at her plate.

Someone had altered hospital records before she was old enough to speak.

Someone had paid a man who disappeared afterwards.

She did not know what that meant.

But she knew one thing.

Whatever had happened, it had happened to her.

She was entitled to know why.

**Vesper Tower — 3:30 p.m.**

Maximilian Vesper was reviewing a quarterly report when Marcus entered his office.

"Your grandfather wants an update on the hospital consultancy."

"I've already read the preliminary report."

"Then you know the company had another major client."

Maximilian looked up.

"Which one?"

"A regional medical records contractor. It handled digitisation projects for several hospitals during the relevant period."

"Was our hospital one of them?"

"Not directly. A subcontractor was."

Maximilian put down his pen.

"Name?"

"Northbridge Data Services."

"Ownership?"

"Currently inactive. Its former directors are being contacted through legal representatives."

Maximilian stood and walked to the window.

"How much of the hospital's records system did they access?"

"We don't know yet. The hospital has retained some old audit records, but the archive is incomplete."

"Then get the original archive."

"We have requested preservation of the remaining records. The hospital's legal team is cooperating, but they won't release patient information without the appropriate authorisation."

"Good. We do this properly."

Marcus nodded.

Maximilian turned back.

"Does Aria know?"

"She knows about Mercer and the payments."

"Nothing about Northbridge?"

"Not yet. There is no confirmed connection beyond the employment trail."

Maximilian nodded.

"Then don't present speculation as fact."

He returned to his desk.

After a moment, he asked:

"Has she agreed to meet us?"

"No."

Maximilian's pen stopped.

"Not even briefly?"

"She hasn't been asked again."

"Why?"

"Because she asked for time."

Maximilian looked at Marcus.

Then, after a pause, he nodded.

"All right."

For a man accustomed to giving instructions and receiving immediate results, waiting was proving to be an unfamiliar exercise.

**Aria's Apartment — 6:18 p.m.**

Aria received an email.

She opened it.

**Application update**

**Subject: Interview Invitation — Junior Accounts Assistant**

She read it twice.

Then she checked the sender.

Then she read it a third time.

"Well."

She placed her phone on the table.

For the first time since leaving the Sterling mansion, she had an appointment that had nothing to do with her birth records.

The interview was scheduled for Thursday at 10:30 a.m.

She replied to confirm.

Then she looked around her apartment.

The crooked bookshelf stood against the wall.

The tap no longer leaked.

The kitchen was small, the furniture inexpensive, and the window overlooked an ordinary street.

It was not impressive.

It was hers.

Aria smiled faintly.

"Thursday, then."

**Vesper Estate — 8:45 p.m.**

Octavian sat in his study with Isolde.

The preliminary investigation report lay between them.

"Northbridge Data Services," Isolde read. "A company that no longer exists."

"Companies disappear every day."

"Not always with their records."

Octavian looked at the report.

"Mercer was given access credentials six days before the births. Someone arranged his position before he was needed."

"And someone ensured the access logs would not tell us what he did."

"Or who instructed him."

Isolde turned another page.

"Do you think the switch was planned from the beginning?"

Octavian remained silent.

Then he answered.

"I think someone knew enough about the birth arrangements to interfere with them."

"That is not the same thing."

"No."

He looked towards the window.

"For twenty years, I believed we had lost a child because someone had taken her from us."

Isolde placed a hand over his.

"And now?"

"Now I wonder how many people knew where she was while we searched."

The old man's expression hardened.

"Find them."

Isolde studied him.

"Without frightening Aria."

"Especially without frightening Aria."

**Sterling Mansion — 9:17 p.m.**

Victoria sat at her desk.

Her laptop displayed the Vesper Foundation event page.

She had found the name of the event coordinator and drafted an email requesting an invitation.

She read it carefully.

Then she deleted the entire message.

It sounded too desperate.

She tried again.

This time, she wrote that she represented the Sterling family's interests in international charitable affairs.

She paused.

The Sterling family had donated to several medical institutions.

That was true.

She had also attended two charity galas.

Also true.

She had never represented the family internationally.

That detail seemed less important.

She clicked send.

Then she leaned back in her chair.

"Let's see how they respond."

Her phone lit up.

An unknown number.

**Unknown:** You are looking in the wrong direction.

Victoria stared at the message.

Her fingers moved over the keyboard.

**Victoria:** Who is this?

No reply.

She waited.

One minute.

Two.

Then another message arrived.

**Unknown:** If you want to understand why Aria was taken into the Vesper family, stop trying to get their attention.

Victoria's expression changed.

She read it again.

Taken into the Vesper family.

The wording bothered her.

Aria had not been taken anywhere.

She had walked into that estate.

Victoria typed.

**Victoria:** What do you know about her?

The message was marked as delivered.

No response followed.

Victoria looked at the screen for a long time.

Then she opened her browser and searched for Daniel Mercer.

The results were almost empty.

She tried Northbridge Data Services.

One outdated business directory appeared.

Victoria frowned.

For the first time, she was not merely irritated by Aria.

She was curious.

And somewhere, someone had just given her a reason to become much more interested.

**11:06 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Damian's secure phone vibrated.

A message from his investigation team.

He opened the attachment.

It contained a scanned document recovered from an archived employment file.

The document was a temporary access authorisation for Daniel Mercer.

The authorisation date was six days before the births.

The signature field was blank.

But at the bottom of the page, beneath the approval section, was a handwritten notation:

**"Approved under private instruction. Do not enter the sponsor's name into the hospital system."**

Damian read it twice.

Then he called Maximilian.

"Wake up."

Maximilian answered after the second ring.

"What happened?"

"We found the authorisation document."

"And?"

"The credentials were not issued through the normal hospital approval process."

A pause.

"Who authorised them?"

"That's the problem."

Damian sent the image.

"The document explicitly says the sponsor's name must not be entered into the system."

Maximilian was silent.

Then he said:

"Send it to Grandfather. Restrict access. Nobody outside the investigation team sees this."

Damian looked at the document again.

For twenty years, the family had been searching for the person who took their heir.

Now they had evidence that someone had arranged the access before the children were born.

And the person behind it had been careful enough to leave no name.

**The investigation had finally found a door.**

It still had no idea who was standing on the other side.

**End of Chapter 6**
