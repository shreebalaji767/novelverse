# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 62 — The Name That Was Never There**

**Saturday — 3:26 p.m.**
.
“My mother's name was Evelyn.”

Octavian's voice cut through the room.

Felix looked up from the courier record.

“I know.”

“Then why did you tell me the recipient was Eleanor Vesper?”

“Because that's what the personnel index returned.”

Octavian pointed at the screen.

“Show me the original entry.”

Felix opened the historical directory.

Three results appeared.

**ELIAS VESPER — Temporary Executive Custodian.**

**EVELYN VESPER — Original Trust Custodian.**

**ELEANOR VESPER — Patriarchal Office, Legacy Records.**

Octavian stared at the third entry.

“Who is that?”

Felix shook his head.

“I don't know. The personnel index contains the name, but there's no employment file attached.”

“Was she an employee?”

“Possibly. Or someone was entered into the system under that name.”

Catherine approached the screen.

“Look at the registration date.”

Felix enlarged the record.

**Created: Twenty-four years ago.**

Catherine's expression changed.

“That was the year the original legacy authority was established.”

Lucian stepped forward.

“The year I signed the authority document.”

Octavian looked at the entry again.

“Find the original registration.”

Felix opened the attached reference.

A message appeared.

ARCHIVE REFERENCE UNAVAILABLE

Then another line appeared beneath it.

**Record transferred to Private Custodial Storage.**

Margaret Ellison went still.

“That's not a normal personnel record.”

Thomas looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because personnel records were never transferred to custodial storage. Only documents relating to the legacy authority were.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“Where was the storage facility?”

Margaret answered quietly.

“Northstar Document Custody.”

**4:02 p.m. — Vesper Estate, Archive Room**

Felix searched the old Northstar records.

The original custody index was still accessible.

He entered the reference.

**NC-2086 / PRIV-07.**

The same archive reference that had appeared in the earlier investigation.

A list of stored documents appeared.

One entry was marked:

**E. VESPER — AUTHORITY RECEIPT.**

Another:

**LA-01 — ORIGINAL CUSTODIAL DECLARATION.**

And a third:

**EX-04 — SEALED ADMINISTRATIVE CORRESPONDENCE.**

Maximilian read the entries.

“Three documents. Same storage reference.”

Catherine pointed at the first.

“Open the authority receipt.”

Felix tried.

Access denied.

He attempted the second.

Access denied.

Then the third.

Access denied.

He leaned back.

“Northstar has retained the records, but the access authority has been withdrawn.”

“By whom?” Damian asked.

Felix checked the status.

“Original custodian.”

Octavian's expression darkened.

“Which original custodian?”

“The system doesn't specify.”

Catherine looked at the screen.

“Then we need the original declaration.”

Margaret nodded.

“Without it, we cannot determine who was authorised to retrieve those documents.”

Thomas looked at the courier receipt.

“And someone already retrieved one.”

**4:47 p.m. — Private Study**

Victoria stood beside the window, speaking on the telephone.

Aria sat at the desk, reading the courier assignment.

The call ended.

“He'll meet me tomorrow,” Victoria said.

Aria looked up.

“Jonathan?”

“Yes.”

“Where?”

“Greyhaven House.”

“Is he coming alone?”

“He said he would.”

Aria considered that.

“Do you believe him?”

Victoria put her phone away.

“I believe he wants to speak to me.”

“That's not quite the same thing.”

“I know.”

She glanced at the receipt.

“Do you think he knew what was in the envelope?”

Aria looked at the document.

“I don't know.”

Victoria's expression tightened.

“He never told me about any of this.”

“He may have thought it was safer not to.”

“Or he may have known more than he admitted.”

“Both are possible.”

Victoria sat down.

“I spent years believing I knew exactly why he took me.”

Aria closed the file.

“You know why he told you he took you.”

Victoria looked at her.

“And now?”

“Now you can ask him whether there's anything else.”

Victoria was quiet.

Then she nodded.

**5:30 p.m. — Black Court Headquarters**

Victor Draven listened to the report without interrupting.

When Thomas finished, Victor looked at the old courier receipt.

“Jonathan Vale delivered an envelope containing confidential medical information to a recipient registered as E. Vesper.”

“Yes,” Thomas replied.

“And the destination was changed after the courier assignment.”

“Yes.”

Victor placed the paper on his desk.

“Who changed it?”

“We don't know.”

“Who gave the instruction?”

“The patriarchal office code.”

Victor's eyes moved towards Vane.

“And you?”

Vane remained still.

“I did not issue the instruction.”

“Did Elias?”

“I cannot confirm that.”

Victor leaned back.

“Then we have an envelope, a courier, a false destination, an unidentified recipient and a dead executive custodian.”

Thomas sighed.

“When you say it like that, it sounds worse.”

“It is worse.”

Victor picked up the receipt.

“Find out whether Jonathan remembers the delivery. Do not approach him before the Vesper family speaks with him.”

Thomas nodded.

Victor added, “And do not let anyone use the Black Court's name to pressure him.”

“Understood.”

Victor looked at the document once more.

“Someone has been moving records through institutions for twenty-four years. I would prefer not to give them another opportunity.”

**Sunday — 9:14 a.m.**

**Greyhaven House**

Jonathan Vale arrived at the appointed time.

Victoria was waiting in the library.

He stopped when he saw her.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Jonathan smiled faintly.

“You look well.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“That's an unusual opening for someone who has been summoned to explain a nineteen-year-old document.”

Jonathan's smile disappeared.

“I suppose it is.”

He sat opposite her.

Aria was present, as were Margaret and Thomas. No security personnel stood inside the room.

Victoria placed the courier receipt on the table.

“Did you deliver this envelope?”

Jonathan studied it.

His eyes moved across the reference number.

Then the destination.

Then the recipient.

“I remember the assignment.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“You remember it?”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I didn't know what was inside.”

“Did you know it was a medical record?”

“I knew it was a sealed envelope from the hospital.”

“Who gave it to you?”

“An administrative courier supervisor.”

“Name?”

Jonathan shook his head.

“I don't remember.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“You remember the envelope but not the person who gave it to you?”

“I remember his face. I don't remember his name.”

Thomas placed a blank sheet of paper in front of him.

“Describe him.”

Jonathan considered it.

“Middle-aged. Tall. Dark hair. A narrow scar near the right side of his chin.”

Margaret looked at Thomas.

Hale's hospital photograph had shown a faint mark near the eyebrow, not the chin.

This was a different description.

Jonathan continued.

“He told me the envelope was going to Northstar Document Custody. I was instructed to deliver it directly and obtain a receipt.”

Victoria pointed to the altered destination.

“Then why does this say Central Women's Hospital?”

Jonathan examined the correction.

“That wasn't on my copy.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“You had another copy?”

“Yes. The original courier assignment.”

“Where is it?”

Jonathan looked at Victoria.

“I kept it.”

**9:42 a.m. — Greyhaven House, Library**

Jonathan retrieved a folder from his vehicle.

Inside was the original courier assignment.

It was faded but intact.

The destination was clearly written:

**NORTHSTAR DOCUMENT CUSTODY.**

The recipient field was blank.

There was no mention of E. Vesper.

Jonathan placed it beside the altered copy.

“The instruction I received was straightforward. Collect the sealed envelope from the hospital. Deliver it to Northstar. Obtain a signature.”

Thomas examined the paperwork.

“Did you deliver it?”

“Yes.”

“Who received it?”

Jonathan hesitated.

“A woman.”

“Name?”

“She signed the receipt.”

“Do you remember it?”

“No.”

Margaret looked at the altered document.

“The hospital copy says you delivered it to Central Women's Hospital.”

Jonathan shook his head.

“I never returned to the hospital.”

Victoria stared at him.

“Then someone changed the destination and used your courier number to create a second delivery record.”

“That seems likely.”

“Why?”

Jonathan looked at her.

“I don't know.”

Victoria's voice dropped.

“Were you carrying my medical records?”

Jonathan's expression changed.

He looked at the envelope reference.

Then at her.

“I don't know.”

“Jonathan.”

“I didn't know you were connected to that hospital record. I was given a sealed envelope. I delivered it to the place I was instructed to deliver it.”

“Did you ever open it?”

“No.”

“Did you ever receive payment?”

“I received my normal courier fee.”

“From whom?”

“Northbridge Data Services.”

Thomas immediately looked at Margaret.

The name had appeared in the earliest part of the investigation.

Northbridge Data Services.

The same company that had handled the first hospital-related courier assignment.

Jonathan continued.

“I kept the assignment because the destination was changed in the system after I completed the delivery. I reported the discrepancy.”

“Who did you report it to?” Thomas asked.

“Northbridge.”

“Did they investigate?”

“They said the duplicate entry would be corrected.”

“Was it?”

“No.”

Victoria stared at him.

“And you never thought to tell me?”

Jonathan met her eyes.

“I didn't know it concerned you.”

**10:16 a.m. — Greyhaven House, Library**

Margaret examined the original assignment.

The document contained a small handwritten notation beside the destination.

**Received — N.C. / 14:32.**

Thomas checked the archive index.

“Northstar's receipt should have the same time.”

Felix, connected remotely, searched the custody records.

A moment later, a scan appeared.

**NC-2086 / PRIV-07.**

The original delivery receipt.

The recipient's signature was visible.

Not E. Vesper.

Not M. Voss.

Not Dorian Hale.

The signature belonged to:

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

Margaret stared at it.

“That isn't my signature.”

Thomas compared the handwriting.

“It resembles yours.”

“It is not mine.”

Jonathan looked at her.

“Then who received the envelope?”

Nobody answered.

Felix enlarged the document.

There was a second notation beneath the signature.

**Custodial transfer accepted under delegated authority.**

The delegation reference was:

**LA-01 / E.V.**

Catherine, who had joined the meeting, stood abruptly.

“Evelyn.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Are you certain?”

“No. But E.V. is the designation used for Evelyn Vesper in the original trust records.”

Margaret stared at the receipt.

“Then someone accepted the envelope using my identity, under Evelyn's custodial authority.”

Thomas looked at the document again.

“And the hospital later recorded a different destination.”

Aria studied the two receipts.

“Then there were two records of the same delivery.”

Felix's voice came through the speaker.

“Possibly two deliveries.”

Everyone turned towards the screen.

He continued.

“The Northstar receipt confirms an envelope was delivered there. The hospital record confirms an envelope was recorded as delivered back to the hospital. We cannot assume they were the same physical envelope.”

Jonathan frowned.

“I only carried one.”

“Then someone may have created a second record without a second delivery,” Felix replied.

Victoria looked at Jonathan.

“Did you see anyone else carrying an envelope?”

“No.”

“Did you see anyone waiting at Northstar?”

Jonathan hesitated.

“Yes.”

“Who?”

“A man standing near the reception desk. He wasn't part of the receiving staff.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“Did you recognise him?”

Jonathan looked at the photograph of Dorian Hale.

“No.”

He studied it for several seconds.

Then he shook his head.

“Not him.”

He pointed at the face.

“The man I saw was older.”

Margaret looked at the signature.

“Could he have been the person who signed for the envelope?”

Jonathan nodded slowly.

“He might have been.”

**11:08 a.m. — Greyhaven House, Private Hallway**

Victoria stepped away from the others.

Jonathan followed her.

“Victoria.”

She stopped.

“Don't call me Lily.”

He paused.

“I'm sorry.”

She looked at him.

“I don't know what you knew. I don't know what you were told. But I need you to understand something.”

Jonathan waited.

“You raised me. You protected me. Whatever happened when I was an infant, I won't pretend those years meant nothing.”

His expression softened.

“But I won't accept that you kept information from me simply because you believed you knew what was best.”

Jonathan lowered his head.

“I understand.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

She looked at him for a long moment.

“Then when you remember something, tell me. Even if it seems unimportant.”

“I will.”

Victoria turned and walked back into the library.

Jonathan remained in the hallway.

For the first time, he seemed to understand that protecting her from the past had not protected their relationship from it.

**12:02 p.m. — Greyhaven House, Library**

Felix sent one final message.

He had located the Northstar document's original storage metadata.

The file had been scanned twice.

The first scan was made on the day Jonathan delivered the envelope.

The second scan was made six weeks later.

The first scan showed Margaret Ellison's forged signature and the delegation reference.

The second contained an additional handwritten note.

**Do not release to either family. Retain until the second transfer is confirmed.**

Below it was another signature.

This one was clear.

**E. VESPER.**

Catherine stared at the screen.

“That is Evelyn's signature.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Are you certain?”

“I saw it on the original trust documents.”

Isolde moved closer.

“Then Evelyn knew about the envelope.”

Catherine nodded slowly.

“Or someone had access to her signature.”

Octavian remained silent.

Aria looked at the note.

“What's the second transfer?”

Thomas answered.

“The transfer that was never completed.”

The room fell quiet.

The old instruction from the earliest hospital records had returned.

Two records were to remain separate until the second transfer was confirmed.

But the second transfer had never been completed.

And now they knew that the envelope connected to those records had been delivered to Northstar under a forged custodial receipt.

Octavian looked at the signature.

“Find the original envelope.”

Felix's voice came through the speaker.

“I've located its storage status.”

“And?”

“Released.”

Margaret's face went pale.

“When?”

Felix checked the record.

“Six weeks after Jonathan's delivery.”

“Who collected it?”

Felix enlarged the final field.

There was no name.

Only a release reference.

**EX-04.**

Octavian stared at the screen.

The same emergency authority that had been used to overturn Martin Hale's audit.

The same authority that had supported the false death registration.

The same authority that had opened the hospital access account.

And now it had been used to remove the envelope containing the original evidence.

Catherine spoke quietly.

“Someone didn't just want the girls separated.”

She looked at the document.

“They wanted the records that could explain why.”

**The envelope had been released under an authority that officially belonged to a dead man.**
