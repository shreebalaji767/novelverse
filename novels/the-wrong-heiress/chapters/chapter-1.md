# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 1 — Congratulations, You're Not Our Daughter**

The Sterling family had invited twelve people to dinner.

Aria Sterling knew something was wrong when she counted thirteen chairs.

She counted again.

Still thirteen.

One chair had been placed at the far end of the dining table, directly opposite Richard Sterling, the head of the family. It was not unusual for important guests to receive special treatment.

It was unusual for the guest to be her.

"Am I being promoted?" Aria asked, pulling out the chair. "Should I prepare a speech?"

Nobody laughed.

That was unfortunate. It had been a perfectly good joke.

Aria sat down and unfolded her napkin.

Across the table, her eldest brother, Adrian, checked his watch. Her second brother, Alexander, was staring at his phone. Sebastian, the youngest of the three, was smiling at something only he understood.

Her mother, Eleanor, adjusted the bracelet around her wrist.

Her father, Richard, remained standing.

Dinner had not started.

That was the second warning.

"Aria," Richard said.

"Yes?"

"We need to discuss something important."

"Is the company bankrupt?"

"No."

"Has Adrian finally been arrested?"

"Aria."

"Then I'm out of guesses."

Richard's expression hardened.

Aria quietly reached for her glass of water.

She was twenty years old. She had grown up in the Sterling household, attended expensive schools, and learned that family dinners were often business meetings with better tablecloths.

She had also learned that when her father said something was important, it usually meant someone was about to have a very unpleasant evening.

She had no idea how unpleasant this one would be.

Richard placed a thick folder on the table.

"Twenty years ago, there was an incident at the hospital where you were born."

Aria stopped drinking.

"What kind of incident?"

"A mistake."

"What kind of mistake?"

Richard opened the folder.

"Two newborn girls were switched."

Silence.

Aria looked at him.

Then at Eleanor.

Then at her brothers.

"All right," she said slowly. "Which one of you was switched?"

Nobody moved.

Her smile faded.

Richard pushed a photograph across the table.

It showed two newborn babies lying in separate hospital bassinets. The photograph was old, slightly faded, and marked with a date.

Aria looked at it.

"That's me?"

"One of them is you."

"Wonderful. We're already off to a promising start."

"Listen carefully," Richard said. "The hospital investigation has confirmed that you are not our biological daughter."

For several seconds, Aria said nothing.

The clock on the wall ticked.

A servant standing near the dining room entrance lowered his eyes.

Aria looked down at the photograph again.

Then she looked up.

"So?"

Richard frowned.

"So?"

"Yes. What happens now?"

Eleanor finally spoke.

"Your biological parents have been identified."

Aria's fingers tightened around the edge of the photograph.

"Are they alive?"

"Yes."

"Do they know?"

"They have been informed."

"Have I met them?"

"No."

Aria nodded once.

"Then I suppose that's something we should arrange."

Richard stared at her.

"You don't seem to understand the situation."

"Then explain it."

"You were raised as a Sterling, but you are not one by blood. The other girl is our biological daughter."

Aria glanced around the table.

"And she's coming here?"

"She has already arrived in the city."

Sebastian finally put his phone away.

"Her name is Victoria."

Aria repeated it quietly.

"Victoria."

"She has had a difficult life," Eleanor said. "We intend to give her everything she should have had."

"That's good."

Richard looked irritated.

"Good?"

"Yes. If she's your daughter, she deserves to meet her family."

Adrian leaned forward.

"You're taking this remarkably well."

Aria looked at him.

"Would you prefer I throw the water?"

"Don't be childish."

"I was considering the expensive vase, but I thought Mother might object."

Sebastian coughed into his hand.

Richard struck the table lightly with his palm.

"Enough."

The room went silent again.

He removed another document from the folder.

"Until the legal arrangements are completed, Victoria will take her rightful place in this family."

Aria waited.

"And?"

"You will need to understand that certain things will change."

"What things?"

"Your position in the household. Your financial arrangements. Your access to family resources."

Aria looked at the document.

There were several pages.

She had not been invited to dinner to hear an explanation.

She had been invited to receive a decision.

"Have you already discussed this with a lawyer?" she asked.

"Of course."

"Have you discussed it with me?"

Richard's jaw tightened.

"We are discussing it now."

"No. You're informing me now."

Alexander finally looked up from his phone.

"Aria, don't make this more difficult than it needs to be."

She turned toward him.

"I haven't done anything yet."

"You're making an issue out of a situation nobody wanted."

"Interesting. I was under the impression that I was one of the people involved."

Eleanor looked uncomfortable.

"Nobody is blaming you."

"That's reassuring."

Richard closed the folder.

"You will continue to receive support until you are settled elsewhere. However, you must accept that this household belongs to Victoria now."

Aria stared at him.

"You're asking me to leave?"

"I'm telling you that the situation has changed."

"That wasn't my question."

Richard held her gaze.

"Yes."

The answer was immediate.

No hesitation.

No apology.

Just one word.

Aria looked at the people around the table.

The father who had taught her to ride a bicycle.

The mother who had sat beside her during childhood illnesses.

The brothers with whom she had fought over television remotes, stolen snacks, and argued about everything from school grades to car keys.

She had never thought of herself as particularly sentimental.

But twenty years was a long time.

And apparently, it was not long enough.

She folded the photograph carefully and placed it on the table.

"All right."

Eleanor blinked.

"Aria..."

"I'll leave."

Richard seemed relieved.

"Good. I expect you to handle this maturely."

Aria stood.

"One question."

"What?"

"Do you want the keys to my car?"

Richard paused.

"Why?"

"Because it's registered under the family company. If I'm no longer part of the family, I assume the car belongs to you."

He exchanged a glance with Adrian.

"Leave it in the garage."

"Fine."

Aria picked up her handbag.

Then she looked at the untouched plates.

"Are we still having dinner?"

Nobody answered.

"Shame," she said. "The food looks expensive."

She walked out.

Behind her, the dining room remained silent.

The next morning, Aria packed.

She owned fewer things than she had expected.

Clothes. Books. A laptop. A few personal belongings. Some photographs.

Twenty years of life fitted into four suitcases and two cardboard boxes.

She paused in front of a framed family photograph.

It had been taken five years earlier during a holiday.

Richard stood in the middle. Eleanor was beside him. Adrian and Alexander looked bored. Sebastian was making a ridiculous face.

Aria stood at the end, laughing at something outside the frame.

She picked it up.

For a moment, she considered leaving it behind.

Then she placed it inside a box.

Not because she wanted to remember the Sterlings.

Because the photograph was hers.

At eleven in the morning, a car arrived to take her to the apartment she had rented.

The Sterling mansion's head housekeeper, Mrs. Bennett, stood near the entrance.

"Miss Aria."

Aria turned.

"Mrs. Bennett."

The older woman looked as though she wanted to say something but could not find the words.

Finally, she held out a small paper bag.

"I made your favourite biscuits."

Aria stared at it.

Then she smiled.

"You're going to make me emotional over biscuits."

Mrs. Bennett's eyes became wet.

"Please take care of yourself."

"I will."

Aria accepted the bag.

"And don't let Sebastian steal the good tea. He has no appreciation for quality."

Mrs. Bennett laughed through her tears.

It was the first genuine laugh Aria had heard since the previous evening.

She placed the bag in her handbag and walked toward the car.

Nobody else came to say goodbye.

That was fine.

At least the biscuits had come.

At 12:17 p.m., Aria Sterling left the Sterling estate.

At 12:18 p.m., Richard Sterling signed the preliminary legal documents recognizing Victoria as his biological daughter.

At 12:21 p.m., Victoria Sterling entered the mansion.

She wore a cream-coloured dress, carried a designer handbag, and walked through the entrance as if the building had been constructed specifically for her arrival.

Eleanor hurried forward.

"Victoria!"

Victoria smiled.

"Mother."

She embraced Eleanor.

Richard approached next.

"My daughter."

Victoria's eyes filled with tears.

"Father."

For a moment, the entire family seemed satisfied.

Then Victoria looked toward the staircase.

"Where is she?"

Eleanor hesitated.

"Who?"

"The girl who was raised here."

Richard answered.

"Aria has left."

Victoria's expression changed.

"Already?"

"She has been given time to settle elsewhere."

Victoria looked around the mansion.

At the paintings.

The furniture.

The staircase.

The servants standing in neat rows.

Then she smiled.

"Good."

Alexander frowned slightly.

"Good?"

Victoria adjusted her handbag.

"I wouldn't want anyone confusing the situation."

Sebastian tilted his head.

"Confusing what?"

"Who belongs here."

She walked toward the staircase.

"Could someone show me my room?"

Eleanor smiled.

"Of course, darling."

Victoria paused.

"And please have the decorations changed. I prefer lighter colours."

The housekeeper lowered her eyes.

"Certainly, Miss Victoria."

Victoria continued upstairs.

Behind her, Richard looked toward the entrance.

For a brief moment, he remembered Aria's final question about the car.

Then he turned away.

**12:43 p.m. — Somewhere far beyond the Sterling estate**

A secure facility beneath an unmarked building received an encrypted transmission.

The message contained three attachments.

A hospital record.

A genetic verification report.

And a photograph.

A man seated behind a dark desk read the report once.

Then again.

His fingers stopped moving.

"Where did this come from?"

The woman standing before him answered carefully.

"The independent verification was completed this morning. The hospital records were recovered from an archived medical repository. We also obtained the original genetic samples."

"Are you certain?"

"Three separate laboratories. Three independent results."

The man looked at the photograph.

A young woman stood outside a modest apartment building, carrying two cardboard boxes.

She was wearing an ordinary shirt and jeans.

Her hair was tied back carelessly.

There was nothing particularly remarkable about the photograph.

Nothing that suggested the person in it was the missing successor of the most powerful private dynasty in the world.

The man slowly stood.

"Notify the Chairman."

"Sir?"

"Immediately."

The woman hesitated.

"Should we inform the family council first?"

"No."

"Then the security director?"

"No."

"Sir, protocol requires—"

"Protocol can wait."

He picked up the secure telephone.

For the first time in twenty years, his voice was unsteady.

"Tell the Chairman we have found her."

**12:51 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian Vesper was in the middle of a chess match when the telephone rang.

He disliked interruptions.

He disliked them even more when he was winning.

"Speak."

The voice on the other end was controlled but urgent.

"Chairman, the verification is complete."

Octavian moved a chess piece.

"And?"

A pause.

"We found your granddaughter."

The piece slipped from his fingers.

It struck the chessboard.

For several seconds, the old man did not speak.

Then he asked a question.

"Where is she?"

"We have her current location."

"Is she safe?"

"Yes, Chairman."

"Does she know?"

"No."

Octavian closed his eyes.

Twenty years.

Twenty years of searching.

Twenty years of false leads, dead ends, bribed officials, sealed records, and people who had disappeared before they could be questioned.

And now—

"Bring her home."

"Should we dispatch the private aircraft?"

Octavian looked toward the window.

Beyond it, the Vesper estate stretched across the hills, its grounds guarded by a security force larger than the Sterling family's entire corporate headquarters.

"No."

The answer surprised the man on the telephone.

"Chairman?"

"Do not frighten her. Do not surround her. Do not make a public announcement. And nobody is to approach her without my permission."

"Understood."

Octavian looked down at the photograph that had just been delivered to his private terminal.

His granddaughter.

Alive.

Somewhere in the world, she was probably eating lunch, worrying about rent, and wondering what to do with the rest of her life.

He pressed a hand against his forehead.

"Inform Lucian and Helena."

"Yes, Chairman."

"And wake the family."

"All of them?"

Octavian looked at the chessboard.

He had forgotten whose turn it was.

"All of them."

**1:06 p.m. — Aria's New Apartment**

Aria stood in the middle of her new living room.

The apartment was small.

The walls were white.

The kitchen was barely large enough for two people.

The bathroom tap made an unpleasant noise.

And the bedroom window overlooked a building whose exterior desperately needed repainting.

She liked it.

It was hers.

She placed the last cardboard box beside the sofa and checked her phone.

Three missed calls from an unknown number.

One message from Sebastian.

Don't do anything stupid. Call me.

Aria stared at the message.

Then she put the phone down.

Her stomach growled.

"Right. Priorities."

She opened the paper bag Mrs. Bennett had given her.

Six biscuits.

She examined them.

"Six? That's a suspiciously precise number."

She ate one.

Then another.

Her phone vibrated again.

Unknown number.

Aria answered.

"Hello?"

A calm male voice spoke.

"Good afternoon. Am I speaking with Miss Aria Sterling?"

"Depends who's asking."

"My name is Marcus Vale. I represent an organization that would like to speak with you regarding a personal matter."

Aria looked at the ceiling.

She had been independent for approximately forty-eight minutes.

Already, someone wanted something from her.

"Is this about the hospital?"

There was a brief silence.

"Yes, Miss."

Aria leaned against the kitchen counter.

"Then I suppose this is going to be a long afternoon."

"Would you be available to meet?"

"Where?"

"That can be arranged according to your convenience."

"Good. Somewhere public."

"Certainly."

"And somewhere with food."

Another pause.

"Food, Miss?"

"I haven't had lunch."

"Of course."

Aria looked at the remaining biscuits.

"And please don't send a limousine. I don't want the neighbours thinking I've joined a criminal organization."

On the other end of the call, Marcus Vale closed his eyes.

He had spent twenty-two years managing the affairs of the Vesper Sovereign Dynasty.

He had negotiated with presidents, corporate chairmen, intelligence chiefs, and people whose names never appeared in public records.

He had never been instructed to arrange a meeting based on the availability of lunch.

"Understood, Miss Sterling."

"Tomorrow is fine."

"Tomorrow. Certainly."

"Goodbye."

The call ended.

Marcus remained perfectly still.

Then he looked at the report on his desk.

**IDENTITY VERIFIED: ARIA VESPER.**

He read the name again.

For twenty years, the world's most powerful family had been searching for its missing heir.

They had finally found her.

And she had just requested a restaurant recommendation.

Marcus slowly reached for his telephone.

"Prepare the family residence."

A voice answered immediately.

"Which arrangements should we make?"

Marcus looked at the photograph.

"All of them."

**End of Chapter 1**
