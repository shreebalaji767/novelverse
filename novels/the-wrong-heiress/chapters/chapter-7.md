# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 7 — An Invitation Nobody Asked For**

**Wednesday — 7:35 a.m.**

Aria had a plan.

It was a simple plan.

Wake up. Eat breakfast. Prepare for Thursday's job interview. Avoid unnecessary complications.

By 7:42 a.m., the plan had already encountered resistance.

Her printer wasn't working.

She stared at it.

The printer stared back in the silent, unhelpful manner of expensive plastic objects.

"You're supposed to print paper."

She pressed the button again.

Nothing.

Aria opened the instruction manual.

The first page contained a warning about electrical safety.

The second page contained a diagram.

The third page contained seventeen steps for connecting the printer to a wireless network.

She closed the manual.

"Tomorrow, I'll buy a new one."

Her phone rang.

Marcus.

Aria looked at the screen.

"Your timing is becoming suspicious."

"Good morning, Miss Aria."

"What happened?"

"Nothing has happened."

"Then why are you calling?"

"I have a request from your grandmother."

Aria paused.

"Which one?"

"Mrs. Isolde Vesper."

"All right."

"She would like to invite you to afternoon tea at the estate on Saturday. Just the two of you, if you prefer."

Aria looked at the printer.

"Is there going to be another sixteen-chair situation?"

"I have been assured there will not."

"Can I bring biscuits?"

There was a brief silence.

"Certainly."

"Then tell her Saturday is fine."

Marcus made a note.

"Would you like transportation arranged?"

"No, thank you."

"Understood."

"And Marcus?"

"Yes?"

"Please don't arrange a printer."

Another silence.

"I wasn't going to."

"Good."

She ended the call.

**Vesper Estate — 8:20 a.m.**

Isolde was having breakfast when Marcus delivered the news.

"She agreed?"

"Yes, ma'am."

"Saturday?"

"Yes."

"And she wants to bring biscuits."

Isolde smiled.

"Wonderful."

Octavian lowered his newspaper.

"What biscuits?"

Marcus looked at him.

"I believe she intends to bring some herself."

"From where?"

"Her apartment, presumably."

Octavian considered this.

"Should we have the kitchen prepare something?"

Isolde gave him a look.

"She is bringing biscuits. We are having tea. We do not need to establish an international catering operation."

"I was asking a simple question."

"You were about to summon six chefs."

Octavian returned to his newspaper.

Marcus decided not to participate.

**Sterling Mansion — 9:10 a.m.**

Victoria received a reply.

She opened the email immediately.

**Vesper Foundation — Events Office**

*Thank you for your interest in the Global Medical Initiative. We appreciate the Sterling family's previous charitable contributions. However, invitations to this year's event are being issued through the Foundation's established selection process. Your request has been forwarded for review.*

Victoria read it.

Then read it again.

Forwarded for review.

Not accepted.

Not rejected.

She decided this was encouraging.

She went downstairs.

Richard was in his office reviewing documents.

"Father."

"Yes?"

"I've contacted the Vesper Foundation."

Richard looked up.

"Why?"

"To discuss charitable cooperation."

"With whom?"

"Their foundation."

Richard put his pen down.

"Victoria, we are not a major partner of the Vesper Foundation."

"We have donated to medical institutions."

"That does not make us their representative."

She looked irritated.

"I know how to conduct myself."

"Then conduct yourself carefully. The Sterlings have business interests to protect."

Victoria's expression cooled.

"Of course. Everything is always about business."

Richard returned to his documents.

"Because business is what keeps this family standing."

Victoria left.

She was beginning to understand that her father would not help her reach the Vespers.

That did not mean she needed his permission.

**Vesper Estate — 11:00 a.m.**

The document found by Damian had been placed inside a secure investigation room.

Maximilian, Damian, Marcus and Felix examined the scanned copy.

Felix enlarged the handwritten notation.

"Look at the ink."

Damian leaned closer.

"What about it?"

"The signature field and the notation were written at different times."

"How can you tell?"

"The handwriting pressure is different. The notation was added after the form had already been completed."

Maximilian studied the page.

"Could it have been added later?"

"Yes. We need the original document to establish that."

Marcus pointed to another detail.

"The document has a reference number."

Felix checked the archive index.

"That number belongs to the hospital's temporary access register."

"Can we retrieve the register?" Maximilian asked.

"Not from the hospital's current system. The old records were transferred to an external archive contractor."

Damian's expression hardened.

"Northbridge?"

Felix nodded.

"Possibly. The transfer inventory lists a batch of hospital administrative records under Northbridge's contract."

Marcus looked at Maximilian.

"So the company that employed Mercer also handled the records that could identify who authorised him."

"That is a connection," Maximilian said. "Not proof of a conspiracy."

Damian nodded.

"I've requested preservation of the remaining archive."

"Good. And no one contacts Mercer directly until we know where he is and what protections may be necessary."

Felix returned to the screen.

"There is something else."

He opened a second file.

"The temporary access register was transferred in two batches. The first batch is accounted for. The second has no receipt confirmation."

"Missing?" Marcus asked.

"Not necessarily. The receiving archive may have failed to record it properly."

Damian stood.

"Find out."

**Thursday — 9:05 a.m.**

Aria stood in front of a mirror.

She wore a pale blue shirt, dark trousers and simple black shoes.

Her hair was neatly arranged.

Her documents were inside a folder.

She checked the address on her phone.

Then she checked it again.

The interview was at 10:30.

She had enough time to take a bus, walk the last few streets and arrive early.

She locked her apartment.

For once, she was going somewhere because she had chosen to.

**Northbridge Business District — 10:17 a.m.**

The accounting firm occupied the third floor of an ordinary office building.

Its sign was modest.

Its reception area was clean.

There were two employees discussing invoices near the entrance.

Aria approached the reception desk.

"Good morning. I have an interview at 10:30."

The receptionist checked her list.

"Aria Sterling?"

"Yes."

"Please take a seat. Mr. Collins will be with you shortly."

Aria sat down.

A few minutes later, a middle-aged man came out of an office.

"Miss Sterling?"

She stood.

"Yes."

"I'm Peter Collins. Please come in."

His office was small, with a desk, two chairs and several filing cabinets.

He offered her a seat.

They discussed her education, basic accounting knowledge, spreadsheet experience and previous responsibilities.

Aria answered honestly.

She had no impressive corporate history.

She was comfortable with numbers, careful with records and willing to learn.

Mr. Collins asked her why she wanted the position.

"I want stable work. I'm comfortable with routine administrative tasks, and I don't mind learning new systems."

He nodded.

"That's a reasonable answer."

He reviewed her application.

"Your previous address is listed as Sterling Mansion."

Aria's expression remained neutral.

"I used to live there."

"May I ask why you left?"

"It's a family matter."

He nodded without pressing.

"Fair enough."

The interview lasted another fifteen minutes.

At the end, he told her they would contact her by Friday.

Aria thanked him and left.

Outside, she checked the time.

10:58.

She had spent less than an hour worrying about whether a small accounting firm would hire her.

It was strangely refreshing.

**Vesper Tower — 11:20 a.m.**

Victoria Sterling arrived at the reception desk.

She wore a tailored cream suit and carried a leather handbag.

"I have an appointment with the Foundation's events department."

The receptionist checked the schedule.

"May I have your name?"

"Victoria Sterling."

The receptionist searched.

"One moment, please."

Victoria waited.

The receptionist made a call.

A minute later, a woman came down from the upper floor.

"Miss Sterling? I'm Clara Bennett, assistant to the events director."

Victoria smiled.

"Finally. I was beginning to think my message had disappeared."

Clara maintained a professional expression.

"Your request was received. We can offer a short discussion about the Foundation's public charitable programmes."

"Excellent."

Victoria followed her upstairs.

The meeting room was small.

There were no senior executives, no family representatives and no elaborate reception.

Clara explained that the Foundation worked with hospitals, universities and regional medical organisations.

Victoria listened.

Then she leaned forward.

"I believe there may be opportunities for cooperation between our families."

Clara looked at her notes.

"Are you proposing a specific charitable contribution?"

Victoria hesitated.

"Not necessarily. I thought it would be useful to establish a relationship."

"Then the best starting point would be a formal proposal from the Sterling Foundation, if your family has one."

Victoria smiled stiffly.

"Of course."

She waited for Clara to say something more.

Nothing came.

"May I ask whether Aria Vesper will attend the upcoming initiative?"

Clara looked up.

"Miss Vesper's attendance is a private family matter. The Foundation cannot comment."

Victoria's fingers tightened around her handbag.

"Naturally."

Clara closed the folder.

"Was there anything else?"

"No."

"Thank you for coming, Miss Sterling."

Victoria left the building with a polite smile.

It lasted until she reached the pavement.

**Vesper Estate — 12:45 p.m.**

Isolde sat in the sunroom with Helena.

A small plate of biscuits sat between them.

Helena picked one up.

"These are lovely."

"They are from the kitchen."

"I know."

Isolde smiled.

"Aria's biscuits will be different."

Helena looked at her.

"You sound very certain."

"She asked if she could bring them. I intend to eat them."

Helena laughed.

For a moment, the two women sat quietly.

Then Helena spoke.

"Do you think she is happy?"

Isolde considered the question.

"I think she is trying to build a life she understands."

"She has been through so much."

"Yes."

"I want to give her everything she missed."

Isolde turned towards her.

"Then begin by giving her the one thing she has asked for."

"What?"

"Room to choose."

Helena nodded.

She knew Isolde was right.

**Friday — 8:40 a.m.**

Aria received an email.

**Subject: Employment Offer — Junior Accounts Assistant**

She opened it.

The accounting firm was offering her the position.

Her starting date was Monday.

She read the salary details carefully.

It was not impressive.

It was enough.

She accepted.

Then she called her landlord to confirm the next month's rent arrangements.

After that, she went shopping for a few things she needed for work.

Her new life was taking shape through ordinary decisions.

She liked that.

**Vesper Estate — 10:15 a.m.**

Damian entered Octavian's study.

"We have located the missing archive transfer."

Octavian looked up.

"Where?"

"The contractor's storage inventory. The records were moved to a secondary facility after Northbridge ceased operations."

"Have they been examined?"

"Not yet. The facility is operated by an independent archive company. We have requested access through the hospital's legal representatives."

"How long?"

"They estimate several days."

Octavian nodded.

"Then we wait."

Damian hesitated.

"There is one additional issue."

"Speak."

"The archive company has confirmed that a portion of the material was removed from the original storage facility before the transfer."

"By whom?"

"They have not identified the person. Their inventory only records that the removal was authorised by a client representative."

Octavian's eyes narrowed.

"Which client?"

"The record lists Northbridge Data Services."

The room became silent.

"Find the representative," Octavian ordered.

**Saturday — 3:00 p.m.**

Aria arrived at Vesper Estate carrying a small paper bag.

Marcus greeted her at the entrance.

"Good afternoon, Miss Aria."

"Hello. I brought biscuits."

Marcus looked at the bag.

"Thank you."

"Don't look so worried. They're not explosive."

"I wasn't concerned about that."

"Good. I'd hate to disappoint you."

He led her towards the sunroom.

Isolde was waiting.

She stood when Aria entered.

"You're here."

"I promised."

Isolde embraced her gently.

"Come and sit."

Aria placed the bag on the table.

"These are from a bakery near my apartment. I wasn't sure what everyone liked."

Isolde opened the bag.

"Then we shall find out."

They sat together.

For a while, they discussed ordinary things.

Aria told her about the apartment, the job she had accepted and the difficulty of assembling furniture.

Isolde listened without interrupting.

"You found work?"

"Yes. I start Monday."

"That is wonderful."

"It's a small accounting firm."

"Work is work. You should be proud."

Aria looked down at her tea.

"Thank you."

Isolde took a biscuit.

Then she asked quietly:

"May I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"Do you feel comfortable here?"

Aria considered the question.

"More than I expected."

Isolde nodded.

"That is enough for now."

Aria looked at her.

"You aren't going to ask me to move in?"

"No."

"Or announce something?"

"No."

"Or introduce me to twenty more relatives?"

Isolde smiled.

"I can promise you that there will not be twenty relatives today."

"That's reassuring."

"However, I cannot promise that your grandfather will never attempt it."

Aria sighed.

"He's the one I should be worried about, isn't he?"

"Most definitely."

They both laughed.

**Vesper Estate — 4:12 p.m.**

Aria was preparing to leave when Isolde accompanied her to the entrance.

A black car was parked near the driveway.

Beside it stood a young man in a dark jacket.

He looked up as they approached.

"Grandmother."

Isolde's expression changed.

"Cassian."

Aria glanced between them.

Cassian smiled.

"Hello. You must be Aria."

She looked at him for a moment.

"That depends. Are you here to sell me something?"

Cassian laughed.

"No. I'm your brother."

Aria blinked.

Then she looked at Isolde.

"One of the four?"

"Yes."

Cassian placed a hand over his chest.

"Cassian. The most charming one."

"That's a competitive title."

"It is. My brothers are difficult people."

Aria studied him.

"You seem very confident."

"I have been told it's one of my flaws."

Isolde shook her head.

"Cassian, don't overwhelm her."

"I wouldn't dream of it."

Aria looked at him again.

"Hello, Cassian."

His smile became a little less theatrical.

"Hello, Aria."

There was a short silence.

Then he stepped aside.

"I won't keep you. I only wanted to say hello."

Aria nodded.

"All right."

She turned towards Isolde.

"Thank you for the tea."

"Come again whenever you wish."

"I will."

Aria walked towards the gate.

Cassian watched her leave.

He had imagined dozens of ways their first conversation might go.

None had involved being asked whether he was selling something.

He decided he liked her.

**Vesper Estate — 4:30 p.m.**

Cassian entered the sunroom.

Isolde was putting the remaining biscuits away.

"How was she?" he asked.

"She was herself."

"That's a remarkably vague answer."

"Then perhaps you should speak to her more often."

Cassian smiled.

"She has a job."

"Yes."

"An ordinary job."

"Yes."

"And she's living alone."

"Yes."

He sat down.

"Do you realise how unusual that is?"

Isolde gave him a look.

"Among our family? Extremely."

Cassian leaned back.

"Maximilian is going to have a difficult time."

"Why?"

"Because he will want to solve every problem she has."

Isolde closed the biscuit tin.

"And what will you do?"

Cassian considered it.

"Try not to become one."

For once, Isolde looked genuinely pleased with his answer.

**Sterling Mansion — 9:25 p.m.**

Victoria sat in front of her laptop.

The Vesper Foundation had not replied to her second email.

She had also received no further message from the unknown number.

She opened her social media account.

A new post from a local business magazine appeared in her feed.

It featured a photograph of the Vesper Tower and a short article about the family's latest industrial expansion.

Victoria read it.

The article mentioned Maximilian Vesper by name.

She searched for the other brothers.

Maximilian.

Damian.

Cassian.

Felix.

She studied their photographs.

Then she opened a new browser tab.

She searched for Aria.

There was almost nothing.

No public profile connecting her to the Vespers.

No announcement.

No photograph from the estate.

Nothing.

Victoria frowned.

"Why are they keeping her hidden?"

She leaned closer to the screen.

For the first time, she wondered whether the Vespers were deliberately keeping Aria away from public attention.

And if so, why?

Her phone vibrated.

An unknown number.

**Unknown:** You have met the wrong people.

Victoria immediately replied.

**Victoria:** Explain.

The answer arrived several seconds later.

**Unknown:** The people you see are not the ones who arranged the switch.

Victoria stared at the message.

Then another arrived.

**Unknown:** And they are not the ones who paid for it.

Victoria's smile disappeared.

She looked towards the closed bedroom door.

For once, she did not feel like the most important person in the room.

She felt like someone had opened a door and was waiting for her to walk through it.

And she had no idea what was waiting on the other side.
