# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 49 — The Signature of a Dead Woman**

**Thursday — 9:52 p.m.**

Catherine read the filing a third time.

“Mara Voss.”

Nobody interrupted her.

Octavian watched his sister.

“You knew her?”

“Yes.”

“Who was she?”

“My legal adviser.”

Lucian looked up sharply.

“Your legal adviser?”

“For the legacy trust. Before Elias became custodian.”

Blackwood leaned forward.

“Was she related to Edmund Voss?”

“No. Different family.”

“Then why does her name appear on the filing?”

Catherine placed the document on the table.

“Because Mara was the only person who understood the original beneficiary restrictions well enough to challenge them.”

Felix opened the historical records.

“Her personnel file says she died in a transport accident.”

Catherine nodded.

“That is what I was told.”

“Who confirmed it?”

“Elias.”

Octavian's expression darkened.

“My brother told you she was dead?”

“Yes.”

“Did you see a death certificate?”

“No.”

“Did you see a body?”

“No.”

Cassian exhaled.

“Another confirmed death with no confirmed body.”

Victoria looked at him.

“Does anyone in this family ever resign?”

**10:07 p.m.**

The filing was immediately challenged.

Blackwood contacted the trust's legal office and requested authentication of the submission.

The response arrived within minutes.

The filing had been submitted using a valid historical legal credential.

It was not a forged signature.

It was a stolen identity.

Felix examined the digital record.

“Someone accessed Mara's dormant legal account.”

“Can we trace the access?” Maximilian asked.

“Not directly. The request passed through the old trust network.”

Blackwood looked concerned.

“That network was supposed to be isolated.”

“It was,” Felix replied.

“Then how?”

Felix displayed the authentication chain.

“The credentials were accepted because the original legal account was never closed.”

Lucian looked at Blackwood.

“Why?”

Blackwood hesitated.

“Because Mara Voss was appointed as an emergency legal representative. Her authority could only be terminated by the original custodian.”

Catherine's face tightened.

“Me.”

“Yes.”

“You told me her account had been closed.”

“I believed it had.”

Catherine turned toward him.

“You believed a great many things.”

Blackwood accepted the rebuke.

Octavian examined the filing.

“Can this request prevent the girls from exercising their rights?”

“Not by itself,” Blackwood replied. “But it can delay verification while the court reviews the alleged incapacity.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“So someone wants to make sure we cannot consent.”

“Correct.”

Aria looked at the filing.

“Then we should ask why someone is so afraid of us saying yes.”

**10:24 p.m.**

At the railway station, Margaret Ellison opened the envelope.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

It contained a list of names.

She read the first one.

Then the second.

Her face changed.

The man beside her watched.

“Do you understand?”

“Yes.”

“Then we need to leave.”

Margaret folded the paper.

“Where did you get this?”

“From Elias's private archive.”

“Who else has seen it?”

“No one.”

She looked at him suspiciously.

“Why should I trust you?”

The man took out a small metal key.

It was old, worn and marked with the Vesper crest.

“Because Elias gave me this before he died.”

Margaret stared at it.

“Who are you?”

The man finally turned toward her.

“Thomas Reed.”

She recognised the name.

The courier who had carried one of the original hospital records.

The man who had delivered documents through the old Northstar route.

“You were involved in the transfers.”

“I delivered sealed packages. I never knew what they contained.”

“Then why are you here?”

“Because Elias gave me an instruction.”

“What instruction?”

Thomas handed her the key.

“If the two girls were ever found, deliver this to them.”

Margaret looked at the key.

“What does it open?”

Thomas answered quietly.

“The room where the original allocation was decided.”

**10:46 p.m.**

Aria was preparing to leave the conference room when Marcus approached.

“There's a message for you.”

“From whom?”

“Thomas Reed.”

Aria stopped.

“Thomas Reed?”

Marcus nodded.

“He says he has evidence concerning the original allocation.”

Victoria immediately stood.

“Where is he?”

“He requested a meeting.”

“Where?”

“An old railway station.”

Damian shook his head.

“No.”

Aria looked at him.

“We haven't heard what he has.”

“He could be working with Rook.”

“Or he could be the courier who carried the original records.”

“Which makes him relevant to the investigation and a potential target.”

Victoria looked at Maximilian.

“Can you verify his identity?”

“Yes.”

“Then do that.”

Maximilian turned to Damian.

“Check his history, current location and known associates. No contact until we know where he is.”

Marcus looked at Aria.

“Would you like me to arrange the meeting?”

Aria considered it.

“Yes. But not tonight.”

Victoria frowned.

“Why?”

“Because it's late, we have an active legal attack, and somebody has just submitted a fraudulent incapacity claim.”

Cassian smiled.

“Practical.”

Aria looked at him.

“I'm going to bed.”

**Friday — 7:15 a.m.**

The next morning, Thomas Reed was missing.

The railway station was empty.

The bench where Margaret had sat was gone.

The building's security cameras had been disconnected.

A single envelope had been left on the floor.

Inside was a photograph.

It showed a young Catherine Vesper standing beside Elias.

Between them was a third person.

A woman in a dark suit.

On the back was written:

**MARA VOSS — ORIGINAL LEGAL REPRESENTATIVE.**

Below it, another sentence:

**She did not die in the accident. She was removed before the allocation was reversed.**

**7:42 a.m.**

Catherine stared at the photograph.

“That's Mara.”

Octavian looked at her.

“You're certain?”

“Yes.”

Lucian examined the image.

“Who removed her?”

Catherine shook her head.

“I don't know.”

Blackwood took the photograph.

“The accident report was filed three days after her disappearance.”

Felix searched the archived records.

“There's no hospital admission, no transport record and no identified remains.”

“Then why was her death accepted?” Maximilian asked.

“Because the report was signed by the family legal office.”

Richard looked at the signature.

“Who signed it?”

Felix enlarged the document.

The name appeared.

**GIDEON ROOK.**

Octavian's expression became murderous.

“Rook declared Mara dead.”

Catherine shook her head.

“He didn't have the authority.”

“Then why did you accept it?”

“Because Elias told me the report had been verified.”

Octavian looked at his sister.

“And you trusted him.”

“Yes.”

For a moment, Catherine seemed older than she had the previous evening.

“He was my brother.”

Nobody spoke.

**8:06 a.m.**

Felix found the next connection.

Mara Voss had been removed from the legal records twenty-three years earlier.

Her last recorded assignment concerned a document called **Schedule C**.

The original allocation schedule was Schedule B.

Schedule C was not a copy.

It was an amendment.

Felix opened the archived index.

The document had three sections:

- Original beneficiary allocation.
- Temporary custody instructions.
- Conditions for restoration.

The first two sections were already known.

The third had never been recovered.

Maximilian leaned over the screen.

“What does it say?”

“Nothing. The document itself is missing.”

Blackwood looked at Catherine.

“Do you remember Schedule C?”

Catherine nodded slowly.

“Yes.”

“What was it?”

“A safeguard.”

“For whom?”

“For the descendants.”

Aria approached the screen.

“What kind of safeguard?”

Catherine looked at her.

“If either child was placed with the wrong household, the trust could not be restored until the person responsible for the placement had been identified.”

Victoria frowned.

“Why would that matter?”

“Because the trust's creator believed that restoring the allocation without identifying the person responsible would expose both children to the same threat again.”

Aria looked at the two empty sections.

“So the trust was never supposed to be restored just because we were found.”

“No,” Catherine said.

“It was supposed to be restored only after the danger was understood.”

**8:32 a.m.**

Octavian received a call.

The caller identified himself as Gideon Rook.

Octavian put the call on speaker.

“Where are you?”

“Somewhere you cannot reach me.”

“Then you have made a mistake.”

Rook laughed softly.

“I have made many mistakes, Octavian. Keeping the trust intact was not one of them.”

“You attempted to steal its voting authority.”

“I attempted to preserve it.”

“By making yourself its administrator?”

“By preventing two inexperienced girls from becoming responsible for assets they do not understand.”

Aria looked at Victoria.

Victoria's expression was openly hostile.

Octavian spoke coldly.

“You have no authority over them.”

“I have more authority than you think.”

“Your appointment is invalid.”

“Perhaps.”

Rook paused.

“But the filing has already been submitted. The legal review will begin. Until then, neither girl can authorise anything.”

Victoria stepped toward the phone.

“You don't get to decide whether we understand our own inheritance.”

Rook went silent.

Then he replied.

“Victoria Sterling. You should be grateful I did not allow the transfer to be completed.”

Victoria's expression changed.

“Did you arrange my removal from the estate?”

“I did not arrange your removal.”

“Did you arrange the reversal?”

“No.”

“Did you know it would happen?”

A pause.

“Yes.”

The room went still.

Aria moved closer to the phone.

“Why didn't you stop it?”

Rook's answer was quiet.

“Because the person who ordered it had already secured the support of someone inside the Vesper family.”

Octavian's face hardened.

“Who?”

Rook did not answer.

Instead, he said:

“Ask your sister why she surrendered the custodianship.”

Catherine stepped forward.

“Gideon.”

“Yes, Catherine?”

“Where is Mara?”

A long silence followed.

Then Rook replied.

“She is closer than you think.”

The call ended.

**8:39 a.m.**

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Then Felix's laptop chimed.

A new message had arrived.

It contained one attachment.

A scanned page from Schedule C.

At the top was a handwritten instruction.

**The trust shall remain inaccessible until both descendants are reunited and the original custodian has been restored.**

Beneath it was a signature.

Not Catherine's.

Not Elias's.

Not Margaret's.

It belonged to the person who had authorised the original trust.

**EVELYN VESPER.**

But there was something else.

A handwritten addition appeared beneath the signature.

**If the custodian cannot be restored, the authority shall pass to the person who witnessed the original allocation.**

Octavian read it.

Then he looked at Catherine.

“Who witnessed it?”

Catherine's face had gone pale.

She whispered a name.

“Helena.”

Lucian turned toward his wife.

Helena stared at the document.

“I wasn't there.”

Catherine shook her head.

“Not you.”

She looked at Octavian.

“Your mother.”

Octavian frowned.

“My mother was the creator.”

“No,” Catherine said. “She was the creator's daughter.”

The room went silent.

Catherine pointed to the signature.

“Evelyn Vesper was not the person who established the trust.”

She turned the page over.

A name had been written on the reverse.

**THE ORIGINAL CREATOR: ANNA VESPER.**

Octavian stared.

He had never heard that name.

Neither had Lucian.

And for the first time, the Vesper family's own history appeared to contain a person who had been erased before the dynasty was even founded.

**End of Chapter 49**
