# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 65 — The Woman Who Signed After Her Death**

**Monday — 6:12 p.m.**

Nobody moved.

The routing slip remained projected on the wall.

**Sender: MARA VOSS**

**Instruction: Correspondence to be retained until direct confirmation from both descendants.**

**Signature date: Six months after official death registration.**

Catherine stared at the document.

“I know her handwriting.”

Octavian folded his hands.

“Handwriting can be copied.”

“I know.”

“Then we need more than recognition.”

Catherine looked at him.

“I also know she would never have signed an instruction like this without checking its legal consequences.”

Lucian examined the date.

“Her death registration was accepted under the emergency administrative review.”

“Yes,” Felix said. “The same review connected to Elias's disputed death verification.”

Damian looked at the screen.

“So the two false death records may be connected.”

“Possibly,” Felix replied. “But we cannot assume the same person prepared both.”

Aria looked at the document.

“Can we establish who submitted this routing slip?”

Felix nodded.

“That's what I'm checking.”

**6:29 p.m.**

The routing slip had been generated by Northstar's old correspondence system.

Felix accessed the surviving metadata.

“There's an originating terminal.”

“Where?” Maximilian asked.

“Central Women's Hospital.”

Catherine's head turned.

“The hospital?”

Felix nodded.

“The document was created using a hospital administrative terminal, not a Northstar terminal.”

Thomas looked at Margaret.

“Then the instruction was issued before the envelope arrived.”

“Correct,” Felix said. “It was attached to the correspondence file during the initial transfer.”

Octavian looked at the date.

“So Mara instructed Northstar to retain the envelope.”

“Apparently.”

“And six months later, someone used her credentials to reaffirm the instruction.”

Felix paused.

“Not exactly.”

He enlarged the metadata.

“The second signature was added six months later, but the original routing slip was not modified. Someone attached a separate confirmation page.”

Catherine looked at it.

“Can you identify the terminal?”

“Not yet. The terminal identifier was partially overwritten during migration.”

“Can you recover it?”

“I can try.”

Cassian leaned back.

“Please do. I would prefer our mystery to stop using deceased people as office equipment.”

Maximilian gave him a warning look.

Cassian raised his hands.

“I am expressing a legitimate concern about administrative standards.”

**7:03 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria was not pleased.

She stood in the family study, holding a printed copy of the routing slip.

Richard sat behind his desk.

Eleanor had joined them.

“You knew about Mara Voss,” Victoria said.

Richard looked at the document.

“I knew her name. I did not know she had been responsible for this correspondence.”

“You knew about the confidential arrangement.”

“Yes.”

“And you didn't tell me.”

“I did not know you were connected to it.”

Victoria laughed once.

“That's becoming a very convenient sentence.”

Richard accepted the criticism without replying.

Eleanor looked at the date.

“Is this the woman who authorised the hospital records?”

“Possibly,” Victoria said. “But the document was signed after her death.”

Richard examined the signature.

“Could it be forged?”

“Yes.”

“Then we shouldn't treat it as evidence that she was alive.”

Victoria looked at him.

“I didn't say she was alive.”

Richard put the paper down.

“What are you suggesting?”

“That someone wanted her name to remain active after her death.”

Eleanor's expression tightened.

“Why?”

Victoria looked at the document again.

“Because someone needed her authority.”

Richard slowly leaned back.

For once, he did not argue.

**8:17 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Felix finally recovered the terminal identifier.

He displayed it.

**CW-HOSP-ADM-04**

Catherine recognised it.

“That was the hospital's old confidential administration terminal.”

Thomas looked at her.

“You've used it?”

“Once. More than twenty years ago.”

“For what?”

“To review the original custodial arrangement.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“You never mentioned this.”

“I didn't think it mattered. I reviewed the arrangement before the girls were born.”

Felix checked the access history.

“The terminal was still active six months after Mara's death.”

Catherine frowned.

“Who had access?”

“Three authorised accounts. Mara Voss. Dr. Simon Weller. And a temporary administrative account.”

“Name?”

Felix opened the record.

**E. PRICE — E.P.12**

Margaret immediately looked up.

“Evelyn Price.”

Thomas nodded.

“The records clerk from the hospital.”

Felix continued.

“Her account was suspended before the routing confirmation was created.”

Catherine frowned.

“Then someone used her account after suspension.”

“Or another person used a temporary replacement account,” Felix said.

Catherine looked at the date.

“Who authorised the replacement?”

Felix opened the associated record.

The approval field contained:

**M. VOSS — EXTERNAL CUSTODIAL AUTHORITY**

The room became quiet.

**8:34 p.m.**

“Someone used Mara's authority to create a replacement account,” Lucian said.

Felix nodded.

“And then used that account to attach a confirmation under Mara's name.”

Octavian looked at Catherine.

“Could Mara have authorised it before her death?”

“No. The replacement was created afterward.”

“Then someone impersonated her.”

“Or someone had access to her dormant credentials.”

Aria looked at the screen.

“Could Evelyn Price tell us who used her account?”

Felix shook his head.

“Not without speaking to her.”

“Then speak to her.”

Damian glanced at Catherine.

“Is she safe to contact?”

Catherine considered the question.

“We have no evidence that she was involved. She may have been another person whose credentials were used.”

“Then we should not approach her as a suspect,” Aria said.

Octavian nodded.

“Agreed.”

Felix opened the personnel record.

Evelyn Price had left Central Women's Hospital nineteen years ago.

Her last recorded address was an apartment complex near the old medical district.

A forwarding address had been added three years later.

**Greyhaven House.**

Thomas looked at Margaret.

“She's been living at Greyhaven?”

Margaret frowned.

“No. That's the former Vesper administrative residence.”

Alistair checked the address.

“Not the estate. The old medical residence used by the family during confidential treatment.”

Catherine went pale.

“I remember that building.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“What?”

“Mara stayed there.”

**Tuesday — 8:42 a.m.**

Greyhaven House had been closed to ordinary visitors for years.

Its medical wing was still maintained, but the residential section had been converted into a private archive.

Evelyn Price's forwarding record showed that she had received mail there for eleven years.

No tenancy record existed.

No employment record existed.

No explanation had been entered.

The house manager met the Vesper delegation at the entrance.

“Miss Price no longer receives correspondence here.”

“When did she leave?” Damian asked.

“Eleven years ago.”

“Where did she go?”

“I wasn't given a forwarding address.”

Catherine looked at him.

“Did she work here?”

The manager hesitated.

“She was assigned to the medical records office.”

“Under whose authority?”

“Mara Voss.”

Catherine's expression hardened.

“Was Mara here?”

The manager looked uncomfortable.

“I was instructed not to discuss private residents.”

Octavian stepped forward.

“I am asking about an administrative assignment, not a patient's medical history.”

The manager considered him.

Then he nodded.

“Miss Price worked here for approximately eighteen months. She maintained a private records register. When she left, she returned her access key.”

“Did she leave anything behind?”

The manager looked towards the old medical wing.

“There was one locked cabinet.”

**9:26 a.m.**

The cabinet was in a small office overlooking the courtyard.

It had no electronic lock.

Only a brass keyhole.

The house manager brought the key register.

“According to this, the cabinet was last opened eleven years ago.”

“By whom?” Lucian asked.

The manager checked.

“Evelyn Price.”

Damian examined the lock.

“Has anyone attempted to open it since?”

“No recorded attempt.”

Thomas looked at the door.

“Again, the distinction matters.”

The manager frowned.

“I understand.”

The cabinet was opened.

Inside were several old patient registers, a correspondence ledger and a small black notebook.

Felix examined the labels.

“Most of these are hospital records.”

Catherine opened the notebook.

The first page contained a handwritten instruction.

**Private custodial correspondence. Do not merge with patient files.**

She turned the page.

A list of names followed.

- Mara Voss
- Evelyn Price
- Simon Weller
- Margaret Ellison
- Alistair Vane

Catherine stopped.

“Vane.”

Octavian looked over her shoulder.

“His name appears here?”

“Yes.”

Felix photographed the page.

The next entry was dated shortly after Mara's official death.

**M.V. account remains active. Replacement access issued to E.P.12. Original authority retained.**

Thomas frowned.

“Who wrote this?”

Catherine examined the handwriting.

“Evelyn Price.”

The next entry was more troubling.

**Instruction received: Maintain correspondence under M.V. authority until direct confirmation from both descendants.**

Margaret looked at Catherine.

“Who gave her that instruction?”

Catherine turned the page.

There was no name.

Only a reference.

**EX-04 / P-01.**

**10:03 a.m.**

Octavian stared at the reference.

“P-01 again.”

Lucian examined the notebook.

“Someone used the patriarchal office authority to keep Mara's credentials active.”

“Or to make it appear that they were active,” Felix said.

Catherine continued reading.

A final entry had been written in the margin.

**Miss Price was instructed to stop recording the original correspondence after the second confirmation. She refused.**

Thomas looked up.

“Who instructed her?”

Catherine shook her head.

“There's no name.”

Aria examined the wording.

“Evelyn Price refused to stop recording?”

“Yes.”

“Then she might have kept another copy.”

Felix looked at the cabinet.

“Possibly.”

He searched the remaining papers.

At the bottom of the cabinet was a narrow envelope.

It contained a single sheet.

A handwritten statement.

**I did not issue the confirmation under Mara Voss's name. I did not authorise the replacement account. I did not release the correspondence. I retained the original register because the official record no longer matched the events I witnessed.**

It was signed:

**Evelyn Price.**

Below the signature was a date.

Eleven years earlier.

And one final line:

**If anyone asks about the second transfer, tell them the recipient was never missing.**

**10:19 a.m.**

Felix read the sentence aloud.

“The recipient was never missing.”

Victoria, who had joined the group by secure call, frowned.

“Then who was the recipient?”

Catherine looked at the statement.

“I don't know.”

Octavian turned towards Margaret.

“Could the second transfer have been completed without a formal record?”

Margaret shook her head.

“Not under the original arrangement. It required the receiving custodian's acknowledgment.”

“Could the acknowledgment have been concealed?”

“Yes.”

Felix looked at the old register.

“Then the transfer might have been completed, but the official record was deliberately left incomplete.”

Thomas considered that.

“Which would explain why someone needed Mara's credentials to maintain the appearance that the transfer was pending.”

Aria looked at the statement again.

“Or Evelyn Price wanted us to look for the recipient rather than the person who signed the confirmation.”

Catherine turned towards her.

“What makes you say that?”

“The wording. She didn't say the transfer happened. She said the recipient wasn't missing.”

Octavian looked at the paper.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he asked:

“Who was the intended recipient?”

Margaret answered carefully.

“Mara Voss.”

Catherine shook her head.

“No.”

Everyone turned.

“Mara was supposed to receive the custodial authority. But the correspondence itself was not addressed to her.”

Felix opened the original routing record.

The sender was Mara Voss.

The recipient field was blank.

The confirmation instruction referred to both descendants.

Catherine looked at the notebook.

“The recipient was not Mara.”

She pointed to the last entry.

“The recipient was someone else.”

**11:02 a.m.**

Felix searched the old correspondence index.

There were only three matching records.

The first was the original Northstar deposit.

The second was the uncompleted custodial transfer.

The third was a private acknowledgment.

Its reference had been partially erased.

Felix restored the remaining characters.

**RC-02.**

Margaret looked at it.

“Receiving Custodian — second descendant.”

Octavian frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“It was an internal classification. The first descendant was assigned the original beneficiary record. The second was assigned the receiving custodian record.”

Victoria looked towards the screen.

“So one of us was supposed to receive the correspondence?”

“Possibly,” Felix said.

Aria folded her arms.

“Which one?”

Felix checked the surviving index.

The answer field had been removed.

But the record included a date.

It was dated before either girl had been taken to her intended household.

Catherine stared at it.

“That cannot be right.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because the receiving custodian was supposed to be appointed after both descendants had been independently identified.”

Felix opened the original trust schedule.

The same classification appeared in the margin.

**RC-02 — Subject to independent identity verification.**

Aria looked at Victoria.

“So the recipient was one of us.”

Victoria's expression tightened.

“But which one?”

Nobody answered.

Then Felix noticed a second line beneath the erased field.

A surviving notation.

**Do not assign by household. Assign by verified identity.**

Catherine slowly sat down.

“They knew the households could be wrong.”

Octavian looked at the two girls.

His expression was unreadable.

The original arrangement had not merely anticipated that the children might be separated.

It had anticipated that someone might deliberately put them in the wrong families.

And the sealed correspondence had been prepared to reach one of them only after their identities were verified.

Aria looked at the record.

“Then we need the original identity assignment.”

Felix nodded.

“I think I know where it is.”

He opened the last surviving index.

The entry was not stored under either girl's name.

It was stored under a reference that had appeared nowhere else.

**ANNA-02.**

Catherine went still.

“Anna?”

Octavian stared at the screen.

“Anna Vesper.”

Felix opened the record.

Only one line remained visible.

**The second descendant shall receive the original custodian's final declaration.**

Beneath it was a blank signature field.

And a note.

**To be completed only in the presence of both descendants.**

Aria looked at Victoria.

Neither spoke.

For the first time, the evidence suggested that the missing declaration had not been intended for the Vesper family, the Sterling family, or even the person who had arranged the original protection.

It had been intended for the two girls.

Together.
