# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 48 — The Authority Nobody Was Allowed to Inherit**

**Thursday — 7:05 p.m.**

“I'm not accepting it.”

Catherine's answer came so quickly that even Blackwood looked surprised.

Octavian studied her.

“You haven't been offered anything.”

“I know what that document means.”

“Then explain it.”

Catherine placed the paper on the table.

“My mother appointed me as alternate representative. That authority was never intended to become permanent. It existed only to protect the trust if the administrator or the primary custodian became compromised.”

Blackwood nodded.

“Correct.”

Victoria frowned.

“Then why does everyone keep treating this like a succession contest?”

“Because the person controlling the trust can influence its voting rights,” Blackwood explained. “And those rights affect several major institutions.”

Aria leaned back.

“So whoever controls the trust doesn't own the dynasty. They can influence parts of it.”

“Precisely.”

“And we don't have to accept anything.”

“No.”

Victoria looked at Catherine.

“Then why have we been dragged into this?”

Catherine met her eyes.

“Because the trust's original beneficiaries were the two of you.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“Were we?”

“Yes.”

“Did anyone ask us?”

“No.”

“Did anyone tell us?”

“No.”

“Then perhaps everyone should stop discussing our inheritance as though we've already agreed to it.”

Blackwood inclined his head.

“That's a reasonable position.”

Cassian smiled.

“I'm beginning to like this trustee.”

Maximilian glanced at him.

“You like anyone who agrees with you.”

“An admirable quality in a trustee.”

**7:22 p.m.**

Felix had been examining the voting structure.

He suddenly stopped.

“There's something wrong.”

Maximilian approached.

“What?”

“The trust's voting rights weren't transferred to Northstar Heritage Holdings.”

Blackwood looked up.

“They attempted to transfer them.”

“No. They attempted to transfer the administrator's authority over the voting rights.”

Felix enlarged the record.

“Look at the transaction classification. The recipient company would have gained authority to issue voting instructions, but the trust would still have retained ownership.”

Lucian frowned.

“Why make the distinction?”

“Because the company could control decisions without owning the assets.”

Blackwood nodded.

“That is correct.”

Felix looked at him.

“You knew?”

“I suspected.”

“Why didn't you say so?”

“I wanted to know whether you would discover it.”

Felix stared.

“That is an incredibly irritating way to conduct an investigation.”

Blackwood smiled faintly.

“Welcome to trusteeship.”

Felix returned to his screen.

“Northstar was going to control the voting instructions across six institutions.”

Maximilian's expression changed.

“Which six?”

Felix displayed the list.

Three financial institutions.

Two infrastructure companies.

One technology holding group.

All connected to the protected trust.

Cassian read the names.

“Those holdings could influence a considerable amount of corporate activity.”

“Exactly,” Maximilian said.

Octavian looked at Blackwood.

“Was Rook planning to sell them?”

“No. He was planning to control them.”

“Why?”

Blackwood closed the folder.

“Because he believes the person who controls the trust should decide who controls the future of the Vesper family.”

Octavian's expression turned cold.

“He has mistaken a protected trust for a throne.”

**7:49 p.m.**

At the old residence, investigators found no sign of forced entry.

Margaret had left voluntarily.

Her suitcase, medication and personal papers were still there.

One thing was missing.

The handwritten letter from Elias.

Damian reviewed the security footage.

“There were two visitors.”

Maximilian stood behind him.

“Rook and Margaret?”

“No. Rook entered at 6:14. A second person entered through the service entrance at 6:19.”

“Who?”

“The camera was disabled for four minutes.”

“Can we identify the vehicle?”

“Already done.”

Damian displayed a photograph.

A dark vehicle had been parked outside the property.

Its registration belonged to a private security company.

Cassian recognised the name.

“That's not Rook's company.”

“No,” Damian replied.

“Whose is it?”

“An old Vesper contractor.”

Octavian approached.

“Which contractor?”

Damian enlarged the registration details.

**Aegis Protective Services.**

Lucian frowned.

“They were dissolved twenty years ago.”

“Officially,” Damian said.

Octavian looked at Voss.

“Did you know?”

Voss studied the image.

“I've seen that insignia before.”

“Where?”

“On the security vehicles assigned to Catherine.”

Catherine turned toward him.

“You told me the company had been disbanded.”

“It had.”

“Then who is using it?”

Voss looked at the photograph again.

“Someone retained the equipment and the old personnel network.”

Catherine's expression became distant.

“Elias.”

Voss shook his head.

“Elias died twenty-two years ago.”

“I know when my brother died.”

“Then why say his name?”

“Because he created Aegis.”

**8:03 p.m.**

The revelation changed the atmosphere.

Octavian looked at his sister.

“Why would Elias create a private security company?”

“To protect the trust's records.”

“From whom?”

“From anyone who tried to seize them.”

Voss spoke carefully.

“Aegis was not an ordinary security contractor. It was a restricted protective network. It had no authority over family assets, but it could secure personnel, documents and locations.”

Lucian frowned.

“Why wasn't it included in the official organisational records?”

“Because it was deliberately kept separate.”

Felix searched the archives.

“There's no active company registration.”

“Look for the original operational charter,” Catherine said.

Felix entered the name.

A result appeared.

Then another.

Then a third.

He stopped.

“That's strange.”

“What?” Maximilian asked.

“The company was dissolved, but its operational charter was never terminated.”

Octavian stared at the screen.

“Who holds the charter?”

Felix opened the record.

A name appeared.

**GIDEON ROOK.**

Nobody spoke.

Catherine looked toward Blackwood.

“Rook wasn't merely the administrator.”

Blackwood nodded.

“He was the operational controller.”

“And Elias allowed it?”

Blackwood hesitated.

“He may have believed Rook was protecting the trust.”

Catherine shook her head.

“No. Elias would never have given him that authority.”

She turned to Octavian.

“Something happened before my disappearance. Something that changed Elias's mind.”

Octavian looked at her.

“What?”

“I don't know.”

**8:26 p.m.**

Aria had been quiet throughout the discussion.

Now she spoke.

“Can I ask something?”

Everyone turned.

“Why did Elias leave that letter for Margaret?”

Catherine looked at her.

“Which letter?”

“The one she was carrying.”

Blackwood answered.

“Because he trusted her with the original allocation instruction.”

“Why?”

“Margaret maintained the records.”

Aria shook her head.

“That's not what I mean. If Elias knew someone was manipulating the trust, why give the evidence to the same person whose credentials were being used?”

Catherine looked thoughtful.

“Because he believed Margaret was innocent.”

“Or because he wanted her to look innocent?”

Victoria turned toward Aria.

“Explain.”

Aria picked up the copy of the transfer record.

“Margaret's credentials were used to submit the reversal. But if Elias trusted her enough to leave the original instruction with her, he might have known she hadn't written it.”

Blackwood nodded slowly.

“Possible.”

“And if Rook was controlling the administrator's authority, he could have used her credentials without her knowledge.”

Felix looked up.

“We already established that.”

“But we haven't established when Margaret discovered it.”

Catherine went still.

Aria continued.

“Maybe she didn't know for years. Maybe she only discovered it when Elias sent the letter.”

Victoria frowned.

“Then why did she keep quiet?”

“Fear. Loyalty. Or because she was protecting something else.”

Catherine looked down at the table.

“Margaret knew where the original declaration was hidden.”

“Could that be why she disappeared?”

“Yes.”

Aria looked at Blackwood.

“Then finding her matters more than finding Rook right now.”

Blackwood nodded.

“I agree.”

Damian immediately began issuing instructions.

**9:11 p.m.**

Margaret Ellison was not in the city.

She had travelled to an old railway station outside the main commercial district.

The building had been closed for renovations and was almost entirely deserted.

She sat on a wooden bench beneath a broken clock.

The letter from Elias rested in her handbag.

A man approached.

He was tall, wearing a dark coat.

Margaret looked up.

“You're late.”

“I had to make sure I wasn't followed.”

“Did you bring it?”

The man placed a small envelope beside her.

Margaret did not touch it.

“What's inside?”

“The original declaration.”

She stared at him.

“Where did you find it?”

“Where Elias left it.”

“That's impossible. I searched that location.”

“Not carefully enough.”

Margaret slowly picked up the envelope.

It was sealed with the old Vesper crest.

“Why are you helping me?”

The man sat beside her.

“Because Rook is about to make a mistake.”

“What mistake?”

“He intends to activate the original voting authority.”

Margaret's face tightened.

“He cannot. The trust requires both beneficiaries' consent.”

“He believes he can bypass that requirement.”

“How?”

The man looked at her.

“By proving that one of the beneficiaries is legally incapable of giving consent.”

Margaret went pale.

“Which one?”

“Both, if necessary.”

She stood.

“He wouldn't.”

“He already submitted the request.”

Margaret clutched the envelope.

“Then we have to warn them.”

The man nodded.

“That's why I'm here.”

He stood and began walking toward the exit.

Margaret followed.

“Wait. Who are you?”

He stopped.

For a moment, he did not turn around.

Then he answered.

“Someone Elias trusted.”

**9:38 p.m.**

Felix received the alert first.

A new legal filing had appeared in the trust's administrative system.

It requested an emergency determination of beneficiary incapacity.

The filing named Aria Vesper and Victoria Sterling.

Maximilian read the document.

“This is fraudulent.”

Blackwood examined the signature.

“It's worse than that.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Explain.”

“The request is supported by an old medical assessment.”

Helena immediately stood.

“What medical assessment?”

Blackwood turned the document around.

It contained two names.

Aria Sterling.

Victoria Shaw.

Both assessments were dated more than twenty years earlier.

Both were marked as preliminary.

Both claimed that the children had been placed under restricted custodial review.

Aria stared at the paper.

“I was a baby.”

Victoria's expression was furious.

“So was I.”

Blackwood nodded.

“Exactly. Neither assessment could establish legal incapacity.”

“Then why submit them?” Lucian asked.

“To create grounds for an emergency review.”

Felix scrolled to the final page.

“There's another problem.”

He enlarged the authorisation section.

The filing had been submitted by an attorney.

The name was familiar.

**Mara Voss.**

Richard stood.

“She's the one who signed CA-17.”

Catherine's face changed.

“No.”

Octavian looked at her.

“What?”

Catherine took the document.

She read the name again.

Then she whispered:

“Mara Voss died twenty-one years ago.”

**End of Chapter 48**
