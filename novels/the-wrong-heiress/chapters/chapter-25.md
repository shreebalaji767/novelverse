# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 25 — The Nurse Who Took the Child**

**Saturday — 7:10 a.m.**

Felix had a problem.

Someone had accessed the old personnel archive.

The request was authorised using the same credentials that had appeared on the hospital transfer records.

M. Voss.

He checked the timestamp.

Then he checked it again.

Then he called Marcus.

“Someone is trying to find Anne Porter.”

Marcus answered immediately.

“Who?”

“Unknown. The archive request was submitted using Mara Voss's authorisation code.”

“Was the file accessed?”

“No. The system rejected the request because the account has been inactive for twelve years.”

“Can you trace the request?”

“I'm trying.”

“Preserve the logs. Don't attempt to contact the account holder.”

Felix frowned.

“I wasn't going to send her a welcome email.”

Marcus ignored that.

“Send the access records to security.”

“Already done.”

**8:30 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Damian stood beside Marcus as the security team examined the access request.

The request had originated through an old administrative gateway.

It had not come directly from Asterion.

It had passed through three separate systems.

“Can we identify the user?” Damian asked.

“Not yet,” Felix replied over the conference screen. “The credentials identify the account, not the person operating it.”

Marcus studied the report.

“Could someone have copied the credentials?”

“Easily. The account was deactivated, but its authorisation history remained in the archive.”

Damian folded his arms.

“So the person knows the old system.”

“Or has access to someone who does,” Felix said.

Marcus looked at the timestamp.

“The request was made after Lydia gave her statement.”

Damian nodded.

“They know we found Anne Porter.”

“Which means we should assume she's at risk.”

Octavian entered.

“Have we located her?”

“Not yet,” Marcus replied.

“Then find her through lawful channels. No one approaches her aggressively. If she is willing to speak, we listen.”

Damian nodded.

**9:45 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria was reviewing the monthly reconciliation when Peter approached.

“Your client has sent another email.”

Aria looked up.

“Have they discovered a fourth version?”

“Fortunately, no.”

“I'm relieved.”

“They've accepted the corrected statement.”

“That's good.”

Peter placed a printed message on her desk.

“They also thanked you.”

Aria read it.

The client had written three paragraphs praising the reconciliation and apologising for the confusion.

She looked up.

“That's surprisingly pleasant.”

“Don't get used to it.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

Peter smiled.

“Keep the file. We may need it next month.”

Aria returned to her work.

Her phone vibrated.

Marcus.

*The nurse who accompanied Infant B out of the hospital has been identified. We are attempting to locate her.*

Aria replied:

*Please let me know when you find her.*

She put the phone away.

**11:20 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

The investigation team received a response from the hospital's former personnel department.

Anne Porter had left the hospital twelve years earlier.

Her last known address was outdated.

However, the hospital had retained a reference letter submitted when she applied for a position at a private medical clinic.

The clinic had since closed.

Its former administrator, however, was still alive.

The team contacted him.

He confirmed that Anne had worked there for almost two years.

She had later moved to another medical facility.

That facility had been acquired by a larger healthcare group.

Marcus reviewed the employment history.

“She's been working in healthcare this entire time.”

Damian nodded.

“Then she's probably still registered professionally.”

“Let's verify that.”

A legal investigator began checking the available professional records.

**12:40 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria found Richard in the study.

He was reading an old file.

She noticed the notebook beside his hand.

“Have you spoken to anyone?”

Richard looked up.

“About what?”

“Lydia Vale. The Vespers. The second child.”

“No.”

Victoria pulled out a chair.

“Someone is trying to find the nurse who took the child from the hospital.”

Richard's expression changed.

“Who told you that?”

“I have my sources.”

“Victoria, stop.”

“Why?”

“Because you don't understand how dangerous this has become.”

She leaned forward.

“Then explain it to me.”

Richard closed the file.

“I don't know who is behind the messages you're receiving.”

Victoria blinked.

“How did you know about the messages?”

He looked at her.

“You think I haven't noticed you receiving them?”

She said nothing.

Richard continued.

“Someone is using your curiosity. They are giving you just enough information to keep you investigating, but never enough to understand what you're involved in.”

Victoria looked away.

“Maybe they're trying to help.”

“Then why are they hiding their identity?”

She had no answer.

Richard opened the notebook again.

“I am going to give the Vespers everything I have.”

Victoria looked at him sharply.

“Everything?”

“Yes.”

“Even the notebook?”

Richard nodded.

She stood.

“Then I want to see it first.”

“No.”

“Father—”

“No, Victoria.”

She stared at him.

For once, Richard did not soften his voice.

**2:15 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The legal investigator returned with a confirmed address.

Anne Porter was currently employed at a private rehabilitation centre.

Her registration was active.

Her employment history was verified.

Marcus placed the report before Octavian.

“She is working there now.”

“Has anyone contacted her?”

“No. We wanted your approval first.”

Octavian considered the report.

“Send a formal request for a voluntary meeting.”

Damian looked at the address.

“If someone has already tried to access her file, we should inform her that her name has appeared in the investigation.”

“Agreed,” Octavian said. “But do not frighten her unnecessarily.”

Marcus contacted the rehabilitation centre.

**3:05 p.m. — Rehabilitation Centre**

Anne Porter received the request in her office.

She read the letter.

Then she read it again.

The name Vesper appeared several times.

She placed the letter on her desk.

A colleague knocked.

“Anne? Are you all right?”

Anne looked up.

“Yes.”

“You look pale.”

“Just tired.”

The colleague glanced at the letter.

“Something important?”

Anne folded it.

“An old matter.”

She put the letter inside a drawer.

After the colleague left, Anne opened her telephone contacts.

She scrolled through several names.

Then stopped at one.

**Daniel Mercer.**

She hesitated.

Finally, she called.

The number was no longer in service.

Anne put the telephone down.

She looked at the letter again.

Twelve years.

She had spent twelve years avoiding this conversation.

And now the family was asking questions.

**4:20 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria arrived for a short visit.

Helena was in the garden.

“Hello, darling.”

“Hi.”

“Would you like tea?”

“Yes, please.”

They sat together.

Aria looked toward the house.

“Has the investigation made progress?”

“We have found the nurse who took the second infant out of the hospital.”

“Have you spoken to her?”

“Not yet. She has been contacted.”

Aria thought about that.

“Do you think she knows where the child went?”

“We hope so.”

“Did she know the child was being taken somewhere other than the hospital?”

“We don't know.”

Aria nodded.

“Then I hope she tells you what happened.”

Helena watched her carefully.

“Are you worried?”

“A little.”

“About the child?”

“Yes.”

Helena reached across the table.

“We all are.”

Aria looked down at her tea.

“I keep thinking about how many people handled this. Someone authorised records. Someone transferred employees. Someone took a baby out of a hospital. And nobody seems to know where the child went.”

Helena's expression tightened.

“I know.”

Aria took a breath.

“It's difficult to understand.”

“It is.”

They sat quietly.

For once, neither tried to fill the silence.

**6:10 p.m. — Rehabilitation Centre**

Anne received another call.

This time, the caller's number was hidden.

She answered.

“Hello?”

A familiar female voice spoke.

“Anne.”

Anne froze.

“Who is this?”

“You know who.”

She stood.

“I told you never to call me again.”

“I need to ask you something.”

“No.”

“Have the Vespers contacted you?”

Anne said nothing.

The woman continued.

“They have found the old records.”

“I don't know anything.”

“You know exactly what happened.”

“I did what I was instructed to do.”

“And you never asked where the child went?”

Anne's voice trembled.

“I asked.”

“And?”

“You told me the receiving authority had changed.”

The woman paused.

“Did you keep anything?”

“No.”

“Think carefully.”

“I kept nothing.”

The woman ended the call.

Anne stood motionless.

Then she locked her office door.

**8:00 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received a call from the rehabilitation centre.

Anne Porter had agreed to meet the Vespers.

However, she had requested a different location.

She did not want the meeting at her workplace.

Marcus informed Octavian.

“Where does she want to meet?”

“A public conference room at a legal services office.”

“Agreed.”

“She also requested that Lydia Vale be present.”

Octavian considered it.

“Ask Lydia whether she agrees.”

Marcus nodded.

**8:35 p.m.**

Lydia accepted immediately.

“I'll come,” she said.

Marcus asked, “Are you comfortable meeting Anne?”

“I haven't seen her in twelve years.”

“Would you prefer to speak separately?”

“No.”

Lydia paused.

“I want to hear what happened to the child.”

Marcus confirmed the meeting for the following morning.

**10:15 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard opened the notebook.

He had finally decided to hand it over.

The first pages contained dates, payment references and names.

The last page contained an entry he had avoided reading for years.

*Original instruction received from L. Vale.*

Below it:

*Second placement to be completed only after confirmation of first placement.*

And beneath that, in different handwriting:

*If the first placement becomes permanent, the second child must not be returned.*

Richard read the sentence several times.

He had never understood what it meant.

He turned the page.

There was another line.

*Do not allow either family to learn that the other child was withheld.*

Richard closed the notebook.

His telephone rang.

He answered.

“Hello?”

A man's voice spoke.

“You're preparing to give the notebook to the Vespers.”

Richard stood.

“Who is this?”

“You know what happens if you do.”

Richard looked at the notebook.

“I've kept quiet for twelve years.”

“And you should continue.”

“No.”

There was a pause.

Then the man said, “You don't know what you're releasing.”

Richard's voice hardened.

“Neither do you.”

He ended the call.

**Saturday — 11:40 p.m.**

At the rehabilitation centre, Anne Porter returned to her office after finishing her shift.

She opened the drawer where she had placed the Vesper invitation.

Beneath it was a small photograph.

It showed a newborn infant wrapped in a hospital blanket.

On the back was a handwritten label.

**INFANT B — 06:42 A.M.**

Anne stared at it.

Then she opened a second drawer and removed a sealed envelope.

She had kept both for twelve years.

Inside the envelope was a small hospital identification band.

The name field was blank.

Only the temporary reference remained.

**INFANT B.**

Anne held it in her palm.

Then she took out her telephone and called Marcus.

He answered.

“Marcus Vale.”

“This is Anne Porter.”

“Ms. Porter. Is everything all right?”

“I need to change something about tomorrow's meeting.”

“Of course.”

“I have something that belongs to the investigation.”

“What is it?”

Anne looked at the identification band.

“The child's hospital identification band.”

Marcus sat upright.

“Do you know where the child went?”

Anne closed her eyes.

“No.”

A pause.

“But I know who collected the child after I left the hospital.”

“Who?”

Anne's voice was barely audible.

“Daniel Mercer.”
