# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 20 — The Employee Who Was Never Dismissed**

**Monday — 7:20 a.m.**

Daniel Mercer had left the hospital twelve years ago.

That much had been established.

What nobody had established was where he went afterward.

Felix Vesper sat in the technology division's archive room, surrounded by screens displaying old corporate records.

He had been awake since four.

A paper cup of coffee sat beside his keyboard.

It was empty.

He picked it up, stared inside, and looked offended.

“Who drank my coffee?”

A technician at the next desk slowly raised his hand.

Felix blinked.

“That was mine.”

“You left it here.”

“I left it here because I intended to drink it.”

The technician looked at the cup.

“It's empty.”

“Yes. That's the problem.”

Marcus entered.

“Have you found anything?”

Felix immediately forgot about the coffee.

“Yes.”

He turned his monitor.

“Daniel Mercer was listed as a temporary hospital administrator. His credentials were disabled after the births. But his employment record with Northbridge Data Services remained active for another eight months.”

Marcus studied the screen.

“Why was that not discovered earlier?”

“Because his personnel record was filed under a different department.”

“Which department?”

Felix pointed to the screen.

“Special Projects.”

Marcus waited.

Felix added, “A department that doesn't appear in any current Northbridge organisational chart.”

“That is not encouraging.”

“It gets worse.”

Felix opened another record.

“His salary was paid through a subsidiary called Bellweather Corporate Services.”

Marcus recognised the name.

“Bellweather handled Greybridge's registration.”

“Exactly.”

Marcus looked at the screen again.

“Can you identify his reassignment destination?”

“Not yet. The original field was deleted, but the system retained a reference number.”

Felix enlarged it.

**SP-09 / ROUTE C**

Marcus took out his telephone.

“Send this to the investigation team.”

Felix nodded.

“And Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“Can you ask someone to bring me coffee?”

Marcus looked at the empty cup.

“Use your own money.”

Felix looked personally betrayed.

**8:40 a.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria arrived at work carrying a small paper bag.

Peter Collins looked up from his desk.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning.”

She placed the bag on the counter.

“I brought something.”

Peter glanced inside.

Biscuits.

“Are these for the office?”

“Yes. Unless you dislike biscuits.”

“I don't dislike biscuits.”

“Good. That would have been an awkward start to Monday.”

Peter smiled.

“You're becoming popular.”

“I've been here long enough to learn where the kettle is. That's a significant professional milestone.”

He handed her a folder.

“Your next assignment.”

Aria opened it.

“Supplier reconciliation?”

“A client has submitted three versions of the same expense statement. None agrees with the bank records.”

She looked through the pages.

“How many versions?”

“Three.”

“Wonderful.”

“Wonderful?”

“I was hoping for something that could be solved without a meeting.”

Peter laughed.

“Try not to sound too disappointed.”

Aria took the folder to her desk.

For the next hour, the office remained pleasantly ordinary.

No secretive meetings.

No mysterious family documents.

Just numbers that refused to agree.

She preferred it that way.

**10:15 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

The investigation team had begun tracing SP-09.

The reference appeared in several Northbridge records, all connected to the same period.

One document listed a transfer of personnel.

Another recorded an equipment allocation.

A third showed a monthly expense account.

The destination remained blank.

Marcus reviewed the material with Damian.

“Could this have been a security reassignment?” Damian asked.

“Possibly,” Marcus replied. “But the department was not part of Northbridge's normal operations.”

“Who authorised it?”

“The approval field contains initials only.”

Damian looked at the screen.

“M.V.”

Marcus said nothing.

They both knew the initials had appeared before.

Mara Voss.

“Do we have proof it's the same person?” Damian asked.

“Not yet.”

Damian leaned back.

“Then we don't treat it as proof.”

Marcus nodded.

“Agreed.”

He sent a request to the former Northbridge records custodian for the original approval ledger.

**11:50 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria had developed a new habit.

She no longer entered her father's study without knocking.

Not because she had suddenly become respectful.

Because Richard had started locking the door.

She stood outside it, holding a folder.

“Father?”

“Come in.”

She opened the door.

Richard was reviewing documents.

“I need to ask you something.”

“About what?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

Richard's hand stopped moving.

Victoria noticed.

Interesting.

“You remember him?”

“I've heard the name.”

“He worked at the hospital.”

Richard put down his pen.

“Why are you asking?”

“Because your account records connect to the same administrative network that employed him.”

“Victoria, I have already explained that I don't know what happened at the hospital.”

“You explained that you authorised the account. You didn't explain why.”

Richard looked tired.

“I gave the Vespers the documents.”

“That's not an answer.”

“It's the only answer I have.”

Victoria placed the folder on his desk.

“Then perhaps you should read this.”

It contained a printed copy of the Northbridge personnel record.

Richard read the first page.

His expression changed when he reached the reassignment entry.

“Where did you get this?”

“From a source.”

“What source?”

“Someone who has been helping me.”

Richard looked up sharply.

“Who?”

Victoria folded her arms.

“Why does that matter?”

“Because you don't know who you're dealing with.”

She laughed.

“You've spent weeks telling me to stop investigating. Now you're worried about my sources?”

“I am telling you to be careful.”

“That's what people say when they don't want questions answered.”

Richard pushed the papers back.

“Leave it alone.”

Victoria picked them up.

“No.”

She walked out.

Richard remained seated.

He had just recognised something in the personnel record.

The approval code was not merely familiar.

He had seen it on a document he had signed twelve years earlier.

**1:30 p.m. — Peter Collins & Associates**

Aria placed a spreadsheet on Peter's desk.

“Finished.”

Peter examined it.

“That was quick.”

“The third statement copied two entries from the first one and then changed the dates.”

“And the bank records?”

“Correct. The invoices were duplicated, not the payments.”

Peter looked at her work.

“Good catch.”

“Thank you.”

“Would you be comfortable explaining this to the client tomorrow?”

Aria hesitated.

“Me?”

“You did the reconciliation.”

“I can explain the numbers.”

“That's all they need.”

She nodded.

“Fine. But if they ask me why their accounts are a mess, I'm forwarding that question to you.”

Peter laughed.

“Deal.”

Aria returned to her desk.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Marcus.

*We have found a possible connection between Daniel Mercer and a former Northbridge department. The investigation is continuing.*

She replied:

*Please let me know when something is confirmed.*

Then she put the phone away.

She had a client meeting to prepare for.

**3:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The former Northbridge records custodian arrived with a sealed archive box.

His name was Harold Finch.

He had retired eleven years earlier and had initially refused to meet anyone.

The formal legal request had changed his mind.

Marcus greeted him.

“Mr. Finch. Thank you for coming.”

“I wasn't sure these records still existed.”

“Neither were we.”

Harold placed the box on the table.

“The company used to keep duplicate approval ledgers. One copy stayed with personnel administration. The other went to the special projects office.”

“Do you remember Daniel Mercer?”

Harold nodded.

“Quiet man. Never caused trouble.”

“Was he dismissed?”

“No.”

Marcus looked at him.

“Are you certain?”

“Absolutely. His employment was transferred.”

“To whom?”

Harold opened the box.

“That's what I couldn't remember.”

He removed a thin ledger and turned several pages.

Then he stopped.

“There.”

Marcus leaned forward.

The entry read:

**Employee: Daniel Mercer**

**Transfer Reference: SP-09**

**Receiving Organisation: Asterion Fiduciary Services**

**Effective Date: 18 November**

**Authorised By: M. Voss**

Marcus looked at Damian.

Asterion.

The same intermediary that had paid M. Voss after the births.

Harold continued.

“He wasn't the only one.”

He turned another page.

A second entry appeared.

**Employee: Miriam Shaw**

**Transfer Reference: SP-11**

**Receiving Organisation: Asterion Fiduciary Services**

**Effective Date: 21 November**

**Authorised By: M. Voss**

Marcus went still.

“Miriam Shaw was transferred too?”

Harold nodded.

“Not permanently, I think. Her employment record remained with the hospital. But someone paid for her services through Northbridge.”

Damian studied the ledger.

“Did she know?”

“I couldn't tell you. I only processed the paperwork.”

Marcus looked at the two entries.

Daniel Mercer.

Miriam Shaw.

Both connected to the same organisation.

Both transferred under the same authority.

And both had been involved in the hospital records.

**4:10 p.m.**

Octavian entered the room.

Marcus handed him the ledger.

Octavian read both entries carefully.

“Contact Miriam Shaw.”

“She has already given a statement,” Marcus replied.

“Ask whether she knew she had been transferred.”

“And Mercer?”

“Find him.”

Damian pointed to the ledger.

“There is another problem.”

Octavian looked at him.

“The receiving organisation was Asterion Fiduciary Services. It was not a hospital records company.”

“Then why were hospital employees transferred there?”

“No obvious reason.”

Octavian closed the ledger.

“Find out what services Asterion was providing at the time.”

Marcus nodded.

He had another question.

“Should we inform Aria?”

“Yes. But only the confirmed facts.”

**6:25 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria returned to her room.

She placed the folder on her desk and opened her laptop.

Her anonymous contact had sent another message.

*You found Mercer.*

Victoria typed.

*He was transferred to Asterion.*

The reply arrived almost immediately.

*Who told you that?*

*I found the personnel record.*

*And Miriam Shaw?*

Victoria stopped.

She hadn't mentioned Miriam.

She stared at the message.

Then typed:

*How do you know about her?*

No reply.

A second message appeared.

*You should stop looking at the people who handled the records.*

Victoria frowned.

*Why?*

*Because they were not the people who made the decision.*

She leaned back.

For the first time, the messages seemed less like helpful guidance and more like instructions.

She typed one final question.

*Who made the decision?*

The screen remained silent.

Then the conversation disappeared.

Victoria stared at the blank message window.

“Unbelievable.”

She closed the laptop.

**8:15 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria sat with Helena in the garden.

Marcus had already informed her about the new records.

“Both Mercer and Miriam were transferred to Asterion?” she asked.

“Yes,” Helena replied.

“Did Miriam know?”

“We don't know yet.”

“And Daniel?”

“Nothing confirmed about his present location.”

Aria looked at the garden lights.

“So the company that handled the payments also received the people involved in the hospital records.”

“That is what the records indicate.”

“That's a rather strange coincidence.”

Helena gave her a small smile.

“It is.”

Aria considered the information.

“Do you think they were paid to stay quiet?”

“It's possible. But we don't know.”

“Then don't assume it.”

Helena nodded.

“We won't.”

Aria stood.

“I should go. I have to explain an accounting problem to a client tomorrow.”

“Are you nervous?”

“A little.”

“You'll do well.”

“I hope so. The client has already submitted three versions of the same statement. I'm not sure what version four will look like.”

Helena laughed.

“Good luck.”

“Thank you.”

**11:40 p.m. — Asterion Records Facility**

A man sat alone in a small office.

He had received a request for an old personnel file.

The request carried the name of the Vesper investigation team.

He opened a locked cabinet.

Inside were folders arranged by year.

He found the one marked **SP-09**.

The file contained Daniel Mercer's employment transfer.

But behind it was another document.

A handwritten instruction.

*If the Vespers recover the personnel ledger, destroy the duplicate placement register.*

The man read it twice.

Then he looked at the date.

Twelve years old.

He picked up his telephone.

“They found the ledger,” he said.

A woman's voice answered.

“Which one?”

“The Northbridge personnel ledger.”

Silence.

“Did they find the placement register?”

“No.”

“Then don't touch it.”

The man looked at the locked cabinet.

“It's still here.”

“Good.”

“What should I do?”

“Nothing.”

The call ended.

He returned the document to the folder.

Then he noticed something he had missed.

The placement register was not filed under Daniel Mercer.

It was filed under a different name.

**ARIA STERLING.**
