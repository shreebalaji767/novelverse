# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 31 — The Name Before Victoria**

**Monday — 8:40 a.m.**

Richard Sterling had not slept.

The notebook lay open on his desk, its final page illuminated by the desk lamp.

*Original birth registration retained by E. Shaw.*

He had read the sentence so many times that the handwriting had begun to look unfamiliar.

He knew what it referred to.

More importantly, he knew who had collected the document.

And he knew why he had never mentioned it.

His phone rang.

Marcus Vale.

Richard answered immediately.

“Mr. Vale.”

“Mr. Sterling. You requested a conversation with the Chairman.”

“I have information about the original birth registration.”

There was a brief silence.

“Please don't move the notebook or any associated documents,” Marcus said. “I'll arrange a meeting.”

“I know where the registration went.”

“Then I suggest we meet as soon as possible.”

Richard looked at the open drawer beside him.

“I believe you should bring Mr. Vesper.”

**Vesper Estate — 10:15 a.m.**

Octavian sat at the head of the conference table.

Lucian, Maximilian, Damian, Cassian, and Felix occupied their usual places. Helena and Isolde sat together. Marcus stood near the presentation screen.

Aria arrived carrying a takeaway coffee.

She looked around.

“Is this another meeting where everyone knows something except me?”

“Not this time,” Cassian said.

Felix raised a hand.

“I know something.”

“That's not reassuring,” Aria replied.

Richard entered with a leather document case.

He looked considerably older than he had during their previous meeting.

Octavian gestured toward the empty chair.

“Sit down, Mr. Sterling.”

Richard sat.

“I should have told you this earlier.”

“That has become a familiar opening,” Damian observed.

Richard ignored him.

“The final page of my notebook mentions an original birth registration retained by Eleanor Shaw. I remember the document. It was kept in the Sterling trust estate's private records room.”

Felix immediately leaned forward.

“Was it an original certificate or a registration extract?”

“The original registration document. It was sealed in a file with the Infant B custody records.”

“Who removed it?” Maximilian asked.

Richard opened his case.

“A woman claiming to represent the administrative office responsible for the custody arrangement. She presented an authorisation signed by Mara Voss.”

“Did you verify the authorisation?” Lucian asked.

“No.”

Richard looked down.

“I accepted the document because the estate's temporary custody arrangement had been established through the same office. Eleanor Shaw signed the release.”

“Where did she send it?” Felix asked.

“To the woman.”

“Name?”

“I don't remember one being provided.”

Cassian folded his arms.

“So a woman arrived with a document, collected an original birth registration, and left without giving anyone a verifiable identity.”

Richard nodded.

“That's correct.”

Aria took a sip of coffee.

“Your security procedures were terrible.”

Nobody disagreed.

Octavian studied Richard.

“Why did you withhold this?”

“Because I was told the registration was confidential, and because I had already begun questioning the arrangement. I thought surrendering the document was the safest way to avoid further involvement.”

“Safest for whom?” Isolde asked quietly.

Richard had no answer.

**11:02 a.m.**

Felix had the estate's old custody archive open on his screen.

“Eleanor Shaw's release entry exists,” he announced. “The original registration was removed from the private records room eleven years ago.”

“Can you identify the recipient?” Maximilian asked.

“Not directly. The authorisation number is preserved, but the recipient field is blank.”

“Of course it is,” Cassian muttered.

Felix continued.

“However, the estate's accounting department retained a processing invoice. The payment went through Bellweather Corporate Services.”

Marcus looked at the screen.

“Another intermediary.”

“Same administrative network,” Felix said. “Different transaction.”

He enlarged the invoice.

The description read:

*Secure transfer — original civil registration. Client reference MV-04.*

Aria looked at the screen.

“MV-04.”

“Same beneficiary reference associated with the confidential placement records,” Felix confirmed.

“Does that prove Mara Voss received it?”

“No. It proves the transfer was paid through an account associated with the same reference. We still need the recipient.”

Octavian turned to Richard.

“Can you contact Eleanor Shaw?”

“She left the estate years ago. I don't know where she is now.”

“Then find her.”

Marcus was already making arrangements.

**12:30 p.m.**

The next discovery came from Margaret Vale.

She had sent Marcus a message after receiving a call from an unfamiliar number.

The caller had asked whether she was still in contact with Lily.

Margaret had refused to answer.

She had then contacted the Vespers.

At Vesper Tower, Marcus placed her message beside the old custody records.

“She believes someone is trying to reach Lily through her.”

Damian's expression hardened.

“Did the caller identify themselves?”

“No,” Marcus replied. “Margaret recorded the number. It was routed through a temporary service.”

Felix examined the call record.

“It's not the same number used for the earlier messages to Victoria. Different service, different routing.”

“So we cannot establish that the same person contacted both,” Maximilian said.

“Correct.”

Aria looked at Margaret's message again.

“Did Lily say anything about wanting to be contacted?”

“No,” Marcus answered. “Margaret has not told her about the investigation. She wants to avoid frightening her.”

“Then don't use her as a shortcut.”

Aria put the message down.

“If someone wants to speak to Lily, they can explain who they are and why. She gets to decide whether she answers.”

Helena nodded.

“Agreed.”

Octavian looked at Marcus.

“Prepare a written request through Margaret. No pressure, no security team appearing at her door, and no demand for personal records.”

Marcus made a note.

“Understood.”

Felix raised one finger.

“There is another issue.”

Everyone looked at him.

“The school record lists Lily Shaw as a child who entered formal education at four. The school retained a previous-name field, but it was deliberately restricted.”

“Can you access it?” Lucian asked.

“Not without the school's authorisation. And I won't attempt to bypass it.”

Cassian smiled faintly.

“Look at that. Responsible technology.”

Felix frowned.

“I am always responsible.”

“You once tried to install a server in the dining room.”

“It was temporary.”

“It occupied the dining room for three weeks.”

“That was a network stability issue.”

Aria stood.

“I'll leave you to your server-related trauma.”

**Sterling Mansion — 2:05 p.m.**

Victoria was reading the anonymous message for the fourth time.

*The child Jonathan took was not you.*

*It was not Aria either.*

She had initially dismissed it.

Now the words irritated her.

If the sender knew so much, why were they refusing to provide evidence?

She opened her messages.

**Victoria:** You said the child wasn't me.

The reply arrived several minutes later.

**Unknown:** I said the child Jonathan took was not the person you believe yourself to be.

Victoria stared at it.

**Victoria:** Stop playing with words. Who was the child?

No answer.

She called the number.

It was disconnected.

Victoria threw her phone onto the sofa.

Richard entered the room.

“You're still investigating?”

Victoria looked at him.

“Someone keeps telling me that the child Jonathan took wasn't me.”

Richard stopped.

“What exactly did they say?”

Victoria repeated the messages.

His expression changed.

“Did they give you any evidence?”

“No.”

“Then stop treating their statements as facts.”

Victoria narrowed her eyes.

“You knew about the child at the estate. You knew about the transfer. You knew Jonathan's name.”

“I knew what I was told.”

“And you never thought to tell me?”

“I told you what I knew.”

“You always say that.”

“Because it is true.”

Victoria stood.

“Who was Lily Shaw?”

Richard went completely still.

Victoria noticed.

“You recognise the name.”

“Where did you hear it?”

“From the investigation.”

Richard looked at her for several seconds.

“Don't contact her.”

Victoria's eyes sharpened.

“Why?”

“Because you don't know who she is.”

“Neither do you.”

Richard said nothing.

Victoria picked up her phone.

For the first time, she was no longer certain that the anonymous sender was helping her.

**Vesper Tower — 4:20 p.m.**

The original registration had not yet been recovered.

But the old custody archive had yielded one more record.

Felix placed it on the conference table.

“Eleanor Shaw's release entry includes a handwritten remark.”

He enlarged the scanned document.

*Recipient instructed to retain original under alternate identity file. No further copies to be issued.*

Below it was a signature.

**M. Voss.**

Lucian read it twice.

“She deliberately separated the registration from the custody records.”

“Yes,” Felix said. “And she ordered that no additional copies be issued.”

Maximilian examined the authorisation number.

“Can we trace the receiving office?”

“I'm working on that. The payment reference leads to a private records facility that closed nine years ago.”

“Where are its archives?” Damian asked.

“That's the problem. The company was dissolved, and its records were transferred twice. The last known custodian was a legal services firm.”

Cassian leaned back.

“So we have a missing registration, a dissolved records company, and an unknown woman who seems very fond of making paperwork disappear.”

“It's a little more complicated than that,” Felix said.

“It always is when you say it.”

Aria looked at the signature.

“Does the registration identify the child?”

“It should contain the original birth details,” Marcus replied. “But until we recover and authenticate it, we cannot say what it establishes.”

“Then recover it.”

Aria said it simply.

“No guessing. No deciding who Lily is because someone sent a mysterious message.”

Octavian nodded.

“Precisely.”

He turned toward Felix.

“Find the legal custodian. Obtain the record through proper channels.”

Felix closed the file.

“I'll start with the dissolution records.”

**Unknown Location — 9:10 p.m.**

A woman sat alone at a desk.

A locked drawer stood open.

Inside it lay an old document, carefully protected in a transparent sleeve.

The document was a birth registration extract.

She read the name printed near the top.

Then she placed it back.

Her phone vibrated.

A message appeared.

**Associate:** The Vespers have located the release record from the Sterling estate.

She typed.

**Woman:** Did they identify the recipient?

**Associate:** Not yet.

She considered the document in the drawer.

**Woman:** Keep Margaret Vale under observation. Do not approach Lily.

**Associate:** And Victoria?

The woman paused.

**Woman:** She must not see the original registration.

She locked the drawer.

For eleven years, the document had remained out of reach.

Now the Vespers were looking for it.

And the name on it was the one thing she could not afford to let them read.
