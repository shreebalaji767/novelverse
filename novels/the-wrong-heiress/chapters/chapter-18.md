# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 18 — The Second Transfer Was Never Finished**

**Thursday — 8:25 a.m.**

Aria was learning an important lesson about office life.

If someone said, “It will only take five minutes,” they were probably lying.

Peter Collins had asked her to check a client's revised expense report.

The client had apparently corrected the previous errors.

Unfortunately, they had introduced new ones.

Aria compared the old and new versions.

Then she walked to Peter's office.

“Can I ask something?”

“Of course.”

“Why did the client change the payment dates?”

Peter looked at the report.

“They shouldn't have.”

“That's what I thought.”

“Ask them for an explanation. Don't amend the records until they respond.”

Aria nodded.

“Understood.”

She returned to her desk.

Her phone vibrated.

A message from Marcus.

*We have authenticated Miriam Shaw's signature. She confirmed that the woman instructed her to keep the two collections separate. The final transfer remains unverified.*

Aria replied:

*Keep looking. Please make sure Miriam's statement is recorded accurately.*

Then she returned to work.

**9:40 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had found the missing archive reference.

The final acknowledgement had not been stored under Northstar Document Custody.

It had been registered under the original client's restricted account.

The account belonged to Crestline Administrative Partners.

Marcus looked at the screen.

“Can we access it?”

“Not directly. Crestline's records were transferred to a private archival company before dissolution.”

“Which company?”

“Halden Records Management.”

Cassian looked up.

“Another records company?”

Felix nodded.

“I know. It's getting repetitive.”

Maximilian studied the dates.

“Was the transfer legal?”

“Yes. The company acquired the physical archives through a documented insolvency process.”

“Then approach them through legal channels,” Octavian instructed.

Marcus made the call.

**11:15 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria had stopped asking Richard questions.

She had discovered that asking him directly produced the same three responses:

He didn't remember.

It was not her concern.

She should stop investigating.

Instead, she was examining the documents herself.

The Crestline correspondence contained a reference to an administrative account.

A number appeared beside it.

**CA-17.**

Victoria searched the family archive.

She found an old account register.

The entry was brief.

*CA-17 — Special administrative expenditure. Approval: R. Sterling.*

Her father had authorised it.

She took a photograph.

Then she noticed another entry beneath it.

*Final confirmation pending.*

Victoria leaned closer.

The entry had been made two weeks after the births.

She stared at the words.

Final confirmation.

What had they been waiting for?

**12:30 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Halden Records Management responded.

It had located a folder connected to Crestline's restricted account.

However, the folder contained no final acknowledgement.

Instead, it contained an inventory of documents transferred to another storage facility.

One item was marked:

**CA-17 / Final Confirmation — Original retained by authorised client representative.**

Marcus read it aloud.

“Original retained by authorised client representative.”

Damian frowned.

“So Crestline kept the original?”

“Or someone acting for Crestline did,” Marcus replied.

Maximilian examined the inventory.

“Who was the representative?”

“Not identified in the inventory.”

Octavian looked at Felix.

“Can you trace the account?”

“I can trace the administrative records. The company is dissolved, but some financial and legal records remain.”

“Proceed.”

Felix nodded.

**2:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The financial records provided a name.

**Richard Sterling.**

His signature appeared on the original approval for the CA-17 account.

Marcus placed the document before Octavian.

“Mr. Sterling authorised the account.”

Octavian studied the signature.

“Does that establish his involvement in the transfer?”

“No. It establishes that he authorised the administrative expenditure. We still need to establish what he knew and what the payment covered.”

Damian looked displeased.

“He has been withholding information.”

“Possibly,” Maximilian replied. “But we need to distinguish withholding information from direct involvement.”

Cassian examined the account summary.

“There's another payment.”

Marcus nodded.

“A final administrative payment was issued two weeks after the births. The description reads: *Completion review and custody confirmation.*”

Octavian's eyes narrowed.

“Who received it?”

“An intermediary account. We're tracing the beneficiary.”

Felix raised his hand.

Cassian looked at him.

“Again?”

“I have a result.”

“Proceed.”

“The intermediary account was held by a private legal services company called **Asterion Fiduciary Services**.”

Marcus looked at the name.

“Can you identify the client?”

“Not yet.”

**4:18 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria placed the account register on Richard's desk.

He looked at it.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

“CA-17,” she said.

Richard closed the folder he had been reading.

“Where did you get this?”

“From the archive.”

“You were told not to remove documents.”

“I'm not discussing where I found it.”

“Victoria—”

“You approved the account.”

Richard looked at the paper again.

“Yes.”

The answer surprised her.

“You admit it?”

“I authorised an administrative account. That is all.”

“What was it for?”

“Records handling.”

“Which records?”

Richard didn't answer.

Victoria stepped closer.

“Were they hospital records?”

“I don't remember the details.”

“You signed the approval.”

“Twelve years ago.”

“And two weeks after the births, someone made a final payment.”

Richard looked up.

“Where did you see that?”

“In the account register.”

He stood.

“Listen to me carefully. There are things you do not understand about that period.”

“Then explain them.”

“I cannot explain something I don't remember.”

Victoria stared at him.

“You expect me to believe that?”

“I expect you to stop.”

She shook her head.

“You don't even know what you're asking me to stop looking for.”

Richard's face tightened.

“Because I don't know what you will uncover.”

Victoria left the room.

This time, Richard did not call after her.

**6:30 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria had just finished dinner when Marcus called.

“We have identified a Sterling family approval connected to the restricted account.”

She sat down.

“Richard?”

“Yes. His signature authorised the account. We have not established whether he knew about the hospital transfers.”

“Does the account connect to the final confirmation?”

“Indirectly. The original confirmation was retained by an authorised client representative. We are tracing that person.”

“Could it have been Richard?”

“Possibly. We don't have proof.”

Aria was quiet.

“Have you told the family?”

“Mr. Vesper has been informed.”

“Good.”

Marcus paused.

“Would you like to review the documents?”

“Not yet. Tell me when you have something that explains what actually happened.”

“Of course.”

She ended the call.

Then she opened her notebook and checked her monthly budget again.

Her electricity bill was due next week.

Mysteries could wait.

Electricity could not.

**Friday — 9:12 a.m.**

Felix found the next connection.

Asterion Fiduciary Services had been dissolved, but its former director was still alive.

His name was **Graham Whitlock**.

He had retired from legal administration and was living in a private residential community.

More importantly, a company record showed that Asterion had acted as an authorised intermediary for Crestline.

Marcus looked at the document.

“Can we contact him?”

“Yes. His solicitor has already confirmed he is willing to discuss the company's old records.”

“Arrange a meeting.”

Felix opened another file.

“There is something else.”

Marcus waited.

“Asterion's account records include a payment made to an individual, not a company.”

“Name?”

Felix enlarged the entry.

**M. Voss.**

Marcus looked at him.

“The woman.”

“Possibly. The account entry gives no identifying information beyond the name.”

“Amount?”

Felix showed him.

The payment was significantly larger than the earlier administrative fees.

Marcus stared at it.

“What's the date?”

“Fourteen days after the births.”

The same date as the final confirmation entry.

Marcus immediately sent the information to Octavian.

**11:30 a.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a message.

*The Vespers have identified the Asterion payment.*

She read it once.

Then again.

“Who gave them the account details?”

“We don't know. The former records company disclosed the archive inventory.”

She put the phone down.

“Have they contacted Whitlock?”

“They have arranged a meeting.”

The woman walked to the cabinet.

“Then we have very little time.”

“Should we approach him?”

“No. He may already have told them everything.”

“What about the original confirmation?”

She turned sharply.

“Find it.”

**2:45 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Octavian read the report.

The woman had received a payment fourteen days after the births.

The final confirmation had been recorded on the same date.

But the purpose of the payment remained unknown.

“Does this prove she was the person who arranged the transfer?” Lucian asked.

“No,” Marcus replied. “It proves that someone using the name M. Voss received money from an intermediary connected to Crestline.”

“Could it be a false identity?”

“Yes.”

Octavian closed the file.

“Then find out who received the money.”

Damian looked at the payment record.

“And Whitlock?”

“Meet him tomorrow,” Octavian said. “We will ask what he remembers.”

**Saturday — 10:00 a.m.**

Graham Whitlock arrived at Vesper Tower with his solicitor.

He was a thin man in his seventies, with a careful manner and a habit of checking every document before answering a question.

Marcus introduced himself.

“Thank you for coming, Mr. Whitlock.”

“I understand this concerns Asterion Fiduciary Services.”

“It does.”

Whitlock sat.

Marcus placed the payment record on the table.

“Do you recognise this transaction?”

Whitlock adjusted his glasses.

“Yes.”

“Can you explain it?”

“It was an authorised payment.”

“To whom?”

“A person registered as M. Voss.”

“Do you remember that person?”

Whitlock considered the question.

“I remember the file. I don't remember the individual.”

“Who authorised the payment?”

Whitlock looked at the document.

“Crestline Administrative Partners.”

“Was there supporting paperwork?”

“Yes.”

“Where is it?”

Whitlock's expression changed.

“The supporting paperwork was not retained by Asterion.”

“Who retained it?”

Whitlock looked at his solicitor.

Then he answered.

“The client representative.”

Marcus leaned forward.

“Do you remember the representative's name?”

Whitlock hesitated.

“Richard Sterling.”

The room became silent.

**10:18 a.m.**

Marcus kept his expression neutral.

“Are you certain?”

“I remember meeting him. He came to our office personally.”

“Was the meeting about the payment?”

“Yes.”

“What did he say?”

Whitlock thought for a moment.

“He wanted confirmation that the payment had been made and that the recipient had acknowledged it.”

“Did he explain the purpose?”

“No. He said it was a confidential administrative matter.”

“Did he mention the hospital?”

Whitlock shook his head.

“Not to me.”

“Did he mention two children?”

“No.”

Marcus placed another document on the table.

It was the original account instruction.

“Do you recognise this?”

Whitlock examined it.

“Yes. This is the instruction we received from Crestline.”

“Who signed it?”

“Richard Sterling.”

Marcus looked at the signature.

It matched.

For the first time, there was direct evidence that Richard Sterling had personally dealt with the intermediary responsible for paying M. Voss.

But there was still no evidence establishing what he knew about the children's identities or the purpose of the transfer.

**11:06 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

After Whitlock left, Marcus briefed Octavian.

“He confirms that Richard Sterling personally collected the payment acknowledgement.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Did Whitlock say Richard knew what the payment was for?”

“No. He said Richard described it as a confidential administrative matter.”

“Did he identify M. Voss?”

“No.”

Octavian remained silent.

Lucian stood near the window.

“My father-in-law knows more than he has told us.”

“Perhaps,” Octavian replied.

Damian looked frustrated.

“Then we should ask him directly.”

“We will,” Octavian said. “But first we need to know what he collected.”

Marcus placed the final document on the desk.

It was a short receipt.

At the bottom was Richard's signature.

Above it, a handwritten line read:

*Original confirmation received and retained by client representative.*

Octavian examined it.

“Where is the original confirmation now?”

Marcus shook his head.

“We don't know.”

He looked at the receipt again.

For twelve years, Richard Sterling had possessed a document that connected him directly to the payment made to M. Voss.

And he had never mentioned it.

**8:50 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard sat alone in his study.

A drawer was open beside him.

Inside lay an old envelope.

He removed it.

The paper had yellowed at the edges.

The front bore the words:

**CA-17 — FINAL CONFIRMATION**

He opened the envelope.

The document inside contained a short statement.

*The second transfer was not completed.*

Richard stared at the sentence.

Then he looked at the signature beneath it.

**Mara Voss.**

His telephone rang.

He did not answer.

For the first time in twelve years, Richard Sterling was looking at a document that clearly contradicted the story he had told himself.

The transfer had never been completed.

And he had known that all along.
