# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 50 — The Woman Before the Dynasty**

**Friday — 9:02 a.m.**

“Anna Vesper.”

Octavian repeated the name as though the document might correct itself.

It did not.

Catherine placed the page beside the original family registry.

“Have you ever seen this name?”

“No.”

“Not in the family archives?”

“No.”

“Not in your mother's private correspondence?”

Octavian looked at her.

“My mother never mentioned an Anna Vesper.”

Catherine nodded.

“Neither did mine.”

Lucian examined the handwritten addition.

“Then why would Evelyn identify Anna as the original creator?”

“Because Evelyn inherited the responsibility,” Catherine replied. “She didn't establish the trust.”

Blackwood leaned forward.

“That's a significant distinction.”

“It's the distinction that explains everything,” Catherine said.

Felix opened the historical registry.

“I'm searching every archived family record.”

“Search the estate's property records too,” Octavian instructed. “Births, deaths, marriages, inheritances. Everything.”

Felix began typing.

Cassian looked at the document.

“Someone has managed to erase an entire person from the official family history.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“Apparently, erasing people is a recurring administrative service.”

Aria looked at the handwritten name.

“Who was she?”

Nobody answered.

**9:28 a.m.**

The first record appeared.

It was a property deed dated more than a century earlier.

The property was a small estate that had later become part of the Vesper family's central holdings.

The original owner was listed as:

**Anna Vesper.**

Felix opened the attached registry.

There was no birth date.

No death date.

No family relationship.

Only one surviving entry.

**Property transferred to Evelyn Vesper under private testamentary instruction.**

Octavian stared at the screen.

“Why wasn't this property included in the family inheritance records?”

“Because it wasn't transferred through the normal inheritance process,” Felix replied.

“Who handled the transfer?”

“An independent legal office.”

“Name?”

Felix enlarged the record.

**Mara Voss & Associates.**

Catherine went still.

“Mara.”

Lucian looked at her.

“She handled the original property transfer?”

“Yes.”

“Then she knew Anna existed.”

“Of course.”

Blackwood examined the date.

“The transfer occurred thirty-one years before the legacy trust was created.”

Octavian looked at the property description.

“Where is this estate?”

Felix brought up the location.

It was not far from the old Vesper family residence.

A place called **Greyhaven House**.

**9:51 a.m.**

Greyhaven House had been sealed for decades.

The building stood behind a stone wall, surrounded by old trees and overgrown gardens.

It had never been sold.

It had never been demolished.

It had simply disappeared from the family's active property records.

Octavian ordered an inspection.

Catherine insisted on accompanying him.

Aria and Victoria agreed to go.

Marcus looked at the arrangements.

“Four vehicles?”

“Three,” Maximilian replied.

“Security?”

“Outside the property.”

“Medical team?”

“On standby.”

Marcus nodded.

“Transport?”

“Already arranged.”

He looked at the list.

“Good. A relatively normal morning.”

Cassian glanced at him.

“You've lowered your standards.”

Marcus did not respond.

**10:37 a.m.**

The front door opened with difficulty.

Dust covered the entrance hall.

Furniture remained beneath white sheets.

The house had not been abandoned carelessly.

It had been preserved.

Catherine walked slowly through the hallway.

“I came here once.”

Octavian looked at her.

“When?”

“When I was young. Our mother brought me.”

“Why?”

“She said I needed to understand where our family came from.”

Aria looked around.

“Did she tell you about Anna?”

“No.”

“Then what did she show you?”

Catherine stopped before a portrait.

A woman stood in the painting.

She wore a simple dark dress and held a book.

She had the same distinctive eyes as Octavian.

But she was not Evelyn.

Catherine approached.

“That's her.”

Octavian stared at the portrait.

“Anna?”

“Yes.”

Victoria examined the frame.

“Why isn't there a name?”

“Because the portrait was removed from the family gallery,” Catherine replied.

Felix found a small brass plaque behind the frame.

It read:

**ANNA VESPER — FIRST KEEPER.**

Blackwood stepped closer.

“First keeper of what?”

Catherine looked toward the staircase.

“The family records.”

**11:04 a.m.**

The room upstairs contained shelves of bound ledgers.

Most were damaged by age.

Some had been carefully sealed.

Felix examined the locks.

“These aren't ordinary archive locks.”

“Can you open them?” Maximilian asked.

“Probably.”

“How long?”

“Depends.”

“On what?”

“Whether the person who designed them was sensible.”

Cassian looked at the intricate mechanism.

“Given our family history, I would prepare for disappointment.”

Felix ignored him.

He studied the lock for several minutes before finding a narrow keyhole beneath the central plate.

Catherine took a small key from her pocket.

“I have this.”

Felix stared at it.

“Where did you get that?”

“My mother gave it to me.”

“You've had it all these years?”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't you mention it?”

“Because nobody asked about a key.”

Felix took it carefully.

The lock opened.

Inside were three ledgers.

One contained property transfers.

Another contained family agreements.

The third was marked:

**THE ORIGINAL TRUST.**

Octavian opened it.

The first page contained a handwritten statement.

**I, Anna Vesper, establish this trust independently of the family estate, so that no descendant may claim ownership over the lives or choices of another.**

Aria read the sentence twice.

Victoria leaned closer.

“That's unusual.”

Blackwood nodded.

“It is.”

Octavian turned the page.

The trust's purpose was described in detail.

It protected a private collection of assets and voting rights.

But its central condition was not financial.

No family branch could control it through marriage, political appointment, corporate position or inheritance alone.

The beneficiaries would have to be identified independently.

And their consent would be required before any transfer.

Aria looked at Catherine.

“So the trust was designed to prevent exactly what happened to us.”

“Yes.”

“Then someone spent twenty-three years trying to defeat its purpose.”

Catherine nodded.

**11:32 a.m.**

The next page contained a list of names.

Three were marked as custodians.

Anna Vesper.

Evelyn Vesper.

Elias Vesper.

Beside each name was a date.

Catherine examined the final entry.

“Elias was never supposed to be the final custodian.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Who was?”

“Neither of us.”

She pointed to a fourth line.

**THE TWO DESCENDANTS.**

Victoria frowned.

“Both of us?”

“Yes.”

“Together?”

“Yes.”

Aria read the next paragraph.

The trust was designed to pass into joint custodianship once both descendants had reached adulthood and independently accepted the responsibility.

Not one successor.

Not a single heir.

Two people with equal authority.

Victoria looked at Blackwood.

“Then why did everyone keep searching for one missing heir?”

“Because the family confused the trust with the dynasty's succession,” he replied.

Octavian closed the ledger.

“That confusion was encouraged.”

Catherine looked at him.

“By whom?”

He did not answer.

**12:01 p.m.**

Felix found a sealed envelope at the bottom of the third ledger.

It was addressed to:

**THE TWO DESCENDANTS.**

The handwriting matched Anna's.

Aria opened it.

The letter was short.

It explained that the trust was not intended to reward the most powerful family member.

It was intended to protect the descendants from becoming instruments of family ambition.

The final paragraph read:

**If you have reached this letter after a separation, do not begin by restoring the inheritance. Begin by finding the person who made the separation necessary. That person may have acted wrongly, but the danger they feared may still exist.**

Victoria looked at Catherine.

“That's what Elias told us.”

Catherine nodded.

“Yes.”

Aria returned to the letter.

“Then the person who reversed the allocation may have been trying to protect us.”

“Possibly,” Blackwood said.

“But that doesn't explain why the instruction sent me to the Sterlings.”

Catherine looked at her.

“No.”

“Or why Victoria was kept at the estate.”

“No.”

“Or why Jonathan had to take her away.”

Catherine's expression tightened.

“No.”

Aria folded the letter.

“Then we're still missing the most important part.”

**12:24 p.m.**

Octavian found it in the final ledger.

A single page had been inserted between two older records.

It was a copy of an agreement dated twenty-four years earlier.

The agreement was signed by Evelyn Vesper, Elias Vesper and Catherine.

The document established a temporary protective arrangement for two expected descendants.

It contained one unusual condition.

**If the descendants are separated before independent identification, the original custodian shall not attempt reunification until the person responsible for the separation has been identified.**

Octavian looked at Catherine.

“You knew.”

“I knew there was a danger.”

“You signed this agreement.”

“Yes.”

“And you never told me?”

“I wasn't permitted to.”

“By whom?”

Catherine pointed to the final paragraph.

It was signed by Evelyn.

**This arrangement shall remain confidential from Octavian Vesper until the descendants are independently located.**

Octavian stared at the signature.

“My mother excluded me.”

“She believed you would act too quickly,” Catherine said.

“On what basis?”

“Because she knew you.”

The answer was painfully simple.

Octavian turned away.

**12:46 p.m.**

A sound came from the ground floor.

Damian immediately moved toward the stairs.

“Stay here.”

Maximilian followed him.

The others remained in the archive room.

A moment later, Damian's voice came through the security channel.

“We have an intruder.”

Catherine went pale.

“Who?”

“Unknown. They entered through the rear service door.”

Damian reached the ground floor.

A man stood in the entrance hall.

He was holding a small black case.

He did not attempt to run.

Instead, he placed the case on the floor.

“I'm here for the ledger.”

Damian raised his weapon.

“Step away from it.”

The man lifted both hands.

“I'm not armed.”

“Who sent you?”

The man looked past Damian.

His eyes settled on Catherine.

“Madam.”

She stared at him.

Then she recognised him.

“Thomas?”

The man nodded.

“I'm sorry it took so long.”

Margaret Ellison emerged from behind him.

She was alive.

And she was carrying a second ledger.

**12:53 p.m.**

Margaret placed the ledger on the table.

“This is the original allocation record.”

Felix opened it.

The first page confirmed the original arrangement.

**Subject 01 — Sterling.**

**Subject 02 — Vesper.**

The second page contained the revised allocation.

The familiar instruction was there.

**Reverse allocation. Do not notify either family.**

But beneath it was an additional line.

A line absent from every previous copy.

**The reversal shall remain temporary. Both descendants must be returned to their original intended households once the external threat has been neutralised.**

Aria stared at the document.

Victoria read it again.

“Someone intended to reverse the switch later.”

Margaret nodded.

“Yes.”

“Who?”

Margaret looked at Catherine.

“Your aunt.”

Catherine went completely still.

Octavian turned.

“Anna?”

Margaret shook her head.

“No.”

She pointed to the signature.

**CATHERINE VESPER.**

The room fell silent.

Victoria's expression hardened.

“You signed it.”

Catherine looked at the document.

Then at the two girls.

“Yes.”

**End of Chapter 50**
