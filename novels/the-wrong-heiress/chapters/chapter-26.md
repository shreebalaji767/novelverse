# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 26 — The Handover That Never Reached Its Destination**

**Friday — 8:42 a.m.**

Marcus Vale had received unusual telephone calls before.

He had been informed about hostile corporate takeovers, attempted assassinations, financial collapses, and one memorable incident involving a foreign minister who had accidentally sent confidential documents to a restaurant.

This call was different.

“Mr. Vale?”

“Yes.”

“This is Anne Porter. We spoke briefly about the hospital records.”

Marcus straightened.

“Yes, Ms. Porter. Is something wrong?”

“I remembered something.”

He glanced at the closed office door.

“What did you remember?”

“Who collected the baby.”

Marcus stopped moving.

“Could you repeat that?”

“Daniel Mercer. He collected the child after I left the hospital.”

For several seconds, Marcus said nothing.

Then he asked, carefully, “Are you certain?”

“I knew him. He worked in hospital administration. I saw him frequently.”

“Did you see him take the child?”

“Yes.”

“Then I need to ask you something important. Are you somewhere safe?”

A pause.

“I am at work.”

“Please remain there. Do not give the identification band to anyone. Do not hand over any documents or photographs. I will arrange a meeting at the location we discussed.”

“Mr. Vale?”

“Yes?”

“I don't want the child to disappear again.”

Marcus looked through the glass wall of his office.

Beyond it, the Vesper Tower continued its ordinary morning.

Employees walked between departments. Assistants carried folders. A security officer spoke into his earpiece.

An entire organisation was functioning normally.

It was about to stop.

“I understand,” Marcus said. “We will not lose sight of this.”

**9:06 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Octavian Vesper listened without interrupting.

Marcus stood beside the conference table. Lucian, Maximilian, Damian, Cassian, Felix, Helena and Isolde were present.

Aria sat near the end of the table, holding a cup of tea.

When Marcus finished, Octavian asked one question.

“Did she identify him without prompting?”

“Yes. She called me and named Daniel Mercer herself.”

Felix had already opened the hospital personnel archive.

“Mercer worked in administration during the relevant period. His personnel photograph is available.”

“Was Anne shown the photograph?” Maximilian asked.

“No,” Marcus replied. “She identified him independently.”

Damian folded his arms.

“Where is Mercer now?”

“Still unlocated.”

“Then we locate him.”

“Legally,” Lucian said.

Damian glanced at his father.

“I was going to say that.”

Cassian leaned back.

“You were going to say it with the expression of a man preparing to personally interrogate every employee of the planet.”

“I don't interrogate employees.”

“You interrogate people who look like employees.”

“That is called security.”

“That is called making receptionists nervous.”

“Enough,” Octavian said.

Silence followed.

Aria placed her cup down.

“What exactly did Anne see?”

Marcus turned to her.

“She says Mercer collected the infant after she left the hospital.”

“Did she see him carrying the baby?”

“She says she did.”

“Then ask her to explain the sequence. Not what she thinks happened. What she actually saw.”

Octavian nodded.

“Agreed.”

Aria looked at Felix's screen.

“And check whether the hospital had a transport record. If someone collected a newborn, there should have been some record of who was responsible.”

Felix nodded enthusiastically.

“I'm checking the personnel archive, transport records, old access logs, and—”

“Felix,” Maximilian interrupted.

“Yes?”

“Breathe.”

Felix inhaled.

Then immediately began typing again.

Helena reached across the table and touched Aria's hand.

“You don't have to attend the meeting if you don't want to.”

“I know.”

“It's going to be difficult.”

Aria considered that.

“I'd rather hear it directly.”

Helena nodded.

Octavian looked at Marcus.

“Arrange the meeting exactly as Anne requested. Lydia Vale must be present. Independent legal counsel. No unnecessary personnel.”

Marcus made a note.

“And the identification band?”

“Photograph it, document its condition, and obtain Anne's permission before handling it,” Aria said.

Marcus looked at her.

“Understood.”

Cassian glanced at the tea.

“Does anyone want something to eat before the investigation consumes the entire building?”

“No,” Maximilian answered.

Felix raised a hand.

“Can I have something?”

Cassian smiled.

“Of course. You're the only person here who admits to having normal human needs.”

“I need a power adapter.”

Cassian lowered his hand.

“Something edible, Felix.”

**10:18 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria Sterling was not having a pleasant morning.

She had spent forty minutes trying to access an old corporate archive and another twenty arguing with a security administrator who insisted that her surname did not constitute authorisation.

She found the distinction insulting.

Her phone rang.

An unknown number.

She answered immediately.

“Who is this?”

“Have you found Daniel Mercer?”

Victoria sat straighter.

“No.”

“Then the Vespers are ahead of you.”

Her expression hardened.

“What do you know about him?”

“More than you do.”

“Then tell me.”

“No.”

Victoria's fingers tightened around the phone.

“You contacted me because you wanted me to investigate.”

“I contacted you because you were useful.”

The words struck harder than Victoria expected.

“Excuse me?”

“Your father authorised the account. Your family has the documents. You have access to information the Vespers cannot obtain without formal requests.”

Victoria's eyes narrowed.

“You said the second child was supposed to correct the first arrangement.”

“Yes.”

“And that someone stopped it.”

“Yes.”

“Was it Daniel Mercer?”

A pause.

“Find him.”

The call ended.

Victoria stared at her screen.

She disliked being ordered around.

She disliked being used even more.

But she disliked the possibility that someone else knew the truth about her birth more than she did.

She opened her laptop again.

This time, she searched for Daniel Mercer.

**11:32 a.m. — Sterling Mansion, Richard's Study**

Richard Sterling was reading an old financial statement when Victoria entered.

“Father.”

He did not look up.

“Knock next time.”

“I did.”

“You knocked while opening the door.”

“It's still a knock.”

Richard put down the statement.

“What do you want?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

His expression changed.

Only slightly.

But Victoria noticed.

“You know him,” she said.

“I have already told you that.”

“You know more than you've told me.”

“Victoria.”

“Was he involved in the hospital transfer?”

Richard stood.

“Where did you get that name?”

“From the records.”

“Which records?”

“That's not important.”

“It is extremely important.”

She crossed her arms.

“The Vespers have found something. They know Mercer collected the second child.”

Richard went very still.

“Who told you that?”

Victoria hesitated.

He noticed.

“Someone is giving you information.”

“So?”

“So you don't know who they are.”

“I know they know more than you.”

Richard's jaw tightened.

“That is not a reason to trust them.”

Victoria scoffed.

“You told me to stop investigating. You told me you didn't remember anything. Now you expect me to believe you suddenly care about who gives me information?”

Richard looked at her for a long moment.

Then he opened the lower drawer of his desk.

Inside lay the old notebook.

He placed his hand on its cover.

“Do you know what happens when someone finds out you are searching for something they have spent twelve years hiding?”

Victoria's expression shifted.

“You're threatening me?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

“I'm telling you that someone already knows.”

He closed the drawer.

Victoria looked at it.

“What is in there?”

“Something I should have given the Vespers already.”

“Then give it to them.”

Richard did not answer.

For the first time, Victoria saw something in her father that she had never associated with him.

Fear.

**12:15 p.m. — Private Conference Centre**

Anne Porter arrived with a small handbag.

She looked tired, but composed.

Lydia Vale was already seated beside the legal representative. Marcus stood near the window, while Octavian, Lucian, Damian and Aria occupied the opposite side of the table.

A secure evidence officer waited nearby.

Anne glanced at Aria.

“Are you the girl?”

Aria nodded.

“Aria.”

Anne looked at her for a moment.

“I've seen your photograph.”

“I hope it was a good one.”

Anne gave a small, unexpected laugh.

“It's a strange thing to meet someone whose name has been in your mind for twelve years.”

Aria didn't know what to say.

So she simply nodded.

Marcus invited Anne to sit.

“Before we begin, I want to confirm that you are here voluntarily. You may stop at any time.”

“I understand.”

“Your statement will be recorded with your consent. We will distinguish what you remember personally from what you were told.”

Anne nodded.

Then she placed her handbag on the table.

“I brought the band.”

The evidence officer photographed the handbag before Anne opened it.

Inside was a small transparent pouch.

A faded hospital identification band lay within it.

Anne carefully removed the pouch and placed it on the table.

The printed words were still visible.

**INFANT B**

Below that was a reference number.

Felix, who had joined the meeting remotely, immediately requested a close-up photograph.

The evidence officer took one.

Anne watched him.

“I kept it because I thought someone would eventually ask what happened.”

Lydia stared at the band.

“You kept it all these years?”

Anne nodded.

“I couldn't explain why. I just couldn't throw it away.”

Aria looked at the tiny band.

It was an ordinary hospital object.

A strip of plastic, a printed label, a number.

Yet it was the only physical thing they had found that had been attached to the missing infant.

Marcus asked, “Ms. Porter, please tell us what happened that day.”

Anne folded her hands.

“I was assigned to the maternity ward. The baby had been marked Infant B. There was an instruction for an external handover.”

“Who presented the instruction?” Lucian asked.

“A woman.”

“Did you know her?”

“No. She had an authorisation document and knew the hospital's administrative procedures. She spoke to Mr. Mercer.”

“Did you hear their conversation?”

“Some of it.”

“What did you hear?”

“She said the child was to be transferred under a private arrangement. Mr. Mercer told her the receiving arrangements had already been confirmed.”

Anne paused.

“I remember that because I asked where the baby was going.”

“What did she say?” Aria asked.

“That the receiving authority had changed.”

The room became quiet.

Anne continued.

“She told me not to concern myself with the destination. I was responsible for ensuring the infant was handed over safely.”

“Did you agree?” Damian asked.

“I asked to see the receiving documents. She refused. I told her I wouldn't release the child without a proper authorisation.”

Anne looked at Lydia.

“Mr. Mercer then showed me an internal instruction. It had the hospital's administrative approval.”

“Was it signed?” Felix asked through the speaker.

“I don't remember the signature. I remember the stamp.”

Felix made a note.

Anne continued.

“I accompanied the infant to the administrative exit. Mr. Mercer was waiting there.”

“Was he alone?” Marcus asked.

“Yes.”

“Did he have a vehicle?”

“There was a dark vehicle outside. I couldn't see the registration number.”

“Did he take the baby?”

“Yes.”

“Did you physically hand the child to him?”

Anne nodded.

“He signed something. I remember asking him to write his name clearly because the carbon copy was difficult to read.”

Felix stopped typing.

“Do you remember what he wrote?”

“Daniel Mercer.”

Felix immediately opened the old personnel archive.

A photograph appeared on the screen.

He turned it towards the conference room camera.

“Is this the man?”

Anne leaned forward.

Her eyes settled on the photograph.

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

“That is Daniel Mercer.”

Marcus looked at the screen.

“Are you certain?”

“I worked with him for months. I would recognise him.”

Aria looked at Anne.

“What happened after you handed the baby over?”

“I returned to the ward. I thought the child was being taken to the receiving facility.”

“Did you see the vehicle leave?”

“No.”

“Did you see anyone else collect the child?”

“No.”

“Did you hear from Mercer again?”

Anne shook her head.

“Three days later, I asked about the transfer. I was told the paperwork had been closed.”

Lydia's face tightened.

“Closed?”

Anne looked at her.

“Yes.”

“Without confirmation?”

“I didn't know that at the time.”

Lydia lowered her eyes.

“I was supposed to receive that confirmation.”

Nobody spoke.

Anne took a breath.

“Later, I asked Daniel Mercer what had happened. He told me the receiving authority had changed again.”

“Did he say where the child went?” Marcus asked.

“No.”

“Did you believe him?”

Anne looked at the band.

“No.”

**1:03 p.m. — The Missing Confirmation**

Felix appeared on the conference room screen again.

He looked unusually serious.

“I found the old transport register.”

Maximilian leaned forward.

“What does it say?”

“There's a handover entry for Infant B. The receiving representative field contains Daniel Mercer's name. The destination field is blank.”

Lydia closed her eyes.

“That is impossible.”

“Apparently not,” Felix said.

“Was the entry completed?”

“Partially. There's a time, an internal authorisation number, and Mercer's signature. But the destination was never entered.”

Marcus asked, “What happened to the confirmation copy?”

“There's a note saying it was to be filed with the external transfer documents. No copy is present in the hospital archive.”

Octavian's voice was quiet.

“Was there any follow-up?”

Felix changed the screen.

“An incident note was created two days later. It states that the temporary infant reference had been closed following an external handover. It also says no subsequent identity confirmation was received.”

Lydia looked at Anne.

“You told me the baby was handed over to Mercer.”

“I did.”

“And you never saw the receiving confirmation?”

“No.”

Lydia's voice trembled.

“Neither did I.”

Anne looked down.

“I thought someone else had it.”

Aria looked at the evidence photograph.

“Can the number on the band be matched to the temporary infant reference?”

Felix nodded.

“I've compared it against the maternity index. The number matches.”

He paused.

“It's the same record.”

“Meaning?” Cassian asked.

“Infant B is the baby recorded in the maternity transfer index. This isn't an unrelated hospital band.”

Octavian's gaze sharpened.

“Does it connect to Subject 01?”

Felix looked at another document.

“The reference number is also cross-referenced in the restricted placement archive. Subject 01.”

The room fell silent.

Anne looked between them.

“Subject 01?”

Marcus answered gently.

“An internal designation used in the placement records.”

Aria kept her eyes on the screen.

“So we have a confirmed connection between Infant B and Subject 01.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“And Daniel Mercer physically collected that child.”

“Yes.”

“Then we know who had the baby after the hospital.”

Aria looked at Anne.

“Thank you for telling us.”

Anne's expression crumpled slightly.

“I should have said something sooner.”

“You didn't know where the child went.”

“I knew something was wrong.”

“You were a nurse, not the person who arranged the transfer.”

Anne looked at her.

Aria continued quietly.

“We need the person who made the decision. And we need to find the child.”

Octavian watched his granddaughter.

He said nothing.

But his hand rested firmly on the table.

**2:26 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The investigation had acquired a new direction.

Daniel Mercer was no longer merely an employee connected to altered hospital records.

He was the last independently identified person to physically receive Infant B.

Felix had assembled a preliminary timeline.

**Daniel Mercer — confirmed connections**

- Hospital administrator during the birth and transfer period.
- Held temporary access credentials used to alter hospital records.
- Received Infant B at the administrative exit.
- Signed the hospital's external handover register.
- Left the hospital's direct employment records shortly afterward.
- Continued working through Northbridge Special Projects.
- Was later transferred to Asterion Fiduciary Services under the authority of M. Voss.

Maximilian studied the timeline.

“The transfer to Asterion happened after the child was handed over.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“Which means Mercer remained connected to the people managing the arrangement.”

“Or was being managed by them,” Cassian added.

Damian pointed to the screen.

“We need his address, financial records, employment history, travel records, and known associates.”

Marcus opened his notebook.

“Legal requests are being prepared.”

“Also check whether anyone has accessed his old personnel records,” Maximilian said.

Felix nodded.

“I already started.”

Aria leaned back.

“There's something else.”

Everyone looked at her.

“Anne said Mercer told her the receiving authority had changed. The hospital instruction said the child was supposed to remain under temporary custody until confirmation.”

Lydia had provided the instruction.

Aria continued.

“So Mercer knew the destination had changed, but the hospital never received the new destination.”

Lucian nodded slowly.

“Yes.”

“Then whoever changed it must have communicated with him directly.”

Cassian smiled faintly.

“Which gives us a person who can tell us who gave the order.”

“Assuming he's willing to talk,” Damian said.

“Assuming we find him first,” Maximilian corrected.

Octavian stood.

“Find him.”

No one mistook the instruction.

**4:41 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman read the report twice.

Anne Porter had met the Vespers.

She had produced the identification band.

And she had identified Daniel Mercer.

The woman placed the report on the table.

For twelve years, the hospital handover had been buried beneath administrative closures, incomplete records, and carefully separated accounts.

Now the evidence had a name.

Daniel Mercer.

She picked up her phone.

“Find him.”

The person on the other end answered immediately.

“We have already been searching.”

“Search harder.”

“He may have changed his identity.”

“Then find the people who helped him.”

A pause.

“And Anne Porter?”

The woman looked at the photograph attached to the report.

The old hospital band.

The reference number.

The child's designation.

“Leave her alone.”

“Understood.”

The woman ended the call.

Then she opened a locked drawer.

Inside was a thin folder.

Its cover carried two words:

**SUBJECT 01**

She removed the top document.

The original placement instruction.

The same instruction that had been withheld from the hospital.

At the bottom was a handwritten note.

*If the first placement becomes permanent, the second child must not be returned.*

The woman stared at it.

Then she turned the page.

There was another note, written in a different hand.

*The receiving authority must never be informed of the original arrangement.*

She closed the folder.

For the first time that afternoon, she looked genuinely troubled.

**7:18 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard Sterling sat alone in his study.

The notebook remained in the drawer.

He had taken it out twice.

Both times, he had put it back.

His phone rang.

Unknown number.

He answered.

“You were told not to give the notebook to them.”

Richard's expression hardened.

“Who are you?”

“You already know what happens if you ignore instructions.”

“I don't know what happened to that child.”

“You know enough.”

Richard stood.

“Daniel Mercer collected the infant. I have learned that much.”

Silence.

Then the caller said, “You should have stopped looking.”

“I didn't know what I was authorising.”

“You were told to keep the families separate.”

“You told me that the arrangement was administrative.”

“It was.”

“A child was taken.”

The caller's voice became colder.

“And you were not responsible for what happened afterward.”

Richard gripped the desk.

“Who was?”

The call ended.

He stood motionless.

Then he opened the drawer.

He took out the notebook.

This time, he did not put it back.

**9:02 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria was reading the message Marcus had sent.

*Anne's identification has been formally recorded. The hospital handover register confirms Daniel Mercer received Infant B. The band matches the Subject 01 reference.*

She read it again.

Then another message arrived.

*The search for Mercer has been expanded through lawful employment, financial, and transport records.*

Aria put her phone down.

She looked at the small photograph of the Sterling family that she had kept since leaving the mansion.

It had been taken years earlier.

Everyone had been smiling.

She had once believed the photograph represented a family.

Now it represented a question she could not answer.

Who had decided that two children could be moved between families like entries in an account book?

Her phone vibrated again.

This time, the message was from an unfamiliar number.

**You should stop asking where Infant B went.**

Aria stared at it.

A second message followed.

**The child was never supposed to reach the Vespers.**

She did not reply.

She forwarded both messages to Marcus.

Then she placed the phone beside the photograph.

For twelve years, someone had known exactly where the missing child was supposed to go.

And someone had made certain the child never arrived.

**This time, the Vespers had a name.**
