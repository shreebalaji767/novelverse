# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 38 — The Photograph of Two Babies**

**Sunday — 7:45 a.m.**

Victoria had placed the photograph on the breakfast table.

Nobody was allowed to touch it.

Richard had tried.

She had slapped his hand away.

Eleanor had asked to see it.

Victoria had turned the photograph facedown.

Now the entire Sterling family was staring at it.

“Where did this come from?” Richard asked.

“An envelope delivered yesterday.”

“Who delivered it?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you contact the police?”

“No.”

Richard rubbed his forehead.

“Victoria—”

“Don't start.”

She turned the photograph over.

The back contained three lines:

*SUBJECT 02 — FIRST PLACEMENT CONFIRMED.*

*SUBJECT 01 — TRANSFER PENDING.*

*REVERSE ALLOCATION AUTHORISED.*

Eleanor looked at Richard.

“Does this mean there were two children at the hospital?”

“Yes,” he replied.

“Then why did you only know about one at the estate?”

Richard looked at the photograph.

“Because I was told one infant would be placed there temporarily. I wasn't given the second child's details.”

Victoria pointed at the picture.

“Someone knew exactly who we were. Someone deliberately changed the allocation. And someone made sure neither family knew.”

Richard had no response.

For once, Victoria wasn't exaggerating.

**Vesper Estate — 9:20 a.m.**

Felix examined the photograph.

He enlarged the hospital background, checked the date, and compared the image against the recovered maternity records.

“The photograph is genuine.”

“Can you identify the children?” Maximilian asked.

“Not from this image alone. The hospital labels are visible, but the names are obscured.”

He enlarged the lower corner.

“Wait.”

A small reference number appeared on the baby's cot.

**CW-118-B.**

Felix opened the maternity index.

“That's the temporary reference used for Infant B.”

Aria looked at the other cot.

“What about the second baby?”

Felix enhanced the image.

A different label was visible.

**CW-118-A.**

Lucian leaned forward.

“Infant A.”

“Exactly,” Felix said.

The two babies had been photographed in the same hospital room.

The photograph was taken two days after their births.

Damian studied the image.

“Why were they together?”

“Possibly routine postnatal observation,” Felix replied. “We need the original hospital record to establish that.”

Octavian looked at the photograph.

“Can we identify who took it?”

Felix examined the back.

“There is a studio processing stamp. The hospital used an external photography service at the time.”

Marcus wrote down the company name.

“Find the photographer.”

**10:40 a.m.**

The former photography service had closed fifteen years earlier.

Its owner, however, was alive.

His name was George Miller.

He remembered the hospital contract.

He remembered taking photographs of newborns.

And he remembered one particular instruction.

“A woman came to the studio,” he said during the video interview. “She requested copies of two photographs from the same hospital room.”

“Did she identify the children?” Marcus asked.

“No. She gave me reference numbers.”

“Do you remember her?”

“Not clearly. Dark hair. Gloves. Very formal.”

Felix exchanged a glance with Damian.

Mara Voss.

George continued.

“She wanted the photographs separated. One copy was to go to the hospital archive. The other was to be delivered privately.”

“Where?”

“I don't remember the address. But I kept the order book.”

He disappeared from the camera.

A moment later, he returned with an old ledger.

He opened it.

The entry was still there.

**CW-118-A / CW-118-B — PRIVATE DUPLICATE**

The recipient field contained one name.

**A. MERCER.**

**12:15 p.m.**

Arthur Mercer's estate records had already been secured by the Vespers' legal team.

Felix searched the archived inventory.

He found a photograph envelope.

It contained two additional prints.

The first showed Infant A.

The second showed Infant B.

On the back of the first photograph was a handwritten note:

*Original allocation: Vesper — Sterling.*

On the second:

*Revised allocation: Sterling — Vesper.*

Aria stared at the notes.

“So they switched the intended destinations.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“Who wrote them?”

“The handwriting matches neither Mara Voss nor Arthur Mercer.”

“Can we identify it?”

“Not yet.”

Octavian examined the photographs.

“Someone changed the allocation after the original arrangement had been approved.”

Lucian looked at the documents.

“And the families were never informed.”

“Correct,” Marcus replied.

Aria pointed to the second note.

“Then why did Victoria end up at the Sterling estate?”

“Because the revised allocation was never completed,” Felix said. “Infant B was delivered to the estate, but the later transfer to the Vespers was interrupted.”

“And I was already with the Sterlings.”

“Yes.”

Aria leaned back.

“So the original plan was for me to go to the Vespers and Victoria to the Sterlings. Someone reversed it. I ended up with the Sterlings, and Victoria was taken away before the second placement could happen.”

“That is consistent with the records,” Felix said.

Victoria, who had joined the meeting, looked at the photographs.

“Who reversed it?”

Nobody answered.

**1:50 p.m.**

Margaret Vale had brought another document.

It was a letter Jonathan had written shortly before his death.

She had discovered it among his personal papers after the earlier investigation began.

“I didn't understand why he kept it,” she said. “I thought it was an unfinished note.”

Victoria took the letter.

The handwriting was familiar.

*I was told to deliver the child to the receiving family. When I arrived, the instruction had changed. The child was to remain under temporary custody until the second placement was confirmed.*

A second paragraph followed.

*I was then told the second child would be taken elsewhere. I asked who had authorised the change. I was told the instruction came from the originating authority.*

Victoria looked up.

“Did he say who told him?”

Margaret shook her head.

“No.”

Victoria continued reading.

*I did not believe the child would be safe if I followed the revised instruction. I took her to someone who would protect her. I know this was not what I was supposed to do. But I could not leave her there.*

The letter ended without a signature.

Victoria folded it carefully.

“He saved me.”

Margaret nodded.

“Yes.”

Victoria looked down at the photograph.

“Then I owe him more than I ever knew.”

**3:25 p.m.**

The Aurelius Consortium received another legal request.

This time, the Vespers had included the photograph references, the original allocation notes, and the payment records.

The request demanded access to Schedule B and the identity of the originating authority.

The consortium's legal department convened an emergency review.

A senior partner read the documents.

Then he turned to the executive responsible for the archived trust.

“Did you know the allocation had been reversed?”

The executive hesitated.

“I knew the schedule had been amended.”

“Who authorised the amendment?”

“The originating authority.”

“Name?”

The executive looked away.

“The name is restricted.”

The partner closed the file.

“Twenty years of secrecy, two missing children, and now the Vespers have evidence of a deliberate allocation reversal. You cannot keep calling this an ordinary confidential arrangement.”

The executive said nothing.

The partner stood.

“Preserve every record. Nothing is to be destroyed.”

**5:10 p.m.**

Aria found Victoria in the garden.

Victoria was holding Jonathan's letter.

“He took me away,” Victoria said.

“Yes.”

“He broke the instruction.”

“Yes.”

“He could have been punished.”

Aria sat beside her.

“He probably knew.”

Victoria looked at the letter.

“I spent years thinking I had been abandoned.”

Margaret had told her Jonathan brought her to safety, but she had never known how much risk he had taken.

“He didn't abandon you,” Aria said.

Victoria nodded.

For a while, neither spoke.

Then Victoria asked, “Do you think the person who changed the allocation wanted us separated?”

Aria considered it.

“I think they wanted something to happen. I don't know what.”

Victoria looked toward the house.

“Everyone keeps saying the same thing.”

“Because it's true.”

Victoria gave a tired laugh.

“You're very annoying when you're reasonable.”

“I've been told.”

**7:40 p.m.**

Felix received a response from Aurelius.

The consortium had agreed to preserve Schedule B but refused immediate disclosure.

However, it had confirmed one important detail.

The amended allocation had been authorised by a private trust representative.

The representative's authority had originated from a document signed before the children's births.

The signature belonged to a person named **Elias Vesper**.

Octavian stood abruptly.

The room fell silent.

Lucian looked at him.

“Elias?”

Octavian took the document.

He examined the signature.

Then he sat down slowly.

“Elias was my younger brother.”

Aria looked at him.

“Was?”

“He died twenty-two years ago.”

Maximilian frowned.

“Why would he have authority over the arrangement?”

Octavian stared at the signature.

“He wouldn't.”

Cassian's usual smile had disappeared.

“Could the signature be forged?”

Octavian examined it again.

“I know my brother's handwriting.”

He looked toward Marcus.

“Find every document connected to Elias Vesper. Every trust, every company, every instruction he issued before his death.”

Marcus nodded.

“Immediately.”

Octavian remained seated.

For the first time since the investigation began, he looked genuinely shaken.

**Unknown Location — 11:55 p.m.**

Mara Voss received the message.

**Associate:** The Vespers have identified Elias Vesper.

She read it twice.

Then she opened a locked cabinet.

Inside was a file marked:

**ORIGINATING AUTHORITY — E. VESPER**

She removed the final document.

It contained a handwritten instruction.

*The children must be separated until the inheritance matter is resolved.*

Mara looked at the date.

It had been signed three months before the births.

She placed the document on the desk.

Then she made a call.

“They have found Elias's signature.”

The voice on the other end remained silent.

Mara continued.

“They will discover the trust arrangement next.”

A pause.

Then the voice asked:

“Do they know what the inheritance matter was?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Mara looked at the document.

“Because if they find out why Elias wanted the children separated, the family will turn against itself.”

She ended the call.

And for the first time, the mystery was no longer about who had switched the babies.

It was about what someone had been trying to protect them from.
