# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 8 — The Woman Who Had Never Existed**

**Monday — 8:05 a.m.**

Aria's first day at work began with a minor disaster.

She had set three alarms.

All three had worked perfectly.

Unfortunately, she had turned them off and gone back to sleep.

She woke up twenty minutes later than planned.

"Wonderful."

She dressed quickly, grabbed her lunch, checked her documents and hurried out.

At 8:32, she was on the bus.

At 9:04, she reached the office.

At 9:08, she was standing in front of Peter Collins.

He glanced at the clock.

"Good morning, Miss Sterling."

"Good morning. I'm sorry. The bus was delayed."

"You're four minutes early."

Aria blinked.

"Oh."

Peter smiled.

"Welcome aboard."

He introduced her to the other employees, showed her the accounting software and explained the firm's filing procedures.

Her desk was small.

Her computer was old.

Her first assignment involved sorting invoices from the previous month.

Aria looked at the stack of documents.

Then at Peter.

"How many?"

"About two hundred."

"All right."

"Is that a problem?"

"No. I was just checking whether I should cancel my retirement plans."

Peter laughed.

By 9:30, she was working.

**Vesper Tower — 9:45 a.m.**

Victoria Sterling had made another decision.

She was going to find out who had been sending her messages.

The unknown number had stopped responding after the last exchange.

She had tried calling it.

The number was unavailable.

She had searched it online.

Nothing.

She had even asked a private investigator employed by the Sterling family to identify the sender.

The investigator had requested more information.

Victoria had none.

She was not accustomed to being unable to obtain something simply because she wanted it.

She entered a small café near Vesper Tower and sat at a corner table.

Her phone vibrated.

**Unknown:** Stop contacting the Vesper Foundation.

Victoria immediately typed.

**Victoria:** Who are you?

**Unknown:** Someone who knows what happened twenty years ago.

Victoria leaned forward.

**Victoria:** What happened?

There was a pause.

**Unknown:** Your family was never supposed to receive the wrong child.

Victoria stopped breathing for a moment.

She read the sentence again.

Then she typed.

**Victoria:** What does that mean?

No reply.

A minute passed.

Then another message appeared.

**Unknown:** Find the woman who signed the original hospital transfer.

Victoria stared at the screen.

**Victoria:** Name?

The reply came quickly.

**Unknown:** There is no name.

Victoria frowned.

**Victoria:** That makes no sense.

**Unknown:** Exactly.

The conversation ended.

Victoria sat motionless.

For the first time, the mystery surrounding Aria's birth seemed larger than a family dispute.

**Vesper Estate — 10:30 a.m.**

Damian stood in the investigation room with a printed archive inventory.

"There's a discrepancy."

Maximilian looked up.

"Which one?"

"The secondary storage facility has provided its original intake list. One file was recorded as received, but it was never entered into the digital catalogue."

"Which file?"

"Hospital transfer authorisations from the year Aria and Victoria were born."

Felix turned from his computer.

"Can they locate it?"

"They're checking physical storage."

Marcus looked at the inventory.

"Who requested the file?"

"That information is missing."

"Convenient," Cassian remarked.

Damian ignored him.

"The archive company says the box was delivered with a damaged seal. They documented the damage but didn't reject the shipment."

Maximilian frowned.

"Why?"

"Because the delivery was authorised by the hospital's legal department."

"Do we have the delivery receipt?"

"Yes."

Damian placed it on the table.

The signature belonged to a person named **Miriam Shaw**.

Felix searched the hospital's historical staff directory.

"She worked in the hospital's legal administration department."

"Current whereabouts?" Maximilian asked.

"Unknown."

Damian looked at the receipt.

"She left the hospital two months after the births."

"Was she connected to Mercer?"

"We haven't established that."

Marcus studied the signature.

"Then we find her."

**Vesper Tower — 11:15 a.m.**

Octavian arrived for a meeting with the senior family executives.

Lucian was already seated.

Maximilian presented the investigation findings.

"We have three confirmed points. Daniel Mercer received temporary system access before the births. His credentials were used in the records system. The archive transfer was later handled by a contractor connected to his former employer."

"And Miriam Shaw?" Octavian asked.

"She authorised the delivery of the hospital records to the archive facility."

"Do we know whether she knew what was inside?"

"No."

Octavian looked at Damian.

"Find her."

"We are checking employment records, tax filings and public business registrations."

"Good."

Lucian leaned forward.

"If she was involved, she may be in danger."

"Or she may be entirely innocent," Maximilian replied.

Octavian nodded.

"Both possibilities must be treated seriously. No intimidation. No threats. We want answers, not frightened witnesses."

Marcus made a note.

Then Octavian added:

"And no one contacts Aria about this until we have something substantial."

**Aria's Workplace — 12:40 p.m.**

Aria was discovering that office work involved an unreasonable number of passwords.

She had already been given access to:

- The accounting system.
- The shared document folder.
- The invoice archive.
- The staff scheduling portal.

She had also been told to change her temporary password.

The system rejected her first choice.

It rejected her second.

It rejected her third.

Aria stared at the monitor.

"Are you protecting the company or hiding nuclear launch codes?"

Her colleague, a woman named Rachel, laughed from the next desk.

"Don't worry. You'll get used to it."

"That's what people say before something becomes permanent."

Rachel smiled.

"You're going to fit in."

Aria returned to the invoices.

At 1:15, she discovered that two supplier payments had been entered twice.

She checked the supporting documents, marked the duplicate entries and informed Peter.

He examined her notes.

"Good catch."

"Both payments have the same invoice number."

"You're right."

"Should I flag the records?"

"Please."

Aria returned to her desk.

It was a small thing.

But it felt satisfying to solve a problem that had a clear answer.

**Sterling Mansion — 2:00 p.m.**

Victoria was searching through the Sterling family's old documents.

She had asked Richard about the hospital where she was born.

He had told her the family had already received all the available records.

She did not believe that.

She found a folder containing her birth certificate, medical summary and several administrative forms.

She checked the dates.

Everything appeared normal.

Then she noticed a hospital name printed on the medical summary.

It was not the hospital mentioned in the family documents.

Victoria frowned.

She called Eleanor.

"Mother?"

"Yes?"

"Which hospital was I born in?"

Eleanor looked up from her book.

"The Central Women's Hospital. Why?"

Victoria showed her the document.

"Then why does this say Westbridge Medical Centre?"

Eleanor took the paper.

She examined it.

"I don't know. Perhaps it was a temporary administrative office."

"That's not what it says."

Eleanor read the document again.

The page was an old transfer summary, dated three days after the births.

It contained no explanation.

Only a reference number.

Victoria felt a strange excitement.

She had found something.

Something that might matter.

**Vesper Estate — 4:25 p.m.**

Felix was reviewing digitised hospital records when he noticed a familiar reference number.

He opened the file.

Then he opened the image Victoria had not seen.

The number matched.

It was a transfer reference from Westbridge Medical Centre.

He called Damian.

"Come here."

Damian entered.

"What is it?"

"Look at this."

Felix displayed the document.

It was an administrative transfer summary.

The hospital name was Westbridge Medical Centre.

The date was three days after the births.

The reference number matched the one on the archive receipt.

Damian examined it carefully.

"Why would a maternity record be transferred there?"

"That's what I'm trying to establish."

"Was Westbridge part of the same hospital network?"

"No. It was an independent medical records facility."

Damian looked at him.

"Does it still exist?"

Felix searched.

"Not under that name."

"Find its successor."

Felix began typing.

A few minutes later, he stopped.

"That's interesting."

"What?"

"Westbridge Medical Centre was not a hospital."

Damian looked at the screen.

"What?"

"It was a private medical records processing office. It handled external documentation and patient file transfers."

"Who owned it?"

Felix opened the corporate record.

"Westbridge Administrative Services. Dissolved twenty-one years ago."

"Director?"

"One listed director. Miriam Shaw."

Damian stared at the screen.

The same Miriam Shaw who had authorised the archive transfer.

He called Maximilian.

"We have a connection."

**Vesper Estate — 5:10 p.m.**

The family gathered in the study.

Damian explained the discovery.

"Miriam Shaw worked for the hospital, authorised the transfer of records, and later became the listed director of the private company that received those records."

Octavian's expression remained unreadable.

"Was the company created before or after the births?"

"Three months before."

"Who funded it?"

"That is under investigation."

Lucian looked troubled.

"She could have been responsible for moving the records."

"Yes," Damian said. "But that does not establish why."

Maximilian folded his arms.

"Could she have been protecting Aria?"

Cassian looked at him.

"Or hiding evidence."

"Both are possible."

Octavian looked at Marcus.

"Has Aria been informed?"

"No."

"Keep it that way until we know what we are dealing with."

**Sterling Mansion — 7:30 p.m.**

Victoria sat at the dining table.

Richard was reviewing a report.

Eleanor was speaking to Adrian.

Victoria placed the old transfer summary in front of her father.

"Have you seen this?"

Richard glanced at it.

"What is it?"

"A hospital transfer record."

He picked it up.

His expression changed slightly.

"Where did you find this?"

"In the family documents."

Richard read it again.

"Why are you looking through these?"

"Because something isn't right."

"Victoria, the hospital investigation is being handled by the Vespers."

"That is exactly the problem."

Richard put down the paper.

"What are you suggesting?"

"That there are records we haven't seen."

Richard looked at her.

"Do not start investigating matters you don't understand."

Victoria's eyes narrowed.

"I understand that my birth records have a reference to a company that no longer exists."

Richard said nothing.

For a moment, she wondered whether he knew something.

Then he returned the document.

"Leave it alone."

Victoria took it.

She did not intend to.

**Aria's Apartment — 8:55 p.m.**

Aria was eating dinner when her phone rang.

Helena Vesper.

She answered.

"Hello?"

"Good evening, Aria. I hope I'm not disturbing you."

"No. I'm eating."

"How was your first day at work?"

Aria smiled faintly.

"Busy. I found two duplicate payments."

"That sounds important."

"It was mostly paperwork."

"Did you enjoy it?"

"I think so. The people are nice."

Helena sounded relieved.

"I'm glad."

There was a brief pause.

Then Helena asked:

"Would you like to come for lunch next Sunday? Just the family, if you're comfortable."

Aria thought about it.

"How many people?"

Helena laughed.

"Your grandparents, your father, your brothers and me."

"That's already more than six."

"Yes."

"Are there going to be additional guests?"

"No."

"Are you certain?"

"I promise."

Aria smiled.

"All right. Sunday."

Helena's voice softened.

"Thank you."

"Goodnight, Helena."

"Goodnight, sweetheart."

After the call, Aria returned to her dinner.

She was beginning to recognise the sound of Helena's voice.

That was something.

**Unknown Location — 11:20 p.m.**

A woman sat at a desk in a dimly lit room.

An old document lay open before her.

She looked at a photograph of two newborn babies.

Then she looked at a handwritten note.

**Miriam Shaw — Locate immediately.**

Her phone vibrated.

A message appeared.

**Unknown:** The Vespers have found the transfer records.

The woman closed her eyes.

Another message followed.

**Unknown:** They are asking about Westbridge.

She stood and walked to a locked cabinet.

Inside were several folders.

One was labelled with a date from twenty years ago.

Another contained a photograph of a young woman.

The woman took the photograph out.

She looked at it for a long time.

Then she placed it inside her handbag.

Her phone vibrated again.

**Unknown:** What should we do?

She typed a reply.

**Woman:** Nothing.

She paused.

Then added:

**Woman:** Not until I know whether the girl remembers.

She turned off the light.

And for the first time in twenty years, someone who had been hiding from the Vespers began preparing to meet Aria.
