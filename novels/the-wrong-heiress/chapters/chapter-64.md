# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 64 — The Envelope That Had Been Returned**

**Monday — 8:16 a.m.**

The cabinet at Greyhaven Storage Annex was still sealed.

That was the first thing Damian reported.

The second was considerably less reassuring.

“The seal is original,” he said over the secure call. “But the cabinet has been opened from the inside.”

Thomas, standing beside him, looked at the locking mechanism.

“Not opened from the inside. The rear panel has been removed and replaced.”

Felix leaned closer to the screen.

“Can you tell when?”

“Not yet.”

“Can you tell who did it?”

Thomas looked at the camera.

“Would you like me to ask the cabinet?”

Felix paused.

“That would be unproductive.”

“Good. We agree.”

Aria sat at the end of the conference table, drinking coffee.

“Can we establish whether the envelope is actually inside?”

Damian nodded.

“After the preservation team documents the cabinet.”

“Then do that.”

Octavian watched her.

“You seem unusually unconcerned.”

“I'm concerned. I'm just not interested in spending the morning arguing with furniture.”

Cassian lowered his head to hide a smile.

Maximilian did not.

“Focus.”

**9:02 a.m. — Greyhaven Storage Annex**

The preservation team completed its inspection.

The cabinet contained three items:

- The original Northstar envelope.
- A thin document sleeve.
- A small metal key attached to a paper tag.

Thomas examined the envelope first.

Its outer seal was intact.

The document sleeve, however, had been opened and resealed.

Margaret checked the label.

“That's not Northstar's original sleeve.”

Damian looked at her.

“How can you tell?”

“The old archive used blue security paper. This is grey.”

Thomas turned the sleeve over.

A handwritten reference appeared on the back.

**E.V. — PRIVATE CORRESPONDENCE**

Margaret went still.

“Evelyn Vesper.”

“Are you certain?” Damian asked.

“No. But the initials and format are consistent with her private correspondence.”

Thomas examined the metal key.

The tag read:

**ROOM 17 — PRIVATE ARCHIVE**

Felix, listening remotely, immediately searched the old facility records.

“There's no Room 17 in the Greyhaven building.”

Margaret considered the tag.

“Then it may refer to another archive.”

“Which one?”

She looked at Octavian's name on the secure call.

“The original Vesper family archive.”

**10:14 a.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian received the key in a sealed evidence container.

He examined the tag without touching the metal.

“Room 17.”

Alistair Monroe, the family historian, was summoned.

He arrived carrying an old catalogue.

“Room 17 was part of the original family archive,” he said. “It was reserved for private custodial correspondence.”

“Does it still exist?” Lucian asked.

“The room exists. The archive was reorganised twice, but that section was never formally decommissioned.”

Felix appeared on the large display.

“Can we access its catalogue?”

Alistair shook his head.

“Not through the current system. Room 17 was excluded from the modern inventory.”

“Why?”

“Because it wasn't an ordinary records room. Access was restricted to the original custodian and one designated administrative officer.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Who was the administrative officer?”

Alistair opened the catalogue.

“According to the surviving register, Margaret Ellison.”

Everyone turned towards her.

Margaret stared at the page.

“I never had a key to Room 17.”

Alistair checked the entry.

“Your name is listed as the designated officer.”

“I was appointed to handle administrative correspondence. I was never given independent access to the room.”

Octavian looked at the key.

“Then someone may have used your appointment to gain access.”

Margaret shook her head.

“Or someone wanted the records to suggest that I had.”

Aria leaned back.

“Can we check whether the key actually opens the room?”

Alistair nodded.

“We can.”

“Then perhaps we should do that before deciding who stole what.”

**11:37 a.m. — Original Vesper Family Archive**

Room 17 was behind a narrow corridor on the archive's oldest floor.

Its door was dark wood, with a brass identification plate.

The plate had no modern electronic lock.

Only a small keyhole.

Alistair stood beside Octavian.

“This room has not been entered in more than twenty years.”

Thomas looked at the door.

“According to the records?”

“According to the access register.”

Thomas gave him a brief look.

“That distinction seems important today.”

The metal key turned.

The lock opened.

Inside, the room was almost entirely empty.

A long desk stood beneath a covered window. Along the walls were narrow cabinets, most of them locked.

A single document box sat on the desk.

Its label read:

**EVELYN VESPER — CUSTODIAL CORRESPONDENCE**

Octavian stopped.

He approached the box slowly.

His mother's name had been written in her distinctive hand.

He recognised it immediately.

But recognition was not proof that the contents had remained untouched.

“Photograph everything,” he instructed.

Felix's voice came through the communication system.

“Already recording.”

Thomas checked the desk.

“There's no dust beneath the box.”

Margaret looked at him.

“Meaning?”

“Someone placed it here recently.”

Octavian opened the box.

Inside lay a thin collection of letters, several folded documents and a small notebook.

The first document was dated twenty-four years earlier.

It was a custodial instruction.

**The two descendants shall remain independent of either family branch until their identities and circumstances are established. Neither shall be compelled to accept responsibility for the trust.**

Aria read it twice.

Then she looked at Octavian.

“Your mother wrote that?”

“Yes.”

“Then she understood the problem.”

Octavian's expression softened.

“She understood it better than most.”

Victoria picked up the next page.

“There's something else.”

It was a list of correspondence references.

One entry had been circled.

**NC-2086 / PRIV-07**

The original Northstar envelope.

Beside it was a date.

The same date as the recorded release.

And beneath it, a handwritten instruction:

**Return to Room 17. Do not place in the central archive.**

**12:06 p.m.**

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Then Felix broke the silence.

“Wait.”

He enlarged the document on his screen.

“The envelope was supposed to return here.”

Margaret nodded slowly.

“That explains the dispatch to Greyhaven. The envelope was sent back to the original archive after its temporary custody period.”

Thomas looked at the metal key.

“But why was it stored at the annex?”

Alistair examined the instruction.

“Room 17 may have been inaccessible at the time. The annex could have been used as temporary storage.”

Octavian looked at the release record.

“Then the missing recipient was not necessarily a person.”

“Correct,” Felix said. “The recipient could have been the archive itself.”

Aria looked at the document.

“Then the missing receipt doesn't prove someone stole it.”

“No,” Felix replied. “But the false courier identity and the altered storage records remain unexplained.”

Victoria pointed to the circled reference.

“Who ordered it returned?”

Octavian examined the signature.

It was simply:

**E.V.**

The same initials as the original deposit instruction.

Margaret looked at the handwriting.

“I believe that is Evelyn's.”

Catherine remained silent.

Octavian noticed.

“Catherine?”

She was examining the notebook.

“There's another date.”

She turned it towards him.

The entry was brief.

**The envelope must not be opened until the second transfer is confirmed. If the transfer fails, return it to the original custodian.**

Below it was another sentence.

**If the original custodian cannot act, no substitute may release the correspondence without the consent of both descendants.**

Aria looked up.

“That's clear enough.”

Victoria nodded.

“Then why was it released under EX-04?”

Octavian's face hardened.

“Because someone used an emergency authority to bypass the instruction.”

**1:18 p.m. — Room 17**

Felix had begun scanning the remaining documents.

He stopped at a folded sheet.

“Grandfather. I found a transfer record.”

Octavian turned.

Felix placed it on the display.

It contained two columns.

**FIRST TRANSFER — COMPLETED**

**SECOND TRANSFER — NOT COMPLETED**

The first transfer referred to the movement of the original envelope from Central Women's Hospital to Northstar.

The second referred to a separate transfer of custodial authority.

A name had been written beside the second entry.

**MARA VOSS.**

Catherine stood abruptly.

“Show me.”

Felix enlarged the signature.

Catherine stared at it.

“That is Mara's handwriting.”

“Are you certain?” Lucian asked.

“I worked with her for years.”

She looked at the date.

“This was the authority she was supposed to receive.”

Margaret approached.

“Then Mara was not simply preserving the hospital records.”

“No,” Catherine replied. “She was meant to become the independent custodian of the sealed correspondence.”

Thomas looked at the record.

“But the transfer was never completed.”

Catherine nodded.

“Which means she never formally received the authority.”

Felix opened another document.

A handwritten note had been attached to the transfer record.

**Do not proceed. Receiving custodian unavailable. Maintain original protection.**

The note was signed:

**E. VESPER.**

Octavian closed his eyes briefly.

His mother had stopped the transfer.

But why?

**2:03 p.m.**

Alistair opened the notebook's final section.

A page had been folded inward.

The writing was faint but legible.

**If Mara cannot be appointed, the correspondence must remain sealed. The descendants must not be informed until the threat is identified.**

Beneath it was a second entry, written in a different hand.

**Elias has agreed to maintain the restriction.**

Octavian looked at the date.

“Elias agreed?”

Alistair nodded.

“That appears to be what the record says.”

Catherine examined the second handwriting.

“I recognise it.”

“Whose?” Aria asked.

“Elias.”

Lucian looked at his father.

“Then Elias knew the envelope was not to be released.”

“Or he knew the restriction existed,” Catherine corrected. “That does not prove he personally approved every later action.”

Felix nodded.

“The emergency release happened six weeks after the original deposit. If Elias had access to EX-04 at that time, someone could have used his authority without his knowledge.”

Thomas looked towards the cabinet.

“And the courier identity was already compromised.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“So the envelope was supposed to stay here, but someone moved it, recorded a fake delivery, and then brought it back.”

Margaret shook her head.

“Not quite. The records suggest it was returned here through the archive's dispatch system. The person who physically placed it in Room 17 may have been someone else.”

Aria looked at the empty document sleeve.

“Then the person who brought it back might have wanted us to find the key.”

Catherine turned towards her.

“Why would they?”

“Because they left the key with the envelope. They knew we'd eventually open the cabinet.”

Octavian looked at the evidence.

It was a reasonable possibility.

Not a conclusion.

**3:26 p.m. — Vesper Estate, Private Study**

The family gathered around the table.

Felix had prepared a timeline.

For once, he had limited it to a single screen.

Maximilian looked impressed.

“Only one screen?”

“I have learned that people stop listening after the third.”

Cassian nodded solemnly.

“A tragedy for your profession.”

Felix ignored him.

“The original envelope was deposited at Northstar. Its first transfer was completed. The second transfer of custodial authority to Mara Voss was never completed.”

He displayed the next record.

“Evelyn ordered the envelope returned to Room 17. The Northstar release was processed under EX-04, using an incomplete recipient record and a courier identity belonging to Thomas Reed.”

Thomas nodded.

“Which I did not authorise.”

“Correct. The envelope was eventually placed in the Greyhaven annex, then returned to Room 17.”

Catherine looked at the timeline.

“We still need to establish who moved it.”

“And why,” Lucian added.

Aria examined the final document.

“What was in the envelope?”

Nobody answered.

The original seal remained intact.

The contents were still unknown.

Octavian looked towards the evidence container.

“We open it.”

Catherine shook her head.

“Not yet.”

Octavian turned.

“We have established its chain of custody as far as possible.”

“We have established the chain of records. Not the chain of physical custody.”

Thomas supported her.

“The outer seal is intact, but the sleeve was replaced. We should have the materials examined first.”

Felix nodded.

“I can verify whether the seal is original.”

Aria looked at Octavian.

“You wanted the truth. Waiting one more day to make sure the document hasn't been tampered with seems reasonable.”

Octavian studied her.

Then he nodded.

“Very well.”

Victoria glanced at Aria.

“That's twice today you've prevented someone from opening something.”

“I'm developing a reputation.”

“An irritating one.”

“Thank you.”

**5:44 p.m. — Vesper Estate, Archive Office**

Felix received an incoming message.

He read it.

Then read it again.

“Grandfather.”

Octavian looked up.

“What?”

“The old Northstar system has produced another record.”

Felix projected it.

It was a correspondence-routing slip dated the same day the envelope had been released.

The recipient field was blank.

But the sender field contained a name.

**MARA VOSS.**

Catherine moved closer.

“That is impossible.”

Felix looked at her.

“Why?”

“Mara's authority had already been suspended.”

“Then someone used her credentials.”

Catherine examined the routing slip.

“No. Look at the signature.”

A handwritten signature appeared at the bottom.

Mara Voss.

Catherine's expression changed.

“That is her signature.”

Octavian stared at the document.

“Could it have been forged?”

“Yes.”

“Could someone have obtained an original signature?”

“Yes.”

“Then what makes you certain?”

Catherine pointed to the date.

“This was signed before the second transfer was cancelled.”

She looked at the others.

“Mara was still alive and authorised to act when this instruction was issued.”

The room fell silent.

Felix opened the associated routing note.

It contained one additional line.

**Correspondence to be retained until direct confirmation from both descendants.**

Aria read it.

“Both descendants?”

Victoria looked at her.

“Us.”

Catherine nodded.

“Yes.”

Octavian stared at the signature.

For years, the family had believed the missing correspondence concerned the protection of two infants.

Now it appeared that someone had intended the two girls themselves to confirm its release.

And the person who had signed the original instruction might have been alive when the arrangement was created.

Catherine spoke quietly.

“If Mara signed this herself, then she knew exactly what the envelope contained.”

Felix looked at the screen.

“And she may still know.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“Then we should find her.”

Catherine did not answer immediately.

She was staring at the routing slip.

Finally, she said:

“There's one problem.”

Octavian looked at her.

“What?”

Catherine pointed to the signature's date.

“Mara Voss was officially declared dead six months before this document was signed.”

**And someone had continued signing in her name.**
