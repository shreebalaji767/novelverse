# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 61 — The Man Who Borrowed a Name**

**Saturday — 8:43 p.m.**

Dorian read the photograph again.

Then he put it down.

“My father just said the man introduced himself as me.”

Martin nodded.

“Yes.”

“And the man in the hospital photograph resembles me.”

“Yes.”

“And both incidents happened around the same period.”

“Yes.”

Dorian looked at Thomas.

“Does anyone else find this increasingly personal?”

Thomas considered the question.

“Your identity has been used in at least two separate administrative incidents.”

“That wasn't the part I meant.”

Martin returned the photograph to its envelope.

“I never met the man again.”

“Did you report him?”

“I reported the attempted impersonation to the review committee.”

“And?”

“They told me the matter would be handled internally.”

Dorian gave a short, humourless laugh.

“Of course they did.”

Martin looked at his son.

“I should have told you.”

“Yes.”

“I believed the matter had ended.”

“It clearly didn't.”

Martin lowered his eyes.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Dorian asked, “Did he sound like me?”

Martin frowned.

“What?”

“The man. Did he sound like me?”

“No. His voice was different.”

“Then how did you believe he was me?”

“I didn't.”

Dorian looked up.

Martin continued.

“I knew he was lying. He showed me a letter bearing your name and credentials. I asked him to identify himself. He gave me your name, but he never claimed to be you personally.”

Thomas leaned forward.

“Then he was using Hale's authority, not necessarily impersonating Hale face-to-face.”

Martin nodded.

“Exactly.”

Dorian looked at the hospital photograph.

“So the person in the corridor might not be the same person who approached my father.”

“Correct,” Thomas said.

“Someone could have used my credentials in one place and my appearance in another.”

“Or the photograph may have been misidentified,” Margaret added.

Dorian sighed.

“I preferred it when I was merely being accused of signing documents.”

**Sunday — 7:32 a.m.**

**Vesper Estate**

Felix had not slept much.

His desk contained the hospital report, the rejected death verification, the emergency review record and a growing collection of notes.

Maximilian entered.

“Have you found anything?”

“Yes.”

“Something useful?”

“Potentially.”

Maximilian looked at the screen.

Felix had opened the original attachment references from the second death verification.

“The medical report number belongs to the confidential admission,” he explained. “But the document was not copied directly from the hospital's standard system.”

“How do you know?”

“The report number was valid, but the document's formatting was different. Someone reconstructed the report using information from the original record.”

Damian entered behind Maximilian.

“Could it have been forged?”

“Yes. But whoever created it had access to the original medical information.”

“Who had access?”

Felix opened the old hospital access list.

“Dr. Weller, the confidential records clerk, and the temporary account registered under M. Voss.”

Maximilian studied the names.

“Can you establish who created the reconstructed report?”

“No. The original file metadata was removed.”

Damian pointed at the report.

“What about the medical officer's signature?”

Felix enlarged it.

“The signature is genuine.”

Maximilian looked at him.

“The officer signed it?”

“Possibly. But the signature was copied from another document. It doesn't establish that the officer examined Elias.”

Damian folded his arms.

“So someone assembled a medical report from a real patient's information, attached a genuine signature, and used it to support an unverified death.”

Felix nodded.

“That is what the evidence suggests.”

Maximilian stared at the screen.

“Find the medical officer.”

**8:16 a.m. — Vesper Estate, Private Dining Room**

Aria arrived carrying a cup of tea.

She found Octavian, Isolde, Lucian, Helena, the brothers, Catherine, Margaret and Thomas already assembled.

Victoria was standing beside a large display.

Aria stopped at the doorway.

“Has anyone eaten?”

Cassian raised a hand.

“I have.”

“Good. Then I'm not interrupting breakfast.”

She sat beside Helena.

Maximilian began explaining the latest discovery.

“The medical report used to support Elias's death registration was reconstructed from the confidential hospital record. The original report number was genuine, but the document itself was not.”

Aria stirred her tea.

“So the person who made it had access to the original patient's medical information.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“And the person who authorised the hospital account?”

“Unknown.”

“Then we still don't know whether M. Voss was the patient, the person who accessed the file, or simply the name used to create the account.”

Catherine nodded.

“Correct.”

Victoria pointed at the display.

“There's another issue. The first death verification was rejected because the identification was insufficient. The second one was accepted using a medical report from a different patient.”

“Which means,” Aria said, “the person who reviewed the second submission either didn't notice the report belonged to someone else or deliberately ignored it.”

Octavian looked at Vane.

“Who reviewed the emergency submission?”

Vane shook his head.

“The committee's composition is not recorded in the surviving register.”

Felix brought up another document.

“Actually, I found a meeting reference.”

He displayed the old emergency review schedule.

**EX-04 — Administrative Review Session**

**Participants:**

- Temporary Executive Custodian
- Patriarchal Private Office
- Independent Administrative Auditor
- Medical Verification Representative

Victoria read the list.

“That's four positions.”

“Yes.”

“Do we know the people?”

“Only one is named in the surviving record.”

Felix highlighted the final line.

**Medical Verification Representative — Dr. Simon Weller.**

Catherine slowly turned towards him.

“Weller.”

Thomas nodded.

“He was the hospital administrator. He approved the M. Voss access account. He received the preservation payment. And he participated in the emergency review.”

Lucian's expression hardened.

“Then he knew the medical report was being used.”

“Possibly,” Maximilian said. “We need to establish what he was shown.”

Aria set down her tea.

“Ask him what he verified.”

Octavian looked at Thomas.

“Bring him here.”

Catherine shook her head.

“Not yet.”

Octavian's gaze shifted to her.

“You keep asking us to wait.”

“Because we have been asking Weller questions without showing him what we know. He has avoided answering. This time, we should show him the reconstructed report and ask one specific question.”

“What question?”

Catherine looked at the document.

“Who gave him the medical information?”

**9:40 a.m. — Central Women's Hospital**

Dr. Simon Weller was not pleased to see them again.

He looked at the group entering his office.

“I have already answered your questions.”

“Not all of them,” Thomas said.

Margaret placed the reconstructed medical report on his desk.

Weller looked at it.

His expression changed.

“Where did you get this?”

“From the second death verification submission,” Felix answered.

Weller did not touch the paper.

“This is not a hospital document.”

“We know,” Maximilian replied.

Weller looked at the report again.

“The patient details are genuine.”

“Then you recognise them?” Catherine asked.

“I recognise the format.”

“That wasn't my question.”

Weller took off his glasses.

“The information was taken from a confidential admission record.”

“Whose record?”

“I cannot tell you.”

Catherine's voice remained steady.

“Was it Mara Voss?”

Weller looked at her.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he replied.

“I never saw a patient admitted under that name.”

Margaret leaned forward.

“Then what did M.V. mean?”

“I don't know.”

“You processed the admission.”

“I processed the preservation request.”

“You authorised the access account.”

“I authorised a temporary account under an administrative instruction.”

“From whom?”

Weller looked down.

“An executive office.”

“Which executive office?”

He shook his head.

“I don't remember.”

Hale placed Martin's rejection report beside the reconstructed medical document.

“My father rejected the first death verification. You participated in the second review. Did you know the medical report belonged to another patient?”

Weller's expression tightened.

“I was not responsible for the identity verification.”

“But you were the medical verification representative.”

“I verified that the report number was valid.”

Hale stared at him.

“You verified the number, not the patient.”

Weller looked away.

“That was my understanding of the assignment.”

Thomas's voice became colder.

“Who assigned it?”

Weller did not answer.

Catherine stood.

“Dr. Weller, you have spent nineteen years allowing other people to believe that this record was a routine administrative matter. It wasn't. It was used to support a death registration that changed the authority structure of an entire family.”

Weller looked up sharply.

“You don't understand what happened.”

“Then explain it.”

“I cannot.”

“Cannot or will not?”

Weller closed his eyes.

“Both.”

**10:27 a.m. — Hospital Courtyard**

The group left the office without obtaining a name.

Hale walked beside Thomas.

“He knew.”

Thomas glanced at him.

“He knew the report wasn't a normal hospital document.”

“He knew it was being used for something.”

“Possibly.”

Hale stopped.

“Why are you still defending him?”

“I'm not defending him. I'm separating what we know from what we suspect.”

Hale looked towards the hospital windows.

“That's a very slow way to catch someone.”

“It's also how we avoid accusing the wrong person.”

Margaret joined them.

“Weller wasn't the only one who knew.”

Catherine turned.

“What do you mean?”

Margaret opened her handbag and removed a folded document.

“This was attached to the original preservation instruction. I found it in the Ellison House archive.”

Thomas unfolded it.

It was a receipt.

The hospital had acknowledged receiving the confidential admission record from an external courier.

The receipt carried a date, a delivery code and a signature.

The signature was not Weller's.

It was not Vane's.

It was not Hale's.

At the bottom was a name.

**MARGARET ELLISON.**

Margaret stared at it.

“I didn't sign this.”

**11:03 a.m. — Hospital Courtyard**

Thomas looked at her.

“Are you certain?”

“Yes.”

“Could it be an earlier signature?”

“No. The handwriting is similar, but the final letter is wrong.”

Hale examined the receipt.

“Someone copied your signature.”

Margaret nodded.

“Again.”

Catherine looked at the delivery code.

“Who was the courier?”

Margaret read it.

“Thomas Reed.”

Thomas took the document.

His face changed.

“That isn't my delivery code.”

Margaret looked at him.

“What?”

“My courier number was TR-08. This says TR-06.”

Felix checked the old courier index on his tablet.

“TR-06 belonged to another courier.”

“Who?”

Felix opened the personnel record.

The name appeared.

**JONATHAN VALE.**

The group went silent.

Thomas read the receipt again.

“Jonathan delivered the confidential record to the hospital.”

Margaret shook her head.

“No. Jonathan was not assigned to that transfer.”

Hale pointed to the signature.

“Then someone used his courier identity.”

Catherine looked at the date.

“Was this before or after he took Victoria from the Sterling estate?”

Felix checked.

“Before.”

Thomas turned towards Margaret.

“Did Jonathan ever tell you he had handled a hospital delivery?”

“No.”

“Did he ever mention a confidential record?”

“No.”

Catherine's expression darkened.

“Then we need to find out whether Jonathan knew what he was carrying.”

**12:14 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Victoria received the news in silence.

Jonathan Vale had raised her as Lily Shaw.

He had taken her from the Sterling estate when she was an infant and placed her somewhere safe.

He had never told her about the hospital record.

Aria watched her carefully.

“Do you think he knew?”

Victoria looked at the receipt.

“I don't know.”

“He may have been used as a courier.”

“He may have.”

Victoria's voice was controlled, but her fingers had tightened around the paper.

“He took me because he believed I wasn't safe. I know that much.”

Aria nodded.

“Then don't let this document decide what he did without knowing the facts.”

Victoria looked at her.

“You think I'm going to defend him?”

“I think you're allowed to be angry and still ask questions.”

Victoria looked down again.

For once, she had no immediate answer.

Across the room, Octavian was already giving instructions.

“Find Jonathan Vale.”

Felix nodded.

“I'll trace the courier assignment.”

“No,” Victoria said.

Everyone turned.

She stood.

“I'll speak to him.”

Octavian's expression tightened.

“This may involve a threat that has existed for decades.”

“Then I'll be careful.”

“Victoria—”

“I know what you're going to say. That this is dangerous, that you have people who can investigate, that I should wait.”

Octavian stopped.

Victoria placed the receipt on the table.

“He raised me. If his name is on this document, I want to hear from him directly.”

Aria looked at Octavian.

“She should.”

The old patriarch considered both girls.

Then he nodded.

“Very well. You may contact him. No one will force you to attend a meeting.”

Victoria looked at him.

“Thank you.”

**2:40 p.m. — Private Study**

Felix finally recovered the original courier assignment.

It had been filed under a different delivery reference.

The record stated:

**TR-06 — Jonathan Vale**

**Collection: Central Women's Hospital**

**Destination: Confidential Records Custody**

**Contents: Sealed medical envelope**

**Authorisation: Executive Office — EX-04**

The collection date matched the receipt.

The destination had been crossed out and replaced.

**Central Women's Hospital — Administrative Records.**

Felix read the alteration twice.

“Someone changed the destination after the assignment was issued.”

Maximilian stood beside him.

“Who authorised the change?”

Felix enlarged the approval field.

There was no name.

Only the code.

**P-01.**

Octavian's office.

Again.

Maximilian looked towards the door.

“Bring this to Grandfather.”

Felix printed the document.

But before he could leave, another notification appeared.

It was a message from an old courier archive.

The delivery had not been completed by Jonathan Vale.

A second person had signed for the envelope.

The name was barely legible.

Felix enlarged it.

Then he stopped breathing for a moment.

The recipient was listed as:

**E. VESPER.**

Not Elias.

Not Octavian.

Just one initial and a surname.

**3:12 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian stared at the recipient field.

“Who is E. Vesper?”

Felix checked the historical personnel records.

“There were three people with that name in the administrative directory at the time.”

“Names.”

“Elias Vesper. Evelyn Vesper. And Eleanor Vesper.”

Isolde looked up.

“Eleanor?”

“My mother,” Octavian said.

The room went quiet.

Eleanor Vesper had died decades earlier.

The original creator of Anna's trust had been Anna Vesper. Her daughter Evelyn had inherited its custodial responsibility.

But this record named Eleanor.

Octavian looked at the courier document.

“My mother never worked in the executive office.”

Catherine stepped closer.

“Then why was an envelope addressed to her?”

Nobody answered.

And for the first time, the investigation had reached a name that belonged to neither the living nor the dead.

It belonged to someone who had been gone long before the girls were born.

**Eleanor Vesper.**
