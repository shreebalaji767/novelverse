# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 39 — The Dead Man's Authority**

**Monday — 8:15 a.m.**

Octavian Vesper had not slept.

This was unusual.

At eighty-two, he considered sleep a responsibility rather than a pleasure. He maintained strict hours, rarely changed his routine, and regarded unnecessary late-night activity as evidence of poor planning.

At 8:15 a.m., he was sitting in his private study with three documents arranged before him.

All three bore his younger brother's signature.

Elias Vesper.

The first was a property transfer.

The second was a private trust authorisation.

The third was the document that had changed the allocation of two newborn children.

Lucian entered.

“You called?”

Octavian pushed the third document across the desk.

“Read it.”

Lucian read the signature first.

Then the date.

Then the instruction.

His expression hardened.

“Elias authorised this?”

“Apparently.”

“Why?”

“That is what I intend to discover.”

Lucian examined the date again.

“Father, Elias died before Aria and Victoria were born.”

“Twenty-two years ago.”

“Then someone used his authority after his death.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Or someone prepared the arrangement before his death and executed it later.”

“That would mean he knew something.”

“Yes.”

Lucian placed the document down.

“Something about the inheritance?”

Octavian didn't answer immediately.

Instead, he opened a drawer and removed an old photograph.

Two men stood outside the original Vesper estate.

One was Octavian.

The other was younger, smiling, and unmistakably Elias.

“Your uncle believed the family had a weakness,” Octavian said.

“What weakness?”

“The succession system.”

**9:30 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had assembled every available record connected to Elias.

The results were not encouraging.

Elias had been a member of the family council, a trustee of several private foundations, and an authorised representative of a dormant inheritance trust.

He had also resigned from all formal positions two years before his death.

“Why would he resign?” Damian asked.

“Officially, personal reasons,” Felix replied.

“Unofficially?”

“Nothing.”

Cassian examined the timeline.

“His trust authority remained active after his resignation?”

“Yes. That is the problem.”

Maximilian looked at the trust registration.

“Who maintained it?”

“Aurelius Consortium.”

“Then Aurelius knew the authority was still active.”

“Or they were instructed not to question it.”

Marcus placed another folder on the table.

“The original trust was created to protect a private inheritance provision. Its beneficiaries are not named in the public registration.”

“Can we identify them?” Lucian asked.

“Not from the registration. The beneficiary schedule is missing.”

Felix raised a finger.

“I found something else.”

Everyone turned.

He displayed a scanned document.

**E. VESPER — CONTINGENCY BENEFICIARY PROVISION**

Below it was a handwritten reference:

**Two descendants. Separate households. No common disclosure.**

Cassian frowned.

“Two descendants?”

Felix nodded.

“Written before the births.”

Maximilian looked at Octavian.

“Did Elias know about the children?”

Octavian's expression became unreadable.

“I don't know.”

**10:45 a.m.**

Aria was having breakfast when Marcus called.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning. I have an update.”

“Is it bad?”

“Complicated.”

“That's usually bad with you.”

Marcus paused.

“We have discovered that your great-uncle may have authorised the original separation arrangement.”

Aria stopped cutting her toast.

“My great-uncle?”

“Elias Vesper. He died before you and Victoria were born.”

“So a dead relative arranged our lives?”

“His signature appears on the relevant documents.”

“That's inconvenient.”

Marcus waited.

Aria took another bite.

“Did he have a reason?”

“We don't know.”

“Then find out.”

“We are doing that.”

“And Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“Please don't let everyone decide I'm supposed to be upset about a man I've never met.”

“I'll make that clear.”

“Thank you.”

She ended the call.

Then she looked at her toast.

It had gone cold.

She considered the situation.

The toast was innocent.

She ate it anyway.

**12:20 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria had requested the original documents from the Vespers.

She wanted to see the signature herself.

Richard objected.

“You don't need to involve yourself in every part of this investigation.”

Victoria looked at him.

“Someone arranged for me to be taken from my family. Someone changed the destination of two newborn children. Someone kept me under another identity for sixteen years.”

She placed both hands on the table.

“I am involved.”

Eleanor quietly set down her teacup.

Richard said nothing.

Victoria continued.

“And I want to know whether the person responsible was a Vesper.”

Richard looked at the document.

“Your grandfather's family has its own history. You cannot assume—”

“I am not assuming anything. I am asking questions.”

She took the document and left.

Richard watched her go.

Eleanor turned toward him.

“She's right.”

“I know.”

“Then stop treating her questions as disobedience.”

Richard looked at the empty doorway.

He had spent years believing that controlling information protected his family.

Now he was beginning to understand how much damage that belief had caused.

**2:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The meeting with Victoria was brief.

Octavian showed her the documents and explained what they had discovered.

Victoria read the handwritten reference twice.

“Two descendants. Separate households.”

“Yes,” Octavian replied.

“Does that mean Aria and I were deliberately separated?”

“It suggests the arrangement was planned before your births.”

“Why?”

“We don't know.”

Victoria looked at the signature.

“Did you know Elias?”

“Very well.”

“Was he cruel?”

Octavian considered the question.

“No.”

“Manipulative?”

“Occasionally.”

Cassian coughed.

Octavian ignored him.

“Your great-uncle was intelligent. He believed that protecting the family sometimes required decisions other people would not understand.”

Victoria returned the document.

“That sounds familiar.”

Octavian looked at her.

“I am not defending him.”

“Good.”

She stood.

“I don't want anyone deciding that because he was a Vesper, whatever he did must have been justified.”

Octavian nodded.

“That is fair.”

Victoria hesitated.

“Does Aria know?”

“Yes.”

“Then don't keep anything from her.”

“I have no intention of doing so.”

Victoria left.

Cassian watched her go.

“She has become considerably less impressed by powerful families.”

“An understandable development,” Maximilian said.

**4:30 p.m. — Vesper Archives**

Alistair Monroe had spent most of the afternoon reviewing Elias's personal correspondence.

He found a sealed envelope inside a box of private council records.

It was addressed to Octavian.

The date was twenty-three years old.

The envelope had never been opened.

Alistair brought it directly to the patriarch.

Octavian recognised the handwriting.

He opened it carefully.

Inside was a single letter.

*Octavian,*

*If you are reading this, I have either failed to convince you or have been prevented from explaining myself.*

*The succession provision is not what you believe it to be.*

*The two children must not be placed together until the original claim has been verified.*

*I have arranged temporary safeguards. Do not allow the trustees to substitute their own interpretation.*

*If the allocation is reversed, the consequences will extend beyond our family.*

*Do not trust the person who offers to resolve the matter quickly.*

*Elias.*

Octavian read it again.

Then a third time.

Lucian stood beside him.

“What does it mean?”

Octavian folded the letter.

“I don't know.”

“Did Elias ever tell you about a succession claim involving two children?”

“No.”

“Then why would he write this?”

Octavian looked toward the archive window.

“Because he knew something I didn't.”

**6:15 p.m. — Aurelius Consortium**

The senior partner had received the same letter's existence through a confidential report.

He immediately requested the original trust file.

The executive responsible for the archive refused.

“The file is protected by the originating authority.”

“The originating authority is dead.”

“The authority remains valid.”

“Then show me the beneficiary schedule.”

The executive hesitated.

“I cannot.”

The partner stood.

“You are aware that the Vespers have identified Elias?”

“Yes.”

“And you still intend to conceal the schedule?”

The executive looked at him.

“You don't understand what is written there.”

“Then explain it.”

The executive closed the file.

“I cannot.”

The partner stared at him for several seconds.

Then he said quietly:

“Someone is going to make this decision for you.”

**9:50 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria found Octavian in the library.

He was reading Elias's letter.

“Can't sleep?” she asked.

“Not tonight.”

She sat opposite him.

“Marcus said you found something.”

Octavian handed her the letter.

Aria read it slowly.

When she finished, she placed it back on the desk.

“Did he explain what the original claim was?”

“No.”

“Did he explain why we couldn't be together?”

“No.”

“Did he explain what happens if the allocation is reversed?”

“No.”

Aria considered the letter.

“He was very good at writing things without explaining anything.”

Octavian gave a quiet laugh.

“Yes. He was.”

“Do you think he was trying to protect us?”

“I think he believed he was.”

“That isn't the same thing.”

“No.”

Aria stood.

“Then find out what he was protecting us from.”

Octavian looked at her.

“I will.”

She reached the doorway, then turned.

“And Grandfather?”

“Yes?”

“Don't let his decisions become mine.”

Octavian's expression softened.

“They won't.”

Aria nodded and left.

**11:40 p.m. — Private Residence**

The Aurelius executive unlocked a small safe.

Inside was the original beneficiary schedule.

He removed it and placed it beside the amended allocation.

The original document contained two names.

**Beneficiary One: Aria Vesper.**

**Beneficiary Two: Victoria Sterling.**

A third line appeared beneath them.

**Contingent authority: Elias Vesper.**

The executive opened the amended document.

The reversal had not been signed by Elias.

It had been authorised under his authority by someone else.

A separate notation identified the person who had submitted the amendment.

**L. VESPER.**

The executive stared at the initials.

Then he picked up his telephone.

“It's time,” he said.

The person on the other end answered.

“Time for what?”

“To tell the Vespers who changed the allocation.”

He looked at the initials again.

“Because it wasn't Elias.”

And somewhere inside the Vesper family, the person who had authorised the reversal was still alive.
