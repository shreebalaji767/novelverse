# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 44 — The Man Who Never Died**

**Saturday — 8:00 a.m.**

Edmund Voss had requested a meeting.

He had also specified three conditions.

First, no armed personnel inside the meeting room.

Second, both descendants had to be present.

Third, Octavian Vesper was not permitted to question him until he had finished his statement.

The third condition caused a problem.

Octavian read it twice.

“Who does he think he is?”

Marcus stood opposite him.

“A man who has apparently been dead for twenty-one years.”

“His administrative status does not improve his manners.”

“I'll communicate your objection.”

“Don't.”

Marcus nodded.

It was the closest thing to agreement he expected to receive.

**9:30 a.m. — Private Conference Centre**

Edmund Voss arrived precisely on time.

He was seventy-one, neatly dressed, and walking with a slight limp.

His appearance was ordinary.

His expression was not.

He looked at Octavian first.

“It's been a long time.”

Octavian did not offer his hand.

“You were reported dead.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because someone wanted me to be.”

“Who?”

Voss looked at Aria and Victoria.

“I'll explain.”

He sat down.

The meeting room contained twelve people.

Voss counted them.

Then he looked at Marcus.

“I asked for no armed personnel.”

“There are none inside this room.”

“Good.”

He placed a small leather folder on the table.

“I was responsible for protecting two unborn children. I failed.”

Victoria crossed her arms.

“Then start explaining.”

**9:45 a.m.**

Voss opened the folder.

The first document was a security report dated three months before the births.

It recorded an attempt to access confidential maternity information.

The second documented an unauthorised request for the children's identities.

The third contained a handwritten instruction.

**Both descendants must remain under separate protective arrangements until the source of the threat is identified.**

Lucian read it.

“Who authorised this?”

“I did.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“You had no authority to change their placements.”

“I didn't change their placements.”

“Your name appears on the security instruction.”

“Because I was responsible for protecting them. I did not authorise the allocation reversal.”

Voss turned toward Felix.

“Have you recovered the original submission?”

Felix nodded.

“Yes.”

“Then you should have noticed the difference.”

Felix frowned.

“What difference?”

“The allocation reversal was submitted under the legacy authority. My security instruction was submitted under the protective office. They were separate systems.”

Marcus leaned forward.

“Are you saying the reversal was not your decision?”

“Correct.”

“Then why were you receiving payments through the same trust?”

Voss looked at him.

“Because I continued protecting the children after my official resignation.”

Octavian's voice was cold.

“From whom?”

Voss opened another document.

“From the person who arranged the reversal.”

**10:20 a.m.**

Voss explained that Elias had discovered someone was attempting to obtain the identities of the expected descendants.

He had suspected the information was being sought to manipulate the inheritance.

Voss was instructed to identify the source.

His investigation uncovered a network of administrative accounts, private intermediaries, and false authorisations.

One name appeared repeatedly.

**Mara Voss.**

Victoria immediately leaned forward.

“Was she working for you?”

“No.”

“Did you know her?”

“I knew the name. I never met her.”

“Then who was she?”

Voss shook his head.

“I don't know her real identity.”

Felix displayed the payment records.

“Mara Voss received money through Asterion Fiduciary Services.”

“I know.”

“Did you authorise those payments?”

“No.”

“Did you investigate them?”

“Yes.”

“What did you find?”

Voss looked at Octavian.

“Someone had access to the legacy authority, the Sterling administrative accounts, and the hospital's temporary records system.”

Lucian's expression changed.

“Those systems were not connected.”

“They weren't supposed to be.”

“Then how?”

“Through the private trust.”

Marcus exchanged a glance with Felix.

The trust had been the common link all along.

**11:05 a.m.**

Aria had been listening quietly.

Now she asked, “If you were protecting us, why did you disappear?”

Voss looked at her.

“Because I discovered that the person arranging the transfers knew where I was investigating.”

“So you left?”

“I was attacked.”

Damian immediately straightened.

“When?”

“Three days after I confronted the person responsible.”

“Where?”

“Outside my residence.”

“Were you injured?”

“Yes.”

Voss rolled up his sleeve.

A long scar ran along his forearm.

“I escaped. My vehicle was later found in a ravine. The body recovered from the wreckage was not mine.”

Octavian's expression darkened.

“You allowed the family to believe you were dead.”

“I had no choice.”

“You could have contacted Elias.”

“I tried.”

Voss looked down.

“He was dead before I could reach him.”

The room fell silent.

**12:00 p.m.**

Voss removed another document.

It was a copy of a message sent to him shortly before the accident.

*The first placement has been completed. The second will be handled separately. Do not interfere.*

The message had no signature.

Below it was a reference.

**CA-17.**

Richard Sterling's account code.

Victoria looked at her father.

Richard's face had gone pale.

“I didn't send that.”

Voss nodded.

“I know.”

“Then why was my account used?”

“Because someone wanted the message to appear connected to your family.”

Richard looked at the document.

“Did you investigate?”

“Yes. The payment originated from an intermediary account. Your authorisation had been copied.”

Richard looked at Octavian.

“I told you I didn't know what CA-17 was being used for.”

Octavian did not respond.

Aria examined the message.

“Did the person who sent it know where Victoria was?”

Voss nodded.

“They knew the temporary custody arrangement.”

“Then they knew about the Sterling estate.”

“Yes.”

“Did they know Jonathan took her?”

Voss hesitated.

“Not immediately.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“When did they find out?”

“Several weeks later.”

“Who?”

“I don't know.”

Victoria's expression tightened.

“You have spent twenty years investigating this. How can you not know?”

“Because every time I reached the person responsible, the trail ended with someone else's identity.”

**1:15 p.m.**

The meeting paused for lunch.

Voss declined the meal.

Aria noticed.

“Are you sure?”

“Yes.”

“There's plenty.”

“Thank you.”

Cassian looked at the untouched plates.

“An entire catering team has been defeated by one man.”

Damian gave him a warning look.

Cassian shrugged.

“I'm simply observing.”

Victoria took a glass of water.

Then she turned toward Voss.

“You said you were responsible for protecting us. Did you ever find out why Jonathan took me?”

Voss nodded.

“He believed the Sterling estate was no longer safe.”

“Was it?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Voss looked at Richard.

“Because someone had issued a new custody instruction.”

Richard frowned.

“I never saw it.”

“You weren't supposed to.”

Voss placed a document before Victoria.

It was a transfer order dated six weeks after her arrival at the estate.

The receiving organisation was blank.

The authorising signature belonged to Daniel Mercer.

And the instruction contained one sentence:

**The child must be removed before the receiving family is notified.**

Victoria stared at it.

“Who was the receiving family?”

Voss answered quietly.

“The Vespers.”

**2:30 p.m.**

The document changed the entire timeline.

The original plan had been for Victoria to remain temporarily at the Sterling estate.

After six weeks, she was to be transferred to the Vespers.

But someone issued a removal order before the transfer could happen.

Jonathan Vale had taken her instead.

Marcus placed the documents in chronological order.

“Then Jonathan's intervention prevented the second placement.”

“Correct,” Voss replied.

Maximilian studied the order.

“Who issued it?”

“I don't know.”

“Was it Mara Voss?”

“Possibly.”

“Was it Elias?”

“No.”

“Why are you certain?”

“Because Elias was already dead.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Then who had authority to issue the order?”

Voss looked toward the original trust document.

“Someone who had access to the legacy authority after Elias died.”

Felix opened the access register.

The final recorded use of the authority was dated six weeks after the children's births.

The user reference was:

**L. ELLISON.**

Alistair looked up.

“Margaret Ellison.”

Voss nodded.

“She was the intermediary.”

“Who instructed her?” Octavian asked.

Voss looked at him.

“That is the question I have never answered.”

**4:10 p.m.**

Voss finally revealed why he had requested Aria and Victoria's presence.

He placed a small envelope before them.

“This belonged to Elias.”

Aria examined the seal.

“What's inside?”

“An instruction addressed to both descendants.”

Victoria did not touch it.

“Why didn't you give it to Octavian?”

“Because Elias specifically instructed me to deliver it only when both children had been identified and were old enough to understand the circumstances.”

Aria looked at Octavian.

“Did you know about this?”

“No.”

Voss pushed the envelope toward them.

Victoria opened it.

Inside was a handwritten letter.

*To the two descendants whose lives have been affected by decisions made before they could speak for themselves.*

*I do not ask you to accept the inheritance.*

*I ask you to understand why the original arrangement was created.*

*The trust was not established to reward a family name. It was established to protect something that must not be controlled by one person.*

*If the allocation has been reversed, do not assume the person who changed it intended to steal your inheritance.*

*The person who changed it may have been trying to prevent something worse.*

*Find the original custodian.*

*Only then will you understand why the children had to be separated.*

The letter ended with Elias's signature.

Victoria looked at Voss.

“Who was the original custodian?”

Voss answered immediately.

“Your great-grandmother.”

Octavian went completely still.

“My mother?”

Voss nodded.

“She created the original trust.”

Octavian stared at the letter.

“My mother died before the succession authority was established.”

“Yes.”

“Then how could she have appointed a custodian?”

Voss opened his folder again.

“Because the trust existed long before the two children were born.”

**6:00 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Alistair retrieved the original trust creation record.

The founding document was more than forty years old.

It had been established by Octavian's mother, **Evelyn Vesper**.

Its purpose was not to transfer wealth.

It was to protect an independent body of assets and voting rights from being controlled by any single branch of the family.

The original custodian was Evelyn herself.

After her death, the custodianship passed to a designated successor.

The name had been removed from the public record.

Felix recovered the sealed appointment.

He opened it.

The designated successor was:

**ELIAS VESPER.**

Octavian stared at the document.

“So Elias inherited the custodianship.”

“Yes,” Alistair replied.

“And he created the legacy authority to protect it.”

“Correct.”

Lucian looked at the original trust.

“Then the children were never meant to inherit merely because they were descendants.”

Felix nodded.

“They were meant to verify the custodianship and protect the trust from being controlled by another person.”

Cassian looked at the documents.

“Which means the inheritance dispute was never the real issue.”

Marcus glanced at Voss.

“What was?”

Voss answered.

“Control of the original trust.”

**9:20 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian met Voss privately.

“Who arranged the allocation reversal?”

“I still don't know.”

“Who ordered Margaret Ellison to submit it?”

“I don't know.”

“Who was paying Mara Voss?”

“I don't know.”

Octavian stared at him.

“You have given us very little.”

Voss accepted the criticism.

“I have one more thing.”

He placed a photograph on the desk.

It showed Elias standing beside a woman outside an old private residence.

Octavian recognised the woman.

His expression changed.

“Who is she?”

Voss looked at him.

“Your sister.”

Octavian slowly picked up the photograph.

He had not seen her in more than thirty years.

**Catherine Vesper.**

His younger sister.

The woman who had left the family after a dispute over the original trust.

The woman officially recorded as deceased nineteen years earlier.

Octavian looked at Voss.

“She's alive?”

Voss nodded.

“She was the person Elias was trying to protect the trust from.”

Octavian stared at the photograph.

Then Voss delivered the final revelation.

“She was also the person who gave me my original assignment.”

**11:55 p.m.**

In a quiet residence several hours away, a woman placed an old photograph into a drawer.

It showed two newborn babies.

One had a hospital band marked Infant A.

The other was marked Infant B.

Beside the photograph was a letter addressed to Catherine Vesper.

She read the final line again.

*The children have been identified. The original custodian's final instruction must now be delivered.*

Catherine closed the letter.

Then she picked up her telephone.

“Contact Margaret Ellison.”

The voice on the other end replied:

“She has already been found.”

Catherine went silent.

After a moment, she said:

“Then there's no reason to wait.”

She looked at the photograph.

“Bring me the girls.”
