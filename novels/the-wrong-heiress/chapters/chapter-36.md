# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 36 — The Signature That Should Never Have Existed**

**Friday — 8:05 a.m.**

Eleanor Shaw had been missing for eleven years.

Not officially.

She had resigned from the Sterling estate, settled her employment matters, and moved away. Her departure had been recorded as an ordinary retirement.

But the Vespers had discovered something unusual.

Three months after her resignation, someone had used her credentials to authorise a confidential payment.

Six months later, the same credentials had been used to request access to the original birth registration.

And six years later, a representative had collected the registration under Victoria Sterling's beneficiary reference.

The woman who had supposedly retired had continued to influence the records long after leaving the estate.

Felix placed the timeline on the conference room screen.

“Eleanor Shaw's credentials were active for almost seven months after her recorded resignation.”

Richard frowned.

“I approved her resignation personally.”

“Did you disable her access?”

“I assumed the estate administrator had done it.”

“Who was the estate administrator?”

Richard looked at the document.

“Mara Voss.”

The room fell silent.

Cassian leaned back.

“An administrator who retained access to the administrator's former employee's credentials. Very reassuring.”

Richard looked furious.

“I didn't know.”

Aria studied the timeline.

“Could Eleanor Shaw have been working with Mara?”

“Possibly,” Felix replied. “But the records don't establish that.”

Octavian turned toward Marcus.

“Find her.”

Marcus nodded.

“We have already located a possible address through her pension records. Our legal representative is confirming it.”

Damian added, “We'll also establish whether she's still using the same identity.”

Aria looked at him.

“Please don't send an army.”

Damian paused.

“Two people.”

“One.”

“Two is safer.”

“One.”

He looked toward Octavian.

Octavian considered it.

“One representative and one security officer nearby. They will not approach unless necessary.”

Damian accepted the compromise.

**9:30 a.m.**

Victoria had returned to Sterling Mansion.

She was sitting in the drawing room when Eleanor entered.

For a moment, they simply looked at each other.

Then Eleanor sat opposite her.

“Richard told me about the photograph.”

Victoria nodded.

“He also told me about the documents.”

Eleanor looked at her hands.

“I don't know how to explain what happened.”

“Then don't explain what you don't know.”

Eleanor looked up.

Victoria's voice was quieter than usual.

“I was Lily for sixteen years. Margaret raised me. Jonathan brought me to her. Then the Sterlings found me, and suddenly I was Victoria.”

“You were always our daughter.”

“Were you looking for me?”

Eleanor hesitated.

Richard had told her that the family had received information about their biological daughter.

But she had not known where that information came from, or how many years had passed before the search began.

“We were told you had been raised elsewhere. We searched when the records became available.”

“Who told you?”

“Richard handled most of it.”

Victoria gave a small, humourless smile.

“Of course he did.”

Eleanor reached for her hand.

“I wish I had known about the estate.”

Victoria withdrew her hand, not angrily, but because she wasn't ready.

“I wish a lot of people had known a lot of things.”

Eleanor nodded.

“I understand.”

“No, you don't.”

Victoria stood.

“But you can start by telling me everything you actually remember. Not what Richard thinks happened. Not what the documents suggest. What you remember.”

Eleanor looked at her daughter.

Then she nodded.

“All right.”

**11:15 a.m.**

The Vesper legal representative arrived at a small residential building.

The address belonged to Eleanor Shaw.

A woman in her late sixties opened the door.

She looked at the visitor, then at the identification card.

“I was wondering when someone would come.”

The representative remained professional.

“Mrs. Shaw?”

“Yes.”

“We're investigating the historical transfer of two infants from Central Women's Hospital. Your name appears in several related records.”

Eleanor Shaw's expression changed.

“I see.”

“Would you be willing to speak with us?”

She looked down the corridor.

Then she stepped aside.

“Come in.”

**11:50 a.m.**

Eleanor Shaw had kept a box of documents.

Not because she wanted to preserve the arrangement.

Because she had never trusted the people who had instructed her to destroy the records.

She placed the box on the table.

Inside were payment slips, employment letters, a few handwritten notes, and a sealed envelope.

The representative looked at the envelope.

“What is that?”

“The instruction I was given when I left the Sterling estate.”

“Who gave it to you?”

“Mara Voss.”

“What did it instruct you to do?”

“To retain one document and destroy the duplicate records.”

“Which document?”

Eleanor opened the envelope.

A handwritten instruction was inside.

*Retain original birth registration under restricted custody. Do not release without direct authorisation from the originating party.*

The signature at the bottom was not Mara Voss's.

It was a different name.

**A. MERCER.**

**12:30 p.m.**

Felix received the scanned document.

He immediately searched the historical records.

“There's an A. Mercer in the Northbridge employment archive.”

Maximilian looked up.

“Daniel Mercer?”

“No. Different person.”

Felix brought up the employee record.

**Arthur Mercer.**

Former director of Northbridge Data Services.

He had authorised the temporary hospital access credentials used by Daniel Mercer.

He had also signed the original administrative agreement for the two-family arrangement.

Damian leaned forward.

“Where is he?”

Felix checked the record.

“Died nine years ago.”

Cassian sighed.

“Another dead administrator.”

“His estate records are available,” Felix replied.

“Wonderful. We can interrogate his filing cabinet.”

Felix ignored him.

“There's a surviving business partner.”

“Name?”

“Martin Hale. Former finance director.”

Octavian looked at Marcus.

“Arrange a meeting.”

**2:10 p.m.**

Eleanor Shaw continued her statement.

She had worked at the Sterling estate for fourteen years.

Her duties included private property administration, document custody, and confidential correspondence.

She had not been involved in the hospital transfers.

She had been contacted by Mara Voss three weeks before the births.

Mara had presented an authorisation signed by Arthur Mercer.

“She told me there were two children involved,” Eleanor explained. “She said the records had to be kept separate because the receiving arrangements were confidential.”

“Did she tell you the children's identities?”

“No.”

“Did she tell you the families?”

“She mentioned the Sterling estate and another receiving family, but not which child belonged where.”

“Why did you sign the payment instruction?”

“I didn't.”

The representative looked at the document.

“But the signature says E. Shaw.”

Eleanor shook her head.

“That is not my signature.”

She pointed to the handwriting.

“The signature is an imitation. My name was entered because I was the property custodian. I never approved that payment.”

“Did you report it?”

“I tried.”

“What happened?”

“Mara told me the matter had been approved by the originating party. She said I would lose my position if I interfered with a confidential family arrangement.”

Eleanor looked down.

“I was frightened. I kept the records I could.”

**4:00 p.m.**

At Vesper Tower, the Vespers reviewed Eleanor Shaw's statement.

Richard listened without speaking.

When the representative finished, he looked at the scanned signature.

“She was telling the truth.”

Octavian looked at him.

“You recognise the handwriting?”

“No. But I recognise Eleanor Shaw. She would never have authorised a payment involving children without asking questions.”

Aria looked at him.

“Yet you authorised CA-17 without asking questions.”

Richard lowered his eyes.

“Yes.”

There was no defence.

Marcus placed the new document beside the original Sterling account register.

“The payment was approved under Arthur Mercer's authority. The Sterling estate was used as an administrative route, but Eleanor Shaw's signature was forged.”

Lucian looked at the signature.

“So the Sterling family did not authorise the original arrangement.”

“Not through this document,” Felix replied. “Richard's separate payment authorisation remains genuine. We still need to establish what he was told when he signed it.”

Richard nodded.

“I'll provide every remaining record.”

Octavian turned toward him.

“You will also explain why you allowed an intermediary to control your estate's confidential records.”

Richard accepted the criticism.

“I will.”

**6:25 p.m.**

Aria found Victoria in the garden.

Victoria was sitting on a bench, looking at the photograph Margaret had given her.

Aria sat at the other end.

“Did you speak to Eleanor?”

Victoria nodded.

“She told me she wants me home.”

“Do you?”

Victoria looked at her.

“I don't know.”

Aria nodded.

“That seems fair.”

Victoria looked back at the photograph.

“Margaret said I don't have to choose between the names.”

“She sounds sensible.”

“She is.”

A brief silence followed.

Victoria looked at Aria.

“Do you hate me?”

Aria considered the question.

“No.”

“Even after everything?”

“Everything what?”

Victoria looked away.

“The way I behaved. The things I said.”

Aria shrugged.

“I didn't like them.”

Victoria waited.

“But I don't hate you.”

Victoria looked down at the photograph.

“I thought becoming a Sterling meant I had finally found somewhere I belonged.”

Aria said nothing.

“Then I found out you were a Vesper. I thought everyone was choosing you over me.”

“I wasn't asking anyone to choose.”

“I know that now.”

Aria stood.

“Good. Because I'm not interested in competing with you.”

Victoria gave a small laugh.

“Of course you're not.”

Aria started toward the house.

Then she stopped.

“Margaret is visiting tomorrow. You should spend time with her.”

Victoria nodded.

“I will.”

**Unknown Location — 10:15 p.m.**

Mara Voss received the news about Eleanor Shaw.

She read the statement summary.

Then she opened a file containing the original agreement.

Arthur Mercer had signed the administrative contract.

Mara had handled the transfers.

Daniel Mercer had executed the hospital handover.

Richard Sterling had provided temporary custody.

Jonathan Vale had taken the child away.

Every person had been given a limited instruction.

Every person had believed someone else had authority.

And none of them had been told the entire purpose.

Mara closed the file.

Her phone rang.

She answered.

“It's done,” the caller said.

“What is?”

“The Vespers have found Eleanor Shaw.”

Mara said nothing.

“They have Arthur Mercer's name.”

“I know.”

“Will they find the originating party?”

Mara looked at the sealed document on her desk.

“They already have the name.”

“Then why are you still waiting?”

“Because the name is not the evidence.”

She opened the document.

At the bottom was a signature.

**AURELIUS CONSORTIUM — PRIVATE TRUST AUTHORITY.**

Mara stared at it.

Then she carefully placed the document back in its envelope.

For the first time in twenty years, she looked genuinely afraid.

The Vespers were no longer investigating a family dispute.

They were approaching the organisation that had paid for the arrangement in the first place.
