# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 55 — The Archive That Was Never Empty**

**Friday — 12:03 a.m.**

The man holding Elias Vesper's note did not turn immediately.

He knew the voice.

He had heard it once before, twenty-two years earlier, in a room where nobody had been permitted to take notes.

“Show yourself,” he said.

A light came on.

Alistair Vane stood near the entrance.

He looked older than the photograph, but unmistakably alive.

The man slowly folded the note.

“You're supposed to be dead.”

Vane glanced at the cabinet.

“So I've been told.”

“Your death was certified.”

“I know.”

“By Elias.”

“I know that too.”

The man stared at him.

“Then why?”

Vane walked towards the cabinet.

“Because Elias understood that a dead executive custodian could be more useful than a living one.”

“Useful to whom?”

Vane stopped.

“That depends on who is asking.”

The man took a step back.

“You've been using the authority.”

“Not all of it.”

“You accessed the vault three weeks ago.”

“Yes.”

“You retrieved the appointment register.”

“No.”

The answer was immediate.

Vane looked at the empty shelves.

“Someone else did.”

“Who?”

“I came here to find out.”

The man almost laughed.

“You expect me to believe that?”

“No. I expect you to verify it.”

Vane pointed at the envelope.

“Read the note again.”

The man unfolded it.

*If Vane is alive, do not let him reach the girls.*

He looked up.

“Elias knew.”

“He suspected.”

“And he wanted you kept away from Aria and Victoria.”

“He wanted them kept away from everyone who could use the trust against them.”

“Are you a threat?”

Vane's expression became unreadable.

“That is the question you should have asked twenty-two years ago.”

**12:11 a.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Felix was still awake.

He had received the photograph of the archive entrance from Nathaniel's security team.

The building's external camera had recorded two people entering.

The first had used the VA-09 key.

The second had entered through the main door.

Felix enlarged the images.

The first person's face was obscured by the camera angle.

The second image was clearer.

He stared at it.

Then he called Maximilian.

“Wake up.”

Maximilian answered after several rings.

“Felix, if this is about a printer—”

“Alistair Vane is alive.”

Silence.

“Where?”

“The old family archive.”

“Who is with him?”

“I don't know.”

“Send me the footage.”

“Already sent.”

Maximilian opened the file.

His expression changed.

“Is that really him?”

“Yes.”

“Call Nathaniel.”

“I already did.”

“Then call Father.”

“Already done.”

Maximilian sat up.

“Who haven't you called?”

Felix thought about it.

“Aria.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because it is midnight.”

“It's an important discovery.”

“It's also midnight.”

Felix looked at the screen.

“She's going to be annoyed when she finds out.”

“Then let her be annoyed in the morning.”

**12:19 a.m.**

Octavian entered the conference room.

Lucian and Blackwood were already there.

Nathaniel displayed the archive footage.

Octavian watched Vane enter.

He watched the second man follow.

Then he watched the two disappear beyond the camera's range.

“Identify the second man.”

“We're working on it,” Nathaniel said.

“Is Vane alone?”

“No.”

“Then he has assistance.”

Blackwood examined the footage.

“Or someone is trying to stop him.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Explain.”

“Elias left a warning specifically about Vane. Someone entered the archive before Vane arrived. That person may have been retrieving something, hiding something, or checking whether the archive was still secure.”

Lucian folded his arms.

“And Vane followed.”

“Or they arranged to meet.”

Catherine appeared on the secure screen.

“I need to see the footage.”

Nathaniel replayed it.

Catherine watched Vane enter.

Her face went pale.

“He's alive.”

Octavian looked at her.

“You knew him.”

“Yes.”

“Did you know he was alive?”

“No.”

“Did you ever suspect?”

Catherine hesitated.

“I suspected his death had been used to conceal his authority. I did not know whether he was alive.”

Blackwood turned towards her.

“What was his relationship with Elias?”

“Complicated.”

“That isn't useful.”

“Vane believed the trust's administrative structure was too vulnerable to family politics. Elias believed Vane wanted more control than the office permitted.”

“And you?”

“I believed both of them were hiding something.”

Octavian's voice hardened.

“Why wasn't I told?”

Catherine looked directly at him.

“Because Elias believed you were being watched.”

Octavian fell silent.

**12:31 a.m.**

The old archive had no working telephone.

The two men stood beneath a weak emergency light.

Vane had removed his coat and placed it on a chair.

The man who had entered first was still holding the note.

“Where is the appointment register?” he asked.

Vane shook his head.

“I don't know.”

“You expect me to believe that too?”

“You seem to have difficulty believing anything.”

“Twenty-two years ago, Elias told Margaret that the register was here.”

“He was wrong.”

“Then where is it?”

Vane looked towards the empty cabinet.

“Elias moved it.”

“To where?”

“I don't know.”

The man studied him.

“You were the executive custodian. You had access to everything.”

“Not everything.”

“You had authority over the archive.”

“Administrative authority. Not ownership.”

“You were responsible for maintaining the records.”

“And I did.”

“Then why is the register missing?”

Vane's expression changed.

“Because someone removed it before Elias died.”

The man stared at him.

“Who?”

Vane reached into his pocket and removed a small folded paper.

He placed it on the cabinet.

It was a copy of an old archive movement record.

One entry had been circled.

**REGISTER VA-09 — REMOVED UNDER EMERGENCY AUTHORITY.**

The date was twenty-two years earlier.

The receiving officer's signature was illegible.

But the authority number was clear.

**LA-01.**

The man looked up.

“Legacy Authority.”

Vane nodded.

“Someone used the same authority that was used to reverse the allocation.”

“Who had access?”

“That's what Elias spent his final months trying to discover.”

“And did he?”

Vane looked towards the darkness at the far end of the archive.

“He found the person who had approved the removal.”

“Who?”

Vane did not answer.

Instead, he asked:

“Do you know why Elias never told Octavian?”

The man frowned.

“No.”

“Because the approval was made in Octavian's name.”

**12:39 a.m.**

At Vesper Estate, Felix was searching the legacy authority records.

He found an old approval entry.

The document had been indexed under the wrong category.

He opened it.

The record was dated twenty-two years earlier.

It authorised the removal of the original executive appointment register.

The approving authority was listed as:

**O. VESPER — PATRIARCHAL OFFICE**

Octavian stood behind him.

“What is that?”

Felix turned the screen.

Octavian read the entry.

Then he read it again.

“I did not approve this.”

Lucian leaned forward.

“Are you certain?”

“I would remember authorising the removal of the executive appointment register.”

“Could it have been submitted under your authority without your knowledge?”

“Yes.”

Blackwood looked at the record.

“Then someone used your credentials.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Who had access?”

Felix opened the old access list.

There were five authorised administrators.

One was Elias.

One was Margaret.

One was the council secretary.

One was the executive custodian.

The fifth entry was different.

It was not a person's name.

It was a department.

**PATRIARCHAL PRIVATE OFFICE.**

Octavian stared at it.

“That office had no authority over the trust.”

“Apparently, someone believed otherwise,” Nathaniel said.

Octavian turned towards him.

“Find the person who submitted it.”

**12:47 a.m.**

Back inside the archive, Vane placed his hands on the cabinet.

“You need to understand something.”

The other man looked at him.

“What?”

“The allocation reversal was not the first use of LA-01.”

“I know.”

“No. You know it was used to remove the register. You don't know what happened before that.”

Vane opened a drawer.

Inside was a narrow strip of paper.

He unfolded it.

There were three entries.

The first concerned the original trust.

The second concerned the appointment of the executive custodian.

The third concerned a request to identify the expected descendants.

The third entry had been rejected.

A handwritten note followed:

*Request denied. No disclosure permitted.*

The authorisation signature was visible.

**O. VESPER.**

The man looked at Vane.

“Octavian rejected the inquiry.”

“Yes.”

“Then someone later used his authority to remove the register.”

“Yes.”

“Was it the same person?”

“I don't know.”

The man shook his head.

“Elias must have known.”

“Elias knew that Octavian had rejected the request. He also knew someone had later used the patriarchal authority to remove the register.”

“Why didn't he tell Octavian?”

“Because he believed the person responsible could access the patriarch's office.”

The man looked at the note.

“So he suspected someone close to Octavian.”

Vane nodded.

“And he was afraid that telling him would expose the children.”

**1:02 a.m.**

Aria's phone vibrated.

She opened one eye.

A message from Marcus.

*Miss Aria, I apologise for the late hour. We have confirmed that Alistair Vane is alive and has entered the old family archive.*

She stared at it.

Another message appeared.

*He is currently with an unidentified individual.*

Aria typed a reply.

*Is anyone in danger?*

Marcus answered.

*We have no confirmation of an immediate threat.*

She sat up.

*Then don't wake me again unless that changes.*

She put the phone down.

It vibrated once more.

*Understood.*

Aria closed her eyes.

Three seconds later, she opened them again.

She picked up the phone.

*And Marcus?*

*Yes, Miss Aria?*

*Tell Felix to stop calling me about historical documents at midnight.*

A reply arrived.

*I will convey the instruction.*

She put the phone down.

**1:08 a.m.**

Victoria was not asleep.

She had received the same update from the Vesper legal office.

She read the message twice.

Then she called Aria.

Aria answered.

“What?”

“Vane is alive.”

“I know.”

“You knew?”

“Marcus told me.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I was going to sleep.”

Victoria paused.

“You're impossible.”

“So I've been informed.”

Victoria looked at the photograph attached to the message.

“Do you think he knows who arranged the reversal?”

“Probably.”

“Then why hasn't he told them?”

“Maybe he doesn't trust them.”

“Who?”

Aria sighed.

“Victoria, it's one in the morning.”

“I know what time it is.”

“Then why are you calling?”

Victoria was silent.

Finally, she said, “I don't want someone else deciding what happens to us.”

Aria's expression softened slightly.

“Neither do I.”

“Then we should be there when they question him.”

“Tomorrow.”

“Why tomorrow?”

“Because tonight I intend to sleep.”

Victoria considered this.

“Fine.”

A pause.

“Aria?”

“Yes?”

“If they find out who signed the approval, I want to hear it directly.”

“So do I.”

They ended the call.

**1:16 a.m.**

The archive's emergency light flickered.

Vane looked towards the corridor.

“We should leave.”

The other man folded the movement record.

“Not without the register.”

“It isn't here.”

“Then we find it.”

Vane shook his head.

“You don't understand. The register isn't the only missing document.”

“What else?”

“The original death verification.”

The man looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because Elias signed it.”

“He certified your death.”

“Yes.”

“But you were alive.”

Vane nodded.

“And Elias knew that.”

The man stared.

“Then the death certificate was deliberately false.”

“Possibly.”

“Why would Elias certify your death?”

Vane looked down at the photograph of himself and Elias.

“Because I asked him to.”

Silence.

The man took a step forward.

“You wanted to be declared dead?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Vane looked at him.

“Because someone had begun using my authority to approach the children.”

The man went still.

“Who?”

Vane reached into his coat and removed a second photograph.

It showed a young woman standing outside a hospital entrance.

The date was written on the back.

The photograph had been taken shortly before the allocation reversal.

Vane placed it beside the old photograph.

“That's the person Elias was trying to keep away from the girls.”

The man looked at the woman.

He recognised her.

His face drained of colour.

“She's dead.”

Vane shook his head.

“That's what the records say.”

He turned towards the archive door.

“Now you understand why I don't trust death certificates.”

**The woman in the photograph was Catherine Vesper.**
