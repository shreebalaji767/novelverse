# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 16 — The Receipt Nobody Was Supposed to Keep**

**Saturday — 8:10 a.m.**

Aria had a problem.

It was not a family problem, a mystery problem or anything involving mysterious companies and suspicious documents.

It was a salary problem.

More specifically, she had discovered that her first month's salary would arrive later than she had expected because the accounting firm processed payments at the end of the month.

She stared at her banking application.

Then she opened her notebook.

Rent. Groceries. Electricity. Transport. Phone bill.

She calculated the remaining amount.

“Fine,” she muttered. “Nobody is starving.”

Her phone rang.

Marcus.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning, Miss Aria. I hope I'm not disturbing you.”

“I'm calculating how much money I can spend without regretting it.”

There was a brief silence.

“I see.”

“Do you?”

“Not entirely.”

Marcus cleared his throat.

“We have a development in the investigation.”

Aria closed her notebook.

“Go ahead.”

“We've located a reference to the second acknowledgement mentioned on Jonathan Vale's receipt. It may have been retained by the recipient.”

“May have been?”

“We have not recovered the document yet.”

“Then keep looking.”

“Of course.”

“And Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“Don't spend money trying to impress me with the investigation.”

“I assure you, Miss Aria, that is not our intention.”

“Good. I have enough people trying to solve problems I haven't asked them to solve.”

Marcus paused.

“I'll remember that.”

**9:25 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

The second acknowledgement had a reference number.

It was written on the back of Jonathan Vale's receipt, beneath the recipient's signature.

The number led to an archived document register maintained by Northstar Document Custody.

The original company had been dissolved, but a successor records firm had acquired some of its remaining physical archives.

Marcus and Felix were reviewing the recovered index.

“Here,” Felix said.

He enlarged an entry.

**Document Reference: NC-2086**

**Description: Special Collection Acknowledgement**

**Origin: Northstar Document Custody**

**Retention: Permanent — Client Instruction**

Marcus read the final line twice.

“Permanent?”

“That's what it says.”

“Who instructed them?”

Felix opened the next field.

The client name was missing.

Instead, the entry contained a code.

**PRIV-07.**

Damian looked over his shoulder.

“Another anonymous account.”

“Not necessarily,” Felix said. “It could be an internal client classification.”

“Can you identify it?”

“I'm trying.”

Marcus turned to the investigator.

“Contact the successor firm. We need the original acknowledgement and its retention history.”

“Understood.”

“Ask them to preserve the archive immediately. No one should remove or alter anything.”

**11:40 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria had not slept particularly well.

Her conversation with Richard continued to bother her.

She had spent years believing that the Sterling family had finally become complete when she was welcomed home.

Now she was finding old payments, unfamiliar companies and references to two separate deliveries.

She didn't like uncertainty.

She especially disliked uncertainty involving her own life.

Her phone vibrated.

*Did you find out why the two deliveries were made?*

She replied:

*Not yet. I found a payment approved by Richard.*

*Then you have a starting point.*

*You said you knew who benefited.*

*I do.*

Victoria sat upright.

*Who?*

The answer arrived several seconds later.

*The people who made sure neither family knew what had happened.*

Victoria frowned.

*That doesn't tell me anything.*

*It tells you where to look.*

*Where?*

*Your father's old correspondence.*

The message ended.

Victoria opened the family archive search on her laptop.

She wasn't going to ask Richard again.

Not until she had something he couldn't dismiss.

**1:15 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The successor records firm responded.

It had located a physical archive box containing documents associated with Northstar Document Custody.

The box was held at a regional storage facility.

However, the firm required formal verification of the original client's authority before releasing the contents.

Marcus reviewed the response.

“That's reasonable.”

Damian looked impatient.

“We can establish the authority.”

“We can. But we need the correct legal documentation.”

Maximilian joined them.

“How long?”

“The firm estimates two business days after receiving the documents.”

Cassian leaned back.

“Two business days. A refreshing reminder that the world does not operate at our preferred speed.”

“Apparently,” Marcus replied, “neither does paperwork.”

Felix looked up from his laptop.

“I can identify PRIV-07.”

Everyone turned toward him.

“It's an internal classification used by Northstar for restricted client files. The code doesn't identify the client, but it does identify the department responsible for authorising access.”

“Which department?” Octavian asked.

Felix opened the record.

“Special Accounts Administration.”

Marcus studied the screen.

“Do we have a surviving employee list?”

“Not yet.”

“Find one.”

**3:30 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria was preparing to visit the supermarket when Helena called.

“Are you free tomorrow?”

“Tomorrow?”

“Sunday lunch.”

Aria smiled.

“I haven't forgotten.”

“I know. I was checking whether you'd like anything particular.”

“Grilled chicken would be nice.”

Helena laughed.

“I had a feeling.”

“And rice. Vegetables. Nothing complicated.”

“Of course.”

Aria hesitated.

“Is Felix going to demonstrate something?”

There was a pause.

“Why do you ask?”

“Because last time he looked like he was planning a presentation.”

Helena laughed again.

“I'll speak to him.”

“Please don't confiscate his tablet.”

“I make no promises.”

After the call, Aria picked up her shopping bag.

She had groceries to buy.

A much more immediate concern than the Vesper investigation.

**Sunday — 12:05 p.m.**

The Vesper family lunch began peacefully.

Aria arrived by taxi, greeted Helena and Isolde, and found everyone already seated.

Felix was not holding a tablet.

This was promising.

“Good afternoon,” she said.

“Good afternoon,” Maximilian replied.

Damian nodded.

Cassian smiled.

Felix looked unusually pleased with himself.

Aria glanced at his hands.

“Where's the tablet?”

“Put away.”

“Excellent.”

“I have a printed document.”

She closed her eyes.

“Felix.”

“It's only one page.”

Cassian laughed into his glass.

Helena gave her youngest son a warning look.

Felix quietly placed the paper beneath his chair.

Lunch proceeded.

The conversation moved between Aria's work, Maximilian's business travel, Damian's security operations and Cassian's complaints about a particularly stubborn negotiation.

Aria listened, asked occasional questions and contributed when she had something to say.

She was beginning to recognise their personalities.

Maximilian liked everything organised.

Damian noticed details other people missed.

Cassian enjoyed making serious situations sound entertaining.

Felix could turn a simple question into a technical discussion lasting several hours.

And Octavian, despite his age, missed very little.

**1:18 p.m.**

During dessert, Felix finally brought out his single-page document.

“I wanted to ask you something.”

Aria looked at the paper.

“What is it?”

“It's a list of technology services available through the family.”

She stared at him.

“Services?”

“Internet security, device support, data recovery, software assistance and technical repairs.”

“That's useful.”

“I thought so.”

“Do I have to use them?”

“No.”

“Pay for them?”

“No.”

“Attend a presentation?”

Felix hesitated.

“Not necessarily.”

Cassian nearly choked on his coffee.

Aria smiled.

“Then I'll keep the paper.”

Felix looked genuinely pleased.

Maximilian took a sip of water.

“Progress.”

**2:07 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

After lunch, Aria walked through the garden with Helena.

The afternoon was pleasant.

For a while, they talked about ordinary things: Aria's job, Helena's reading habits and the difficulty of finding reliable household repair services.

Then Helena asked quietly, “Are you happy here?”

Aria looked at her.

“I am.”

“Good.”

“But I still want my own apartment.”

Helena nodded.

“I understand.”

“I don't want to move because everyone feels guilty.”

“You don't have to.”

Aria looked toward the house.

“I'm glad I met all of you. I just need time.”

Helena took her hand.

“Take all the time you need.”

Aria smiled.

That was enough.

**Monday — 9:45 a.m.**

The successor records firm had completed its preliminary review.

The archive box contained the acknowledgement.

It also contained an accompanying document.

Marcus received the scanned copies at his desk.

The first page was the acknowledgement signed by M. Voss.

The second page was a delivery instruction.

He read it carefully.

**Document Reference: NC-2086**

**Collection: Special Package — Restricted**

**Received by:** M. Voss

**Released to:** Authorised Representative

**Final destination:** Withheld under client instruction

**Verification:** Two independent signatures required

Marcus frowned.

“Two signatures?”

The second signature appeared at the bottom.

It belonged to a person identified only as:

**E. Shaw.**

Marcus immediately forwarded the document to Octavian.

A minute later, the telephone rang.

“Bring the original to my office,” Octavian said.

“I'm on my way.”

**10:20 a.m. — Octavian's Office**

The document lay on the desk.

Octavian studied the signatures.

“E. Shaw.”

Marcus nodded.

“We're checking whether this was Eleanor Shaw, formerly Miriam Shaw, the hospital administrator who gave her statement.”

Damian looked at the document.

“Could it be her?”

“It's possible,” Marcus replied. “But the signature has not been authenticated.”

Octavian turned to Maximilian.

“Contact her through her legal representative. Do not confront her.”

Maximilian nodded.

“And find out who authorised the second signature.”

Marcus pointed to another line.

“There is a reference to an accompanying instruction sheet. The archive firm hasn't located it yet.”

“Why?”

“The index lists it as a separate attachment. It may have been stored elsewhere.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“Then locate it.”

**11:03 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria found the correspondence.

It was in a folder labelled *Administrative Matters — Historical*.

The document was dated twelve years earlier.

It contained a short instruction from the Sterling family office to Crestline Administrative Partners.

The wording was formal.

*Please confirm completion of the agreed records-handling arrangement. Any correspondence relating to the matter must remain within the authorised administrative channel.*

Victoria read it again.

The document did not mention the hospital.

It did not mention the children.

But the date matched the period surrounding the births.

She photographed it.

Then she called Richard.

He answered after the fourth ring.

“What?”

“I found another document.”

“Victoria, not now.”

“It's dated twelve years ago. It's addressed to Crestline.”

Richard said nothing.

“Did you authorise this?”

“I don't remember.”

“That's becoming your favourite answer.”

“Stop searching the archive.”

“Why?”

“Because you are going to make things worse.”

Victoria's voice sharpened.

“For whom?”

Richard ended the call.

Victoria stared at her phone.

Then she sent the photograph to the unknown number.

**12:36 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received the photograph.

She enlarged it.

Her expression changed.

The document was not supposed to have survived.

She read the instruction twice.

Then she called her associate.

“Where is the second acknowledgement?”

“The Vespers have recovered it.”

“And the accompanying instruction?”

“Still missing.”

The woman closed her eyes.

“Then we have a problem.”

“Should we retrieve the document?”

“No. It is already in their possession.”

“What about the Sterling girl?”

The woman looked at the photograph again.

“Victoria has found the correspondence.”

“Is that dangerous?”

“Not yet.”

She placed the photograph beside an old file.

The file contained a list of names.

One name had been crossed out.

Another had been circled.

**Eleanor Shaw.**

The woman touched the paper.

“She signed twice,” she whispered.

**4:10 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received confirmation from Miriam Shaw's legal representative.

Miriam had agreed to review the scanned acknowledgement.

She would not confirm its authenticity without seeing the original.

Marcus arranged a supervised meeting for Wednesday.

Then Felix sent another message.

*I found the storage reference for the missing instruction sheet.*

Marcus opened the attachment.

The sheet had been transferred to a separate archive under a different client classification.

The reference was:

**PRIV-07 / SECOND TRANSFER / ORIGINAL**

Marcus read the words.

Then he looked at the delivery acknowledgement.

Two signatures.

Two deliveries.

A missing instruction.

And a woman who had signed a document connected to both.

He picked up his telephone.

“Mr. Vesper, we have located the reference for the original instruction sheet.”

A pause.

“Where is it?”

“Another archive. We're arranging access.”

“Proceed.”

Marcus looked once more at the file.

For twelve years, the truth had been divided into separate documents.

Now the original instruction was within reach.

And whoever had arranged the transfers had just learned that the Vespers were getting closer.
