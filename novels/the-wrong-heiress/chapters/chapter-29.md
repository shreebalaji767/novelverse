# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 29 — The Courier Who Took the Wrong Child**

**Sunday — 10:02 p.m.**

Marcus had heard Richard Sterling's statement.

He had also heard the silence that followed it.

“Mr. Sterling,” he said carefully, “are you certain?”

“I am certain about what I was told.”

“You said Jonathan Vale changed the instruction.”

“Yes.”

“Did you witness him doing so?”

“No.”

“Did you see him take the child?”

“No.”

“Then we need to establish exactly what you know.”

Richard exhaled sharply.

“I know that Mara Voss arranged for the infant to be brought to the estate. I know she told me the child would eventually be transferred to the Vespers. I know the arrangement was suspended. And I know she later told me Jonathan Vale had interfered.”

Marcus wrote everything down.

“Did she say how?”

“She said he had taken the child somewhere he had no authority to take it.”

“Where?”

“She didn't say.”

“Did she tell you why?”

Richard hesitated.

“She said the child was no longer available for transfer.”

Marcus looked towards the conference room.

“Mr. Sterling, we need you to come to Vesper Tower. Bring the original notebook and any documents you have.”

“I am already on my way.”

“Please do not travel alone.”

“I understand.”

The call ended.

Marcus immediately contacted Octavian.

**10:24 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Octavian stood at the head of the conference table.

The family had gathered again.

Richard's latest statement had changed the investigation.

Until now, the team had believed the Sterling estate was an intermediate location where Infant B had been held before an unknown transfer.

Now there was a possible explanation.

Jonathan Vale had intervened.

The courier who had delivered the second set of documents might also have removed the child.

Cassian looked at the transport record.

“That's an extraordinary amount of responsibility for a courier.”

“Unless he wasn't acting as a courier,” Maximilian replied.

Damian crossed his arms.

“He could have been working for someone else.”

Felix shook his head.

“His company was registered as private logistics. It wasn't a conventional courier service.”

“Did you find any other deliveries?” Lucian asked.

“Several. Most were routine document transfers. A few were marked Route C.”

“Any involving children?” Helena asked.

“Not in the records we have.”

Aria looked at the estate photograph.

“Why would Jonathan take the baby?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Cassian leaned forward.

“Possibilities include a personal connection, a separate instruction, or a decision to prevent the original transfer.”

“Or someone paid him,” Damian said.

“Possible. But we have no evidence of that.”

Octavian looked at Marcus.

“Richard is coming?”

“Yes.”

“Good. We will hear his complete account before drawing conclusions.”

**11:07 p.m. — Vesper Tower, Private Conference Room**

Richard arrived carrying a briefcase.

He looked older than he had the previous day.

Marcus escorted him inside.

Octavian remained standing.

“Mr. Sterling.”

“Mr. Vesper.”

They exchanged a brief nod.

Richard placed the briefcase on the table.

“The notebook.”

The legal representative documented its condition before opening it.

Richard sat down.

“I should have brought this earlier.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Yes.”

Richard accepted the answer.

He opened the notebook to the relevant pages.

The first entry was dated twelve years earlier.

*Original instruction received from L. Vale.*

The next entry:

*Second placement to be completed only after confirmation of first placement.*

Then the different handwriting:

*If the first placement becomes permanent, the second child must not be returned.*

And below it:

*Do not allow either family to learn that the other child was withheld.*

Maximilian studied the handwriting.

“Who wrote the additional notes?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you recognise the handwriting?”

“No.”

Richard turned another page.

There was a short entry.

*D. Mercer to complete initial transfer.*

And below it:

*J. Vale responsible for subsequent delivery.*

Felix looked up.

“That's important.”

Richard nodded.

“I didn't understand it when I wrote it.”

“Why did you write it?” Aria asked.

Richard looked at her.

“Because I was keeping records of the administrative arrangement.”

“You were keeping records of something you didn't understand?”

Richard looked uncomfortable.

“I knew enough to be cautious. I didn't know enough to understand the purpose.”

Aria held his gaze.

“Did you ask?”

“Yes.”

“And what did they tell you?”

“That the records of two families had to remain separate until the process was completed.”

“Which two families?”

Richard hesitated.

“The Sterlings and the Vespers.”

The room went quiet.

Helena looked at Lucian.

Octavian's expression did not change.

Richard continued.

“I was told one child would be placed with the Sterlings. The other would be transferred to the Vespers.”

Aria looked down at the notebook.

“So the original plan involved two children and two families.”

“Yes.”

“Was Victoria supposed to be the child sent to the Sterlings?”

Richard looked at her.

“I don't know.”

“Was I supposed to be sent to the Vespers?”

“I don't know.”

Aria nodded slowly.

“Then we still don't know which child was supposed to go where.”

Richard lowered his eyes.

“No.”

**11:39 p.m. — The Original Arrangement**

Felix projected the notebook entries alongside the recovered records.

The pieces began to align.

The original instruction had involved two separate placements.

Subject 02 had been provisionally placed with the Sterling family.

Subject 01 had been assigned temporary custody through the Sterling trust estate.

The second placement had depended on confirmation of the first.

But the notebook contained a warning that neither family should learn the other child had been withheld.

Maximilian looked at Richard.

“Who told you the first placement had become permanent?”

“Mara Voss.”

“When?”

“Several weeks after the children were transferred.”

“What did she mean by permanent?”

“She said the first family had accepted the child.”

Richard looked at Aria.

“I assumed she meant you.”

Aria's expression remained unreadable.

“You assumed?”

“Yes.”

“But the first placement could have referred to Victoria.”

Richard looked down.

“I understand that now.”

Cassian tapped the table.

“Then we have a serious ambiguity. The documents use Subject 01 and Subject 02, but we still don't know which biological child each designation represents.”

Felix nodded.

“And the temporary custody of Subject 01 doesn't establish the child's identity.”

Octavian looked at Richard.

“Why did you keep the document saying the second transfer was not completed?”

Richard's face tightened.

“Because I was afraid.”

“Of whom?”

“Mara Voss. The people behind her. I didn't know who they were.”

“You knew the arrangement involved children.”

“Yes.”

“And you remained silent.”

Richard did not defend himself.

“Yes.”

Aria looked at him.

“Why didn't you tell anyone when you found out the second transfer had failed?”

Richard's answer came slowly.

“Because I thought the first placement had already been completed. I thought the child in our family was safe. I believed the second arrangement had been cancelled for reasons I wasn't authorised to know.”

“That doesn't explain why you kept quiet.”

“No.”

He looked at her.

“It doesn't.”

**Monday — 12:18 a.m.**

The conference continued.

Felix displayed the old transport records.

“Jonathan Vale's company was responsible for the final movement from the Sterling estate. The Route C reference appears on both the company records and the estate payment register.”

Marcus looked at Richard.

“Did Mara tell you Jonathan had taken the infant?”

“She said he had interfered with the transfer.”

“Did she say he had taken the child?”

“Yes.”

“Did she say where?”

“No.”

“Did she say whether he had been authorised by anyone?”

“She said he had no authority.”

Damian leaned forward.

“Did you believe her?”

Richard looked at him.

“I didn't know what to believe.”

“Did you report it?”

“No.”

Damian's expression hardened.

Richard accepted it.

“I should have.”

Aria looked at the transport record.

“Jonathan died eleven years ago.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“Then we need to find out what happened to the child before he died.”

Margaret Vale's name appeared on the screen.

Jonathan's sister.

The person who had given the Vespers his old papers.

Marcus nodded.

“We'll contact her again.”

Octavian looked at the legal representative.

“Arrange a formal meeting. We will not pressure her.”

**8:14 a.m. — Margaret Vale's Home**

Margaret answered the door herself.

Marcus stood outside with a legal representative.

She recognised him immediately.

“Mr. Vale.”

“Good morning, Ms. Vale.”

“Have you found something?”

“Yes. Something important.”

She stepped aside.

“Come in.”

They sat in the living room.

Marcus explained the new evidence.

Jonathan's company had transported Infant B from the Sterling estate.

The transport record was signed by Jonathan.

The final destination had not been recorded.

Margaret listened without interrupting.

When Marcus finished, she looked at him.

“My brother never told me he transported a baby.”

“Did he ever mention the Sterling estate?”

“No.”

“Did he mention a child?”

Margaret hesitated.

“He mentioned a family.”

“What family?”

“I don't know. He said someone had asked him to move a child away from a place where the child wasn't wanted.”

Marcus leaned forward.

“When did he say that?”

“Several days after the delivery.”

“Did he explain who had asked him?”

“No. He was angry.”

“What did he say exactly?”

Margaret thought.

“He said, ‘They wanted me to deliver a child to a family that had never agreed to receive one.’”

Marcus went still.

“Did he say which family?”

“No.”

“Did he say where he took the child?”

Margaret shook her head.

“He told me it was safer not to know.”

She stood and walked to a cabinet.

Then she returned with a small wooden box.

“I found this after you collected the first batch of his papers. I didn't know whether it was relevant.”

She placed it on the table.

Inside were several folded notes, an old photograph, and a small envelope.

Marcus looked at the envelope.

It was addressed in Jonathan's handwriting.

**For Margaret. If anything happens to me.**

**9:02 a.m. — Margaret's Living Room**

Margaret opened the envelope.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

Jonathan's handwriting was hurried.

*Margaret,*

*If anyone asks about the child, I did not abandon the baby.*

*I was told to take the child somewhere safe. I followed the instruction because the people who arranged the original transfer were not going to protect the child.*

*I do not know whether I did the right thing.*

*But I know I could not leave the baby with them.*

There was no signature.

Margaret's hands trembled.

“He wrote this before he died.”

Marcus read it again.

“Did he ever explain who gave him the instruction?”

“No.”

“Did he mention a destination?”

Margaret shook her head.

“He only said the child was safe.”

The legal representative asked gently, “Did he leave anything else?”

Margaret looked at the wooden box.

“There is one more thing.”

She removed the photograph.

It showed Jonathan standing beside a small building.

He was holding a cardboard box.

Beside him stood a woman.

Her face was partially turned away.

Marcus looked closely.

The woman wore a dark coat and held a folder.

He recognised the photograph's date.

It had been taken three days after Infant B left the Sterling estate.

Margaret pointed to the woman.

“Jonathan said she was the person who helped him.”

Marcus felt his stomach tighten.

“Did he give you her name?”

Margaret shook her head.

“No. He said she was the only person who knew where the child had gone.”

**10:26 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix enlarged the photograph.

The woman was visible only from the side.

Her face was partially obscured by the angle.

But her clothing and posture matched the description given by Miriam Shaw and Helen Ward.

Cassian looked at the image.

“Mara Voss.”

“Possibly,” Maximilian said.

Felix compared it against the old hospital photograph.

“The height and build are consistent. We cannot establish identity from this image alone.”

Marcus looked at the date.

“Three days after the infant left the estate.”

“Correct,” Felix said.

“Where was the building?”

Felix scanned the background.

“There is a street sign.”

He enlarged it.

A name appeared.

**ASHFORD LANE.**

Cassian looked at him.

“Can you identify the building?”

“I'm working on it.”

“Please work faster.”

“I am!”

Aria entered the room.

“What happened?”

Marcus turned the photograph towards her.

“We found another image of the woman connected to the transfer.”

Aria studied it.

“Do we know who she is?”

“Not conclusively.”

“Do we know where the photograph was taken?”

“Not yet.”

Aria looked at Jonathan.

“He looks different here.”

Cassian raised an eyebrow.

“Different how?”

“Less like a courier.”

Felix frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“He's not holding documents. He's carrying a box. He looks like someone who is helping another person move something.”

Cassian examined the photograph again.

“That is a fair observation.”

Aria pointed to the woman.

“And she's not standing beside him like a client. She's looking at him.”

Marcus nodded.

“Meaning?”

“Meaning they may have been working together.”

Felix returned to his computer.

“I can compare the building's exterior against historical property photographs.”

Aria stepped back.

“Then do that.”

**11:43 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria found Richard in the dining room.

He looked exhausted.

“You gave them the notebook.”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“They found records proving the child was delivered to the estate.”

Victoria sat opposite him.

“Was it me?”

Richard looked at her.

“I don't know.”

She laughed once, without humour.

“Of course.”

“Victoria—”

“Did the Vespers find out where the child went?”

“They found that Jonathan Vale took the child.”

“Then the child could have been me.”

“Yes.”

“Or Aria.”

“Yes.”

Victoria stared at him.

“Do you understand what that means?”

Richard nodded.

“It means we need to establish the identities.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“And if that child was me?”

Richard said nothing.

She looked away.

For once, she did not have a speech prepared.

She had spent years believing that her biological identity gave her a rightful place in the Sterling family.

Now she was being confronted with the possibility that the very records which had established her identity might have been manipulated.

“Did Mother know?” she asked.

“I don't know.”

“Did you ask her?”

“Not yet.”

Victoria stood.

“Then ask her.”

**1:15 p.m. — Sterling Mansion, Eleanor's Sitting Room**

Eleanor listened to Richard's explanation.

At first, she looked confused.

Then alarmed.

Finally, angry.

“You approved the estate for an infant?”

“Yes.”

“And you never told me?”

“I was told it was a confidential arrangement.”

“Richard, that was our family property.”

“I know.”

“An infant was kept there?”

“Yes.”

Eleanor stood.

“Who was the child?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you see the child?”

“No.”

“Did you ever ask me to visit the estate?”

“No.”

Eleanor stared at him.

“Then why was I never informed?”

Richard looked at her.

“I was told the less people who knew, the better.”

Eleanor shook her head.

“I remember the estate. I remember being told it was closed for maintenance.”

Richard looked up.

“Who told you?”

“Your office.”

“Was anyone there?”

“No.”

“Did you ever meet Eleanor Shaw?”

Eleanor paused.

“No.”

Richard sat back.

“Then you knew nothing?”

Eleanor's expression hardened.

“I knew the estate was being used for something confidential. I did not know a child was involved.”

Victoria stood in the doorway.

She had heard everything.

“Then none of us knew anything?”

Neither parent answered.

**3:32 p.m. — Vesper Tower, Research Division**

Felix finally identified the building in Jonathan's photograph.

It was a small private residence on Ashford Lane.

The property had been owned by a charitable organisation that provided temporary accommodation to families in crisis.

The organisation had closed eleven years earlier.

Its director was still alive.

Felix opened the archived records.

“There's something else.”

Maximilian approached.

“What?”

“The charity received a donation three days after Jonathan's photograph was taken.”

“From whom?”

“An anonymous trust.”

“Amount?”

Felix displayed the figure.

Cassian whistled softly.

“That is not a casual donation.”

“No. It funded the organisation's operations for almost two years.”

Marcus studied the record.

“Did the donation include conditions?”

Felix opened the attached agreement.

The first page contained ordinary charitable terms.

The second page contained a handwritten clause.

*One child may remain under confidential protection. No identity or family information is to be disclosed without written authorisation from the designated guardian.*

Aria read it.

“Designated guardian?”

Felix scrolled down.

The guardian's name was not printed.

Instead, there was a reference.

**GUARDIAN FILE: MV-04.**

Cassian looked at the screen.

“The same beneficiary reference from the unpaid Crestline account.”

Maximilian nodded.

“Then the person who was supposed to receive the final payment may have become the child's guardian.”

Marcus looked at the record.

“Can we identify MV-04?”

Felix shook his head.

“Not yet.”

Aria looked at the photograph again.

“Then we have a place where the child was taken, a charity that protected the child, and a guardian who was never named.”

Octavian, who had entered quietly, listened to the summary.

“Locate the former director.”

Marcus nodded.

“Immediately.”

**6:48 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a message.

*The Vespers have identified Ashford Lane.*

She read it.

Then another message arrived.

*They have also found the charitable organisation and the guardian reference.*

For several seconds, she remained motionless.

Then she opened the drawer containing the Subject 01 folder.

Inside was a photograph.

A child, several years older than the infant in the hospital records.

The child's face was visible.

The woman looked at it.

Then she closed the drawer.

“Find the former director,” she said into the telephone.

“Should we contact him?”

“No.”

“Then what should we do?”

“Find out whether he still has the original guardian register.”

The associate hesitated.

“And if the Vespers have already contacted him?”

The woman looked towards the window.

“Then we are running out of time.”

**9:17 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria received a message from Marcus.

*We have identified a former charitable organisation that appears to have provided temporary protection to Infant B. Its records include an unnamed guardian reference, MV-04. The former director is being located.*

She read it.

Then she asked:

*Do we know whether the child remained there?*

Marcus replied:

*Not yet.*

Aria looked at the photograph of her childhood.

She thought about Victoria.

She thought about the child in the estate photograph.

And she thought about Jonathan Vale's letter.

*I did not abandon the baby.*

The courier had not simply disappeared with an infant.

He had apparently believed he was protecting one.

But from whom?

Aria put her phone down.

Then it vibrated again.

An unfamiliar number.

**You are looking for the wrong child.**

She stared at the message.

Another arrived.

**The child Jonathan took was not the child the Vespers were supposed to receive.**

Aria did not respond.

She forwarded the messages to Marcus.

Then she sat quietly.

For the first time, she wondered whether the investigation had been following two separate children all along.

And whether someone had deliberately made sure they never learned which child was which.
