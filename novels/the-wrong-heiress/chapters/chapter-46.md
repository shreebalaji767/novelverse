# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 46 — The Document Catherine Never Wanted Found**

**Thursday — 1:05 p.m.**

Octavian Vesper had discovered three things in the old administrative archive.

The first was that someone had accessed it remotely.

The second was that the access had been authorised using his own credentials.

The third was that the document he had come to retrieve was missing.

He stood in the archive's central room, staring at an empty drawer.

Felix examined the terminal.

“Someone used your authority token.”

“I know.”

“The access record says you entered the archive at 12:26.”

“I was in the east wing at 12:26.”

“I know that too.”

“Then why are you asking?”

“Because I wanted to establish whether you had developed the ability to be in two places simultaneously.”

Octavian looked at him.

Felix returned to the terminal.

“Still researching.”

Maximilian stood beside the open drawer.

“What was kept here?”

“The original custodial declaration.”

Lucian frowned.

“Elias's declaration?”

“No. My mother's.”

Octavian's mother, Evelyn Vesper, had created the legacy trust.

Her declaration established its purpose, defined its independent custodianship, and restricted the circumstances under which its protected assets could be transferred.

It was the document that had started everything.

And someone had removed it.

“Was there a copy?” Damian asked.

“One.”

“Where?”

Octavian looked at the empty drawer.

“Catherine had it.”

Cassian slowly turned.

“Your sister has the original declaration?”

“She was entrusted with it.”

“By your mother?”

“Yes.”

Felix stopped typing.

“Then why did everyone believe Elias was the original custodian?”

“Because he was appointed to administer the trust after Catherine relinquished the position.”

“Did she relinquish it voluntarily?”

Octavian did not answer immediately.

“No.”

**1:32 p.m.**

At Elysium Island, Catherine was reading the message Elena had received.

**ARCHIVE ACCESS CONFIRMED. ORIGINAL DECLARATION REMOVED.**

Margaret Ellison stood near the door.

“You should leave.”

Catherine continued reading.

“You've said that before.”

“This is different.”

“Everything is different when the consequences finally arrive.”

Margaret approached the table.

“Octavian will come looking for you.”

“He already knows I'm alive.”

“He doesn't know why you disappeared.”

“He will.”

“And when he discovers what happened to Elias?”

Catherine looked up.

“Then he will have to decide whether he wants the truth or another comfortable explanation.”

Margaret's expression tightened.

“You cannot blame him for what happened.”

“I don't.”

“Then why are you speaking as though he is responsible?”

“Because he was responsible for the family.”

Catherine placed the message down.

“Not for every crime committed inside it. But for what he chose not to investigate.”

Margaret looked away.

For a moment, neither woman spoke.

Then Elena entered.

“There's another problem.”

Catherine waited.

“Someone contacted the Sterling family.”

Margaret looked up sharply.

“Which one?”

“Richard Sterling.”

Catherine's expression changed.

“What did they tell him?”

“That the original declaration concerns his daughter.”

Margaret closed her eyes.

Catherine stood.

“Who sent it?”

“An anonymous account.”

Catherine walked toward the window.

“Then the person who removed the document isn't trying to hide anymore.”

**2:10 p.m.**

Richard Sterling received the message while reviewing a financial report.

It contained one attachment.

A photograph of an old document.

At the top was the Vesper family seal.

At the bottom was a handwritten note:

**The Sterling household was never meant to receive the Vesper child. The original custodian approved the arrangement. Ask Octavian why he allowed it.**

Richard read it twice.

He immediately called Lucian.

Lucian answered on the second ring.

“What is it?”

“Someone has sent me a document.”

“What document?”

“Something concerning the original allocation.”

Richard forwarded the photograph.

Lucian opened it.

His expression darkened.

“Where did you get this?”

“An anonymous message.”

“Don't respond to it.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

“Richard, do not contact anyone connected to this document.”

Richard hesitated.

“Does it prove Catherine arranged the reversal?”

“No.”

“It says the original custodian approved the arrangement.”

“It says someone wants you to believe that.”

Richard looked at the photograph again.

There was a handwritten notation beneath the seal.

**Custodial acceptance — C.V.**

He had seen those initials before.

“Lucian.”

“What?”

“Who was the original custodian?”

Lucian looked toward the conference room.

“Catherine.”

Richard went silent.

“Then the message may be telling the truth.”

“Or it may be using a genuine signature to support a false conclusion.”

Richard leaned back.

“Do you think she ordered the children switched?”

“I don't know.”

“Do you think Octavian knows?”

Lucian's answer was immediate.

“He knows more than he has told us.”

**2:48 p.m.**

Aria was not particularly interested in being summoned to another family meeting.

She had already spent most of the afternoon listening to people discuss documents that nobody had seen in their entirety.

She entered the conference room with Victoria.

Richard was there.

So were Lucian, Octavian, Maximilian and Cassian.

Felix had three screens open.

“Why is Richard here?” Victoria asked.

Richard looked at her.

“I received a message.”

“From whom?”

“Unknown.”

Aria sat down.

“What's in it?”

Richard showed them the photograph.

Victoria examined the notation.

“C.V.”

“Catherine Vesper,” Lucian said.

Victoria looked at Octavian.

“Was she the custodian?”

“Yes.”

“Then why was Elias handling the trust?”

“Catherine relinquished the position.”

“Why?”

Octavian's expression tightened.

“She believed the trust was being compromised.”

Aria studied the photograph again.

“Did she relinquish it before or after the allocation was reversed?”

Octavian looked at Felix.

Felix searched the records.

“According to the surviving appointment register, three months before the reversal.”

“Was that her decision?”

“Yes,” Octavian said.

Aria looked at him.

“Did you ask her why?”

The question landed harder than it should have.

Octavian took a moment.

“No.”

“Why not?”

“Because I believed she had made her decision.”

Aria returned the photograph to Richard.

“Then perhaps someone should have asked.”

Nobody argued.

Victoria crossed her arms.

“So Catherine gave up control of the trust, disappeared, and then someone reversed the allocation using her old authority?”

“That's what the records suggest,” Maximilian said.

“Then why would the person who reversed it use her initials?”

Felix looked up.

“That's the problem.”

He displayed the original record.

“C.V. appears on the custodial acceptance line. But the reversal itself was submitted through Margaret Ellison's credentials.”

Victoria frowned.

“So the initials don't prove Catherine approved the reversal.”

“Correct.”

“Then the anonymous message is deliberately combining two separate events.”

Felix nodded.

“Exactly.”

Aria looked at the document.

“Someone wants us to blame Catherine before we meet her.”

Cassian leaned forward.

“Which suggests they believe we might actually listen to her.”

**3:20 p.m.**

The meeting with Catherine was postponed.

Not cancelled.

Postponed.

The distinction came through Elena Voss, who sent a new message with a different proposed location and a revised condition.

Catherine would meet Aria and Victoria at a private residence on Elysium Island.

They could bring one person each.

No other family members would be permitted inside.

Damian objected immediately.

“One person each is not sufficient.”

“It's their decision,” Lucian said.

“It's a security risk.”

“Then we assess the location.”

Voss, who had been listening silently, spoke.

“She will not agree to an armed escort.”

Damian looked at him.

“Does she have a reason to fear one?”

“Yes.”

“What reason?”

Voss paused.

“Your family has been looking for her for nineteen years. She has been looking for the people who arranged the reversal for just as long.”

“That doesn't answer the question.”

“It is the answer I have.”

Aria looked at Victoria.

“I'll go.”

Victoria nodded.

“So will I.”

Helena's face tightened.

“Are you certain?”

“No,” Aria said. “But I want to know why she has been involved in our lives since before we were born.”

Victoria glanced at her.

“And I want to know whether she knows who arranged my removal from the estate.”

Octavian stood.

“I will not permit either of you to walk into an unknown situation.”

Aria met his gaze.

“You don't have to permit it.”

The old man stopped.

She continued, calmly.

“We're not asking you to approve our lives. We're telling you what we've decided.”

Octavian looked at her for a long moment.

Then he nodded once.

“Then I will respect your decision.”

**4:06 p.m.**

The old residence had been closed for years.

It stood on the northern side of Elysium Island, away from the main estate buildings.

The security team inspected the grounds before Aria and Victoria arrived.

No armed personnel entered the house.

Marcus accompanied Aria.

Maximilian accompanied Victoria.

They entered through separate doors and met in the central hall.

Catherine was waiting beside a fireplace.

She was smaller than Aria had expected.

Not physically imposing.

Not particularly theatrical.

She wore a dark grey suit and held a walking stick.

Her eyes moved from Aria to Victoria.

For several seconds, she said nothing.

Then she spoke.

“Aria.”

Aria did not approach.

“Catherine.”

Catherine turned to Victoria.

“Victoria Sterling.”

Victoria's expression sharpened.

“You know my name.”

“I know both of your names.”

“Then you know we didn't come here to be welcomed.”

A faint smile crossed Catherine's face.

“Good. I dislike ceremonial conversations.”

She gestured toward the chairs.

Neither girl sat.

Catherine lowered her hand.

“Very well.”

Aria studied her.

“Did you arrange the switch?”

“No.”

“Did you know it would happen?”

Catherine's expression became serious.

“I knew someone was preparing to alter the allocation.”

“Who?”

“I don't know.”

Victoria's patience visibly thinned.

“You were the original custodian. You had access to the trust. You appointed Edmund Voss. You knew about us before we were born. And you don't know who did it?”

“I know who had the authority to do it.”

“Who?”

“Margaret Ellison.”

Victoria stared at her.

“Then she did it.”

“No.”

Catherine's answer was immediate.

“Margaret submitted the instruction. That does not mean she originated it.”

Aria stepped closer.

“Did you ask her to?”

“No.”

“Did you know she was going to?”

“No.”

“Then why did you give up the custodianship?”

Catherine looked at her.

“Because I discovered that someone inside the family had been using the trust to purchase influence inside the Vesper family.”

She paused.

“And because I realised the two children were being treated as instruments in a dispute that had begun long before either of you existed.”

Victoria's voice hardened.

“Who was fighting over us?”

Catherine looked between them.

“Not over you.”

“Then what?”

“The authority that would eventually belong to you.”

Aria frowned.

“We haven't agreed to inherit anything.”

“I know.”

“Then stop speaking as though it's already decided.”

Catherine lowered her head slightly.

“You're right.”

The answer surprised Aria.

Catherine continued.

“The trust was never meant to make either of you obedient to the Vesper family. It was created to prevent that.”

Victoria looked sceptical.

“Then why were we separated?”

Catherine's face changed.

“Because I made a decision.”

Silence.

Marcus glanced at Aria.

Maximilian's attention sharpened.

Catherine gripped her walking stick.

“I did not reverse the allocation. But I did know that the original arrangement was no longer safe.”

Aria stared at her.

“What did you do?”

Catherine looked directly at her.

“I ordered Edmund Voss to protect both children.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“And then?”

Catherine took a breath.

“Then I disappeared.”

**4:22 p.m.**

Victoria stepped forward.

“You disappeared and left us there?”

“I believed the protection would continue.”

“You believed?”

“Yes.”

“That's convenient.”

Catherine accepted the accusation without protest.

“I was wrong.”

Victoria looked away.

Aria remained still.

“Why did you disappear?”

“Because someone inside the family had discovered that I intended to transfer the trust's custodial authority to an independent body.”

“Who?”

“I don't know.”

Victoria gave a sharp laugh.

“Another mystery.”

Catherine looked at her.

“You have every right to be angry.”

“I'm not asking for permission.”

“No. You aren't.”

Aria asked the next question.

“Did you know Jonathan Vale took Victoria from the Sterling estate?”

Catherine nodded.

Victoria turned sharply.

“You knew?”

“Not immediately. I learned several weeks later.”

“Did you contact him?”

“Yes.”

“What did you tell him?”

“To keep you away from the people who had arranged the transfer.”

Victoria's expression changed.

“Did you tell him to raise me as Lily?”

“No. That was his decision.”

“Did you know where I was?”

“For a time.”

“Why didn't you come?”

Catherine looked down.

“Because the people searching for you were still using the trust's records. If I approached you, I would have led them to you.”

Victoria's hands tightened.

“You could have told me.”

“You were a child.”

“I was a child when everyone else decided what my life would be.”

Catherine did not answer.

For the first time, she looked genuinely ashamed.

Aria watched her carefully.

There was no satisfaction in the woman's expression.

No obvious attempt to manipulate them.

But regret did not erase consequences.

“Who was searching for Victoria?” Aria asked.

Catherine raised her eyes.

“Someone who believed the two of you had to remain separate.”

“Why?”

“Because if you were reunited, the trust could be independently verified.”

Victoria frowned.

“And that would be bad?”

“For the person who had been controlling its assets.”

Aria went still.

“Someone has been using the trust?”

Catherine nodded.

“Not merely using it. Redirecting its voting rights.”

Marcus's expression changed.

“That would mean the trust's holdings have been influencing corporate decisions.”

“Yes.”

Maximilian looked at Catherine.

“How long?”

“Twenty-three years.”

The room fell silent.

Catherine looked at the two girls.

“The person responsible has spent more than two decades making sure neither of you could examine the trust.”

Aria's voice was quiet.

“Who is it?”

Catherine looked toward the closed door.

Then she answered.

“Your grandmother's former legal adviser.”

Victoria frowned.

“Elias?”

“No.”

Catherine's eyes returned to them.

“Someone who was never supposed to have access to the trust at all.”

**4:31 p.m.**

A sudden knock sounded from the hall.

Marcus turned.

A security officer entered.

“Mr. Vesper. We have a situation.”

Maximilian stepped forward.

“What happened?”

“An individual has arrived at the estate claiming to represent the trust's third independent trustee.”

Catherine's face drained of colour.

“Who?”

The officer checked his tablet.

“Adrian Blackwood.”

Catherine looked at Aria.

Then at Victoria.

“That is impossible.”

Aria frowned.

“Why?”

Catherine gripped her walking stick.

“Because Adrian Blackwood died six years ago.”

**End of Chapter 46**
