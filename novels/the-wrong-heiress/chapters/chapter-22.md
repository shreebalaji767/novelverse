# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 22 — The Child Without a Family**

**Wednesday — 7:15 a.m.**

Felix had printed the Subject 01 record.

He had also printed the Subject 02 record.

He had placed both documents side by side on his desk.

Then he had drawn a line between them.

Then he had erased the line.

Then he had drawn it again.

Marcus watched him.

“Are you finished?”

“No.”

“You've been doing that for twenty minutes.”

“Because the records are inconsistent.”

“Explain.”

Felix pointed at the first document.

“Subject 02 was assigned to the Sterling family. The placement was provisional, but active.”

He pointed at the second.

“Subject 01 had no receiving family. Its identity verification was withheld. Three months later, Mara Voss closed the placement.”

Marcus studied the dates.

“So one child was placed. The other wasn't.”

“Correct.”

“Why?”

Felix shrugged.

“That's the question.”

Marcus looked at him.

“Can you identify Subject 01?”

“Not from these records.”

“Can you identify the hospital?”

“Not yet. The reference number belongs to a separate archive.”

Marcus sighed.

“Then find that archive.”

Felix nodded.

“Already looking.”

**9:00 a.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria arrived for the meeting.

She found Octavian, Isolde, Lucian and Helena in the study.

Marcus stood beside a table with several documents arranged neatly.

“Good morning.”

Helena smiled.

“Good morning, darling.”

Aria sat down.

“So. What did you find?”

Octavian gestured toward the documents.

“A placement record from the organisation that handled the old hospital transfers.”

Marcus explained the findings carefully.

The first record concerned Aria.

It identified her as Subject 02 and listed the Sterling family as the receiving family.

The placement had been marked provisional.

The second record concerned Subject 01.

That child had no assigned receiving family.

Three months later, Mara Voss had closed the placement.

Aria read the documents.

“Wait.”

She looked up.

“Does this mean someone planned to move me somewhere else?”

“We cannot say that yet,” Marcus replied.

“Then what does provisional mean?”

“It means the placement was not considered final.”

“Why?”

“We don't know.”

Aria looked at the second document.

“And who was Subject 01?”

“We don't know that either.”

She leaned back.

“So we're looking for another child.”

“Possibly,” Octavian said.

“Possibly?”

“We cannot establish that Subject 01 was a living child from this document alone. The terminology suggests it, but we need supporting evidence.”

Aria nodded.

“Good. I don't want everyone deciding what happened before we know.”

Isolde watched her quietly.

“You are right.”

**9:45 a.m.**

Aria picked up the placement record again.

“There's something I don't understand.”

Marcus waited.

“If they were planning two placements, why did they only complete one?”

“We're investigating that,” he said.

“And why did the second record stay open for three months?”

“We don't know.”

She put the paper down.

“Then find the person who closed it.”

Octavian nodded.

“We intend to.”

Aria stood.

“That's all I wanted to know.”

Helena looked surprised.

“You don't want to stay?”

“I have work.”

Lucian almost spoke.

Then he stopped himself.

Aria noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing. I was going to ask if you wanted someone to drive you.”

She smiled faintly.

“No, thank you. I can manage.”

He nodded.

“Of course.”

She left.

Octavian watched the door close.

“She is handling this better than I expected.”

Isolde glanced at him.

“She is handling it in her own way. Don't confuse that with not being affected.”

Octavian said nothing.

**11:20 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

The investigation team had located an old archive reference.

It belonged to Central Women's Hospital.

Unlike the Northbridge files, this archive had never been transferred to Asterion.

It had been stored by an independent medical records contractor.

The contractor had since been acquired twice.

Marcus reviewed the legal department's findings.

“The current owner has agreed to preserve the records. They require a formal request before releasing anything.”

“Submit it,” Octavian said.

“There's another issue.”

“What?”

“The archive reference identifies two maternity records, but the patient names are sealed.”

“Can the records be examined?”

“Under supervised legal review, yes.”

Octavian nodded.

“Arrange it.”

Damian looked at the reference.

“Could this identify Subject 01?”

“It might,” Marcus replied.

“Then we should be there.”

**1:10 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria sat across from Richard.

She had brought another set of documents.

Richard looked at them without touching them.

“What now?”

“I found something interesting.”

“Victoria, I have asked you to stop.”

“You've asked me many things.”

She placed the documents on the table.

“Subject 01 and Subject 02.”

Richard looked up.

“Where did you get those names?”

“Does that matter?”

“Yes.”

Victoria smiled.

“Then perhaps you should tell me what you know.”

Richard read the documents.

His expression changed.

“Where did you get this?”

“I told you. I have sources.”

“Who?”

“Someone who knows more than you do.”

Richard pushed the papers away.

“Do not trust that person.”

Victoria's smile disappeared.

“You keep saying that.”

“Because I mean it.”

“Then tell me something useful.”

Richard looked at her.

“I don't know who Subject 01 was.”

“Did you know there was another child?”

“No.”

“Did you know the placement was provisional?”

Richard didn't answer immediately.

Victoria noticed.

“You did.”

“I saw a reference to a second placement. I didn't know it referred to another child.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I didn't understand it.”

Victoria stood.

“You don't understand anything, do you?”

Richard's face hardened.

“Enough.”

She gathered the documents.

“Fine. I'll find someone who does.”

**2:35 p.m. — Central Women's Hospital Archive**

The legal review began under supervision.

The archive contractor provided a sealed storage container containing the original maternity transfer register and related administrative documents.

The Vesper representatives were not permitted to remove anything.

They could inspect and photograph authorised pages.

A records specialist opened the container.

Marcus stood beside Damian.

The first document was a hospital transfer index.

Two entries appeared on the same date.

The first concerned the infant later identified as Aria Vesper.

The second was listed under a temporary medical reference.

**INFANT B — MATERNITY UNIT**

The identity field was blank.

Damian leaned closer.

“Is there a name elsewhere?”

The specialist checked the linked index.

“No name. Only a temporary reference.”

“Was the infant discharged?”

The specialist turned another page.

The discharge field was marked:

**TRANSFERRED — AUTHORISED EXTERNAL HANDOVER**

Marcus looked at the specialist.

“Who authorised it?”

The specialist checked the signature.

“Hospital administrator.”

“Name?”

The signature was difficult to read.

But the typed authorisation field was clear.

**D. MERCER**

Damian looked at Marcus.

Daniel Mercer.

The same administrator who had later been transferred to Asterion.

**3:05 p.m.**

They continued.

The next document was an external handover receipt.

It contained a date, a time, and the receiving organisation.

But the organisation field had been partially covered by a correction label.

The records specialist examined it under magnification.

A few letters remained visible.

**...BELLWEATHER...**

Marcus photographed the page.

“Can you identify the original entry?”

“Not without examining the underlying paper. That would require a separate conservation procedure.”

“Arrange it.”

The specialist nodded.

Damian examined the final page.

It was a hospital incident note.

*Temporary infant reference closed following external handover. No subsequent identity confirmation received.*

He read it again.

“No subsequent identity confirmation,” he repeated.

Marcus looked at him.

“So the hospital never received confirmation of where the child went.”

“Correct.”

Damian looked at the blank identity field.

“Then where did the child go?”

Nobody answered.

**5:15 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix received the photographs.

He enlarged the handover receipt.

The remaining letters were enough to identify the company.

Bellweather Corporate Services.

He opened the corporate registration records.

The company had provided administrative services, mail handling and temporary document management.

But it had also maintained a private client account.

The account was closed twelve years earlier.

Felix found the reference.

**BCS-PRIVATE-02**

He searched for associated documents.

A result appeared.

**CLIENT INSTRUCTION — INFANT HANDOVER**

Felix stopped breathing for a moment.

He opened the file.

The document was incomplete.

Only the first page had survived.

It stated:

*The infant is to remain under temporary custody pending confirmation of receiving authority.*

Below it was a handwritten note.

*Do not disclose the receiving authority to hospital personnel.*

The signature was familiar.

**M. VOSS**

Felix called Marcus.

“I found something.”

Marcus answered immediately.

“What?”

“Bellweather had a private client instruction concerning Infant B.”

“Read it.”

Felix did.

Marcus's expression changed.

“Send it to the legal team. Preserve the original source.”

“Already done.”

Marcus looked toward the office windows.

The investigation had finally found a document that directly connected the second infant to the same woman.

But the receiving authority was still missing.

**7:00 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria sat alone in her room.

Her anonymous contact had sent another message.

*The second child was never placed.*

She typed:

*Where did the child go?*

The reply came.

*That is what you should be asking.*

*Who was responsible?*

*You already have the answer.*

Victoria stared at the screen.

She typed:

*Mara Voss?*

The response was immediate.

*No.*

Then another message arrived.

*Ask your father who signed the original hospital instruction.*

Victoria looked toward the door.

She stood and walked downstairs.

**7:18 p.m.**

Richard was in his study.

Victoria entered without knocking.

“Who signed the original hospital instruction?”

Richard looked up.

“What?”

“The instruction concerning Infant B.”

His expression changed.

“Where did you hear that?”

“Answer me.”

Richard stood.

“I never saw an instruction concerning Infant B.”

“Then why are you frightened?”

“Victoria, leave this alone.”

“No.”

She placed her phone on the desk.

“Someone told me to ask you.”

Richard looked at the message.

His face went pale.

“Who sent this?”

Victoria took the phone back.

“Why?”

Richard sat down.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Then he opened a locked drawer and removed a small notebook.

He turned several pages.

There was a handwritten entry.

A name.

A date.

And a reference to a hospital authorisation.

Richard stared at it.

“I thought this had nothing to do with the children.”

Victoria leaned forward.

“What is it?”

Richard closed the notebook.

“Something I should have checked twelve years ago.”

**9:40 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a report.

*The Vespers have recovered the Bellweather instruction.*

She read it.

“Did they find the receiving authority?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“And Victoria has asked Richard about the original hospital instruction.”

The woman looked up.

“Already?”

“She received a message.”

The woman was silent.

Then she picked up her telephone.

“Richard has the notebook,” she said.

The person on the other end replied.

“Yes.”

“Then he must not give it to the Vespers.”

“He may refuse.”

The woman looked at the photograph on her desk.

Two hospital wristbands lay beside it.

One read:

**INFANT A**

The other:

**INFANT B**

“Then remind him,” she said, “what happened the last time he ignored my instructions.”

**11:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received an urgent message from the legal team.

The conservation specialist had completed a preliminary examination of the original handover receipt.

The correction label had been lifted without damaging the underlying paper.

The original receiving organisation was now readable.

Marcus opened the photograph.

**VESPER LIFE SCIENCES — PRIVATE MATERNITY TRANSFER**

He stared at the words.

Then he called Octavian.

“Sir, we have identified the receiving organisation for Infant B.”

Octavian answered.

“Who?”

Marcus looked at the document again.

“Vesper Life Sciences.”

Silence.

For the first time in the investigation, the missing infant's destination pointed directly to the Vesper organisation itself.
