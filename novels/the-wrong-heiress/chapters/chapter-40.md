# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 40 — The Initials on the Document**

**Tuesday — 7:10 a.m.**

Octavian Vesper had summoned the family council.

Not the extended council.

Not the corporate board.

The family.

Every member was present.

Aria sat beside Helena, holding a cup of tea. She had been informed that the meeting concerned the original allocation documents, but nobody had explained why everyone looked so serious.

Felix connected his laptop to the wall display.

“Yesterday, Aurelius confirmed that Elias Vesper did not personally sign the allocation reversal.”

Octavian nodded.

“Continue.”

“The amendment was submitted under Elias's authority. However, the person who submitted it used a separate authorisation code.”

The screen displayed three letters.

**L. VESPER.**

Lucian stared at them.

“Who had access to that code?”

“According to the old trust records, three people.”

Felix displayed the names.

- Elias Vesper.
- Octavian Vesper.
- Lucian Vesper.

Silence followed.

Cassian slowly turned toward his father.

“That's an unpleasantly short list.”

Lucian looked at Felix.

“Are you certain?”

“I'm certain about the archived access records. I cannot yet prove who physically submitted the amendment.”

Maximilian folded his arms.

“Then we need the original submission.”

“We've requested it,” Marcus said. “Aurelius has not released it.”

Octavian looked at the initials again.

“L. Vesper.”

Aria raised her hand slightly.

“Can I ask something?”

Everyone turned.

“Why does the document say L when none of those three names starts with L?”

Felix blinked.

Then he looked at the screen.

For several seconds, he said nothing.

Cassian leaned forward.

“That is an excellent question.”

Felix began typing.

“I assumed it was an internal designation.”

“An assumption,” Maximilian said.

“Yes. An assumption I should have checked.”

He opened the trust's original authority register.

A fourth entry appeared.

**LEGACY AUTHORITY — L. VESPER.**

The room became very quiet.

**8:05 a.m.**

Alistair Monroe arrived carrying a large archival box.

“I found this in the private historical collection.”

Octavian looked up.

“What is it?”

“Elias's correspondence concerning the legacy authority.”

He placed the box on the table.

Inside were old letters, council minutes, and a sealed document.

Octavian recognised the seal.

“Open it.”

Alistair carefully broke the seal.

The document was dated twenty-four years earlier.

It described a special authority established by the Vesper family council.

The authority could be activated only when a succession dispute involved more than one direct descendant.

Its purpose was to protect the inheritance until the identities and eligibility of the descendants had been verified.

At the bottom were two signatures.

**Elias Vesper.**

**Octavian Vesper.**

Lucian read the final paragraph.

“The authority could only be exercised by the designated custodian.”

“Who was the custodian?” Damian asked.

Alistair turned the page.

The name was printed clearly.

**Lady Isolde Vesper.**

Everyone looked toward the grandmother.

Isolde had been silent throughout the meeting.

She set down her teacup.

“Show me the document.”

**8:20 a.m.**

Isolde read the document.

Her expression did not change.

Octavian watched her.

“Do you remember this?”

“Yes.”

“Did you activate the authority?”

“No.”

“Did you submit the allocation reversal?”

“No.”

Lucian leaned forward.

“Mother, the records show your authority code was used.”

Isolde looked at him.

“Then someone used it without my permission.”

Felix checked the access register.

“The authority was accessed from a physical terminal. The system didn't require a second authentication because the custodian's original credentials were still valid.”

“Where was the terminal?” Maximilian asked.

“Old Vesper administrative residence. The building was decommissioned seventeen years ago.”

Damian frowned.

“Who had access?”

“According to the records, the family custodian and the chief administrative secretary.”

Marcus looked at Octavian.

“Who was the secretary?”

Alistair answered.

“Margaret Ellison.”

Octavian's eyes narrowed.

“She died.”

“Sixteen years ago,” Alistair confirmed.

Cassian leaned back.

“So our suspect is either a dead secretary or someone who borrowed her credentials.”

“Neither possibility is particularly reassuring,” Damian said.

Aria looked at the document.

“Could the authority have been used before the babies were born?”

Felix checked the date.

“Yes. The amendment was submitted three months before the births.”

“Then the person didn't need to know what happened afterward.”

“Correct.”

Aria nodded.

“That's important.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because everyone keeps treating the person who changed the allocation as if they must have known how the whole thing would end. Maybe they only knew what they wanted to happen at the beginning.”

Nobody answered.

It was a reasonable distinction.

And it changed the investigation.

**10:50 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria received a call from Marcus.

He explained the new discovery.

When he mentioned Isolde's credentials, Victoria went quiet.

“Are they accusing her?”

“No. Lady Isolde has denied authorising the amendment. The credentials may have been misused.”

“Can you prove that?”

“Not yet.”

“Then don't let anyone accuse her without evidence.”

Marcus paused.

“I'll make sure the investigation remains factual.”

Victoria ended the call.

Richard was standing near the window.

“You're involving yourself in Vesper family affairs again.”

Victoria turned.

“I'm involved in the matter because I'm one of the children.”

“You are a Sterling.”

“And Aria is a Vesper. That doesn't change what happened to either of us.”

Richard looked away.

Victoria picked up her handbag.

“I'm going to see Margaret.”

“Why?”

“Because Jonathan's papers might contain something about the person who changed the instruction.”

Richard did not stop her.

**12:15 p.m. — Margaret Vale's Residence**

Margaret placed a small wooden box on the table.

“I found this after our last conversation,” she said.

Victoria opened it.

Inside were Jonathan's personal effects.

A watch.

A set of keys.

Several photographs.

And a folded sheet of paper.

Victoria unfolded it.

It was a handwritten address.

Below it were two words.

**ELLISON HOUSE.**

Margaret frowned.

“Jonathan never explained that.”

Victoria looked at the address.

“Do you know where it is?”

“I think so. It was an old administrative residence used by the Vespers.”

Victoria immediately called Marcus.

**2:00 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Felix confirmed the address.

Ellison House was the same building that had housed the old administrative terminal.

Damian organised a security team.

Octavian insisted on going personally.

Isolde stopped him.

“You are not going to enter an abandoned building without knowing what is inside.”

“I am aware of the risks.”

“You are also eighty-two.”

“I have not forgotten.”

“Good. Then remember it.”

Cassian looked at Maximilian.

“I believe Grandmother has won.”

“She usually does,” Maximilian replied.

The investigation team arrived at Ellison House shortly afterward.

The building had been sealed for years.

Dust covered the entrance hall.

The old terminal room was locked.

Felix examined the access panel.

“Someone opened this recently.”

Damian immediately signalled the security team.

They entered.

The room was empty.

The terminal had been removed.

But a small filing cabinet remained.

Inside was an envelope.

It bore the Vesper family seal.

Octavian opened it.

The contents consisted of one sheet.

A handwritten instruction:

*The original allocation must be reversed. The Vesper child must enter the Sterling household. The Sterling child must remain under temporary custody until the second placement is confirmed.*

No signature.

Only the date and a reference number.

**LA-01.**

Felix photographed it.

“That's the authority reference.”

Octavian stared at the paper.

“Someone planned the entire arrangement.”

Aria looked at the final sentence.

“Not the entire arrangement.”

Octavian turned.

“The second placement was supposed to be confirmed. It never was.”

She pointed to the words.

“Whoever wrote this expected both children to end up with their intended families eventually.”

Lucian looked at the document.

“And someone stopped that from happening.”

“Jonathan,” Damian said.

“Possibly,” Marcus replied. “But we still don't know who issued the later instructions.”

**4:45 p.m.**

Felix found another record hidden behind the filing cabinet.

It was a maintenance log.

The last entry was dated six years earlier.

**Terminal removed. Storage location: Private Archive 04.**

“Where is Private Archive 04?” Maximilian asked.

Felix checked the building registry.

“Vesper Tower basement.”

Marcus looked at him.

“That's here?”

“Yes.”

They returned to the tower.

The archive was located behind the old records department.

Its door had been sealed during a building renovation.

The security team opened it.

Inside were several cabinets.

Most contained obsolete administrative files.

One cabinet had a single label.

**LEGACY AUTHORITY — RESTRICTED.**

Felix opened it.

There was no terminal.

No computer.

Only a ledger.

He opened the first page.

The ledger contained access records for the legacy authority.

The final entry was dated three months before the births.

**Accessed by: M. ELLISON.**

Felix looked up.

“Margaret Ellison.”

Octavian examined the entry.

“Impossible. She was not authorised to use the custodian's credentials.”

“Unless someone gave them to her,” Marcus said.

Isolde's expression hardened.

“Margaret was my secretary for eleven years. She would not have acted without instruction.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Are you certain?”

“Yes.”

Isolde closed the ledger.

“Someone instructed her.”

**8:30 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The Aurelius executive received confirmation that the Vespers had recovered the Ellison House instruction.

He opened the original Schedule B.

The amendment was attached to a second document.

This one contained the actual authorisation.

The signature was not Isolde's.

It was Margaret Ellison's.

But the signature alone was not the most important detail.

Beneath it was a handwritten statement:

*Submitted under the direct instruction of the family custodian.*

The executive read the statement twice.

Then he called his contact.

“They have found Ellison's name.”

“Did they find the authorisation?”

“Yes.”

“Then they will come looking for the person who instructed her.”

The executive hesitated.

“Should I tell them?”

The voice answered immediately.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because the person who gave that instruction is still part of the Vesper family.”

The call ended.

**11:15 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian stood alone in the library.

Isolde entered.

“You should rest.”

He did not turn.

“Did you instruct Margaret?”

“No.”

“Did you know she accessed the authority?”

“No.”

Octavian looked at her.

“Then someone used your name, your credentials, and your secretary.”

Isolde walked to the desk.

“Someone wanted the arrangement to appear as though it came from me.”

“Why?”

She looked at the old photograph of Elias.

“Because my authority was the only one that could change the allocation without attracting immediate suspicion.”

Octavian's voice lowered.

“Who knew that?”

Isolde remained silent for a moment.

Then she said:

“Three people.”

“Who?”

“Elias. Margaret. And the person who created the authority.”

Octavian frowned.

“Who created it?”

Isolde looked directly at him.

“You did.”

For the first time that evening, Octavian had no answer.

Isolde continued.

“And you did not create it alone.”

She placed a second document on the desk.

It was the original founding record of the legacy authority.

Two signatures appeared at the bottom.

**OCTAVIAN VESPER.**

**LUCIAN VESPER.**

Octavian stared at his son's signature.

Lucian had been twenty-six years old when the authority was created.

And he had never mentioned it.
