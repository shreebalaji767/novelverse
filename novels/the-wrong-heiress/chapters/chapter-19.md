# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 19 — The Document Richard Sterling Hid**

**Sunday — 7:30 a.m.**

Richard Sterling had not slept.

The envelope lay on his desk.

He had taken it out of the drawer three times during the night and put it back twice.

The document inside was brief.

The second transfer was not completed.

He had known that sentence for twelve years.

What he had never known was what happened afterward.

His telephone rang.

This time, he answered.

“Hello?”

A man's voice came through.

“Mr. Sterling. We need to discuss the old arrangement.”

Richard stood.

“Who is this?”

“You know who.”

“Where have you been?”

“Keeping away from people who ask questions.”

Richard glanced at the envelope.

“The Vespers have found the records.”

There was silence.

Then the man said, “How much?”

“Enough.”

“Have they found the confirmation?”

“Not yet.”

“Then keep it that way.”

Richard's grip tightened around the telephone.

“You told me the matter was finished.”

“I told you the first stage was finished.”

“You said both families would be protected.”

“I said the arrangement would be completed.”

Richard's voice dropped.

“What happened to the second transfer?”

The man ended the call.

Richard stared at the telephone.

Then he closed the envelope and locked it inside the drawer.

**9:45 a.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria arrived for Sunday lunch.

She found Helena in the sitting room with Isolde.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning, darling,” Helena said.

Isolde smiled.

“Did you have a pleasant week?”

“Mostly. My client at work has an interesting relationship with paperwork.”

“Interesting?”

“They keep sending corrections that create more errors.”

“People can be remarkably talented at making simple things complicated.”

Aria nodded.

“That's exactly what I thought.”

Cassian entered.

“Are we discussing the accounting profession?”

“Unfortunately,” Aria replied.

“Excellent. I have a meeting tomorrow that could benefit from your expertise.”

“Does it involve invoices?”

“Worse. People.”

Aria sighed.

“Then I charge extra.”

Cassian laughed.

**10:30 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received a message from the investigator assigned to Richard Sterling's old financial records.

The message contained a photograph of an envelope.

The label was clearly visible.

**CA-17 — FINAL CONFIRMATION**

Marcus immediately called Octavian.

“Mr. Vesper, we've located a document that may be in Richard Sterling's possession.”

“Where?”

“His private office. The investigator obtained a photograph from an old inventory record. The envelope was listed among documents retained personally by Mr. Sterling.”

“Does the inventory describe its contents?”

“Yes. One sentence: 'Second transfer not completed.'”

Octavian stood.

“Arrange a formal request for access to the original.”

Marcus hesitated.

“Mr. Sterling may refuse.”

“Then we will ask him directly.”

**11:12 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard was informed that the Vespers wanted a meeting.

He knew why.

He looked at the locked drawer.

Then he called his eldest son.

“Adrian. I need you at the house.”

“Is something wrong?”

“Yes.”

“What happened?”

“The Vespers have found the old records.”

Adrian was silent.

“Which records?”

“The hospital arrangement.”

“Father…”

“Come home.”

Richard ended the call.

He opened the drawer again.

The envelope remained where he had placed it.

For a moment, he considered destroying it.

Then he stopped.

If he destroyed it now, the Vespers would know exactly what it contained.

And he would have no explanation left.

**12:05 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Lunch was served.

Aria noticed that Octavian seemed distracted.

Not unusually stern.

Not angry.

Just occupied.

“Is something wrong?” she asked.

Octavian looked at her.

“There is a development in the investigation.”

“Something important?”

“Yes.”

“Does it concern the hospital?”

“Yes.”

Aria set down her glass.

“What happened?”

“We believe we have located a document retained by Richard Sterling. It may explain why the second transfer was never completed.”

Aria was quiet.

“Does he know you found it?”

“He has been informed that we wish to speak with him.”

“Will he come?”

“We don't know.”

Aria looked at Helena.

“Do you think he knows more than he told me?”

Helena didn't answer immediately.

“I think there are questions he hasn't answered.”

Aria nodded.

“Then ask him.”

Octavian looked at her.

“We will.”

She returned to her meal.

She had no intention of defending Richard.

But she also had no intention of deciding what he had done before hearing the truth.

**1:40 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Adrian arrived.

Richard showed him the envelope.

Adrian read the document.

His expression changed.

“You kept this?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I didn't know what else to do with it.”

Adrian looked at his father.

“Did you know the second transfer hadn't been completed?”

Richard nodded.

“Then why did you never tell us?”

“I believed the matter would be resolved.”

“When?”

Richard said nothing.

Adrian put the document down.

“Father, what exactly did you agree to?”

Richard rubbed his forehead.

“I authorised a confidential administrative arrangement. I was told it concerned the protection of family records.”

“By whom?”

“A representative.”

“Who?”

Richard hesitated.

“I don't remember his full name.”

Adrian stared at him.

“You remember the arrangement, the payments and the document. But not the person?”

“I remember the name he used.”

“Which was?”

Richard looked at the envelope.

“Jonathan Vale.”

Adrian went still.

“The courier?”

Richard nodded.

**2:05 p.m.**

Adrian sat down.

“Why would a courier arrange a confidential administrative account?”

“He wasn't the only person involved.”

“Who else?”

“A woman.”

Richard looked at the document.

“She called herself Mara Voss.”

Adrian stared at him.

The same name appearing in the Vesper investigation.

“Did you meet her?”

“Once.”

“What did she tell you?”

“That the hospital records had been separated for administrative reasons. She said the families would receive confirmation once the transfer was complete.”

“Which families?”

Richard hesitated.

“Both.”

Adrian's expression hardened.

“Did you know children had been switched?”

“No.”

“Did you know Aria was involved?”

“No.”

“Did you know Victoria was involved?”

Richard shook his head.

“I was told the records were being handled separately. I didn't know what was inside the second package.”

Adrian stood.

“Then you need to tell the Vespers everything.”

Richard looked at him.

“I know.”

**3:30 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Richard arrived with Adrian.

Marcus greeted them and escorted them to Octavian's office.

Octavian stood.

“Mr. Sterling.”

“Mr. Vesper.”

They shook hands.

Richard placed the envelope on the table.

“I believe this belongs to your investigation.”

Marcus opened it.

Inside was the original confirmation.

He read the sentence.

*The second transfer was not completed.*

Octavian examined the paper.

“Why did you retain this?”

Richard looked uncomfortable.

“Because I was told the matter would be resolved.”

“By whom?”

“A woman calling herself Mara Voss.”

“Did you know her real identity?”

“No.”

“Did you know what the second transfer involved?”

“No.”

“Then why did you authorise the payment?”

Richard looked down.

“She said it was necessary to complete the administrative arrangement.”

“Did she explain the arrangement?”

“She told me the records of two families had to remain separate until the process was finished.”

Octavian's expression remained cold.

“What process?”

“I don't know.”

Damian leaned forward.

“You paid someone without knowing what they were doing?”

Richard looked at him.

“I was told the arrangement had been reviewed by legal advisers.”

“Which advisers?”

Richard didn't answer.

**4:05 p.m.**

Octavian turned to Marcus.

“Record his statement.”

Marcus began taking notes.

Richard continued.

“The first payment was made before the births. The second was made after. I was told both were part of the same administrative process.”

“Did you know about the hospital access credentials?” Marcus asked.

“No.”

“Daniel Mercer?”

“No.”

“Miriam Shaw?”

“I don't remember that name.”

“Northbridge Data Services?”

“I remember the company name. I don't remember what work it performed.”

“Did you ever receive a copy of the original transfer instructions?”

Richard shook his head.

“No. Only the confirmation.”

Octavian studied him.

“Why did you not disclose this when Aria's identity was confirmed?”

Richard looked up.

“I was afraid.”

“Of what?”

“Of what the documents might reveal.”

Octavian's voice hardened.

“You were afraid of the truth?”

Richard lowered his eyes.

“I was afraid of what I might have been involved in.”

The room fell silent.

**4:42 p.m.**

Marcus placed the document beside the recovered instruction sheet.

The dates matched.

The payment had been issued.

The acknowledgement had been retained.

But the second transfer had not been completed.

“Mr. Sterling,” Marcus said, “did Mara Voss ever contact you again?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Several weeks after the births.”

“What did she say?”

“She told me the second stage was delayed.”

“Did she explain why?”

“No.”

“Did she request another payment?”

Richard nodded.

“Did you pay it?”

“No. I refused.”

“Why?”

Richard looked at the document.

“Because I had begun to suspect something was wrong.”

Octavian leaned forward.

“What happened after you refused?”

“She said the arrangement would be completed without my assistance.”

“Did she threaten you?”

“No. She simply stopped contacting me.”

Marcus made a note.

“Did she ever tell you where the second transfer was supposed to go?”

Richard shook his head.

“No.”

Octavian looked at him for a long moment.

“Then we have only part of the truth.”

Richard nodded.

“I know.”

**6:20 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria was preparing to leave when Marcus approached.

“Miss Aria.”

“Yes?”

“We have received a statement from Richard Sterling.”

She looked at him.

“And?”

“He admits authorising payments to an intermediary connected to Mara Voss. He says he did not know the purpose of the hospital records transfer.”

Aria was silent.

“Does he know who arranged the switch?”

“Not according to his statement.”

“Do you believe him?”

Marcus chose his words carefully.

“We have not independently verified every part of his account.”

“That's not what I asked.”

Marcus paused.

“I believe he knows more than he originally admitted. Whether he knows the complete truth remains unclear.”

Aria nodded.

“That sounds fair.”

She adjusted her handbag.

“Thank you for telling me.”

Marcus escorted her to the entrance.

She left without asking for a car.

**8:55 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received a message.

*Richard Sterling has handed over the final confirmation.*

She read it.

Then she stood.

“Did he tell them about me?”

“He identified Mara Voss.”

The woman smiled faintly.

“Good.”

The associate looked confused.

“Good?”

“He has finally told them something useful.”

“What about the second transfer?”

She turned toward the cabinet.

“The second transfer was never completed.”

“Then why did you tell Richard it would be?”

The woman looked at him.

“Because he was never supposed to know what the second transfer actually meant.”

She opened the cabinet.

The two newborn photographs remained inside.

Beside them was a third envelope.

Its label read:

**FINAL DESTINATION — UNCONFIRMED**

She removed it.

Inside was a photograph of a hospital corridor.

A woman stood near the maternity ward entrance.

Her face was clearly visible.

The woman stared at the photograph.

Then she placed it back.

“Find Daniel Mercer,” she said.

The associate nodded.

“And this time, don't lose him.”

**10:15 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus received a late message from Felix.

*I found an old Northbridge archive entry mentioning Daniel Mercer.*

Marcus opened the attachment.

It was a personnel record.

Mercer had been marked as having left the company twelve years earlier.

But beneath the departure date was a handwritten note.

*Employee reassigned under private instruction.*

Marcus read it again.

Reassigned.

Not dismissed.

Not resigned.

Reassigned.

He called Octavian.

“Mr. Vesper, we have a new development.”

Octavian answered immediately.

“What is it?”

“Daniel Mercer may not have disappeared after leaving the hospital records project. Northbridge's own personnel file indicates he was reassigned.”

A pause.

“To where?”

“The destination field is blank.”

Octavian looked toward the darkened windows.

“Find it.”

Marcus ended the call.

For twelve years, Daniel Mercer had been treated as a missing administrator.

Now there was evidence that someone had deliberately moved him.

And whoever had done it might still know where he was.
