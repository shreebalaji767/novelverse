# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 12 — Three Brothers, One Lunch, and a Very Suspicious Address**

**Sunday — 10:42 a.m.**

Aria had learned three things about visiting the Vesper Estate.

First, the gates were unnecessarily impressive.

Second, the people inside were considerably less frightening than the gates suggested.

Third, never ask how many people were coming to lunch unless prepared to receive an answer involving a family tree.

She stepped out of the taxi, adjusted the strap of her handbag and paid the driver.

Marcus was waiting near the entrance.

“Good morning, Miss Aria.”

“Morning. Is everyone inside?”

“Yes.”

“Everyone everyone?”

Marcus paused.

“Your immediate family.”

“That sounds like a carefully chosen phrase.”

“It is.”

Aria narrowed her eyes.

“Are there any unexpected guests?”

“No.”

“Press?”

“No.”

“Business executives pretending to be relatives?”

“Not today.”

“Excellent. I can work with that.”

Marcus opened the door.

**11:03 a.m. — Vesper Estate**

Helena greeted Aria in the entrance hall and hugged her after Aria opened her arms.

“You came,” Helena said.

“I said I would.”

“I know. I still like seeing you arrive.”

Aria smiled.

Isolde appeared behind her, elegant as ever.

“Good morning, Grandmother.”

“Good morning, darling.”

Aria glanced toward the sitting room.

“Where is everyone?”

“Waiting.”

“That sounds ominous.”

“It is only lunch.”

“People say that before all sorts of things.”

Isolde laughed and led her inside.

Three men stood near the windows.

Aria recognised Cassian immediately.

The other two turned toward her.

Maximilian Vesper stood straight-backed in a dark suit, his expression controlled and observant. Damian was dressed more casually, though his posture suggested he had never once forgotten where an exit was. The youngest, Felix, was holding a tablet and appeared to be debating whether approaching a person required a specific protocol.

Lucian stepped forward.

“Aria, these are your brothers.”

Maximilian approached first.

“I'm Maximilian. It's good to finally meet you.”

“Nice to meet you.”

He offered his hand. She shook it.

“I hope the family hasn't made this uncomfortable.”

“Not particularly.”

His expression softened slightly.

“Good.”

Damian followed.

“Damian.”

“Aria.”

He shook her hand, then seemed to consider saying something else.

“I'm glad you're here.”

It was simple, and sincere.

“Thank you.”

Felix was last.

He looked at her, then at the tablet, then back at her.

“Hello.”

“Hello.”

“I prepared a list of things you might need.”

“Things I might need?”

“Household equipment. Technology. Security arrangements. Transportation options. Financial services. Medical contacts. Emergency contacts.”

Aria blinked.

“That's quite a list.”

“I removed the unnecessary items.”

Cassian leaned against the window.

“He originally had a spreadsheet with seven tabs.”

“Eight,” Felix corrected.

“Eight?”

“The last one was optional.”

Aria looked at Felix.

“What was on the optional tab?”

“Emergency replacement refrigerators.”

There was a brief silence.

Aria nodded thoughtfully.

“Good to know someone is planning ahead.”

Felix looked delighted.

Maximilian closed his eyes for a moment.

Cassian laughed.

**12:16 p.m. — Lunch**

The dining room was considerably less intimidating than the first time Aria had visited.

There were fewer chairs, fewer staff members hovering around the table, and no visible evidence of anyone having prepared a military operation.

Aria sat beside Helena.

Octavian occupied the head of the table, with Isolde beside him. Lucian sat opposite Helena. The brothers filled the remaining places.

Aria looked around.

“Much better.”

Octavian raised an eyebrow.

“Better than what?”

“Last time, I thought you were expecting a visiting delegation.”

“I was expecting my granddaughter.”

“You had enough chairs for a visiting delegation.”

“An understandable precaution.”

Isolde gave him a disapproving look.

“Octavian.”

He quietly returned to his soup.

Lunch began.

Maximilian asked about her work.

“How is the accounting firm?”

“Good. Busy, but manageable.”

“What sort of work are they giving you?”

“Invoice entries, payment records, reconciliations. Mostly routine accounts work.”

“Are you comfortable with it?”

“Yes. I'm still learning their system, but the people are helpful.”

Maximilian nodded.

“If you ever need—”

“I'm sure I can ask.”

He stopped.

“Of course.”

Aria appreciated that he hadn't finished the sentence with an offer to purchase the company.

Damian asked, “How are you getting to work?”

“Taxi. Sometimes public transport.”

“Do you travel alone?”

“Usually.”

His expression tightened.

Aria noticed.

“I'm not asking for a security team.”

“I wasn't offering one.”

“You were thinking about it.”

Damian glanced at Cassian.

Cassian lifted his glass.

“I am not involved in this conversation.”

“You are always involved,” Damian replied.

“It's a gift.”

Aria smiled.

“I'm fine with my current arrangements. If I need help, I'll ask.”

Damian considered her answer.

“All right.”

No argument followed.

Aria quietly approved.

Felix had been unusually silent for several minutes.

Finally, he turned his tablet toward her.

“I have a question.”

“Go ahead.”

“Do you prefer laptops with larger displays or lighter models?”

Aria looked at the screen.

It contained several laptop specifications.

“Why?”

“I thought you might need one.”

“I already have a laptop.”

Felix frowned.

“Is it adequate?”

“It's working.”

“That wasn't my question.”

“It's adequate.”

He seemed to accept this, though not happily.

“Understood.”

A moment later, he asked, “Do you use external storage?”

“Sometimes.”

“What capacity?”

“Felix,” Maximilian said.

“I am asking a normal question.”

“You are interrogating her about storage devices.”

“Storage is important.”

Aria took another bite of lunch.

“I'll let you know if my laptop starts asking for retirement.”

Felix stared at her.

Then he laughed.

It was a small, surprised sound, as though he hadn't expected himself to make it.

Helena watched him with a smile.

**1:08 p.m. — After Lunch**

Coffee and tea were served in the sitting room.

Aria sat on the sofa with Helena and Isolde. The brothers settled nearby.

For a while, the conversation remained ordinary.

Cassian complained about a meeting that had lasted three hours.

Maximilian corrected his description of the meeting.

Damian said neither of them had been forced to attend.

Felix quietly informed everyone that the meeting could have been replaced by a six-page report.

“Six pages?” Cassian asked. “You are becoming sentimental.”

“Four pages if the participants read quickly.”

Aria looked at Helena.

“Is this how every family conversation goes?”

“Usually.”

“I'm beginning to understand why you invited me for lunch. You needed another person.”

Helena laughed.

Octavian watched Aria over his cup.

“Are you comfortable coming here?”

“Yes.”

“Then come whenever you wish. Not because anyone expects you to.”

Aria looked at him.

“I appreciate that.”

He nodded.

There was no speech about bloodlines, duties or inheritance.

For once, the subject was allowed to remain untouched.

**2:14 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

While Aria was having lunch, Marcus received an update from the investigation team.

The former owner of the demolished commercial building had been located.

His name was **Edmund Hale**.

He was seventy-four, retired, and living in a quiet residential district. His property records confirmed that he had owned the building listed in Thomas Reed's notebook.

More importantly, archived lease documents showed that a small office on the building's second floor had been rented to a company called **Greybridge Records & Dispatch**.

The lease began eleven days before the hospital births.

It ended four months later.

Greybridge had no listed employees, no permanent telephone number and no surviving business registration beyond a short-lived trading certificate.

Northbridge Data Services had paid the first three months of rent.

Marcus read the documents twice.

“Do we have evidence Greybridge received the package?”

“Not yet,” the investigator replied. “The lease establishes a connection to the address. It does not establish what happened inside the office.”

“Any delivery records?”

“Not in the surviving building files. We have requested the courier company's archived manifests.”

Marcus studied the old address.

“Has Mr. Hale agreed to speak with us?”

“He has. He requested that we arrange a formal appointment.”

“Arrange one. No unannounced visit.”

The investigator nodded.

Marcus forwarded the update to Octavian.

Then he added one sentence:

*The connection is credible, but the purpose of the office remains unverified.*

He had learned that the distinction mattered.

**3:02 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria placed two photocopies on Richard's desk.

“Westbridge Administrative Services,” she said. “And Greybridge Records & Dispatch.”

Richard looked up sharply.

“Where did you get that second name?”

“From the documents I found.”

“Which documents?”

“An old transfer summary. A delivery reference. Some correspondence.”

Richard stood.

“Victoria, where did you get these?”

“From the family records.”

“Those records were not yours to remove.”

“They were sitting in a cabinet.”

“That doesn't make them yours.”

Victoria ignored the rebuke.

“Why did our family have documents referring to Westbridge?”

Richard said nothing.

“And why does the name Greybridge appear beside a hospital delivery?”

His expression hardened.

“You have no idea what you're looking at.”

“Then explain it.”

“I told you to stop.”

“You told me to stop because you don't want me to know something.”

Richard walked around the desk.

“You are making assumptions about documents you don't understand.”

“Then help me understand them.”

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Richard finally picked up the photocopy.

“Where is the original?”

“Back where I found it.”

“Put it back exactly where it was.”

Victoria folded her arms.

“You're not denying anything.”

“I'm telling you to leave this alone.”

She gathered the photocopies.

“Fine.”

Richard watched her leave.

He did not believe her.

**4:27 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Aria was preparing to leave.

Helena walked her to the entrance.

“Will you come next Sunday?”

“I'm working during the week, but Sunday should be fine.”

“Good.”

“Just don't invite anyone else.”

Helena smiled.

“I won't.”

Behind them, Felix appeared with his tablet.

“Aria?”

She turned.

“Yes?”

“I didn't send you anything.”

“That's good.”

“I wanted to make that clear.”

“Why?”

“Because I had prepared a package.”

Maximilian appeared behind him.

“Which I stopped.”

Felix looked wounded.

“It was useful.”

“It contained three laptops.”

“One was a backup.”

“And a backup for the backup,” Cassian added, arriving with a cup of coffee.

Aria stared at Felix.

“Do you have a refrigerator backup too?”

Felix hesitated.

“Not for you.”

“Comforting.”

She smiled and stepped outside.

Damian stood near the entrance.

“Your taxi is here.”

“Thank you.”

He didn't offer an escort.

She noticed that, too.

“See you next Sunday,” she said.

Damian nodded.

“See you.”

Aria got into the taxi.

As the car pulled away, Helena remained at the entrance, watching until it turned beyond the gates.

“She seems comfortable,” Lucian said quietly.

“She is,” Helena replied. “When we let her be.”

Octavian joined them.

“Then we continue doing that.”

**8:41 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The appointment with Edmund Hale was confirmed for Tuesday morning.

The investigation team had also recovered an old municipal directory entry for Greybridge Records & Dispatch.

It listed a single contact name.

**M. Vale.**

Marcus read it.

He checked the date.

Then he checked the source document again.

The name appeared only once, in a handwritten entry beside the company's temporary office registration.

He immediately sent it to the investigation team.

“Find out who M. Vale was. Verify the identity before making any assumptions.”

He looked at the photograph of the old building.

A company had appeared eleven days before the births, rented an office, received money from Northbridge, and disappeared four months later.

Someone had arranged the office.

Someone had arranged the delivery.

And someone had made sure the hospital records travelled through several different hands.

Marcus closed the file.

For the first time, the investigation had reached a place where the people involved might have left something behind.

Not just a name.

Not just a payment.

A person who had actually seen the operation.

And on Tuesday, they would finally ask him what he remembered.
