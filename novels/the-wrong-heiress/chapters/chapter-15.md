# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 15 — The Courier Who Left Two Receipts**

**Thursday — 8:35 a.m.**

Aria was beginning to suspect that Peter Collins had a personal disagreement with lunch breaks.

Every morning, he arrived with a fresh stack of documents. Every afternoon, he discovered another reason why someone needed something checked before the day ended.

Today, the problem was a client who had submitted a handwritten expense ledger.

Aria studied the page.

Then she looked at Nina.

“Do you understand this?”

Nina leaned over.

“I understand that someone has written numbers.”

“That's encouraging.”

“I'm not sure they're the correct numbers.”

“Less encouraging.”

They spent the next twenty minutes comparing the handwritten entries with the bank statements.

Eventually, Aria found the issue.

“Here.”

Nina leaned closer.

“What's that?”

“The same payment was recorded twice. But the second entry has a different date and a slightly different amount.”

“Could be two payments.”

“It could. But the bank statement shows only one transaction.”

Nina sighed.

“Why do people make accounts so difficult?”

“Perhaps they get bored when everything matches.”

Nina laughed.

Peter appeared at the doorway.

“Are we solving the client's problems?”

“Trying to,” Aria replied.

“Good. Please continue.”

He disappeared.

Nina watched him go.

“He drinks tea like it's a medical requirement.”

“Maybe it is.”

**9:20 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Jonathan Vale's death certificate had been verified.

He had died eleven years earlier in a road accident. His employment history showed intermittent work as a private courier, including several assignments for Northbridge Data Services.

Marcus stood before Octavian's desk.

“Vale was registered as a collection representative for Northstar Document Custody. He also worked for Northbridge.”

“Did he deliver the hospital package?”

“We don't know.”

“Can we establish his assignments?”

“We're looking for his surviving records.”

Damian looked at the report.

“Who was his next of kin?”

“A sister. Her name is Margaret Vale. She lives outside the city.”

“Has anyone contacted her?”

“Not yet. We wanted to verify the relationship first.”

Octavian nodded.

“Approach her respectfully. Explain why we're asking. We have no reason to frighten a family member who may know nothing about this.”

Maximilian looked at the employment record.

“Why would Northbridge use two couriers for the same chain?”

“Possibly separate collection and delivery arrangements,” Marcus replied. “But that's speculation.”

Cassian leaned back.

“Or they wanted to keep the people handling the documents from knowing the entire route.”

Nobody answered immediately.

Octavian closed the file.

“Find out what Vale actually did. We will not build conclusions on guesses.”

**11:45 a.m. — Vesper Tower, Records Division**

Felix had found something.

It was not a photograph, a bank transfer or a secret recording.

It was a spreadsheet.

A very old spreadsheet.

The document had been recovered from a backup drive belonging to Northbridge Data Services. Most of its contents were routine delivery entries, but one line had survived in a partially damaged form.

ARCHIVED DELIVERY ENTRY

**Job reference:** NB-4417

**Collection point:** Greybridge Records & Dispatch

**Collector:** J. Vale

**Destination:** Special Custody — Route B

**Status:** Completed

**Additional instruction:** Separate acknowledgement required.

Marcus read it carefully.

“Route B?”

Felix nodded.

“There was also a Route A entry.”

He opened the next record.

**Job reference:** NB-4416

**Collection point:** Central Women's Hospital

**Collector:** T. Reed

**Destination:** Northstar Document Custody

**Status:** Completed

**Additional instruction:** Personal delivery.

Marcus looked at the two entries.

“Two deliveries.”

“Two consecutive job references,” Felix said. “One from the hospital, one from Greybridge.”

“Same day?”

“Yes.”

“Can we establish the time?”

“Not from this spreadsheet. The timestamp fields are damaged.”

Marcus studied the entries again.

“Do we know what Route B contained?”

“No.”

“Then we don't know whether Vale carried the hospital records.”

“Correct.”

Marcus sent the records to Octavian.

A few seconds later, Octavian's reply arrived.

*Preserve both entries. Locate the original job sheets.*

**1:18 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria had spent the morning searching the old family archive.

She had found another reference to Crestline Administrative Partners.

This time, it was in a document signed by Richard.

The payment had been approved by the Sterling family office.

The description was vague.

*Administrative reconciliation and confidential records handling.*

Victoria read the phrase several times.

Confidential records handling.

She took a photograph and sent it to the unknown number.

*This is what I found. What does it mean?*

The reply came shortly afterward.

*It means your father authorised a payment connected to the records.*

*Did he arrange the switch?*

No answer.

Victoria waited.

Then:

*You are asking the wrong question.*

She frowned.

*What question should I ask?*

*Why were two separate deliveries made?*

Victoria sat very still.

Two deliveries.

The hospital records.

The second package.

She typed:

*What was in the second delivery?*

The reply came after a pause.

*Find out.*

Victoria stared at the screen.

She was beginning to understand that the person messaging her knew more than they were willing to say.

That irritated her more than anything else.

**3:05 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Margaret Vale agreed to meet the Vesper investigators.

She was in her late sixties and worked part-time at a local community centre.

Marcus met her with Damian.

Margaret listened quietly as Marcus explained the reason for their visit.

“My brother was a courier,” she said. “He delivered all sorts of things. Documents, parcels, legal papers. Sometimes he complained about clients who expected him to work at midnight.”

“Did he ever mention Northbridge Data Services?” Marcus asked.

“Yes. They were one of his regular clients.”

“Did he keep work records?”

“He kept everything.”

Damian looked up.

“Everything?”

Margaret gave a small smile.

“He was convinced that one day someone would accuse him of losing something. He had receipts for deliveries from twenty years ago.”

“Do you still have them?”

“Some. His daughter took most of his personal papers after he died. I kept a small box of things he left at my house.”

Marcus leaned forward.

“Would you be willing to let us examine them?”

Margaret considered the request.

“I don't want to hand over originals.”

“We can make copies in your presence.”

“All right.”

She paused.

“But I want to know what this is about.”

Marcus did not hide the basic facts.

“Several years ago, a hospital's records were transferred through a series of companies. We believe your brother may have handled one of the deliveries.”

Margaret's expression changed.

“Hospital records?”

“Yes.”

“Did he do something wrong?”

“We have no evidence of that.”

She looked at him for a moment.

Then she nodded.

“I'll bring the box tomorrow.”

**5:30 p.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria had just finished dinner when Helena called.

“Hello, Grandmother.”

“Good evening, darling. Are you busy?”

“Not really. I'm home.”

“Would you like to come for lunch on Sunday?”

Aria smiled.

“Is this the same lunch we discussed last week?”

“Yes.”

“Just family?”

“Just family.”

“Then yes.”

Helena sounded pleased.

“Good. Felix has promised not to prepare another equipment package.”

“I'm starting to worry about what he's planning instead.”

Helena laughed.

“He's been trying to decide whether to show you his research laboratory.”

Aria paused.

“Is it safe?”

“Perfectly.”

“Then perhaps another time. I would like to know what he actually works on, but I don't want a three-hour presentation.”

“I'll tell him.”

After the call, Aria put her phone down.

She had a feeling that the next Sunday lunch would involve at least one technological demonstration.

She decided not to worry about it until Sunday.

**Friday — 10:12 a.m.**

Margaret arrived at Vesper Tower carrying a small cardboard box.

She placed it on the table.

“That's everything I found.”

Marcus thanked her.

The box contained old delivery receipts, folded invoices, a few photographs and several handwritten notes.

Damian carefully sorted the documents.

Then he stopped.

“Marcus.”

A receipt lay between two invoices.

It was dated twelve years earlier.

The reference number matched the Northbridge spreadsheet.

**NB-4417.**

The collection point was Greybridge Records & Dispatch.

The collector's signature was unmistakable.

Jonathan Vale.

But the destination field had been completed by hand.

It read:

**Northstar Document Custody — Special Collection.**

Marcus examined the reverse side.

There was another signature.

A different name.

**M. Voss.**

Margaret looked at the paper.

“I remember that name.”

Marcus raised his head.

“You do?”

“My brother mentioned it once. He said a woman had been very particular about a delivery.”

“What did he say about her?”

Margaret thought for a moment.

“He said she refused to sign the normal receipt. She wanted a separate acknowledgement.”

Damian looked at Marcus.

“Separate acknowledgement.”

Margaret nodded.

“He thought it was ridiculous. He told me the woman had brought her own paperwork and insisted he sign it before handing over the package.”

Marcus looked down at the receipt.

“Did he tell you what was inside?”

“No.”

“Did he say where it went?”

Margaret shook her head.

“He only said it wasn't going to the address written on the outside.”

The room fell silent.

Marcus turned the receipt over again.

There was a small handwritten note beneath the signature.

*Second copy retained by recipient.*

He looked at the investigators.

“Find the second copy.”

**11:40 a.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman listened as her associate reported the latest development.

“They found the receipt.”

“Which one?”

“The Greybridge collection receipt. Jonathan Vale's signature is confirmed.”

She closed her eyes briefly.

“And the reverse?”

“The name M. Voss is visible.”

The woman stood.

“Did they find the second acknowledgement?”

“Not yet.”

“Then they haven't found the most important document.”

The associate hesitated.

“What should we do?”

“Nothing yet.”

She walked to the cabinet.

The two newborn photographs were still inside.

Beside them lay a thin folder containing the original transfer instructions.

She removed a single sheet.

The handwriting was familiar.

*First transfer complete.*

Beneath it was another line.

*Second transfer pending confirmation.*

She folded the paper and returned it to the folder.

“Find out who has the second acknowledgement,” she said.

“And if the Vespers find it first?”

The woman looked toward the locked cabinet.

“Then we will have to move before they understand what it means.”

**12:16 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus stood alone in the records room.

The delivery receipt was on the table before him.

The first delivery had been made by Thomas Reed.

The second had been collected by Jonathan Vale.

Both had passed through Northbridge.

Both had involved Northstar.

And both had required separate acknowledgement.

He looked at the note written on the back.

*Second copy retained by recipient.*

The recipient had signed as M. Voss.

But the name was not the only thing that mattered.

Someone had kept a second copy.

Someone had expected the transaction to be questioned.

And somewhere, possibly still intact, was a document that could reveal exactly what had been delivered.

Marcus picked up his telephone.

“Please inform Mr. Vesper. We have evidence of two separate deliveries and a recipient who retained a second acknowledgement.”

He paused.

“Also, I want the original courier records preserved before anyone contacts another witness.”

He ended the call.

For twelve years, the truth had been divided between different people, different companies and different pieces of paper.

Now the pieces were beginning to come together.

And the next document might finally explain why two deliveries had been necessary.
