# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 11 — A Very Ordinary Lunch**

**Saturday — 11:40 a.m.**

Aria was beginning to regret agreeing to lunch.

Not because she disliked Cassian.

He seemed perfectly pleasant.

The problem was the restaurant.

She had suggested a small Italian restaurant near the city centre.

Cassian had replied that he knew the place.

Then he had asked whether she preferred a quiet table.

That had been reasonable.

What was less reasonable was the message she received ten minutes later.

**Cassian:** I've booked a table for two.

**Aria:** Good.

**Cassian:** The restaurant manager has also offered us the private dining room.

**Aria:** Why?

**Cassian:** Apparently, he thinks privacy is important.

**Aria:** We're eating lunch, not negotiating a peace treaty.

**Cassian:** I'll cancel it.

**Aria:** Thank you.

She put her phone away.

At least he was learning.

**12:15 p.m. — Bellini Restaurant**

Cassian was waiting outside when Aria arrived.

He wore a dark shirt and trousers, looking considerably less formal than she had expected.

"Hello."

"Hello."

"Did you find the place easily?"

"Yes. It wasn't difficult."

"Good. I was worried you might get lost."

Aria looked at him.

"Do I look like someone who gets lost between two streets?"

Cassian smiled.

"I've only known you for a few minutes. I didn't want to make assumptions."

"Wise."

He opened the restaurant door.

Inside, the manager approached them.

"Mr. Vesper. Welcome."

Cassian nodded.

"Thank you. Just the table we booked, please."

"Certainly."

The manager glanced at Aria.

"Your sister?"

Cassian smiled.

"Yes."

Aria looked at the manager.

"Unfortunately, he has already admitted it."

Cassian laughed.

The manager seemed relieved that nobody was going to complain.

They were seated near a window.

The restaurant was busy, but not crowded.

Aria looked at the menu.

"These prices are reasonable."

Cassian glanced at the menu.

"Are they?"

"Yes."

He looked at the prices again.

For a moment, he wondered whether his understanding of reasonable had been damaged by years of corporate lunches.

"What would you recommend?" Aria asked.

"The mushroom risotto is good. So is the grilled chicken."

"Grilled chicken again."

Cassian smiled.

"Is that your favourite?"

"It's reliable."

"An excellent quality in food."

She ordered the chicken.

Cassian ordered pasta.

For the first few minutes, they talked about ordinary things.

Her new job.

His work.

The city.

The restaurant's questionable music selection.

Then Cassian asked:

"Can I ask you something?"

"That depends."

"How are you finding the family?"

Aria considered it.

"They're kind."

"That's good."

"They're also very formal."

Cassian laughed.

"That sounds accurate."

"Your grandfather seems to believe every conversation needs an official meeting."

"He's been doing that for eighty-two years. I don't think he'll stop now."

Aria smiled.

"And your grandmother is terrifying."

Cassian looked delighted.

"She likes you."

"That's what worries me."

He laughed again.

For a while, the conversation became easier.

**Vesper Estate — 1:05 p.m.**

Octavian was reviewing the latest investigation report when Marcus entered.

"Mr. Cassian is having lunch with Miss Aria."

Octavian looked up.

"I know."

"Would you like an update?"

"No."

Marcus nodded.

Then he remained standing.

Octavian glanced at him.

"Something else?"

"Mr. Felix has identified the former courier company's insurance broker."

"Good."

"The broker's records indicate that the vehicle was insured under a commercial policy paid by Northbridge."

"Does that help?"

"It confirms the company maintained control over the vehicle, even though it was registered to Thomas Reed."

Octavian leaned back.

"Find the driver."

"Yes, sir."

"And Marcus?"

"Yes?"

"Do not interrupt lunch to tell Cassian."

Marcus paused.

"I had no intention of doing so."

"Good."

**Bellini Restaurant — 1:20 p.m.**

Aria was halfway through lunch when Cassian's phone vibrated.

He glanced at it.

His expression changed slightly.

Aria noticed.

"Work?"

"Yes."

"Important?"

"Probably."

"Then answer it."

Cassian shook his head.

"It can wait."

"Are you sure?"

He looked at her.

"You're having lunch with me. I don't want to spend it staring at my phone."

Aria shrugged.

"Fair enough."

He turned the phone face down.

She took another bite.

After a moment, Cassian asked:

"Have you spoken to Sebastian recently?"

Aria considered.

"Not really."

"He's been worried about you."

"Has he?"

"Yes."

She looked at him.

"Why?"

Cassian hesitated.

"Because he used to know you."

Aria lowered her fork.

"Did he?"

Cassian realised his mistake.

"He grew up with you. He remembers you."

Aria nodded.

"That's different."

Cassian didn't argue.

She continued eating.

Then she asked:

"Do you think they all blame themselves?"

"Who?"

"Your family. For not finding me."

Cassian's expression softened.

"Some of them do."

"That isn't their fault."

"I know."

"Do they?"

Cassian was quiet.

Then he answered honestly.

"Not always."

Aria looked out of the window.

"I don't want to spend the rest of my life making people feel guilty for something they couldn't control."

Cassian nodded.

"Then don't."

She looked back at him.

"That's easy to say."

"Yes. But it's still true."

**Vesper Tower — 2:15 p.m.**

Damian received a call from the investigation team.

They had located Clara Reed.

She was willing to speak about her father's former work.

Damian arranged a meeting through the family's legal representatives.

Before ending the call, he asked:

"Did she recognise Northbridge?"

The investigator answered.

"She said her father mentioned the company once."

"What did he say?"

"He said it had been one of his most unusual clients."

Damian's expression hardened.

"Anything else?"

"She remembers him saying that he was paid to deliver sealed packages, not ordinary documents."

Damian paused.

"How many deliveries?"

"She doesn't know."

"Ask her to think carefully. We need dates, locations and anything she remembers."

He ended the call.

Then he contacted Maximilian.

**Bellini Restaurant — 2:40 p.m.**

Lunch was almost finished.

Cassian paid the bill.

Aria reached for her wallet.

"I can pay for my share."

Cassian looked at her.

"I invited you."

"That doesn't mean you have to pay for everything."

"I know."

He smiled.

"Then let me pay this time. You can choose the place next time."

Aria considered it.

"All right."

They left the restaurant.

Outside, Cassian asked:

"Would you like me to arrange a car?"

"No, thank you. I'll take a taxi."

"All right."

He did not argue.

Aria appreciated that.

She walked towards the main road.

Then she turned.

"Thank you for lunch."

"You're welcome."

"And Cassian?"

"Yes?"

"You're less exhausting than I expected."

He placed a hand over his chest.

"I'll take that as a compliment."

"It was."

She smiled and left.

Cassian watched her taxi disappear into traffic.

Then he looked at his phone.

Three missed calls.

He sighed.

"Back to work."

**Vesper Estate — 3:30 p.m.**

The family gathered in the investigation room.

Clara Reed's preliminary statement had arrived.

Her father, Thomas Reed, had worked as a private courier.

He had not been a regular delivery driver.

He had accepted occasional assignments from companies requiring secure document transport.

Northbridge Data Services had been one of his clients.

Clara remembered one particular delivery.

Her father had returned home late that evening and argued with her mother.

He had said the package contained hospital records.

He had also said he had been instructed not to open it.

Damian read the statement.

"Do we know the delivery date?"

"Not yet," Marcus said.

"Find it."

Felix checked the vehicle insurance records.

"There are only four relevant dates."

"Which one matches the hospital transfer?"

"The earliest one."

Damian looked at the screen.

The date was two days after the births.

Maximilian leaned forward.

"Where was the package delivered?"

"Clara doesn't remember the address. She remembers the building had a private reception desk."

Cassian entered the room.

"That could be half the offices in the city."

Damian nodded.

"Which is why we need the delivery receipt."

Octavian looked at the statement.

"Did Thomas Reed keep personal records?"

"His daughter believes he did," Marcus replied. "She remembers a locked metal box in his study."

"Where is it now?"

"His family still has it."

"Arrange a visit."

**Sterling Mansion — 5:10 p.m.**

Victoria was no longer satisfied with searching the family filing cabinets.

She wanted to know what Richard was hiding.

She entered his study without knocking.

"Father."

Richard looked up.

"Victoria. What is it?"

"I need to ask you something about the hospital."

His expression changed.

"What now?"

"Did you ever receive a request to transfer medical records from Westbridge?"

Richard slowly closed the folder in front of him.

"Why?"

"Because I found a document."

"What document?"

"A transfer summary."

Richard stood.

"Where is it?"

"In my room."

"Bring it here."

Victoria hesitated.

"Why?"

"Because I want to see it."

She studied him.

Then she said:

"You know something."

Richard's expression hardened.

"Do not accuse me of things you don't understand."

"Then explain."

He looked at her for a long moment.

Finally, he said:

"I received several administrative documents from the hospital after your birth. They were routine."

"Why were they transferred to Westbridge?"

"I don't remember."

"You're lying."

Richard's voice rose.

"Enough."

Victoria went quiet.

He continued:

"You have a home, a family and a future. Stop digging into matters that will not benefit you."

Victoria looked at him.

Then she turned and walked out.

Richard remained standing.

He picked up his phone.

He dialled a number he had not called in years.

No one answered.

**Vesper Estate — 7:15 p.m.**

Miriam Shaw sat across from Damian.

She had been asked to review the courier company's name.

When she saw it, she became visibly uncomfortable.

"Thomas Reed."

"You know him?" Damian asked.

"I met him once."

"When?"

"After the records transfer."

"What happened?"

"He came to collect a signed delivery acknowledgement."

"From you?"

"Yes."

"Did he say anything?"

Miriam thought carefully.

"He asked whether the package had been sent to the correct address."

"Which address?"

"He didn't tell me."

"Did you give him an answer?"

"I told him the transfer had been completed."

Damian studied her.

"Did he seem concerned?"

"Yes."

"About what?"

"He asked whether the package contained original records."

"And?"

"I told him I wasn't authorised to discuss the contents."

Miriam looked down.

"He said someone had paid him extra to ensure the package was delivered personally."

Damian leaned forward.

"Who?"

"He didn't say."

"Did you ask?"

"No."

"Why?"

Miriam looked at him.

"Because by then, I wanted nothing more to do with the matter."

**Sterling Mansion — 9:40 p.m.**

Victoria sat on her bed.

She had received another message.

**Unknown:** Did Richard tell you anything?

**Victoria:** No.

**Unknown:** Did he recognise the transfer documents?

Victoria stared at the screen.

**Victoria:** Yes.

**Unknown:** Then he knows more than he admits.

Victoria typed:

**Victoria:** What exactly happened twenty years ago?

The reply took longer than usual.

**Unknown:** Two families were meant to receive two different children.

Victoria's expression tightened.

**Victoria:** They did receive two different children.

**Unknown:** Not according to the original arrangement.

Victoria stared at the words.

She typed again.

**Victoria:** What arrangement?

No reply.

She called the number.

Unavailable.

Victoria threw her phone onto the bed.

Then she picked it up and opened the photographs of the documents again.

For the first time, she considered that the truth might be more complicated than Aria being the wrong girl in the Sterling household.

**Vesper Estate — 11:05 p.m.**

Octavian stood alone in the study.

A new report had arrived.

The courier's daughter had found the metal box.

Inside were delivery receipts, old invoices and a small notebook.

One entry had been circled.

**Northbridge — Special Delivery.**

Beside it was an address.

The address belonged to a building that no longer existed as a commercial office.

It had been demolished twelve years earlier.

But its former owner was still alive.

Octavian read the name.

Then he called Damian.

"Tomorrow morning, I want the former owner identified and located."

"Yes, Grandfather."

"And Damian?"

"Yes?"

"Do not approach anyone until we know what they are likely to do."

Damian understood.

The trail was finally leading somewhere.

But every new answer seemed to reveal another person who had been involved.

And somewhere between the hospital, the archive and the mysterious delivery, the original arrangement was waiting to be uncovered.

**For the first time, the Vespers had an address.**
