# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 60 — The Auditor Who Refused to Sign**

**Saturday — 2:19 p.m.**

Nobody looked at Dorian Hale.

They were all looking at the name on Felix's screen.

**MARTIN HALE.**

Dorian slowly pulled out a chair.

“My father retired from the audit department twenty years ago.”

Maximilian studied the document.

“He rejected the first death verification.”

“Yes.”

“And you never mentioned that?”

“Because I didn't know.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Your father never told you?”

“My father and I have not discussed Vesper administrative records over dinner for the past twenty years.”

Cassian nodded thoughtfully.

“A regrettable failure of family communication.”

Nobody acknowledged him.

Dorian continued.

“He was an auditor. He reviewed documents. He didn't tell me about every rejected submission.”

Felix opened the second page.

“His handwritten note says the person presented did not match the available identification record.”

Catherine leaned forward.

“Then he saw the person.”

“Possibly,” Maximilian said. “The wording doesn't prove he personally examined the body.”

Dorian pointed to the signature.

“He would have known whether the document was genuine.”

“Can you contact him?” Thomas asked.

“Yes.”

“Will he speak to us?”

Dorian looked at the document.

“If he remembers this, he will.”

Octavian's voice was firm.

“Arrange the meeting.”

Dorian stood.

“I'll speak to him first.”

“Why?”

“Because he's my father, not an archive.”

Octavian considered that.

Then nodded.

“Very well.”

**3:06 p.m. — Dorian Hale's Residence**

Martin Hale lived in a modest house outside the financial district.

He opened the door himself.

At seventy-six, he was still upright, neatly dressed and visibly unimpressed by unexpected visitors.

He looked at his son.

“You could have called.”

“I did.”

“You called while I was eating.”

“You were eating at three in the afternoon.”

“I was having lunch late.”

Dorian stepped aside.

“There's something you need to see.”

Martin noticed Thomas and Margaret behind him.

His expression changed.

“I see.”

He looked at Margaret.

“Miss Ellison.”

“Mr. Hale.”

Then his eyes moved to Thomas.

“Reed.”

Thomas gave a small nod.

Martin looked past them.

“Is this about the Vesper family?”

“Yes,” Dorian replied.

Martin sighed.

“Of course it is.”

He let them in.

**3:18 p.m. — Martin Hale's Study**

The old auditor examined the rejected death verification.

He did not need a magnifying glass.

He remembered it.

“I refused this.”

Dorian sat opposite him.

“Why?”

“Because the identification was inadequate.”

“Did you see the body?”

Martin looked at him.

“Yes.”

The room became still.

Margaret leaned forward.

“You saw Elias Vesper?”

“I saw a man presented as Elias Vesper.”

“And you couldn't identify him?”

“I could not confirm he was Elias.”

Thomas placed the photograph from the hospital corridor on the desk.

Martin glanced at it.

Then looked again.

“Where did you get this?”

“Central Women's Hospital,” Margaret said.

Martin studied the face.

“That's not the same man.”

Dorian frowned.

“What?”

“The person in this photograph is not the person I saw.”

Hale leaned forward.

“Are you certain?”

“I spent twenty-seven years auditing records where one incorrect identity could move millions. I learned to be certain about what I could establish and cautious about everything else.”

Martin tapped the death verification.

“This man had a different facial structure. The medical report claimed the injuries made identification difficult. I refused to accept that as sufficient.”

“What happened after you rejected it?” Thomas asked.

“Nothing for several months. Then a new submission arrived.”

“Was it the same body?”

“I don't know. The second submission contained a new medical confirmation and a different supporting statement.”

“Who signed it?”

Martin turned the document.

“The medical officer signed again. The administrative countersignature was different.”

Dorian looked at the signature.

“Mine.”

Martin nodded.

“I never believed you had signed it.”

Dorian stared at him.

“You knew?”

“I knew the signature was suspicious. I didn't know whether your credentials had been used or someone had copied your handwriting.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because I was instructed not to discuss a confidential audit with anyone outside the review.”

“Even your son?”

“Especially my son, if he was employed by the same institution.”

Dorian looked away.

Thomas asked the next question.

“Who instructed you?”

Martin's expression hardened.

“The review committee.”

“Who chaired it?”

“I don't remember the chairman's name.”

“You don't remember?”

“I remember the committee's office. I remember the paperwork. I remember that the meeting was held in the patriarchal administrative building.”

Margaret looked at him.

“Who requested the review?”

Martin hesitated.

“A man who claimed to represent the temporary executive custodian.”

“Name?”

Martin shook his head.

“He didn't provide one.”

**4:02 p.m. — Martin Hale's Study**

Martin rose and went to a filing cabinet.

He removed a narrow folder.

“I kept this because I disagreed with the decision.”

Inside was a copy of his original rejection report.

There were three handwritten observations.

1. The physical identification was inconclusive.
2. The medical confirmation did not establish identity.
3. The supporting administrative authority was not independently verified.

At the bottom, Martin had added:

**Further certification should not be accepted without direct confirmation from the original executive custodian.**

Dorian read it.

“You knew the second submission was questionable.”

“I knew the evidence was insufficient.”

“Then why was it accepted?”

Martin gave him a tired look.

“Because I was an auditor, not the person who controlled the register.”

Thomas pointed to the final page.

“Did you keep the second submission?”

“No. I was not given a copy.”

“Who received it?”

“The executive office.”

Margaret studied the rejection report.

“Was your objection ever formally withdrawn?”

“No.”

“Was it overruled?”

Martin nodded.

“By an emergency administrative review.”

“Who approved that?”

Martin opened the folder's back cover.

A small notation had been written there.

**P-01 / EX-04.**

Octavian's office code.

The same emergency access classification.

Dorian looked at the notation.

“Someone used the patriarchal office code to overrule an independent audit.”

Martin shook his head.

“Someone claimed the authority to do so.”

“That distinction isn't helping.”

“It should. You have spent your career learning that a document is not the same thing as the authority it claims to represent.”

Dorian fell silent.

Martin placed the report on the table.

“I refused to certify the death. I never changed that position.”

**5:30 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian read the report twice.

When he finished, he placed it on the desk.

“Someone overruled the auditor.”

“Yes,” Dorian said.

“Using my office code.”

“Yes.”

“And my brother's authority.”

“Possibly.”

Octavian looked at Vane.

“Could the emergency review have been issued without my approval?”

Vane answered carefully.

“The code could be used by someone holding the private authority seal. But the seal alone would not establish your personal consent.”

“Who held it?”

“Your office secretary maintained it. Elias had access to the emergency authority register.”

“And the executive custodian?”

“Could request its use.”

Octavian turned to Catherine.

“You knew Elias had access.”

“I knew he had temporary executive authority. I did not know someone had used it to override an auditor.”

“Could he have approved it himself?”

“Yes.”

“Would he have?”

Catherine looked at the report.

“I don't know.”

Octavian's face tightened.

It was an answer he disliked.

It was also the only honest one.

**6:14 p.m. — Private Dining Room**

Victoria had been reading Martin's report.

She put it down.

“There's something wrong with the dates.”

Felix looked up.

“What?”

“The first verification was rejected. Then the second was accepted. But Martin says he never received the second submission.”

“Correct.”

“Then who decided the second submission was sufficient?”

Maximilian answered.

“The emergency review committee.”

Victoria shook her head.

“That isn't what I mean. Who actually examined the evidence?”

Nobody answered.

She pointed to the report.

“Martin examined the first body and refused to certify it. The second submission apparently used a different medical confirmation. But we don't know whether anyone examined the second body at all.”

Felix stopped typing.

“That's possible.”

“It's more than possible. The second submission could have used the same photograph and a different report.”

Damian looked at him.

“Can we verify that?”

Felix opened the document archive.

“The first submission included a photograph. The second submission's attachment list is incomplete.”

Victoria leaned back.

“So the second death verification may not have been based on a second examination.”

Cassian smiled faintly.

“That's a very unpleasant possibility.”

“It's a very ordinary possibility,” Victoria replied. “Someone wanted the death registered. They had already been refused once. They changed the paperwork until someone accepted it.”

Aria, who had been quietly reading the documents, looked up.

“Then the important question isn't whether Elias died.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“What is it?”

“Who was the person they wanted the registry to recognise as Elias?”

Silence settled over the room.

Aria continued.

“If the first body wasn't identified, and the second submission didn't establish a new examination, then we don't even know whether the same person was involved in both attempts.”

Felix slowly nodded.

“I can compare the attachment references.”

“Do that,” Maximilian said.

**7:08 p.m. — Felix's Office**

The comparison took less than twenty minutes.

Felix opened the first submission's attachment list.

Then the second.

His expression changed.

“That's strange.”

Damian came closer.

“What?”

“The second submission contains a medical report number that doesn't exist in the hospital's records.”

“Could it be a transcription error?”

“I checked the original index. The number belongs to a different patient.”

Maximilian looked at the screen.

“Who?”

Felix opened the index.

The patient's name was withheld.

Only the admission date and hospital department were visible.

**Central Women's Hospital.**

**Confidential admission.**

**Reference: M.V.**

For several seconds, Felix did not speak.

Then he opened the original hospital preservation ledger.

The dates matched.

The second death verification had used a medical report belonging to the same confidential admission whose file had been removed nineteen years ago.

Catherine stood abruptly.

“That is impossible.”

Felix looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because that admission was the reason I went to the hospital.”

Aria looked between them.

“Someone used the confidential patient's medical report to support Elias's death registration?”

Felix nodded.

“It appears so.”

Thomas placed both documents side by side.

The hospital file had been preserved under the reference M.V.

Its contents had been removed.

And a medical report from that admission had later been used to support the death of Elias Vesper.

Dorian looked at his father.

Martin had gone pale.

“You refused to certify the first body,” Thomas said quietly.

Martin nodded.

“And someone used a confidential hospital report to make the second submission acceptable.”

Martin stared at the two documents.

“They didn't just falsify a death registration.”

He looked at Octavian.

“They used another patient's medical record to do it.”

Octavian's voice was dangerously calm.

“Find out whose medical report it was.”

Felix looked at the reference.

“M.V.”

Catherine's hands had gone still.

“Mara Voss,” she whispered.

But Aria shook her head.

“Not yet.”

Catherine looked at her.

“We don't know that the initials identify the patient,” Aria said. “We only know someone used a record carrying those initials.”

Catherine slowly sat down.

The distinction was small.

The consequences were not.

**8:16 p.m. — Vesper Estate, Archive Room**

After the others left, Martin Hale remained with Dorian.

He stood before the old death verification report.

“There is something else,” he said.

Dorian turned.

“What?”

“When I rejected the first submission, the man who brought it to me returned the following morning.”

“Who was he?”

“I don't know.”

“Did he identify himself?”

“He said he represented the executive custodian.”

Dorian waited.

Martin looked at the photograph of the unidentified man in the hospital corridor.

“He had a scar near his left eyebrow.”

Dorian looked at the photograph again.

The resemblance to him was obvious.

But the scar was not.

The man in the photograph had a faint mark beneath the eyebrow.

Martin pointed to it.

“That's the same mark.”

Dorian stared at the image.

“You're saying the person at the hospital may have been the man who submitted the first death verification?”

“I am saying the man who brought me the first submission had a similar mark. I cannot identify him from this photograph.”

Dorian took the photograph.

“Did he give you a name?”

Martin hesitated.

“Yes.”

“What name?”

Martin looked directly at his son.

“He called himself Dorian Hale.”

The room went silent.

Outside, the estate's security lights came on.

Dorian looked down at the photograph.

For nineteen years, someone had been using his identity.

And now they had a possible reason.

**They had wanted Elias Vesper declared dead.**
