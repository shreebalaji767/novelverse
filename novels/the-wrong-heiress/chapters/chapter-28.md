# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 28 — The Child Who Was Supposed to Become a Sterling**

**Sunday — 12:08 a.m.**

Marcus read the destination document a third time.

The address belonged to a private estate outside the city.

The property had been held by the Sterling Family Trust.

The trust had been established decades earlier to manage private family assets, residential properties, and confidential charitable arrangements.

It had never appeared in the hospital records.

It had never appeared in the Vesper investigation.

And according to the archived transport instruction, it had been the intended destination for Infant B.

Marcus called Octavian again.

“Sir, the document identifies the Sterling Family Trust as the receiving authority.”

“I heard you.”

“The destination was authorised before the hospital handover.”

“Who signed it?”

“An authorised representative of Crestline Administrative Partners. The signature is illegible on the scan.”

“Was the destination actually reached?”

“The document confirms the intended destination, not the completed delivery.”

Octavian was silent for a moment.

“Then do not confuse an instruction with an event.”

“Understood.”

“Secure the original record. Verify the address, the ownership history, and every person connected to the property. Do not approach the Sterlings until we know what happened there.”

Marcus looked at the document again.

“Yes, sir.”

“And Marcus?”

“Yes?”

“Richard Sterling is to be protected.”

“Even after this?”

“Especially after this.”

**12:41 a.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard had not slept.

He was sitting in his study when the door opened.

Victoria stood there in a dressing gown.

“Why are you still awake?”

Richard closed the notebook.

“Go to bed.”

“You've been on the telephone for an hour.”

“I have work.”

“At midnight?”

“Victoria.”

She stepped inside.

“Is it about the notebook?”

Richard looked at her.

“You shouldn't be involved.”

“I'm already involved.”

“No. You're curious.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“Do you think I'm stupid?”

“No.”

“Then stop treating me like a child.”

Richard looked at the notebook.

For a moment, he considered telling her everything.

Then he remembered the unknown caller.

He remembered the warning.

And he remembered how little he actually knew.

“Tomorrow,” he said.

Victoria narrowed her eyes.

“What happens tomorrow?”

“I am giving the Vespers the notebook.”

Her expression changed.

“You're giving them our family records?”

“I'm giving them the information I should have given them years ago.”

“And what about us?”

Richard looked at her.

“What about us?”

“What happens if those records prove that the Sterling family was involved?”

Richard didn't answer.

Victoria's voice lowered.

“Father, what if they prove I was never supposed to be here?”

He looked at her for a long moment.

“You are my daughter.”

“That isn't what I asked.”

“I don't know what happened to the second child.”

“But you know something.”

Richard looked away.

Victoria understood.

He was hiding something again.

She left without another word.

**7:16 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix had not gone home.

His hair was untidy, his shirt was wrinkled, and he had three empty coffee cups beside his keyboard.

Maximilian entered the research room.

“Have you slept?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

Felix thought.

“Recently.”

“Define recently.”

“Within the last twenty-four hours.”

Maximilian stared at him.

“That isn't reassuring.”

Felix turned towards the main display.

“I have the Sterling trust records.”

The screen showed the history of the property.

The trust had been created thirty-one years earlier.

The estate had been purchased through an independent holding company.

Its registered purpose was residential and administrative custody.

Twelve years ago, a new beneficiary instruction had been filed.

The instruction had authorised temporary accommodation for one infant under a confidential placement arrangement.

Maximilian read the entry.

“Who approved it?”

“Richard Sterling's signature appears on the supporting authorisation.”

“Does it prove he knew the child was being sent there?”

“No. The document only confirms his approval of the trust's use for temporary custody.”

“Who was responsible for receiving the child?”

Felix enlarged another field.

**PROPERTY CUSTODIAN: ELEANOR SHAW.**

Maximilian frowned.

“Eleanor Shaw?”

“Not the same person as Miriam Shaw. Different identity, different records.”

“Can you verify that?”

“Yes. This Eleanor Shaw was a private estate administrator employed by the trust. She resigned four months after the handover.”

“Where is she now?”

Felix opened another record.

“Dead. Eleven years ago.”

Maximilian exhaled.

“Who replaced her?”

“No replacement was registered.”

“Then who maintained the estate?”

Felix opened the property management records.

A company named Westbridge Administrative Services had been responsible for maintenance.

Maximilian looked at him.

“Westbridge again.”

“Yes.”

“Who owned it?”

“Crestline.”

The same name appeared for the fourth time.

Maximilian rubbed his forehead.

“Find the property caretaker. Find the neighbours. Find the delivery records. And establish whether the estate was occupied after the handover.”

Felix nodded.

“Already started.”

Maximilian glanced at the coffee cups.

“After breakfast.”

Felix stared at him.

“I'm working.”

“You're deteriorating.”

“That's an unprofessional assessment.”

“It's an accurate one.”

**8:32 a.m. — Aria's Apartment**

Aria was preparing breakfast when Marcus called.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning. Is everything all right?”

“We have an important development.”

She stopped cutting the fruit.

“What happened?”

“We found the intended destination for Infant B.”

“Where?”

“The Sterling Family Trust.”

Aria remained quiet.

Marcus continued.

“The trust authorised temporary custody at a private estate. We are still verifying whether the child reached the property.”

“Who was supposed to receive the baby?”

“The trust's property custodian was Eleanor Shaw.”

“Was she related to Miriam?”

“No evidence of a connection. The names are similar, but the records identify two different people.”

“Is she alive?”

“No. She died eleven years ago.”

Aria looked at the kitchen counter.

“So we found the destination, but not the child.”

“Correct.”

“Did Richard know?”

“His signature appears on the trust's supporting authorisation. We don't yet know what he understood.”

Aria took a breath.

“Don't assume he knew everything just because he signed something.”

Marcus paused.

“That is consistent with our current assessment.”

“And don't let anyone confront him before we know what the document means.”

“Understood.”

She ended the call.

For several seconds, she stood still.

The Sterling family had raised her.

They had given her a home, an education, and a childhood.

Then, when they discovered she was not biologically related to them, they had asked her to leave.

Now the investigation suggested that the same family trust might have been used to receive another infant.

Aria did not know what to feel.

She didn't know whether the child had ever reached the estate.

She didn't know whether the child had been Victoria.

And she didn't know whether the Sterlings had been told the truth.

She finished preparing breakfast.

There was no point allowing uncertainty to ruin the only part of the morning she could control.

**9:47 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Victoria arrived without an appointment.

The reception desk called Evelyn.

Evelyn looked at the visitor notification.

“Ms. Sterling?”

“Yes.”

“Does she have an appointment?”

“No.”

“Then she cannot enter the executive floor.”

Victoria had already started walking towards the lift.

Evelyn followed.

“Ms. Sterling.”

Victoria turned.

“I need to speak to someone.”

“Please wait at reception.”

“This concerns my family.”

“Then you should arrange an appointment through the appropriate channel.”

Victoria gave her a cold smile.

“You people are very fond of procedures.”

“They prevent misunderstandings.”

“I'm not here to misunderstand anything.”

Evelyn looked at her.

“Then waiting should not be difficult.”

Victoria stared at her.

Then, reluctantly, she returned to reception.

**10:02 a.m. — Sterling Trust Estate**

The property had been abandoned for years.

Its gates were rusted.

The gardens were overgrown.

The main house remained intact, but several windows had been boarded up.

A legal representative, two investigators, and a property records specialist entered under the authority of the trust's archived documentation and the necessary permissions.

Damian accompanied them.

He inspected the grounds before entering.

“Someone maintained this place after the original staff left.”

The property specialist looked at the maintenance records.

“Westbridge paid for repairs for almost two years after the handover.”

“Then?”

“Payments stopped.”

“Who occupied the house during that period?”

“We don't know.”

Damian examined the entrance.

There were no signs of recent forced entry.

Inside, the house smelled of dust and damp wood.

The furniture had been removed.

The rooms were empty.

In the study, the investigators found an old filing cabinet.

Most drawers were empty.

One was locked.

The legal representative authorised its opening.

Inside were several folders.

One was marked:

**PRIVATE CUSTODY — INFANT B**

Damian stepped closer.

The investigator carefully removed it.

The first document was a property receipt.

**DELIVERY RECEIVED: 14:35**

**RECEIVING PERSON: E. SHAW**

**ITEM: ONE INFANT — TEMPORARY CUSTODY**

**DELIVERED BY: D. MERCER**

Damian read it twice.

“That's confirmation.”

The legal representative nodded.

“It confirms that Mercer delivered the infant to this property.”

“Who signed?”

“E. Shaw.”

Damian looked at the next document.

It was a handwritten note.

*Infant received in good condition. Temporary arrangements pending confirmation of permanent placement.*

There was no reference to a hospital.

No reference to a family.

No identity.

Only the words:

**INFANT B.**

Damian's expression hardened.

“Photograph everything.”

**10:29 a.m. — Vesper Tower**

Felix received the photograph.

He compared the delivery time with the hospital handover register.

The hospital entry showed Mercer leaving with Infant B at 2:17 p.m.

The estate receipt showed delivery at 2:35 p.m.

Eighteen minutes.

Felix stared at the figures.

“That's close.”

Maximilian stood beside him.

“Close enough to support the same journey?”

“Possibly. The distance between the hospital and the estate is consistent with the timing.”

“Can you establish the route?”

“Not yet.”

Felix enlarged the receipt.

“The signature matches the property custodian's archived employment signature.”

“Are you certain?”

“It's a strong match. We need a handwriting examiner for formal confirmation.”

Maximilian nodded.

“Good. What else?”

Felix opened the folder.

“There are several maintenance records, two custody reports, and a later instruction.”

He clicked.

The instruction was dated four days after the infant's arrival.

*Temporary custody remains in effect. Receiving authority has not confirmed acceptance. Do not initiate further transfer without written authorisation.*

Maximilian's eyes narrowed.

“Four days later, the receiving authority still hadn't accepted the child.”

“Yes.”

“Who signed?”

Felix enlarged the signature.

**M. VOSS.**

Cassian entered the room.

“Of course.”

Felix opened the next document.

It was dated three days later.

*The infant is to remain at the property until further notice.*

A handwritten addition followed.

*Do not contact Vesper Life Sciences.*

Maximilian looked at the screen.

“Someone specifically prohibited contact with the facility.”

Cassian's expression lost its usual amusement.

“That means the cancellation wasn't an administrative mistake.”

**11:14 a.m. — Sterling Trust Estate**

Damian entered the bedroom.

There was a small wooden cabinet beside the wall.

Inside, beneath a layer of dust, lay an envelope.

He opened it carefully.

The envelope contained several photographs.

One showed the estate's former property custodian, Eleanor Shaw, standing in the garden.

Another showed a small cot.

The third showed a newborn wrapped in a white blanket.

Damian stopped.

The infant's face was partly visible.

A hospital identification band was attached to the wrist.

He called the investigator.

“Photograph these.”

The investigator approached.

“Is that the child?”

“It appears to be.”

Damian looked at the back of the photograph.

There was a handwritten date.

The same date as the estate receipt.

And a name.

**SUBJECT 01.**

He turned the photograph over again.

There was another line beneath it.

*Temporary care until the receiving family is confirmed.*

The receiving family.

Not the receiving facility.

Damian looked at the investigator.

“Secure everything.”

**12:06 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The photograph appeared on the main conference screen.

Octavian stood in front of it.

Lucian, Isolde, Helena, Maximilian, Cassian, Felix, and Marcus were present.

Aria had arrived shortly before.

She stared at the photograph.

The infant was too young to identify.

There were no distinctive features she could recognise.

Only a hospital band and a blanket.

“Is this definitely the same infant?” she asked.

Felix nodded.

“The band number is visible in the original photograph. It matches the hospital reference for Infant B.”

“And the estate receipt confirms the delivery?”

“Yes.”

“Then the baby reached the property.”

Octavian nodded.

“Correct.”

Aria looked at the handwritten note.

“Temporary care until the receiving family is confirmed.”

Cassian leaned forward.

“That wording is important.”

“Why?” Helena asked.

“Because the original arrangement appears to have involved a receiving family, not simply a medical facility.”

Maximilian looked at the cancelled Vesper Life Sciences admission.

“Yet the facility was prepared to receive the infant.”

“Perhaps as an intermediary,” Marcus said.

Felix shook his head.

“The records don't establish that. The facility admission was cancelled before the infant reached us.”

Lucian looked at the photograph.

“Then where was the child supposed to go after the estate?”

No one answered.

Aria looked at the receipt.

“Who decided the receiving family?”

Marcus replied.

“We don't know.”

“Then we need to find the person who gave the instruction to keep the baby there.”

Octavian turned towards Felix.

“Who issued the next instruction?”

Felix opened the document.

“M. Voss.”

“And after that?”

Felix opened the final page.

His expression changed.

“What is it?” Maximilian asked.

“The property records show that the custody arrangement was terminated six weeks after the infant arrived.”

“By whom?”

Felix enlarged the signature.

**D. MERCER.**

Damian's voice came through the secure conference connection.

“I have the termination document here. It says the infant was transferred to an authorised receiving party.”

Aria leaned forward.

“Who received the child?”

Damian looked at the document.

“The recipient's name is missing.”

**1:22 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria was waiting in Richard's study.

When he entered, she stood.

“Did you know?”

Richard stopped.

“Know what?”

“Was a baby brought to the Sterling trust estate twelve years ago?”

His expression changed.

“Where did you hear that?”

“From the Vespers.”

Richard closed the door.

“What exactly did they find?”

“A delivery receipt signed by Eleanor Shaw. A custody record. A photograph.”

Richard sat down slowly.

Victoria watched him.

“Father?”

He looked at her.

“I approved the use of the estate.”

“Why?”

“I was told it was temporary.”

“For whom?”

“I was not given a name.”

“Did you know a baby was coming?”

Richard hesitated.

“Yes.”

Victoria's face hardened.

“And you didn't tell me?”

“I didn't know the child was you.”

“You didn't know?”

“I was told there was an administrative placement. I was not told the identity.”

“Did you see the child?”

“No.”

“Did you ever ask?”

Richard looked away.

“I asked whether the arrangement had been completed.”

“And?”

“I was told it had been suspended.”

Victoria stared at him.

“Who told you?”

“Mara Voss.”

“Did you ask what happened to the baby?”

“Yes.”

“What did she say?”

Richard looked at the notebook.

“She said the child had been transferred under a separate instruction.”

Victoria's voice became quiet.

“Where?”

“She didn't tell me.”

Victoria stepped back.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then she asked the question that had been troubling her since the beginning.

“Was that child me?”

Richard looked at her.

“I don't know.”

Victoria's eyes filled with anger.

“You don't know anything.”

“I know what I was told.”

“You signed the trust documents. You approved the property. You knew a baby was coming. And you never asked who it was?”

Richard's voice hardened.

“I asked what I was authorised to ask.”

Victoria laughed bitterly.

“That's convenient.”

“Victoria—”

“No. You don't get to tell me what is convenient.”

She turned and left.

Richard remained seated.

The notebook lay open in front of him.

**3:45 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The Vespers had received the complete estate archive.

The records established several facts.

- Daniel Mercer collected Infant B from the hospital.
- Mercer delivered Infant B to the Sterling trust estate.
- Eleanor Shaw formally accepted temporary custody.
- The infant remained at the property for at least six weeks.
- The planned transfer to Vesper Life Sciences was cancelled.
- A later instruction authorised another transfer.
- The identity of the final recipient was missing.

There was one more document.

A property expense record dated two days before the infant's departure.

It listed food, medical supplies, and a payment to a private transport service.

The payment description read:

**TRANSFER — ROUTE C.**

Felix looked at the record.

“Route C again.”

Cassian folded his arms.

“Northbridge used Route C for Mercer's reassignment.”

Maximilian nodded.

“Then the same route may have been used to move the infant.”

Damian spoke through the conference connection.

“I found the transport company's name.”

Felix brought it onto the screen.

**GREYHAVEN PRIVATE LOGISTICS.**

Marcus opened the company record.

“Still operating?”

“No,” Felix said. “It was dissolved ten years ago.”

“Who owned it?”

“An individual named—”

He stopped.

Everyone looked at him.

“Who?”

Felix enlarged the registration record.

The registered director was:

**JONATHAN VALE.**

Lydia Vale's brother.

The deceased courier.

Cassian slowly leaned back.

“So Jonathan wasn't merely carrying documents.”

Maximilian looked at the records.

“He may have transported the child.”

Aria stared at the screen.

“Did his sister know?”

Marcus shook his head.

“Margaret only knew about his document deliveries. She never mentioned a child.”

“Then we need to speak to her again.”

Octavian nodded.

“Tomorrow.”

**6:18 p.m. — Unknown Location**

The woman received the estate photographs.

She examined the infant's identification band.

Then she read the delivery receipt.

Her expression remained calm until she reached the termination document.

**TRANSFERRED TO AUTHORISED RECEIVING PARTY.**

She turned the page.

The recipient's name was missing.

The woman called her associate.

“They found the estate.”

“Yes.”

“And the photograph?”

“Yes.”

“Did they identify the final recipient?”

“No.”

She closed the file.

“Then we still have time.”

The associate hesitated.

“What about Jonathan Vale?”

“He is dead.”

“His sister may have his records.”

The woman looked at the photograph again.

“Then make sure the Vespers do not find what he kept.”

**9:11 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Richard had finally decided to surrender the notebook.

He placed it inside a briefcase.

Then he checked the lock.

His phone rang.

Unknown number.

He answered.

“You have made a serious mistake.”

Richard's expression hardened.

“You arranged for a child to be brought to my family's property.”

The caller said nothing.

“You told me it was temporary. You told me the child would be transferred. You never told me where.”

“You agreed to the arrangement.”

“I agreed to temporary custody.”

“And the child was kept safe.”

“For six weeks.”

A pause.

Richard's voice dropped.

“Where did the child go?”

The caller ended the call.

Richard looked at the briefcase.

Then he picked up his coat.

He was no longer waiting until tomorrow.

**9:46 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

Marcus was reviewing the latest transport records when his phone rang.

Richard Sterling.

He answered immediately.

“Mr. Sterling?”

“I am coming to your office.”

“Is something wrong?”

“I have the notebook. And I have remembered something.”

Marcus stood.

“What?”

Richard's voice was unsteady.

“The woman who arranged the first transfer told me the child was not supposed to remain at the estate.”

“Who was the child supposed to go to?”

Richard took a breath.

“She said the receiving family had already been selected.”

“Which family?”

Richard hesitated.

Then he said:

“Your family.”

Marcus went silent.

“What?”

“The child was supposed to be delivered to the Vespers.”

The line remained open.

Richard continued.

“But after six weeks, the instruction changed.”

Marcus gripped the phone.

“Who changed it?”

Richard's answer came quietly.

“Jonathan Vale.”

**The dead courier had not merely carried the records.**

**He had taken the child.**
