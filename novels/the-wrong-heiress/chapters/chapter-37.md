# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 37 — The Name Behind the Money**

**Saturday — 8:10 a.m.**

Felix had discovered something unpleasant.

The Aurelius Consortium had not merely appeared in the records.

It had appeared in almost every important transaction.

He displayed the payment history across the conference room screen.

“Arthur Mercer received an initial administrative payment from a private trust associated with Aurelius. Northbridge received funding for the hospital records project from the same trust. Crestline received payments through Bellweather. Asterion handled the subsequent transfers.”

Maximilian examined the figures.

“So Aurelius financed the operation.”

“Possibly. The trust records establish a financial connection. They don't establish who inside Aurelius ordered it.”

Cassian looked at the screen.

“Someone spent a considerable amount of money to make two children disappear into administrative paperwork.”

Aria sat with a notebook.

“Why would Aurelius care about two babies?”

Nobody answered immediately.

Octavian finally spoke.

“Because the arrangement may have involved more than the children.”

Lucian looked at him.

“You believe they were targeting the Vesper family?”

“I believe we have not yet established their objective.”

Damian folded his arms.

“Should we approach Aurelius?”

“No,” Octavian replied. “Not until we know what they know.”

Felix raised a hand.

“I found another record.”

He opened a scanned trust instruction.

The document was dated three months before the births.

Its wording was brief:

*The two-family placement must be completed without direct involvement from either receiving household. All communications shall pass through the appointed administrative representatives.*

The authorisation field contained a code.

**AC-09.**

Below it was another line.

*The original instruction remains valid unless withdrawn by the originating authority.*

Aria looked at the screen.

“Who was the originating authority?”

Felix enlarged the final section.

The field was blank.

**9:35 a.m.**

Richard Sterling arrived with another document case.

He had spent the morning searching the private family archive.

“I found the original agreement for CA-17,” he said.

Marcus accepted the papers.

“Is it signed?”

“Yes. By me.”

Richard placed the agreement on the table.

The first page described a confidential administrative arrangement involving two family records.

The second page contained payment terms.

The third page listed the parties responsible for processing.

The final page contained Richard's signature.

Aria examined the wording.

“You signed this?”

“Yes.”

“Did you read it?”

Richard looked at her.

“Yes.”

“Then what did you think you were agreeing to?”

Richard took a breath.

“I was told that two families had requested confidential handling of sensitive family records. The arrangement would prevent personal information from entering public systems. I believed the Sterling estate was being used as a temporary administrative address.”

“Did you know children were involved?”

“I knew an infant would be placed under temporary custody. I did not know there were two.”

Richard paused.

“I was told the second placement would be handled separately.”

Octavian looked at him.

“Who told you?”

“Mara Voss.”

“Did she mention Aurelius?”

“No.”

“Did she mention the Vespers?”

“No.”

Aria studied the document again.

“So you signed a confidential agreement, accepted a child into your estate, and never asked who the child was supposed to be.”

Richard lowered his eyes.

“Yes.”

“That's unbelievable.”

“I know.”

**11:00 a.m.**

Margaret Vale arrived at Vesper Estate.

Victoria met her at the entrance.

For a moment, she simply stood there.

Then Margaret opened her arms.

Victoria stepped into the embrace.

Neither spoke for several seconds.

Eventually, Margaret pulled back.

“You look tired.”

Victoria gave a small smile.

“Everyone keeps telling me that.”

“Perhaps you should sleep.”

“I've been sleeping.”

“Then perhaps you should eat.”

Victoria glanced toward the dining room.

“That's what everyone here says.”

Margaret laughed.

“Some things never change.”

They walked inside together.

At the dining table, Helena had prepared a quiet breakfast.

Margaret looked around.

“It's a beautiful house.”

Victoria sat down.

“It's enormous.”

“Are you comfortable here?”

“I don't know.”

Margaret nodded.

“You don't have to decide today.”

Victoria looked at her.

“You really don't mind?”

“Mind what?”

“If I stay here. Or go back to the Sterlings. Or stay somewhere else.”

Margaret smiled.

“I raised you to make your own decisions.”

Victoria looked down.

For years, she had believed that belonging meant being chosen.

Margaret had never made her earn it.

**12:40 p.m.**

Felix received an unexpected response from the Aurelius trust administrator.

The trust's archived correspondence included a letter addressed to Arthur Mercer.

It had been sent six months before the births.

The letter contained one instruction:

*The receiving families must remain unaware of the original allocation until both placements have been confirmed.*

Maximilian read it twice.

“Original allocation.”

Cassian looked at the screen.

“So the families were not supposed to know which child belonged to which family?”

“That's what it says,” Felix replied.

Lucian frowned.

“Why would anyone arrange that?”

“Because the allocation was being controlled by someone outside both families,” Octavian said.

Aria leaned forward.

“Does the letter identify the children?”

“No,” Felix replied.

“Then we're still missing the most important part.”

He nodded.

“There is an attachment reference, but the attachment itself is missing.”

Marcus looked at the reference number.

**AC-09 / SCHEDULE B.**

“Where was Schedule B stored?”

Felix checked.

“An Aurelius legal office.”

“Can we retrieve it?”

“Not without their cooperation or a court order.”

Octavian considered the problem.

“Then we will request it formally.”

Cassian looked surprised.

“Formally?”

“Yes.”

“That's refreshingly ordinary.”

Octavian gave him a cold look.

Cassian sat straighter.

**2:15 p.m.**

At the Aurelius Consortium headquarters, a senior executive received the Vesper request.

He read it once.

Then again.

The request sought access to an archived trust schedule connected to a twenty-year-old administrative arrangement.

He called the legal department.

“Who authorised this?”

The lawyer examined the request.

“The Vesper family.”

The executive put down the paper.

“Why?”

“They are investigating the transfer of two infants.”

The executive stared at him.

“Two infants?”

“Yes.”

He stood and walked toward the window.

“Does the archive contain Schedule B?”

“Yes.”

“Who has access?”

“Only the former trust administrator and the current records custodian.”

“Contact them.”

The lawyer hesitated.

“Should we release the document?”

The executive turned.

“No.”

“On what grounds?”

“Confidentiality.”

The lawyer nodded.

The executive looked at the request again.

Then he made a second call.

**3:05 p.m.**

Mara Voss received a message.

**Associate:** Aurelius has received the Vesper request.

She read it.

A second message followed.

**Associate:** They have refused access to Schedule B.

Mara closed her eyes.

For twenty years, she had believed the missing schedule would remain buried.

Now the Vespers knew it existed.

She opened the locked drawer and removed the original registration extract.

Then she took out a second document.

It contained the names of both infants.

One had been assigned to the Vesper family.

The other to the Sterling family.

But the allocation had been crossed out.

A handwritten replacement instruction appeared beneath it.

*First placement: Sterling estate.*

*Second placement: Vesper Life Sciences.*

The signature was not Mara's.

It belonged to someone whose name had never appeared in the hospital records.

She folded the document and placed it in a separate envelope.

**Vesper Estate — 5:30 p.m.**

Aria was sitting with Isolde in the garden when Marcus approached.

“There's been a development.”

Aria looked up.

“Good or bad?”

“Complicated.”

“That's not a category.”

“It's the most accurate one.”

He explained that Aurelius had refused the formal request for Schedule B.

Isolde looked toward the house.

“Then they know what is in it.”

“Or they simply don't want confidential records released,” Marcus replied.

Aria closed her notebook.

“Can they legally refuse?”

“Our legal team is reviewing the basis of the refusal.”

“Then let them review it.”

Marcus nodded.

Aria looked toward the garden gate.

“Are we going to keep investigating until we know who ordered the arrangement?”

“Yes.”

“And after that?”

Marcus paused.

“That depends on what we discover.”

Aria considered this.

“Fine.”

Isolde smiled.

“You don't seem frightened.”

“I am.”

Isolde looked at her.

Aria shrugged.

“I just don't see the point in pretending I'm not.”

Isolde reached over and gently squeezed her hand.

**8:50 p.m.**

Victoria received an envelope at the Sterling mansion.

There was no return address.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

*You were not the only child whose identity was changed.*

Beneath it was a photograph.

A little girl stood beside a hospital window.

Victoria recognised herself.

But there was another child in the photograph.

A baby in a cot beside her.

The photograph was dated two days after the births.

On the back, someone had written:

**SUBJECT 02 — FIRST PLACEMENT CONFIRMED.**

Victoria stared at the image.

Then she called Margaret.

“Did Jonathan ever show you a photograph of another baby?”

Margaret was quiet.

“No. Why?”

Victoria looked at the picture.

“Because I think there was another child.”

**Unknown Location — 11:20 p.m.**

The Aurelius executive stood in a private office.

A folder lay open on his desk.

The cover read:

**AC-09 — TWO-FAMILY PLACEMENT**

He turned to the final page.

The original allocation schedule was still attached.

Two names were listed.

**SUBJECT 01 — STERLING**

**SUBJECT 02 — VESPER**

A red line had been drawn through both entries.

Below it, a new instruction had been written:

*Reverse allocation. Do not notify either family.*

The executive closed the folder.

Then he called someone.

“They've found the arrangement.”

A voice answered.

“Who?”

“The Vespers.”

There was a long silence.

Finally, the voice said:

“Then we have a problem.”

The executive looked at the photograph of the two infants.

“No,” he replied.

“We have had a problem for twenty years.”
