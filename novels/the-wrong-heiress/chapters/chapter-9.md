# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 9 — The Woman at the Door**

**Tuesday — 7:20 a.m.**

Aria had developed a routine.

Wake up.

Breakfast.

Work.

Return home.

Dinner.

Sleep.

It was a perfectly respectable routine, and she intended to protect it.

Unfortunately, her phone had other plans.

A message from Marcus appeared.

**Marcus:** Good morning, Miss Aria. I wanted to inform you that our investigation has identified another person connected to the hospital records.

**Aria:** Is this person alive?

**Marcus:** As far as we know.

**Aria:** Excellent. That's progress.

**Marcus:** Her name is Miriam Shaw.

Aria stared at the name.

It meant nothing to her.

**Aria:** What did she do?

**Marcus:** She worked in the hospital's legal administration department and later authorised the transfer of certain records.

**Aria:** Do you know why?

**Marcus:** Not yet.

**Aria:** Then let me know when you do.

She put her phone away.

She had learned that mysterious names were not particularly useful without explanations.

**Vesper Estate — 8:40 a.m.**

Damian was not pleased.

The search for Miriam Shaw had produced an address, but the property had been sold several years earlier.

The current owner had no information about her.

Her last recorded employment was also outdated.

Her bank accounts had been closed.

Her tax records showed no recent activity.

It was as if she had simply stopped existing.

"She didn't disappear without leaving a trace," Damian said.

Maximilian looked at the report.

"Then someone removed the trace."

"Possibly."

Cassian sat across from them.

"Or she changed her name."

Felix looked up.

"That would explain the lack of current records."

"Can you find her?"

"I can search for people with matching historical details. But without a confirmed date of birth or a current address, it will take time."

Damian nodded.

"Start with her former business associates."

Marcus entered.

"There's another development."

Maximilian looked up.

"What?"

"A woman has requested a private meeting with the Vesper family."

"Who?"

"She gave the name Eleanor Shaw."

Damian stood immediately.

"Shaw?"

Marcus nodded.

"She claims to have information about the hospital switch."

The room fell silent.

"Where is she?" Maximilian asked.

"At Vesper Tower. Reception has kept her in a private waiting room."

Damian picked up his phone.

"Don't let her leave."

Marcus gave him a look.

"Politely, I hope."

Damian was already walking.

**Vesper Tower — 9:05 a.m.**

The woman sat quietly in the waiting room.

She appeared to be in her late fifties.

Her clothes were modest, her hair neatly arranged, and her hands rested on a small handbag.

When Damian entered, she stood.

"Mr. Vesper?"

"Yes. You're Eleanor Shaw?"

"Yes."

"You requested this meeting."

"I did."

"Why?"

She looked towards the closed door.

"Because I know what happened twenty years ago."

Damian studied her.

"How?"

"I worked at the hospital."

"Under what name?"

The woman hesitated.

Then she answered.

"Miriam Shaw."

Damian did not react.

He simply closed the door.

**Vesper Tower — 9:18 a.m.**

The meeting room contained Damian, Maximilian, Marcus and Miriam.

A glass of water sat untouched in front of her.

Damian placed a copy of the temporary access authorisation on the table.

"Do you recognise this?"

Miriam looked at the document.

Her expression changed.

"Yes."

"Did you arrange Daniel Mercer's access?"

"No."

"Did you authorise the transfer of the hospital records?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Miriam lowered her eyes.

"Because I was instructed to."

"By whom?"

"I don't know."

Damian leaned forward.

"You authorised a transfer without knowing who requested it?"

"I was given a sealed instruction."

"By whom?"

"A woman."

"Name?"

"I never knew it."

Maximilian studied her.

"Describe her."

"She was in her thirties. Well dressed. She carried an official-looking document. She knew the hospital's administrative procedures."

"Did you verify her authority?"

Miriam shook her head.

"No."

Damian's expression hardened.

"Then why did you comply?"

"Because the instruction came through the hospital's private administrative office. I believed it had been approved by senior management."

"Did you check?"

"No."

Silence followed.

Miriam looked at the document again.

"I was wrong."

**Vesper Tower — 9:45 a.m.**

Maximilian asked the next question.

"What happened to the records?"

Miriam took a slow breath.

"I transferred the files to Westbridge Administrative Services. The instruction was to preserve the original records separately from the hospital's main archive."

"Who collected them?"

"A courier."

"Name?"

"I don't know."

"Company?"

"I don't remember."

Damian placed another document on the table.

"Was this the courier's receipt?"

Miriam looked at it.

Then she shook her head.

"No."

"Explain."

"The person who collected the files signed a different document. I was told the receipt would be included in the transfer package."

"Was it?"

"I don't know."

Damian glanced at Marcus.

The missing receipt was beginning to matter.

"Did you know the records concerned two newborn children?"

Miriam's hands tightened.

"Yes."

"Did you know they had been switched?"

She closed her eyes.

"Not at the time."

Maximilian's expression changed.

"At what time did you learn?"

"Several weeks later."

"How?"

Miriam looked up.

"Someone called me."

"Who?"

"The same woman."

"What did she say?"

Miriam swallowed.

"She told me that the records must remain separate. She said the children were safe and that the matter would be resolved later."

"Did you believe her?"

"I wanted to."

Damian's voice became colder.

"That is not an answer."

Miriam looked at him.

"No. I didn't know what to believe."

**Vesper Tower — 10:20 a.m.**

Miriam reached into her handbag.

Damian immediately looked at her hands.

She stopped.

"May I?"

He nodded.

She removed a small envelope and placed it on the table.

"I kept this."

Maximilian opened it.

Inside was a photograph.

A woman stood outside a hospital entrance.

Her face was partially turned away from the camera.

On the back was a handwritten date.

The date was two days before the births.

Damian examined it.

"Who took this?"

"I did."

"Why?"

"Because I was frightened."

"Of what?"

Miriam looked at the photograph.

"She had come to the hospital several times. She knew details that ordinary visitors shouldn't have known. She asked questions about the maternity ward, the records department and the security arrangements."

Maximilian leaned forward.

"Did you report her?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because she had documents that appeared legitimate. And because she knew the names of senior administrators."

Damian placed the photograph in a protective sleeve.

"Do you know where she is now?"

"No."

"Do you know whether she was involved in the switch?"

Miriam looked at him.

"I know she was involved in the records transfer."

She paused.

"But I don't know whether she arranged the switch."

**Vesper Tower — 11:05 a.m.**

The meeting ended without an arrest, an accusation or a dramatic confession.

Miriam was not detained.

She was asked to remain available for further questioning, and her statement was recorded through the family's legal representatives.

Damian watched her leave.

"She's hiding something."

Maximilian looked at him.

"Perhaps."

"She admitted to transferring the records without verifying the authority."

"That is serious."

"She also kept the photograph for twenty years."

"That may mean she was frightened. It may mean she was protecting herself. We don't know."

Marcus joined them.

"Should we tell Octavian?"

"Immediately," Maximilian said.

**Aria's Workplace — 12:30 p.m.**

Aria was having a perfectly ordinary day.

She had completed the invoice reconciliation, organised the previous month's payment records and corrected a spreadsheet formula.

Peter Collins approached her desk.

"Miss Sterling."

"Yes?"

"Could you check the supplier statement for the Eastwood account?"

"Certainly."

He handed her the file.

She examined it.

After a few minutes, she noticed a mismatch.

"The statement includes a payment that isn't in our ledger."

Peter looked over her shoulder.

"Which one?"

"This one. It has been credited by the supplier, but we haven't recorded it."

He checked the document.

"You're right. Good catch."

Aria smiled.

"At least the numbers are behaving today."

Peter laughed.

"Don't get too comfortable."

She returned to work.

Her phone vibrated once.

A message from Marcus.

**Marcus:** We have identified Miriam Shaw. She has agreed to provide a statement.

Aria read it.

**Aria:** Did she explain what happened?

**Marcus:** Not completely.

**Aria:** Then continue investigating.

She put her phone away.

She was not going to spend every afternoon waiting for another piece of her past to appear.

**Vesper Estate — 2:15 p.m.**

Octavian studied the photograph.

Isolde sat beside him.

"Do you recognise her?" she asked.

"No."

"Could she be connected to the hospital staff?"

"Possibly."

He looked at Damian.

"Have the photograph examined."

"Already requested."

"Any identifying features?"

"Not enough for a reliable match. The image is old, and the face is partly obscured."

Octavian looked at Miriam's statement.

"She says the woman had access to senior administrative information."

"Yes."

"Then someone inside the hospital was helping her."

"That's likely."

Isolde turned towards him.

"Or someone had stolen the information."

Octavian nodded.

"Either way, we need the original records."

He put the photograph down.

"Find the courier."

**Sterling Mansion — 4:50 p.m.**

Victoria was furious.

Her private investigator had returned with almost nothing.

Miriam Shaw had not appeared in any current public records.

Daniel Mercer was equally difficult to locate.

The investigator had found no reliable connection between the two beyond their involvement with the hospital's old administrative system.

Victoria paced across her room.

"How can two people simply disappear?"

Her phone vibrated.

The unknown number.

**Unknown:** Did you find Miriam?

Victoria froze.

She typed immediately.

**Victoria:** Yes. The Vespers found her.

**Unknown:** What did she tell them?

**Victoria:** I don't know. I'm not in their meetings.

A pause.

**Unknown:** Then you need to find the courier.

Victoria stared at the screen.

**Victoria:** Why?

**Unknown:** Because the courier collected the original records.

**Victoria:** Who sent the courier?

No answer.

Victoria typed again.

**Victoria:** You know who arranged this.

The reply came after almost a minute.

**Unknown:** I know who benefited.

Victoria's fingers stopped.

**Victoria:** Who?

The message did not arrive.

Instead, the number went offline.

Victoria threw her phone onto the bed.

"Unbelievable."

She picked it up again almost immediately.

**Vesper Estate — 7:30 p.m.**

The family gathered for dinner.

Aria was not there.

The subject of the conversation was Miriam Shaw.

Lucian looked at the photograph.

"She could be the key to everything."

"She could also be a minor participant," Maximilian replied.

Damian shook his head.

"She handled the records transfer. That makes her important."

"Important doesn't mean responsible for the switch."

Octavian listened.

Then he spoke.

"We have spent twenty years looking for our granddaughter. We will not ruin the investigation by deciding what happened before we have evidence."

Cassian leaned back.

"And the woman in the photograph?"

"Find her," Octavian said.

Felix raised his tablet.

"There may be a way to identify her."

Everyone looked at him.

"The photograph was taken outside the hospital's east entrance. That entrance had a security camera."

Damian frowned.

"Those recordings would be long gone."

"Not necessarily. The hospital's old security system backed up selected still images into its incident archive."

Maximilian looked at him.

"Can you access them?"

"With the hospital's permission, yes."

Octavian nodded.

"Request the archive."

**Unknown Location — 10:40 p.m.**

The woman from the photograph sat at a desk.

A laptop displayed a message.

**Miriam Shaw has spoken to the Vespers.**

She read it without expression.

Another message followed.

**The photograph has been handed over.**

She stood.

For several seconds, she looked at a closed cabinet.

Then she opened it.

Inside were old documents, photographs and a small envelope marked with the date of the births.

She removed one sheet.

It was a handwritten instruction.

The ink had faded, but the words remained readable.

**Keep the children separate. Do not allow either family to discover the arrangement until the second transfer is complete.**

She read it once.

Then she folded it carefully.

Her phone rang.

She answered.

"Yes?"

A man's voice spoke on the other end.

"They have found Miriam."

"I know."

"They will reach the archive next."

"Then we have less time than I thought."

"Should I contact her?"

The woman looked at the photograph on her desk.

"No."

"Then what?"

She was silent for a moment.

"Find Daniel Mercer."

The man hesitated.

"He's been missing for years."

"Then stop looking for where he went."

She placed the photograph inside the envelope.

"Find out who helped him leave."

And for the first time, the investigation had begun moving in two directions.

One towards the people who had hidden the truth.

The other towards the person who had been trying to keep it buried.
