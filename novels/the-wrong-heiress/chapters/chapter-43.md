# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 43 — The Man Who Was Supposed to Protect Them**

**Friday — 8:35 a.m.**

Blackwood House had an unusual security arrangement.

There were no visible guards.

No reception desk.

No impressive entrance hall.

Instead, every visitor was required to surrender their electronic devices, sign a confidentiality declaration, and wait in a small room containing precisely three chairs.

Cassian examined the room.

“Three chairs.”

Damian looked at him.

“Yes.”

“Are we expecting a third person?”

“Possibly.”

“Or is the furniture simply optimistic?”

Maximilian ignored them.

Aria sat down.

She had brought a packet of biscuits.

Marcus noticed.

“Did you bring those for the meeting?”

“No. I brought them because I was hungry.”

“Of course.”

Victoria arrived five minutes later.

She looked at the biscuits.

“Are those the only refreshments?”

“Yes,” Aria replied.

Victoria stared at her.

“You came to a meeting with the most powerful private dynasty in the world carrying supermarket biscuits?”

“They were on sale.”

Cassian quietly took one.

“Excellent financial judgment.”

**9:00 a.m.**

Adrian Blackwood entered.

He was in his late seventies, with silver hair and a measured manner.

He greeted Octavian first.

“It's been a long time.”

“Too long.”

Blackwood turned to Isolde.

“My lady.”

“Adrian.”

He then looked at Aria and Victoria.

“So these are the two descendants.”

Victoria raised an eyebrow.

“We have names.”

Blackwood smiled faintly.

“My apologies. Aria and Victoria.”

He sat down.

“Before we begin, I want to make something clear. I am not here to defend the trust, Elias, or anyone involved in the original arrangement.”

“Then why are you here?” Lucian asked.

“To explain what I know.”

Blackwood opened a leather folder.

He removed the photograph.

Octavian immediately recognised it.

“Where did you get that?”

“Elias gave it to me.”

“Who is the third man?”

Blackwood placed the photograph on the table.

“His name was **Edmund Voss**.”

Felix immediately began searching his records.

“Was he part of the Vesper administration?”

“No. He was the family's chief protective adviser.”

Damian looked up.

“Chief protective adviser?”

Blackwood nodded.

“He was responsible for identifying threats against the family, including threats involving succession.”

Octavian's expression hardened.

“He was supposed to protect the children.”

“Yes.”

Blackwood paused.

“He was also the person who first requested their identities.”

**9:25 a.m.**

Blackwood explained that Edmund Voss had served the Vesper family for almost thirty years.

He had access to private medical arrangements, security protocols, family movements, and succession records.

He had been trusted by Elias.

He had also been trusted by Octavian.

Shortly before the births, Voss requested the expected children's identities from the family medical office.

The request was rejected.

Three days later, the same request was submitted using Margaret Ellison's credentials.

The medical office approved it.

“Did he receive the information?” Maximilian asked.

“Not directly. The records were transferred to a restricted archive.”

“Then how did the children become targets?”

Blackwood looked at the photograph.

“Because Edmund knew there were two children.”

Octavian's jaw tightened.

“Why would he want their identities?”

“Elias believed Voss intended to use the succession provision to control the inheritance.”

Cassian leaned forward.

“Control it how?”

Blackwood opened another document.

“By ensuring that only one descendant could ever satisfy the conditions.”

Victoria frowned.

“Why would that benefit him?”

“Because the trust's controlling rights would pass through the designated successor. The person who controlled the verification process could influence the appointment.”

Aria looked at him.

“So he wanted to control whoever inherited.”

“Precisely.”

“And Elias found out?”

“Yes.”

Blackwood removed another letter.

“He confronted Voss. Shortly afterward, the allocation reversal was submitted.”

**10:10 a.m.**

Felix examined the letter.

The date was three days before the reversal.

It was addressed to Edmund Voss.

*You were appointed to protect the family, not to determine its future.*

*The children are not instruments of your authority.*

*If you continue attempting to obtain their identities, I will remove you from the protective office.*

The letter was signed by Elias.

Octavian read it silently.

“Did Elias remove him?”

Blackwood shook his head.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because Voss disappeared before the formal removal could be completed.”

Damian frowned.

“Disappeared?”

“He resigned. His official departure was recorded as a private retirement.”

“Was he investigated?”

“No. Elias believed he had left the matter behind.”

Octavian looked at him.

“And you?”

“I did not believe him.”

Blackwood opened the final page.

“Edmund Voss continued receiving payments from a private account after his resignation.”

Marcus examined the transaction records.

“Who funded the account?”

Blackwood looked at Octavian.

“The Vesper estate.”

Silence.

**11:00 a.m.**

Octavian's expression was cold.

“Who authorised those payments?”

Blackwood answered carefully.

“The payments were made under a standing security contract.”

“Who signed it?”

“Lucian Vesper.”

Lucian stared at him.

“I never signed a contract with Edmund Voss after his resignation.”

Blackwood nodded.

“I know.”

Felix searched the archived records.

“The contract was renewed automatically through an administrative authority.”

“Which authority?” Maximilian asked.

Felix checked.

“Legacy Authority LA-01.”

The same authority used to reverse the children's allocation.

Cassian leaned back.

“So someone kept paying the man who was supposed to protect them, using the same authority that changed their destinations.”

Blackwood nodded.

“Yes.”

Victoria looked at Octavian.

“Was he still working for the family when I was taken from the Sterling estate?”

“No,” Octavian said.

Blackwood corrected him.

“His official position had ended. His payments had not.”

Victoria looked back at Blackwood.

“Then he may have been involved in the second transfer.”

“He may have been.”

“Do you know where he is?”

Blackwood closed the folder.

“No.”

**12:30 p.m.**

Aria had been quiet throughout the meeting.

Now she spoke.

“Can I ask something?”

Blackwood turned toward her.

“Certainly.”

“You said Edmund wanted to control the inheritance. But why did he need to separate us?”

“Because the trust required both descendants to verify the original claim.”

“Then if we were raised together, we could have compared everything.”

“Correct.”

“So separating us made it easier to control the information.”

Blackwood nodded.

“Yes.”

Aria considered this.

“Did he actually succeed?”

Blackwood looked at her.

“For a time.”

“But now we're both here.”

“Yes.”

“And neither of us wants the inheritance.”

Blackwood gave a small smile.

“That complicates matters.”

Victoria crossed her arms.

“It doesn't complicate anything. We haven't agreed to participate.”

Blackwood nodded.

“Your decision is your own.”

Aria looked at the photograph.

“Then find Edmund Voss. He seems to be the person who can explain why all of this happened.”

**2:15 p.m. — Vesper Tower**

The investigation team began tracing Edmund Voss.

His official records showed:

- Former Chief Protective Adviser.
- Resigned twenty-two years earlier.
- Pension account closed.
- Private security contract terminated.
- No verified address after resignation.

Felix searched the payment records.

He found a recurring transfer.

The recipient was an account registered to a company called **Northgate Risk Advisory**.

The company had been dissolved.

But its bank account had remained active for another six years.

“Who closed it?” Damian asked.

Felix checked the final transaction.

“Margaret Ellison.”

Cassian sighed.

“Margaret again.”

Marcus looked at the payment date.

“This was after her official resignation.”

“Exactly,” Felix replied.

Maximilian pointed to the final entry.

“Where was the money transferred?”

Felix opened the destination record.

A private account.

The beneficiary name was hidden behind a legal restriction.

But the bank's internal reference remained visible.

**EV-09.**

Octavian stared at the screen.

“Edmund Voss.”

**4:40 p.m. — Sterling Mansion**

Victoria received a call from an unknown number.

She answered.

“Who is this?”

A man's voice replied.

“You have been asking about your original placement.”

Victoria sat down.

“Who are you?”

“Someone who knows what happened.”

“Then tell me.”

A pause.

“You should stop trusting the Vespers.”

Victoria's expression hardened.

“Why?”

“Because they knew the children were in danger before you were born.”

“Who knew?”

“The family.”

“Which member?”

The caller did not answer.

Victoria stood.

“If you want me to believe you, give me something verifiable.”

The caller said one final sentence.

“Ask Octavian why he never reported Edmund Voss missing.”

Then the call ended.

Victoria immediately contacted Marcus.

**6:00 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian listened to Victoria's account.

“Did the caller identify himself?”

“No.”

“Did he provide evidence?”

“No.”

Marcus placed the recovered security records on the desk.

“There is something else.”

He displayed an old personnel report.

**EDMUND VOSS — STATUS: DECEASED.**

The report was dated twenty-one years earlier.

The death had been recorded following a private vehicle accident.

But the supporting evidence was incomplete.

No body had been identified.

No independent medical confirmation existed.

Octavian looked at the report.

“Who approved this?”

Marcus checked the signature.

“Margaret Ellison.”

Isolde slowly closed her eyes.

“Then she knew.”

**9:45 p.m. — Unknown Residence**

An elderly man watched the news on a television with the sound turned off.

The screen displayed a photograph of the Vesper family.

The headline mentioned the recovery of the original succession instrument.

He switched off the television.

A woman entered carrying a folder.

“They have found the payment records.”

The man nodded.

“Of course they have.”

“They also found your old personnel report.”

He looked at her.

“Did they find the accident file?”

“No.”

“Good.”

The woman placed the folder on the table.

Inside was a copy of the original protective assignment.

The final line read:

**EDMUND VOSS — RESPONSIBLE FOR THE SAFETY OF BOTH DESCENDANTS.**

The man looked at it for a long time.

Then he said:

“Tell them I am willing to meet.”

The woman hesitated.

“After all these years?”

“Yes.”

“Why now?”

He looked toward the dark television.

“Because they are finally asking the right question.”

He closed the folder.

“Who ordered me to protect the children?”

**11:20 p.m. — Vesper Estate**

Marcus received the message.

He read it twice before handing his phone to Octavian.

The message contained a location and a name.

**Edmund Voss requests a meeting.**

Octavian looked at the address.

Then at the old photograph.

“He's alive.”

Isolde stood beside him.

“Apparently.”

Octavian looked toward the family portraits.

For twenty years, he had believed that the man responsible for protecting his family had died.

Now that man wanted to meet.

And he had waited until both children were adults before coming forward.

Octavian picked up the telephone.

“Arrange the meeting.”

Then he added:

“And this time, nobody leaves the room without telling us the truth.”
