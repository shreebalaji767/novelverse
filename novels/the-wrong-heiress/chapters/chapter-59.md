# **THE WRONG HEIRESS**

## **Chapter 59 — The Authority of a Dead Man**

**Saturday — 9:42 a.m.**

“Read it again.”

Octavian stood behind Felix's chair.

Felix enlarged the document.

“Temporary replacement account M. Voss. Created through Ellison House terminal LA-01. Authorised under the temporary executive authority assigned to Elias Vesper.”

“Not signed by Elias?”

“No.”

“Not personally approved by him?”

“We don't have that evidence.”

Octavian stared at the screen.

“My brother's authority was used to create a hospital access account under a name that may have belonged to his niece.”

Catherine's expression hardened.

“Or someone used his authority to make it appear that way.”

“Either possibility is unacceptable.”

“Neither becomes true simply because we dislike the alternative.”

Octavian turned towards her.

“You are defending him?”

“I am refusing to accuse a dead man without proof.”

For a moment, the room was silent.

Cassian quietly moved a glass away from the edge of the table.

He had learned that when Octavian and Catherine disagreed, nearby objects had an unfortunate tendency to become involved.

Maximilian pointed at the document.

“The terminal record gives us a location, not an operator. We need the access register.”

Margaret Ellison stood beside the desk.

“I may be able to recover it.”

Everyone looked at her.

“You kept the register?” Thomas asked.

“I kept a copy of the temporary access ledger. The original was removed from Ellison House years ago.”

“By whom?”

“I don't know.”

Margaret looked at the terminal reference.

“But I remember the day the account was created.”

Catherine turned.

“You were there?”

“Yes.”

“Why didn't you tell us?”

“Because I didn't know what the account was for.”

Margaret paused.

“And because I was instructed not to ask.”

**10:15 a.m. — Ellison House**

Margaret unlocked a narrow cabinet in the old administrative office.

Inside were several ledgers, sealed envelopes and a small metal box.

Thomas watched her remove a thin notebook.

“This is the temporary access ledger?”

“Yes. It records who entered the building and which terminal they used.”

She opened it.

The relevant date appeared halfway down the page.

**10:12 a.m. — Executive terminal LA-01 accessed.**

**Authority: Temporary Executive Custodian.**

**Purpose: Administrative verification.**

The operator field contained a single initial.

**M.**

Thomas leaned closer.

“Margaret?”

“No.”

“Catherine?”

“No.”

“Then who?”

Margaret turned the page.

The same initial appeared again, followed by a note.

**Access conducted remotely. Physical attendance not required.**

Hale frowned.

“Someone accessed the terminal remotely?”

“Yes.”

“Was remote access permitted?”

“Only with executive approval.”

“Whose approval?”

Margaret checked the next entry.

There was no signature.

Only a reference number.

**EX-04.**

Thomas looked at Vane.

“Does that mean anything to you?”

Vane examined the notation.

“It was an emergency executive access classification.”

“Who could use it?”

“The executive custodian, or someone holding delegated authority.”

“Who held delegated authority?”

Vane did not answer immediately.

“Elias.”

Catherine's eyes narrowed.

“Was he the only one?”

“No.”

“Who else?”

Vane looked at the notebook.

“The person who maintained the emergency authority register.”

Margaret's expression changed.

“That register was not kept at Ellison House.”

“Where was it kept?” Hale asked.

Vane looked at him.

“In the patriarchal private office.”

**11:03 a.m. — Vesper Estate**

Octavian listened without interrupting.

When Vane finished, he asked one question.

“Who maintained my private office register?”

Vane answered carefully.

“Your executive secretary at the time.”

“Name.”

“Arthur Bell.”

Felix searched the old personnel records.

“Arthur Bell retired twenty years ago. He died eleven years later.”

Octavian nodded once.

“I remember him.”

“Did he have authority to grant remote access?” Maximilian asked.

“No,” Vane replied. “He could record approvals. He could not create them.”

“Then someone had to authorise the access.”

“Yes.”

“Where is the approval?”

Vane looked at Octavian.

“In the patriarchal office archive.”

Octavian rose.

“Then we will examine it.”

Catherine stood too.

“I am coming.”

Octavian glanced at her.

“You are not required to.”

“I didn't ask whether I was required.”

He held her gaze for a moment.

Then nodded.

**11:48 a.m. — Patriarchal Private Office**

The office had barely changed in two decades.

The furniture was dark wood. The curtains were heavy. The walls held portraits of former Vesper patriarchs.

Octavian opened the archive cabinet himself.

Inside were several bound registers.

Felix found the relevant volume.

The emergency access request was there.

**EX-04 — Remote Executive Access.**

The authorising office was listed as the Patriarchal Private Office.

The authorising officer's name was not recorded.

Instead, there was a code.

**P-01.**

Octavian looked at it.

“That is my office code.”

Lucian frowned.

“Could someone have used it without your knowledge?”

“Not without access to the private authority seal.”

“Where was the seal?”

“In this office.”

Damian examined the cabinet.

“Was it secured?”

“Yes.”

“Who had access?”

Octavian looked towards the old office door.

“Myself. My secretary. And my brother.”

Catherine's face became still.

“Elias had access to your private office?”

“He was my younger brother.”

“That isn't an answer.”

“It is the reason.”

Catherine looked at the register again.

“Was his access ever formally revoked?”

Octavian said nothing.

Felix searched the personnel archive.

A few seconds later, he stopped.

“Grandfather.”

Octavian turned.

“What?”

“The access record says Elias's authority remained active until the day his death was formally registered.”

“That's correct.”

“But the death registration was entered six months after his reported death.”

Lucian looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because the original death confirmation was disputed. The registration was delayed pending independent verification.”

Vane's expression changed.

“Who disputed it?”

Octavian looked towards him.

“You tell me.”

Vane lowered his eyes.

“I was not the executive custodian at that time.”

“Then who was?”

Vane answered quietly.

“Alistair Vane.”

The room fell silent.

Hale looked at him.

“You were supposed to be dead.”

“I have been reminded.”

**12:26 p.m. — Patriarchal Private Office**

Vane stood before the register.

He had not touched it since the discovery.

“Elias's death was reported to me,” he said. “I received a preliminary confirmation. It was incomplete.”

“Why?” Maximilian asked.

“Because the body had not been independently identified.”

Octavian's expression darkened.

“You never told me.”

“I was preparing to.”

“And then?”

“Someone submitted a second confirmation.”

“Who?”

“The medical officer.”

“Name?”

Vane looked towards Felix.

“Find the original death verification.”

Felix opened the archive.

He found the document within minutes.

The medical officer's signature was present.

The second signature belonged to Elias's executive office.

The certification had been countersigned by a temporary administrative representative.

**D. HALE.**

Everyone turned towards Dorian Hale.

Hale stared at the paper.

“I didn't sign that.”

Margaret stepped closer.

“Your signature is there.”

“I resigned before the final death registration.”

“Your credentials were still active,” Thomas said.

Hale shook his head.

“My credentials were supposed to have been deactivated.”

Vane looked at the document.

“The countersignature only confirmed receipt of the medical verification.”

Hale's voice was cold.

“I never received it.”

Felix compared the signature against Hale's authenticated personnel records.

“The handwriting is similar.”

“Similar?” Hale repeated.

“Not identical.”

Hale leaned over the page.

There were small differences in the final stroke of the surname and the spacing between the initials.

He pointed at them.

“Whoever wrote this knew my signature.”

Margaret looked at the old hospital photograph.

The man who resembled Hale.

The access account registered under M. Voss.

The death verification bearing Hale's signature.

Thomas slowly placed the documents together.

“Someone has been using identities to move authority between institutions.”

Catherine looked at him.

“Not just identities.”

She pointed at the dates.

“Authority.”

The same person—or the same operation—had been able to make a hospital clerk, a family office and a death registry accept instructions from people who might never have issued them.

**1:17 p.m. — Private Dining Room**

Aria arrived with Marcus.

She had agreed to attend the meeting after receiving a short explanation. She had also made it clear that she would leave if it became another family council.

The family was already assembled.

Victoria sat opposite her, reading a copy of the hospital access document.

Aria took the empty chair.

“What's happened?”

Felix placed three documents on the table.

“Someone used Elias's temporary executive authority to create the M. Voss hospital account. The same authority was linked to a remote access request under Octavian's office code. We also found a death verification countersigned using Dorian Hale's credentials.”

Aria looked at the papers.

“Was Hale there?”

“No,” Margaret said.

“Was Elias there?”

“We don't know,” Felix replied.

“Was the person using the authority actually authorised?”

“No.”

Aria nodded.

“So we have several documents showing that someone could make institutions believe an instruction came from someone else.”

“Precisely,” Maximilian said.

Victoria put down her copy.

“Then the question is who had the ability to do it.”

“And who benefited,” Cassian added.

Aria looked at the death verification.

“Why was Elias's death registration delayed?”

Octavian answered.

“The body had not been independently identified.”

“Then why did everyone treat him as dead?”

“Because the second confirmation was accepted.”

Aria looked at Hale's forged signature.

“By people who thought he had signed it.”

Hale nodded.

“Yes.”

Aria leaned back.

“Then don't start by asking who killed Elias.”

Octavian studied her.

“What would you ask?”

“Who needed everyone to believe he was dead?”

Nobody spoke.

Victoria slowly looked up.

“That would explain why his authority stayed active.”

“Maybe,” Aria said. “Or why someone wanted it to appear active.”

Felix's eyes brightened.

“Those are two different possibilities.”

“Good. Keep both.”

Victoria looked at the documents again.

“And if Elias was alive when someone used his authority?”

“Then we need to know whether he approved it,” Aria replied.

Octavian's expression became unreadable.

For the first time in years, the question surrounding his younger brother was no longer merely whether Elias had been murdered.

It was whether his death had been used as an administrative tool.

**2:02 p.m. — Private Dining Room**

A new message arrived on Felix's computer.

He read it.

Then read it again.

“What is it?” Maximilian asked.

“The external audit department has located its copy of the old council attendance register.”

Hale leaned forward.

“Mine?”

“Yes. Your attendance at the meeting is independently confirmed.”

“Good.”

“But there's another document attached.”

Felix opened it.

It was a copy of the death verification request.

The external audit department had received it six months before the official death registration.

The request had been rejected.

Reason:

**Insufficient independent identification.**

The rejection was signed by two auditors.

One of them had added a handwritten note.

**The individual presented for verification does not match the available identification record.**

Octavian stared at the sentence.

“Presented?”

Felix enlarged the attachment.

There was a second page.

A photograph had been attached to the rejected verification request.

It showed a man lying in a hospital bed.

His face was partially obscured by bandages.

The date was visible.

The location was not.

Catherine stood.

“That's Elias.”

Octavian moved closer.

He stared at the photograph for a long time.

Then he looked at the rejection note.

“The auditors refused to identify him.”

“Yes,” Felix said.

“And someone submitted another verification.”

“Yes.”

“Using Hale's credentials.”

“Yes.”

Octavian's hands tightened.

“Find the two auditors.”

Felix looked at the document.

“One is deceased.”

“And the other?”

Felix checked the personnel entry.

His expression changed.

“The other is alive.”

“Name?”

Felix read it aloud.

“Martin Hale.”

Dorian Hale went completely still.

“That is my father.”
